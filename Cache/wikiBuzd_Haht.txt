<p><b>Buzád II Hahót, <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">O.P.</a></b>, also <b>Buzád the Great</b> or <b>Buzád the Elder</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Hahót nembeli (II.) Buzád</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Magnus Buzad</i>; c. 1180 – April 1241), was a Hungarian nobleman and soldier, who served as the first known <a href="/wiki/Banat_of_Severin" class="mw-redirect" title="Banat of Severin">Ban of Severin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos201149_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos201149-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He later gave up his position in society and entered the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>.
</p><p>Buzád was killed during a <a href="/wiki/Mongol_invasion" class="mw-redirect" title="Mongol invasion">Mongol invasion</a> of his homeland, and is now honored as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the Catholic Church, for which he has been <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> and is also known as <b>Blessed Buzád</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Boldog Buzád</i>).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ancestry_and_family">Ancestry and family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Buz%C3%A1d_Hah%C3%B3t&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Ancestry and family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>[...] They are sprung from the counts of Orlamund. The first to come was called Hadolch, whose son was called by the like name of Hadolch and also Arnold. From them sprang Banus Buzad. The people of this country could not pronounce Hadolch, and so he was called by the similar name of Hohold. [...]</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite><i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Pictum" title="Chronicon Pictum">Illuminated Chronicle</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<p>Buzád was born into the Buzád branch of the <a href="/wiki/Hah%C3%B3t_(genus)" title="Hahót (genus)">Hahót clan</a>, the son of Buzád I (died 1192).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarkó2006451_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarkó2006451-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to <i>magister</i> <a href="/wiki/%C3%81kos_(chronicler)" title="Ákos (chronicler)">Ákos</a>, the founder of the Hahót kindred was Buzád's grandfather, a certain German knight <a href="/wiki/Hahold_I_Hah%C3%B3t" title="Hahold I Hahót">Hahold I</a>, who himself was a descendant of the <a href="/wiki/Orlam%C3%BCnde" title="Orlamünde">Counts of Weimar-Orlamünde</a> and settled down in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a> in 1163 upon the invitation of <a href="/wiki/Stephen_III_of_Hungary" title="Stephen III of Hungary">Stephen III of Hungary</a> to fight against his usurper uncle <a href="/wiki/Stephen_IV_of_Hungary" title="Stephen IV of Hungary">Stephen IV of Hungary</a> and his allies, the <a href="/wiki/Cs%C3%A1k_(genus)" title="Csák (genus)">Csáks</a>. Buzád's brother was Arnold I (died c. 1234), who erected the family monastery at <a href="/wiki/Hah%C3%B3t,_Zala" title="Hahót, Zala">Hahót</a>. Buzád had four sons from his unidentified wife: Buzád III (his presumptive heir, who, however, predeceased his father around November 1239), <a href="/wiki/Cs%C3%A1k_I_Hah%C3%B3t" title="Csák I Hahót">Csák I</a>, <a href="/wiki/Voivode_of_Transylvania" title="Voivode of Transylvania">Voivode of Transylvania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tristan_Hah%C3%B3t" title="Tristan Hahót">Tristan</a> and Lancelot.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The two latter names represent the spread of chivalric culture in the Hungarian elite, specifically the Hahót kindred.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKurcz1988248_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKurcz1988248-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Buz%C3%A1d_Hah%C3%B3t&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a non-authentic <a href="/wiki/Charter" title="Charter">charter</a>, Buzád served as the <a href="/wiki/Isp%C3%A1n" title="Ispán">Ispán</a> (<i><a href="/wiki/Comes" title="Comes">comes</a></i>) of <a href="/wiki/Gy%C5%91r_County" title="Győr County">Győr County</a> in 1209.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011157_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011157-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> There is no record of him receiving any official positions for the coming two decades. In authentic contemporary records, Buzád and his (unidentified) descendants were first mentioned in 1215, when a certain knight of the queen's court, Wilermus, sold his lands between the <a href="/wiki/Mur_(river)" title="Mur (river)">Mura</a> and <a href="/wiki/Drava" title="Drava">Drava</a> rivers for 200 <a href="/wiki/Mark_(unit)" title="Mark (unit)">marks</a> to them.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020732_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020732-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1217, King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_II_of_Hungary" title="Andrew II of Hungary">Andrew II of Hungary</a> commissioned Buzád to determine the borders of those lands which he had donated to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Zagreb" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Zagreb">cathedral chapter of Zagreb</a> in that donation letter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020732_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020732-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> According to a late 17th-century family tree of his kindred, Buzád participated in the first phase of the <a href="/wiki/Fifth_Crusade" title="Fifth Crusade">Fifth Crusade</a> (1217–18), led by King Andrew II, albeit the anonymous author confused his name with the name of his grandfather and clan founder Hahold, but the rest of the data is the same as his life path.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETantalics200916–17_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETantalics200916–17-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hah%C3%B3t_nemzets%C3%A9g%C5%91s.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Hah%C3%B3t_nemzets%C3%A9g%C5%91s.jpg/220px-Hah%C3%B3t_nemzets%C3%A9g%C5%91s.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="242" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ca/Hah%C3%B3t_nemzets%C3%A9g%C5%91s.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="237" data-file-height="261" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hah%C3%B3t_nemzets%C3%A9g%C5%91s.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>There is speculation that this miniature of <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Pictum" title="Chronicon Pictum">Illuminated Chronicle</a></i> depicts Buzád, instead of his grandfather <a href="/wiki/Hahold_I_Hah%C3%B3t" title="Hahold I Hahót">Hahold I</a>, who most historians assume</div></div></div>
<p>He functioned as the Ispán of <a href="/wiki/Bihar_County" title="Bihar County">Bihar County</a> in 1222.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011139_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011139-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He was dismissed from his position, when a group of discontented barons staged a <a href="/wiki/Coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" title="Coup d&#39;état">coup d'état</a> in the spring of 1222. Following their failure, Buzád was the head of <a href="/wiki/Pozsony_County" title="Pozsony County">Pozsony County</a> between 1222 and 1224.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011183_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011183-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> During that time there were emerging tensions between King Andrew II and his son, <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV_of_Hungary" title="Béla IV of Hungary">Béla</a>. The latter rebelled against his father's rule, who was forced to share his realms with his heir. Buzád became a supporter of Béla, as a result of which he had to follow his lord into exile to Austria in 1223 (he already resided in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> in December of that year). In February 1224, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> sent a letter to Buzád – still referred to with the title Ispán of Pozsony–acknowledging his splendid allegiance to Duke Béla, risking his wealth and other goods and the dangers to which he was exposed by it. On the same day, the pope wrote to King Andrew II, warning him not to harm the property of Buzád and the other lords, who took refuge at the court of Duke <a href="/wiki/Leopold_VI,_Duke_of_Austria" title="Leopold VI, Duke of Austria">Leopold VI</a> with Béla.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020735_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020735-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> After reconciliation between father and son, Buzád returned to Hungary in the spring of 1224, and became the Ispán of <a href="/wiki/Vas_County_(former)" title="Vas County (former)">Vas County</a> in 1225, which then belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Slavonia" title="Duke of Slavonia">Duchy of Slavonia</a>, the realm of Béla.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarkó2006451_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarkó2006451-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Buzád served as the Ban of Severin from 1226 to c. 1232, when Béla governed <a href="/wiki/Transylvania" title="Transylvania">Transylvania</a> <i>de facto</i> independently from the king, holding the title of <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Transylvania" title="Duke of Transylvania">Duke of Transylvania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos201149_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos201149-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1233, Buzád called himself "former ban" (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">quondam banus</i>) in a charter; as a result former archontological and genealogical works of Hungarian historians (e.g. János Karácsonyi and Mór Wertner) referred to him as the <a href="/wiki/Ban_of_Slavonia" title="Ban of Slavonia">Ban of Slavonia</a> (1226–1228/9). Nevertheless it is more likely that Buzád held the office of Ban of Severin, because of his close relationship with Béla, and there is also reason to believe he came into contact during that period with the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican friars</a>, who were engaged in proselytizing among the <a href="/wiki/Cuman_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Cuman people">Cuman people</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011292_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011292-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Contrary to this, Romanian historian Sorin Forțiu claimed Buzád actually served as Ban of Slavonia, as he is perhaps identical with that office-holder with the "B" initial letter in his name, who was referred to by Duke Béla in his charter issued on 26 May 1224 at <a href="/wiki/Toplica_(river)" title="Toplica (river)">Toplica</a>, just after his return to Slavonia. Forțiu argued that the former publishers of the original diploma have misinterpreted the text, considering the abbreviation as a omission. According to this theory, he held the dignity relatively for a short time in the first half of 1224, between the terms of <a href="/wiki/Solomon_Atyusz" title="Solomon Atyusz">Solomon Atyusz</a> and <a href="/wiki/Michael,_son_of_Ampud" title="Michael, son of Ampud">Michael, son of Ampud</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020736–740_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020736–740-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> By 1228, Duke Béla's supporters took power in the royal council after another wave of dissatisfaction against Andrew's rule. The king was forced to authorize his son to revise his previous land grants throughout Hungary. Without referring to specific dignity, Ban Buzád appeared as a member of the royal council along with several other partisans of Béla – for instance, <a href="/wiki/Mojs_I" title="Mojs I">Mojs</a> and <a href="/wiki/Denis_Tomaj" title="Denis Tomaj">Denis Tomaj</a> – in that year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020741_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForțiu2020741-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alongside his cousin, <a href="/wiki/Michael_Hah%C3%B3t" title="Michael Hahót">Michael Hahót</a>, Buzád appeared as a witness in the so-called Kehida Diploma of 1232, where the royal servants of <a href="/wiki/Zala_County_(former)" title="Zala County (former)">Zala County</a> urged King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_II_of_Hungary" title="Andrew II of Hungary">Andrew II of Hungary</a> to recognise their verdict as compulsory, because <a href="/wiki/Atyusz_III_Atyusz" title="Atyusz III Atyusz">Atyusz III Atyusz</a> refused to give back the land of <i>Wezmech</i> to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Veszpr%C3%A9m" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Veszprém">Diocese of Veszprém</a>. Buzád served as Ispán of <a href="/wiki/Sopron_County" title="Sopron County">Sopron County</a> in 1232.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011198_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2011198-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> For this reason historian Attila Zsoldos considered that Buzád left Béla's court to return Andrew's loyalty by that year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2016446_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZsoldos2016446-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_life">Monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Buz%C3%A1d_Hah%C3%B3t&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Buzád came from the most powerful Bánfi family [sic] in Hungary. He had grown up when he was trespassing his riches and fame, and he left his rank on his sons, and with great enthusiasm he commenced a monastic life in the Dominican order. As he gained experience in secular sciences, he soon became a tireless propagator of God's Word. When the Tartars [Mongols] broke into Hungary, and they perished the <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">servants of God</a> with exceptional cruelty, the Prior commanded their monks to flee, but Buzád did not care about the threat to his life, asking him to let stay and console the Christian people. He was so persistently asking for his [the Prior] permission, that he could stay. After his companions were in safe, Buzád set out to die for Christ and encouraged the people to do the same. When the Tatars' troops were nearby, the handful of flocks were blessed [by Buzád] with glorious intentions of the glorious death endured for Christ. He himself in the church, as if in a crucifixion, prayed with prowess at the altar. So he offered himself for an <a href="/wiki/Burnt_offering_(Judaism)" title="Burnt offering (Judaism)">entirely burnt sacrifice</a>, and he was killed on 8 December 1243 [sic]. After the barbarians retreated, the returning brothers found his beheaded corpse, which was pierced with spears, and they mourned him very. One brother had mourned Buzád for three days, while he did not take food and drink, when he was astonished, and heard the mourned martyr say to him, "Did not Christ have to suffer before entering his glory? The sufferings of the present time are not proportionate to the future glory. " The brother was no longer mourned for that, but rejoiced.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite>Gábor Hevenesi: <i>Ungaricae Sanctitatis Indicia</i> (1692)</cite></div></blockquote>
<p>Around 1233, Buzád joined the Dominican Order, giving up his political career and forsaking all property. As a charter dated 14 February 1233 mentioned, he already lived in a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Pest,_Hungary" title="Pest, Hungary">Pest</a>. Then his eldest son Buzád III inherited his main estate and centre <a href="/wiki/Zalaszabar" title="Zalaszabar">Szabar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVándor199650_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVándor199650-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> According to tradition narrated by a contemporary chronicler <a href="/wiki/Thomas_of_Cantimpr%C3%A9" title="Thomas of Cantimpré">Thomas of Cantimpré</a>, not willing to leave the monastery, the <a href="/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe" title="Mongol invasion of Europe">invading Mongols</a> killed Buzád before the altar in the middle of April 1241, shortly after the disastrous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Mohi" title="Battle of Mohi">Battle of Mohi</a>. Buzád was beatified by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> because of his martyrdom and self-sacrifice.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarkó2006451_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarkó2006451-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The narration of his martyrdom was preserved by <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> scholar and theologian Gábor Hevenesi at the end of the 17th century in his work <i>Ungaricae Sanctitatis Indicia</i> (1692).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Honors">Honors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Buz%C3%A1d_Hah%C3%B3t&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Honors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In honor of Hahót, a wooden sculpture was erected in 2009 at <a href="/wiki/Hah%C3%B3t,_Zala" title="Hahót, Zala">Hahót</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zala_County" title="Zala County">Zala County</a>, which village was founded by his clan. The lifesize statue depicts the noble, with one hand holding a sword and a <a href="/wiki/Latin_cross" title="Latin cross">Latin cross</a> in the other, referring to his secular and ecclesiastical careers. <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Vigh" title="László Vigh">László Vigh</a>, a member of the <a href="/wiki/National_Assembly_(Hungary)" title="National Assembly (Hungary)">Hungarian National Assembly</a>, gave a speech during consecration, where he said the youth should follow persons who lived out their lives with God's love and honest work, instead of false role models.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> A lookout at Zajk Hill near <a href="/wiki/Letenye" title="Letenye">Letenye</a> ("<i>Boldog Buzád Kilátó</i>"), designed by <a href="/wiki/Imre_Makovecz" title="Imre Makovecz">Imre Makovecz</a> and constructed after his death, is also dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
