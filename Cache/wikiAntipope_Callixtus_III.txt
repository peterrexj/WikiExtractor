<p><b>Callixtus III</b> (also <b>Calixtus III</b> or <b>Callistus III</b>; died between 1180 and 1184) was an <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> from September 1168 until his resignation in August 1178. He was the third antipope elected in opposition to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> during the latter's struggle with the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa" title="Frederick Barbarossa">Frederick Barbarossa</a>.
</p><p>Callixtus' baptismal name was John. He entered the <a href="/wiki/Vallombrosan" class="mw-redirect" title="Vallombrosan">Vallombrosan</a> monastery of Struma near <a href="/wiki/Arezzo" title="Arezzo">Arezzo</a> as a boy. In November 1158, the emperor placed the Vallombrosan under imperial protection. By that time, John was the abbot of Struma and one of the most important supporters of the emperor in <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. After the disputed <a href="/wiki/1159_papal_election" title="1159 papal election">papal election of 1159</a>, he supported the imperial candidate <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159%E2%80%931164)" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159–1164)">Victor IV</a> over Alexander III. He was rewarded by Victor with the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Albano" title="Roman Catholic Suburbicarian Diocese of Albano">cardinal-bishopric of Albano</a>, but was not immediately consecrated, rather continuing on as abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Victor's successor, <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Paschal_III" title="Antipope Paschal III">Paschal III</a>, on 20 September 1168, his supporters gathered in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to elect a new (anti-)pope. John was chosen that same month. He took the name Callixtus and sent Umfredo, (pseudo-)cardinal of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Susanna" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Susanna">Santa Susanna</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Germany" title="Kingdom of Germany">Germany</a> to inform Frederick of his election.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He found the emperor at an <a href="/wiki/Hoftag" title="Hoftag">imperial diet</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a> in June 1169. Frederick, who was in negotiations with Alexander III to end the schism at the time, did not immediately recognize Callixtus' election.<sup id="cite_ref-DoP_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Boso_(cardinal_of_Santa_Pudenziana)" title="Boso (cardinal of Santa Pudenziana)">Boso of Santa Pudenziana</a>, a partisan of Alexander III, Frederick was "inwardly deeply grieved" by the election of Callixtus, whom he had never met.<sup id="cite_ref-JBF_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JBF-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Nevertheless, Frederick expressed his intention to go with an army to Italy in 1170 to restore the unity of the Church. No such expedition took place, however.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Late in 1169, Callixtus sent Umfredo and Sigewin, cardinal-bishop of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Viterbo" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Viterbo">Viterbo</a>, as <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legates</a> to Germany. They brought a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> for <a href="/w/index.php?title=Baldwin_I_(archbishop_of_Bremen)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Baldwin I (archbishop of Bremen) (page does not exist)">Baldwin I</a>, the new archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Prince-Archbishopric_of_Bremen" title="Prince-Archbishopric of Bremen">Bremen</a>. Frederick ordered a collection in Germany for Callixtus.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Frederick recognized Callixtus only after his negotiations with Alexander broke down. The antipope was primarily a bargaining chip with which the emperor could pressure Alexander, and had only limited geographical support in comparison to Victor IV and Paschal III. He held Rome itself, but was acknowledged only in part of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, in his native Tuscany and in the <a href="/wiki/Rhineland" title="Rhineland">Rhineland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DoP_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He and his legates issued privileges for several monasteries in the <a href="/wiki/Upper_Rhine" title="Upper Rhine">Upper Rhineland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He mainly resided in <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DoP_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1173, Callixtus sent Martin, cardinal-bishop of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Frascati" title="Roman Catholic Suburbicarian Diocese of Frascati">Tusculum</a>, to Germany as legate to help mediate an alliance between Frederick and King <a href="/wiki/Louis_VII_of_France" title="Louis VII of France">Louis VII of France</a>. For the next three years nothing is known of Callixtus' activities. Frederick, when it became politically expedient, after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano" title="Battle of Legnano">defeat of Legnano</a>, switched his backing to Alexander. In November 1176, he signed the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pact_of_Anagni&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pact of Anagni (page does not exist)">Pact of Anagni</a>, which did, however, protect Callixtus by granting him an abbacy and restoring the cardinals he had created to their former offices. These terms were confirmed in the final <a href="/wiki/Peace_of_Venice" class="mw-redirect" title="Peace of Venice">Peace of Venice</a> in July 1177.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-DoP_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Callixtus did not immediately recognize the Peace of Venice, but in 1178 Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Christian_I_(archbishop_of_Mainz)" title="Christian I (archbishop of Mainz)">Christian of Mainz</a>, the imperial chancellor, marched an army into Italy to retake the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal State</a> and Rome for Alexander. This rendered Callixtus' position at Viterbo untenable, and he fled to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Monte_Albano&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Monte Albano (page does not exist)">Monte Albano</a>. On 29 August 1178, Callixtus submitted to Alexander III at <a href="/wiki/Tusculum" title="Tusculum">Tusculum</a> and abjured the schism.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The pope entertained him with a feast.<sup id="cite_ref-DoP_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some of his obstinate partisans sought to substitute a new antipope, and chose one Lando di Sezze under the name of <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Innocent_III" title="Antipope Innocent III">Innocent III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alexander appointed Callixtus rector of <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a>. He still held that position in 1180, but by 1184 he was dead.<sup id="cite_ref-KJ_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KJ-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
