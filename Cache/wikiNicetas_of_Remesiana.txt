<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Nicetas</b> (c. 335–414) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Remesiana" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Remesiana">Bishop of Remesiana</a> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Bela_Palanka" title="Bela Palanka">Bela Palanka</a>, <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>), which was then in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> province of <a href="/wiki/Dacia_Mediterranea" title="Dacia Mediterranea">Dacia Mediterranea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicetas_of_Remesiana&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nicetas promoted <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> sacred music for use during <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">the Eucharistic worship</a> and reputedly composed a number of liturgical hymns, among which some twentieth-century scholars number the major Latin  <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymn</a> of praise, <i><a href="/wiki/Te_Deum" title="Te Deum">Te Deum</a></i>, traditionally attributed to <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine</a>. He is presumed to be the missionary to the barbarian <a href="/wiki/Thracian_tribe" class="mw-redirect" title="Thracian tribe">Thracian tribe</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Bessi" title="Bessi">Bessi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Because of his missionary activity, his contemporary and friend, <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_Nola" title="Paulinus of Nola">Paulinus of Nola</a>, lauded him poetically for instructing in the Gospel barbarians changed by him from wolves to sheep and brought into the fold of peace, and for teaching to sing of Christ with Roman heart bandits, who previously had no such ability.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, it is doubtful whether these barbarians really were barbarians, or whether their mention is only a poetical topos. Indeed, Paulinus, who wrote a quite classical Latin poetry, probably used existing poetical authorities. For Dacia, where Nicetas was from, the poetical authority was <a href="/wiki/Ovid" title="Ovid">Ovid</a>, although the Dacia (probably the province Dacia Mediterranea) of that time did not correspond with the Getia where Ovid had been banished to.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 398, Nicetas made a pilgrimage to Nola to visit the grave of <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Nola" title="Felix of Nola">Felix of Nola</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lengthy excerpts survive of his principal doctrinal work, <i>Instructions for Candidates for Baptism</i>, in six books. They show that he stressed the orthodox position in trinitarian doctrine. They contain the expression "<a href="/wiki/Communion_of_saints" title="Communion of saints">communion of saints</a>" about the belief in a mystical bond uniting both the living and the dead in a certain hope and love. No evidence survives of previous use of this expression, which has since played a central role in formulations of the Christian creed.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> is on 22 June.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
