<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">John Roberts</div><br /><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">O.S.B.</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Priest and Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 1577<br /><a href="/wiki/Trawsfynydd" title="Trawsfynydd">Trawsfynydd</a>, Wales</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">10 December 1610 (aged 32 - <p><b>John Roberts</b> (1577 – 10 December 1610) was a <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, and was the first <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">Prior</a> of St. Gregory's, <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>, France (now <a href="/wiki/Downside_Abbey" title="Downside Abbey">Downside Abbey</a>). Returning to England as a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> priest during the period of <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusancy</a>, he was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a>. He is venerated as a saint by the Roman Catholic church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_conversion_to_Catholicism">Early life and conversion to Catholicism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Roberts_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and conversion to Catholicism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:242px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg/240px-Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="161" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg/360px-Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg/480px-Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="428" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gelli_Goch_-_geograph.org.uk_-_386814.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gelli Goch, home of John Roberts</div></div></div>
<p>Roberts was born in 1577 in <a href="/wiki/Trawsfynydd" title="Trawsfynydd">Trawsfynydd</a>, a small village in <a href="/wiki/Snowdonia" title="Snowdonia">Snowdonia</a>, north <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, the son of John and Anna Roberts of Rhiw Goch Farm.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Welsh_peers_and_baronets" title="Welsh peers and baronets">Welsh nobility</a> and was descended from the Welsh Princes,<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-orthodox_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthodox-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was a farmer. Roberts was baptised into the <a href="/wiki/Church_in_Wales" title="Church in Wales">Anglican</a> faith inside the local parish <a href="/wiki/Trawsfynydd#Church_of_St_Madryn" title="Trawsfynydd">church of St Madryn</a> but is said to have received his early education from an elderly man who had been a <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian monk</a> at <a href="/wiki/Cymer_Abbey" title="Cymer Abbey">Cymer Abbey</a> just outside <a href="/wiki/Dolgellau" title="Dolgellau">Dolgellau</a> until its <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">dissolution</a> by <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII" title="Henry VIII">Henry VIII</a> in 1537. He attended <a href="/wiki/St._John%27s_College,_Oxford" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John&#39;s College, Oxford">St. John's College, Oxford</a> in February, 1595 before leaving after two years to study law at <a href="/wiki/Furnival%27s_Inn" title="Furnival&#39;s Inn">Furnival's Inn</a>, London.<sup id="cite_ref-southwark_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-southwark-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:242px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg/240px-Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="122" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg/360px-Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg/480px-Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="325" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Eglwys_Santes_Madryn_-_geograph.org.uk_-_535229.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Madryn's Church, <a href="/wiki/Trawsfynydd" title="Trawsfynydd">Trawsfynydd</a>, where Roberts was baptised</div></div></div>
<p>During his travels in Europe, he left behind both the law and his former faith as he converted to Catholicism on a visit to <a href="/wiki/Notre_Dame_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Notre Dame Cathedral">Notre Dame Cathedral</a> in Paris. He moved on to Spain and joined <a href="/wiki/San_Benito_el_Real,_Valladolid" title="San Benito el Real, Valladolid">St Benedict's Monastery</a>, <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a>, and became a member of this community in 1598,<sup id="cite_ref-southwark_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-southwark-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> where he was known as Brother John of <a href="/wiki/Meirionnydd" title="Meirionnydd">Merioneth</a> in reference to his birthplace.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Benedictine_missionary">Benedictine missionary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Roberts_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Benedictine missionary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From Valladolid he was sent to make his novitiate at <a href="/wiki/San_Mart%C3%ADn_Pinario" class="mw-redirect" title="San Martín Pinario">San Martín Pinario</a>, <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a>, where he made his <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">profession</a> towards the end of 1600. Having completed his studies he was <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders" title="Holy orders">ordained</a>, and set out for England on 26 December 1602. Although observed by a Government spy, Roberts and his companions succeeded in entering the country in April 1603, where he was appointed vicar of the English monks of the Spanish Congregation on the Mission.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was arrested and banished on 13 May.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He reached <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>, in northern France, on 24 May. Soon he managed to return to England; he worked among the <a href="/wiki/Black_Death#Recurrence" title="Black Death">plague</a> victims in London. In 1604, while embarking for Spain with four <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulants</a>, including William Scott (later known as <a href="/wiki/Maurus_Scott" title="Maurus Scott">Maurus Scott</a>) he was again arrested. Not recognized as a priest, he was released and again banished, but he returned to England at once.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 5 November 1605, while Justice Grange was searching the house of Mrs. Percy, first wife of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Percy_(plotter)" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Percy (plotter)">Thomas Percy</a>, who was involved in the <a href="/wiki/Gunpowder_Plot" title="Gunpowder Plot">Gunpowder Plot</a>, he found Roberts there and arrested him. Though acquitted of any complicity in the plot itself, Roberts was imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Gatehouse_Prison" title="Gatehouse Prison">Gatehouse Prison</a> at <a href="/wiki/Westminster" title="Westminster">Westminster</a> for seven months and then exiled again in July, 1606.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Foundation_of_St._Gregory.27s_monastery.2C_Douai"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_St._Gregory's_monastery,_Douai">Foundation of St. Gregory's monastery, Douai</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Roberts_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundation of St. Gregory&#039;s monastery, Douai">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>This time he was absent for some fourteen months, nearly all of which he spent at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> (now in northern France) where he founded and became the first <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of a house for the English Benedictine monks who had entered various Spanish <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a>. This was the beginning of the monastery of St. Gregory's at Douai. This community of monks was banished from France in 1795 at the French Revolution and travelled to England where they settled at <a href="/wiki/Downside_Abbey" title="Downside Abbey">Downside Abbey</a>, Somerset in 1814.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_England_and_martyrdom">Return to England and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Roberts_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Return to England and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Roberts returned to England in October 1607 and in December he was again arrested and placed in the Gatehouse at Westminster, from which he escaped after some months. After his escape, he lived for about a year in London, but in May 1609 was taken to <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a>. He might have been executed, but Antonie de la Broderie, the French ambassador, interceded on his behalf, and his sentence was reduced to banishment.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Roberts again visited Spain and Douai, but returned to England, for a fifth time, within a year. He was captured again on 2 December 1610; the arresting men arrived just as he was finishing saying <a href="/wiki/Mass_(Roman_Rite)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mass (Roman Rite)">Mass</a> in a house, having been followed by former priest turned spy John Cecil, who had compiled a dossier on Roberts for <a href="/wiki/James_VI_and_I" title="James VI and I">James I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-open_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-open-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was taken to Newgate in his <a href="/wiki/Vestment" title="Vestment">vestments</a>. On 5 December he was tried and found guilty under the Act forbidding priests to minister in England, and on 10 December was <a href="/wiki/Hanging,_drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanging, drawing and quartering">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a>, at the age of thirty-three,<sup id="cite_ref-southwark_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-southwark-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> along with Thomas Somers, at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn, London</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>It was usual for the prisoner to be disembowelled while still alive, but he was very popular among the poor of London because of the kindness he had shown them during the plague and the large crowd which gathered at his execution would not allow this.<sup id="cite_ref-southwark_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-southwark-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They insisted he be hanged to the death so as not to feel the pain. His heart was then held aloft by the executioner who proclaimed: "Behold the heart of the traitor!" But the angry crowd did not provide the standard response of "Long live the King!"; there was deathly silence.<sup id="cite_ref-open_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-open-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Roberts_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The introduction of the cause of <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in his Decree of 4 December 1886. On 25 October 1970,<sup id="cite_ref-bbcwales_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbcwales-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Roberts was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> as one of the representative <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p><p>Roman Catholic Bishop <a href="/wiki/Edwin_Regan" title="Edwin Regan">Edwin Regan</a> said: "Although the name St John Roberts isn't as well known today, he is a major figure in our religious history." He was the first monk to return to Britain following the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a>; the hostility between the Catholics and Protestants was at its height at this stage, when a Catholic priest could only expect to live for approximately two years in Britain during that period.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 17 July 2010, <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Seraphim_of_Glastonbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan Seraphim of Glastonbury">Metropolitan Seraphim of Glastonbury</a> of the British Orthodox Church, accompanied by Deacon Theodore de Quincey, attended an Ecumenical Service at <a href="/wiki/Westminster_Cathedral" title="Westminster Cathedral">Westminster Cathedral</a> in celebration of the 400th anniversary of the Martyrdom of John Roberts. Abba Seraphim noted that as a Londoner he wanted to honour the humanitarian and pastoral ministry of Roberts to Londoners; and that all those who are conscious of the problems of exercising Christian ministry in times of persecution would immediately value Roberts's determination as well as realising the extraordinary sacrifice he made to fulfil his priestly vocation. Large contingents from Wales were in attendance and the service was bi-lingual.<sup id="cite_ref-orthodox_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthodox-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> <a href="/wiki/Rowan_Williams" title="Rowan Williams">Rowan Williams</a> addressed the congregation in both English and <a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>. It was the first time Welsh had been spoken in a ceremony at Westminster Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-bbcwales_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbcwales-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The choral piece, "Beatus Juan de Mervinia" in both <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and Welsh, was specially commissioned for the service from the Welsh composer Brian Hughes.<sup id="cite_ref-bbcwales_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbcwales-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Roberts is commemorated by a tourist trail from St Madryn's church Trawsfynydd to Cymer Abbey near Dolgellau, and by an exhibition in the Llys Ednowain Heritage Centre in Trawsfynydd.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Roberts_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The body of Roberts was recovered by a group that included <a href="/wiki/Maurus_Scott" title="Maurus Scott">Maurus Scott</a> and taken to St. Gregory's, Douai, but disappeared during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. An arm was found in the possession of the Spanish royal family before being returned to the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Santiago_de_Compostela" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela">Cathedral of Santiago de Compostela</a>, where he had served as a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Fingers preserved as <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> went to Downside Abbey, to <a href="/wiki/Erdington_Abbey" title="Erdington Abbey">Erdington Abbey</a>, to the Sacred Cross Church in <a href="/wiki/Gellilydan" title="Gellilydan">Gellilydan</a> near Robert's birthplace, to <a href="/wiki/Tyburn_convent" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn convent">Tyburn convent</a> and to <a href="/wiki/St_Joseph%27s_Convent,_Taunton" title="St Joseph&#39;s Convent, Taunton">St Joseph's Convent, Taunton</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
