<p><b>Itala Mela</b> (28 August 1904 – 29 April 1957) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> who was a lapsed Christian until a sudden conversion of faith in the 1920s and as a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine oblate</a> virgin assumed the name of "<b>Maria della Trinità</b>". Mela became one of the well-known mystics of the Church during her life and indeed following her death. She also penned a range of theological writings that focused on the <a href="/wiki/Trinity_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinity (Christianity)">Trinity</a>, which she deemed was integral to the Christian faith.
</p><p>Mela was proclaimed to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 12 June 2014 after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 14 December 2015 the pope also approved a miracle attributed to her intercession which allowed for her beatification to take place.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Mela was beatified in <a href="/wiki/La_Spezia" title="La Spezia">La Spezia</a> on 10 June 2017 and Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presided over the celebration on the pope's behalf; the miracle in question concerned the <a href="/wiki/Resurrection" title="Resurrection">revival</a> of an Italian newborn, whose body was in state of clinical <a href="/wiki/Brain_death" title="Brain death">brain death</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-itamela_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-itamela-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Itala_Mela&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Itala Mela was born on 28 August 1904 in <a href="/wiki/La_Spezia" title="La Spezia">La Spezia</a> to Pasquino Mela and Luigia Bianchini; both were <a href="/wiki/Atheist" class="mw-redirect" title="Atheist">atheist</a> teachers.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She spent her childhood in the care of her maternal grandparents from 1905 to 1915 as her parents worked and her grandparents prepared Mela for her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> and <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Catholic Church)">Confirmation</a>; she made both on 9 May 1915 and 27 May 1915 respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The death of her brother Enrico at the age of nine (27 February 1920) challenged Mela's perception of her Christian faith, and she wrote of her feelings to the loss: "After his death, nothing". As a result, she eschewed her Christian faith and slipped into atheism.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However she had a sudden reawakening of her faith on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Immaculate_Conception" title="Feast of the Immaculate Conception">Feast of the Immaculate Conception</a> (8 Dec. 1922) after rediscovering <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>; her faith deepened with the motto she took being: "Lord, I shall follow You unto the darkness, unto death".<sup id="cite_ref-IS_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mela became a member of <a href="/wiki/Italian_Catholic_Federation_of_University_Students" title="Italian Catholic Federation of University Students">FUCI</a> in 1923, where she met future pope <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Montini" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni Battista Montini">Giovanni Battista Montini</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster" title="Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster">Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster</a> at the meetings there; she also met the priests Divo Barsotti and <a href="/wiki/Agostino_Gemelli" title="Agostino Gemelli">Agostino Gemelli</a>. At such meetings, Montini and both the politicians <a href="/wiki/Aldo_Moro" title="Aldo Moro">Aldo Moro</a> and <a href="/wiki/Giulio_Andreotti" title="Giulio Andreotti">Giulio Andreotti</a> served as major influences upon her. She was the main friend of <a href="/wiki/Angela_Gotelli" title="Angela Gotelli">Angela Gotelli</a>, a teacher of classic letters and a Roman Catholic partisan who was close to the polical ideas of <a href="/wiki/Aldo_Moro" title="Aldo Moro">Aldo Moro</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mela passed her studies in 1922 with recognition of being a brilliant student and was enrolled at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Genoa" title="University of Genoa">University of Genoa</a> on the following 11 November, where she later received a <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Letters" title="Bachelor of Letters">degree in letters</a> in 1928 as well as in classical studies.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mela experienced her first vision of God on 3 August 1928 as a beam of light at the <a href="/wiki/Tabernacle" title="Tabernacle">tabernacle</a> in a church of a seminary at <a href="/wiki/Pontremoli" title="Pontremoli">Pontremoli</a>, beginning a long stream of visions in her life. She departed for <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> at this time, and chose as her confessor <a href="/wiki/Adriano_Bernareggi" title="Adriano Bernareggi">Adriano Bernareggi</a>. Her true calling as a Benedictine oblate came in 1929 and solidified to the point where she commenced her novitiate. It concluded on 4 January 1933 when she made her profession in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Paolo_fuori_le_Mura" class="mw-redirect" title="San Paolo fuori le Mura">San Paolo fuori le Mura</a> making her four vows. As a sign of her new life, Mela assumed the name of "Maria della Trinità". Mela returned to her hometown in 1933. From 1936 she received ecstasies and visions. Her mother died in 1937.
</p><p>Mela presented an idea for a memorial to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> in 1941, and the pope accepted the Memorial of Mary of the Trinity. In <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> from 5–15 October 1946, Mela composed a series of spiritual exercises for the benefit of the faithful; the exercises were well received.
</p><p>From 18 February to 24 April 1957, despite her <a href="/wiki/Aphasia" title="Aphasia">aphasia</a>, she managed to make herself understood with gestures and with her eyes, her smile and the nod of her head. On 25 April at 4 p.m., after a new attack, she went into a <a href="/wiki/Coma" title="Coma">coma</a> for four days and on 29 April at 6.30 p.m., while the bells of the Church of St. John and St. Augustine ring for the Eucharistic blessing, Itala dies. She was clothed in the wedding dress of one of her pupils who had become a Carmelite, a dress that had been worn by her for the day of her vesting. The little chamber in which she passed away was the object of the homage of an innumerable crowd.
</p><p>Her remains were later transferred to the <a href="/wiki/La_Spezia_Cathedral" title="La Spezia Cathedral">La Spezia Cathedral</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Crypta" title="Crypta">crypta</a> in 1983.
</p><p>Itala Mela is commemorated on April 28 because 29 April is the feast of St.<a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a> and St. <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of Italy, and their liturgical memory would take precedence over that of Itala mela.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Itala_Mela&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process started in La Spezia in its diocese on 29 April 1968 which granted Mela the title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>; the process spanned until 21 November 1976 and was validated in Rome on 2 October 1992. In 1983, at the canonical reconnaissance that are regularly carried out in the case of beatification processes, his body was found intact.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Following the local process, all of Mela's writings were approved in 1979 and permitted for evaluation in the cause.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> then was compiled and submitted in 2003 to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> for further evaluation. On the occasion of the <a href="/wiki/2006" title="2006">2006</a> Convention of Italian Churches in Verona, the churches of <a href="/wiki/Liguria" title="Liguria">Liguria</a> chose Itala Mela as an exemplary witness to their heritage of <a href="/wiki/Faith" title="Faith">faith</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spirituality" title="Spirituality">spirituality</a>.
</p><p>It was on 12 June 2014 that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved that Mela had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> thus declared her to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.
</p><p>On 14 December 2015, the pope also approved a miracle attributed to the intercession of Mela which would allow for her beatification to take place; it was celebrated at Piazza Europa in La Spezia on 10 June 2017 with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presiding over the celebration on the pope's behalf. The following afternoon after his <a href="/wiki/Angelus#Modern_usage" title="Angelus">Angelus</a> address - given on the <a href="/wiki/Trinity_Sunday#Catholicism" title="Trinity Sunday">Solemnity of the Most Holy Trinity</a> - Pope Francis referred to her beatification in which he mentioned the <a href="/wiki/Real_presence#Catholic:_Objective,_substantial_and_entire" class="mw-redirect" title="Real presence">real presence</a> of "<a href="/wiki/God_the_Father" title="God the Father">God the Father</a>, <a href="/wiki/God_the_Son" title="God the Son">Son</a> and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity#God_the_Holy_Spirit" title="Holy Spirit in Christianity">Holy Spirit</a> who abides in the chamber of our heart".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> assigned to this cause is Dr. Andrea Ambrosi. Her writings altogether occupy more than 42 volumes.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Her original writings are kept in the Diocesan Archive at the Episcopal Seminary in Sarzana. Itala Mela's findings were typewritten by the nuns of the Monastery of Santa Maria del Mare in Castellazzo. Later, a commission of experts verified the fidelity of the typewritten copies to the originals. The archive preserves both Itala Mela's autograph writings and typewritten copies (which are the only ones that can be consulted by the public).
</p><p>In honour of the Blessed Itala Mela, Father Claudio Grana OCD composed the Italian hymn titled <i>Se uno mi ama davvero </i> (If one truly loves me).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> (n°. 1278<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>). The strophes are derived from the <i>Elevazione alla Trinità</i> (Elevation to the Trinity) of St. <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_the_Trinity" title="Elizabeth of the Trinity">Elizabeth of the Trinity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In her honour, the Municipality of La Spezia named a stairway between Via XXVII Marzo and Via dei Colli.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Trinitarian_indwelling">The Trinitarian indwelling</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Itala_Mela&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The Trinitarian indwelling">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Trinitarian indwelling is a spiritual experience proposed by Itala Mela to help the faithful live their lives in the light of the Trinity. It consists of trying to do everything in union with the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, relying on their guidance and always trying to do God's will.
</p><p>Trinitarian indwelling is an experience that can be lived in any situation of life, not just in a convent or in a place of prayer. For Itala Mela, in fact, daily life is the place where it is possible to meet God and bear witness to one's faith, through concrete gestures of love and charity towards others.
</p><p>the Trinitarian indwelling finds foundation in the Gospel according to John, chapter 10, verses from 22 to 39 and in particular in the words "The Father is in me and I in Him". Thus, the three divine Persons inhabit each other. According to Itala Mela, man, created in the image and likeness of God, is made to live the same life as God through the Trinitarian indwelling of the soul and body. It is not a mere intellectual fact, but an experience that concerns the person in his entirety of soul and body. Anyone who coherently lives as a <a href="/wiki/Son_of_God" title="Son of God">Son of God</a> can also experience this communion of love with God and with his neighbour.
</p><p>Itala Mela lived its first experiences of trinitarian indwelling after having prayed the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Office of the Hours</a>, received the Eucharist during holy mass or having practiced an intense <a href="/wiki/Eucharistic_adoration" title="Eucharistic adoration">Eucharistic adoration</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Itala Mela immolated herself, offering her joyful suffering and infirmity to God so that other people could experience the Trinitarian indwelling, for the forgiveness of their sins and their eternal salvation.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Trinitarian indwelling theology was also described by <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_the_Trinity" title="Elizabeth of the Trinity">Elizabeth of the Trinity</a>. It was anticipated by the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Diuturnum_illud&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Diuturnum illud (page does not exist)">Diuturnum illud</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> and then confirmated by the encyclical <i><a href="/wiki/Mystici_corporis" class="mw-redirect" title="Mystici corporis">Mystici corporis</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pius XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
