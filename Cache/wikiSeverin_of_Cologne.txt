<p><b>Severin of Cologne</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Severinus</i>) was the third <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cologne">Bishop of Cologne</a>, living in the later 4th century. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severin_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Severin is said in 376 to have founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in the then <a href="/wiki/History_of_Cologne" title="History of Cologne">Colonia Agrippina</a> in honour of the martyrs <a href="/wiki/Pope_Cornelius" title="Pope Cornelius">Cornelius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cyprian" title="Cyprian">Cyprian</a>, from which developed the later <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Severin" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Severin">Basilica of St. Severin</a>. Severin is notable as a prominent opponent of <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bonato_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bonato-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to legend, Severinus was taking a walk in a field while still a priest when he heard a voice tell him he would one day be bishop of Cologne. When he asked when that would happen, he was told when his staff buds and flowers. Immediately, he stuck his staff into the ground, it budded and he was called to Cologne.<sup id="cite_ref-Butlers_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butlers-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severin_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Severin was highly venerated in Cologne early on. His bones are today preserved in a gold shrine in the choir of St Severin's Church in Cologne. That seen today is a reconstruction of 1819, as the medieval shrine was melted down for the gold in the period of French rule, c. 1795–98.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Its opening in 1999 corroborated the documented transfer of the bones of bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Wigfried&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Wigfried (page does not exist)">Wigfried</a> of Cologne (924–953), as it was possible to date the old inner wooden shrine by the latest <a href="/wiki/Dendrochronology" title="Dendrochronology">dendrochronological</a> techniques to the year 948.<sup id="cite_ref-Bonato_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bonato-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> An ancient cloth, probably <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a>, was also discovered, with which the wooden box was lined. The saint's feast day is 23 October.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="As_a_name">As a name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severin_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: As a name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Scandinavian name "<a href="/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_(given_name)" class="mw-redirect" title="Søren (given name)">Søren</a>" is derived from Saint Severin.
</p>
