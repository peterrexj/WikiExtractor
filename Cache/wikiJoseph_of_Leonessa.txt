<p><b>Joseph of Leonessa</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">O.F.M. Cap.</a>, (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Giuseppe da Leonessa</i>)
(1556 – February 4, 1612) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Leonessa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <b>Eufranio Desiderio</b> at <a href="/wiki/Leonessa" title="Leonessa">Leonessa</a>, a small town then in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, now in the <a href="/wiki/Lazio" title="Lazio">Lazio</a>.  It is said that from a young age he showed a remarkably religious bent of mind; he used to erect little <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altars</a> and spend much time in prayer before them, and often he would gather his companions and induce them to pray with him.
</p><p>Whilst yet a boy he used to take the <a href="/wiki/Flagellation#Christianity" title="Flagellation">discipline</a> on Fridays in company with the <a href="/wiki/Confraternity" title="Confraternity">Confraternity</a> of the Holy Savior. He was educated by his uncle, who had planned a suitable marriage for him, but in his sixteenth year he fell sick of a fever, and upon his recovery, without consulting his guardian, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> reform of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan Order">Franciscan Order</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-franme_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-franme-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He made his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> of the Carcerelle near <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>.
</p><p>As a friar he was remarkable for his great <a href="/wiki/Abstinence" title="Abstinence">abstinence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-franme_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-franme-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> "Brother Ass", he would say to his body, "there is no need to feed thee as a noble horse would be fed: thou must be content to be a poor ass." In 1599, the year before the <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christian)">Jubilee</a> year, he fasted the whole year by way of preparation for gaining the <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgence</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="At_Constantinople">At Constantinople</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Leonessa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: At Constantinople">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1587 he was sent by the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of his Order to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> to minister to the Christians held captive there. Arrived there he and his companions lodged in the Galata district in a derelict house of <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> monks, actually the St. Benedict high school. The poverty in which the friars lived attracted the attention of the <a href="/wiki/Turkish_people" title="Turkish people">Turks</a>, who went in numbers to see the new missionaries. He was very solicitous in ministering to the captive Christians in the <a href="/wiki/Galley" title="Galley">galleys</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>'s navy. Every day he went into the city to preach, and he was at length thrown into prison and only released at the intervention of the <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venetian</a> agent.<sup id="cite_ref-franme_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-franme-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Urged on by zeal he at last sought to enter the palace to preach before <a href="/wiki/Murad_III" title="Murad III">Sultan Murad III</a> and tried to convert Muslims, but he was seized and condemned to death. For three days he hung on the <a href="/wiki/Gallows" title="Gallows">gallows</a>, held up by two hooks driven through his right hand and foot; his legends state that he was then miraculously released by an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_Italy">Return to Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Leonessa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Return to Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Returning to Italy, he took with him a <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> who had <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostatized</a>, and who was reconciled to the church on their arrival in Rome. Joseph now took up the work of home missions in his native province, sometimes preaching six or seven times a day. In the Jubilee year of 1600 he gave the <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lenten</a> <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a> at <a href="/wiki/Otricoli" title="Otricoli">Otricoli</a>, a town through which crowds of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> passed on their way to Rome. Many of them being very poor, Joseph supplied them with food; he also washed their clothes and cut their hair. At <a href="/wiki/Todi" title="Todi">Todi</a> he cultivated with his own hands a garden, the produce of which was for the poor.
</p><p>He died at <a href="/wiki/Amatrice" title="Amatrice">Amatrice</a> in 1612.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XIV" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XIV">Benedict XIV</a> in 1746. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is kept on February 4, within the Franciscan Order. In his hometown, there is a church and sanctuary of San Giuseppe da Leonessa.  The main street is named after him, the Corso San Giuseppe.  Devotion to him is largely in central Italy; churches in <a href="/wiki/Otricoli" title="Otricoli">Otricoli</a>, <a href="/wiki/San_Lorenzo_Nuovo" title="San Lorenzo Nuovo">San Lorenzo Nuovo</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Rivodutri" title="Rivodutri">Rivodutri</a> contain paintings of him.
</p>
