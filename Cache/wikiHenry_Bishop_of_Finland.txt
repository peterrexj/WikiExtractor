<p><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a> <b>Henry</b> (<a href="/wiki/Finnish_language" title="Finnish language">Finnish</a>: <i lang="fi">Henrik</i>; <a href="/wiki/Swedish_language" title="Swedish language">Swedish</a>: <i lang="sv">Henrik</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Henricus</i>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 20 January 1156<sup id="cite_ref-year_of_death_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-year_of_death-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) was a medieval <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Clergy" title="Clergy">clergyman</a>. He came to Sweden with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_IV" title="Pope Adrian IV">Nicholas Breakspeare</a> in 1153 and was most likely designated to be the new <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Uppsala" title="Archbishop of Uppsala">Archbishop of Uppsala</a>, but the independent church province of Sweden could only be established in 1164 after the civil war, and Henry would have been sent to organize the Church in Finland, where Christians had already existed for two centuries.
</p><p>According to legend, he entered <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a> together with King <a href="/wiki/Eric_IX_of_Sweden" title="Eric IX of Sweden">Saint Eric</a> of Sweden and died as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>, becoming a central figure in the local <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. However, the authenticity of the accounts of his life and ministry are widely disputed and there are no historical records of his birth, existence or death.
</p><p>Together with his alleged murderer, peasant <a href="/wiki/Lalli" title="Lalli">Lalli</a>, Henry is an important figure in the early <a href="/wiki/History_of_Finland" title="History of Finland">history of Finland</a>. His feast is celebrated by the majority Lutheran <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_of_Finland" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church of Finland">Church of Finland</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> as well as by the <a href="/wiki/Catholicism_in_Finland" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholicism in Finland">Catholic Church of Finland</a>. He is commemorated in the <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgical calendar">liturgical calendars</a> of several <a href="/wiki/Lutheranism" title="Lutheranism">Lutheran</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> churches.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Vita_and_miracula"><i>Vita</i> and <i>miracula</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Vita and miracula">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The legend of Bishop Henry's life, or his <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a></i>, was written 150 years after his time, at the end of the 13th century, and contains little concrete information about him. He is said to have been an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a>-born bishop in <a href="/wiki/Uppsala" title="Uppsala">Uppsala</a> at the time of King <a href="/wiki/Eric_IX_of_Sweden" title="Eric IX of Sweden">Eric the Saint</a> of Sweden in the mid-12th century, ruling the peaceful kingdom with the king in heavenly co-existence. To tackle the perceived threat from the non-Christian <a href="/wiki/Finns" title="Finns">Finns</a>, Eric and Henry were forced to do battle with them. After they had conquered Finland, baptized the people and built many churches, the victorious king returned to Sweden while Henry (<i>Henricus</i>) remained with the Finns, more willing to live the life of a preacher than that of a high bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The legend draws to a conclusion as Henry attempted to give a canonical punishment to a murderer. The accused man became enraged and killed the bishop, who was thus considered to be a martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The legend strongly emphasizes that Henry was a Bishop of Uppsala, not a <a href="/wiki/List_of_Bishops_of_Turku" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Bishops of Turku">Bishop of Finland</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> which became a conventional claim later on, also by the church itself.<sup id="cite_ref-chronicon_2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronicon_2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He stayed in Finland out of pity, but was never appointed as a bishop there. The legend does not state whether there had been bishops in Finland before his time or what happened after his death; it does not even mention his burial in Finland. The <i>vita</i> is so void of any concrete information about Finland that it could have been created anywhere.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Latin is scholastic and the grammar is in general exceptionally good.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Piispa_Henrik_ja_Lalli.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Piispa_Henrik_ja_Lalli.jpg/300px-Piispa_Henrik_ja_Lalli.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="228" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/Piispa_Henrik_ja_Lalli.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="386" data-file-height="294" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Piispa_Henrik_ja_Lalli.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bishop Henry surrounded by his successors as depicted in <i><a href="/wiki/Missale_Aboense" title="Missale Aboense">Missale Aboense</a></i>.</div></div></div>
<p>Henry's <i>Vita</i> is followed by the more local <i>miracula</i>, a list of eleven <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> that various people were said to have experienced sometime after the bishop's death. With the exception of a priest in <a href="/wiki/Skara" title="Skara">Skara</a> who suffered a stomach ache after mocking Henry, all miracles seem to have taken place in Finland. The other miracles, which usually occurred following prayer to Bishop Henry, were:<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ol><li>The murderer lost his scalp when he put the bishop's hat on his head</li>
<li>The Bishop's finger was found the next Spring</li>
<li>A boy was raised from the dead in Kaisala</li>
<li>A girl was raised from the dead in <a href="/wiki/Vehmaa" title="Vehmaa">Vehmaa</a></li>
<li>A sick woman was healed in <a href="/wiki/Sastamala" title="Sastamala">Sastamala</a></li>
<li>A <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> called Erlend had his headache healed</li>
<li>A blind woman got back her eyesight in <a href="/wiki/Kyr%C3%B6" class="mw-redirect" title="Kyrö">Kyrö</a></li>
<li>A man with a paralyzed leg could walk again in Kyrö</li>
<li>A sick girl was healed</li>
<li>A group of fishermen from <a href="/wiki/Kokem%C3%A4ki" title="Kokemäki">Kokemäki</a> survived a storm</li></ol>
<p>Most versions of Henry's legend only include a selection of these miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Development_of_the_legend">Development of the legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Development of the legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg/220px-The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="306" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg/330px-The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/48/The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg 2x" data-file-width="431" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Cathedral_of_Turku.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Turku" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Turku">Cathedral of Turku</a> was the center of Henry's cult.</div></div></div>
<p>Henry and his <a href="/wiki/First_Swedish_Crusade" title="First Swedish Crusade">crusade to Finland</a> were also a part of the legend of King Eric. The appendix of the early 13th century <i><a href="/wiki/V%C3%A4stg%C3%B6talagen" title="Västgötalagen">Västgötalagen</a></i>, which has a short description of Eric's memorable deeds, also makes no reference to Henry or the crusade.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Henry and the crusade do not appear until a version of Eric's legend that dates to 1344. Similarities in the factual content and phraseology regarding the common events indicate that either one of the legends has acted as the model for the other.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Henry's legend is commonly considered to have been written during the 1280s or 1290s at the latest, for the consecration of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Turku" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Turku">Cathedral of Turku</a> in 1300, when his alleged remains were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> there from <a href="/wiki/Nousiainen" title="Nousiainen">Nousiainen</a>, a parish not far from <a href="/wiki/Turku" title="Turku">Turku</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Absence_from_the_historical_record">Absence from the historical record</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Absence from the historical record">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Yet, even as late as in the 1470s, the crusade legend was ignored in the <i>Chronica regni Gothorum</i>, a chronicle of the history of Sweden, written by Ericus Olai, the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">Canon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Uppsala_cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Uppsala cathedral">Uppsala cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Noteworthy in the development of the legend is that the first canonically elected <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Turku" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Turku">Bishop of Turku</a>, <a href="/wiki/Johan_(archbishop_of_Uppsala,_died_1291)" title="Johan (archbishop of Uppsala, died 1291)">Johan</a> (1286–1289) of <a href="/wiki/Poles" title="Poles">Polish</a> origin, was elected as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Uppsala" title="Archbishop of Uppsala">Archbishop of Uppsala</a> in 1289, after three years in office in Turku. The Swedish bishops of Finland<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> before him, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Bero" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Bero">Bero</a>, Ragvald and Kettil, had apparently been selected by the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Sweden" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Sweden">King of Sweden</a>. Related to the new situation was also the appointment of the <a href="/wiki/Benedict,_Duke_of_Finland" title="Benedict, Duke of Finland">king's brother</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Finland" title="Duke of Finland">Duke of Finland</a> in 1284, which challenged the Bishop's earlier position as the sole authority on all local matters. Johan was followed in Turku by <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Magnus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Magnus">Bishop Magnus</a> (1291–1308), who had been born in Finland.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1291 a document by the <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">cathedral chapter</a> makes no reference to Henry even though it mentions the cathedral and election of the new bishop many times.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> A papal letter by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV" title="Pope Nicholas IV">Pope Nicholas IV</a> from 1292 has the Virgin Mary as the sole <i>patrona</i> in Turku.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Appearance_in_the_historical_record">Appearance in the historical record</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Appearance in the historical record">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The first mention of Bishop Henry in historical sources is from 1298, when he is mentioned along with king Eric in a document from a provincial synod of Uppsala in Telge. This document, although mentioned many times as a source over the centuries, was not correctly dated until 1910.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:203px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Seal_of_bishop_benedictus_of_finland.gif" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/Seal_of_bishop_benedictus_of_finland.gif" decoding="async" width="201" height="232" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="201" data-file-height="232" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Seal_of_bishop_benedictus_of_finland.gif" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Henry pictured in the seal of Bishop Benedictus of Turku in 1332.</div></div></div>
<p>The legend itself is also first referred in a letter by the Archbishop of Uppsala in 1298, where Eric and Henry are mentioned together as martyrs who needed to be prayed to for the sake of the situation in <a href="/wiki/Karelia" title="Karelia">Karelia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Linna_1989_113_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Linna_1989_113-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> associating their alleged crusade to Finland with the new expeditions against <a href="/wiki/Novgorod" class="mw-redirect" title="Novgorod">Novgorod</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Swedish-Novgorodian_Wars" class="mw-redirect" title="Swedish-Novgorodian Wars">war between Novgorod and Sweden</a> for the control of Karelia had started in 1293. The first certain appearance of Henry's image in the seal of the Bishop of Turku is not until 1299.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The first mention of Henry of Uppsala being the patron saint of Turku cathedral is not until 14 August 1320, when he is mentioned as the second patron of the cathedral after Virgin Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> When he is later addressed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> as the <i>patronus</i> of the Cathedral of Turku along with the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>, and referred to as a saint, it was in the year 1391.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_95_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_95-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>  Some sources claim that Henry was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1158, but this information has been traced to a late publication by <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Vastovius" title="Johannes Vastovius">Johannes Vastovius</a> in 1623 and is generally regarded as a fabrication.<sup id="cite_ref-canonization2_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canonization2-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thus, Henry's veneration as a saint and his relation to King Eric seem to have emerged in the historical record at the same time in the mid-1290s with strong support from the church. This correlates with the start of the war against Novgorod. Sources do not support the popular assumption that Henry's cult developed in Nousiainen and gradually spread among ordinary people before official adoption. In 1232, the church in Nousiainen was consecrated only to the Virgin Mary,<sup id="cite_ref-letter_1232_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter_1232-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> and it was not until 1452 that Henry was mentioned as the <i>patronus</i> of Nousiainen.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Lunds_domkyrka.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Lunds_domkyrka.jpg/220px-Lunds_domkyrka.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Lunds_domkyrka.jpg/330px-Lunds_domkyrka.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Lunds_domkyrka.jpg/440px-Lunds_domkyrka.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Lunds_domkyrka.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Henry was also venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Lund_Cathedral" title="Lund Cathedral">Cathedral of Lund</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Despite the high-profile start of Henry's <i>cultus</i>, it took more than 100 years for the veneration of Saint Henry to gain widespread acceptance throughout Sweden. As of 1344 there were no relics of the bishop in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Uppsala" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Uppsala">Cathedral of Uppsala</a>. According to one biographer, Henry's <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> was rare outside the Diocese of Turku throughout the 14th century.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Vadstena_Abbey" title="Vadstena Abbey">Vadstena Abbey</a> near <a href="/wiki/Link%C3%B6ping" title="Linköping">Linköping</a> seems to have played a key role in establishment of Henry's legend elsewhere in Sweden in the early 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Henry never received the highest <i><a href="/wiki/Totum_duplex" title="Totum duplex">totum duplex</a></i> veneration in Uppsala nor was he made a <i>patronus</i> of the church there, which status he had both in Turku and Nousiainen.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the end of the Roman Catholic era in Sweden, Henry was well established as a local saint. The dioceses in Sweden and elsewhere venerating Henry were as follows, categorized by his local ranking:<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ol><li><i>Totum duplex</i>: Turku, <a href="/wiki/Link%C3%B6ping" title="Linköping">Linköping</a>, <a href="/wiki/Str%C3%A4ngn%C3%A4s" title="Strängnäs">Strängnäs</a></li>
<li><i>Duplex</i>: Uppsala, <a href="/wiki/Lund" title="Lund">Lund</a> (<a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Denmark</a>), <a href="/wiki/V%C3%A4ster%C3%A5s" title="Västerås">Västerås</a>, <a href="/wiki/V%C3%A4xj%C3%B6" title="Växjö">Växjö</a></li>
<li><i>Semiduplex</i>: <a href="/wiki/Nidaros" title="Nidaros">Nidaros</a> (<a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a>)</li>
<li><i>Simplex</i>: <a href="/wiki/Skara" title="Skara">Skara</a><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup></li></ol>
<p>Henry seems to have been known in northern <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, but he was largely ignored elsewhere in the Roman Catholic world.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:122px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Graduale_Aboense.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Graduale_Aboense.jpg/120px-Graduale_Aboense.jpg" decoding="async" width="120" height="167" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Graduale_Aboense.jpg/180px-Graduale_Aboense.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Graduale_Aboense.jpg/240px-Graduale_Aboense.jpg 2x" data-file-width="557" data-file-height="775" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Graduale_Aboense.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Original <i>Gaudeamus omnes</i>.</div></div></div>
<p>In the Bishopric of Turku, the annual feast day of Henry was January 20 (<i>talviheikki</i>, "Winter Henry"), according to traditions the day of his death. Elsewhere his memorial was held already on January 19,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> since more prominent saints were already commemorated on January 20. After the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a>, Henry's day was moved to the 19th in Finland as well.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> The existence of the feast day is first mentioned in 1335, and is known to have been marked in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">liturgical calendar</a> from the early 15th century onwards. Another memorial was held on June 18 (<i>kesäheikki</i>, "Summer Henry") which was the day of the translation of his relics to the Cathedral of Turku.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p><i>Gaudeamus omnes</i> ("Let all rejoice"), a <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">Gregorian</a> <a href="/wiki/Introit" title="Introit">introit</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in honor of Henry has survived within the late 14th or early 15th century <i>Graduale Aboense</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>According to legend, establishment of the church of Finland was entirely the work of the saint-king Eric of Sweden, assisted by the bishop from the most important diocese in the country. The first half of the legend describes how the king and the bishop ruled Sweden like 'two great lights' with feelings of 'internal love' toward each other, emphasizing the peaceful coexistence of the secular and ecclesiastical rule during a happy era when 'predatory wolves' could not hit their 'poisonous teeth against the innocent'.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The reality was quite different – Eric's predecessor, Eric himself and two of his successors were all murdered almost within a decade, one of the bloodiest times for the Swedish royalty. In the 1150s, the Bishop of Uppsala was also in a bitter fight with the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Link%C3%B6ping" title="Bishop of Linköping">Bishop of Linköping</a> over which see would become archiepiscopal.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> The crusade itself is described as a brief and bloodless event that was only performed to bring the "blind and evil heathen people of Finland" under Christian order.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The writer of the legend seems to have been especially interested in presenting the bishop as a humble martyr. He has fully ignored his place of death and burial and other "domestic" Finnish interests, which were much more apparent in folk traditions. The legend and folk traditions eventually influenced each other, and the church gradually adopted many additional details to its saint bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Folk_traditions">Folk traditions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Folk traditions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Among the many folk traditions about Henry, the most prominent is the folk poem "The Death-lay of Bishop Henry" (<i>Piispa Henrikin surmavirsi</i>). The poem almost completely ignores Henry's life and ministry and concentrates on his death.<sup id="cite_ref-death_lay_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-death_lay-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Henry.27s_origins"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Henry's_origins">Henry's origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Henry&#039;s origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to the poem, Henry had grown up in "Cabbage Land" (<i>Kaalimaa</i>), which has puzzled Finnish historians for centuries. The name might be connected to a coastal area in northern Finland Proper called <i><a href="/wiki/Kalanti" title="Kalanti">Kaland</a></i>, which is also mentioned in conjunction with an unrelated early preacher in <a href="/wiki/Vesilahti" title="Vesilahti">Vesilahti</a>, upper <a href="/wiki/Satakunta_(historical_province)" title="Satakunta (historical province)">Satakunta</a>, whose local name was "Fish of Kaland" (<i>Kalannin kala</i>, also known as <i>Hunnun herra</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Bishop <a href="/wiki/Mikael_Agricola" title="Mikael Agricola">Mikael Agricola</a> wrote in his <i><a href="/wiki/Se_Wsi_Testamenti" title="Se Wsi Testamenti">Se Wsi Testamenti</a></i> in 1548, that the earliest Swedish settlers in Finland had come from <a href="/wiki/Gotland" title="Gotland">Gotland</a> to the islets on the coast of Kaland, being harassed by Finns and seeking help from their relatives in Sweden.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It has also been suggested that the name might be related to <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Gaelic language">Gaelic</a>, which would presumably have referred to the bishop's <a href="/wiki/Scot" class="mw-redirect" title="Scot">Scottish</a> origins, though the legend gives him as a native Englishman.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Folk traditions have no information on the crusade whatsoever. King Eric is briefly mentioned in the death-lay's preface as Henry's concerned "brother". Henry appears as a lone preacher who moved around southwestern Finland more or less on his own. Besides the name, he has only little in common with the Henry in the church <i>vita</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_246_256_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_246_256-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Kokem%C3%A4ki" title="Kokemäki">Kokemäki</a> is often mentioned in traditions as a place where Henry preached. Kokemäki was later one of the central parishes in <a href="/wiki/Satakunta" title="Satakunta">Satakunta</a>. This province was first mentioned in historical documents in 1331.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG/220px-Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="155" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG/330px-Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG/440px-Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4736" data-file-height="3328" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Henrik_Lalli_Ekman.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting of Lalli killing Henry. A romanticized drawing from the 19th century. Painting by <a href="/w/index.php?title=C._A._Ekman&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="C. A. Ekman (page does not exist)">C. A. Ekman</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>The death-lay's version of the bishop's death was different from the vita. The bishop's killer was called <a href="/wiki/Lalli" title="Lalli">Lalli</a>. Lalli's wife Kerttu falsely claimed to him that upon leaving the manor, their ungrateful guest Henry, travelling around on his own in the middle of winter, had without permission or recompense, through violence, taken food, cake from the oven and beer from the cellar, for himself and hay for his horse, and left nothing but ashes. This is supposed to have enraged Lalli so that he immediately grabbed his skis and went in pursuit of the thief, finally chasing Henry down on the ice of Lake <a href="/wiki/K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6nj%C3%A4rvi" title="Köyliönjärvi">Köyliönjärvi</a> in <a href="/wiki/Eura" title="Eura">Eura</a>. There he killed him on the spot with an axe.<sup id="cite_ref-death_lay_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-death_lay-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> Lalli then proceeded to steal the late holy man's hat, called a <a href="/wiki/Mitre" title="Mitre">mitre</a>, and place it on his own head. When Lalli's mother questioned him about where he found the hat, he attempted to take it off, but with it came his scalp. Lalli then died a painful death. The 17th century lay version smugly comments that:<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p>Now the bishop is in joy,<br />
Lalli in evil torture.<br />
The bishop sings with the angels,<br />
Performs a joyful hymn.<br />
Lalli is skiing down in hell.<br />
His left ski slides along.<br />
Into the thick smoke of torture.<br />
With his staff he strikes about:<br />
Demons beset him cruelly.<br />
In the swelter of hell<br />
They assail his pitiful soul.
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<p>The 17th century lay version of the tale was intended to be performed during the annual pilgrimage along Henrik's final route.
</p><p>In some versions of the poem, considered older, Lalli's weapon was a sword. The axe was the murder weapon of Saint Olaf, who was very popular in Finland and may have influenced Henry's legend.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> However, since Lalli is not portrayed as a member of the upper class, it is unlikely that he possessed an expensive weapon like a sword, and the axe is a more historically likely choice for Henry's murder.
</p><p>Before his death, Henry instructed the coachman to gather his remaining body parts in a cloth tied with blue string, place it in a cart drawn by a stallion. When the stallion broke, he was to replace it with an ox, and when the ox stopped, he was to build a church.<sup id="cite_ref-death_lay_37-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-death_lay-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> This is where Henrik's remains were to be buried.
</p><p>Medieval folk traditions enumerate the pestilences and misfortunes which befell Lalli after his slaying of the bishop. His hair and scalp are said to have fallen out as he took off the bishop's cap, taken as a trophy. Removing the bishop's ring from his finger, just bones remained. Eventually he ran into a lake and drowned himself.<sup id="cite_ref-death_lay_37-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-death_lay-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-judas_fable_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-judas_fable-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Development_of_folk_traditions">Development of folk traditions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Development of folk traditions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Basically the death-lay is a simple story of a short-tempered man who falls victim of his "bad-mouthed wife's" sharp tongue. The poem has no pity for Lalli, and he is not depicted as a hero in a story whose true antagonist is Kerttu. The depiction of Henry's death built on an independent tradition that was once in direct competition with that of the legend, which is largely forgotten today. It remains unknown whether the two traditions were built around the same person.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_246_256_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_246_256-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The poem, following the traditional <a href="/wiki/Kalevala" title="Kalevala">Kalevala</a> metre, has survived as several 17th and 18th century literations from various parts of Finland. Some of its elements appear in earlier works, but it hardly dates older than the <i>vita</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_253_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_253-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> There is debate on whether the original poem was constructed by one or more individuals. The writer has however had superficial understanding of the church legends.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_253_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_253-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Both Lalli (Laurentius) and Kerttu (Gertrud) are originally German names, which might indicate that the poem was partly constructed on foreign models, whose influence is visible in other aspects, too. The way Lalli is manipulated to commit the crime and what happens to him later seem to be taken from a medieval <a href="/wiki/Judas_Iscariot" title="Judas Iscariot">Judas</a> fable.<sup id="cite_ref-judas_fable_44-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-judas_fable-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Extensive borrowing from unrelated Finnish legends from the pre-Christian era has taken place as well, leaving quite little original material left at all.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Based on finds from medieval church ruins in the tiny island of <a href="/wiki/Kirkkokari" title="Kirkkokari">Kirkkokari</a> ("Church Rock", previously known as the "Island of Saint Henry") in Lake Köyliönjärvi, the bishop's veneration began in the latter half of the 14th century, well after Henry had received his official status as a local saint, and 200 years after his alleged death.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/St._Henry%27s_Chapel" title="St. Henry&#39;s Chapel">A small granary</a> in the nearby <a href="/wiki/Kokem%C3%A4ki" title="Kokemäki">Kokemäki</a>, claimed to have been the bishop's place of rest the night before his death, could not be dated earlier than the late 15th century in <a href="/wiki/Dendrochronology" title="Dendrochronology">dendrochronological</a> examinations.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, the poem's claim that Henry was buried in Nousiainen was already held to be true around 1300, when his alleged bones were translated from Nousiainen to the Cathedral of Turku. A mid-15th century <i>Chronicon episcoporum Finlandensium</i> also confirmed Köyliö as the place of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-chronicon_1_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronicon_1-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> Neither place is mentioned in the <i>vita</i> in any way. The church seems to have gradually complemented its own legends by adopting elements from the folk traditions, especially during the 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_253_45-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_253-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historical_sources">Historical sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Historical sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Today, the legend of Henry is challenged by some historians to the point of being labelled as pure imagination. Completely invented saints were not exceptional in Europe,<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> and there is no direct evidence of either the crusade or Henry.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bishop's alleged violent death however, is no reason to doubt his potential existence, as many bishops were murdered during the turmoils of the 12th and 13th centuries, although most were not elevated to sainthood. <a href="/wiki/Saxo_Grammaticus" title="Saxo Grammaticus">Saxo Grammaticus</a> said of the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Fotevik" title="Battle of Fotevik">Battle of Fotevik</a> in 1134 that never had so many bishops been killed at the same time. Notable bishops that died violently included the Archbishop of Uppsala in 1187,<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Estonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Estonia">Bishop of Estonia</a> in 1219<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Link%C3%B6ping" title="Bishop of Linköping">Bishop of Linköping</a> in 1220.<sup id="cite_ref-eesti_ajalugu_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eesti_ajalugu-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Uppsala">Bishop of Uppsala</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Bishop of Uppsala">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There is no historical record of a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Uppsala" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Uppsala">Bishop of Uppsala</a> called Henry during the reign of King Eric (about 1156–1160). Early phases of the diocese remain obscure up to the point of <a href="/wiki/Stefan_(archbishop_of_Uppsala)" title="Stefan (archbishop of Uppsala)">Stefan</a>, who was appointed as the archbishop in 1164.<sup id="cite_ref-Åsbrink_1935_p_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Åsbrink_1935_p-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A certain Henry is mentioned in <i>Incerti scriptoris Sueci chronicon primorum in ecclesia Upsalensi archiepiscoporum</i>, a chronicle of Uppsala archbishops, before Coppmannus and Stefan, but after Sverinius (probably mentioned in German sources in 1141/2 as "<a href="/wiki/Bishop_Siwardus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Siwardus">Siwardus</a>"<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup>), Nicolaus and Sweno.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup> Besides the name, the chronicle knows that he was martyred and buried in Finland in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Turku" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Turku">Cathedral of Turku</a>. Latest research dates the chronicle to the early 15th century when Henry's legend was already established in the kingdom, leaving only little significance to its testimony.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg/220px-Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="139" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg/330px-Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg/440px-Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg 2x" data-file-width="650" data-file-height="410" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gamla_uppsala_kyrka.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Gamla_Uppsala" title="Gamla Uppsala">Gamla Uppsala</a> Church, the site of the Uppsala bishops and archbishops until 1273.</div></div></div>
<p>A late 15th century <i>legenda nova</i> claimed that Henry had come to Sweden in the retinue of <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> Nicholas Breakspear, the later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_IV" title="Pope Adrian IV">Pope Adrian IV</a>, and appointed as the Bishop of Uppsala by him. Even though <i>legenda nova</i> states 1150 as the year of the crusade, it is certain from other sources that Nicholas really was in Sweden in 1153. It is not known whether this was just an inference by the writer, based on the fact that also Nicholas was an Englishman.<sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup> However, there is no information about anyone called as Henry accompanying the legate in any source describing the visit, nor him appointing a new bishop in Uppsala.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup> Another claim by <i>legenda nova</i> was that Henry was translated to Turku cathedral already in 1154, which certainly was false since the cathedral was built only in the 1290s.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_59_61-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_59-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> In the late 16th century, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Paulus_Juusten" title="Paulus Juusten">Paulus Juusten</a> claimed that Henry had been the Bishop of Uppsala for two years before the crusade. Based on these postulates, early 20th-century historians assembled 1155 as the year of the crusade and 1156 as the year of Henry's death.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_59_61-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_59-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> Historians from different centuries have also suggested various other years from 1150 to 1158.<sup id="cite_ref-year_of_death_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-year_of_death-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Contradicting these claims, the medieval <i>Annales Suecici Medii Aevi</i><sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> and the 13th century legend of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Botvid" title="Saint Botvid">Saint Botvid</a><sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup> mention some Henry as the Bishop of Uppsala (<i>Henricus scilicet Upsalensis</i>) in 1129, participating in the consecration of the saint's newly built church.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup> He is apparently the same Bishop Henry who died at the Battle of Fotevik in 1134, fighting along with the Danes after being banished from Sweden. Known from the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Roskildense" title="Chronicon Roskildense">Chronicon Roskildense</a></i> written soon after his death and from <a href="/wiki/Saxo_Grammaticus" title="Saxo Grammaticus">Saxo Grammaticus</a>' <i><a href="/wiki/Gesta_Danorum" title="Gesta Danorum">Gesta Danorum</a></i> from the early 13th century, he had fled to Denmark from <a href="/wiki/Sigtuna" title="Sigtuna">Sigtuna</a>, the see of the early Uppland bishops before it was moved a few kilometers to its later location in Uppsala sometime before 1164.<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup> He is ignored in all Swedish bishop chronicles, unless he is the same Henry who was later redated to the 1150s. That would make the claim about him coming to Finland with King Eric a late innovation, where memory about a killed bishop in Uppsala sometime in the 12th century was reused in a new context.<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Noteworthy also, is a story written down by <a href="/wiki/Adam_of_Bremen" title="Adam of Bremen">Adam of Bremen</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Gesta_Hammaburgensis_ecclesiae_pontificum" title="Gesta Hammaburgensis ecclesiae pontificum">Gesta Hammaburgensis ecclesiae pontificum</a></i> (Deeds of <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Bremen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Bremen">Bishops of the Hamburg</a> Church) from 1075/6 about a certain foreigner called <i>Hericus</i>, who was slain and martyred while preaching among the <i><a href="/wiki/Sueones" class="mw-redirect" title="Sueones">Sueones</a></i>. Adam had heard the story from King <a href="/wiki/Sweyn_II_of_Denmark" title="Sweyn II of Denmark">Sweyn II of Denmark</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup> According to some historians, resemblance to later legend about an English-born <i>Henricus</i>, who was allegedly slain and martyred in Finland, is too striking to be a coincidence.<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">&#91;69&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Finland">Bishop of Finland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Bishop of Finland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>No historical source remains that would confirm the existence of a bishop named Henry in Finland. However, papal letters mentioning an unidentified Bishop of Finland in 1209, 1221, 1229 and 1232 have survived.<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">&#91;70&#93;</a></sup> Some copies of another papal letter from 1232 call the bishop as "N.",<sup id="cite_ref-letter_1232_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter_1232-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> but the letter "N" may originally have also been something resembling it. The first certainly known Bishop of Finland is <a href="/wiki/Thomas_(bishop_of_Finland)" title="Thomas (bishop of Finland)">Thomas</a>, who is first mentioned in 1234.<sup id="cite_ref-71" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-71">&#91;71&#93;</a></sup> It is however possible, that <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Fulco" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Fulco">Fulco</a>, the Bishop of Estonia mentioned in sources from 1165 and 1171,<sup id="cite_ref-rebane_72-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rebane-72">&#91;72&#93;</a></sup> was the same as Folquinus, a legendary Bishop of Finland at the end of the 12th century, but this remains only a theory.<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">&#91;73&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:B-Alexander_III1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/B-Alexander_III1.jpg/150px-B-Alexander_III1.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="216" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/B-Alexander_III1.jpg/225px-B-Alexander_III1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/B-Alexander_III1.jpg/300px-B-Alexander_III1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="355" data-file-height="512" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:B-Alexander_III1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> was the first Pope to address the situation of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic church">Catholic</a> mission in <a href="/wiki/Estonia" title="Estonia">Estonia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>No Bishop or Diocese of Finland is mentioned in a papal letter from 1171 (or 1172) by the seemingly well-informed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>, who otherwise addressed the situation of the church in Finland. The Pope mentions that there were preachers, presumably from Sweden, working in Finland and was worried about their bad treatment by the Finns.<sup id="cite_ref-74" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-74">&#91;74&#93;</a></sup> The Pope had earlier in 1165 authorized the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Fulco" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Fulco">first missionary Bishop of Estonia</a> to be appointed, and was a close acquaintance of both <a href="/wiki/Eskil_of_Lund" title="Eskil of Lund">Eskil</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lund" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lund">Archbishop of Lund</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Stefan_(archbishop_of_Uppsala)" title="Stefan (archbishop of Uppsala)">Stefan</a>, the Archbishop of Uppsala, who both had spent time with him in France where he had been exiled in the 1160s. Following the situation in Estonia, the Pope personally interfered in the Estonian mission in 1171, ordering assistance for the local Bishop Fulco from Norway.<sup id="cite_ref-rebane_72-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rebane-72">&#91;72&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>No surviving list of bishops or dioceses under the Archbishop of Uppsala from 1164, 1189, 1192, 1233, 1241 or 1248 contains any reference to Finland, neither factual or propagandist. No claim about a Swedish bishop in Finland is made in any other source from the era prior to the so-called <a href="/wiki/Second_Swedish_Crusade" title="Second Swedish Crusade">Second Swedish Crusade</a> in 1249.<sup id="cite_ref-varhaiskeskiajan_lahteita_75-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-varhaiskeskiajan_lahteita-75">&#91;75&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Anders_Sunesen.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Anders_Sunesen.jpg/220px-Anders_Sunesen.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="328" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Anders_Sunesen.jpg/330px-Anders_Sunesen.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Anders_Sunesen.jpg/440px-Anders_Sunesen.jpg 2x" data-file-width="950" data-file-height="1417" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Anders_Sunesen.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Anders_Sunesen" title="Anders Sunesen">Anders Sunesen</a> (sitting) was the first to inform the Pope about a bishop in Finland.</div></div></div>
<p>The first mention of a bishop in Finland is from a papal letter in 1209. It was sent to <a href="/wiki/Anders_Sunesen" title="Anders Sunesen">Archbishop Anders</a> of Lund by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> as a reply to the Archbishop's earlier letter which has not survived. According to the Archbishop, the Bishop of the newly established church in Finland was dead, apparently from natural causes since his passing away is mentioned to have been "lawful", and the see had been vacant for some time. The Archbishop had complained to the Pope how difficult it was to get anyone to be a bishop in Finland and planned to appoint someone without formal adequacy, who was already working in Finland. The Pope approved of Archbishop's suggestion without questioning his opinions.<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">&#91;76&#93;</a></sup> It is noteworthy that the Archbishop of Uppsala, <a href="/wiki/Valerius_(archbishop_of_Uppsala)" title="Valerius (archbishop of Uppsala)">Valerius</a> (1207–1219/1224), was also in Denmark at the time, temporarily exiled from Sweden after having allied with the deposed <a href="/wiki/Sverker_II_of_Sweden" title="Sverker II of Sweden">King Sverker</a>, yet another exile in Denmark.<sup id="cite_ref-Åsbrink_1935_p_55-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Åsbrink_1935_p-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Whether the appointment of the said preacher ever took place, remains unknown. Note should be taken that the King of Sweden at the time was <a href="/wiki/Eric_X_of_Sweden" title="Eric X of Sweden">Eric</a>, a grandson of his better known namesake Eric the Saint. Eric had taken over Sweden in 1208 and was crowned king two years later. The Pope who had strongly sided with Sverker, ignored him at first, but finally recognized him in 1216, commenting many requests that he had apparently made ever since having taken the office. Based on the papal letter that year, Eric seems to have had a plan to invade some country that allegedly had been "taken from the heathens by his predecessors" and was allowed to install a bishop there.<sup id="cite_ref-77" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-77">&#91;77&#93;</a></sup> Similar letters were sent to the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Denmark">King of Denmark</a> in 1208 and 1218, who is known to have meant <a href="/wiki/Estonia" title="Estonia">Estonia</a> both times.<sup id="cite_ref-varhaiskeskiajan_lahteita_75-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-varhaiskeskiajan_lahteita-75">&#91;75&#93;</a></sup> Sweden also <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Lihula" title="Battle of Lihula">attacked Estonia</a> in 1220.<sup id="cite_ref-eesti_ajalugu_54-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eesti_ajalugu-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup> Eric died of illness 1216. Almost nothing is known about his time as the king.<sup id="cite_ref-Linna_1989_113_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Linna_1989_113-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nevertheless, someone was eventually appointed and installed as the new bishop, since <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> sent a letter directly to an unnamed Bishop of Finland in 1221. According to the letter, Archbishop Valerius<sup id="cite_ref-78" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-78">&#91;78&#93;</a></sup> had followed the situation in Finland and sent a report to the Pope, worried about a threat from unidentified "barbarians". It is notable that when the Pope quoted Valerius in his letter, he calls the church in Finland to have been established "newly", the same claim that Anders had made 12 years earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-79" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-79">&#91;79&#93;</a></sup> The list of Swedish bishops which survives from this era is from king John Sverkerson's coronation from the year 1219 and it mentions the bishops which have been present at the coronation. Finland as well as Wäxjö are not among those five, which so seem to have been all the bishops of the Swedish realm at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-80" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-80">&#91;80&#93;</a></sup> So the Finnish bishop's possible position under Uppsala's primacy is highly improbable.<sup id="cite_ref-varhaiskeskiajan_lahteita_75-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-varhaiskeskiajan_lahteita-75">&#91;75&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite so many high-ranking church representatives being involved in the 1209/1221 arrangements, later chronicles are fully ignorant on the situation in Finland at the time, or if there was even a bishop then. The first 13th-century bishop is said to have been <a href="/wiki/Thomas_(bishop_of_Finland)" title="Thomas (bishop of Finland)">Thomas</a>, and his predecessor remains unknown. According to 15th and 16th century chronicles, Henry was followed by bishops <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Rodulff" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Rodulff">Rodulff</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Folquinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Folquinus">Folquinus</a>, after whom there was a 25–30-year gap before Thomas.<sup id="cite_ref-chronicon_2_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronicon_2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-chronicon_1_49-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronicon_1-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> However, according to the papal letter Ex tuarum no such gap has ever existed, since the archbishop of Lund
was given the right to anoint a new bishop to Finland in 1209 after the death of the previous. So the logic and datings of the sixteenth-century writers must be esteemed as false. The date 1209 is far too early for a Dominican like Thomas to step into the office, and so Rodolphus, the first real bishop of Finland and his successor Folquinus must be considered as 13th-century bishops nominated and appointed by the Danish and not by the Swedes.<sup id="cite_ref-81" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-81">&#91;81&#93;</a></sup> As an extra proof of this the ancient Finnish taxation system of church taxes has its roots in Denmark, not in Sweden.<sup id="cite_ref-82" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-82">&#91;82&#93;</a></sup> And the same goes to lay taxes especially in the Åland Islands and to the old Finnish monetary system.<sup id="cite_ref-83" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-83">&#91;83&#93;</a></sup> As J. W. Ruuth already nearly a hundred years ago pointed out, Finland was at that time a Danish and not a Swedish mission territory, where the Danes according to the Danish annals made there expeditions in 1191, 1202 and possible even 1210<sup id="cite_ref-84" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-84">&#91;84&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:352px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG/350px-Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG" decoding="async" width="350" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG/525px-Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG/700px-Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="1413" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Henrik_Cruisade_Nousiainen.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Henry's (empty) sarcophagus in the church of Nousiainen with depictions of the crusade.</div></div></div>
<p>Henry was allegedly buried in <a href="/wiki/Nousiainen" title="Nousiainen">Nousiainen</a>, from where his bones—or at least something that was thought to be his bones—were transported to <a href="/wiki/Turku" title="Turku">Turku</a> in 1300.<sup id="cite_ref-85" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-85">&#91;85&#93;</a></sup> In addition to traditions, the only source connecting Nousiainen to early bishops is a letter signed by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_(bishop_of_Finland)" title="Thomas (bishop of Finland)">Bishop Thomas</a> in Nousiainen in 1234.<sup id="cite_ref-86" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-86">&#91;86&#93;</a></sup> Archaeological excavations of pre-Catholic cemeteries in Nousiainen and surrounding parishes show a clear discontinuation of traditions in the early 13th century, but no abrupt changes are apparent in the religious environment among the 12th century finds.<sup id="cite_ref-87" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-87">&#91;87&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Whatever the case, the bishop's grave seems to have been traced to Nousiainen latest after his elevation to sainthood. A number of medieval documents mention that the bishop's grave continued to be located in the local church, presumably meaning that all the bones had not been translated to Turku.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_108_88-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_108-88">&#91;88&#93;</a></sup> The church was later adorned with a grandiose 15th century <a href="/wiki/Cenotaph" title="Cenotaph">cenotaph</a>, whose replica can be found in the <a href="/wiki/National_Museum_of_Finland" title="National Museum of Finland">National Museum of Finland</a> in <a href="/wiki/Helsinki" title="Helsinki">Helsinki</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-89" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-89">&#91;89&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Henrik_sormi.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Henrik_sormi.JPG/150px-Henrik_sormi.JPG" decoding="async" width="150" height="138" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Henrik_sormi.JPG/225px-Henrik_sormi.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Henrik_sormi.JPG/300px-Henrik_sormi.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2816" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Henrik_sormi.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Henry's finger depicted in the seal of <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Turku" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Turku">Bishopric of Turku</a> from 1618.</div></div></div>
<p>Most of the bones in Turku were still in place in 1720 when they were catalogued for a transfer to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petersburg" title="Saint Petersburg">Saint Petersburg</a> during the Russian occupation of Finland in the <a href="/wiki/Great_Northern_War" title="Great Northern War">Great Northern War</a>. The man behind the idea was Swedish Count <a href="/wiki/Gustaf_Otto_Douglas" title="Gustaf Otto Douglas">Gustaf Otto Douglas</a> who had defected to the Russian side during the war and was in charge of the <a href="/wiki/Greater_Wrath" class="mw-redirect" title="Greater Wrath">grim occupation</a> of Finland.<sup id="cite_ref-90" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-90">&#91;90&#93;</a></sup> What happened to the bones after that, remains unknown. According to some sources, the Russian vessel transporting the relics sank on the way.<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_108_88-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_108-88">&#91;88&#93;</a></sup> However, it is generally acknowledged that a piece of Henry's <a href="/wiki/Ulna" title="Ulna">ulna</a> had been placed in <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Hemming" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Hemming">Bishop Hemming</a>'s reliquarium that was built in 1514 and treasured in the cathedral. Also enclosed was a piece of parchment stating the bone belonged to Henry. During the restoration work of the cathedral, the relic was relocated to the <a href="/wiki/Finnish_National_Board_of_Antiquities" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnish National Board of Antiquities">National Board of Antiquities</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-relic_news_91-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relic_news-91">&#91;91&#93;</a></sup> It was later placed inside the altar of <a href="/wiki/St._Henry%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Henry&#39;s Cathedral">St. Henry's Cathedral</a> in Helsinki.<sup id="cite_ref-92" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-92">&#91;92&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1924, a jawless skull and several other bones were found in a sealed closet in the Cathedral of Turku. These are also referred to as Henry's relics in popular media and even by the church, even though that designation remains speculative and the bones may have belonged to another saint. The bones are currently stored in the Cathedral of Turku.<sup id="cite_ref-relic_news_91-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-relic_news-91">&#91;91&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Henry.27s_status_today"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Henry's_status_today">Henry's status today</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_(bishop_of_Finland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: Henry&#039;s status today">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:102px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg/100px-Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg.png" decoding="async" width="100" height="115" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg/150px-Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg/200px-Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="575" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Nousiainen.vaakuna.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Henry in the modern coat of arms of <a href="/wiki/Nousiainen" title="Nousiainen">Nousiainen</a>.</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:102px;"><a href="/wiki/File:K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg/100px-K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg.png" decoding="async" width="100" height="118" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg/150px-K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg/200px-K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6.vaakuna.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Miter" class="mw-redirect" title="Miter">Miter</a> in the coat of arms of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A4kyl%C3%A4" title="Säkylä">Säkylä</a> refers to Bishop Henry, while the <a href="/wiki/Axe" title="Axe">axe</a> refers to his murderer Lalli.</div></div></div>
<p>Although Henry has never been officially <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>, he has been referred to as a saint since as early as 1296 according to a papal document of the time,<sup id="cite_ref-heikkila_95_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heikkila_95-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> and continues to be called as such today as well.<sup id="cite_ref-93" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-93">&#91;93&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-94" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-94">&#91;94&#93;</a></sup> On the basis of the traditional accounts of Henry's death, his recognition as saint took place prior to the founding of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_the_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of the Saints">Congregation for the Causes of the Saints</a> and the official <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> process of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Henry is currently commemorated on January 19 on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints_(Lutheran)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints (Lutheran)">calendar of commemorations</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_America" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church in America">Evangelical Lutheran Church in America</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_Canada" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church in Canada">Evangelical Lutheran Church in Canada</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-95" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-95">&#91;95&#93;</a></sup> January 19 is also Henry's <a href="/wiki/Name_day" title="Name day">name day</a> (so-called "<a href="/w/index.php?title=Heikinp%C3%A4iv%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Heikinpäivä (page does not exist)">Heikinpäivä</a>") in Sweden and Finland.<sup id="cite_ref-96" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-96">&#91;96&#93;</a></sup> He continues to be remembered as a local observance in the Catholic Church of Finland, whose <a href="/wiki/St._Henry%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Henry&#39;s Cathedral">sole cathedral</a> is <a href="/wiki/Dedication_of_churches" title="Dedication of churches">dedicated</a> to him.<sup id="cite_ref-97" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-97">&#91;97&#93;</a></sup> The cathedral was consecrated in 1860 and is headed by fr. Marco Pasinato.<sup id="cite_ref-98" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-98">&#91;98&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Kirkkokari" title="Kirkkokari">The Kirkkokari island</a> in Lake Köyliönjärvi remains the only Catholic place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> in Finland, with a memorial service held every year on second Sunday in June before the <a href="/wiki/Midsummer" title="Midsummer">Midsummer</a> festival. Also the medieval 140&#160;km countryside route, the <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Henry%27s_Way" title="Saint Henry&#39;s Way">Saint Henry's Way</a></i>, from Köyliö to Nousiainen has been marked all the way for people willing to walk through it.<sup id="cite_ref-99" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-99">&#91;99&#93;</a></sup> Association of "Ecumenical pilgrimage of St. Henry" has been organized around the event.<sup id="cite_ref-100" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-100">&#91;100&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Based on folk traditions about the bishop's activities, the municipalities of <a href="/wiki/Nousiainen" title="Nousiainen">Nousiainen</a>, <a href="/wiki/K%C3%B6yli%C3%B6" title="Köyliö">Köyliö</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kokem%C3%A4ki" title="Kokemäki">Kokemäki</a> use images from Henry's legend in their coats of arms.<sup id="cite_ref-101" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-101">&#91;101&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today, Henry and his alleged murderer Lalli remain two of the best-known persons from the mediæval history of Finland.<sup id="cite_ref-102" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-102">&#91;102&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<li id="cite_note-13"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-13">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The so-called "chancellor bishops of Finland" have been shown to have been a creation of later historians, especially by Juusten in his chronicle of the Finnish Bishops during the 16th century, by Herman Shück in Historisk Tidskrift 2/1963 in his article "Kansler och capella regis under folkungatiden". As to Ragvald and Kettil, the title "chancellor" rests solely on Juustens imagination and is in contradiction with the real and known chancellors of the time. As to Bero, who is mentioned already in the so-called "Palmskölds fragment" probably from the time of Turku bishop Magnus Tavast from the 15th century (dating by Jarl Gallén in "De engelska munkarna i Uppsala – ett katedralkloster på 1100-talet, <i><a href="/wiki/Historisk_Tidskrift_f%C3%B6r_Finland" title="Historisk Tidskrift för Finland">Historisk Tidskrift för Finland</a></i> 1976, p.17). According to Schück, Bero might have been the King's chaplain.</span>
