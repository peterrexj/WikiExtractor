<p><b>William Wyndham Grenville, 1st Baron Grenville</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Ireland" title="Privy Council of Ireland">PC (Ire)</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society" title="Fellow of the Royal Society">FRS</a></span> (25 October 1759&#160;&#8211;&#32;12 January 1834) was a British <a href="/wiki/Pittite_Tory" class="mw-redirect" title="Pittite Tory">Pittite Tory</a> politician who served as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of the United Kingdom</a> from 1806 to 1807, but was a supporter of the <a href="/wiki/Whigs_(British_political_party)" title="Whigs (British political party)">Whigs</a> for the duration of the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars" title="Napoleonic Wars">Napoleonic Wars</a>.  As prime minister, his most significant achievement was the <a href="/wiki/Abolition_of_the_slave_trade" class="mw-redirect" title="Abolition of the slave trade">abolition of the slave trade</a> in 1807.  However, his government failed to either make peace with <a href="/wiki/First_French_Empire" title="First French Empire">France</a> or to accomplish <a href="/wiki/Catholic_emancipation" title="Catholic emancipation">Catholic emancipation</a> and it was dismissed in the same year.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Grenville was the son of the Whig Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/George_Grenville" title="George Grenville">George Grenville</a>. His mother, <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Grenville" title="Elizabeth Grenville">Elizabeth</a>, was the daughter of the Tory statesman <a href="/wiki/Sir_William_Wyndham,_3rd_Baronet" title="Sir William Wyndham, 3rd Baronet">Sir William Wyndham, 3rd Baronet</a>. He had two elder brothers: <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Grenville" title="Thomas Grenville">Thomas</a> and <a href="/wiki/George_Nugent-Temple-Grenville,_1st_Marquess_of_Buckingham" title="George Nugent-Temple-Grenville, 1st Marquess of Buckingham">George</a>. He was thus uncle to the <a href="/wiki/1st_Duke_of_Buckingham_and_Chandos" class="mw-redirect" title="1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos">1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos</a>.
</p><p>He was also related to the Pitt family by marriage since <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham</a>, had married his father's sister <a href="/wiki/Hester_Pitt,_Countess_of_Chatham" title="Hester Pitt, Countess of Chatham">Hester</a>. The younger Grenville was thus the first cousin of <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a>.
</p><p>Grenville was educated at <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a>; <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>; and <a href="/wiki/Lincoln%27s_Inn" title="Lincoln&#39;s Inn">Lincoln's Inn</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_career">Political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Grenville entered the <a href="/wiki/British_House_of_Commons" class="mw-redirect" title="British House of Commons">House of Commons</a> in February 1782 as member for the <a href="/wiki/Borough_of_Buckingham" class="mw-redirect" title="Borough of Buckingham">borough of Buckingham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911581_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911581-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He soon became a close ally of the prime minister, his cousin <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a>. In September, he became secretary to the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland" title="Lord Lieutenant of Ireland">Lord Lieutenant of Ireland</a>, who was his brother <a href="/wiki/George_Nugent-Temple-Grenville,_1st_Marquess_of_Buckingham" title="George Nugent-Temple-Grenville, 1st Marquess of Buckingham">George</a>. He left the House the following year and served in the government as <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a> from 1784 to 1789. In 1789, he served briefly as <a href="/wiki/Speaker_of_the_House_of_Commons_(United_Kingdom)" title="Speaker of the House of Commons (United Kingdom)">Speaker of the House of Commons</a> before he entered the cabinet as <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a> and resigned his other posts.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911581_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911581-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He became <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Lords" title="Leader of the House of Lords">Leader of the House of Lords</a> when he was raised to the peerage the next year as <b>Baron Grenville</b>, of <a href="/wiki/Wotton_Underwood" title="Wotton Underwood">Wotton under Bernewood</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="Buckinghamshire">County of Buckingham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:William_Wyndham_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/William_Wyndham_Grenville%2C_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg/220px-William_Wyndham_Grenville%2C_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="274" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/William_Wyndham_Grenville%2C_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg/330px-William_Wyndham_Grenville%2C_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/William_Wyndham_Grenville%2C_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg/440px-William_Wyndham_Grenville%2C_1st_Baron_Grenville_by_Gainsborough_Dupont.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2400" data-file-height="2989" /></a><figcaption>Lord Grenville by <a href="/wiki/Gainsborough_Dupont" title="Gainsborough Dupont">Gainsborough Dupont</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1790</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1791, he succeeded the <a href="/wiki/Francis_Osborne,_5th_Duke_of_Leeds" title="Francis Osborne, 5th Duke of Leeds">Duke of Leeds</a> as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_(UK)" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs (UK)">Foreign Secretary</a>. Grenville's decade as Foreign Secretary was dramatic with the <a href="/wiki/Wars_of_the_French_Revolution" class="mw-redirect" title="Wars of the French Revolution">Wars of the French Revolution</a>. During the war, Grenville was the leader of the party that focused on the fighting on the continent as the key to victory and opposed the faction of <a href="/wiki/Henry_Dundas" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry Dundas">Henry Dundas</a>, which favoured war at sea and in the colonies.
</p><p>Grenville left office with Pitt in 1801 over the issue of <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">George III</a>'s refusal to assent to <a href="/wiki/Catholic_emancipation" title="Catholic emancipation">Catholic emancipation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Grenville did part-time military service at home as Major in the <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire_Yeomanry" class="mw-redirect" title="Buckinghamshire Yeomanry">Buckinghamshire Yeomanry</a> cavalry in 1794 and as lieutenant-colonel in the South Buckinghamshire volunteer regiment in 1806.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his years out of office, Grenville became close to the opposition Whig leader <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox" title="Charles James Fox">Charles James Fox</a>, and when Pitt returned to office in 1804, Grenville sided with Fox and did not take part.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>After Pitt's death in 1806, Grenville became the head of the "<a href="#Ministry_of_All_the_Talents">Ministry of All the Talents</a>", a coalition between Grenville's supporters, the <a href="/wiki/Foxite" title="Foxite">Foxite</a> Whigs, and the supporters of former Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Henry_Addington,_1st_Viscount_Sidmouth" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry Addington, 1st Viscount Sidmouth">Lord Sidmouth</a>, with Grenville as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury" title="First Lord of the Treasury">First Lord of the Treasury</a> and Fox as Foreign Secretary as joint leaders. Grenville's cousin <a href="/wiki/William_Windham" title="William Windham">William Windham</a> served as Secretary of State for War and the Colonies, and his younger brother, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Grenville" title="Thomas Grenville">Thomas Grenville</a>, served briefly as First Lord of the Admiralty.
</p><p>The Ministry ultimately accomplished little and failed either to make peace with France or to accomplish Catholic emancipation, the later attempt resulting in the ministry's dismissal in March 1807. It had one significant achievement, however, in the abolition of the <a href="/wiki/Slave_trade" class="mw-redirect" title="Slave trade">slave trade</a> in 1807.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Post-premiership">Post-premiership</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Post-premiership">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg/220px-Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="289" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg/330px-Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg/440px-Lord_Grenville_as_Chancellor_of_Oxford_by_William_Owen.jpg 2x" data-file-width="718" data-file-height="944" /></a><figcaption>Lord Grenville as <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_(education)" title="Chancellor (education)">Chancellor</a> of <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">Oxford</a>, by <a href="/wiki/William_Owen_(painter)" title="William Owen (painter)">William Owen</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1809-25</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>In the years after the fall of the ministry, Grenville continued in opposition by maintaining his alliance with <a href="/wiki/Charles_Grey,_2nd_Earl_Grey" title="Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey">Lord Grey</a> and the <a href="/wiki/British_Whig_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig Party">Whigs</a>, criticising the <a href="/wiki/Peninsular_War" title="Peninsular War">Peninsular War</a> and, with Grey, refusing to join <a href="/wiki/Robert_Jenkinson,_2nd_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Robert Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool">Lord Liverpool</a>'s government in 1812.
</p><p>In the postwar years, Grenville gradually moved back closer to the Tories but never again returned to the cabinet. In 1815, he separated from his friend <a href="/wiki/Charles_Grey,_2nd_Earl_Grey" title="Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey">Charles Grey</a> and supported the war policy of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Jenkinson,_2nd_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Robert Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool">Lord Liverpool</a>. In 1819, when the Marquess of Lansdowne brought forward his motion for an inquiry into the causes of the distress and discontent in the manufacturing districts, Grenville delivered a speech advocating repressive measures.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911582-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His political career was ended by a stroke in 1823.
</p><p>Grenville also served as <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_(education)" title="Chancellor (education)">Chancellor</a> of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">University of Oxford</a> from 1810 until his death in 1834.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Historians find it hard to tell exactly which separate roles Pitt, Grenville and Dundas played in setting war policy toward France but agree that Grenville played a major role at all times until 1801. The consensus of scholars is that war with France presented an unexpected complex of problems. There was a conflict between secular ideologies, the conscription of huge armies, the new role of Russia as a continental power and especially the sheer length and cost of the multiple coalitions.
</p><p>Grenville energetically worked to build and hold together the Allied coalitions and paid suitable attention to smaller members such as Denmark and Sardinia. He negotiated the complex alliance with Russia and Austria. He hoped that with British financing, they would bear the brunt of the ground campaigns against the French.
</p><p>Grenville's influence was at the maximum during the formation of the Second Coalition. His projections of easy success were greatly exaggerated, and the result was another round of disappointment. His resignation in 1801 was caused primarily by the king's refusal to allow Catholics to sit in Parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:CITESHORT" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:CITESHORT"><span title="More information is required to link this short citation to its long citation. (May 2021)">incomplete short citation</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Dropmore_House">Dropmore House</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Dropmore House">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg/220px-A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="308" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg/330px-A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg/440px-A_Pair_of_Broad_Bottoms.jpg 2x" data-file-width="976" data-file-height="1365" /></a><figcaption>A caricature of <a href="/wiki/Saartjie_Baartman" class="mw-redirect" title="Saartjie Baartman">Saartjie Baartman</a>, Lord Grenville, and <a href="/wiki/Richard_Brinsley_Sheridan" title="Richard Brinsley Sheridan">Richard Brinsley Sheridan</a> by William Heath</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Dropmore_Park" title="Dropmore Park">Dropmore House</a> was built in the 1790s for Lord Grenville. The architects were <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Wyatt" title="Samuel Wyatt">Samuel Wyatt</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_Heathcote_Tatham" title="Charles Heathcote Tatham">Charles Tatham</a>. Grenville knew the spot from rambles during his time at <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a> and prized its distant views of his old school and of <a href="/wiki/Windsor_Castle" title="Windsor Castle">Windsor Castle</a>. On his first day in occupation, he planted two cedar trees. At least another 2,500 trees were planted. By the time he died, his <a href="/wiki/Pinetum" class="mw-redirect" title="Pinetum">pinetum</a> contained the biggest collection of <a href="/wiki/Conifer" title="Conifer">conifer</a> species in Britain. Part of the post-millennium restoration is to use what survives as the basis for a collection of some 200 species.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lord Grenville married <a href="/wiki/Anne_Pitt" class="mw-redirect" title="Anne Pitt">Anne</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pitt,_1st_Baron_Camelford" title="Thomas Pitt, 1st Baron Camelford">Thomas Pitt, 1st Baron Camelford</a>, in 1792. The marriage was childless. He died in January 1834, aged 74, when the barony became extinct.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Hereditary_Peerage">Hereditary Peerage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Hereditary Peerage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>He was given a <a href="/wiki/Hereditary_Peer" class="mw-redirect" title="Hereditary Peer">Hereditary Peerage</a> in 1790 allowing him to sit in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>. He sat with the <a href="/wiki/Whigs_(British_political_party)" title="Whigs (British political party)">Whig Party</a> Benches. He took the title of <b>1st Baron Grenville</b>. This title became extinct upon his death in 1834 as he had no surviving heir.</li></ul>
