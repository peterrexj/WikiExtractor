<p><b>Saints Theodora and Didymus</b> (died 304) are <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> whose <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">legend</a> is based on a 4th-century <i>acta</i> and the word of <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Saint Ambrose</a>. The pair were <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in the reigns of co-ruling <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperors" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperors">Roman Emperors</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximianus</a>. St. Theodora should not be confused with another <a href="/wiki/Theodora_of_Alexandria" title="Theodora of Alexandria">St. Theodora of Alexandria</a> commemorated on September 11.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodora_and_Didymus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theodora was a young noblewoman of <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> who had refused to offer sacrifice to the Roman gods.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Standing trial before the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> Eustratius, she bravely confessed herself a Christian. The prefect asked why she had not married, pointing out that she was of a noble family and beautiful and could have her choice of husbands.  She replied that she had dedicated herself to God, and had resolved to remain a virgin for the name of Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eustratius had her imprisoned, giving her time to reconsider, and he threatened to have her taken to a brothel if she persisted in her disobedience. Three days later, Theodora was brought again to trial, but remained resolute.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Accordingly, Theodora was taken to a brothel. Dissolute youths began to argue which of them should be the first to have her. The Christian Didymus, dressed in soldier's garb, entered and chased the profligates out. Didymus exchanged clothes with her, and she escaped.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  When a man came to despoil the virgin, Didymus revealed himself.  Didymus was taken prisoner and brought to the prefect, where he was condemned to death.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Saint Ambrose says that Theodora could not allow her savior to die alone and that she joined Didymus before Proculus.
</p><p>Didymus and, according to Ambrose, Theodora were beheaded. Didymus's body was burned. They are not included in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, the official list of saints of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> Church.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The story of Theodora and Didymus is almost identical to that of <a href="/wiki/Antonia_and_Alexander" class="mw-redirect" title="Antonia and Alexander">Saints Antonia and Alexander</a>.
</p><p>The theme of the story might reflect the institution of <a href="/wiki/Religious_prostitution" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious prostitution">religious prostitution</a>, prevalent in the ancient <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a>, as remembered in a highly-disapproving Christian tradition.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Adaptations">Adaptations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodora_and_Didymus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Adaptations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pierre_Corneille" title="Pierre Corneille">Pierre Corneille</a> wrote in 1645 a <a href="/wiki/Tragedy" title="Tragedy">tragedy</a> <i>Theodore, virgin and martyr</i>, based on this story, but he transferred it to Antioch. It was a signal failure, removed after only five performances. Corneille's play was the basis of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Boyle" title="Robert Boyle">Robert Boyle</a>'s version, <i>The Martyrdom of Theodora, and of Didymus</i>, privately composed and circulated in 1648-9 then published by Boyle in a revised edition in 1687. Boyle's version was read into the eighteenth century, with <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Johnson" title="Samuel Johnson">Samuel Johnson</a> commenting that it was "the first 'attempt to employ the ornaments of romance in the decoration of religion.'"<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The oratorio <i><a href="/wiki/Theodora_(Handel)" title="Theodora (Handel)">Theodora</a></i> composed by <a href="/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel" title="George Frideric Handel">George Frideric Handel</a> in 1749 was based on the story of Theodora and Didymus.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saints</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Antonina and Alexander</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Virgin and Martyr, Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">3rd century<br /><a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">May 3, 313<br />Asia Minor</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/June_10_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="June 10 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">June 10</a> (Orthodoxy)<br /><a href="/wiki/May_3" title="May 3">May 3</a> (Catholicism)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Antonina_and_Alexander">Antonina and Alexander</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodora_and_Didymus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Antonina and Alexander">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><b>Saints Antonina and Alexander</b> were <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> of 313, and they are <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> whose <i>acta</i> are legendary.  The story of the two is nearly identical to that of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Theodora_and_Didymus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints Theodora and Didymus">Saints Theodora and Didymus</a>.
</p><p>Under <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, all Roman women of suitable age were commanded to marry and, if possible, produce children.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> At the same time, Diocletian persecuted Christians.  The legend of Antonina and Alexander has the former being forced to a <a href="/wiki/Brothel" title="Brothel">brothel</a> (the penalty for women who refused to wed), where a Christian soldier named Alexander came to her in the guise of a customer. Instead of deflowering her, he traded clothes with her, allowing Antonina to escape.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Alexander was discovered very soon after, and both Antonina and Alexander were executed by being burned alive.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"Antonina" and "Alexander" may be <a href="/wiki/Mnemonic" title="Mnemonic">mnemonic</a> device names that made oral repetition of the tale easier, and so it is possible, if not likely, that they are invented names.  Whether Theodora and Didymus were the originals or not, it is possible that these legends are shadows of an actual martyrdom that had been lost to the story tellers. The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Antonina and Alexander in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> is May 3.
</p>
