<p><b>Titus Brandsma</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Carmelites" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Carmelites">OCarm</a> (<small>Dutch pronunciation:&#32;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="nl-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Dutch" title="Help:IPA/Dutch">[ˈtitys ˈbrɑntsmaː]</a></span>; born <i>Anno Sjoerd Brandsma</i>; 23 February 1881&#160;&#8211;&#32;26 July 1942) was a <a href="/wiki/Dutch_people" title="Dutch people">Dutch</a> <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> and professor of <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a>. Brandsma was vehemently opposed to <a href="/wiki/Nazism" title="Nazism">Nazi ideology</a> and spoke out against it many times before the <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">Second World War</a>. He was imprisoned at the <a href="/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp" title="Dachau concentration camp">Dachau concentration camp</a>, where he was murdered. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in November 1985 as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">martyr</a> of the faith and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> on 15 May 2022 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Titus_Brandsma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Megen_panorama,_kerk,_latijnse_school,_klooster,_hof_van_lof.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Megen_panorama%2C_kerk%2C_latijnse_school%2C_klooster%2C_hof_van_lof.JPG/220px-Megen_panorama%2C_kerk%2C_latijnse_school%2C_klooster%2C_hof_van_lof.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="89" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Megen_panorama%2C_kerk%2C_latijnse_school%2C_klooster%2C_hof_van_lof.JPG/330px-Megen_panorama%2C_kerk%2C_latijnse_school%2C_klooster%2C_hof_van_lof.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Megen_panorama%2C_kerk%2C_latijnse_school%2C_klooster%2C_hof_van_lof.JPG/440px-Megen_panorama%2C_kerk%2C_latijnse_school%2C_klooster%2C_hof_van_lof.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2671" data-file-height="1080" /></a><figcaption>The grounds of the Franciscan friary in <a href="/wiki/Megen" title="Megen">Megen</a> where Brandsma did his high school studies</figcaption></figure>
<p>Brandsma was born Anno Sjoerd Brandsma to Titus Brandsma (died 1920) and his wife Tjitsje Postma (died 1933) at Oegeklooster, near <a href="/wiki/Hartwerd" title="Hartwerd">Hartwerd</a>, in the Province of <a href="/wiki/Friesland" title="Friesland">Friesland</a> in 1881.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents, who ran a small dairy farm, were devout and committed Catholics, a minority in a predominantly <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinist</a> region. With the exception of one daughter, all of their children (three daughters and two sons) entered <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">religious orders</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From the age of 11, Brandsma pursued his secondary studies in the town of <a href="/wiki/Megen" title="Megen">Megen</a>, at a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> run <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> for boys considering a priestly or religious vocation.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carmelite_friar">Carmelite friar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Titus_Brandsma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Carmelite friar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brandsma entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the Carmelite friars in <a href="/wiki/Boxmeer" title="Boxmeer">Boxmeer</a> on 17 September 1898, where he took the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> "Titus" (in honour of his father) by which he is now known. He professed his first <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">vows</a> in October 1899.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ordained a priest in 1905, Brandsma was knowledgeable in Carmelite <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mysticism</a> and was awarded a <a href="/wiki/Doctorate_of_philosophy" class="mw-redirect" title="Doctorate of philosophy">doctorate of philosophy</a> at Rome in 1909. From 1909 to 1923 he lived in <a href="/wiki/Oss" title="Oss">Oss</a> and worked as a writer and teacher.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> From 1916 on, he initiated and led a project to translate the works of <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila" title="Teresa of Ávila">Teresa of Ávila</a> into Dutch.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1919 he founded and for two years acted as head of a secondary school in Oss—the present day Titus Brandsma Lyceum.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1921, Brandsma worked to resolve a controversy concerning Belgian artist <a href="/wiki/Albert_Servaes" title="Albert Servaes">Albert Servaes</a>' depiction of the <a href="/wiki/Stations_of_the_Cross" title="Stations of the Cross">Stations of the Cross</a>. From this came his series of meditations on each of the 14 stations.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>One of the founders of the Catholic University of Nijmegen (now <a href="/wiki/Radboud_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Radboud University">Radboud University</a>), Brandsma became a professor of philosophy and the history of mysticism at the school in 1923. He later served as <i><a href="/wiki/Rector_(academic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Rector (academic)">rector magnificus</a></i> (1932–33).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was noted for his constant availability to everyone, rather than for his scholarly work as a professor. Brandsma also worked as a journalist and was the ecclesiastical adviser to Catholic journalists by 1935. He stayed at the friary of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Our_Lady_of_Mount_Carmel_Kinsale&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Our Lady of Mount Carmel Kinsale (page does not exist)">Our Lady of Mount Carmel Kinsale</a>, where he practiced English. That same year he traveled for a lecture tour of the United States and Canada, speaking at various institutions of his order.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On the occasion of his visit to a Carmelite seminary in <a href="/wiki/Niagara_Falls,_Ontario" title="Niagara Falls, Ontario">Niagara Falls, Ontario</a>, Brandsma wrote of the <a href="/wiki/Niagara_Falls" title="Niagara Falls">falls</a> that "I not only see the riches of the nature of the water, its immeasurable potentiality; I see God working in the work of his hands and the manifestation of his love."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_death">Imprisonment and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Titus_Brandsma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Netherlands" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of the Netherlands">invasion of the Netherlands</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Third_Reich" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Reich">Third Reich</a> in May 1940, Brandsma's long-term fight against the spread of Nazi ideology and for educational and press freedom brought him to the attention of the Nazis.
</p><p>In January 1942 he undertook to deliver by hand a letter from the <a href="/wiki/Bishops%27_Conference_of_the_Netherlands" title="Bishops&#39; Conference of the Netherlands">Conference of Dutch Bishops</a> to the editors of Catholic newspapers in which the bishops ordered them not to print official Nazi documents, as was required under a new law by the German occupiers. He had visited fourteen editors before being arrested on 19 January at the Boxmeer monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After being held prisoner in <a href="/wiki/Scheveningen" title="Scheveningen">Scheveningen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Amersfoort" title="Amersfoort">Amersfoort</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Cleves" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleves">Cleves</a>, Brandsma was transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp" title="Dachau concentration camp">Dachau concentration camp</a>, arriving there on 19 June. His health quickly gave way, and he was transferred to the camp hospital. He died on 26 July 1942, from a lethal injection administered by a nurse<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> of the <a href="/wiki/Allgemeine_SS" title="Allgemeine SS">Allgemeine SS</a>, as part of their <a href="/wiki/Nazi_human_experimentation" title="Nazi human experimentation">program of medical experimentation</a> on the prisoners.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_canonization">Veneration and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Titus_Brandsma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brandsma is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> within the Catholic Church. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in November 1985 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is observed within the Carmelite order on 27 July. 
</p><p>On 25 November 2021, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> recognized a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> attributed to the intercession of Brandsma, who "was killed in hatred of the faith", and authorized the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> to advance Brandsma's cause for sainthood.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>On 4 March 2022, a <a href="/wiki/Papal_consistory" title="Papal consistory">papal consistory</a> opened the way for his canonization<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and set the date of the canonization ceremony to 15 May 2022, together with <a href="/wiki/Charles_de_Foucauld" title="Charles de Foucauld">Charles de Foucauld</a> and <a href="/wiki/List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Francis" title="List of saints canonized by Pope Francis">eight others</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On Sunday, 15 May 2022, in front of more than 50,000 people from around the world, Pope Francis canonized Brandsma<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and nine other saints at a Mass in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p><p>"It is good to see that, through their evangelical witness, these Saints have fostered the spiritual and social growth of their respective nations and also of the entire human family", the pope said during the Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Titus_Brandsma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 2005, Brandsma was chosen by the inhabitants of <a href="/wiki/Nijmegen" title="Nijmegen">Nijmegen</a> as the greatest citizen to have lived there. A memorial church dedicated to him now stands in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brandsma's studies on mysticism was the basis for the establishment in 1968 of the Titus Brandsma Institute in Nijmegen, dedicated to the study of <a href="/wiki/Spirituality" title="Spirituality">spirituality</a>. It is a collaboration between the Dutch Carmelite friars and <a href="/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen" title="Radboud University Nijmegen">Radboud University Nijmegen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his biography of Brandsma, <i>The Man behind the Myth</i>, Dutch journalist Ton Crijnen claims that Brandsma's character consisted of some vanity, a short temper, extreme energy, political innocence, true charity, unpretentious piety, thorough decisiveness, and great personal courage. His ideas were very much those of his own age and modern as well. He offset contemporary Catholicism's negative theological opinion about Judaism with a strong disaffection for any kind of <a href="/wiki/Antisemitism" title="Antisemitism">antisemitism</a> in Hitler's Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brandsma was honoured by the city of Dachau with a street adjoining the former camp, albeit one of the narrowest streets in the town.
</p><p>Brandsma participated in the international <a href="/wiki/Esperanto" title="Esperanto">Esperanto</a> movement<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> and is considered a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/International_Union_of_Catholic_Esperantists" title="International Union of Catholic Esperantists">International Union of Catholic Esperantists</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
