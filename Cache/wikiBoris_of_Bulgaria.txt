<p><b>Knyaz Boris I</b> (also <i>Bogoris</i>), venerated as <b>Saint Tsar Boris I (Mihail) the Baptizer</b>  (<a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Church Slavonic language">Church Slavonic</a>: <span lang="cu">Борисъ А҃  / Борисъ-Михаилъ</span> <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_language" title="Bulgarian language">Bulgarian</a>: <span lang="bg">Борис I / Борис-Михаил</span>; died 2 May 907), was the ruler of the <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">First Bulgarian Empire</a> in 852&#8211;889. At the time of his <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> in 864, Boris was named Michael after his godfather, Emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_III" title="Michael III">Michael III</a>. The historian <a href="/wiki/Steven_Runciman" title="Steven Runciman">Steven Runciman</a> called him one of the greatest persons in history.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite a number of military setbacks, the reign of Boris I was marked with significant events that shaped <a href="/wiki/History_of_Bulgaria" title="History of Bulgaria">Bulgarian</a> and European history. With the <a href="/wiki/Christianization_of_Bulgaria" title="Christianization of Bulgaria">Christianization of Bulgaria</a> in 864 paganism (i.e. <a href="/wiki/Tengrism" title="Tengrism">Tengrism</a>) was abolished. A skillful diplomat, Boris I successfully exploited the conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Papacy</a> to secure an <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Bulgarian Orthodox Church"> autocephalous Bulgarian Church</a>, thus dealing with the nobility's concerns about Byzantine interference in Bulgaria's internal affairs.
</p><p>When in 885 the disciples of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints Cyril and Methodius">Saints Cyril and Methodius</a> were banished from <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a>, Boris I gave them refuge and provided assistance which saved the <a href="/wiki/Glagolithic" class="mw-redirect" title="Glagolithic">Glagolithic</a> and later promoted the development of the <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic script</a> in Preslav and the Slavic literature. After he abdicated in 889, his eldest son and successor tried to restore the old pagan religion but was deposed by Boris I. During the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Preslav" title="Council of Preslav">Council of Preslav</a> which followed that event, the Byzantine clergy was replaced with <a href="/wiki/Bulgarians" title="Bulgarians">Bulgarians</a>, and the Greek language was replaced with what is now known as <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Old Church Slavonic</a>.
</p><p>He is regarded as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>, as the Prince and baptizer of Bulgaria, and as <a href="/wiki/Equal-to-the-Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Equal-to-the-Apostles">Equal-to-the-Apostles</a>, with his feast day observed on <a href="/wiki/May_2_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="May 2 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">May 2</a> and in Synaxis of all venerable and holy Fathers of Bulgaria (movable holiday on the 2nd Sunday of Pentecost).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name_and_titles">Name and titles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Name and titles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Boris-Mihail._Knyaz,_852-889.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Boris-Mihail._Knyaz%2C_852-889.jpg/300px-Boris-Mihail._Knyaz%2C_852-889.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="151" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Boris-Mihail._Knyaz%2C_852-889.jpg/450px-Boris-Mihail._Knyaz%2C_852-889.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Boris-Mihail._Knyaz%2C_852-889.jpg/600px-Boris-Mihail._Knyaz%2C_852-889.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="403" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Boris-Mihail._Knyaz,_852-889.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Coin of Boris-Mihail. <a href="/wiki/Knyaz" title="Knyaz">Knyaz</a>, struck in 852–889.</div></div></div>
<p>The most common theory is that the <a href="/wiki/Boris_(given_name)" title="Boris (given name)">name Boris</a> is of <a href="/wiki/Bulgar_language" title="Bulgar language">Bulgar</a> origin.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> After his official act of conversion to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, Boris adopted the Christian name <a href="/wiki/Michael_(given_name)" title="Michael (given name)">Michael</a>. He is sometimes called Boris-Michael in historical research.
</p><p>The only direct evidence of Boris's title are his seals and <a href="/wiki/Ballshi_inscription" title="Ballshi inscription">the inscription</a> found near the town of <a href="/wiki/Ballsh" title="Ballsh">Ballsh</a>, modern <a href="/wiki/Albania" title="Albania">Albania</a>, and at <a href="/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria" title="Varna, Bulgaria">Varna</a>. There he is called by the Byzantine title "<a href="/wiki/Archon" title="Archon">Archon</a> of Bulgaria", which is usually translated as "ruler", and in the 10-11th centuries also as "Knyaz" (Кнѧзъ, Bulg.).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In the Bulgarian sources from that period, Boris I is called "Knyaz" or "Tsar" ("Царь", Bulg.), and during the <a href="/wiki/Second_Bulgarian_Empire" title="Second Bulgarian Empire">Second Bulgarian Empire</a>, "Tsar".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In modern historiography Boris is called by different titles. Most historians accept that he changed his title after his conversion to Christianity. According to them, before the baptism he had the title <a href="/wiki/Khan_(title)" title="Khan (title)">Khan</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> or <a href="/wiki/Kanasubigi" title="Kanasubigi">Kanasubigi</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and after that <a href="/wiki/Knyaz" title="Knyaz">Knyaz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reign">Reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Central_Europe_in_the_9th_century">Central Europe in the 9th century</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Central Europe in the 9th century">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The early 9th century marked the beginning of a fierce rivalry between the <a href="/wiki/Greek_East_and_Latin_West" title="Greek East and Latin West">Greek East and Latin West</a>, which would ultimately lead to the <a href="/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism" title="East–West Schism">schism</a> between the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p><p>As early as 781, the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empress" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Empress">Empress</a> <a href="/wiki/Irene_of_Athens" title="Irene of Athens">Irene</a> began to seek a closer relationship with the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty" title="Carolingian dynasty">Carolingian dynasty</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">Papacy</a>. She negotiated a marriage between her son, <a href="/wiki/Constantine_VI" title="Constantine VI">Constantine</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Rotrude" title="Rotrude">Rotrude</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> by his third wife <a href="/wiki/Hildegard,_wife_of_Charlemagne" class="mw-redirect" title="Hildegard, wife of Charlemagne">Hildegard</a>. Irene went as far as to send an official to instruct the Frankish princess in Greek; however, Irene herself broke off the engagement in 787, against her son's wishes. When the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Nicaea" title="Second Council of Nicaea">Second Council of Nicaea</a> of 787 reintroduced the <a href="/wiki/Veneration_of_icons" class="mw-redirect" title="Veneration of icons">veneration of icons</a> under Empress Irene, the result was not recognized by Charlemagne since no Frankish emissaries had been invited even though Charlemagne was by then ruling more than three provinces of the old Roman empire. While this improved relations with the Papacy, it did not prevent the outbreak of a war with the Franks, who took over <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento" title="Duchy of Benevento">Benevento</a> in 788.
</p><p>When Charlemagne was proclaimed Emperor of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_III" title="Pope Leo III">Leo III</a>, the Pope was effectively nullifying the legitimacy of Irene. He certainly desired to increase the influence of the papacy and to honour his protector Charlemagne. Irene, like many of her predecessors since <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a>, was too weak to protect Rome and its much reduced citizenry and the city was not being ruled by any emperor. Thus, Charlemagne's assumption of the imperial title was not seen as an usurpation in the eyes of the Franks or Italians. It was, however, seen as such in Byzantium, but protests by Irene and her successor <a href="/wiki/Nicephorus_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicephorus I">Nicephorus I</a> had no great effect.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Mojm%C3%ADr_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Mojmír I">Mojmír I</a> managed to unite some Slavic princes and established <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a> in 833. His successor, <a href="/wiki/Rastislav_of_Moravia" title="Rastislav of Moravia">Rastislav</a>, also fought against the Germans.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Both states tried to maintain good relations with Bulgaria on account of its considerable military power.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_campaigns">Military campaigns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Military campaigns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:414px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Balkans850.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Balkans850.png/412px-Balkans850.png" decoding="async" width="412" height="339" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Balkans850.png/618px-Balkans850.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2d/Balkans850.png 2x" data-file-width="628" data-file-height="517" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Balkans850.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bulgaria under rule of Boris I</div></div></div>
<p>Boris I was the son and successor of <a href="/wiki/Presian" class="mw-redirect" title="Presian">Presian I</a> of Bulgaria. In 852 he sent emissaries to <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Francia" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Francia">Eastern Francia</a> to confirm the peace treaty of 845.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> At the time of his accession he threatened the Byzantines with an invasion, but his armies did not attack,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and he received a small area in <a href="/wiki/Strandzha" title="Strandzha">Strandzha</a> to the southeast.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The peace treaty was not signed, however, although both states exchanged temporary delegations.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In 854 the Moravian Prince Rastislav persuaded Boris I to help him against East Francia. According to some sources, some Franks bribed the Bulgarian monarch to attack <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The Bulgarian-Slav campaign was a disaster, and Louis scored a great victory and invaded Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> At the same time the <a href="/wiki/Croato-Bulgarian_Wars" class="mw-redirect" title="Croato-Bulgarian Wars">Croats</a> waged <a href="/wiki/Croatian%E2%80%93Bulgarian_battle_of_854" title="Croatian–Bulgarian battle of 854">a war</a> against the Bulgarians. Both peoples had coexisted peacefully up to that time, suggesting that the Croats were paid by Louis to attack Bulgaria and distract Boris' attention from his alliance with Great Moravia.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Kanasubigi" title="Kanasubigi">Kanasubigi</a> Boris could not achieve any success, and both sides exchanged gifts and settled for peace.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> As a result of the military actions in 855, the peace between Bulgaria and Eastern Francia was restored, and Rastislav was forced to fight against Louis alone. In the meantime, a conflict between the Byzantines and Bulgarians had started in 855–856, and Boris, distracted by his conflict with Louis, lost Philippopolis (<a href="/wiki/Plovdiv" title="Plovdiv">Plovdiv</a>), the region of <a href="/wiki/Zagore_(region)" title="Zagore (region)">Zagora</a>, and the ports around the <a href="/wiki/Gulf_of_Burgas" title="Gulf of Burgas">Gulf of Burgas</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Black_Sea" title="Black Sea">Black Sea</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_army" title="Byzantine army">Byzantine army</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Michael_III" title="Michael III">Michael III</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Caesar_(title)" title="Caesar (title)">caesar</a> <a href="/wiki/Bardas" title="Bardas">Bardas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Serbia">Serbia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Serbia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Bulgar%E2%80%93Serb_War_(853)" title="Bulgar–Serb War (853)">Bulgar–Serb War (853)</a></div>
<p>After the death of Knez <a href="/wiki/Vlastimir_of_Serbia" class="mw-redirect" title="Vlastimir of Serbia">Vlastimir of Serbia</a> circa 850, his state was divided between his sons. Vlastimir and Boris' father had fought against each other in the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian%E2%80%93Serbian_Wars_(medieval)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bulgarian–Serbian Wars (medieval)">Bulgarian-Serbian War of 839–842</a>, which resulted in a Serbian victory, and Boris sought to avenge that defeat. In 853 or 854, the Bulgarian army led by <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_of_Bulgaria" title="Vladimir of Bulgaria">Vladimir-Rasate</a>, the son of Boris I, invaded Serbia, with the aim of replacing the Byzantine overlordship over the Serbs. The Serbian army was led by <a href="/wiki/Mutimir_of_Serbia" title="Mutimir of Serbia">Mutimir</a> and his two brothers; they defeated the Bulgarians, capturing Vladimir and 12 <a href="/wiki/Boyars" class="mw-redirect" title="Boyars">boyars</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB141_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB141-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Boris I and Mutimir agreed to peace (and perhaps an alliance<sup id="cite_ref-EB141_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB141-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>), and Mutimir sent his sons <a href="/wiki/Pribislav_of_Serbia" title="Pribislav of Serbia">Pribislav</a> and <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Mutimirovi%C4%87" title="Stefan Mutimirović">Stefan</a> to the border to escort the prisoners, where they exchanged items as a sign of peace. Boris himself gave them "rich gifts", while he was given "two <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">slaves</a>, two <a href="/wiki/Falcon" title="Falcon">falcons</a>, two <a href="/wiki/Dog" title="Dog">dogs</a>, and 80 <a href="/wiki/Fur" title="Fur">furs</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> An internal conflict among the Serbian brothers resulted in Mutimir banishing the two younger brothers to the Bulgarian court.<sup id="cite_ref-EB141_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB141-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-S15_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-S15-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Mutimir, however, kept a nephew, <a href="/wiki/Petar_of_Serbia" title="Petar of Serbia">Petar</a>, at his court for political reasons.<sup id="cite_ref-WMPG_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WMPG-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The reason for the feud is not known, though it is postulated that it was a result of treachery.<sup id="cite_ref-WMPG_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WMPG-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> Petar would later defeat <a href="/wiki/Pribislav_of_Serbia" title="Pribislav of Serbia">Pribislav</a>, Mutimir's son, and take the Serbian throne.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Motivations_for_baptism_and_conversion_to_Christianity">Motivations for baptism and conversion to Christianity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Motivations for baptism and conversion to Christianity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg/250px-BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="122" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg/375px-BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg/500px-BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1110" data-file-height="541" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:BorisIMichaelBaptism.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Depiction in the <a href="/wiki/Madrid_Skylitzes" title="Madrid Skylitzes">Madrid Skylitzes</a> of Boris I's baptism.</div></div></div>
<p>There are a number of versions as to why Boris converted to Christianity. Some historians attribute it to the intervention of <a href="/wiki/Anna,_daughter_of_Presian_I" title="Anna, daughter of Presian I">his sister</a> who had already converted while being at Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson,_1999,_p.80_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson,_1999,_p.80-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>  Another story mentions a Greek slave in the ruler's court.<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson,_1999,_p.80_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson,_1999,_p.80-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>  A more mythological version is the one in which Boris is astonished and frightened by an icon of <a href="/wiki/Judgement_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Judgement day">Judgement day</a> and thus decides to adopt Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson,_1999,_p.80_31-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson,_1999,_p.80-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Richard B. Spence sees the decision as deliberate, practical, and politic.<sup id="cite_ref-Spence_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spence-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For a variety of diplomatic reasons, Boris became interested in converting to Christianity. In order to both extend his control over the Slavic world and gain an ally against one of the most powerful foes of the <a href="/wiki/Bulgars" title="Bulgars">Bulgars</a>, the Byzantine Empire, Boris sought to establish an alliance with <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a> against Ratislav of Moravia.<sup id="cite_ref-Sullivan_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sullivan-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> Through this alliance, Louis promised to supply Boris with missionaries, which would have effectively brought the Bulgars under the Roman Church.<sup id="cite_ref-Barford_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barford-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>  However, late in 863, the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> under Emperor Michael III declared war on Boris and the Bulgars during a period of famine and natural disasters.  Taken by surprise, Boris was forced to make peace with the Byzantines, promising to convert to Christianity according to the eastern rites, in exchange for peace and territorial concessions in <a href="/wiki/Thrace" title="Thrace">Thrace</a> (he regained the region of Zagora recently recovered by the Byzantines).<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>  At the beginning of 864, Boris was secretly baptized at <a href="/wiki/Pliska" title="Pliska">Pliska</a> by an embassy of Byzantine clergymen, together with his family and select members of the Bulgarian nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>  With Emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_III" title="Michael III">Michael III</a> as his godfather, Boris also adopted the Christian name Michael.<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson,_1999,_p.80_31-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson,_1999,_p.80-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Separate from diplomatic concerns, Boris was interested in converting himself and the Bulgarians to Christianity to resolve the disunity within the Bulgarian society. When he ascended to the throne, the Bulgars and Slavs were separate elements within Boris' kingdom, the minority Bulgars constituting a military aristocracy. Richard Spence compares it to the relationship between the Normans and Saxons in England.<sup id="cite_ref-Spence_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spence-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Religious plurality further contributed to divisions within the society. The Slavs had their own <a href="/wiki/Slavic_mythology" class="mw-redirect" title="Slavic mythology">polytheistic belief system</a> while the Bulgar elite believed in <a href="/wiki/Tengri" title="Tengri">Tangra</a>, the Sky God, or God of Heaven.
The arrival of Methodius and his followers introduced the Cyrillic alphabet, freeing the Bulgarians from dependence on Greek as a written and liturgical language. A Slavic <a href="/wiki/Christian_culture" title="Christian culture">Christian culture</a> developed that helped unify the realm.<sup id="cite_ref-Spence_32-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spence-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Baptism_of_the_Bulgarians_and_the_establishment_of_the_Bulgarian_Church">Baptism of the Bulgarians and the establishment of the Bulgarian Church</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Baptism of the Bulgarians and the establishment of the Bulgarian Church">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Christianization_of_Bulgaria" title="Christianization of Bulgaria">Christianization of Bulgaria</a></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:57-manasses-chronicle.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/57-manasses-chronicle.jpg/250px-57-manasses-chronicle.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="172" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/57-manasses-chronicle.jpg/375px-57-manasses-chronicle.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/57-manasses-chronicle.jpg/500px-57-manasses-chronicle.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1774" data-file-height="1224" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:57-manasses-chronicle.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Depiction in the Manases Chronicle of Boris I' baptism.</div></div></div>
<p>After his baptism, the first major task that Boris undertook was the baptism of his subjects and for this task he appealed to Byzantine priests between 864 and 866.<sup id="cite_ref-Antonova_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Antonova-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> At the same time Boris sought further instruction on how to lead a Christian lifestyle and society and how to set up an <a href="/wiki/Autocephalous" class="mw-redirect" title="Autocephalous">autocephalous</a> church from the Byzantine Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Photius_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Photius I of Constantinople">Photios</a>.  Photios' answer proved less than satisfactory, and Boris sought to gain a more favorable settlement from the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Papacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>  Boris dispatched emissaries led by the <a href="/wiki/Kavhan" class="mw-redirect" title="Kavhan">kavhan</a> <a href="/wiki/Peter_(diplomat)" title="Peter (diplomat)">Peter</a> with a long list of questions to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> at Rome in August 866, and obtained 106 detailed answers, detailing the essence of <a href="/wiki/Religion" title="Religion">religion</a>, <a href="/wiki/Law" title="Law">law</a>, <a href="/wiki/Politics" title="Politics">politics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Convention_(norm)" title="Convention (norm)">customs</a> and personal <a href="/wiki/Faith" title="Faith">faith</a>.  Stemming from his concerns with the baptism of the Bulgarians, Boris also complained to Nicholas about the abuses perpetrated by the Byzantine priests responsible for baptizing the Bulgarians and how he could go about correcting the consequences resulting from these abuses. The pope temporarily glossed over the controversial question of the autocephalous status desired by Boris for his church and sent a large group of missionaries to continue the conversion of Bulgaria in accordance with the western rite.<sup id="cite_ref-Norwich_2011_74_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norwich_2011_74-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Duffy,_2006,_p._103_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy,_2006,_p._103-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Bulgaria's shift towards the Papacy infuriated Patriarch Photios, who wrote an encyclical to the eastern clergy in 867 in which he denounced the practices associated with the western rite and Rome's ecclesiastical intervention in Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy,_2006,_p._103_40-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy,_2006,_p._103-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> This occasioned the <a href="/wiki/Photian_Schism" class="mw-redirect" title="Photian Schism">Photian Schism</a>, which was a major step in the rift between the eastern and western churches.
</p><p>To deliver his response to Boris’ questions, Pope Nicholas I sent two bishops to Bulgaria: Paul of Populonia and Formosus of Porto. The Pope expected that these priests would execute their episcopal responsibilities to address Boris’ concerns, but did not intend for them to be elevated to the positions that they assumed in the Bulgar hierarchy. In Bulgaria, the activities of Bishop Formosus (later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Pope Formosus</a>) met with success, until the pope rejected Boris' request to nominate Formosus as archbishop of Bulgaria. Nicholas justified the rejection of the request by arguing that it was “uncanonical to transfer an already established bishop from one see to another”. The new <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_II" title="Pope Adrian II">Pope Adrian II</a> refused Boris' request for a similar nomination of either Formosus or Deacon Marinus (later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Marinus_I" title="Pope Marinus I">Pope Marinus I</a>), after which Bulgaria began to shift towards Constantinople once again.  At the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Council_of_Constantinople_(Roman_Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourth Council of Constantinople (Roman Catholic)">Fourth Council of Constantinople</a> in 870 the position of the Bulgarian church was reopened by Bulgarian envoys, and the eastern patriarchs adjudicated in favor of Constantinople.  This determined the future of the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Bulgarian Orthodox Church</a>, which was granted the status of an autocephalous archbishopric by the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Constantinople">Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> and an archbishop of its own. Later in the 870s, the Patriarch of Constantinople surrendered Bulgaria to the Papacy, but this concession was purely nominal, as it did not affect the actual position of Bulgaria's autocephalous church.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Christianization of the Bulgarians as a result of Boris’ actions had profound effects not only on the religious belief system of the Bulgarians but also the structure of the Bulgarian government. Upon embracing Christianity, Boris took on the title of Tsar and joined the community of nations that embraced Christ, to the great delight of the Eastern Roman Empire, which was later given the name Byzantium by historians.<sup id="cite_ref-Norwich_2011_74_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norwich_2011_74-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Toward the end of his reign, Boris began to increase the number of native Bulgarian clergy. Consequently, Boris began to send Bulgarians to Constantinople to obtain a monastic education and some of these Bulgarians returned to their homeland to serve as clergymen.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2013)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> In 885, Boris was presented with a new opportunity to establish a native clergy when Slavic-speaking disciples of St. Cyril and St. Methodius were forced to flee from Moravia after a German-inspired reaction to the death of the apostle.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2013)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Changes_to_Bulgarian_culture_brought_on_by_Clement_and_Naum">Changes to Bulgarian culture brought on by Clement and Naum</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Changes to Bulgarian culture brought on by Clement and Naum">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Borismeetingdisciples.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Borismeetingdisciples.JPG/250px-Borismeetingdisciples.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="175" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Borismeetingdisciples.JPG/375px-Borismeetingdisciples.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Borismeetingdisciples.JPG/500px-Borismeetingdisciples.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1533" data-file-height="1074" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Borismeetingdisciples.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Knyaz Boris I meeting the disciples of Saints Cyril and Methodius</div></div></div>
<p>In 886 Boris' governor of <a href="/wiki/Belgrade" title="Belgrade">Belgrade</a> welcomed the disciples of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cyril_and_Saint_Methodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cyril and Saint Methodius">Saint Cyril and Saint Methodius</a>, who were exiled from <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a> into Bulgaria and sent them on to Boris in <a href="/wiki/Pliska" title="Pliska">Pliska</a>. Boris happily greeted two of these disciples, <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Ohrid" title="Clement of Ohrid">Clement of Ohrid</a> and <a href="/wiki/Naum_of_Preslav" class="mw-redirect" title="Naum of Preslav">Naum of Preslav</a>, who were of noble Bulgarian Slavic origin. To utilize the disciple's talents, Boris commissioned Clement to be a “teacher” in the province of <a href="/wiki/Kutmichevitsa" title="Kutmichevitsa">Kutmichevitsa</a>.
</p><p>Both Clement and Naum were instrumental in furthering the cultural, linguistic and spiritual works of Cyril and Methodius.<sup id="cite_ref-Civita_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Civita-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> They set up educational centers in <a href="/wiki/Preslav_Literary_School" title="Preslav Literary School">Pliska</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Ohrid_Literary_School" title="Ohrid Literary School">Ohrid</a> to further the development of Slavonic letters and <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a>. Clement later trained thousands of Slavonic-speaking priests who replaced the Greek-speaking clergy from Constantinople still present in Bulgaria. The script that was originally developed by Cyril and Methodius is known as the <a href="/wiki/Glagolitic_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Glagolitic alphabet">Glagolitic alphabet</a>.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Ohrid" title="Clement of Ohrid">Clement of Ohrid</a> and <a href="/wiki/Naum_of_Preslav" class="mw-redirect" title="Naum of Preslav">Naum of Preslav</a> created (or rather compiled) the new script which was called <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic</a> and was declared the official alphabet in Bulgaria in 893. <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Old Bulgarian</a> was declared as the official language in the same year. In the following centuries this script was adopted by other <a href="/wiki/Slavic_peoples" class="mw-redirect" title="Slavic peoples">Slavic peoples</a> and states. The introduction of Slavic liturgy paralleled Boris' continued development of churches and monasteries throughout his realm.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Reactions_to_religious_conversion">Reactions to religious conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Reactions to religious conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Conversion to Christianity met great opposition among the Bulgarian elite. Some refused to become Christians while others apostatized after baptism and started a rebellion against Boris for forcing them to be baptized. Some people did not object necessarily to the Christian religion but to the fact that it was brought by foreign priests, which, as a result, established external foreign policy. By breaking the power of the old cults, Boris reduced the influence of the boyars, who resisted the khan's authority.<sup id="cite_ref-Spence_32-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spence-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> In the summer of 865 a group of Bulgar aristocrats (boyars) started an open revolt.<sup id="cite_ref-Anderson,_1999,_p.80_31-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anderson,_1999,_p.80-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Boris ruthlessly suppressed it and executed 52 boyars together with their entire families.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> Thus the Christianization continued.
</p>
<h2><span id="End_of_Boris.27_reign"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="End_of_Boris'_reign">End of Boris' reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: End of Boris&#039; reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 889 Boris abdicated the throne and became a monk. His son and successor <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_of_Bulgaria" title="Vladimir of Bulgaria">Vladimir</a> attempted a pagan reaction, which brought Boris out of retirement in 893. Vladimir was defeated and Boris had him blinded, his wife shaved and sent to a monastery. Boris gathered the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Preslav" title="Council of Preslav">Council of Preslav</a> placing his third son, Tsar <a href="/wiki/Simeon_I_of_Bulgaria" title="Simeon I of Bulgaria">Simeon I of Bulgaria</a>, on the throne, threatening him with the same fate if he too apostatized. Boris returned to his monastery, emerging once again in c. 895 to help Simeon fight the <a href="/wiki/Magyars" class="mw-redirect" title="Magyars">Magyars</a>, who had invaded Bulgaria in alliance with the Byzantines. After the passing of this crisis, Boris resumed monastic life and died in 907. The location of his retreat, where perhaps he was interred, is not certain; it may be near Preslav, or Pliska, or in a monastery near <a href="/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria" title="Varna, Bulgaria">Varna</a> or Ravda.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Boris_I_of_Bulgaria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/St._Boris_Peak" title="St. Boris Peak">St. Boris Peak</a> on <a href="/wiki/Livingston_Island" title="Livingston Island">Livingston Island</a> in the <a href="/wiki/South_Shetland_Islands" title="South Shetland Islands">South Shetland Islands</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antarctica" title="Antarctica">Antarctica</a> is named for Boris I of Bulgaria.
</p><p>Boris I's life is featured in the <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0344952">1985 film "Boris I"</a> (Борис Първи), with <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Danailov" title="Stefan Danailov">Stefan Danailov</a> in the title role.
</p>
