					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Turketil&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Turketil">Turketil</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Abbot of Crowland and perhaps also of Bedford Abbey</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG/120px-Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG" decoding="async" width="120" height="160" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG/180px-Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG/240px-Croyland_Abbey_%26_Parish_Church_of_Crowland.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1944" data-file-height="2592" /></a><figcaption>Croyland Abbey.</figcaption></figure><p><b>Thurcytel</b> (or <b>Thurkytel</b>) (died 28 June 975?) was <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Crowland" title="Abbot of Crowland">abbot of Crowland</a> and perhaps also of <a href="/wiki/Bedford_Abbey" title="Bedford Abbey">Bedford Abbey</a>.
</p><p>Thurcytel of Crowland is known from the unreliable history of <a href="/wiki/Crowland_Abbey" title="Crowland Abbey">Crowland Abbey</a> attributed to <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Ingulf" title="Pseudo-Ingulf">Pseudo-Ingulf</a>, an account full of <a href="/wiki/Anachronism" title="Anachronism">anachronisms</a> including the claim that Thurcytel was <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chancellor of England">Lord Chancellor of England</a>. The gist of this account is that Thurcytel is a kinsman and servitor of several <a href="/wiki/Kings_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Kings of England">Kings of England</a>, from <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Elder" title="Edward the Elder">Edward the Elder</a> onwards, and fights at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Brunanburh" title="Battle of Brunanburh">battle of Brunanburh</a>. He retires from secular life in the reign of King <a href="/wiki/Eadred_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Eadred of England">Eadred</a> to become abbot of Crowland, which he has refounded and endowed with lands and treasures in 948. Pseudo-Ingulf's account indicates that Thuryctel died on 28 June 975.
</p><p>Thurcytel of Crowland has long been identified with the Thurcytel who was abbot of Bedford Abbey at about the same time. Lewis, however, notes that "the case is not clear-cut". This Thurcytel was a kinsman of <a href="/wiki/Oscytel" title="Oscytel">Oscytel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a>, and, thus, also of Oscytel's successor <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Worcester" title="Oswald of Worcester">Oswald of Worcester</a>. The <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i>  records that Archbishop Oscytel was buried at Bedford Abbey by Thurcytel in 971. Thurcytel of Bedford was said by some late sources to have been expelled and to have joined the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a> of <a href="/wiki/Old_St_Paul%27s_Cathedral" title="Old St Paul&#39;s Cathedral">St Paul's</a> in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> where he had once been a priest.
</p>
