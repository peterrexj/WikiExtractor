					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Modestus_(Apostle_of_Carinthia)&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Modestus (Apostle of Carinthia)">Modestus (Apostle of Carinthia)</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:203px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Modestus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Modestus.jpg" decoding="async" width="201" height="342" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="201" data-file-height="342" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Modestus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Window depicting Modestus in the church of <a href="/wiki/Liesing" title="Liesing">Liesing</a>, Austria</div></div></div>
<p><b>Modestus</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 720 – before 772),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> called the <b>Apostle of Carinthia</b> or <b>Apostle of Carantania</b>, was most probably an <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland" title="Gaelic Ireland">Irish</a> monk and the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_mission" title="Anglo-Saxon mission">evangeliser</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Carantanians" title="Carantanians">Carantanians</a>, an <a href="/wiki/Slavic_settlement_of_the_Eastern_Alps" title="Slavic settlement of the Eastern Alps">Alpine Slavic</a> people settling in the south of present-day <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> and north-eastern <a href="/wiki/Slovenia" title="Slovenia">Slovenia</a>, who were among the ancestors of present-day <a href="/wiki/Slovenes" title="Slovenes">Slovenes</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Modestus_(Apostle_of_Carantania)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Modestus was an Irishman by birth, a disciple of St. <a href="/wiki/Vergilius_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Vergilius of Salzburg">Fergil</a>. He may have come to the <a href="/wiki/History_of_Bavaria" title="History of Bavaria">Bavarian</a> lands under Duke <a href="/wiki/Odilo,_Duke_of_Bavaria" title="Odilo, Duke of Bavaria">Odilo</a> in the wake of Fergil (Vergilius), who, about 767 was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Salzburg">Bishop of Salzburg</a>.
</p><p>Upon the request of Prince Cheitmar or Hotimir<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> of <a href="/wiki/Carantania" title="Carantania">Carantania</a> to <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianize</a> his people, Bishop Vergilius dispatched Modestus around the year 755, together with four priests and a deacon "and other inferior clerks"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> as a missionary with the rank of a <i>chorepískopos</i> (<a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Χωρεπίσκοπος</span>), i.e. a <a href="/wiki/Chorbishop" title="Chorbishop">chorbishop</a> responsible for the people in the countryside.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBateson1894_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBateson1894-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Modestus received authority as a bishop, but probably, after the Irish custom, was without a definite see. It is only in the late anonymous life of <a href="/wiki/Gebhard_of_Constance" title="Gebhard of Constance">Gebhard of Constance</a>, that he is called bishop of Liburnia.
</p><p>Cheitmar's predecessor Borut had accepted Bavarian overlordship about 740 and Modestus' missionary work in Carantania was meant to stabilise the country against the invading <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Avars" title="Pannonian Avars">Avars</a>. It was described  in the <i><a href="/wiki/Conversio_Bagoariorum_et_Carantanorum" title="Conversio Bagoariorum et Carantanorum">Conversio Bagoariorum et Carantanorum</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> written around 870 as a memorandum of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Salzburg">Salzburg archbishop</a> Adalwin in a court hearing before the <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a> against Bishop <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints Cyril and Methodius">Methodius</a>, the apostle of the Slavs in the Frankish <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Slavs#Principality" class="mw-redirect" title="Pannonian Slavs">Principality of Lower Pannonia</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a>. In the document, the Archdiocese of Salzburg emphasised the achievements of Modestus as an argument of their merits in converting the Slavs.
</p><p>According to the chronicles, he built three Christian churches: 
</p>
<ul><li>"ad Undrimas" (probably at Ingering in the area of present-day <a href="/wiki/Gaal,_Styria" title="Gaal, Styria">Gaal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spielberg,_Styria" title="Spielberg, Styria">Spielberg</a> in <a href="/wiki/Upper_Styria" title="Upper Styria">Upper Styria</a>), spelt by the manuscripts in a variety of ways, believed to be a valley situated between S. Vitus and Maria-Saal;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBateson1894_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBateson1894-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>at "Liburnia civitate", corresponding to the former <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> episcopal see of <a href="/wiki/Teurnia" title="Teurnia">Teurnia</a> (today's Sankt Peter im Holz near <a href="/wiki/Spittal_an_der_Drau" title="Spittal an der Drau">Spittal an der Drau</a> in <a href="/wiki/Carinthia_(state)" class="mw-redirect" title="Carinthia (state)">Carinthia</a>), the site of which is probably now marked by a field called Lurnfeld;</li>
<li>and "ecclesiam Sanctae Mariae", a church of the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a> in an unnamed place, most probably located near the centre of the Slav principality at Karnburg (<a href="/wiki/Slovene_language" title="Slovene language">Slovene</a>: <i lang="sl">Krnski grad</i>), which would make it <a href="/wiki/Maria_Saal" title="Maria Saal">Maria Saal</a> (<i>Gospa sveta</i>) on the Carinthian <a href="/wiki/Zollfeld" title="Zollfeld">Zollfeld</a> plain.</li></ul>
<p>His church was thus in the immediate vicinity of the area that has served as a political and cultural centre of the region through the ages, close to:
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg/220px-Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg/330px-Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/10/Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg/440px-Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1984" data-file-height="1488" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Zollfeld_Blick_von_Karnburg_nach_Maria_Saal.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>View from Karnburg to Maria Saal</div></div></div>
<ul><li>the <a href="/wiki/Magdalensberg" title="Magdalensberg">Magdalensberg</a> mountain where a large settlement dating from  the Celtic <a href="/wiki/Noricum" title="Noricum">Noricum</a> kingdom is being excavated;</li>
<li>the remains of Roman <a href="/wiki/Virunum" title="Virunum">Virunum</a>, capital city of the later Roman province of Noricum, at the foot of Magdalensberg;</li>
<li>the <a href="/wiki/Karnburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Karnburg">Karnburg</a> complex which served as the political centre of the Slav principality of Carantania, with the <a href="/wiki/Prince%27s_Stone" title="Prince&#39;s Stone">Prince's Stone</a> (<a href="/wiki/Slovene_language" title="Slovene language">Slovene</a>: <i lang="sl">Knežji kamen</i>) nearby;</li>
<li>the <i><a href="/wiki/Kaiserpfalz" title="Kaiserpfalz">Kaiserpfalz</a></i> of Karnburg, the 9th century <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> seat palatine of the Duke, King and Emperor <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Carinthia" title="Arnulf of Carinthia">Arnulf of Carinthia</a>;</li>
<li>the <a href="/wiki/Duke%27s_Chair" title="Duke&#39;s Chair">Duke's Chair</a>, symbol of the legal authority in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Carinthia" title="Duchy of Carinthia">Duchy of Carinthia</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a>;</li>
<li>the medieval ducal capital of <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Veit_an_der_Glan" title="Sankt Veit an der Glan">Sankt Veit</a>;</li>
<li>the modern capital of the State of Carinthia, <a href="/wiki/Klagenfurt" title="Klagenfurt">Klagenfurt</a>.</li></ul>
<p>Modestus is said to have baptised <a href="/wiki/Domitian_of_Huy" title="Domitian of Huy">St. Domitian</a>, a Carinthian prince, at Milstadt, which may, perhaps, be identical with Adandrinas.
</p><p>According to the older manuscripts, Modestus stayed in Carinthia till his death; one late manuscript says he died in France.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBateson1894_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBateson1894-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The most likely year of his death was 763, although other dates also appear in sources. No traces of his church of St. Mary have been discovered.
</p><p>Dempster calls him a companion of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a>, and Ferrarius says he is mentioned in Boniface's life. It is probable that in both cases St. Fergil was meant.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBateson1894_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBateson1894-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A manuscript by him, 'ad ecclesiam suam,' was said to be at Salzburg, and a volume of his letters at Strasburg. Neither manuscript can now be traced.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBateson1894_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBateson1894-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Modestus_(Apostle_of_Carantania)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His alleged tomb is shown in the present Gothic church of Maria Saal, which was built six centuries later, replacing an earlier <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a> church probably from the 12th century.
</p><p>Due to his success in converting the <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> Carantanian Slavs to Christianity, Modestus was honoured by the popular denomination "Apostle of Carinthia". His feast is celebrated on 5 December.
</p>
