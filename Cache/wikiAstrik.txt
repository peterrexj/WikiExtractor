<p><b>Saint Astrik of Pannonhalma</b> (also known as <b>Anastasius, Astericus, Ascrick, Astrissicus</b>) (died c. 1030/1040) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the 11th century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Astrik&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Radla was a Czech or Croat from Bohemia, who was a monk in Hungary. He probably received the habit at Brevnov, taking the name of Anastasius, of which Astrik is the equivalent.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Astrik accompanied <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Saint Adalbert</a> in the latter's missionary work to the <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemians</a> and became the first abbot of <a href="/wiki/B%C5%99evnov_Monastery" title="Břevnov Monastery">Břevnov Monastery</a>. When Adalbert failed to consolidate his position in Bohemia, and left Prague, Astrik Radla went to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a> to help the missionaries among the <a href="/wiki/Magyars" class="mw-redirect" title="Magyars">Magyars</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hcm_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hcm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He first served the wife of Duke <a href="/wiki/G%C3%A9za" title="Géza">Géza</a>. In 997 Astrik became the first abbot of the Benedictine abbey of St. Martin's (<a href="/wiki/Pannonhalma_Archabbey" title="Pannonhalma Archabbey">Pannonhalma Archabbey</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-hcm_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hcm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the first ecclesiastical institution in Hungary, founded by Géza.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He then served Géza's son, <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a>, and became the first <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Hungarian people">Hungarian</a> Church.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Astrik served as Stephen's ambassador to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Pope Sylvester II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  The pope recognized Stephen as <a href="/wiki/List_of_Hungarian_rulers" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Hungarian rulers">King of the Hungarians</a>.  Soon after Astrik's return Stephen was crowned by Astrik, with a royal crown sent by Pope Sylvester, granted no doubt at the instance of the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Otto_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Otto III">Emperor Otto III</a>, in 1001.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Discovery_of_remains">Discovery of remains</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Astrik&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Discovery of remains">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Assumption_Cathedral,_Kalocsa" class="mw-redirect" title="Assumption Cathedral, Kalocsa">Assumption Cathedral of Kalocsa</a> was extensively restored between 1907 and 1912, under the direction of architect <a href="/wiki/Ern%C5%91_Foerk" title="Ernő Foerk">Ernő Foerk</a>. Under the sanctuary, a red marble archiepiscopal tomb was excavated in 1910 in the place of the original 11th-century cathedral. In addition to the intact skeleton, a gilded silver-headed <a href="/wiki/Crosier" title="Crosier">crosier</a>, a silver chalice, <a href="/wiki/Paten" title="Paten">paten</a>, golden rings, crosses, <i><a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a></i> with three jeweled gold pins, and textile remnants were found. Foerk estimated the age of the grave and thought its 11th-century origin, identified the corpse with Astrik, as the grave laid in the central axis of the first cathedral, a usual resting place for the church founders. Foerk also analogized the crosier with the near-contemporary pastoral staff of <a href="/wiki/Anno_II" title="Anno II">Anno II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">Archbishop of Cologne</a> (d. 1075). However Jesuit art historian <a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Braun&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Joseph Braun (page does not exist)">Joseph Braun</a> analyzed the chalice and the textiles based on the pictures sent, but he did not deal with the other objects. Thereafter he dated the grave to the turn of the 12th and 13th centuries. Accordingly, he identified the skeleton as the corpse of 12–13th-century archbishops <a href="/wiki/Saul_Gy%C5%91r" title="Saul Győr">Saul Győr</a> or <a href="/wiki/Ugrin_Cs%C3%A1k,_Archbishop_of_Kalocsa" title="Ugrin Csák, Archbishop of Kalocsa">Ugrin Csák</a>. The diocese's librarian Pál Winkler emphasized that Ugrin was killed in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Mohi" title="Battle of Mohi">Battle of Mohi</a> (1241) and his body was never found. Moritz Dreger considered the textiles of Byzantine origin. In 1912, the tombstone was relocated into the new Archbishop's Crypt with the epitaph "OSSA ANONYMI AEPPI COLOCEN. SAEC. XII. / SAULI DE GYŐR /? / 1192–1202 /".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuzás20142–4_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuzás20142–4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In January–February 2014, the tomb was re-examined during archeological excavations, which aimed to define the exact age of the skeleton. The identification was supported by the AMS radiocarbon examination that was conducted on the two phalanxes acquired from the skeleton. The result of the <a href="/wiki/Carbon-14" title="Carbon-14">Carbon-14</a> (radiocarbon) examination defined the date of death of the archbishop buried in the grave at (cal AD) 1001–1030, which date coincides with the scanty historical data on Astrik. Accordingly, the skeleton was identified with the corpse of Astrik, and not Saul Győr.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuzás20147_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuzás20147-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
