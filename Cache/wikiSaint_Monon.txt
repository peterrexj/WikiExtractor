<p>Saint <b>Monon</b> (or <b>Mono, Muno, Monone</b>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;645</span>) was a Scottish hermit and martyr.
His feast day is 18 October.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_legacy">Life and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Monon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Monon was born in Scotland and moved to the <a href="/wiki/Ardennes" title="Ardennes">Ardennes</a>, where he lived as a hermit.
He was murdered around 645&#160;AD in <a href="/wiki/Nassogne" title="Nassogne">Nassogne</a> in what is now Belgium.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOctober_18,_Orthodox_England_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOctober_18,_Orthodox_England-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
His body, which had been beheaded, was placed in an oratory.
Pilgrims began to visit his shrine, and it was said that many miracles occurred.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELocal_beer_on_a_legendary_background_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELocal_beer_on_a_legendary_background-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
He is honoured in the Ardennes as a protector of livestock, and is traditionally shown standing with a cow.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint-Monon,_Open_Churches_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint-Monon,_Open_Churches-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
Pilgrims to his shrine used to rub grass or leaves on the reliquary of Saint Monon, then give it to their cattle to protect them from epidemics.
They would also hang a flower or a bouquet in their stable.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChapelle_Saint-Monon_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChapelle_Saint-Monon-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Chapelle Saint-Monon in Hubermont, <a href="/wiki/La_Roche-en-Ardenne" title="La Roche-en-Ardenne">La Roche-en-Ardenne</a>, is a small schist building built around 1658, renovated in 1817 and restored in 1982.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChapelle_Saint-Monon_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChapelle_Saint-Monon-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
A <a href="/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture" title="Gothic Revival architecture">neogothic</a> church built in 1850 in Jévigné, <a href="/wiki/Lierneux" title="Lierneux">Lierneux</a>, is dedicated to the saint, and has a polychrome wood statue of him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint-Monon,_Open_Churches_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint-Monon,_Open_Churches-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
His name is given to a local beer brewed in the village of Ambly, Nassogne, named Saint-Monon.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELocal_beer_on_a_legendary_background_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELocal_beer_on_a_legendary_background-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Monon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a> wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921), 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Monon (St.) M. (Oct. 18)<br />(Date uncertain.) A Scottish Saint who crossed over to the Continent and lived as a hermit in the Forest of the Ardennes, where he was done to death by men of evil life. He is famous for miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921198_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921198-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Monon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> (1710–1773) wrote in his <i>Lives of the Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i> under October 18, 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St. Monon, M. He left Scotland, his native country, and retired into the forest of Ardennes, where he led a holy life in the seventh century. He was massacred in his cell by robbers, and was buried in the village of Nassaw, which at present belongs to the abbey of St. Hubert. His tomb was rendered famous by miracles. There was a church dedicated under his invocation near the city of St. Andrew’s in Scotland, called to this day Monon’s Kirk. See Molanus, addit. ad Usuard, and King, in Calend.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1799437_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1799437-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
