<p><b>Francisco Solano y Jiménez</b>, OFM (also known as <b>Francis Solanus</b>; 10 March 1549 – 14 July 1610) was a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> in South America, belonging to the Order of Friars Minor (the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>), and is honored as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Solanus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born 10 March 1549 in <a href="/wiki/Montilla" title="Montilla">Montilla</a>, the third child of Mateo Sánchez Solano and Ana Jiménez. He was educated by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>, but felt drawn to the poverty and <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penitential</a> life of the Franciscan friars. At the age of twenty, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> at Montilla,<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> entering the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at St. Lawrence Friary, which was located in a place of great natural beauty. The community there belonged to the Reformed observance within the Order, following a very strict routine of prayer, silence and <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasting</a>. Francis followed this regimen rigorously, always going barefoot, <a href="/wiki/Abstinence" title="Abstinence">abstaining</a> from meat, and wearing a <a href="/wiki/Hairshirt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hairshirt">hairshirt</a> throughout that entire year. As a result, however, his health was permanently affected, leaving him sick and fatigued.
</p><p>Solano was solemnly professed in 1569.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was then sent to the friary of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Loreto" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of Loreto">Our Lady of Loreto</a> in <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a> for his <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> studies. There he learned not only <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> but developed his musical talents. He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> in 1576, a ceremony his mother was unable to attend due to her poor health. He was then named Master of Ceremonies for the community. Still a lover of simplicity, Francis made a small <a href="/wiki/Room" title="Room">cell</a> for himself by the chapel of the friary, made of clay and reeds.
</p><p>After completing his final theological studies, Solano was assigned as an itinerant preacher to the surrounding villages of the region. He was eventually given a license as a <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>. During this period, he requested that he might be allowed to go to <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>, with the hope of achieving <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> for preaching the Catholic faith. He was denied this request. At that point, Solano shifted his vision to the American missions.
</p><p>After the death of his father,  Solano returned to his hometown of Montilla to care for his mother. During that time, he gained the reputation of a <a href="/wiki/Wonderworker" class="mw-redirect" title="Wonderworker">wonderworker</a>, as a number of people were cured of their afflictions through his intercession. In 1583, A pestilence having broken out at Granada, he tended the sick and dying.<sup id="cite_ref-rcl_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rcl-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="New_World">New World</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Solanus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: New World">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Spanish <a href="/wiki/King" title="King">King</a> <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II</a> requested the Franciscans to send missionaries to preach the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> in the Americas. In 1589 Solanus sailed from Spain to the New World, and having landed at Panama, crossed the isthmus and embarked on a vessel that was to convey him to <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During the journey, a storm at sea crashed his ship against the rocks not far from Peru. The crew and the passengers abandoned the ship, but Francis stayed with the slaves that were on board. After three days, they were rescued.<sup id="cite_ref-rcl_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rcl-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For twenty years Solano worked at evangelizing the vast regions of <a href="/wiki/Tucum%C3%A1n_Province" title="Tucumán Province">Tucuman</a> (present day northwestern <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Paraguay" title="Paraguay">Paraguay</a>. He had a skill for languages and succeeded at learning many of the regions' native tongues in a fairly short period. It is claimed he could also address tribes of different tongues in one language yet be understood by them all.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Being a musician as well, Solano also played the violin frequently for the natives. He is often depicted playing this instrument.
</p><p>After that came Solano's appointment as <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">guardian</a> of the Franciscan friary in <a href="/wiki/Lima" title="Lima">Lima</a>, Peru.  Further, he filled the same office for the friaries of his Order in Tucuman and Paraguay.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_(1645).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_%281645%29.jpg/200px-San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_%281645%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="136" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_%281645%29.jpg/300px-San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_%281645%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_%281645%29.jpg/400px-San_Francisco_Solano_y_el_toro_por_Murillo_%281645%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="542" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Francisco Solano and the Bull</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Murillo" title="Bartolomé Esteban Murillo">Murillo</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Around 1601 he was called to Lima, Peru, where he tried to recall the Spanish colonists to their baptismal integrity.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It is said that Solanus predicted the devastating 1619 <a href="/wiki/Earthquake" title="Earthquake">earthquake</a> of <a href="/wiki/Trujillo,_Peru" title="Trujillo, Peru">Trujillo</a>, Peru. He died at Lima in 1610.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Solanus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Solanus was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a> in 1675, and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> in 1726.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His feast is kept throughout the Franciscan Order on 24 July (except currently the United States, where it is celebrated on 14 July).
</p><p>Francis Solanus is the patron saint of Montilla. The Parish Church of San Francisco Solano is built on the site of the former house where he was born.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He is also patron of Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Paraguay and Peru.<sup id="cite_ref-rcl_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rcl-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Solanus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Francisco_Solano" title="Mission San Francisco Solano">Mission San Francisco Solano</a>, the northernmost mission along <a href="/wiki/California" title="California">California's</a> <a href="/wiki/El_Camino_Real_(California)" title="El Camino Real (California)">El Camino Real</a>, was named in 1823 for Francisco Solano.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Humahuaca" title="Humahuaca">Humahuaca</a>, every day at precisely 12 noon, on the city hall belltower, heavy copper doors slowly open and a life-sized animated wooden, dramatic-looking statue of San Francisco Solano appears for about two minutes and gives his <a href="/wiki/Benediction" title="Benediction">benediction</a> to the silent crowd amassed on the village plaza.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
