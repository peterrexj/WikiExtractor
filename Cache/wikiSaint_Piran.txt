<p><b>Piran</b> or <b>Pyran</b> (<a href="/wiki/Cornish_language" title="Cornish language">Cornish</a>: <i lang="kw">Peran</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Piranus</i><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>), died c. 480,<sup id="cite_ref-MICHAEL-PIRAN-ORTH_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MICHAEL-PIRAN-ORTH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HASLAM56_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HASLAM56-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> was a 5th-century <a href="/wiki/Cornish_people" title="Cornish people">Cornish</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and saint, possibly of Irish origin. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tin" title="Tin">tin</a>-<a href="/wiki/Mining" title="Mining">miners</a>, and is also generally regarded as the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, although <a href="/wiki/Saint_Michael" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Michael">Michael</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petroc" title="Saint Petroc">Petroc</a> also have some claim to this title.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;note 2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The consensus of scholarship has identified the "Life" of Piran as a copy of that of the Irish saint <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir" title="Ciarán of Saigir">Ciarán of Saigir</a> with the names changed.<sup id="cite_ref-BUTLER_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BUTLER-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SCHAFF_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SCHAFF-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HASLAM_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HASLAM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;note 3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;note 4&#93;</a></sup> While we cannot be certain of Piran’s origins, it is generally accepted that he was Irish, that he spent time in Wales and later was expelled from Ireland because of his powerful preaching of the Gospel of Jesus Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-PIRAN-ORTH-WAYBACK_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PIRAN-ORTH-WAYBACK-19">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Having been thrown into the sea tied to a mill stone, he miraculously arrived on the shores of Cornwall where he built his tiny oratory and continued his work of evangelism, founding communities.<sup id="cite_ref-PIRAN-ORTH-WAYBACK_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PIRAN-ORTH-WAYBACK-19">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran%27s_Flag" title="Saint Piran&#39;s Flag">Saint Piran's Flag</a>, a white cross on a black background, is the <a href="/wiki/Flags_of_Cornwall" class="mw-redirect" title="Flags of Cornwall">county flag of Cornwall</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran%27s_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Piran&#39;s Day">Saint Piran's Day</a> falls on 5 March.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Suggested_Irish_origins">Suggested Irish origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Piran&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Suggested Irish origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Piran is the most famous of all the <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> said to have come to Cornwall from Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-STANTON_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-STANTON-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BUTLER_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BUTLER-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SCHAFF_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SCHAFF-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HASLAM_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HASLAM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> By at least the 13th century, since <a href="/wiki/Brittonic_languages" title="Brittonic languages">Brittonic languages</a> and <a href="/wiki/Goidelic_languages" title="Goidelic languages">Goidelic languages</a> regularly alternate p and k sounds (see <a href="/wiki/P-Celtic_and_Q-Celtic" class="mw-redirect" title="P-Celtic and Q-Celtic">the classification of Celtic languages</a> for an explanation), he had become identified as the Irish saint <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir" title="Ciarán of Saigir">Ciarán of Saigir</a> who founded the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Saighir" title="Saighir">Seir-Kieran</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Offaly" title="County Offaly">County Offaly</a>.
</p><p>The Celtic Scholar <a href="/wiki/Charles_Plummer" title="Charles Plummer">Charles Plummer</a> suggested that <a href="/wiki/Ciaran_of_Clonmacnoise" class="mw-redirect" title="Ciaran of Clonmacnoise">Ciaran of Clonmacnoise</a> was the patron saint of Cornwall, challenging the more broadly accepted belief that he was <a href="/wiki/Ciaran_of_Saigir" class="mw-redirect" title="Ciaran of Saigir">Ciaran of Saigir</a>. The difference in spelling is for dialect or linguistical reasons between the two <a href="/wiki/Insular_Celtic_languages" title="Insular Celtic languages">Insular Celtic languages</a>. Brytonic was categorized as <a href="/wiki/P-Celtic" class="mw-redirect" title="P-Celtic">P-Celtic</a>, as it replaced the harder 'c' or k sound in the <a href="/wiki/Goidelic_languages" title="Goidelic languages">Goidelic languages</a> with the softer letter 'p'.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> On the other hand, Goidelic was seen by scholars as being <a href="/wiki/Q-Celtic" class="mw-redirect" title="Q-Celtic">Q-Celtic</a>, as one of the earliest <a href="/wiki/Ogham" title="Ogham">Ogham</a> inscriptions used a 'Q' represented by <a href="/wiki/Ceirt" title="Ceirt">Queirt</a>, which was symbolised by the <a href="/wiki/Apple" title="Apple">Apple Tree</a> to phonetically pronounce the k sound, although Q was later replaced by the letter 'C' in the Old Irish alphabet.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The fourteenth-century <i>Life of Saint Piran</i>, probably written at <a href="/wiki/Exeter_Cathedral" title="Exeter Cathedral">Exeter Cathedral</a>, is a complete copy of an earlier <a href="/wiki/Middle_Irish" title="Middle Irish">Middle Irish</a> life of Ciarán of Saighir, with different parentage and a different ending that takes into account Piran's works in Cornwall, and especially details of his death and the movements of his Cornish <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a>; thus "excising the passages which speak of his burial at Saighir" (<a href="/wiki/Doble,_G._H." class="mw-redirect" title="Doble, G. H.">Doble</a>).  However, there is no shrine to him in Ireland.
</p><p>5 March is the traditional feast day of both Saint Ciarán of Saighir and Saint Piran.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;note 5&#93;</a></sup> However the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Calendar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Launceston,_Cornwall" title="Launceston, Cornwall">Launceston</a> Church records an alternative date of 18 November for the latter.<sup id="cite_ref-Launceston_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Launceston-26">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In Perranzabuloe parish Perran Feast is traditionally celebrated on the last Monday in October. On the previous Sunday there are services at the site of St Piran's Oratory and in the parish church of St Piran.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Views_from_modern_scholars">Views from modern scholars</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Piran&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Views from modern scholars">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Charles_Plummer" title="Charles Plummer">Charles Plummer</a> suggested that Piran might, instead, be identified with <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Ciarán of Clonmacnoise</a>, who founded the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Clonmacnoise" title="Clonmacnoise">Clonmacnoise</a> also in <a href="/wiki/County_Offaly" title="County Offaly">County Offaly</a>, but this is doubtful since this saint is believed to have died of <a href="/wiki/Yellow_fever" title="Yellow fever">yellow fever</a> at the age of thirty-two and was buried at Clonmacnoise. His father is, however, sometimes said to have been a <a href="/wiki/Cornish_people" title="Cornish people">Cornishman</a>.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Joseph_Loth" title="Joseph Loth">Joseph Loth</a>, moreover, has argued, on detailed <a href="/wiki/Philology" title="Philology">philological</a> grounds, that the two names could not possibly be identical.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Hunter_Doble" title="Gilbert Hunter Doble">G. H. Doble</a> thought that Piran was a <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welshman</a> from <a href="/wiki/Glamorgan" title="Glamorgan">Glamorgan</a>, citing the lost chapel once dedicated to him in <a href="/wiki/Cardiff" title="Cardiff">Cardiff</a>.</li>
<li>David Nash Ford accepts the Ciarán of Clonmacnoise identification, whilst further suggesting that Piran's father in the Exeter life, Domuel, be identified with Dywel ab Erbin, a fifth-century prince of <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> and Cornwall).</li>
<li>The St Piran Trust has undertaken research which<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> suggests that Piran was either Ciarán of Saighir or a disciple, as indicated by James Brennan of Kilkenny and T. F. G. Dexter, whose thesis is held in the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Cornwall_Museum" title="Royal Cornwall Museum">Royal Cornwall Museum</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup></li>
<li>Professor <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Orme" title="Nicholas Orme">Nicholas Orme</a> writes in his <i>Churches of Medieval Exeter</i>, that "it may well be that Piran was the inspiration for the Kerrian dedication (in <a href="/wiki/Exeter" title="Exeter">Exeter</a>), albeit believed (as Piran usually was) to be identical with <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir" title="Ciarán of Saigir">Ciarán</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-ORME_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ORME-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Also, the saint of the church in Exeter was <i>Keranus</i> or <i>Kyeranus</i> [Queranus] in Latin documents, with <i>Kerrian</i> being the local vernacular pronunciation.<sup id="cite_ref-ORME_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ORME-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Piran&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Flag_of_Cornwall.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Flag_of_Cornwall.svg/220px-Flag_of_Cornwall.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Flag_of_Cornwall.svg/330px-Flag_of_Cornwall.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Flag_of_Cornwall.svg/440px-Flag_of_Cornwall.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="300" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran%27s_Flag" title="Saint Piran&#39;s Flag">Saint Piran's Flag</a> consists of a white cross on a black field</figcaption></figure>
<ul><li>The Irish tied him to a mill-stone, rolled it over the edge of a cliff into a stormy sea, which immediately became calm, and the saint floated safely over the water to land upon the sandy beach of <a href="/wiki/Perranzabuloe" title="Perranzabuloe">Perranzabuloe</a> in Cornwall. His first disciples are said to have been a badger, a fox, and a bear<sup id="cite_ref-st_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-st-30">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>He landed in Cornwall, and there established himself as a hermit. His sanctity and his austerity won for him the veneration of all around, and the <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgy" title="Thaumaturgy">gift of miracles</a>, with which he was favoured, brought many to seek his charitable aid.<sup id="cite_ref-STANTON_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-STANTON-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>He was joined at Perranzabuloe by many of his Christian converts and together they founded the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Lanpiran&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Lanpiran (page does not exist)">Abbey of Lanpiran</a>, with Piran as abbot.</li>
<li>Piran 'rediscovered' tin-smelting (tin had been <a href="/wiki/Smelting" title="Smelting">smelted</a> in Cornwall since before the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Britain" title="Roman Britain">Romans'</a> arrival, but the methods had since been lost) when the tin in his black hearthstone, which was evidently a slab of <a href="/wiki/Cassiterite" title="Cassiterite">tin-bearing ore</a>, was smelted out of it and rose to the top in the form of a white cross (thus the image on the flag).<sup id="cite_ref-st_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-st-30">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Piran&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg/220px-Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="242" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg/330px-Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg/440px-Blason_de_la_famille_de_saint-pezran.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="660" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Roll_of_arms" title="Roll of arms">Roll of arms</a> of Saint-Piran's family</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Oratoire-de-saint-peran-trezilide.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Oratoire-de-saint-peran-trezilide.jpg/220px-Oratoire-de-saint-peran-trezilide.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="350" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c7/Oratoire-de-saint-peran-trezilide.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="329" data-file-height="524" /></a><figcaption>St Piran's Oratory at <a href="/wiki/Tr%C3%A9zilid%C3%A9" title="Trézilidé">Trézilidé</a>, Finistère</figcaption></figure>
<p>Piran was reportedly executed by Theodoric or Tador, King of
Cornwall in 480, about the time of <a href="/wiki/Vortigern" title="Vortigern">Vortigern</a> (Usher's Prim. 869). It is also said that at his death,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;note 6&#93;</a></sup> the remains of the Blessed Martin the Abbot which he had brought from Ireland were buried with him at <a href="/wiki/Perranzabuloe" title="Perranzabuloe">Perranzabuloe</a>.
</p><p>His own remains were subsequently exhumed and redistributed to be venerated in various <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquaries</a>. <a href="/wiki/Exeter_Cathedral" title="Exeter Cathedral">Exeter Cathedral</a> was reputed to be the possessor of one of his arms, while according to an inventory, St Piran's Old Church, Perranzabuloe, had a reliquary containing his head and also a hearse in which his body was placed for processionals. In 1443, Cornish nobleman, Sir <a href="/wiki/John_Arundell_(1366%E2%80%931435)" title="John Arundell (1366–1435)">John Arundell</a> bequeathed money in his will for the preservation of the head of St Piran in the chapel at Perranzabuloe.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The churches at Perranuthnoe and Perranarworthal were dedicated to Piran and holy wells at Perranwell and <a href="/wiki/Probus,_Cornwall" title="Probus, Cornwall">Probus</a>, Cornwall are named after him. In <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> St. Peran, Loperan and Saint-Perran are also named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> The former Methodist chapel at <a href="/wiki/Laity_Moor" title="Laity Moor">Laity Moor</a> has served as the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> Church of <a href="/wiki/Archangel_Michael" class="mw-redirect" title="Archangel Michael">Archangel Michael</a> and Holy Piran since 1996.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The earliest documented link to the design of the <a href="/wiki/St_Piran%27s_Flag" class="mw-redirect" title="St Piran&#39;s Flag">St Piran's Flag</a> with Piran is on the coat of arms of the <i>de Saint-Péran</i> or <i>Saint-Pezran</i> (pronounced <i>Péran</i>) family from <a href="/wiki/Cornouaille" title="Cornouaille">Cornouaille</a> in Brittany. The earliest evidence known comes from the 15th century, with the arms being <i>De sable à la croix pattée d'argent.</i> (a black shield with a white <a href="/wiki/Cross_patt%C3%A9e" title="Cross pattée">cross pattée</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Mount_St._Piran" title="Mount St. Piran">Mount St. Piran</a> is a mountain in <a href="/wiki/Banff_National_Park" title="Banff National Park">Banff National Park</a> near <a href="/wiki/Lake_Louise,_Alberta" title="Lake Louise, Alberta">Lake Louise</a>, Alberta, Canada, named after the saint. St Piran's crab, <i><a href="/wiki/Clibanarius_erythropus" title="Clibanarius erythropus">Clibanarius erythropus</a></i>, was also named in his honour, in 2016.
</p>
<h3><span id="St_Piran.27s_Day"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St_Piran's_Day">St Piran's Day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Piran&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: St Piran&#039;s Day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/St_Piran%27s_Day" title="St Piran&#39;s Day">St Piran's Day</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg/220px-St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg/330px-St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a5/St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg/440px-St_Piran%27s_Cross_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63222.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="436" /></a><figcaption>St Piran's Cross in the dunes at Perranzabuloe</figcaption></figure>
<p>St Piran's Day on 5 March is popular in Cornwall and the term 'Perrantide' has been coined to describe the week prior to this day. Many Cornish-themed events occur in the Duchy and also in areas in which there is a large community descended from Cornish emigrants. The village of <a href="/wiki/Perranporth" title="Perranporth">Perranporth</a> ('Porthpyran' in <a href="/wiki/Cornish_language" title="Cornish language">Cornish</a>) hosts the annual <a href="/wiki/Pan-Celticism" title="Pan-Celticism">inter-Celtic</a> festival of <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20040612123804/http://www.an-daras.com/lp/lp_intro.htm">'Lowender Peran'</a>, which is also named in honour of him.
</p><p>The largest St Piran's Day event is the march across <a href="/wiki/Penhale_Sands" title="Penhale Sands">the dunes</a> to St Piran's cross which hundreds of people attend, generally dressed in black, white and gold, and carrying the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran%27s_Flag" title="Saint Piran&#39;s Flag">Cornish Flag</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> A play of the Life of St Piran, in Cornish, has been enacted since 2000 at the event. Daffodils are also carried and placed at the cross. Daffodils also feature in celebrations in <a href="/wiki/Truro" title="Truro">Truro</a>, most likely due to their 'gold' colour. Black, white and gold are colours associated with Cornwall due to St Piran's Flag (black and white), and the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Cornwall" title="Duchy of Cornwall">Duchy</a> Shield (gold coins on black).
</p><p>In 2006 Cornish MP <a href="/wiki/Dan_Rogerson" title="Dan Rogerson">Dan Rogerson</a> asked the government to make 5 March a public holiday in Cornwall to recognise celebrations for St Piran's Day.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
In 2010, a short movie about St. Piran was made and premiered at the Heartland Film Festival.
</p>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1011085734">.mw-parser-output .reflist{font-size:90%;margin-bottom:0.5em;list-style-type:decimal}.mw-parser-output .reflist .references{font-size:100%;margin-bottom:0;list-style-type:inherit}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns-2{column-width:30em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns-3{column-width:25em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns{margin-top:0.3em}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns ol{margin-top:0}.mw-parser-output .reflist-columns li{page-break-inside:avoid;break-inside:avoid-column}.mw-parser-output .reflist-upper-alpha{list-style-type:upper-alpha}.mw-parser-output .reflist-upper-roman{list-style-type:upper-roman}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-alpha{list-style-type:lower-alpha}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-greek{list-style-type:lower-greek}.mw-parser-output .reflist-lower-roman{list-style-type:lower-roman}</style><div class="reflist">
<div class="mw-references-wrap"><ol class="references">
<li id="cite_note-11"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-11">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The cult of Saint Michael was largely due to the Norman Earls of Cornwall, while that of Saint Petroc was the most important in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Cornwall" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Cornwall">Diocese of Cornwall</a> since he was the founder of the monastery of Bodmin the most important in the diocese and, with St Germans, the seat of the bishops. He was the patron of the diocese and of Bodmin.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-13"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-13">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">According to an article in the 1859 edition of the <i>Ulster Journal of Archaeology</i>:
<dl><dd><ul><li>"...St. Ciaran, under the name Piran, [is the patron saint] of more than one church in Cornwall."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></li></ul></dd></dl>
</span></li>
<li id="cite_note-16"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-16">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Rev. Richard Stanton notes the following in his <i>Menology of England and Wales</i> (1892):
<dl><dd><ul><li>"According to <b><a href="/wiki/John_Lanigan_(historian)" title="John Lanigan (historian)">Lanigan</a></b> (<i>Hist</i>., i., 22, and ii., 9), St. Piran is known in Ireland as <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir" title="Ciarán of Saigir">Kieran of Saigir</a>, and was a pupil of St. <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finian</a> in the sixth century. He is generally spoken of as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ossory" title="Bishop of Ossory">Bishop of Ossory</a>, but the Irish accounts do not mention his going to England."</li>
<li>"<a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Queranus</a>, mentioned by Whitford and Wilson on 9 Sep., and honoured in Scotland, is said by <b><a href="/wiki/Alexander_Forbes_(bishop_of_Brechin)" title="Alexander Forbes (bishop of Brechin)">Forbes</a></b> (<i>Kal. of Scottish Saints</i>, p. 435) to be St. Piran of Cornwall. "<sup id="cite_ref-STANTON_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-STANTON-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li></ul></dd></dl>
</span></li>
<li id="cite_note-18"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-18">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">"Piran or Kiaran (of Saighir)".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-25"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-25">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">"5 March.- St. Piran's day is a miners' holiday. St. Piran is the patron saint of "tinners," and is popularly supposed to have died drunk. (However, this appears to conflate Piran with St. Pyr of Caldey.)"As drunk as a Piraner" is a Cornish proverb."<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-31"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-31">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">"At length, however, worn out with age and infirmity, St Piran called his followers around him, and, having addressed them for the last time, desired a grave to be prepared. He then took leave of them, and, descending into it with calmness, his spirit departed on the 5th day of March, about 480. He rests, continues an old narrative of his life, in Cornwall, on the shore of the <i>Severn</i> sea, fifteen miles from Petroc-stowe or <a href="/wiki/Padstow" title="Padstow">Padstow</a>, and twenty-five from <a href="/wiki/Mousehole" title="Mousehole">Mousehole</a> (two ancient harbours of Cornwall, the former on the north, and the latter on the south coast)." ("Severn sea" = Bristol channel, but now called Celtic sea.)
<sup id="cite_ref-HASLAM56_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HASLAM56-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></span>
</li>
