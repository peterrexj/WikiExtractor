<p><b>Pope Innocent III</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Innocentius III</i>; 1160 or 1161 – 16 July 1216),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> born <b>Lotario dei Conti di Segni</b> (anglicized as <b>Lothar of <a href="/wiki/Conti_di_Segni" class="mw-redirect" title="Conti di Segni">Segni</a></b>), was the head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 8 January 1198 until his death in 16 July 1216.
</p><p>Pope Innocent was one of the most powerful and influential of the medieval popes. He exerted a wide influence over the Christian states of Europe, claiming supremacy over all of Europe's kings. He was central in supporting the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>'s reforms of ecclesiastical affairs through his <a href="/wiki/Decretal" title="Decretal">decretals</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Lateran_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourth Lateran Council">Fourth Lateran Council</a>. This resulted in a considerable refinement of Western <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a>. He is furthermore notable for using <a href="/wiki/Interdict" title="Interdict">interdict</a> and other censures to compel princes to obey his decisions, although these measures were not uniformly successful.
</p><p>Innocent greatly extended the scope of the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a>, directing crusades against <a href="/wiki/Muslim_Iberia" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim Iberia">Muslim Iberia</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> as well as the <a href="/wiki/Albigensian_Crusade" title="Albigensian Crusade">Albigensian Crusade</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Cathar" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathar">Cathars</a> in southern France. He organized the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Crusade" title="Fourth Crusade">Fourth Crusade</a> of 1202&#8211;1204, which ended in the <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Constantinople" title="Sack of Constantinople">sack of Constantinople</a>. Although the attack on Constantinople went against his explicit orders, and the Crusaders were subsequently excommunicated, Innocent reluctantly accepted this result, seeing it as the <a href="/wiki/Will_of_God" title="Will of God">will of God</a> to reunite the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>. In the event, the sack of Constantinople and the subsequent period of <i><a href="/wiki/Frankokratia" title="Frankokratia">Frankokratia</a></i> heightened the hostility between the Latin and Greek churches; the Byzantine Empire was restored <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire_under_the_Palaiologos_dynasty" title="Byzantine Empire under the Palaiologos dynasty">in 1261</a>, albeit much weaker.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore2003102–134_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore2003102–134-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Lotario de' Conti was born in <a href="/wiki/Gavignano" title="Gavignano">Gavignano</a>, Italy, near <a href="/wiki/Anagni" title="Anagni">Anagni</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His father, Count Trasimondo of Segni, was a member of a famous house, the <a href="/wiki/Conti_di_Segni" class="mw-redirect" title="Conti di Segni">Conti di Segni</a> (Counts of Segni), which produced nine popes, including <a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Alexander IV</a> and <a href="/wiki/Innocent_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent XIII">Innocent XIII</a>. Lotario was the nephew of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_III" title="Pope Clement III">Pope Clement III</a>; his mother, Clarissa Scotti (Romani de Scotti), was from the same noble Roman family.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilliams199825_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams199825-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lotario received his early education in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, probably at the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbey of St Andrea al Celio, under Peter Ismael;<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> he studied theology in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> under the theologians <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Poitiers" title="Peter of Poitiers">Peter of Poitiers</a>, Melior of Pisa, and <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Corbeil" title="Peter of Corbeil">Peter of Corbeil</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and (possibly) jurisprudence in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, according to the <i>Gesta</i> (between 1187 and 1189).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> As pope, Lotario was to play a major role in the shaping of canon law through conciliar canons and decretal letters.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Alexander III</a> (30 August 1181) Lotario returned to Rome and held various ecclesiastical offices during the short reigns of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_III" title="Pope Lucius III">Lucius III</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_III" title="Pope Urban III">Urban III</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VIII" title="Pope Gregory VIII">Gregory VIII</a>, and Clement III, being ordained a <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">Subdeacon</a> by Gregory VIII and reaching the rank of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Priest">Cardinal-Priest</a> under Clement III in 1191.
</p><p>As a cardinal, Lotario wrote <i><a href="/wiki/De_miseria_humanae_conditionis" class="mw-redirect" title="De miseria humanae conditionis">De miseria humanae conditionis</a></i> (On the Misery of the Human Condition).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Moore_1981_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Moore_1981-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The work was very popular for centuries, surviving in more than 700 <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscripts</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Although he never returned to the complementary work he intended to write, <i>On the Dignity of Human Nature</i>, <a href="/wiki/Bartolomeo_Facio" title="Bartolomeo Facio">Bartolomeo Facio</a> (1400–1457) took up the task writing <i>De excellentia ac praestantia hominis</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Election_to_the_papacy">Election to the papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Election to the papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/1198_papal_election" title="1198 papal election">1198 papal election</a></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png/220px-Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="189" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png/330px-Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png/440px-Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png 2x" data-file-width="606" data-file-height="521" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_of_Innocent_III.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Arms of Innocent III at <a href="/wiki/Santo_Spirito_in_Sassia" title="Santo Spirito in Sassia">Santo Spirito in Sassia</a>, Rome</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Celestine_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Celestine III">Celestine III</a> died on 8 January 1198. Before his death he had urged the <a href="/wiki/College_of_Cardinals" title="College of Cardinals">College of Cardinals</a> to elect <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_di_San_Paolo" title="Giovanni di San Paolo">Giovanni di San Paolo</a> as his successor, but Lotario de' Conti was elected pope in the ruins of the ancient <a href="/wiki/Septizodium" title="Septizodium">Septizodium</a>, near the <a href="/wiki/Circus_Maximus" title="Circus Maximus">Circus Maximus</a> in Rome after only two ballots on the very day on which Celestine III died. He was only thirty-seven years old at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He took the name Innocent III, maybe as a reference to his predecessor <a href="/wiki/Innocent_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent II">Innocent II</a> (1130–1143), who had succeeded in asserting the papacy's authority over the emperor (in contrast with <a href="/wiki/Celestine_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Celestine III">Celestine III</a>'s recent policy).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Reassertion_of_papal_power">Reassertion of papal power</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Reassertion of papal power">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_(FindID_235228).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_%28FindID_235228%29.jpg/220px-Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_%28FindID_235228%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="115" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_%28FindID_235228%29.jpg/330px-Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_%28FindID_235228%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_%28FindID_235228%29.jpg/440px-Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_%28FindID_235228%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="983" data-file-height="515" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Papal_Bulla_of_Innocent_III_(FindID_235228).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Bulla_(seal)" title="Bulla (seal)">Bulla</a> of Innocent III</div></div></div>
<p>As pope, Innocent III began with a very wide sense of his responsibility and of his authority. During Innocent III's reign, the papacy was at the height of its powers. He was considered to be the most powerful person in Europe at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 1198, Innocent wrote to the prefect Acerbius and the nobles of Tuscany expressing his support of the medieval political <a href="/wiki/Theory_of_the_sun_and_the_moon" class="mw-redirect" title="Theory of the sun and the moon">theory of the sun and the moon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> His papacy asserted the absolute spiritual authority of his office, while still respecting the temporal authority of kings.<sup id="cite_ref-Muldoon_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muldoon-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There was scarcely a country in Europe over which Innocent III did not in some way or other assert the supremacy which he claimed for the papacy. He <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_IX_of_Leon" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso IX of Leon">Alfonso IX of Leon</a>, for marrying a near relative, <a href="/wiki/Berengaria_of_Castile" title="Berengaria of Castile">Berengaria</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso VIII">Alfonso VIII</a>, contrary to the laws of the Church, and effected their separation in 1204. For similar reasons he <a href="/wiki/Annulled" class="mw-redirect" title="Annulled">annulled</a>, in 1208, the marriage of the crown-prince, <a href="/wiki/Afonso_II_of_Portugal" title="Afonso II of Portugal">Alfonso of Portugal</a>, with <a href="/wiki/Urraca_of_Castile,_Queen_of_Portugal" title="Urraca of Castile, Queen of Portugal">Urraca</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VIII_of_Castile" title="Alfonso VIII of Castile">Alfonso of Castile</a>. From <a href="/wiki/Pedro_II_of_Aragon" class="mw-redirect" title="Pedro II of Aragon">Pedro II of Aragon</a> he received that kingdom in vassalage and crowned him king at Rome in 1204.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Muslim recapture of <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> in 1187 was to him a divine judgment on the moral lapses of Christian princes. He was also determined to protect what he called "the liberty of the Church" from inroads by <a href="/wiki/Secularity" title="Secularity">secular</a> princes. This determination meant, among other things, that princes should not be involved in the selection of <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishops</a>, and it was focused especially on the "<a href="/wiki/Patrimonium_Sancti_Petri" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrimonium Sancti Petri">patrimonium</a>" of the papacy, the section of central Italy claimed by the popes and later called the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>. The patrimonium was routinely threatened by <a href="/wiki/House_of_Hohenstaufen" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Hohenstaufen">Hohenstaufen</a> German kings who, as Roman emperors, claimed it for themselves. <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry VI">Emperor Henry VI</a> expected his infant son <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick</a> to bring Germany, Italy, and Sicily under a single ruler, which would leave the Papal States exceedingly vulnerable.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Henry's early death left his 3-year-old son, Frederick, as king of Sicily. Henry VI's widow, <a href="/wiki/Constance_of_Sicily" class="mw-redirect" title="Constance of Sicily">Constance of Sicily</a>, ruled over Sicily for her young son before he reached the age of majority. She was as eager to remove German power from the kingdom of Sicily as was Innocent III. Before her death in 1198, she named Innocent as guardian of the young Frederick until he reached his maturity. In exchange, Innocent was also able to recover papal rights in Sicily that had been surrendered decades earlier to King <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_Sicily" title="William I of Sicily">William I of Sicily</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_IV" title="Pope Adrian IV">Pope Adrian IV</a>. The Pope invested the young Frederick II as King of Sicily in November 1198. He also later induced Frederick II to marry <a href="/wiki/Constance_of_Aragon" title="Constance of Aragon">Constance of Aragon</a>, the widow of King <a href="/wiki/Emeric_of_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Emeric of Hungary">Emeric of Hungary</a>, in 1209.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Involvement_in_Imperial_elections">Involvement in Imperial elections</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Involvement in Imperial elections">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Innocent was concerned that the marriage of Henry VI and Constance of Sicily gave the Hohenstaufens a claim to all the Italian peninsula with the exception of the Papal States, which would be surrounded by Imperial territory.<sup id="cite_ref-Muldoon_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Muldoon-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of <a href="/wiki/Henry_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry VI, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Henry VI</a>, who had recently also conquered the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily" title="Kingdom of Sicily">Kingdom of Sicily</a>, the succession became <a href="/wiki/German_throne_dispute" title="German throne dispute">disputed</a>: as Henry's son <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick</a> was still a small child, the partisans of the <a href="/wiki/Hohenstaufen" title="Hohenstaufen">Staufen</a> dynasty elected Henry's brother, <a href="/wiki/Philip,_Duke_of_Swabia" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip, Duke of Swabia">Philip, Duke of Swabia</a>, king in March 1198, whereas the princes opposed to the Staufen dynasty elected <a href="/wiki/Otto_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto, Duke of Brunswick</a>, of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Welf" title="House of Welf">House of Welf</a>. King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_France" title="Philip II of France">Philip II of France</a> supported Philip's claim, whereas <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_England" title="Richard I of England">King Richard I of England</a> supported his nephew Otto.<sup id="cite_ref-Comyn,_pg._275_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Comyn,_pg._275-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
In 1201, the pope openly espoused the side of Otto IV, whose family had always been opposed to the house of <a href="/wiki/Hohenstaufen" title="Hohenstaufen">Hohenstaufen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bryce,_pg._206_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bryce,_pg._206-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup></p><blockquote>
<p>It is the business of the pope to look after the interests of the Roman empire, since the empire derives its origin and its final authority from the papacy; its origin, because it was originally transferred from Greece by and for the sake of the papacy; ... its final authority, because the emperor is raised to his position by the pope who blesses him, crowns him and invests him with the empire. ...Therefore, since three persons have lately been elected king by different parties, namely the youth [Frederick, son of Henry VI], Philip [of Hohenstaufen, brother of Henry VI], and Otto [of Brunswick, of the Welf family], so also three things must be taken into account in regard to each one, namely: the legality, the suitability and the expediency of his election. ...Far be it from us that we should defer to man rather than to God, or that we should fear the countenance of the powerful. ...On the foregoing grounds, then, we decide that the youth should not at present be given the empire; we utterly reject Philip for his manifest unfitness and we order his usurpation to be resisted by all ... since Otto is not only himself devoted to the church, but comes from devout ancestors on both sides, ... therefore we decree that he ought to be accepted and supported as king, and ought to be given the crown of empire, after the rights of the Roman church have been secured. "Papal Decree on the choice of a German King, 1201"<sup id="cite_ref-MS_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MS-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The confusion in the Empire allowed Innocent to drive out the imperial feudal lords from <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a> and <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, who had been installed by Emperor Henry VI.<sup id="cite_ref-Comyn,_pg._277_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Comyn,_pg._277-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> On 3 July 1201, the papal legate, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Bishop">Cardinal-Bishop</a> Guido of Palestrina, announced to the people, in the cathedral of Cologne, that Otto IV had been approved by the pope as Roman king and threatened with <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunication</a> all those who refused to acknowledge him. At the same time, Innocent encouraged the cities in <a href="/wiki/March_of_Tuscany" title="March of Tuscany">Tuscany</a> to form a league called the <a href="/wiki/League_of_San_Genesio" class="mw-redirect" title="League of San Genesio">League of San Genesio</a> against German imperial interests in Italy, and they placed themselves under Innocent's protection.<sup id="cite_ref-Comyn,_pg._277_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Comyn,_pg._277-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In May 1202, Innocent issued the decree <i>Venerabilem</i>, addressed to the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Z%C3%A4hringen" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Zähringen">Duke of Zähringen</a>, in which he explained his thinking on the relation between the papacy and the Empire. This decree was afterwards embodied in the <i><a href="/wiki/Corpus_Juris_Canonici" title="Corpus Juris Canonici">Corpus Juris Canonici</a></i> and contained the following items:
</p>
<ul><li>The German princes have the right to elect the king, who is afterwards to become emperor. This right was given them by the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_See" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic See">Apostolic See</a> when it transferred the imperial dignity from the Greeks to the Germans in the person of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>.</li>
<li>The right to investigate and decide whether a king thus elected is worthy of the imperial dignity belongs to the pope, whose office it is to anoint, consecrate, and crown him; otherwise it might happen that the pope would be obliged to <a href="/wiki/Anointing" title="Anointing">anoint</a>, <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrate</a>, and crown a king who was excommunicated, a <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretic</a>, or a <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a>.</li>
<li>If the pope finds that the king who has been elected by the princes is unworthy of the imperial dignity, the princes must elect a new king or, if they refuse, the pope will confer the imperial dignity upon another king; for the Church stands in need of a patron and defender.</li>
<li>In case of a double election the pope must exhort the princes to come to an agreement. If after a due interval they have not reached an agreement they must ask the pope to arbitrate, failing which, he must of his own accord and by virtue of his office decide in favour of one of the claimants. The pope's decision need not be based on the greater or less legality of either election, but on the qualifications of the claimants.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>Despite papal support, Otto could not oust his rival Philip until the latter was murdered in a private feud. His rule now undisputed, Otto reneged on his earlier promises and set his sights on reestablishing Imperial power in Italy and claiming even the Kingdom of Sicily. Given the papal interest to keep Germany and Sicily apart, Innocent now supported his ward, King Frederick of Sicily, to resist Otto's advances and restore the Staufen dynasty to the Holy Roman Empire. Frederick was duly elected by the Staufen partisans.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The conflict was decided by the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Bouvines" title="Battle of Bouvines">Battle of Bouvines</a> on 27 July 1214, which pitted Otto, allied to King <a href="/wiki/John_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="John of England">John of England</a> against Philip II Augustus. Otto was defeated by the French and thereafter lost all influence. He died on 19 May 1218, leaving Frederick II the undisputed emperor. Meanwhile, King John was forced to acknowledge the Pope as his feudal lord and accept <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Langton" title="Stephen Langton">Stephen Langton</a> as <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1994_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1994-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In his turn, Frederick II would later become a bitter opponent of the papacy once his empire was secure.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feudal_power_over_Europe">Feudal power over Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Feudal power over Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Innocent III played further roles in the politics of Norway,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> France, Sweden, Bulgaria, Spain and England.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1994_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1994-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> At the request of England's King John, Pope Innocent III declared the <a href="/wiki/Magna_Carta" title="Magna Carta">Magna Carta</a> annulled, which resulted in a rebellion by the English barons who rejected the disenfranchisement.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Crusades_and_suppression_of_heresy">Crusades and suppression of heresy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Crusades and suppression of heresy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg/220px-Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="102" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg/330px-Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg/440px-Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="703" data-file-height="327" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Albigensian_Crusade_01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Innocent launched the <a href="/wiki/Albigensian_Crusade" title="Albigensian Crusade">Albigensian Crusade</a> against the Cathars.</div></div></div>
<p>Pope Innocent III was also a zealous protector of the Catholic faith and a strenuous opponent of heresy. His chief activity was turned against the <a href="/wiki/Albigenses" class="mw-redirect" title="Albigenses">Albigenses</a> who had become so numerous and aggressive that they were no longer satisfied with just being adherents of heretical doctrines but even endeavoured to spread their heresy by force.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> They were especially numerous in a few cities of Northern and Southern France. During the first year of his pontificate, Innocent sent the two <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> monks Rainer and Guido to the Albigenses in France to preach to them the true doctrines of the Catholic faith and dispute with them on controverted topics of religion. The two Cistercian missionaries were soon followed by <a href="/wiki/Diego_of_Osma" class="mw-redirect" title="Diego of Osma">Diego, Bishop of Osma</a>, then by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Saint Dominic</a> and the two papal legates. <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Castelnau" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter of Castelnau">Peter of Castelnau</a> and Raoul.
</p><p>When, however, these missionaries were ridiculed and despised by the Albigenses, and the papal legate Castelnau was assassinated in 1208, Innocent resorted to force. He ordered the bishops of Southern France to put under interdict the participants in the murder and all the towns that gave shelter to them. He was especially incensed against <a href="/wiki/Raymond_VI,_Count_of_Toulouse" title="Raymond VI, Count of Toulouse">Count Raymond of Toulouse</a> who had previously been excommunicated by the murdered legate and whom the pope suspected as the instigator of the murder. The count protested his innocence and submitted to the pope but the pope placed no further trust in him. He called upon the King of France, <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_Augustus_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip II Augustus of France">Philip II</a> to raise an army for the suppression of the Albigenses. Under the leadership of <a href="/wiki/Simon_de_Montfort,_5th_Earl_of_Leicester" title="Simon de Montfort, 5th Earl of Leicester">Simon of Montfort</a> a cruel campaign ensued against the Albigenses which, despite the protest of Innocent, soon turned into a war of conquest.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During the siege of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9ziers" title="Béziers">Béziers</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Arnaud_Amalric" title="Arnaud Amalric">leader</a> of the crusader assault famously declared upon being asked how to distinguish <a href="/wiki/Cathars" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathars">Cathars</a> from <a href="/wiki/Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholics">Catholics</a> at the besieged town "<a href="/wiki/Caedite_eos._Novit_enim_Dominus_qui_sunt_eius" class="mw-redirect" title="Caedite eos. Novit enim Dominus qui sunt eius">Caedite eos. Novit enim Dominus qui sunt eius</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> which translates as: "Slay them all, God will recognize his own."  This statement is often cited as "Kill them all and let God sort them out."
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Albigensian_Crusade" title="Albigensian Crusade">Albigensian Crusade</a> led to the deaths of approximately 20,000 men, women and children, Cathar and Catholic alike, decimating the number of practising Cathars and diminishing the region's distinct culture.<sup id="cite_ref-Cheney_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cheney-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> The conflict took on a political flavor, directed not only against the heretics, but also the nobility of <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> and vassals of the <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon" title="Crown of Aragon">Crown of Aragon</a>, and finally brought the region firmly under the control of the king of France. <a href="/wiki/Peter_II_of_Aragon" title="Peter II of Aragon">King Peter II of Aragon</a>, Count of Barcelona, was directly involved in the conflict, and was killed in the course of the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Muret" title="Battle of Muret">Battle of Muret</a> in 1213. The conflict largely ended with the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1229)" title="Treaty of Paris (1229)">Treaty of Paris of 1229</a>, in which the integration of the <a href="/wiki/Occitania" title="Occitania">Occitan</a> territory in the French crown was agreed upon.
</p><p>Pope Innocent III spent a majority of his tenure as Pope (1198–1216) preparing for a great crusade on the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. His first attempt was the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Crusade" title="Fourth Crusade">Fourth Crusade</a> (1202–1204) which he decreed by the papal bull <i><a href="/wiki/Post_Miserabile" title="Post Miserabile">Post miserabile</a></i> in 1198.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Unlike past popes, Innocent III displayed interest in leading the crusade himself, rather than simply instigating it and allowing secular leaders to organize the expedition according to their own aspirations.<sup id="cite_ref-Cheney_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cheney-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Innocent III's first order of business in preaching the crusade was to send missionaries to every Catholic state to endorse the campaign. He sent <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Capua_the_Elder" title="Peter of Capua the Elder">Peter of Capua</a> to the kings of France and England with specific instructions to convince them to settle their differences, resulting in a truce of five years between the two nations, beginning in 1199. The intent of the truce was not to allow the two kings to lead the crusade, but rather to free their resources to assist the Crusade. For the army's leadership, Innocent aimed his pleas at the knights and nobles of Europe,<sup id="cite_ref-Cheney_26-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cheney-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> succeeding in France, where many lords answered the pope's call, including the army's two eventual leaders, Theobald of Champagne and <a href="/wiki/Boniface_I,_Marquess_of_Montferrat" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface I, Marquess of Montferrat">Boniface</a>, marquis of Montferrat. The pope's calls to action were not received with as much enthusiasm in England or Germany, and the expedition became mainly a French affair.<sup id="cite_ref-Clayton_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clayton-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Fourth Crusade was an expensive endeavor. Innocent III chose to raise funds with a new approach: requiring all clergy to donate one fortieth of their income. This marked the first time a pope ever imposed a direct tax on the clergy. He faced many difficulties in collecting this tax, including corrupt tax collectors and disregard in England. He also sent envoys to <a href="/wiki/John,_King_of_England" title="John, King of England">King John</a> of England and <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_France" title="Philip II of France">King Philip</a> of France, who pledged to contribute to the campaign,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and John also declared his support for the clerical tax in his kingdom. The crusaders too contributed funds: Innocent declared that those who took the crusader's vow, but could no longer fulfill it, could be released by a contribution of funds. The pope put Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Hubert_Walter" title="Hubert Walter">Hubert Walter</a> in charge of collecting these dues.<sup id="cite_ref-Cheney_26-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cheney-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the onset of the crusade, the intended destination was Egypt, as the Christians and Muslims were under a truce at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-Clayton_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clayton-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> An agreement was made between the French Crusaders and the Venetians. The Venetians would supply vessels and supplies for the crusaders and in return the crusaders would pay 85,000 marks (£200,000).<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Innocent gave his approval of this agreement under two conditions: a representative of the pope must accompany the crusade, and the attack on any other Christians was strictly forbidden. The French failed to raise sufficient funds for payment of the Venetians. As a result, the Crusaders diverted the crusade to the Christian city of <a href="/wiki/Zadar" title="Zadar">Zadar</a> at the will of the Venetians, to subsidize the debt. This diversion was adopted without the consent of Innocent III, who threatened excommunication to any who took part in the attack. A majority of the French ignored the threat and attacked Zadar, and were excommunicated by Innocent III, but soon were forgiven so as to continue the crusade. A second diversion then occurred when the crusaders decided to conquer Constantinople, the capital of the Byzantine Empire, at the behest of the exiled Byzantine prince <a href="/wiki/Alexios_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexios IV">Alexios IV</a>. This diversion was taken without any knowledge by Innocent III, and he did not learn of it until after the city had been captured.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Innocent III was heavily opposed to the attack on Constantinople and sent many letters warning the crusaders to not sack the city. Innocent III excommunicated the crusaders who attacked Byzantine cities, but was unable to physically halt or overturn their actions. The attack on Constantinople led to the start of the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Empire" title="Latin Empire">Latin Empire</a>'s rule of Constantinople, which lasted for the next sixty years.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Francis_of_Assisi">Francis of Assisi</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Francis of Assisi">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1209, <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> led his first eleven followers to Rome to seek permission from Pope Innocent III to found a new religious order which was ultimately granted.<sup id="cite_ref-chest107_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chest107-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Upon entry to Rome, the brothers encountered Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Guido_of_Assisi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guido of Assisi (page does not exist)">Guido of Assisi</a>, who had in his company <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_di_San_Paolo" title="Giovanni di San Paolo">Giovanni di San Paolo</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Bishop_of_Sabina" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Bishop of Sabina">Cardinal-Bishop of Sabina</a>. The cardinal, who was the confessor of Pope Innocent III, was immediately sympathetic to Francis and agreed to represent Francis to the pope. Reluctantly, Pope Innocent agreed to meet with Francis and the brothers the next day. After several days, the pope agreed to admit the group informally, adding that when God increased the group in grace and number, they could return for an official admittance. The group was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Francis_of_Assisi_and_His_World_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Francis_of_Assisi_and_His_World-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> This was important in part because it recognized Church authority and protected his followers from possible accusations of heresy, as had happened to the <a href="/wiki/Waldensians" title="Waldensians">Waldensians</a> decades earlier. Though Pope Innocent initially had his doubts, following a dream in which he saw Francis holding up the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. John Lateran">Basilica of St. John Lateran</a> (the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> of Rome, thus the 'home church' of all Christendom), he decided to endorse Francis's order. This occurred, according to tradition, on 16 April 1210, and constituted the official founding of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan Order">Franciscan Order</a>. The group, then the "Lesser Brothers" (<i>Order of Friars Minor</i> also known as the <i>Franciscan Order</i>), preached on the streets and had no possessions. They were centered in Porziuncola and preached first in Umbria, before expanding throughout Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_religious_orders">Other religious orders</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Other religious orders">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The lesser religious orders which Pope Innocent III approved are the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Ghost" title="Order of the Holy Ghost">Hospitallers of the Holy Ghost</a> on 23 April 1198, the <a href="/wiki/Trinitarians" title="Trinitarians">Trinitarians</a> on 17 December 1198, and the <a href="/wiki/Humiliati" title="Humiliati">Humiliati</a>, in June 1201.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fourth_Council_of_the_Lateran">Fourth Council of the Lateran</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Fourth Council of the Lateran">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 15 November 1215, Pope Innocent III convened the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Fourth Council of the Lateran">Fourth Lateran Council</a> which was considered to be the most important Church council of the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. By its conclusion, it issued seventy reformatory decrees. Among other things, it encouraged creating schools and holding clergy to a higher standard than the laity. Canon 18 forbade clergymen to participate in the practice of the <a href="/wiki/Trial_by_ordeal" title="Trial by ordeal">judicial ordeal</a>, effectively banning its use.<sup id="cite_ref-Pennington_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pennington-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In order to define fundamental doctrines, the council reviewed the nature of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Eucharist" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Eucharist">Holy Eucharist</a>, the ordered annual confession of sins, and prescribed detailed procedures for the election of bishops. The council also mandated a strict lifestyle for clergy. Canon 68 states: Jews and Muslims shall wear a special dress to enable them to be distinguished from Christians so that no Christian shall come to marry them ignorant of who they are.<sup id="cite_ref-je_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-je-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Canon 69 forbade "that Jews be given preferment in public office since this offers them the pretext to vent their wrath against the Christians."<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> It assumes that Jews blaspheme Christ, and therefore, as it would be "too absurd for a blasphemer of Christ to exercise power over Christians",<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Jews should not be appointed to public offices.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Innocent_III_bas-relief_in_the_U.S._House_of_Representatives_chamber.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Innocent_III_bas-relief_in_the_U.S._House_of_Representatives_chamber.jpg/220px-Innocent_III_bas-relief_in_the_U.S._House_of_Representatives_chamber.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="277" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/Innocent_III_bas-relief_in_the_U.S._House_of_Representatives_chamber.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="318" data-file-height="400" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Innocent_III_bas-relief_in_the_U.S._House_of_Representatives_chamber.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Innocent III honored by the <a href="/wiki/U.S._House_of_Representatives" class="mw-redirect" title="U.S. House of Representatives">U.S. House of Representatives</a></div></div></div>
<p>The Council had set the beginning of the Fifth Crusade for 1217, under the direct leadership of the Church. After the Council, in the spring of 1216, Innocent moved to northern Italy in an attempt to reconcile the maritime cities of <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a> and <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> by removing the excommunication cast over Pisa by his predecessor Celestine III and concluding a pact with Genoa.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Innocent III, however, died suddenly at <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a><sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> on 16 July 1216.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore2003288_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore2003288-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Perugia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Perugia">cathedral of Perugia</a>, where his body remained until <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> had it transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Lateran" title="Lateran">Lateran</a> in December 1891.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.encyclopedia.ott.michael.1910-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Innocent is one of two popes (the other being <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a>) among the 23 historical figures depicted in marble relief portraits above the gallery doors of the <a href="/wiki/U.S._House_of_Representatives" class="mw-redirect" title="U.S. House of Representatives">U.S. House of Representatives</a> in honor of their influence on the development of American law.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
