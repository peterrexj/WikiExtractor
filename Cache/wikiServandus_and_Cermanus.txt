					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Servandus and Germanus</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">~305 AD</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">October 23</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Depicted as young soldiers</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Cádiz</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Servandus and Germanus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Servando y San Germán</i>) (d. 305 AD) were <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> martyrs who are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a>. They were killed near <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A1diz" title="Cádiz">Cádiz</a>. Tradition states that they were from <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain" title="Mérida, Spain">Mérida</a>, and sons of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Marcellus_the_Centurion" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Marcellus the Centurion">Saint Marcellus the Centurion</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2009)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> They joined the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Army">Roman Army</a> and were imprisoned after being identified as Christians. They made new converts in prison. During the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, the <i>vicarius</i> of Mérida, Viator, tortured them and imprisoned them once again. Viator then planned to take them to <a href="/wiki/Mauritania_Tingitana" class="mw-redirect" title="Mauritania Tingitana">Mauritania Tingitana</a> and had them walk barefoot and in chains from Mérida to Cádiz. Viator failed to find a boat that could take them and they were decapitated on a hill of the <i>fundus Ursianus</i> in the <i>conventus Gaditanus</i>. The body of Germanus was buried at Mérida and that of Servandus at Cádiz, and then later <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cadizpasion.net_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cadizpasion.net-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The hill of <i>fundus Ursianus</i> has been identified with <i>Cerro de los Mártires</i> (San Fernando) and with <i>Cerro de Torrejosa</i> near Facinas (Tarifa).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Servandus_and_Cermanus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Servandus and Germanus are mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, <a href="/wiki/Usuard" title="Usuard">Usuard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado</a>, as well as the <a href="/wiki/Mozarabic" class="mw-redirect" title="Mozarabic">Mozarabic</a> <a href="/wiki/Breviary" title="Breviary">Breviary</a>, and in the Breviaries of <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, <a href="/wiki/Salamanca" title="Salamanca">Salamanca</a>, among others.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron saints">patron saints</a> of Cádiz (officially since 1619).<sup id="cite_ref-cadizpasion.net_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cadizpasion.net-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On the Sunday closest to October 23 they celebrate in the town of <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando,_C%C3%A1diz" title="San Fernando, Cádiz">San Fernando</a> the festival of Saints Servandus and Germanus, carrying statues of the saints in a procession.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sculptor <a href="/wiki/Luisa_Rold%C3%A1n" title="Luisa Roldán">Luisa Roldán</a> (1650–1704), called <i>La Roldana</i>, made sculptures of these two saints at Cádiz.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
