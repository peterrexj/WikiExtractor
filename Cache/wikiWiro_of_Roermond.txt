<p><b>Wiro of Roermond</b>, also <b>Wiro</b> or <b>Wera of Utrecht</b> (and possibly also the same as <b>Vira of Northumbria</b>; d. c. 700) is a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the 8th century. His feast day is May 8. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wiro_of_Roermond&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wiro was a native of <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon England">Anglo-Saxon England</a>, probably of <a href="/wiki/Northumberland" title="Northumberland">Northumberland</a>. He seems to have been associated with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Willibrord" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Willibrord">Saint Willibrord</a>, and to have been appointed the second <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Utrecht_(695%E2%80%931580)" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Utrecht (695–1580)">bishop of Utrecht</a> in about 739–741.
</p><p>
Between 746 to 747, Wiro was one of eight bishops, along with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">St. Boniface</a>, who wrote a letter to <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelbald_of_Mercia" title="Æthelbald of Mercia">Æthelbald, King of Mercia</a> reproving him for various dissolute and irreligious acts including: stealing ecclesiastical revenue, violating church privileges, imposing forced labour on the clergy, and fornicating with nuns.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The letter implored Æthelbald to take a wife and abandon the sin of lust:</p><blockquote><p>We therefore, beloved son, beseech Your Grace by Christ the son of God and by His coming and by His kingdom, that if it is true that you are continuing in this vice you will amend your life by penitence, purify yourself, and bear in mind how vile a thing it is through lust to change the image of God created in you into the image and likeness of a vicious demon. Remember that you were made king and ruler over many not by your own merits but by the abounding grace of God, and now you are making yourself by your own lust the slave of an evil spirit.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p>He was a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> and preached in the region of the <a href="/wiki/Meuse" title="Meuse">Maas</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>, where his legend associates him with the priest Plechelm and the deacon Otger. Later he founded St. Peter's Abbey on land given to him by <a href="/wiki/Pippin_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin II">Pippin II</a> in the present <a href="/wiki/Sint_Odilienberg" class="mw-redirect" title="Sint Odilienberg">Sint Odilienberg</a> near <a href="/wiki/Roermond" title="Roermond">Roermond</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a> with Otger. Wiro is buried there.
</p><p>It is not clear to what extent his <i>vita</i> is historical. It seems clear that the monastery was founded towards the end of the 7th century.
</p><p>He may be identical with the missionary bishop Vira from Northumbria, mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a>, among others.
</p><p>The Basilica of Saints Wiro, Plechelm and Otger at Sint Odilienberg is dedicated to Wiro and his companions.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wiro_of_Roermond&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wiro9.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Wiro9.jpg/220px-Wiro9.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="217" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Wiro9.jpg/330px-Wiro9.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Wiro9.jpg/440px-Wiro9.jpg 2x" data-file-width="777" data-file-height="767" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wiro9.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Reliquary shrine of Wiro, Plechelm and Otger in St. Odilienberg</div></div></div>
<p>Veneration of Wiro and his two companions began early in Roermond. The monastery he founded was transferred to Roermond, in 1361, accompanied by his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, which were lost during the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a>. They were re-discovered later in the 16th century, and a feast day was celebrated for some years to commemorate the rediscovery. In 1881 the original grave was found in the former abbey, and most of the bones were returned to it.
</p><p>Wiro's feast day is 8 May, but in Roermond 11 May. Since the Middle Ages his skull has been in <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a>, where he was the patron saint of the bishopric. Pilgrimages are still made to his grave in Roermond.
</p>
