<p><b>Sebastian Valfrè</b> (9 March 1629 – 30 January 1710) was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> and a member of the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Saint_Philip_Neri" title="Oratory of Saint Philip Neri">Oratory of Saint Philip Neri</a>. He is called the <b>Apostle of Turin</b> for his long years of service to the people of that city, where he served as the <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a> of the local Oratory for many years.<sup id="cite_ref-TBO_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TBO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sebastian_Valfr%C3%A8&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8,_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8%2C_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg/220px-Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8%2C_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="368" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8%2C_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg/330px-Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8%2C_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8%2C_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg/440px-Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8%2C_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1348" data-file-height="2256" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ritratto_di_Sebastiano_Valfr%C3%A8,_1834-1835_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0009_B.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sebastiano Valfrè heals a cripple. Lithograph, 1834-1835.</div></div></div>
<p>Valfrè was born in <a href="/wiki/Verduno" title="Verduno">Verduno</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Savoy" title="Duchy of Savoy">Duchy of Savoy</a>, now in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Cuneo" title="Province of Cuneo">Province of Cuneo</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> region of Italy. He was born to a family of poor farmers,<sup id="cite_ref-oxford_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxford-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but through struggle and effort he managed to attend various universities in the region, until he graduated from the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Turin" title="University of Turin">University of Turin</a>.
</p><p>Valfrè joined the Oratory of Saint Philip Neri in Turin on 26 May 1651, the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> of their founder, St. <a href="/wiki/Philip_Neri" title="Philip Neri">Philip Neri</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-oxford_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxford-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest on the following 24 February. He was awarded the degree of <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Theology" title="Doctor of Theology">Doctor of Theology</a> in 1656.
</p><p>In his ministry to the people of Turin, Valfrè gained a reputation for caring for the sick and as a <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> with deep insight. He became the confessor for Duke <a href="/wiki/Victor_Amadeus_II" title="Victor Amadeus II">Victor Amadeus II</a> of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Savoy" title="House of Savoy">House of Savoy</a> and many of the members of the <a href="/wiki/Court_(royal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Court (royal)">royal court</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He had a strong concern too, though, for the poor and needy of the kingdom, finding help for the widows and orphans and providing comfort to prisoners, whom he would visit. He eventually was offered the office of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Turin" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Turin">Archbishop of Turin</a>, but declined the appointment.<sup id="cite_ref-TBO_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TBO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Valfre is known for his service to the poor during the famine of 1678-80 and the 17-week siege of Turin during the war between Piemonte and Louis XIV. He is still invoked as patron of military chaplains for his ministry to soldiers during the war.<sup id="cite_ref-nbo_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nbo-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Valfrè had a great devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Shroud_of_Turin" title="Shroud of Turin">Shroud of Turin</a>, and he can be seen in an illustration from the period supervising some repair work done on it in 1694.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He also had a concern for the religious education of the people, personally instructing many in the teachings of the Catholic faith as found in the <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">Catechism</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>. Additionally, out his devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Mother" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Mother">Blessed Mother</a>, he inspired the duke to erect the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Superga" title="Basilica of Superga">Basilica of Superga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-TBO_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TBO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Valfrè's interests and influence were not limited to the duchy. He helped to found the Pontifical Academy of Ecclesiastical Nobles in Rome in 1701, which was established to train diplomats for the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>. Under its current name of the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Ecclesiastical_Academy" title="Pontifical Ecclesiastical Academy">Pontifical Ecclesiastical Academy</a>, it still fulfills that function for the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City_State" class="mw-redirect" title="Vatican City State">Vatican City State</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Valfrè died in Turin on 30 January 1710,<sup id="cite_ref-nbo_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nbo-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and his remains are now preserved in a silver urn in the Oratory Church of Turin. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> on 15 July 1834, with his feast day celebrated by the Congregation of the Oratory on 30 January.<sup id="cite_ref-TBO_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TBO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
