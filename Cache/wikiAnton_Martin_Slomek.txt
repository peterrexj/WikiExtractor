<p><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <b>Anton Martin Slomšek</b> (26 November 1800 – 24 September 1862) was a Slovene <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> prelate who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lavant" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lavant">Bishop of Lavant</a> from 1846 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He served also as an author and poet as well as a staunch advocate of the nation's culture. He served in various parishes as a simple priest prior to his becoming a bishop in which his patriotic activism increased to a higher degree since he advocated writing and the need for education. He penned textbooks for schools including those that he himself opened and he was a vocal supporter of <a href="/wiki/Ecumenism" title="Ecumenism">ecumenism</a> and led efforts to achieve greater dialogue with other faiths with an emphasis on the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification had its origins in the 1930s, when petitions were lodged for a formal cause to commence; this all culminated on 19 September 1999, when <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> presided over the late bishop's beatification in <a href="/wiki/Maribor" title="Maribor">Maribor</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anton_Martin_Slom%C5%A1ek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anton Martin Slomšek was born as the eighth child to the peasants Marko Slomšek and Marija née Zorko on 26 November 1800 in <a href="/wiki/Styria_(Slovenia)" title="Styria (Slovenia)">Styria</a> in <a href="/wiki/Slovenia" title="Slovenia">Slovenia</a>. The priest Blaž Slomšek (1708–1740) was his paternal uncle and Janez Slomšek (1831–1909) was his paternal cousin Gregorius' son.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He underwent his theological and philosophical studies since 1821 (a classmate was the poet <a href="/wiki/France_Pre%C5%A1eren" title="France Prešeren">France Prešeren</a>) in order to enter the priesthood and he was later ordained as such on 8 September 1824 in <a href="/wiki/Klagenfurt" title="Klagenfurt">Klagenfurt</a>. He celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> on 26 September at <a href="/wiki/Olimje" title="Olimje">Olimje</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He first served as a parish chaplain at <a href="/wiki/Bizeljsko" title="Bizeljsko">Bizeljsko</a> and then at <a href="/wiki/Nova_Cerkev" title="Nova Cerkev">Nova Cerkev</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> From 1829 until 1838 he served as the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a> of <a href="/wiki/Seminarians" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminarians">seminarians</a> at Klagenfurt. In 1838 he became the parish priest at <a href="/wiki/Vuzenica" title="Vuzenica">Vuzenica</a>. In 1844 he relocated to <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Andr%C3%A4" title="Sankt Andrä">Sankt Andrä</a> and headed the school in Lavant while also serving as the cathedral canon there.<sup id="cite_ref-EN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He became the parish priest in <a href="/wiki/Celje" title="Celje">Celje</a> in March 1846 just prior to his episcopal appointment. In one of his final appointments, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a>, made Slomšek the new Bishop of Lavant and he received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> a couple of months later in <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>, although he did not celebrate his formal installation until September 1859, when he first moved to his new see.
</p><p>He strove for religious education in schools and for education in <a href="/wiki/Slovenian_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Slovenian language">Slovene</a>; he began writing numerous books on the matter. Slomšek was considered to be an excellent preacher as well as a tireless and modest cleric. The bishop oversaw the construction of new schools and he himself issued textbooks for students and edited others while also publishing his own sermons and episcopal statements.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He also wrote songs and some of which (like the toast "En hribček bom kupil") achieved great social standing and some are still sung at present. Together with <a href="/wiki/Andrej_Einspieler" title="Andrej Einspieler">Andrej Einspieler</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anton_Jane%C5%BEi%C4%8D" title="Anton Janežič">Anton Janežič</a> he was the co-founder of the Hermagoras Association which is the oldest Slovene publishing house.<sup id="cite_ref-EN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He founded movements for greater <a href="/wiki/Ecumenism" title="Ecumenism">ecumenism</a> efforts. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> entrusted him with the mission to renew the religious life in <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monasteries and so he made a series of apostolic visits to see these places. He also invited the Vincentians to settle in his diocese and in 1846 began the publication <i>Drobtinice</i> for his diocese. He also founded the Brotherhood of Saints Cyril and Methodius in 1851 for greater ecumenism with the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Slomšek also supported those in the missions and prompted the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">Spiritual Exercises</a>.
</p><p>Slomšek died on 24 September 1862 after having suffered a series of stomach ailments for some time. His remains are interred in the <a href="/wiki/Maribor_Cathedral" title="Maribor Cathedral">Maribor Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anton_Martin_Slom%C5%A1ek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>The beatification process opened in Maribor in an informative process that investigated his life and his virtues which later received validation on 2 December 1994 from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a>; the postulation sent the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> in 1995 to the C.C.S. for assessment. Historians first approved the cause on 9 January 1996 as did theologians on 2 April 1996 and the C.C.S. on 7 May 1996. His life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> was confirmed on 13 May 1996 and this allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to title him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.
</p><p>The miracle for his beatification was investigated and received C.C.S. validation on 20 June 1996 prior to a medical panel approving it on 4 February 1997; theologians did so also on 20 June 1997 as did the C.C.S. on 1 July 1998. John Paul II approved this miracle on 3 July 1998 and beatified Slomšek in Maribor while in Slovenia on 19 September 1999.
</p>
