<p><b>Phebronia of Nisibis</b>, also known as <b>Phebronia of Sebapte</b>, was a nun at <a href="/wiki/Nisibis" class="mw-redirect" title="Nisibis">Nisibis</a> (modern-day <a href="/wiki/Nusaybin" title="Nusaybin">Nusaybin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>). She suffered persecution under <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, who offered her freedom if she renounced her faith and married his nephew, Lysimachus, who had been leaning towards conversion to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. Febronia refused and was tortured, suffered mutilation and death. Lysimachus, witnessing her suffering, converted.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Phebronia's tomb can be found in a monastery named after her in the village of Himo, near the city of <a href="/wiki/Qamishli" title="Qamishli">Qamishli</a> in <a href="/wiki/Al-Hasakah_Governorate" title="Al-Hasakah Governorate">northeastern Syria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Phebronia is one of the 140 Colonnade saints whose images adorn <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a>. She is known as a Holy Virgin Martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the Coptic Orthodox Church, her feast day is 1 Epip which corresponds to 8 July (<a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>) or 25 June (<a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
