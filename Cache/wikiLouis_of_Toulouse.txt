<p><b>Saint Louis of Toulouse</b> (9 February 1274 – 19 August 1297), also known as <b>Louis of Anjou</b>, was a Neapolitan prince  of the <a href="/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou" title="Capetian House of Anjou">Capetian House of Anjou</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">Catholic bishop</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_of_Toulouse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Louis was born in <a href="/wiki/Brignoles" title="Brignoles">Brignoles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a> (or in Italy, at <a href="/wiki/Nocera_Inferiore" title="Nocera Inferiore">Nocera</a>, where he spent a part of his early life), the second son of King <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_Naples" title="Charles II of Naples">Charles II of Naples</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maria_Arpad_of_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Maria Arpad of Hungary">Mary of Hungary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMusto200378_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMusto200378-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father, Charles, became <a href="/wiki/King_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Naples">king of Naples</a> in 1285 and was taken prisoner in Italy, during the war with King <a href="/wiki/Peter_III_of_Aragon" title="Peter III of Aragon">Peter III of Aragon</a> that followed the <a href="/wiki/Sicilian_Vespers" title="Sicilian Vespers">Sicilian Vespers</a>. Charles obtained his own freedom by giving over his three sons as hostages.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEToynbee192952_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEToynbee192952-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Louis and his brothers were taken to <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a>, where they were placed under the care of <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan friars</a> for their education and held for seven years.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrieco2013137_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrieco2013137-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Impressed by one of the friars in particular, Arnauld de Villeneuve, Louis took up the study of philosophy and theology.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJohnson2007351_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson2007351-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Though still held in captivity, Louis was made <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lyon">archbishop of Lyon</a> as soon as he reached his majority. When his older brother died of plague in 1295,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPryds200026_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPryds200026-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Louis also became <a href="/wiki/Heir_apparent" title="Heir apparent">heir apparent</a> to his father's kingdom; however, when he was freed that same year, Louis went to Rome and gave up all claims to the Angevin inheritance in favor of his brother <a href="/wiki/Robert,_King_of_Naples" title="Robert, King of Naples">Robert</a> and announced that instead he would take the Franciscan vows of <a href="/wiki/Poverty,_chastity,_and_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Poverty, chastity, and obedience">poverty, chastity, and obedience</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArnold2014290_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArnold2014290-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 5 February 1297, Louis was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Toulouse">Bishop of Toulouse</a> by Boniface VIII,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETolan2009259_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETolan2009259-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> where his uncle <a href="/wiki/Alphonse,_Count_of_Poitiers" title="Alphonse, Count of Poitiers">Alphonse</a> had until recently been count, but had died in 1271 leaving no heir. Here Louis stood in an ambivalently dynastic and ecclesiastical position, in a territory between Provence and Aquitaine that was essential to Angevin interests. Despite the princely standing that had won him this important appointment at the age of about 22, Louis rapidly gained a reputation for serving the poor, feeding the hungry, and ignoring his own needs. After just six months, however, apparently exhausted by his labors, he abandoned the position of bishop. Shortly thereafter he died at <a href="/wiki/Brignoles" title="Brignoles">Brignoles</a> of a fever, possibly <a href="/wiki/Typhoid" class="mw-redirect" title="Typhoid">typhoid</a>, at age 23.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETolan2009259_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETolan2009259-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two music theory treatises, <i>De musicae commendacione</i> and <i>Sentencia in musica sonora subiecti</i>, are sometimes attributed to him, but are now thought to be the work of <a href="/wiki/Lodewijk_Heyligen" title="Lodewijk Heyligen">Lodewijk Heyligen</a> (1304–1361).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGiger2001?_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGiger2001?-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_of_Toulouse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg/220px-31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="403" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg/330px-31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg/440px-31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2808" data-file-height="5144" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:31_-_Toulouse_-_Basilique_Saint-Sernin_-_Crosse_de_Saint-Louis_de_Toulouse_PalissyPM31000771.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Basilique_Saint-Sernin_de_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilique Saint-Sernin de Toulouse">Basilique Saint-Sernin de Toulouse</a> - Lacrosse of Louis of Toulouse</div></div></div>
<p>Procedures for the canonization of Louis were quickly urged.  His case was promoted by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_V" title="Pope Clement V">Pope Clement V</a> in 1307, and he was canonized by John XXII on 7 April 1317<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrunner2011231_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunner2011231-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> with the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bull</a> <i>Sol oriens</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECusatoGeltner2009139_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECusatoGeltner2009139-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> His brother <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Anjou" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert of Anjou">Robert</a> at Naples who owed his crown to Louis commissioned a great altarpiece from <a href="/wiki/Simone_Martini" title="Simone Martini">Simone Martini</a>, depicting Louis being crowned by angels as he simultaneously crowned Robert.
</p><p>The cult of Saint Louis of Toulouse took hold in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002326_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002326-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> His nephew <a href="/wiki/Charles_I_of_Hungary" title="Charles I of Hungary">Charles I of Hungary</a> (1307–1342) exalted his image and veneration, consecrating churches and a monastery in the settlement of <a href="/wiki/Lipova,_Arad" title="Lipova, Arad">Lippa</a> in his honor, and giving the name of the saint to his eldest son, <a href="/wiki/Louis_I_of_Hungary" title="Louis I of Hungary">Louis I of Hungary</a> (1342–1382).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002326_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002326-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Louis of Toulouse was not otherwise widely venerated in the rest of Europe, but the Franciscans embraced him, keeping his day in their calendar and removing his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> in 1423 to <a href="/wiki/Valencia_(city_in_Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="Valencia (city in Spain)">Valencia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEToynbee1929231_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEToynbee1929231-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> where he was made its patron saint.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Chronicon Pictum, Saint Louis of Toulouse, Louis of Anjou, Bishop of Toulouse, House of Árpád, medieval, Hungarian chronicle, book, illumination, illustration, history" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg/220px-K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="259" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg/330px-K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg/440px-K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg 2x" data-file-width="622" data-file-height="732" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:K%C3%A9pes_kr%C3%B3nika_-_140.oldal_-_Toulouse-i_Szent_Lajos.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Louis of Toulouse. He raises his right hand in blessing and the royal crown lies at his feet, symbolizing the fact that he renounced the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a> by joining the <a href="/wiki/Christian_Church" title="Christian Church">Church</a>. The Hungarian <a href="/wiki/Capetian_House_of_Anjou" title="Capetian House of Anjou">Anjou</a> coat of arms is on his breast. (<a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Pictum" title="Chronicon Pictum">Chronicon Pictum</a>, 1358)</div></div></div>
<p>Louis can be recognized in <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> as a young bishop, usually wearing a brown or grey Franciscan habit under his cope.  The cope is usually decorated with the French fleur-de-lys.  Sometimes there is a discarded crown by his feet.
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Polyphony" title="Polyphony">polyphonic</a> <a href="/wiki/Motet" title="Motet">motet</a>, <i>Flos</i>/<i>Celsa</i>/<i>Quam magnus pontifex</i>, was written in honor of Louis's canonization in 1317. The piece appears anonymously in the <a href="/wiki/Ivrea_Codex" title="Ivrea Codex">Ivrea Codex</a> and has been attributed by modern scholars to <a href="/wiki/Philippe_de_Vitry" title="Philippe de Vitry">Philippe de Vitry</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Luis_Obispo_de_Tolosa" title="Mission San Luis Obispo de Tolosa">Mission San Luis Obispo de Tolosa</a>, a Franciscan mission in California founded in 1772, is named for him as are the surrounding city and county of <a href="/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo,_California" title="San Luis Obispo, California">San Luis Obispo, California</a>. <a href="/wiki/MERU,_Holland" class="mw-redirect" title="MERU, Holland">Kolleg St Ludwig</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vlodrop" title="Vlodrop">Vlodrop</a>, the Netherlands, was dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Max_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Max-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> A Vlodrop hotel is also named for Saint Ludwig.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
