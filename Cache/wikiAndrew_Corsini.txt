<p><b>Andrea Corsini</b> (30 November 1302 – 6 January 1373 or 1374<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-hunter_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunter-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> and professed member from the <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelites</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Fiesole" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Fiesole">Bishop of Fiesole</a> from 1349 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Corsini led a wild and dissolute life until a rebuke from his mother moved him to go to the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Carmine,_Florence" title="Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence">Santa Maria del Carmine</a> church where he resolved to join the Carmelites as a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>. He exercised various roles in the order, until reluctantly he accepted his episcopal position. In order to accept that position, he imposed greater <a href="/wiki/Mortification_in_Roman_Catholic_teaching" class="mw-redirect" title="Mortification in Roman Catholic teaching">mortifications</a> upon himself than that required by the order, and dedicated himself to the plight of the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Devotion to the late bishop became so profound after his death that miracles were reported at his tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-BL_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BL-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The longstanding and popular devotion to Corsini led to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a> confirming his beatification on 21 April 1440 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> canonizing him as a saint on 22 April 1629.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Corsini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Andrew Corsini was born in <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> on 30 November 1302 into the noble and illustrious <a href="/wiki/Corsini" title="Corsini">Corsini</a> family,<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> one of twelve children born to Nicholas Corsini and Peregrina (some sources suggest Gemma) degli Stracciabende. He was named in honor of <a href="/wiki/Andrew_the_Apostle" title="Andrew the Apostle">Saint Andrew</a> whose feastday it was.<sup id="cite_ref-BL_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BL-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Before his birth, his parents dedicated him to God, under the protection of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin">Blessed Virgin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fnd_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fnd-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was wild in his youth; extravagance and vice were normal to him and it pained his devout mother. His parents severely rebuked him for his behavior, and he resolved to amend his ways and try to live up to their expectations. He went to the Carmelite monastery at the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Carmine,_Florence" title="Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence">Santa Maria del Carmine church</a> to consider what course to take and despite the entreaties of his dissolute friends, decided to become a friar.<sup id="cite_ref-fnd_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fnd-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carmelite_friar">Carmelite friar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Corsini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Carmelite friar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Corsini joined the <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelites</a> in Florence in 1318 for his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> and began a life of great <a href="/wiki/Mortification_in_Roman_Catholic_teaching" class="mw-redirect" title="Mortification in Roman Catholic teaching">mortification</a>. He was ordained to the priesthood in 1328 and said his first Mass in a hermitage so as to avoid the customary family celebrations.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Corsini began preaching in Florence, and was then sent for his studies to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a> and later to <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a>, where he resided in with his cousin Cardinal Pietro Corsini. He returned to Florence in 1332 and was chosen as prior of his convent.<sup id="cite_ref-fnd_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fnd-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He became known as the "Apostle of Florence". In 1348 he was appointed as the order's Tuscan Provincial during the General Chapter meeting in <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop">Bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Corsini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 13 October 1349, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VI" title="Pope Clement VI">Pope Clement VI</a> appointed him <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Fiesole" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Fiesole">Bishop of Fiesole</a>. Upon learning of this appointment, the reluctant Corsini went into hiding.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> An inscription on his tomb states that "he was snatched from the Carmel to the church and the miter of Fiesole". This perhaps gave rise to the legend that he fled, and that a child discovered him at the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/charterhouse" class="extiw" title="wikt:charterhouse">charterhouse</a> at <a href="/wiki/Enna" title="Enna">Enna</a>, and he later accepted the nomination as bishop as the result of a vision.
</p><p>He redoubled his austerities as bishop, wearing a <a href="/wiki/Hair_shirt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hair shirt">hair shirt</a> and sleeping on a bed of vine-branches. At <a href="/wiki/Fiesole" title="Fiesole">Fiesole</a>, just northeast of Florence, he gained a reputation as a peacemaker between rival political factions and for his care of the poor. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_V" title="Pope Urban V">Pope Urban V</a> sent him to <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> to heal the breach between the nobles and the people.<sup id="cite_ref-BL_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BL-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> "His family connections made him acceptable to the nobility and his life of poverty endeared him to the poor and he did succeed in bringing peace."<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Corsini appointed two vicars to aid him in governing his diocese, and enforced discipline amongst the diocesan priests.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A number of miraculous healings were attributed to his intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-sanctoral_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sanctoral-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was reported that in 1372 or 1373,<sup id="cite_ref-hunter_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunter-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> as he celebrated <a href="/wiki/Midnight_Mass" title="Midnight Mass">Midnight Mass</a> on <a href="/wiki/Christmas_Eve" title="Christmas Eve">Christmas Eve</a>, that the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin">Blessed Virgin</a> appeared to him and told him he would leave this world on the <a href="/wiki/Epiphany_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Epiphany (Christian)">Three Kings' feast</a>. It came to pass that he fell ill on <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a> night and died as foretold, on 6 January 1373 or 1374.<sup id="cite_ref-hunter_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hunter-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> His remains were moved to Florence in the evening of 2 February 1374 and were later found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a> upon exhumation in 1385. The location of his burial was damaged in 1771 but his remains were left undisturbed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Corsini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giovan_Battista_Foggini,_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini,_1683-86,_01.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Giovan_Battista_Foggini%2C_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini%2C_1683-86%2C_01.JPG/200px-Giovan_Battista_Foggini%2C_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini%2C_1683-86%2C_01.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Giovan_Battista_Foggini%2C_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini%2C_1683-86%2C_01.JPG/300px-Giovan_Battista_Foggini%2C_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini%2C_1683-86%2C_01.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Giovan_Battista_Foggini%2C_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini%2C_1683-86%2C_01.JPG/400px-Giovan_Battista_Foggini%2C_urna_di_sant%27andrea_corsini%2C_1683-86%2C_01.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3292" data-file-height="2031" /></a><figcaption>Tomb.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Miracles so multiplied at his death that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a> permitted a public devotion to him, although it was not confirmed until later.<sup id="cite_ref-sanctoral_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sanctoral-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Eugene_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a> beatified Andrew Corsini on 21 April 1440. Among the miracles attributed to Corsini's intervention was the Florentine victory over the Milanese at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Anghiari" title="Battle of Anghiari">Battle of Anghiari</a> on 29 June 1440. Petitions were lodged in 1465 and 1466 to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_II" title="Pope Paul II">Pope Paul II</a> requesting the canonization, and the pope appointed a commission to investigate the matter, though it came to no conclusion. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> canonized Corsini on 22 April 1629.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1675 after his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> the members of the Corsini house had the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Carmine,_Florence#Corsini_Chapel" title="Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence">Corsini Chapel</a> built in the Carmelite church of Santa Maria del Carmine as a more suitable resting place for his remains. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> - born Lorenzo Corsini - erected in the Roman <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Saint John Lateran">Basilica of Saint John Lateran</a> a magnificent chapel dedicated to his kinsman.<sup id="cite_ref-OC_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1702 or 1703 a statue in his honor was commissioned and placed along the colonnade in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.
</p>
