<p><b>Swithun Wells</b> (c. 1536 – 10 December 1591) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who was executed during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a>. Wells was a country gentleman and one time schoolmaster whose family sheltered hunted priests. He himself often arranged passage from one safehouse to another. His home in Gray's Inn Lane (where he was hanged) was known to welcome <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusants</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Swithun_Wells&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:292px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg/290px-Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="193" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg/435px-Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg/580px-Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="427" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Brambridge_House_-_geograph.org.uk_-_63994.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Colden_Common#Brambridge_House" title="Colden Common">Brambridge House</a>, Hampshire,  birthplace of Swithun Wells</div></div></div>
<p>Wells was born at Brambridge House, <a href="/wiki/Hampshire" title="Hampshire">Hampshire</a> in 1536, of a wealthy country family,<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was christened with the name of the local saint and bishop <a href="/wiki/Swithun" title="Swithun">Swithun</a>. He was the youngest of the five or six sons of Thomas Wells of <a href="/wiki/Colden_Common" title="Colden Common">Brambridge</a>, by Mary, daughter of John Mompesson. During the Reformation, his family contributed to the secret funerals of Catholics at the local cemetery, and their house was a place of refuge for priests. Wells was well-educated, a poet, musician, and sportsman. Among his travels, he had been to Rome, and had a working knowledge of Italian.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>At one time he was tutor to the household of the <a href="/wiki/Henry_Wriothesley,_2nd_Earl_of_Southampton" title="Henry Wriothesley, 2nd Earl of Southampton">Earl of Southampton</a>, and was for many years a schoolmaster at <a href="/wiki/Monkton_Farleigh" title="Monkton Farleigh">Monkton Farleigh</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>. In 1582 he came under suspicion for his popish sympathies and on 25 May 1582, the Privy Council ordered a search to be made for him. It was at that time that he gave up the school. During this period, he attended <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestant</a> services, but in 1583, was reconciled to the Catholic Church. He actively supported priests, organizing their often dangerous journeys from one safehouse to another. In 1585 he went to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, where he purchased a house in <a href="/wiki/Gray%27s_Inn_Road" title="Gray&#39;s Inn Road">Gray's Inn Lane</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainwright_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainwright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Their home was a centre of hospitality for recusants.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrests">Arrests</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Swithun_Wells&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Arrests">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In June 1586, he was arrested with seminarians <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Rawlins" title="Alexander Rawlins">Alexander Rawlins</a> and Christopher Dryland and imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a>, but was released 4 July when his nephew posted bail. On 9 August 1586, he was examined for supposed complicity in the <a href="/wiki/Babington_Plot" title="Babington Plot">Babington Plot</a>, and on 30 November 1586, he was discharged from the Fleet prison. At one point he went to Rome on a mission for the <a href="/wiki/Henry_Wriothesley,_3rd_Earl_of_Southampton" title="Henry Wriothesley, 3rd Earl of Southampton">Earl of Southampton</a>, but he returned to England to work in the English Catholic underground. He was again examined 5 March 1587, and on this occasion speaks of the well known recusant, George Cotton of <a href="/wiki/Warblington" title="Warblington">Warblington</a>, Hampshire, as his cousin.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainwright_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainwright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1591, <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Gennings" title="Edmund Gennings">Edmund Gennings</a> was saying Mass at Wells's house, when the priest-hunter <a href="/wiki/Richard_Topcliffe" title="Richard Topcliffe">Richard Topcliffe</a> burst in with his officers. The congregation, not wishing the Mass to be interrupted, held the door and beat back the officers until the service was finished, after which they all surrendered peacefully. Wells was not present at the time, but his wife was; she and Gennings were arrested along with another priest by the name of <a href="/wiki/Polydore_Plasden" title="Polydore Plasden">Polydore Plasden</a>, and three laymen named John Mason, <a href="/wiki/Sidney_Hodgson" class="mw-redirect" title="Sidney Hodgson">Sidney Hodgson</a>, and Brian Lacey. Wells was immediately arrested and imprisoned on his return.<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was charged under the 1585 (27 Elizabeth Cap 2) <i>Act Against Jesuits, Seminary Priests and Other Such Disobedient Subjects</i>. At his trial, he said that he had not been present at the Mass, but wished he had been.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Swithun_Wells&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Wells was sentenced to die by <a href="/wiki/Hanging" title="Hanging">hanging</a>, and a gibbet was erected outside his own house on 10 December 1591<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On his way to the scaffold Swithun caught sight of an old friend in the crowd and said to him, "Goodbye my dear.  Goodbye to our nice hunting companies.  Now I have something much more important to do."<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After he had climbed the ladder, Topcliffe called for a minister, who attempted to persuade Wells to confess to following false doctrine and traitorous priests. Wells turned and responded, "although I heard you say somewhat, yet it is but one doctor's opinion, and he also a very young one." The young minister was so daunted that he had no reply.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Topcliffe then baited Wells, saying that "Dog-bolt Papists! you follow the Pope and his <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">Bulls</a>; believe me, I think some bulls begot you".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Wells responded in kind: "if we have bulls to our fathers, thou hast a cow to thy mother".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He then immediately begged pardon and asked Topcliffe not to provoke him when he was trying to focus on other matters.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Wells was buried in St Andrew's Churchyard in Holborn.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His wife, Alice, was reprieved, but died in prison in 1602. Swithun's eldest brother Gilbert, suffered much both personally and economically for his faith. He died as a known recusant after losing the property, but it was later restored to the family by <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_England" title="Charles II of England">King Charles II</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Swithun_Wells&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Swithun_Wells_Church,_Allington_Lane,_Fair_Oak_(June_2019)_(5).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/St_Swithun_Wells_Church%2C_Allington_Lane%2C_Fair_Oak_%28June_2019%29_%285%29.JPG/220px-St_Swithun_Wells_Church%2C_Allington_Lane%2C_Fair_Oak_%28June_2019%29_%285%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="135" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/St_Swithun_Wells_Church%2C_Allington_Lane%2C_Fair_Oak_%28June_2019%29_%285%29.JPG/330px-St_Swithun_Wells_Church%2C_Allington_Lane%2C_Fair_Oak_%28June_2019%29_%285%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/St_Swithun_Wells_Church%2C_Allington_Lane%2C_Fair_Oak_%28June_2019%29_%285%29.JPG/440px-St_Swithun_Wells_Church%2C_Allington_Lane%2C_Fair_Oak_%28June_2019%29_%285%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2600" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Swithun_Wells_Church,_Allington_Lane,_Fair_Oak_(June_2019)_(5).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Swithun Wells Church in <a href="/wiki/Fair_Oak" title="Fair Oak">Fair Oak</a>, Hampshire</div></div></div>
<p>Swithun Wells was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 15 December 1929, and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 25 October 1970, as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His feast day, along with that of the other thirty-nine martyrs, was 25 October, but since the new calendar for England and Wales was approved by the Vatican in 2000, the Saints and Blessed Martyrs from the Reformation Period have been celebrated on 4 May as "The English Martyrs".  This date coincides with the Anglican Church of England celebration of the English Saints and Martyrs of the Reformation Era.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>St Swithun Wells Catholic Primary School is located in <a href="/wiki/Chandler%27s_Ford" title="Chandler&#39;s Ford">Chandler's Ford</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Eastleigh" title="Eastleigh">Eastleigh</a>, Hampshire.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  St Swithun Wells Church opened in nearby <a href="/wiki/Fair_Oak" title="Fair Oak">Fair Oak</a> in 1978,<sup id="cite_ref-WR-75023_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-75023-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and gives its name to the wider Catholic Parish of St Swithun Wells, which covers six Catholic churches in south and west Hampshire.<sup id="cite_ref-DioPompey-PB-57_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DioPompey-PB-57-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
