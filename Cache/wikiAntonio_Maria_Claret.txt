<p><b>Anthony Mary Claret</b>, CMF (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Antoni Maria Claret i Clarà</i>; Spanish: <i lang="es">Antonio María Claret y Clarà</i>; December 23, 1807 – October 24, 1870) was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Spain" title="Catholic Church in Spain">Spanish Catholic</a> archbishop and missionary, and was <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> of <a href="/wiki/Isabella_II_of_Spain" title="Isabella II of Spain">Isabella II of Spain</a>. He founded the <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> of Missionary Sons of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, commonly called the <a href="/wiki/Claretians" title="Claretians">Claretians</a>.
</p><p>In addition to the Claretians, which in the early 21st century had over 450 houses and 3100 members, with missions in five continents, Claret founded or drew up the rules of several communities of religious sisters. His zealous life and the wonders he wrought, both before and after his death, testified to his sanctity. Claret is the patron saint of weavers.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><b>Anthony Maria Claret i Clarà</b> was born in <a href="/wiki/Sallent_de_Llobregat" class="mw-redirect" title="Sallent de Llobregat">Sallent</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Comarques_of_Catalonia" title="Comarques of Catalonia">county</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bages" title="Bages">Bages</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Barcelona" title="Province of Barcelona">Province of Barcelona</a>, on December 23, 1807, the fifth of the eleven children of Juan and Josefa Claret. His father was a woollen manufacturer. As a child he enjoyed pilgrimages to the nearby Shrine of Our Lady of Fussimanya.<sup id="cite_ref-clarpub_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clarpub-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Claret received an elementary education in his native village, and at the age of twelve became a <a href="/wiki/Weaver_(occupation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Weaver (occupation)">weaver</a>. At the age of eighteen, he went to Barcelona to specialize in his trade as a <a href="/wiki/Jacquard_loom" class="mw-redirect" title="Jacquard loom">Jacquard loom</a> programmer, and remained there until he was 20 years old. Meanwhile, he devoted his spare time to study and became proficient in Latin, French and <a href="/wiki/Engraving" title="Engraving">engraving</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Frightened that his love of programming was causing him to become obsessed and burned out, and recognizing a call to <a href="/wiki/Religious_life" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious life">religious life</a>, he left Barcelona. He wished to become a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> but finally entered the diocesan seminary at <a href="/wiki/Vic" title="Vic">Vic</a> in 1829, and was ordained on June 13, 1835, on the feast of St. <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">Anthony of Padua</a>. He received a <a href="/wiki/Benefice" title="Benefice">benefice</a> in his native parish, where he continued to study theology until 1839; but as missionary work strongly appealed to him, he proceeded to Rome. There he entered the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> but had to leave due to ill health.  He then returned to Spain and exercised his pastoral ministry in <a href="/wiki/Viladrau" title="Viladrau">Viladrau</a> and <a href="/wiki/Girona" title="Girona">Girona</a>, attracting notice through his efforts on behalf of the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In an area despoiled by the <a href="/wiki/Carlist_Wars" title="Carlist Wars">Carlist civil war</a>, he added the practice of rustic medicine to his other endeavors.
</p><p>Recalled by his superiors to Vic, Claret was sent as Apostolic Missionary throughout <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a> which had suffered from French invasions. He travelled from one mission to the next on foot. An eloquent preacher fluent in the <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan language</a>, he drew people from miles around. After a lengthy time in the pulpit, he would spend long hours in the confessional. He was said to have had the gift of discernment of consciences. In 1848 Claret's life was threatened by anti-clerical enemies and he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a> where he gave retreats for 15 months. His missions were so well attended that he often preached from an improvised pulpit in the plaza before the church.<sup id="cite_ref-clarpub_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clarpub-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Claretians">Claretians</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Claretians">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On his return to Spain, he established the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Missionary_Sons_of_the_Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Missionary Sons of the Immaculate Heart of Mary">Congregation of the Missionary Sons of the Immaculate Heart of Mary</a> (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20060425194428/http://www.claretians.org/site/PageServer?pagename=clr_homepage">The Claretians</a>) on July 16, 1849, the Feast of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Mount_Carmel" title="Our Lady of Mount Carmel">Our Lady of Mount Carmel</a>, and founded the great religious library at Barcelona which was called "Librería Religiosa" (now "Librería Claret").<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> gave approval to the congregation on December 22, 1865.<sup id="cite_ref-clusa_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clusa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop">Archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_(Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_%28Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid%29.jpg/220px-San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_%28Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="277" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_%28Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid%29.jpg/330px-San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_%28Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_%28Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid%29.jpg/440px-San_Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Claret_%28Museo_Nacional_del_Romanticismo_de_Madrid%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="797" data-file-height="1004" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of Archbishop Anthony Mary Claret by <a href="/wiki/Luis_de_Madrazo" title="Luis de Madrazo">Luis de Madrazo</a> (<a href="/wiki/Museum_of_Romanticism_(Madrid)" title="Museum of Romanticism (Madrid)">Museum of Romanticism</a>, <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>)</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>, at the request of the Spanish crown (Queen-regnant Isabella II of Spain), appointed him <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Santiago_de_Cuba" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Santiago de Cuba">Archbishop of Santiago de Cuba</a>, in 1849. He was consecrated at Vic in October 1850. Before he embarked, he made three separate pilgrimages: to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Pillar" title="Our Lady of the Pillar">Our Lady of the Pillar</a>, patroness of Spain; to the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_of_Montserrat" title="Virgin of Montserrat">Virgin of Montserrat</a>, patroness of Catalonia; and to Our Lady of Fussimanya, near his home village.<sup id="cite_ref-clarpub_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clarpub-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Santiago <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> was reorganized, clerical discipline strengthened, and over 9,000 marriages validated within the first two years of his arrival. He erected a hospital and numerous schools. Three times he made a <a href="/wiki/Canonical_visitation" title="Canonical visitation">visitation</a> of the entire diocese, giving local missions incessantly.<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Among his great initiatives were trade or vocational schools for disadvantaged children and credit unions for the use of the poor. He wrote books about rural spirituality and agricultural methods, which he himself tested first. On August 25, 1855, he founded the Religious of Mary Immaculate together with <a href="/wiki/Maria_Antonia_Paris" title="Maria Antonia Paris">Maria Antonia Paris</a>.  The congregation was considered as the first women religious institute in Cuba. He also visited jails and hospitals, defended the oppressed and denounced racism. His work stirred up opposition and at <a href="/wiki/Holgu%C3%ADn" title="Holguín">Holguín</a> his cheek was stabbed by a would-be assassin, who was associated with <a href="/wiki/Freemasonry" title="Freemasonry">freemasonry</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-clusa_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clusa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Claret obtained a commutation of the assailant's death sentence to a term in prison.<sup id="cite_ref-Miller_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miller-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Claret was an exceptional preacher with incredible charisms: witnesses said his body would become transfigured while preaching or in prayer, he would levitate up to six feet off the ground at times in front of credible witnesses, he stopped a series of earthquakes in Cuba by kneeling on the ground and placing his palms to the earth while uttering prayers, he could calm terrible storms by raising a hand to the sky and blessing the storm clouds, he experienced apparitions of both Jesus and Mary, and was even seen walking on water. In addition, a supernatural light that radiated from his body while he was saying Mass was seen by many. It was so intense at times that one witness said he saw the light radiate from his body behind the altar all the way to the sacristy. Queen Isabella of Spain even produced a written statement solemnly declaring that she had personally witnessed this phenomenon.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On September 3, 1859, Claret claimed he had heard Jesus tell him that there were three great evils that were descending upon mankind: the first was a series of enormous, horrifying wars; the second, the four powerful demons of pleasure, love of money, false reasoning and a will separated from God. Finally, in addition to a grievance he had with certain Christians who had left the church, Jesus told Claret that the third chastisement would be brought about by Communism, an unknown, fledgling movement that only had hundreds of followers at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Padre_Claret,_de_Jean_Laurent.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Padre_Claret%2C_de_Jean_Laurent.jpg/220px-Padre_Claret%2C_de_Jean_Laurent.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="366" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Padre_Claret%2C_de_Jean_Laurent.jpg/330px-Padre_Claret%2C_de_Jean_Laurent.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/58/Padre_Claret%2C_de_Jean_Laurent.jpg 2x" data-file-width="394" data-file-height="655" /></a><figcaption>Photograph of Saint Anthony Mary Claret, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1862</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>Two years after the September 3 warning, during benediction of the Blessed Sacrament in August 1861, Claret again said he was warned interiorly by Jesus that Communism was to be the great foe of humanity. The remedy, Jesus told him, would include devotion to the Blessed Sacrament (also known as the Eucharist) and the Rosary.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In February 1857, Claret was recalled to Spain by Queen Isabella II, who made him her confessor. He obtained permission to resign his Cuban see and was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Titular_see" title="Titular see">titular see</a> of <a href="/wiki/Traianoupoli" title="Traianoupoli">Trajanopolis</a>. His influence was now directed solely to help the poor and to propagate learning; he lived frugally and took up his residence in an Italian hospice. For nine years he was <a href="/wiki/Rector_(academia)" title="Rector (academia)">rector</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Escorial" class="mw-redirect" title="Escorial">Escorial</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastic</a> school, where he established a scientific laboratory, a museum of natural history, a library, college and schools of music and languages. In 1868, a new revolution dethroned the queen and sent her with her family into exile. Claret's life was also in danger, so he accompanied her to France.<sup id="cite_ref-clusa_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clusa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This gave him the opportunity to preach the Gospel in Paris. He stayed with them for a while, then went to Rome where he was received by Pope Pius IX.
</p><p>He continued his popular missions and distribution of  books wherever he went in accompanying the Spanish Court. When Isabella recognized the new, secular government of a united Italy, he left the Court and hastened to take his place by the side of the pope. At the latter's command, however, he returned to Madrid with <a href="/wiki/Faculty_(instrument)" class="mw-redirect" title="Faculty (instrument)">faculties</a> for absolving the queen from the censures she had incurred.<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Last_years">Last years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Last years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1869 he went to Rome to prepare for the <a href="/wiki/First_Vatican_Council" title="First Vatican Council">First Vatican Council</a>. Owing to failing health he withdrew to <a href="/wiki/Prades,_Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es-Orientales" title="Prades, Pyrénées-Orientales">Prada de Conflent</a> in the French Pyrenees, where he was still harassed by his Spanish enemies; shortly afterwards he retired to the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> abbey at <a href="/wiki/Fontfroide" class="mw-redirect" title="Fontfroide">Fontfroide</a>, <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>, southern France, where he died on October 24, 1870, aged 62.
</p><p>His remains were buried in the Catalan city of Vic, in the County of <a href="/wiki/Osona" title="Osona">Osona</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anthony Mary Claret wrote 144 books. By his sermons and writings he contributed greatly to bring about the revival of the <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan language</a>, although most of his works were published in Spanish, especially during his stay in Cuba and Madrid. His <i>Autobiography of Anthony Mary Claret</i> is an <a href="/wiki/Autobiography" title="Autobiography">autobiography</a> that, according to the introduction, was written from 1861 to 1862 at the command of Anthony's <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a>, Joseph Xifré.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVinas19763_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVinas19763-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The autobiography is divided into chapters. Chapter XVIII is about Anthony's "inner enlightenments." In this chapter, Anthony claims to have received <a href="/wiki/Private_revelation" title="Private revelation">private revelation</a>. In section 694, he claims that on August 26, 1861, he experienced an <a href="/wiki/Eucharistic_miracle" title="Eucharistic miracle">Eucharistic miracle</a>. In section 695, he claims that, the next day, he had a vision of Jesus speaking of the future.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVinas1976114–115_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVinas1976114–115-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His printed works number more than one hundred, including <i>"La escala de Jacob"</i>; <i>"Máximas de moral la más pura"</i>; <i>"Avisos"</i>;
<i>"Catecismo explicado con láminas"</i>; <i>"La llave de oro"</i>;
<i>"Selectos panegíricos"</i> (11 volumes); <i>"Sermones de misión"</i> (3 volumes); <i>"Misión de la mujer"</i>; <i>"Vida de Sta. Mónica"</i>; <i>"La Virgen del Pilar y los Francmasones."</i>
</p><p>In addition to the Claretians, which in the early 21st century had over 450 houses and 3100 members, with missions in five continents, Archbishop Claret founded or drew up the rules of several communities of religious sisters.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His zealous life and the wonders he wrought, both before and after his death, testified to his sanctity. Information was sought in 1887 and he was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1899. His relics were transferred to the mission house at Vic in 1897, at which time his heart was found <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>. His grave is visited by many pilgrims.
</p><p>Anthony Mary Claret was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in Rome by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on February 24, 1934. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> 16 years later by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on May 7, 1950.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> included him in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1960, and fixed his feast on October 23, where it remained for nine years until the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the calendar moved it to the day of his death, October 24, which had been the feast of Saint <a href="/wiki/Raphael_(archangel)" title="Raphael (archangel)">Raphael the Archangel</a> since 1921.
</p><p>Anthony Mary Claret is the patron saint of weavers.<sup id="cite_ref-Miller_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miller-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Educational_legacy">Educational legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Mary_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Educational legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Many educational institutions ranging from kindergarten to undergraduate school are named after Claret and run by the Claretians in Europe, South America, Africa and Asia. They are located in Catalonia (Barcelona, <a href="/wiki/Valls" title="Valls">Valls</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sabadell" title="Sabadell">Sabadell</a>), rest of Spain (<a href="/wiki/Madrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Valencia, Spain">Valencia</a>), <a href="/wiki/Colombia" title="Colombia">Colombia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Cali" title="Cali">Cali</a>), <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Republic" title="Dominican Republic">Dominican Republic</a> (<a href="/wiki/Santo_Domingo" title="Santo Domingo">Santo Domingo</a>), <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a> (<a href="/wiki/Trujillo,_Peru" href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Bah%C3%ADa_Blanca" title="Bahía Blanca">Bahía Blanca</a>), <a href="/wiki/Venezuela" title="Venezuela">Venezuela</a> (<a </p>
