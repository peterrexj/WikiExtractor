<p><b>St. Tassac</b> (also <b>Tassach</b>; died c. AD 497) was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> saint, born in the first decade of the 5th century, died c. 497 and whose feast day falls on the 14 April.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tassac&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Tassach%27s_church,_Raholp_(2)_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/St_Tassach%27s_church%2C_Raholp_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg/220px-St_Tassach%27s_church%2C_Raholp_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/St_Tassach%27s_church%2C_Raholp_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg/330px-St_Tassach%27s_church%2C_Raholp_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/St_Tassach%27s_church%2C_Raholp_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg/440px-St_Tassach%27s_church%2C_Raholp_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_542235.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="426" /></a><figcaption>St Tassach's church in Raholp, near <a href="/wiki/Downpatrick" title="Downpatrick">Downpatrick</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>He was one of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>'s disciples, and when St Patrick founded the <i>Church of St Tassach</i> he placed St Tassac in charge of it. The Church of Raholp was situated approximately 1 mile south of <a href="/wiki/Saul,_County_Down" title="Saul, County Down">Saul</a> on the Bannaghan Road in Raholp and was ultimately merged as a Church into the Irish Diocese of Down (now the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Down_and_Connor" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Down and Connor">Diocese of Down and Connor</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Some believe that the Church of Raholp was situated in <a href="/wiki/Downpatrick" title="Downpatrick">Downpatrick</a>, which is approximately 2.5 miles south south west of Saul, but this is open to debate as the ruins of the original Church building are to be found in Raholp. The remains of the church and adjoining lands are protected by law.
</p><p>St Tassac was a skilled artisan who made crosiers, patens, chalices, credences, shrines, and crosses for many of the churches founded by St Patrick, but is remembered primarily for the fact that he was selected by St Patrick to be with him in his last moments and to administer the Holy Viaticum to him.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This event is chronicled in "<a href="/wiki/M%C3%ADche%C3%A1l_%C3%93_Cl%C3%A9irigh" title="Mícheál Ó Cléirigh">The Martyrology of Donegal</a>"; "Tassach of Raholp gave the Body of Christ to Saint Patrick before his death in the monastery of Saul".
</p><p>Since the 19th century, St Tassac has sometimes been confused, with <a href="/wiki/Assicus" title="Assicus">Saint Assicus</a> of <a href="/wiki/Elphin,_County_Roscommon" title="Elphin, County Roscommon">Elphin</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Roscommon" title="County Roscommon">County Roscommon</a>, who had the same types of skill and is said to have died in the same year, and with St Assam (or Assan).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prayer_to_St_Tassac">Prayer to St Tassac</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tassac&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Prayer to St Tassac">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="poem">
<p><i>Saint Tassac.</i><br />
<br />
<i>You were one of the first to follow Saint Patrick</i><br />
<i>and you administered the Holy Viaticum to him at the last.</i><br />
<i>Please be at my side when it is my turn to leave this earth and help me so that, like you, I will be with Christ forever.</i><br />
&#160;<br />
<i>Like Moses' disciples adorning the tabernacle,</i><br />
<i>you were a skilled artist and craftsman</i><br />
<i>and are now with Christ.</i><br />
<i>Please be at my side ensuring that whilst doing my daily chores you</i><br />
<i>ensure that I will always do as Christ requires from me.</i>
</p>
</div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Raholp">Raholp</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tassac&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Raholp">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In Raholp and the surrounding area numerous houses are named "Tassac", "Tassac mór" and "Tassac beag". Translated into English from the Irish Gaelic these are "Tassac", "Large (or Big)" Tassac" and "Small (or Little) Tassac".
</p>
