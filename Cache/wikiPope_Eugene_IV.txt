<p><b>Pope Eugene IV</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Eugenius IV</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Eugenio IV</i>; 1383 – 23 February 1447), born <b>Gabriele Condulmer</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 3 March 1431 to his death in February 1447. Condulmer was a Venetian, and a nephew of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XII" title="Pope Gregory XII">Pope Gregory XII</a>. In 1431, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>. His tenure was marked by conflict first with the <a href="/wiki/Colonna_family" title="Colonna family">Colonna</a>, relatives of his predecessor <a href="/wiki/Martin_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Martin V">Martin V</a>, and later with the <a href="/wiki/Conciliar_movement" class="mw-redirect" title="Conciliar movement">Conciliar movement</a>. In 1434, due to a complaint by Fernando Calvetos, bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>, Eugene IV issued the bull "<a href="/wiki/Creator_Omnium" title="Creator Omnium">Creator Omnium</a>", rescinding any recognition of Portugal's right to conquer those islands, still pagan. He excommunicated anyone who enslaved newly converted Christians, the penalty to stand until the captives were restored to their liberty and possessions. In 1443 Eugene decided to take a neutral position on territorial disputes between Portugal and Castile regarding rights claimed along the coast of Africa. He also issued "<a href="/wiki/Dundum_ad_nostram_audientiam" title="Dundum ad nostram audientiam">Dundum ad nostram audientiam</a>", which was the legal basis for the creation of <a href="/wiki/Jewish_ghettos_in_Europe" title="Jewish ghettos in Europe">Jewish ghettos in Europe</a>.
</p><p>He is the last pope to date to take on the pontifical name "Eugene".
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Eugene_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Condulmer was born in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> to a rich merchant family. He entered a community of <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular_of_San_Giorgio_in_Alga" title="Canons Regular of San Giorgio in Alga">Canons Regular of San Giorgio in Alga</a> in his native city. At the age of twenty-four he was appointed by his maternal uncle, Pope Gregory XII, as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Siena">Bishop of Siena</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, the political leaders objected to a bishop who was not only young but also a foreigner. Therefore, he resigned the appointment, becoming instead his uncle's papal treasurer, <a href="/wiki/Protonotary" class="mw-redirect" title="Protonotary">protonotary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Priest">Cardinal Priest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Clemente" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of San Clemente">Basilica of San Clemente</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-augnet_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-augnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_V" title="Pope Martin V">Pope Martin V</a> named him Cardinal Priest of the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_in_Trastevere" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere">Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere</a>. He also served as papal legate at Picenum in the March of Ancona.<sup id="cite_ref-augnet_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-augnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Eugene_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Condulmer was quickly elected to succeed Martin V in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1431" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1431">papal conclave of 1431</a>. He was crowned as Eugene IV at <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> on 11 March 1431. By a written agreement made before his election he pledged to distribute to the cardinals one-half of all the revenues of the Church and promised to consult with them on all questions of importance, both spiritual and temporal. He is described as tall, thin, with a winning countenance, although many of his troubles were owing to his own want of tact, which alienated parties from him.<sup id="cite_ref-Loughlin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loughlin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Upon assuming the papal chair, Eugene IV took violent measures against the numerous <a href="/wiki/Colonna" class="mw-redirect" title="Colonna">Colonna</a> relatives of his predecessor Martin V, who had rewarded them with castles and lands. This at once involved him in a serious contest with the powerful house of Colonna that nominally supported the local rights of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> against the interests of the Papacy.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A truce was soon arranged.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Conciliar_reform_and_papal_misfortunes">Conciliar reform and papal misfortunes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Eugene_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Conciliar reform and papal misfortunes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Florence" title="Council of Florence">Council of Florence</a></div>
<p>By far the most important feature of Eugene IV's pontificate was the great struggle between the Pope and the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Basel" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Basel">Council of Basel</a> (1431–39), the final embodiment of the <a href="/wiki/Conciliar_movement" class="mw-redirect" title="Conciliar movement">Conciliar movement</a>. On 23 July 1431, his legate <a href="/wiki/Giuliano_Cesarini" class="mw-redirect" title="Giuliano Cesarini">Giuliano Cesarini</a> opened the council, which had been convoked by Martin V. Canon Beaupère of Besançon,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> who had been sent from Basel to Rome, gave the pope an unfavourable and exaggerated account of the temper of the people of Basel and its environs.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Distrustful of its purposes and emboldened by the small attendance, the Pope issued a bull on 18 December 1431 that dissolved the council and called a new one to meet in eighteen months at <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>. The council resisted this expression of papal prerogative. Eugene IV's action gave some weight to the contention that the <a href="/wiki/Curia" title="Curia">Curia</a> was opposed to any authentic measures of reform. The council refused to dissolve; instead they renewed the resolutions by which the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Constance" title="Council of Constance">Council of Constance</a> had declared a council superior to the Pope and ordered Eugene IV to appear at Basel. A compromise was arranged by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Sigismund,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Sigismund, Holy Roman Emperor">Sigismund</a>, who had been crowned emperor at Rome on 31 May 1433. By its terms, the Pope recalled his <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bull</a> of dissolution, and, reserving all the rights of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>, acknowledged the council as <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecumenical">ecumenical</a> on 15 December 1433 except for the initial unapproved sessions that contained <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canons</a> which exalted conciliar authority above that of the pope.<sup id="cite_ref-Loughlin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loughlin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>These concessions also were due to the invasion of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> by the former Papal <a href="/wiki/Condottiero" title="Condottiero">condottiero</a> <a href="/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Fortebraccio" title="Niccolò Fortebraccio">Niccolò Fortebraccio</a> and the troops of <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Maria_Visconti" title="Filippo Maria Visconti">Filippo Maria Visconti</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Piccinino" title="Niccolò Piccinino">Niccolò Piccinino</a> in retaliation for Eugene's support of Florence and Venice against Milan (see also <a href="/wiki/Wars_in_Lombardy" title="Wars in Lombardy">Wars in Lombardy</a>). This situation led also to establishment of an insurrectionary <a href="/wiki/Republic" title="Republic">republic</a> at Rome controlled by the <a href="/wiki/Colonna_family" title="Colonna family">Colonna family</a>. In early June 1434, disguised in the robes of a Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, Eugene was rowed down the center of the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a>, pelted by stones from either bank, to a <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florentine</a> vessel waiting to pick him up at <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica_(archaeological_site)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ostia Antica (archaeological site)">Ostia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-delia_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-delia-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The city was restored to obedience by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Vitelleschi" title="Giovanni Vitelleschi">Giovanni Vitelleschi</a>, the militant Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Recanati" title="Recanati">Recanati</a>, in the following October.<sup id="cite_ref-Loughlin_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loughlin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In August 1435 a peace treaty was signed at Ferrara by the various belligerents. The Pope moved to <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> in April 1436. His condottieri <a href="/wiki/Francesco_I_Sforza" title="Francesco I Sforza">Francesco I Sforza</a> and Vitelleschi in the meantime reconquered much of the Papal States. Traditional Papal enemies such as the <a href="/wiki/Prefetti_di_Vico" title="Prefetti di Vico">Prefetti di Vico</a> were destroyed, while the Colonna were reduced to obedience after the destruction of their stronghold in <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> in August 1436.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Resurgence">Resurgence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Eugene_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Resurgence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Meanwhile, the struggle with the council sitting at Basel broke out anew. Eugene IV at length convened a rival council at <a href="/wiki/Ferrara" title="Ferrara">Ferrara</a> on 8 January 1438 and <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> the <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelates</a> assembled at Basel.<sup id="cite_ref-Stieber_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stieber-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> King <a href="/wiki/Charles_VII_of_France" title="Charles VII of France">Charles VII of France</a> had forbidden members of the clergy in his kingdom from attending the counsel in Ferrara, and introduced the decrees of the Council of Basel, with slight changes, into France through the <a href="/wiki/Pragmatic_Sanction_of_Bourges" title="Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges">Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges</a> (7 July 1438).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The King of England and the Duke of Burgundy, who felt that the council was partial to France, decided not to recognize the council at Basel.<sup id="cite_ref-Stieber_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stieber-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Castile, Aragon, Milan, and Bavaria withdrew support.<sup id="cite_ref-MacCaffrey_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MacCaffrey-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Council of Basel suspended Eugene on 24 January 1438, then formally deposed him as a <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretic</a> on 25 June 1439. In the following November the council elected the ambitious <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_VIII_of_Savoy" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus VIII of Savoy">Amadeus VIII, Duke of Savoy</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> as <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> under the name of <a href="/wiki/Felix_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix V">Felix V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MacCaffrey_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MacCaffrey-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The Diet of Mainz had deprived the Pope of most of his rights in the Empire (26 March 1439).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Florence, where the council of Ferrara had been transferred as a result of an outbreak of the plague, a union with the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> was effected in July 1439, which, as the result of political necessities, proved but a temporary bolster to the papacy's prestige.<sup id="cite_ref-Stieber_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stieber-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This union was followed by others of even less stability. Eugene IV signed an agreement with the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenians</a> on 22 November 1439, and with a part of the <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church" title="Syriac Orthodox Church">Jacobites</a> of Syria in 1443, and in 1445 he received some of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_East" title="Church of the East">Nestorians</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Maronites" title="Maronites">Maronites</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He did his best to stem the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Turkish</a> advance, pledging one-fifth of the papal income to a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a> which set out in 1443, but which met with overwhelming defeat at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Varna" title="Battle of Varna">Battle of Varna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Cardinal Cesarini, the papal legate, perished in the rout.
</p><p>Eugene's rival Felix V in the meantime obtained scant recognition, even in the Empire. Eventually Holy Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick III, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick III</a> moved toward acceptance of Eugene. One of the king's ablest advisers, the humanist Aeneas Sylvius Piccolomini, who was later to be <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_II" title="Pope Pius II">Pope Pius II</a>, made peace with Eugene in 1442. The Pope's recognition of the claim to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> of King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_V_of_Aragon" title="Alfonso V of Aragon">Alfonso V of Aragon</a> (in the treaty of <a href="/wiki/Terracina" title="Terracina">Terracina</a>, approved by Eugene at Siena somewhat later) withdrew the last important support in Italy from the Council of Basel.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1442 Eugene, Alfonso and Visconti sent <a href="/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Piccinino" title="Niccolò Piccinino">Niccolò Piccinino</a> to reconquer the March of <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a> from Francesco Sforza; but the defeat of the allied army at the Battle of Montolmo pushed the Pope to reconcile with Sforza.
</p><p>So enabled, Eugene IV made a victorious entry into Rome on 28 September 1443 after an exile of nearly ten years.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His protests against the Pragmatic Sanction of Bourges were ineffectual, but by means of the <a href="/wiki/Concordat_of_the_Princes" class="mw-redirect" title="Concordat of the Princes">Concordat of the Princes</a>, negotiated by Piccolomini with the electors in February 1447, the whole of <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> declared against the antipope.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This agreement was completed only after Eugene's death.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Slavery">Slavery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Eugene_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Slavery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Creator_Omnium" title="Creator Omnium">Creator Omnium</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sicut_Dudum" class="mw-redirect" title="Sicut Dudum">Sicut Dudum</a></div>
<p>Christianity had gained many converts in the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a> by the early 1430s. However, the ownership of the lands had been the subject of dispute between <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castille" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Castille">Kingdom of Castille</a>. The lack of effective control had resulted in periodic raids on the islands to procure slaves. As early as the Council of Koblenz in 922, the capture of Christians as slaves by other Christians had been condemned.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Acting on a complaint by Fernando Calvetos, bishop of the islands,<sup id="cite_ref-Housley_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Housley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Pope Eugene IV issued a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">Papal bull</a>, "<a href="/wiki/Creator_Omnium" title="Creator Omnium">Creator Omnium</a>", on 17 December 1434, annulling previous permission granted to Portugal to conquer those islands still pagan. Eugene excommunicated anyone who enslaved newly converted Christians, the penalty to stand until the captives were restored to their liberty and possessions.<sup id="cite_ref-Raiswell_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Raiswell-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1434, Eugene issued <i>Regimini Gregis Dominici</i> forbidding the enslavement of Christian Canarians, and followed this with an order to suspend further conquest in order to allow the Franciscans to continue their work peacefully.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Portuguese soldiers continued to raid the islands in 1435, and Eugene issued a further edict <i><a href="/wiki/Sicut_Dudum" class="mw-redirect" title="Sicut Dudum">Sicut Dudum</a></i> that prohibited wars being waged against the islands and affirming the ban on enslavement. Eugene condemned the enslavement of the peoples of the newly colonized Canary Islands and, under pain of excommunication, ordered all such slaves to be immediately set free.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
Eugene went on to say that, "If this is not done when the fifteen days have passed, they incur the sentence of excommunication by the act itself, from which they cannot be absolved, except at the point of death, even by the Holy See, or by any Spanish bishop, or by the aforementioned Ferdinand, unless they have first given freedom to these captive persons and restored their goods." The specific reference to Spanish bishops and Bishop Ferdinand of San Marcial del Rubicón in Lanzarote suggests that the Portuguese were not the only ones engaged in slave raids in the Canaries.
</p><p>Eugene tempered <i>Sicut Dudum</i> in September 1436 with the issuance of a papal bull in response to complaints made by <a href="/wiki/King_Edward_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="King Edward of Portugal">King Edward of Portugal</a> that allowed the Portuguese to conquer any unconverted parts of the Canary Islands.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> According to Raiswell (1997), any Christian would be protected by the earlier edict but the un-baptized were implicitly allowed to be enslaved.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following the arrival of the first African captives in <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a> in 1441, <a href="/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry the Navigator">Prince Henry</a> asked Eugene to designate Portugal's raids along the West African coast as a crusade, a consequence of which would be the legitimization of enslavement for captives taken during the crusade. On 19 December 1442, Eugene replied by issuing the bull <i><a href="/wiki/Illius_qui_se_pro_divini" title="Illius qui se pro divini">Illius qui se pro divini</a></i>, in which he granted full remission of sins to those who took part in any expeditions against the Saracens.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In 1443, in the bull <i>Rex regum</i>, the Pope took a neutral position on territorial disputes between Portugal and Castile regarding rights claimed in Africa.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard Raiswell interprets the bulls of Eugene as helping in some way the development of thought which perceived the enslavement of Africans by the Portuguese and later Europeans "as dealing a blow for Christendom".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Joel S Panzer views <i>Sicut Dudum</i> as a significant condemnation of slavery, issued sixty years before the Europeans found the New World.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Eugene_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg/200px-Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg/300px-Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg/400px-Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg 2x" data-file-width="960" data-file-height="1280" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Papa_Eugenio_IV.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Pope Eugene at the <a href="/wiki/Florence_Cathedral" title="Florence Cathedral">Florence Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>Although his pontificate had been so stormy and unhappy that he is said to have regretted on his deathbed that he ever left his monastery,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Watanabe_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Watanabe-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Eugene IV's victory over the Council of Basel and his efforts on behalf of church unity nevertheless contributed greatly to the breakdown of the conciliar movement and restored the papacy to a semblance of the dominant position it had held before the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> (1378–1417).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This victory had been gained, however, by making concessions to the princes of Europe. Thereafter, the papacy had to depend more for its revenues on the Papal States.
</p><p>Eugene was dignified in demeanour, but inexperienced and vacillating in action and excitable in temper. Bitter in his hatred of <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a>, he nevertheless displayed great kindness to the poor. He laboured to reform the <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monastic</a> orders, especially the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscans</a>, and was never guilty of <a href="/wiki/Nepotism" title="Nepotism">nepotism</a>. Although austere in his private life, he was a sincere friend of <a href="/wiki/Art" title="Art">art</a> and learning, and in 1431 he re-established the <a href="/wiki/University" title="University">university</a> at Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He also consecrated <a href="/wiki/Florence_Cathedral" title="Florence Cathedral">Florence Cathedral</a> on 25 March 1436.
</p><p>Eugene died in Rome on 23 February 1447<sup id="cite_ref-augnet_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-augnet-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was buried at Saint Peter's by the <a href="/wiki/List_of_extant_papal_tombs" title="List of extant papal tombs">tomb of Pope Eugene III</a>. Later his tomb was transferred to <a href="/wiki/San_Salvatore_in_Lauro" title="San Salvatore in Lauro">San Salvatore in Lauro</a>, a parish church on the other bank of the <a href="/wiki/Tiber_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiber River">Tiber River</a>.
</p>
