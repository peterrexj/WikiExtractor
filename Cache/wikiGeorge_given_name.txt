<p><b>George</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><small>English: </small><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span><span title="/ɔːr/: &#39;ar&#39; in &#39;war&#39;">ɔːr</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span></span>/</a></span></span>) is a masculine given name derived from the <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> <b><a href="/wiki/Ge%C5%8Drgios" class="mw-redirect" title="Geōrgios">Geōrgios</a></b> (<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Γεώργιος</span></span>; <small><a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Ancient Greek</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="grc-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Greek" title="Help:IPA/Greek">[ɡeɔː&#769;rɡi.os]</a></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Modern_Greek" title="Modern Greek">Modern Greek</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="el-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Greek" title="Help:IPA/Greek">[ʝeˈorʝi.os]</a></span>).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The name gained popularity due to its association with the <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a> (died 23 April 303), a member of the <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Guard" title="Praetorian Guard">Praetorian Guard</a> who was sentenced to death for his refusal to renounce <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, and prior to that, it might have been a <a href="/wiki/Theophoric_name" title="Theophoric name">theophoric name</a>, with origins in <a href="/wiki/Zeus_Georgos" title="Zeus Georgos">Zeus Georgos</a>, an early title of the Greek god <a href="/wiki/Zeus" title="Zeus">Zeus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Today, it is one of the most commonly used names in the Western world, though its religious significance has waned among modern populations. Its diminutives are <b>Geordie</b> and <b>Georgie</b>, with the former being limited primarily to residents of England and <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. The most popular feminine forms in the <a href="/wiki/Anglosphere" title="Anglosphere">Anglosphere</a>, are <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(name)" title="Georgia (name)"><b>Georgia</b></a>, <b><a href="/wiki/Georgiana" title="Georgiana">Georgiana</a></b>, and <a href="/wiki/Georgina_(name)" title="Georgina (name)"><b>Georgina</b></a>.

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Etymology_and_origins">Etymology and origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=George_(given_name)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Etymology and origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Its original Greek form, <b>Georgios</b>, is based on the Greek word <i>georgos</i> (γεωργός) 'farmer'. The word <i>georgos</i> itself is ultimately a combination of two Greek words: <i>ge</i> (γῆ) 'earth, <a href="/wiki/Soil" title="Soil">soil</a>' and <i>ergon</i> (ἔργον) 'work'. <a href="/wiki/Aelius_Herodianus" title="Aelius Herodianus">Aelius Herodianus</a> (fl. 2nd century AD), a Roman-era Greek grammarian and writer, determined Georgios to be a <a href="/wiki/Theophoric_name" title="Theophoric name">theophoric name</a>, or a name created to honor of deity, a nod to <a href="/wiki/Zeus_Georgos" title="Zeus Georgos">Zeus Georgos</a>, or "<a href="/wiki/Zeus" title="Zeus">Zeus</a> the Farmer" in English. In the early stages of <a href="/wiki/Greek_mythology" title="Greek mythology">Greek mythology</a>, before Zeus took on a major role in the <a href="/wiki/Greek_mythology" title="Greek mythology">Greek pantheon</a> as ruler of all the gods and goddesses, he was <a href="/wiki/Sacrifice" title="Sacrifice">sacrificed</a> to as an <a href="/wiki/Agricultural_god" class="mw-redirect" title="Agricultural god">agricultural god</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Tutelary_deity" title="Tutelary deity">patron</a> of <a href="/wiki/Crop" title="Crop">crops</a> and <a href="/wiki/Harvest" title="Harvest">harvests</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The name took on religious significance to followers of <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christianity">Early Christianity</a> in 303 with the supposed martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Georgios</a>, a Roman soldier of Greek heritage.  While the story's historical accuracy is subject to debate, his character took on real importance to the <a href="/wiki/Christian_Church" title="Christian Church">Christian Church</a>, with Georgios and its variants being used as <a href="/wiki/Baptismal_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptismal name">baptismal names</a> and by religious officials and Christian monarchs, though it did not become common among the <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">laity</a> until after the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>.
</p>
