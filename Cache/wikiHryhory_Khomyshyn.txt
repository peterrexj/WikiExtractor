<p><b>Hryhoriy Khomyshyn</b> (also <i>Hryhorij Khomyshyn</i>, <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Григорій Лукич Хомишин</span>, <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Grzegorz Chomyszyn</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic">Ukrainian Greek Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hieromartyr" title="Hieromartyr">hieromartyr</a>.
</p><p>Khomyshyn was born on 25 March 1867 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Hadynkivtsi" title="Hadynkivtsi">Hadynkivtsi</a>, eastern <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Eastern_Europe)" title="Galicia (Eastern Europe)">Galicia</a>, in what is now <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast" title="Ternopil Oblast">Ternopil Oblast</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He graduated from Lviv <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> on 18 November 1893.<sup id="cite_ref-ugcc_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ugcc-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He continued to study <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Vienna" title="University of Vienna">University of Vienna</a> from 1894 to 1899, and in 1902, <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Andrey_Sheptytsky" title="Andrey Sheptytsky">Andrey Sheptytsky</a> appointed Khomyshyn the <a href="/wiki/Rector_(academia)" title="Rector (academia)">rector</a> of the Greek Catholic Theological Seminary in <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1904, he was <a href="/wiki/Consecrated" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated">consecrated</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Catholic_Metropolitan_Archdiocese_of_Ivano-Frankivsk" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Catholic Metropolitan Archdiocese of Ivano-Frankivsk">bishop for Stanyslaviv</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk" title="Ivano-Frankivsk">Ivano-Frankivsk</a>) at <a href="/wiki/St._George%27s_Cathedral,_Lviv" title="St. George&#39;s Cathedral, Lviv">St. George's Cathedral</a>, taking the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_heraldry" title="Ecclesiastical heraldry">episcopal motto</a> "Подъ твою милость" (<a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic" title="Church Slavonic">Church Slavonic</a> for "<a href="/wiki/Sub_tuum_praesidium" title="Sub tuum praesidium">Beneath thy mercy</a>"). Throughout his tenure, spanning over four decades, he was considered the <a href="/wiki/Hierarchy_of_the_Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Hierarchy of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church">second most powerful figure</a> in the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Unlike Sheptytsky, Khomyshyn believed that the UGCC should adopt a more westward orientation, further emphasizing the Uniate Church's relationship with Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This meant introducing <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_Latinisation" title="Liturgical Latinisation">Latinized</a> practicies such as the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="Gregorian calendar">Gregorian calendar</a> and a strict adherence to <a href="/wiki/Clerical_celibacy_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Clerical celibacy (Catholic Church)">clerical celibacy</a>, which were met with controversy in his eparchy.<sup id="cite_ref-scarecrow_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scarecrow-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-yale_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-yale-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the 1930s, Khomyshyn was responsible for organizing the <a href="/wiki/Christian_Social_Movement_in_Ukraine" title="Christian Social Movement in Ukraine">Ukrainian Catholic People's Party</a>, which briefly held seats in the <a href="/wiki/Sejm" title="Sejm">Sejm</a> and <a href="/wiki/Senate_of_Poland" title="Senate of Poland">Senate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-scarecrow_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scarecrow-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  He is noted as being one of only a handful of members of the Catholic hierarchy in <a href="/wiki/Interwar_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Interwar Poland">interwar Poland</a> to publicly oppose <a href="/wiki/Anti-Semitism" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-Semitism">anti-Semitism</a>; his tolerance towards <a href="/wiki/Galitzianers" class="mw-redirect" title="Galitzianers">Galician Jews</a> likely owing to his own experience as part of Poland's Ukrainian minority.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> As a result of his moderate approach to Ukrainian nationalism, he would be labeled a "sellout" by the <a href="/wiki/Organization_of_Ukrainian_Nationalists" title="Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists">OUN</a> and was left fearing for his life.<sup id="cite_ref-yale_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-yale-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Khomyshyn was first arrested in 1939 by the <a href="/wiki/NKVD" title="NKVD">NKVD</a>. A critic of the Soviet system, having called the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Front_(World_War_II)" title="Eastern Front (World War II)">occupying forces</a> "fierce beasts animated by ... the devil,"<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> he was arrested again in April 1945, and was then deported to <a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>. In prison, he was tortured and advised to renounce the <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a>, which he refused to do.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Khomyshyn died in the <a href="/wiki/Lukyanivska_Prison" title="Lukyanivska Prison">Lukyanivska Prison</a> hospital in Kyiv on 17 January 1947.  He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 June 2001, as one of <a href="/wiki/List_of_people_beatified_by_Pope_John_Paul_II#2001" title="List of people beatified by Pope John Paul II">Mykolai Charnets'kyi and the 24 companion martyrs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ugcc_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ugcc-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Sesquicentennial" class="mw-redirect" title="Sesquicentennial">sesquicentennial</a> of his birth in 2017 was marked by celebrations in both Ukraine and Poland, along with examinations of the bishop's social impact in academic circles.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quote">Quote</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hryhoriy_Khomyshyn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Quote">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>"At the Kyiv prison the interrogations were conducted by Interrogator Dubok. He was a horrible sadist. He investigated my case... This Dubok told me himself how he had killed the bishop: 'So you, Khomyshyn, spoke out against communism?' The bishop, as always, replied resolutely: 'I did and I will.' Did you fight against Soviet authority?' 'Yes, I did and I will!' Then Dubok became outraged and grabbed some books written by the bishop, which lay on the table in front of him, and started cruelly beating His Excellency with them, on his head and everywhere else." - From the testimony of Father Petro Heryliuk-Kupchynysky.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
