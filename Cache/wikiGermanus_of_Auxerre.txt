<p><b>Germanus of Auxerre</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Germanus Antissiodorensis</i>; <a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Garmon Sant</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Germain l'Auxerrois</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 378 – c. 442–448 AD) was a western Roman clergyman who was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Auxerre" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Auxerre">bishop of Autissiodorum</a> in Late Antique <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>. He abandoned a career as a high-ranking government official to devote his formidable energy towards the promotion of the church and the protection of his "flock" in dangerous times, personally confronting, for instance, the barbarian king "<a href="/wiki/Goar" title="Goar">Goar</a>". In <a href="/wiki/Roman_Britain" title="Roman Britain">Britain</a> he is best remembered for his journey to combat <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagianism</a> in or around 429 AD, and the records of this visit provide valuable information on the state of <a href="/wiki/Sub-Roman_Britain" title="Sub-Roman Britain">post-Roman British society</a>. He also played an important part in the establishment and promotion of the Cult of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Alban" title="Saint Alban">Saint Alban</a>. The saint was said to have revealed the story of his martyrdom to Germanus in a dream or holy vision, and Germanus ordered this to be written down for public display. Germanus is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in both the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Orthodox</a> churches, which commemorate him on 31 July.
</p><p>The principal source for the events of his life is the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Germani" title="Vita Germani">Vita Germani</a></i>, a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> written by <a href="/wiki/Constantius_of_Lyon" title="Constantius of Lyon">Constantius of Lyon</a> around 480, and a brief passage added onto the end of the <i><a href="/wiki/Passio_Albani" title="Passio Albani">Passio Albani</a></i>, which may possibly have been written or commissioned by Germanus. Constantius was a friend of <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Lupus_of_Troyes" title="Lupus of Troyes">Lupus of Troyes</a>, who accompanied Germanus to Britain, which provided him with a link to Germanus.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG/220px-P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="366" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG/330px-P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG/440px-P1010345_Paris_Ier_Eglise_Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois_statue_Saint-Germain_reductwk.JPG 2x" data-file-width="901" data-file-height="1500" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Saint Germanus of Auxerre.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Germanus was the son of Rusticus and Germanilla, and his family was one of the noblest in Gaul in the latter portion of the fourth century. He received the very best education provided by the distinguished schools of <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Lyons">Lyons</a>, and then went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he studied eloquence and civil law. He practiced there before the tribunal of the prefect for some years with great success. His high birth and brilliant talents brought him into contact with the court, and he married Eustachia, a lady highly esteemed in imperial circles. The emperor sent him back to Gaul, appointing him one of the six dukes, entrusted with the government of the Gallic provinces. He resided at Auxerre.<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At length he incurred the displeasure of the bishop, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Amator" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Amator">Saint Amator</a> by hanging hunting trophies on a certain tree, which in earlier times had been the scene of pagan worship. Amator remonstrated with him in vain. One day when the duke was absent, the bishop had the tree cut down and the trophies burnt. Fearing the anger of the duke, who wished to kill him, he fled and appealed to the prefect Julius for permission to confer the tonsure on Germain. This being granted, Amator, who felt that his own life was drawing to a close, returned. When the duke came to the church, Amator caused the doors to be barred and gave him the tonsure against his will, telling him to live as one destined to be his successor, and forthwith made him a deacon.<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When in a short time Amator died, Germain was unanimously chosen to fill the vacant see, being consecrated 7 July, 418. His education now served him in good stead in the government of the diocese, which he administered with great sagacity. He distributed his goods among the poor, practised great austerities, and built a large monastery dedicated to Saints <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_and_Damian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cosmas and Damian">Cosmas and Damian</a> on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Yonne_(river)" title="Yonne (river)">Yonne</a>, whither he was wont to retire in his spare moments.<sup id="cite_ref-macerlean_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macerlean-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Visit_to_Britain">Visit to Britain</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Visit to Britain">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG/220px-St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="265" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG/330px-St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG/440px-St_Patrick_being_taught_by_St_Germanus.JPG 2x" data-file-width="490" data-file-height="590" /></a><figcaption>Stained glass window at <a href="/wiki/Gloucester_Cathedral" title="Gloucester Cathedral">Gloucester Cathedral</a> depicting <a href="/wiki/St_Patrick" class="mw-redirect" title="St Patrick">St Patrick</a> being taught by Saint Germanus.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Around 429, shortly after the Romans had withdrawn from Britain, a Gaulish assembly of bishops chose Germanus and <a href="/wiki/Lupus_of_Troyes" title="Lupus of Troyes">Lupus, Bishop of Troyes</a>, to visit the island. It was alleged that <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagianism</a> was rife among the British clergy, led  by a British bishop's son named Agricola. Germanus went to combat the threat and satisfy the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> that the British church would not break away from the <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustinian</a> teachings of <a href="/wiki/Divine_grace" title="Divine grace">divine grace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On the way to Britain they passed through Nanterre, where Germanus noticed in the crowd which met them a young girl, whom he bade live as one espoused to Christ, and who later became St. <a href="/wiki/Genevi%C3%A8ve" class="mw-redirect" title="Geneviève">Geneviève</a> of Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Germanus and Lupus confronted the British clergy at a public meeting before a huge crowd in Britain. The Pelagians were described as being 'conspicuous for riches, brilliant in dress and surrounded by a fawning multitude'. The bishops debated and, despite having no popular support, Germanus was able to defeat the Pelagians using his superior rhetorical skills.
</p><p>Constantius also recounts the miraculous healing of the blind daughter of 'a man with <a href="/wiki/Tribune" title="Tribune">tribunician</a> power'.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This use of the word tribune may imply the existence of some form of post-Roman government system. However, in Constantius' lifetime tribune had acquired a looser definition, and often was used to indicate any military officer, whether part of the Imperial army or part of a town militia.
</p><p>Germanus led the native Britons to a victory against <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Pictish</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saxon_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Saxon people">Saxon</a> raiders, at a mountainous site near a river, of which <a href="/wiki/Mold,_Flintshire" title="Mold, Flintshire">Mold</a> in North <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> is the traditional location. The enemy approaching, the former general put himself at the head of the Christians. He led them into a vale between two high mountains, and ordered his troops shout when he gave them a sign. When the Saxon pirates came near them, he cried out thrice, Alleluia, which was followed by the whole army of Britons. The sound echoed from the hills with a noise so loud that the barbarians, judging from the shout that they were facing a mighty army, flung down their arms and ran away, leaving behind their baggage and booty.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Germanus_and_the_Cult_of_Saint_Alban">Germanus and the Cult of Saint Alban</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Germanus and the Cult of Saint Alban">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Immediately after the debate with the Pelagians, Germanus gave thanks for his victory at the grave of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Alban" title="Saint Alban">Saint Alban</a>, which was likely in some sort of tomb or basilica. Some translators use the word "shrine"; this word, which is not the original Latin, would have suggested that the cult of Saint Alban had been established before Germanus' visit to Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-Garcia:_Saint_Alban_and_the_Cult_of_Saints_in_Late_Antique_Britain_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Garcia:_Saint_Alban_and_the_Cult_of_Saints_in_Late_Antique_Britain-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Vermaat:_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermaat:-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-biddle_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biddle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> That night, Germanus claimed that Saint Alban came to him in a dream, revealing the details of his martyrdom. Some scholars <sup id="cite_ref-Wood:_Germanus,_Alban_and_Auxerre_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wood:_Germanus,_Alban_and_Auxerre-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>   have even interpreted the relevant passage in a postscript to the <i><a href="/wiki/Passio_Albani" title="Passio Albani">Passio Albani</a></i> as implying the saint's name was unknown before it was revealed to Germanus but other scholars <sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> have disputed this. 
When Germanus awoke, he had the account written down in <a href="/wiki/Tituli" class="mw-redirect" title="Tituli">tituli</a>, possibly to be engraved on the walls or illustrated placards at a church site, either in Britain, or in Auxerre.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It has been suggested that this account formed the basis of the <i>Passio Albani</i>, the foundational text of all information about Saint Alban:<sup id="cite_ref-Sharpe:_The_late_antique_passion_of_St_Alban_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sharpe:_The_late_antique_passion_of_St_Alban-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> while this is fundamentally unprovable the evidence is strong that the <i>Passio</i> originated within the circle of Germanus at Auxerre.  Germanus then deposited some of the bones of continental saints in the basilica, and took a sample of the earth at the site of Alban's martyrdom, which still bore the marks of the martyr's blood. Some have advanced arguments for the antiquity of the <a href="/wiki/Cult_of_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Cult of saints">cult</a> of Saint Alban (for example <a href="/wiki/Martin_Biddle" title="Martin Biddle">Martin Biddle</a><sup id="cite_ref-biddle_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biddle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>), while others suggest it was a rather artificial creation from the final years of Roman Britain, or soon after. Some have, in fact, concluded that Germanus, himself, was responsible for the creation as well as the promotion of the cult.<sup id="cite_ref-Garcia:_Saint_Alban_and_the_Cult_of_Saints_in_Late_Antique_Britain_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Garcia:_Saint_Alban_and_the_Cult_of_Saints_in_Late_Antique_Britain-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Wood:_Germanus,_Alban_and_Auxerre_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wood:_Germanus,_Alban_and_Auxerre-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> although this has been disputed by others <sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Germanus may have made a second visit to Britain in the mid 430s<sup id="cite_ref-Thompson_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thompson-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Wood_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wood-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> or mid 440s, though this is contested by some scholars<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> who suggest it may be a 'doublet' or variant version of the visit that has been mistaken as describing a different visit and erroneously included as such by Constantius, according to whom Germanus was joined by Severus, Bishop of Trier and met Elafius, "one of the leading men of the country". Germanus is said to have cured Elafius' enfeebled son by a miracle that served to persuade the population that Gaulish Catholicism rather than Pelagianism was the true faith.
</p><p>According to a legend recorded in the <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Brittonum" title="Historia Brittonum">Historia Brittonum</a></i> of circa 829 it is during this second visit, around 447, that Germanus is said to have condemned for <a href="/wiki/Incest" title="Incest">incest</a> and other crimes Guorthigern, identified with the '<a href="/wiki/Vortigern" title="Vortigern">Vortigern</a>' of Welsh tradition. Vortigern ordered his daughter to bring her child to Germanus and name the bishop as its father. The scheme having failed, Vortigern was cursed by Germanus and the council of Britons, and fled into Wales followed by Germanus and the clergy. They pursued him to a castle on the river Teifi, where they fasted and prayed for three days and three nights. Finally, fire fell from heaven consuming the castle, the guilty king, and his company.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> While the story appears to have no historical basis, it does indicate in what reverence Germanus was held in the Church in that part of the land.
</p><p>He died in <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> while petitioning the Roman government for leniency for the citizens of <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armorica</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> against whom Aëtius had dispatched the <a href="/wiki/Alans" title="Alans">Alans</a> on a <a href="/wiki/Punitive_expedition" title="Punitive expedition">punitive expedition</a>. Germanus had famously confronted Goar, the king of the Alans, so Constantius's Life relates.
</p><p>Based on the scanty evidence, some scholars have argued that his death should be dated to 442 or 448, and others that it should be dated to c. 437.<sup id="cite_ref-Thompson_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thompson-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Wood_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wood-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cult">Cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Auxerre_003.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Auxerre_003.JPG/180px-Auxerre_003.JPG" decoding="async" width="180" height="135" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Auxerre_003.JPG/270px-Auxerre_003.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Auxerre_003.JPG/360px-Auxerre_003.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="900" /></a><figcaption>A view of Auxerre's old town with the Abbey of Saint Germanus in the background.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Germanus' tomb continues to be venerated in the church of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Germain_d%27Auxerre" title="Abbey of Saint-Germain d&#39;Auxerre">Abbey of Saint-Germain d'Auxerre</a>, which although now part of municipal museum remains open for worship at stated times. There is a tradition of a <a href="/wiki/Panegyric" title="Panegyric">panegyric</a> on the Sunday nearest to or preceding his festival in July.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Cult" title="Cult">cult</a> of Saint Germanus of Auxerre spread in northern <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, hence the church <a href="/wiki/Saint-Germain_l%27Auxerrois" title="Saint-Germain l&#39;Auxerrois">Saint-Germain l'Auxerrois</a> facing the <a href="/wiki/Louvre" title="Louvre">Louvre</a> in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>. His cult is clearly distinguished from that of the homonymous <a href="/wiki/Germain_of_Paris" title="Germain of Paris">Saint Germanus of Paris</a>. He is associated with the church at <a href="/wiki/Charonne_(Paris_M%C3%A9tro)" class="mw-redirect" title="Charonne (Paris Métro)">Charonne</a> in the east of Paris and the cult of <a href="/wiki/Genevieve" title="Genevieve">Saint Genevieve</a> (Genoveva) in <a href="/wiki/Nanterre" title="Nanterre">Nanterre</a> to the west of the city, both situated on the late <a href="/wiki/Roman_road" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman road">Roman road</a> network. His journey to Britain is commemorated in his dedications at Siouville and at Saint-Germain-les-Vaux in the <a href="/wiki/Cotentin" class="mw-redirect" title="Cotentin">Cotentin</a> (Manche).
</p><p>In the 2004 edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, Germanus is listed under July 31. He is described as 'passing at Ravenna, a bishop who defended Britain against the Pelagian heresy and travelled to make peace for Armorica'.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Great_Britain">In Great Britain</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: In Great Britain">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The former <a href="/wiki/St_German%27s_Priory" title="St German&#39;s Priory">priory church</a> at <a href="/wiki/St_Germans,_Cornwall" title="St Germans, Cornwall">St Germans</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a> bears his name and was in late Saxon times the seat of a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_St_Germans" title="Bishop of St Germans">bishop</a>. A few other churches in England are dedicated to the saint, including <a href="/wiki/St_Germain%27s_Church,_Edgbaston" title="St Germain&#39;s Church, Edgbaston">St Germain's Church, Edgbaston</a>, in Birmingham, <a href="/wiki/St_Germanus%27_Church,_Faulkbourne" title="St Germanus&#39; Church, Faulkbourne">St Germanus' Church, Faulkbourne</a>, in Essex and <a href="/wiki/St_Germanus%27_Church,_Rame" title="St Germanus&#39; Church, Rame">St Germanus' Church, Rame</a>, in Cornwall, as well as the church at <a href="/wiki/Germansweek" title="Germansweek">Germansweek</a> in <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>. The name of the civil parish of <a href="/wiki/Wiggenhall_St_Germans" title="Wiggenhall St Germans">Wiggenhall St Germans</a> in Norfolk reflects a church dedication.
</p><p>In Wales, Germanus is remembered as an early influence on the Celtic Church. In the current <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> liturgical calendar for Wales,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> he is commemorated on August 3, July 31 being designated for <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Adamsdown" title="Adamsdown">Adamsdown</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cardiff" title="Cardiff">Cardiff</a>,  <a href="/wiki/St_German%27s_Church" title="St German&#39;s Church">St German's Church</a> is a <a href="/wiki/Church_in_Wales" title="Church in Wales">Church in Wales</a> (Anglican) parish church by <a href="/wiki/George_Frederick_Bodley" title="George Frederick Bodley">Bodley</a>, opened  in 1884.
</p><p>Germanus is traditionally credited with the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Sodor_and_Man" title="Diocese of Sodor and Man">Diocese of Sodor and Man</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Man" title="Isle of Man">Isle of Man</a>, though this may have been a different man of a similar name. In the medieval tradition Germanus was frequently conflated with the <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> Saint Garmon, who is commemorated in several <a href="/wiki/North_Wales" title="North Wales">North Wales</a> <a href="/wiki/Placename" class="mw-redirect" title="Placename">placenames</a> and church dedications, including <a href="/wiki/Capel_Garmon" title="Capel Garmon">Capel Garmon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Llanarmon,_Gwynedd" title="Llanarmon, Gwynedd">Llanarmon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Llanarmon_Dyffryn_Ceiriog" title="Llanarmon Dyffryn Ceiriog">Llanarmon Dyffryn Ceiriog</a>, <a href="/wiki/Llanarmon_Mynydd_Mawr" title="Llanarmon Mynydd Mawr">Llanarmon Mynydd Mawr</a>, <a href="/wiki/Llanarmon-yn-I%C3%A2l" title="Llanarmon-yn-Iâl">Llanarmon-yn-Iâl</a>, <a href="/wiki/St_Garmon%27s_Church,_Llanfechain" title="St Garmon&#39;s Church, Llanfechain">Llanfechain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Castle_Caereinion" title="Castle Caereinion">Castle Caereinion</a>. However, <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould </a> and Fisher argued that Saint Garmon is a different Saint Germanus, who was born in <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armorica</a> (northern France) <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;410</span>, visited <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a> in Ireland in about 440, visited Britain in about 462, then returned to Ireland and was appointed Bishop of Man in 466, and died on the Isle of Man <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;474</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-SBG77_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SBG77-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Fictional_portrayals_of_Germanus">Fictional portrayals of Germanus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Auxerre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Fictional portrayals of Germanus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Hilaire_Belloc" title="Hilaire Belloc">Hilaire Belloc</a> refers to Germanus in his humorous poem, <i>The Pelagian Drinking Song</i> from <i>The four men / a farrago</i> (1912):<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p>And then with his stout Episcopal staff<br />
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">So thoroughly whacked and banged</span><br />
The heretics all, both short and tall,<br />
<span class="mw-poem-indented" style="display: inline-block; margin-inline-start: 1em;">They rather had been hanged.</span>
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<ul><li>Germanus's visit to Britain is the subject of the <a href="/wiki/Welsh_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Welsh (language)">Welsh language</a> radio play <i>Buchedd Garmon</i> (1936) by <a href="/wiki/Saunders_Lewis" title="Saunders Lewis">Saunders Lewis</a>.</li>
<li>Germanus appears many times in <a href="/wiki/Jack_Whyte" title="Jack Whyte">Jack Whyte</a> series of novels '<a href="/wiki/A_Dream_of_Eagles" title="A Dream of Eagles">A Dream of Eagles</a>' (1992–2005) and the series "The Golden Eagle" (2005) .</li>
<li>Germanus appears in the TV movie <i><a href="/wiki/St._Patrick:_The_Irish_Legend" title="St. Patrick: The Irish Legend">St. Patrick: The Irish Legend</a></i> (2000) where he helps Patrick achieve his mission to convert the Irish.</li>
<li>In <a href="/wiki/Valerio_Massimo_Manfredi" title="Valerio Massimo Manfredi">Valerio Massimo Manfredi</a>'s 2002 novel <i>The Last Legion</i> and in the <a href="/wiki/The_Last_Legion" title="The Last Legion">2007 film</a> based on the novel, Germanus dies in Britain after his arrival on the island to lead the Romans and Britons against the barbarian <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a>. In the film, before he dies the Bishop recites his last words as a prophecy and founds the last legion of Britain, entrusting them with their standard: a silver dragon with a purple tail.</li>
<li>Germanus appears in the 2004 film <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur_(2004_film)" title="King Arthur (2004 film)"><i>King Arthur</i></a>, although his second and final mission to Britain took place twenty years before the year in which the movie is set. He is portrayed by Italian actor <a href="/wiki/Ivano_Marescotti" title="Ivano Marescotti">Ivano Marescotti</a>.</li></ul>
