<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a></div>
<p><b>Pope Pius XII</b> (1939–1958)   <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> numerous  saints, including <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maria_Goretti" title="Maria Goretti">Maria Goretti</a>. He beatified <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Saints">The Saints</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Pius_XII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The Saints">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first canonizations of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> were two women, the founder of a female order, <a href="/wiki/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier" title="Mary Euphrasia Pelletier">Mary Euphrasia Pelletier</a>, and a nanny and housekeeper, <a href="/wiki/Gemma_Galgani" title="Gemma Galgani">Gemma Galgani</a>. Pelletier had  a reputation for opening new ways for Catholic charities, helping people in difficulties with the law, who so far were neglected by the system and the Church. Galgani was an unknown woman whose virtue, charity and devotion became model by her canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-Lehnert,_163_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lehnert,_163-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pius_X">Pius X</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Pius_XII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pius X">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 29 May 1954, less than three years after his beatification, Pius X was canonized, following recognition of two more miracles. The first involved Francesco Belsami, an attorney from <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> who had a fatal <a href="/wiki/Pulmonary" class="mw-redirect" title="Pulmonary">pulmonary</a> <a href="/wiki/Abscess" title="Abscess">abscess</a>, who was cured after placing a picture of Pope Pius X upon his chest. The second miracle involved Sr. Maria Ludovica Scorcia, a nun who had a serious <a href="/wiki/Nervous_system" title="Nervous system">neurotropic</a> <a href="/wiki/Virus" title="Virus">virus</a>, and who, after several novenas, was entirely cured. The Canonization mass was presided over by Pius XII at Saint Peter's Basilica before a crowd of about 800,000<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> of the faithful and church officials at St. Peter's Basilica. Pius X became the first Pope to be canonized since the 17th century.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Maria_Goretti">Maria Goretti</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Pius_XII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Maria Goretti">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Saint Maria Goretti as a <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> saint of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Maria's mother, nicknamed "Mamma Assunta" by her neighbors, was present at the ceremony; she was the first mother ever to attend the canonization ceremony of her child, along with her four remaining sons and daughters. Her murderer Alessendro also was present at the canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Because of the huge number of visitors, the canonisation of Maria Goretti by Pope Pius XII,  was held outside at Piazza San Pietro on 24 June 1950. The Pope spoke, not as before in Latin, but in Italian. "We order and declare, that the blessed Maria Goretti can be venerated as a Saint and We introduce her into the Canon of Saints". Some 500,000 people, among them a majority of youth, had come from around the World. Pope Pius asked them:
</p>
<ul><li>Young people, pleasure of the eyes of Jesus, are you determined to resist any attack on your chastity with the help of grace of God?</li></ul>
<p>A resounding Yes was the answer.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Innocent_XI">Innocent XI</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Pius_XII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Innocent XI">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a>, personally a holy man, was highly controversial even hated, because of his opposition to the French monarchy and its aspirations for European hegemony; but also for his family's engagement in money-lending.  He opposed  French attempts to usurp the traditional liberties of the Church, for example in nominating its bishops. He opposed <a href="/wiki/Gallicanism" title="Gallicanism">Gallicanism</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Gallican_Liberties" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallican Liberties">Gallican Liberties</a> demanded by the <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> king. The case for his canonization was introduced in 1714 but the strong emotional and political influence of France forced a postponement until Pope Pius XII, who reintroduced the case, announced his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> on 7 October 1956.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Placet_Eugenio">Placet Eugenio</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Pius_XII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Placet Eugenio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <i>Placet Eugenio</i> was the required final approval  by the Pope of proposed candidates.  Not all, who had passed the Vatican tribunals, received the <i>Placet Eugenio</i>. In one instance, Pius found, that the candidate, supposedly a model of virtue,  had consistently used foul language.<sup id="cite_ref-Lehnert,_162_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lehnert,_162-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  Refusing to accept the Vatican defense, that this kind of language was custom in that region, he stopped the proceedings shortly before their conclusion. Another person, belonging to a large religious order, was refused the honour of the altars, because he turned out to be a chain smoker. "Monsignore, as long as I am alive, this Causa will not find approval". This case too was almost completed and the relatives and members of the order were quite disappointed.<sup id="cite_ref-Lehnert,_162_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lehnert,_162-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="American_Saints">American Saints</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=List_of_saints_canonized_by_Pope_Pius_XII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: American Saints">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>According to Halecki and Murray,  Pius has shown a particular interest in the discovery and recognition of sanctity among American religious leaders and pioneers of the Church movement. The first "American" saint was canonized during his pontificate, when <a href="/wiki/Mother_Cabrini" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother Cabrini">Mother Cabrini</a>, an Italian born nun with American citizenship, was raised to sainthood in St. Peter's Basilica.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Pius also accelerated the canonization of other Americans, including  American born <a href="/wiki/Mother_Seton" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother Seton">Mother Seton</a> of <a href="/wiki/Emmitsburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Emmitsburg">Emmitsburg</a>,  <a href="/wiki/Maryland" title="Maryland">Maryland</a>,  founder  of the Sisters of Charity. Fourteen  months after his death,  she was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> and was later beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> and canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>. Pope Pius supported also the case of  an  <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">American Indian</a>  woman from the <a href="/wiki/Mohawk_nation" class="mw-redirect" title="Mohawk nation">Mohawk</a> tribe, Saint <a href="/wiki/Kateri_Tekakwitha" title="Kateri Tekakwitha">Kateri Tekakwitha</a>, who was declared  "<a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>" by him in 1943.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
