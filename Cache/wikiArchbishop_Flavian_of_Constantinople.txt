<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color: #DFB0DF;"><div style="display:inline" class="fn">Flavian of Constantinople</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-subheader role">Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople</td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Installed</th><td class="infobox-data">446</td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Term ended</th><td class="infobox-data">449</td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Personal details</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Denomination</th><td class="infobox-data category"><a href="/wiki/Chalcedonian_Christianity" title="Chalcedonian Christianity">Chalcedonian Christianity</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Flavian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Flavianus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Φλαβιανος</span></span>, <i>Phlabianos</i>; <abbr title="died">d.</abbr>&#160;11&#160;August 449), sometimes <b>Flavian I</b>, was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> from 446 to 449. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Consecration_as_archbishop_and_imperial_dispute">Consecration as archbishop and imperial dispute</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavian_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Consecration as archbishop and imperial dispute">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flavian was a presbyter and the guardian of the sacred vessels of the great Church of Constantinople and, according to <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_Kallistos_Xanthopoulos" title="Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos">Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos</a>, was reputed to lead a saintly life, when he was chosen to succeed <a href="/wiki/Proclus_of_Constantinople" title="Proclus of Constantinople">Proclus</a> as Archbishop of Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecration</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a> was staying at <a href="/wiki/Chalcedon" title="Chalcedon">Chalcedon</a>. His eunuch <a href="/wiki/Chrysaphius" title="Chrysaphius">Chrysaphius</a> attempted to extort a present of gold to the Emperor but as he was unsuccessful, he began to plot against the new Archbishop by supporting the <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">archimandrite</a> <a href="/wiki/Eutyches" title="Eutyches">Eutyches</a> in his dispute with Flavian.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Home_Synod_of_Constantinople">Home Synod of Constantinople</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavian_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Home Synod of Constantinople">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flavian presided at a council of forty <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishops</a> at Constantinople on November 8, 448, to resolve a dispute between the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sardis" title="Sardis">Sardis</a> and two bishops of his province. <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Dorylaeum" title="Eusebius of Dorylaeum">Eusebius</a>, bishop of <a href="/wiki/Dorylaeum" title="Dorylaeum">Dorylaeum</a>, presented an <a href="/wiki/Indictment" title="Indictment">indictment</a> against <a href="/wiki/Eutyches" title="Eutyches">Eutyches</a>. The speech of Flavian remains, concluding with this appeal to the bishop of Dorylaeum: "Let your reverence condescend to visit him and argue with him about the true faith, and if he shall be found in very truth to err, then he shall be called to our holy assembly, and shall answer for himself." Eventually the synod deposed Eutyches.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_Council_of_Ephesus">Second Council of Ephesus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavian_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Second Council of Ephesus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>However, as Eutyches protested against this verdict and received the support of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Dioscorus_I_of_Alexandria" title="Pope Dioscorus I of Alexandria">Pope Dioscorus I of Alexandria</a>, the Emperor convoked <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Ephesus" title="Second Council of Ephesus">another Council to Ephesus</a>. At this council, which assembled on August 8, 449, Flavian was beaten during the sessions of this council by impudent monks led by a certain Barsumas.  Flavian was then deposed, exiled,<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and the council reinstated Eutyches.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavian_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flavian died on August 11, 449, at <a href="/wiki/Hypaepa" title="Hypaepa">Hypaepa</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lydia" title="Lydia">Lydia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a> and was buried obscurely.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Aftermath">Aftermath</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavian_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Aftermath">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_I" title="Pope Leo I">Pope Leo I</a>, whose legates had been ignored at the council, protested, first calling the council a "robber synod", and declared its decisions void.
</p><p>After Theodosius II died in 450, his sister Pulcheria returned to power, marrying the officer <a href="/wiki/Marcian" title="Marcian">Marcian</a>, who became Emperor. The new Imperial couple had Flavian's remains brought to Constantinople<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in a way that, in the words of a chronicler, more resembled "a triumph .. than a funeral procession". The <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>, called in 451, condemned Eutyches, confirmed Pope Leo's <i>Tome</i> (letter 28)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and canonized Flavian as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p><p>In the Roman Catholic Church St. Flavian is commemorated on February 18, the date assigned to him in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>.  <a href="/wiki/Flavian_of_Ricina" title="Flavian of Ricina">Flavian of Ricina</a> is sometimes identified with him.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
