<p><b>Philip Powell</b> (sometimes spelled <b>Philip Powel</b>) (2 February 1594 &#8211; 30 June 1646) was a <a href="/wiki/Lawyer" title="Lawyer">lawyer</a> who became a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, serving as a <a href="/wiki/Mission_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (Christian)">missionary</a> in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> during the period of <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusancy</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Martyred" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyred">martyred</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Powell_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Powell is usually said to have been born in <a href="/wiki/Trallong" title="Trallong">Trallong</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brecknockshire" title="Brecknockshire">Brecknockshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>. From his youth he was a student of <a href="/wiki/Law" title="Law">law</a>, taught principally by David Baker, (who would later become a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> himself, taking the name <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Baker" title="Augustine Baker">Augustine Baker</a>). At the age of sixteen he went to study at one of the <a href="/wiki/Inns_of_Court" title="Inns of Court">Inns of Court</a>, <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, and afterwards practiced <a href="/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system)" title="Civil law (legal system)">civil law</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Powell_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Three or four years later he received the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a>, becoming part of the community of St. Gregory at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> (now at <a href="/wiki/Downside_Abbey" title="Downside Abbey">Downside Abbey</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Bath,_Somerset" title="Bath, Somerset">Bath</a>). In 1618 he was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and in 1622 left Douai to go on <a href="/wiki/Mission_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (Christian)">mission</a> in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>. In around 1624 he became <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> to the Poyntz family at <a href="/wiki/Leighland,_Somerset" class="mw-redirect" title="Leighland, Somerset">Leighland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a>.
</p><p>When the <a href="/wiki/English_Civil_War" title="English Civil War">English Civil War</a> broke out he retired to <a href="/wiki/Yarnscombe" title="Yarnscombe">Yarnscombe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Parkham" title="Parkham">Parkham</a> in <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>. He then served for six months as chaplain to the Catholic soldiers in <a href="/wiki/George_Goring,_Lord_Goring" title="George Goring, Lord Goring">General Goring</a>'s army in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, and, when that force was disbanded, took ship for <a href="/wiki/South_Wales" title="South Wales">South Wales</a>. The vessel was captured on 22 February 1646, and Powell was recognised and denounced as a priest.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_martyrdom">Imprisonment and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Powell_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 11 May he was sent to London and confined in St. Catherine's Gaol, <a href="/wiki/Southwark" title="Southwark">Southwark</a>, where his treatment brought on a severe attack of <a href="/wiki/Pleurisy" title="Pleurisy">pleurisy</a>. His trial, which had been fixed for 30 May, did not take place until 9 June, at <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster#Westminster_Hall" title="Palace of Westminster">Westminster Hall</a>. He was found guilty of being a priest and was <a href="/wiki/Hanging,_drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanging, drawing and quartering">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a>. It is recorded that when informed of his death sentence, Powell exclaimed "Oh what am I that God thus honours me and will have me to die for his sake?" and called for a glass of <a href="/wiki/Sherry" title="Sherry">sack</a>.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/Pius_XI" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius XI">Pius XI</a> in 1929.
</p>
