<p><b>Eugène de Mazenod</b>, OMI (born <b>Charles-Joseph-Eugène de Mazenod</b>; 1 August 1782 – 21 May 1861) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> aristocrat and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_France" title="Catholic Church in France">Catholic</a> priest. Mazenod founded the congregation of the <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Oblates_of_Mary_Immaculate" title="Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate">Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate</a>. 
</p><p>When he was eight years old, Mazenod's family fled the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, leaving their considerable wealth behind. As refugees in <a href="/wiki/List_of_historic_states_of_Italy#After_the_Wars_of_Succession_of_the_18th_century" title="List of historic states of Italy">Italy</a>, they were poor, and moved from place to place. He returned to France at the age of twenty and later became a priest. Initially focused on rebuilding the Church in France after the Revolution, their work soon spread, particularly to <a href="/wiki/British_North_America" title="British North America">Canada</a>. Mazenod was appointed <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Marseille" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Marseille">Bishop of Marseille</a> in 1837, and Archbishop in 1851.
</p><p>Bishop de Mazenod was beatified on October 19, 1975, and was canonized twenty years later on 3 December 1995.  The Catholic Church commemorates him with an optional <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)#Obligatory_and_optional_memorials" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a> on 21 May, the anniversary of his death.
</p><p>Three schools are named for him, in the Australian cities of <a href="/wiki/St_Eugene_College" title="St Eugene College">Brisbane</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mazenod_College,_Perth" title="Mazenod College, Perth">Perth</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mazenod_College,_Victoria" title="Mazenod College, Victoria">Melbourne</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Refugee">Refugee</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Refugee">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Eugène de Mazenod was born on 1 August 1782 and baptized the following day in the <a href="/wiki/%C3%89glise_de_la_Madeleine_(Aix-en-Provence)" title="Église de la Madeleine (Aix-en-Provence)">Église de la Madeleine</a> in <a href="/wiki/Aix-en-Provence" title="Aix-en-Provence">Aix-en-Provence</a>. His father, Charles Antoine de Mazenod, was one of the Presidents of the Court of Finances, and his mother was Marie Rose Joannis. Eugène began his schooling at the College Bourbon, but this was interrupted by the events of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. With the approach of the French revolutionary forces, the family  fled to <a href="/wiki/List_of_historic_states_of_Italy" title="List of historic states of Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eugène became a boarder at the College of Nobles in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> (<a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>), but a move to <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Venice</a> meant the end to formal schooling.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> With their money running out, Eugène's father sought various employments, none of which were successful. His mother and sister returned to France - eventually seeking a divorce so as to be able to regain their  seized property. Eugène was fortunate to be welcomed by the Zinelli family in Venice. One of their sons, the priest Bartolo Zinelli, took special care of Eugène and saw to his education in the well-provided family library where the young adolescent spent many hours each day. Don Bartolo was a major influence in the human, academic and spiritual development of Eugène.
</p><p>Once again the French army chased the émigrés  from Venice, forcing Eugène and his father and two uncles to seek refuge in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Naples</a> for less than a year, and finally to flee to <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily" title="Kingdom of Sicily">Sicily</a>. Here Eugène was invited to become part of the household of the Duke and Duchess of Cannizaro as a companion to their two sons. Being part of the high society of Sicily became the opportunity for Eugène to rediscover his noble origins and to live a lavish style of life. He took to himself the title of 'Comte' ("Count") de Mazenod, did all the courtly things, and dreamed of a bright future.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion">Conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3><p>
At the age of twenty, Eugène returned to France and lived with his mother in Aix en Provence. Initially he enjoyed all the pleasures of Aix as a rich young nobleman, intent on the pursuit of pleasure and money - and a rich girl who would bring a good dowry. Gradually he became aware of how empty his life was,<sup id="cite_ref-omi_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-omi-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and began to search for meaning in more regular church involvement, reading and personal study, and charitable work among prisoners. His journey came to a climax on Good Friday, 1807 when he was 25 years old. Looking at the sight of the Cross, he had a religious experience.  He recounted the spiritual experience in his retreat journal: </p><blockquote><p>Can I forget the bitter tears that the sight of the cross brought streaming from my eyes one Good Friday? Indeed they welled up from the heart, there was no checking them, they were too abundant for me to be able to hide them from those who like myself were assisting at that moving ceremony. I was in a state of mortal sin and it was precisely this that made me grieve…Blessed, a thousand times blessed, that he, this good Father, notwithstanding my unworthiness, lavished on me all the richness of his mercy.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Priest">Priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1808, he began his studies for the priesthood at the <a href="/wiki/Saint-Sulpice_Seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Sulpice Seminary">Saint-Sulpice Seminary</a> in Paris and was ordained a priest at <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a> (<a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>), on 21 December 1811.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Since Napoleon had expelled the Sulpician priests from the seminary, Eugène stayed on as a formator for a semester. As a member of the Seminary, notwithstanding personal risk, Eugène committed himself to serve and assist <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a>, who at this time was a prisoner of emperor <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleon I">Napoleon I</a> at <a href="/wiki/Fontainebleau" title="Fontainebleau">Fontainebleau</a>. In this way he experienced at firsthand the suffering of the post-Revolutionary Church.
</p><p>On his return to Aix, Father de Mazenod asked not to be assigned to a parish but to dedicate himself fully to evangelizing those who were not being reached by the structures of the local church: the poor who spoke only the Provençal language, prisoners, youth, the inhabitants of poor villages who were ignorant of their faith.<sup id="cite_ref-omi_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-omi-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The goal of his priestly preaching and ministry was always to lead others to develop themselves fully as humans, then as Christians and finally to become saints.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Oblates_of_Mary_Immaculate">Oblates of Mary Immaculate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Oblates of Mary Immaculate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 25  January 1816, "impelled by a strong impulse from outside of himself" he invited other priests to join him in his life of total oblation to God and to the most abandoned of Provence. Initially called "Missionaries of Provence," they dedicated themselves to evangelization through preaching parish missions in the poor villages, youth and prison ministry. In 1818 a second community was established at the Marian shrine of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Laus" title="Our Lady of Laus">Notre Dame du Laus</a>. This became the occasion for the missionaries to become a religious congregation, united through vows and the evangelical counsels. Changing their name to Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate, the group received papal approbation on 17 February 1826.<sup id="cite_ref-Morice_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Morice-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foreign_Missions">Foreign Missions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Foreign Missions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1841, Bishop Bourget of Montreal invited the Oblates to Canada. At the same time, there was an outreach to the <a href="/wiki/British_Isles" title="British Isles">British Isles</a>. This was the beginning of a history of missionary outreach to the most abandoned peoples in Canada, United States, Mexico, England and Ireland, Algeria, Southern Africa and Ceylon during the founder's lifetime. In 200 years this zeal spread and took root in the establishment of the Oblates in nearly 70 countries. 
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop">Bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After having aided for some time his uncle, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fortun%C3%A9_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fortuné de Mazenod (page does not exist)">Fortuné de Mazenod</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortun%C3%A9_de_Mazenod" class="extiw" title="fr:Fortuné de Mazenod">fr</a>&#93;</span>, the aged Bishop of Marseilles, in the administration of his diocese, Father De Mazenod was called to Rome and, on 14 October 1832, consecrated titular Bishop of Icosium, which title in 1837 he exchanged for that of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Marseille" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Marseille">Bishop of Marseilles</a>, a position he held until his death in 1861. During his episcopacy, he commissioned <a href="/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_la_Garde" title="Notre-Dame de la Garde">Notre-Dame de la Garde</a>, an ornate <a href="/wiki/Neo-Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Neo-Byzantine">Neo-Byzantine</a> <a href="/wiki/Basilicas_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Basilicas in the Catholic Church">basilica</a> on the south side of the old port of Marseille. He favoured the moral teachings of <a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Liguori" title="Alphonsus Liguori">Alphonsus Liguori</a>, whose theological system he was the first to introduce in France, and whose first biography in French he caused to be written by one of the Oblates.<sup id="cite_ref-Morice_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Morice-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He inspired local priest <a href="/wiki/Joseph-Marie_Timon-David" title="Joseph-Marie Timon-David">Joseph-Marie Timon-David</a> to found the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">Congregation of the Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> in Marseille in 1852.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In spite of his well-known outspokenness, he was made a Peer of the French Empire, and in 1851 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> gave him the pallium.<sup id="cite_ref-Morice_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Morice-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Mazenod&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg/220px-Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg/330px-Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/85/Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg/440px-Caloocan_Malabon_Valenzuela_Districts_01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3888" data-file-height="5184" /></a><figcaption>Diocesan Shrine and Parish at Our Lady of Grace, <a href="/wiki/Caloocan" title="Caloocan">Caloocan</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Some forty-five years after his death, the Diocese of Marseille opened a three-year-long investigation for the cause for Bishop de Mazenod's canonization. In 1935, the cause was opened by the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Congregation_of_Rites" title="Sacred Congregation of Rites">Sacred Congregation of Rites</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">Vatican</a>. In May 1970, the Congregation for the Causes of Saints recognized the heroic virtue of Eugène's life, and he was proclaimed "<a href="/wiki/The_Venerable#Catholic" title="The Venerable">venerable</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Five years later, after the same congregation attributed miracles of healing to Eugène's intercession, and, following that, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> Bishop de Mazenod in Rome on 19 October 1975.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
In December 1994, the Congregation for Saints approved another miracle attributed to Blessed Eugène's intercession; <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">John Paul II</a> celebrated his Mass of <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Catholic_Church" title="Canonization">canonization</a> in 1995, on 3 December.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In his homily at the Mass, celebrated on the First Sunday of <a href="/wiki/Advent" title="Advent">Advent</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> the Pope proclaimed Saint Eugène a "Man of Advent", saying:</p><blockquote><p>Eugène de Mazenod, whom the Church today proclaims a saint, was a man of Advent, a man of the Coming. He not only looked forward to that Coming, but... he dedicated his whole life to preparing for it. His waiting reached the intensity of heroism, that is, it was marked by a heroic degree of faith, hope and apostolic charity. Eugène de Mazenod was one of those apostles who prepared the modern age, our age.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
