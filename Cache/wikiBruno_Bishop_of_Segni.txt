<p><b>Bruno di Segni</b> (c. 1045 – 18 July 1123) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> and professed member from the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Order of Saint Benedict</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Segni" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Segni">Bishop of Segni</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Montecassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Montecassino">Abbot of Montecassino</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He studied under the Benedictines in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> before being appointed a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Siena_Cathedral" title="Siena Cathedral">cathedral chapter of Siena</a>. He he was invited to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> where he became a bishop and counselled four consecutive popes.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He served as an abbot in <a href="/wiki/Montecassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Montecassino">Montecassino</a> but his chastising <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a> on the Concordat of Ponte Mammolo in 1111 prompted the pope to relieve him of his duties as abbot and order Bruno to return to his diocese, where he died just over a decade later.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Bruno's canonization was celebrated on 5 September 1181 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_III" title="Pope Lucius III">Pope Lucius III</a> who presided over the celebration in the late bishop's diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_career">Early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bruno was born circa 1045 in <a href="/wiki/Solero_(AL)" class="mw-redirect" title="Solero (AL)">Solero</a> either to nobles or parents of modest means named Andrea and Guglielmina.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He spent his theological education in a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of Santa Perpetua near his town in <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">college</a> there where he also studied humanities and the liberal arts.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was around this time that he wrote one of his earliest works, <i>Expositio in psalterium Gallicanum</i>, dedicated to his bishop, Ingo of Asti (1072-1079).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He became a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a> in 1073, and recollects his life among the canons;<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> perhaps his <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> belongs to the same time,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and around that time was assigned as a pastor there.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> This happened after he decided to go to <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Montecassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Montecassino">Montecassino</a> to be a monk but during the trip fell ill in Siena where he remained subject to the needs of Bishop Rudolfus (1073–1083), who named Bruno as a canon of the cathedral Chapter.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bruno became noted for his defending orthodox faith and for his extensive knowledge of <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Scripture" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred Scripture">Sacred Scripture</a> and great piousness. Canon Bruno was in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in 1079,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and participated in the Sixth Roman Synod of Pope Gregory VII in February 1079. He was one of those who spoke to the assembly on the theory of <a href="/wiki/Transubstantiation" title="Transubstantiation">transubstantiation</a> and the theology of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>, against the teaching of <a href="/wiki/Berengarius_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Berengarius of Tours">Berengarius of Tours</a>; at the synod Berengarius renounced his heresy and was absolved by Pope Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In Rome he was a guest of <a href="/wiki/Peter_Igneus" title="Peter Igneus">Pietro Igneo</a>, the Bishop of Albano.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Pope Gregory appointed the Bishop of Albano to go to Segni with Bruno, and to persuade the canons of the cathedral Chapter to elect Bruno as their bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Bruno was appointed as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Segni" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Segni">Bishop of Segni</a><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> in 1079 after the canons of the cathedral of Segni selected him. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a> himself, a good friend of Bruno who often sought his counsel, <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">consecrated him a bishop</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Peter the Deacon, when the previous bishop of Segni, Erasmus, came to die, the pope asked Bruno to step in as administrator of the Church of Segni. As is traditional in such cases, Bruno pleaded his unworthiness and attempted to refuse. The pope was determined, however, and Bruno set off for Segni. In the meantime, the pope sent a mitre to the leaders of the church at Segni, ordering them to have Bruno consecrated their bishop. When Bruno realized what was going on, he attempted to flee, but in the middle of the night on the road he met a lady dressed in imperial regalia whose face shone like the sun (a personification of the Church of Segni), who chided him for fleeing from his bride. Struck by this, Bruno returned and allowed himself to be consecrated bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During May and June 1081, the Emperor-elect Henry IV brought his army and his <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Clement III</a> (Wibert of Ravenna) to the neighborhood of Rome, and besieged the city. He had to withdraw to the north in the summer, but next year he returned and spent the entire Lenten season.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> At one of these times, Bruno was travelling from Segni to Rome when Adolfo di Segni, a supporter of Henry IV, who was ambitious to become Lord of Segni in place of the bishop, seized and imprisoned Bruno for three months.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> He was freed and returned to Rome. King Henry returned at the end of 1082 for a siege of seven months, during which fierce fighting in and around the Leonine City caused Pope Gregory to seek refuge in the <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27_Angelo" class="mw-redirect" title="Castel Sant&#39; Angelo">Castel Sant' Angelo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Bruno was imprisoned once more, in the Castel Sant'Angelo alongside the pope.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Hartmuth Hoffmann remarks that it is doubtful that during the following decade he would have had the opportunity to deal with his own diocese as usual.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bishop Bruno of Segni attended the eighth Roman synod of Pope Gregory VII, on 4 May 1082.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Career_in_Roman_curia">Career in Roman curia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Career in Roman curia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_III" title="Pope Victor III">Pope Victor III</a> (1086–1087)<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> named him the Librarian of the Holy Roman Church,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> and he held the position until he left for Montecassino in 1099.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> By 1 July 1089, Cardinal Giovanni Gaetani, Cardinal Deacon of S. Maria in Cosmedin, was papal chancellor.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a> was <a href="/wiki/1088_papal_election" title="1088 papal election">elected on 12 March 1088</a>. Bishop Bruno accompanied Pope Urban on his tour of southern Italy, and was present at Salerno on 14 January 1093, where he subscribed a grant of privileges to Abbot Peter of Cava.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> On 24 August 1093, he was present at the monastery of S. Maria de Panso, a dependency of Montecassino, where the pope consecrated the major church, and Bruno consecrated the chapel in the cloister.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> He subscribed a papal document dated 6 February 1094 at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Nuova" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Maria Nuova">Santa Maria Nuova</a> in Rome;<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> he also subscribed on 19 March and 5 April.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:312px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II,_h._1655.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II%2C_h._1655.png/310px-Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II%2C_h._1655.png" decoding="async" width="310" height="255" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II%2C_h._1655.png/465px-Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II%2C_h._1655.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II%2C_h._1655.png/620px-Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II%2C_h._1655.png 2x" data-file-width="750" data-file-height="617" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Zurbar%C3%A1n_-_La_visita_de_San_Bruno_a_Urbano_II,_h._1655.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Zurbarán&#160;: S. Bruno and Pope Urban II<br /> oil on canvas c. 1630</div></div></div>
<p>In 1095, Pope Urban was invited by Countess <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Tuscany" title="Matilda of Tuscany">Matilda of Tuscany</a> to visit her domains. Urban held a council at Piacenza during the first week of March, and was gratified and encouraged by the reception he received from every quarter. He determined to carry his message of a crusade into France, his homeland. Bruno had connections to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a>, and is found in the papal entourage at Tarascon near Avignon on 11 September 1095, at <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> on 13 September, and at <a href="/wiki/Cluny" title="Cluny">Cluny</a> on 25 October.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Bruno accompanied the pope to the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Clermont" title="Council of Clermont">Council of Clermont</a> on 18 November 1095, where the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a> was inaugurated. After the council, Bruno accompanied the pope to <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a> from 23–31 December. On 31 December the pope consecrated the new basilica in the monastery of S. Martial, and Bishop Bruno was present.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The papal party then proceeded to <a href="/wiki/Charroux,_Vienne" title="Charroux, Vienne">Charroux</a> before going on to <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a> on 22 January 1096. Bruno accompanied Urban II to <a href="/wiki/Moyenmoutier" title="Moyenmoutier">Moyenmoutier</a>, near Tours, where, on 3 March, he was invited to consecrate the <i>capella infirmorum</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>  They were at <a href="/wiki/Tours,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Tours, France">Tours</a> from 3–25 March, where the pope held a synod; and then to Poitiers again from 29–31 March. Their last two destinations were <a href="/wiki/N%C3%AEmes" title="Nîmes">Nîmes</a> on 12 July, where Pope Urban held a synod, and <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Gilles" title="Abbey of Saint-Gilles">Saint-Gilles</a> on 20 July.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> In August 1096, the papal suite began its return to Italy, and was in Asti on 9 September; they reached Rome by Christmas.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Urban II died in Rome on 29 July 1099, and <a href="/wiki/1099_papal_election" title="1099 papal election">was succeeded</a> on 13 August 1099 by Cardinal Rainerius da Bieda, who took the name <a href="/wiki/Paschal_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Paschal II">Paschal II</a>.
</p>
<div style="clear:both;"></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bruno_and_Paschal_II">Bruno and Paschal II</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Bruno and Paschal II">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the summer of 1100, Pope Paschal embarked on a tour of south Italy. Bishop Bruno accompanied him, and subscribed a papal privilege for the benefit of Abbot Peter of Cava at Salerno on 30 August 1100.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bernhard Gigalski argues that it was in the second half of 1102, perhaps between August and December, that Bishop Bruno entered the monastic state at Montecassino. Bruno had accompanied Pope Paschal on his trip to Benevento, and had taken part in Paschal's synod there. During the trip he became seriously ill, and decided to resign his secular pursuits and enter the cloister.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> He points to the statement in the anonymous life (IV. 5)<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> that it had been five years (<i>quinquennio peracto</i>) after his entry that Bruno was elected abbot on 13 November 1107.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> Peter the Deacon of Montecassino adds that the people of Segni were put out by Bruno's decision, and complained to Pope Paschal. The pope sent messengers to Bruno, ordering him to "take care of his sheep" and always make himself available to the pope for ecclesiastical cases; the pope also demanded to know why he retired to a monastery without papal permission. Bruno refused the pope and the people of Segni, which caused the pope to ask Abbot Oderisius of Montecassino to allow Bruno to serve the Roman church for forty days every year.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Paschal visited <a href="/wiki/Civita_Castellana" title="Civita Castellana">Civita Castellana</a> in September and early October 1105.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Bruno subscribed a bull confirming Henricus in the office of provost of S. Donato on 8 September.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He served as a legate to France twice in 1104 and 1106. He undertook a mission to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">Kingdom of France</a> along with Prince <a href="/wiki/Bohemond_I_of_Antioch" title="Bohemond I of Antioch">Bohemond I of Antioch</a> for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a> in 1106, and held a council at Poitiers on 25 June 1106. The principal purpose of the mission was the preaching of the crusade.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> The pope spent the first two weeks of November 1106 in <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a>, and on 2 November 1106 Bishop Bruno of Segni subscribed .<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> He remained with the pope for several months after his return to Italy, before returning to his cloister.
</p><p>Abbot Oderisius had died on 2 December 1105, and had been succeeded by Abbot Otto, who died on 1 October 1107. Forty-four days later, on 13 November 1107, Bruno of Segni was elected <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of Montecassino.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In October 1108, Pope Paschal visited Montecassino on his way to Benevento. Bruno accompanied Pope Paschal to attend the synod.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>  In the synod he repeated his objections to lay investiture, and he forbade clerics from wearing expensive secular clothes. He also consecrated Landolfo as Archbishop of Benevento on 8 November 1108.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bruno was present in Segni on 4 June 1109, to welcome the pope who had come to celebrate the canonization for Bruno's old friend <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Anagni" title="Peter of Anagni">Pietro di Anagni</a>. Bruno is credited with having written the <i>postulatio</i>. Peter, a Crusader and church builder, had died only four years earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Paschal made no objection to the pluralism involved in Bruno's episcopal and abbatial offices, until Bruno condemned the pope's signing of the Concordat of Ponte Mammolo in 1111.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> The pope and sixteen cardinals, including Bishop Pietro of Porto, had held captive for sixty-one days, while <a href="/wiki/Henry_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry V, Holy Roman Emperor">King Henry V</a> pressed the pope to agree to his solution to the investiture controversy.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> In order to persuade the king to a compromise, Paschal granted the emperor a <i>privilegium</i> in the matter of investiture.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> On 18 April, at Ponte Mammolo on the Anio River, the cardinals who prisoners along with the pope were compelled to sign the papal promise to observe the agreement which Henry had drawn up.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> The <i>privilegium</i> was immediately denounced in all quarters. Bruno was minded to write a letter directly to Pope Paschal, complaining that he had heard that his enemies were telling Paschal that he was speaking badly about the pope; he condemned the treaty entered into so treacherously; he asked Paschal to confirm once again his apostolic constitution against investiture.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> When the bishops of Lucca and Parma and the ministers of the Camaldolese and Vallombrosian chapters asked his views on investiture as heresy and what the pope was thinking, Bruno imprudently wrote that the pope did not like him and did not consult him; but that he himself continued to follow the policy of Gregory VII and Urban II. He invited his correspondents to share his statements with whomever they wished.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> At a council held at the Lateran in  March 1112, Paschal was compelled to retract his grant and modify his opinions in the investiture controversy. Bishop-Abbot Bruno, a  vigorous opponent of investiture in any case, was present at the Lateran council of 1112.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> He made his opinion public in an extreme way in the Lateran synod of March 1116.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> Paschal gave another speech justifying his conduct, and Bruno arose to say, "Let us praise God and give Him thanks, because our lord and head repents that he had subscribed to that  heresy." This produced an uproar, challenging Bruno's claim that investiture was a heresy, and claiming that Paschal had come to an agreement with King Henry under duress.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_the_diocese">Return to the diocese</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Return to the diocese">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bruno's chastising the pope led to the frustrated Paschal II in turn chastising Bruno for shirking his duties, and obliged him to resign his position as the abbot of Montecassino and return to Segni as its bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> Paschal sent a letter to the monks of Montecassino, ordering them to cease their obedience to Bruno and to proceed to elect a new abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> Abbot Bruno departed Montecassino on 13 October 1111, and was succeeded by Abbot Gerardus on 17 October 1111.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bruno once made a serious error when he claimed that priests who committed simoniacal acts could not perform the Sacraments but he was proven to be wrong since it did not undo the sacrament of ordination despite how severe it was.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Pope Paschal died on 21 January 1118, he was succeeded by Paschal's chancellor and supporter, Cardinal Giovanni Gaetani (<a href="/wiki/Gelasius_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Gelasius II">Gelasius II</a>, which left Bruno's situation unchanged.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup> The signing of the <a href="/wiki/Concordat_of_Worms" title="Concordat of Worms">Concordat of Worms</a> on 23 September 1122, and its ratification by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Calixtus_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Calixtus II">Pope Calixtus II</a> at the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="First Council of the Lateran">First Lateran Council</a> on 27–30 March 1123, ended the discussion over lay investiture.<sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bishop Bruno died at <a href="/wiki/Segni" title="Segni">Segni</a> in mid-1123, on 3 July or 31 August, and was buried in the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup> His feast day as a saint, 18 July, is sometimes taken as his date of death.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Cardinal_.3F"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinal_?">Cardinal&#160;?</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Cardinal ?">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There is a dispute as to whether or not Bruno had been made a cardinal.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> It is said that he declined the cardinalate, while other sources suggest he had been made the Cardinal-Bishop of Segni even though the <a href="/wiki/Suburbicarian_diocese" title="Suburbicarian diocese">suburbicarian diocese</a> had not existed at that stage. Some scholars suggest that Urban II named him as cardinal in 1086, while others believe that he was named a cardinal on 18 July 1079.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bruno's published works are considered to be <a href="/wiki/Exegetical" class="mw-redirect" title="Exegetical">exegetical</a> for the most part. He condemned simoniacal practices in a document written prior to 1109 entitled <i>Libellus de simoniacis</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup> He authored commentaries on the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Job" title="Book of Job">Book of Job</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a> as well as on the four <a href="/wiki/Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospels">Gospels</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Bruno also wrote on the lives of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo IX</a> and Pietro di Anagni. There are 145 homilies of his that are still preserved.<sup id="cite_ref-MJ_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His works are collected in: J. P. Migne (ed.), <i>Patrologiae Latinae Collectio</i>, Vols. <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://books.google.com/books?id=qRpAAQAAMAAJ&amp;printsec=frontcover&amp;dq=editions:1kXE7eDwhtwC&amp;hl=en&amp;newbks=1&amp;newbks_redir=0&amp;sa=X&amp;ved=2ahUKEwjUmLygkLD0AhVKkWoFHbGNB5sQ6wF6BAgGEAE#v=onepage&amp;q&amp;f=false">CLXIV</a> and <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://books.google.com/books?id=yRpAAQAAMAAJ&amp;dq=editions%3A1kXE7eDwhtwC&amp;source=gbs_similarbooks">CLXV</a>.
</p><p>An anonynmous "Life of Bruno" (also called "Acta Brunonis") was written in the second half of the 12th century.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_Segni)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bruno's canonization was celebrated under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_III" title="Pope Lucius III">Pope Lucius III</a> on 5 September 1181 in Segni. He is considered the patron saint for Segni.<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup> In 1584, a special Office of the liturgy in his honor was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII" title="Pope Gregory XIII">Pope Gregory XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
