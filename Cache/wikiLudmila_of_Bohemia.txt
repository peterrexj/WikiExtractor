<p><b>Ludmila of Bohemia</b> (c. 860 &#8211; 15 September 921) is a <a href="/wiki/Czech_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Czech people">Czech</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Christianity">Orthodox</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholics">Roman Catholics</a>. She was born in <a href="/wiki/M%C4%9Bln%C3%ADk" title="Mělník">Mělník</a> as the daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Sorbs" title="Sorbs">Sorbian</a> prince <a href="/wiki/Slavibor" title="Slavibor">Slavibor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Saint Ludmila was the grandmother of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Wenceslaus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Wenceslaus">Saint Wenceslaus</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who is widely referred to as Good King Wenceslaus. Saint Ludmila was canonized shortly after her death. As part of the process of canonization, in 925, Wenceslaus moved her remains to <a href="/wiki/St._George%27s_Basilica,_Prague" title="St. George&#39;s Basilica, Prague">St. George's Basilica, Prague</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludmila_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ludmila was married to <a href="/wiki/Bo%C5%99ivoj_I_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bořivoj I of Bohemia">Bořivoj I of Bohemia</a>, the first Christian <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bohemia">Duke of Bohemia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in 873. The couple converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> through the efforts of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Methodius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ott_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Their efforts to convert <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a> to Christianity were initially not well received,<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and pagans drove them from their country for a time.  Eventually the couple returned and ruled for several years before retiring to <a href="/wiki/Tet%C3%ADn_(Beroun_District)" title="Tetín (Beroun District)">Tetín</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Beroun" title="Beroun">Beroun</a>.
</p><p>Bořivoj was succeeded by their son <a href="/wiki/Spytihn%C4%9Bv_I_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Spytihněv I of Bohemia">Spytihněv</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Spytihněv was succeeded by his brother <a href="/wiki/Vratislav_I_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Vratislav I of Bohemia">Vratislav</a>. When Vratislav died in 921, his son <a href="/wiki/Wenceslas_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Wenceslas I, Duke of Bohemia">Wenceslas</a> became the next ruler of Bohemia.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It had been primarily Ludmila who raised her grandson, and she acted as regent for him.
</p>
<h2><span id="Ludmila_and_Drahom.C3.ADra"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Ludmila_and_Drahomíra">Ludmila and Drahomíra</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludmila_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ludmila and Drahomíra">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg/220px-Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="126" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg/330px-Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg/440px-Dalimilova_kronika_vrazda_Ludmily.jpg 2x" data-file-width="989" data-file-height="567" /></a><figcaption>Murder of Saint Ludmila</figcaption></figure>
<p>Wenceslaus' mother, <a href="/wiki/Drahom%C3%ADra" title="Drahomíra">Drahomíra</a>, became jealous of Ludmila's influence over Wenceslaus. She had two noblemen, Tunna and Gommon (probably of Frankish or Varangian descent) murder Ludmila in Tetín, and part of Ludmila's narrative states that she was strangled<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> with her veil. Initially, Ludmila was buried at St. Michael's at Tetín.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ludmila was canonized shortly after her death. As part of the process of canonization, in 925 Wenceslaus moved her remains to <a href="/wiki/St._George%27s_Basilica,_Prague" title="St. George&#39;s Basilica, Prague">St. George's Basilica, Prague</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She is venerated as a patroness of Bohemia. She is considered to be a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Bohemia, converts, <a href="/wiki/Duchess" class="mw-redirect" title="Duchess">duchesses</a>, those with problems with in-laws, and <a href="/wiki/Widow" title="Widow">widows</a>. Her feast day is celebrated on September 16th.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k" title="Antonín Dvořák">Antonín Dvořák</a> composed his oratorio <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Ludmila_(oratorio)" title="Saint Ludmila (oratorio)">Saint Ludmila</a></i> between September 1885 and May 1886. The work was commissioned by the publisher Littleton for the <a href="/wiki/Leeds_Festival_(classical_music)" title="Leeds Festival (classical music)">Leeds Festival</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
