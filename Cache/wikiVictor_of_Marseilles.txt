<p><b>Victor of Marseilles</b> (died c. 290) was an <a href="/wiki/Egyptians" title="Egyptians">Egyptian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a>. He is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victor_of_Marseilles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Victor is said to have been a <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a> officer in <a href="/wiki/Marseille" title="Marseille">Marseille</a>, who publicly denounced the worship of <a href="/wiki/Cult_image" title="Cult image">idols</a>. For that, he was brought before the Roman <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefects</a>, Asterius and Eutychius, who later sent him to the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was then racked, beaten, dragged through the streets, and thrown into prison, where he converted three other Roman soldiers, Longinus, Alexander, and Felician, who were subsequently beheaded. After refusing to offer <a href="/wiki/Incense" title="Incense">incense</a> to a statue of the Roman god <a href="/wiki/Jupiter_(god)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jupiter (god)">Jupiter</a>, Victor kicked it over with his foot. The emperor ordered that he be put to death by being ground under a millstone, but the millstone broke while Victor was still alive. He was then beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victor_of_Marseilles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Abbaye_Saint-Victor_(Marseille).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Abbaye_Saint-Victor_%28Marseille%29.jpg/150px-Abbaye_Saint-Victor_%28Marseille%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Abbaye_Saint-Victor_%28Marseille%29.jpg/225px-Abbaye_Saint-Victor_%28Marseille%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Abbaye_Saint-Victor_%28Marseille%29.jpg/300px-Abbaye_Saint-Victor_%28Marseille%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1224" data-file-height="1632" /></a><figcaption>Abbey of St Victor</figcaption></figure>
<p>Victor and the three other Roman soldiers he converted &#8211; Longinus, Alexander and Felician &#8211; were killed near the end of the 3rd century. In the 4th century, <a href="/wiki/John_Cassian" title="John Cassian">John Cassian</a> built a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> over the site where their bodies had been buried in a cave, which later became a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbey and <a href="/wiki/Minor_basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Minor basilica">minor basilica</a>.  This is the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St_Victor,_Marseille" title="Abbey of St Victor, Marseille">Abbey of St Victor</a> (<i>Abbaye Saint-Victor</i>).
</p><p>Saint Victor's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>, along with Saints Longinus, Alexander and Felician, is celebrated on July 21.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victor_of_Marseilles&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Victor is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Tallinn" title="Tallinn">Tallinn</a>, the capital of <a href="/wiki/Estonia" title="Estonia">Estonia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His life and martyrdom are celebrated in the scenes depicted on the high altar of <a href="/wiki/St._Nicholas%27_Church,_Tallinn" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Nicholas&#39; Church, Tallinn">St. Nicholas' Church, Tallinn</a>.
</p>
