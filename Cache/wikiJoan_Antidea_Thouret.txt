<p><b>Jeanne-Antide Thouret</b> (27 November 1765 - 24 August 1826;<sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-4412_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-4412-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret2_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> also called <b>Joan Antide Thouret</b> and <b>Jane Antide</b><sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-4412_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-4412-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cna-thouret2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-thouret2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>) was born  in <a href="/wiki/Sancey" title="Sancey">Sancey</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Franche-Comt%C3%A9" title="Franche-Comté">Franche-Comté</a> region of eastern France, on November 27, 1765, the fifth child of a poor<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret2_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cna-thouret2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-thouret2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and "deeply Christian family".<sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-4412_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-4412-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was baptized the day she was born and was named after her godmother. She had three older brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-thouret-life_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thouret-life-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Thouret "felt a strong attraction to the stricter religious life and at the same time to the service of the poor"<sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-4412_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-4412-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> at a young age. Her mother died when she was sixteen years old; she cared for her family and siblings, despite conflict with her aunt who disagreed with her father's decision to allow her to care for her siblings. When she was 22, against the wishes of her family who wanted her to marry, Thouret entered the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul</a> to serve the poor and work in hospitals, first in <a href="/wiki/Langres" title="Langres">Langres</a> and then in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret2_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cna-thouret2_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-thouret2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-thouret-life_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thouret-life-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> While a <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulant</a>, she had what she described as her first "encounter" with <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">St. Vincent de Paul</a>, establishing what she considered the close father-daughter relationship with him that lasted her whole life.<sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-4412_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-4412-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>On August 15, 1797, Thouret founded a school for poor girls in <a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on" title="Besançon">Besançon</a> and on April 11, 1799, founded the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity" title="Sisters of Charity">Sisters of Charity</a> and with two young women, founded a soup kitchen for the poor and a free school for girls, also in Besançon. During the French Revolution, when religious communities were suppressed and many priests and religious were killed, she was ordered to return to her family's home, but she refused and was badly beaten when she tried to escape the authorities. Thouret returned to Sancy in 1797, where she founded a small school for girls and worked with the sick until she had to flee to Switzerland, Germany, and then back to Switzerland in 1799, where she opened a school, hospital, and a congregation called the Institute of the Daughters of St. Vincent de Paul, which eventually expanded into France and Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cna-thouret_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-thouret-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From May to September 1802, Thouret revised a Rule of Life for her community. Accompanied by several sisters "attracted by her ideal of life",<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> she opened new schools and hospitals for the poor. On September 23, 1802, she began working with prisoners in <a href="/wiki/Bellevaux" title="Bellevaux">Bellevaux</a>, teaching, providing them with food, and organizing the prisoners' "work permitting them to receive a salary".<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1807, her community was officially named the "Sisters of Charity of Besançon". On May 8, 1810, she and some sisters travelled to <a href="/wiki/Savoy" title="Savoy">Savoy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thonon-les-Bains" title="Thonon-les-Bains">Thonon</a>, and a little later, with eight sisters, to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, where she cared for "Incurables" at a hospital. She also opened a school and a pharmacy at a convent they were given. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a> approved their community, which he named “Sisters of Charity under the protection of St. Vincent de Paul", on July 23, 1819. As of 2020, there were 4,000 sisters, spread over 27 countries, in the Sisters of Charity community, who provide a large variety of services for the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-453_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-453-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> "Community life, the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Paschal_mystery" title="Paschal mystery">Paschal Mystery</a> are today, as they were for [Thouret], the key elements of their life".<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thouret died of natural causes in Naples on August 24, 1826. She was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on May 23, 1926 and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on January 14, 1934 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cna-thouret_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-thouret-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-453_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-453-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Her statue appears among the 39 founders of religious orders at <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> in Vatican City.<sup id="cite_ref-rogatti-453_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rogatti-453-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is May 23.<sup id="cite_ref-DoC-thouret_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DoC-thouret-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
