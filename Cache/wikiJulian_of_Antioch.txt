<p><b>Julian of Antioch</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Julianus</i>; <abbr title="died">d.</abbr>&#160;AD&#160;305&#160;<abbr title="sometime between">x</abbr> 311),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> variously distinguished as <span class="nowrap"><b>Julian the Martyr</b>,</span> <span class="nowrap"><b>Julian of Tarsus</b>,</span> <span class="nowrap"><b>Julian of Cilicia</b>,</span> and <span class="nowrap"><b>Julian of Anazarbus</b>,</span> was a 4th-century <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">saint</a>. He is sometimes confused with the <a href="/wiki/Julian_and_Basilissa" title="Julian and Basilissa">St Julian</a> who was martyred with his wife <a href="/wiki/Julian_and_Basilissa" title="Julian and Basilissa">Basilissa</a>.
<span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span><span class="anchor" id="History"></span><span class="anchor" id="Legend"></span>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julian_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Senate" title="Roman Senate">senatorial</a> rank, he was killed during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">persecutions</a> of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. 
</p><p>His legend states that he was subjected to terrible tortures, and paraded daily for a whole year through the various cities of <a href="/wiki/Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a>. He was then sewn up in a sack half-filled with scorpions, sand, and vipers, then cast into the sea. The waters carried his body to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, and he was buried there before his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> to Antioch.<sup id="cite_ref-Hagiography_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hagiography-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julian_of_Antioch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Saint_John_Chrysostom" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John Chrysostom">Saint John Chrysostom</a> preached a <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homily</a> in Julian's honor at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Hagiography_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hagiography-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> whose chief <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> was said to be the final resting place for Julian's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> and was known in his honor.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast Day">feast day</a> is June 21 in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> Church, and March 16 in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
