<p><b>Saint Budoc of Dol</b> (also <b>Budeaux</b> or <b>Beuzec</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Dol" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Dol">Bishop of Dol</a>, venerated after his death as a saint in both <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> (now in France) and <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> (now in England). Saint Budoc is the patron of Plourin Ploudalmezeau in <a href="/wiki/Finist%C3%A8re" title="Finistère">Finistère</a> where his relics are preserved. His feast day was celebrated on 8 December, the date still used in Devon, but in Brittany this was moved to 9 December.<sup id="cite_ref-ods_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ods-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Budoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name Budoc, or Beuzec, means "saved from the waters" from the Breton <i>beuziñ</i> meaning "drown"; but <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Baring-Gould</a> finds this "fanciful".<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In old Celtic, <i>boudi</i> means "victory" and "profit".<sup id="cite_ref-doble_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doble-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Budoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Baring-Gould suggests that the princess Azenor fled Brittany with her young son due to dynastic conflict. Arriving first in Cornwall, they then proceeded to Ireland, where Budoc became a monk. They later returned to Brittany, landing at <a href="/wiki/Porspoder" title="Porspoder">Porspoder</a> near <a href="/wiki/Brest,_France" title="Brest, France">Brest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Hunter_Doble" title="Gilbert Hunter Doble">G.H. Doble</a> is of the opinion that Budoc was a once famous abbot whose chief establishment was on the Breton coast.<sup id="cite_ref-doble_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doble-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a> of Breton <a href="/wiki/Saint_Winwaloe" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Winwaloe">Saint Winwaloe</a> describes Budoc as a teacher living on the island of <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Ele-de-Br%C3%A9hat" title="Île-de-Bréhat">Laurea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ods_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ods-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Later Budoc succeeded <a href="/wiki/Samson_of_Dol" title="Samson of Dol">Saint Samson</a> and <a href="/wiki/Magloire" title="Magloire">Magloire</a> as bishop of Dol and ruled for 26 years (according to the 10th century vita of Magloire and the 11th century 'Chronicle of Dol'). Baring-Gould distinguishes between the abbot Budoc and the successor to bishop <a href="/wiki/Magloire" title="Magloire">Magloire</a> at Dol.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Budoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Budoc is reputed to have been grandson of the King Even of Brest. His mother, Princess <a href="/wiki/Saint_Senara" title="Saint Senara">Azenor</a> of Brest, had been falsely accused of infidelity by her jealous stepmother, who had thrown the pregnant Azenor into the sea in a cask. (A tower of the <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Brest" title="Château de Brest">Château de Brest</a> is named for her.) Azenor invoked the help of <a href="/wiki/Brigid_of_Kildare" title="Brigid of Kildare">Saint Brigid</a>. The cask drifted for five months. Shorty after Azenor's baby was born, the cask washed ashore on the coast of Ireland. The story echoes Greek myth.<sup id="cite_ref-ods_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ods-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A villager summoned the abbot of Beau Port, near <a href="/wiki/Waterford" title="Waterford">Waterford</a>, and the child was christened the next day.  Azenor became the washer-woman of the monastery, and Budoc was raised there.<sup id="cite_ref-ods_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ods-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Azenor's stepmother fell ill, and upon her deathbed she recanted the evil lies she had spread. Azenor's husband sailed in search of Azenor, and arriving in Ireland, the couple was reconciled, but both died before they could return to Brittany. (However, a tradition in <a href="/wiki/Cornouaille" title="Cornouaille">Cornouaille</a> has Azenor founding a religious establishment at <a href="/wiki/Cap_Sizun" title="Cap Sizun">Cap Sizun</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Budoc became a monk.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He eventually left Ireland, sailing in a stone trough that landed at Porspoder.<sup id="cite_ref-doble_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doble-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Budoc_in_South-West_England">Budoc in South-West England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Budoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Budoc in South-West England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Budoc is reputed to have sailed across the <a href="/wiki/Plymouth_Sound" title="Plymouth Sound">Plymouth Sound</a>, until he found an inlet on the Devon side of the <a href="/wiki/River_Tamar" title="River Tamar">River Tamar</a>. He landed in Budshead Creek, part of the present district of <a href="/wiki/Plymouth" title="Plymouth">Plymouth</a> called <a href="/wiki/St_Budeaux" title="St Budeaux">St Budeaux</a>. His supposed activity suggests the foundation of an early church in Plymouth.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, there is no evidence of the name in Devon prior to the 16th century. There is also an ancient church said to have been dedicated by him at <a href="/wiki/Budock" class="mw-redirect" title="Budock">Budock</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, and there was once one in <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> too.<sup id="cite_ref-doble_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-doble-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Saint Budoc's feast day is celebrated in Devon on 8 December.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Troparion_of_Saint_Budoc">Troparion of Saint Budoc</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Budoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Troparion of Saint Budoc">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><i>Thou wast miraculously preserved from the ocean's fury</i><br />
<i>and, being sustained by the hand of God,</i><br />
<i>thou didst devote thyself to his service, O Hierarch Budoc.</i><br />
<i>Being showered with both temporal and spiritual honours both in <a href="/wiki/Armagh" title="Armagh">Armagh</a> and in Dol,</i><br />
<i>thou didst labour to win souls for Christ,</i><br />
<i>therefore we implore thine aid,</i><br />
<i>begging Christ our God that he will save our souls</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-groups.yahoo.com_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-groups.yahoo.com-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Budoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Two stained-glass windows in the chancel of Saint-Budoc Church in Porspoder depict scenes from the life of the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-patrim_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrim-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
