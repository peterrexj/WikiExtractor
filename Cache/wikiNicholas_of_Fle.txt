<p><b>Nicholas of Flüe</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Niklaus von Flüe</i>; 1417 &#8211; 21 March 1487) was a Swiss <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and ascetic who is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is sometimes invoked as <b>Brother Klaus</b>. A farmer, military leader, member of the assembly, councillor, judge and mystic, he was respected as a man of complete moral integrity. He is known for having fasted for over twenty years. Brother Klaus's counsel to the <a href="/wiki/Diet_of_Stans" class="mw-redirect" title="Diet of Stans">Diet of Stans</a> (1481) helped prevent war between the Swiss cantons.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1417,<sup id="cite_ref-:3_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Nicholas was born in the village Flüeli near Sachseln, in the canton of <a href="/wiki/Unterwalden" title="Unterwalden">Unterwalden</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:3_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> as the eldest son of wealthy peasants. He had two brothers named Eglof and Peter.<sup id="cite_ref-:6_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:6-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The families surname von Flüe comes from a rock (Fluh=Flüe).<sup id="cite_ref-:6_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:6-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was baptized in <a href="/wiki/Kerns,_Switzerland" title="Kerns, Switzerland">Kerns</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1431/1432 he accompanied his father to the local peasants council and was therefore admitted as a member of the free peasants of Obwalden.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>   
</p><p>At the age of 21 he enrolled in the army and during the <a href="/wiki/Old_Z%C3%BCrich_War" title="Old Zürich War">Old Zürich War</a>, waged against the <a href="/wiki/Canton_of_Zurich" class="mw-redirect" title="Canton of Zurich">canton of Zurich</a> by the rest of the <a href="/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy" title="Old Swiss Confederacy">Old Swiss Confederacy</a>, Nicholas distinguished himself as a soldier and took part in the <a href="/wiki/Bad_Ragaz#History" title="Bad Ragaz">Battle of Ragaz</a> in 1446.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He later took up arms again in the so-called Thurgau war against <a href="/wiki/Sigismund,_Archduke_of_Austria" title="Sigismund, Archduke of Austria">Archduke Sigismund of Austria</a> in 1460. It was thanks to Nicholas' influence that a house of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order#Divisions/nuns" title="Dominican Order">Dominican nuns</a>,  the convent of St. Katharinental, where many Austrians had fled after the capture of <a href="/wiki/Diessenhofen" title="Diessenhofen">Diessenhofen</a>, escaped being destroyed by the Swiss confederates.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>They farmed in the municipality of <i>Flüeli</i> in the alpine foothills, above <a href="/wiki/Sachseln" title="Sachseln">Sachseln</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Lake_Sarnen" title="Lake Sarnen">Lake Sarnen</a>. He also continued to serve in the military to the age of 37, rising to the rank of captain. He reportedly fought with a sword in one hand and a <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> in the other. After leaving military service, he became a councillor for his <a href="/wiki/Canton_of_Switzerland" class="mw-redirect" title="Canton of Switzerland">canton</a> and then in 1459, for nine years, served as a judge. He declined the opportunity to serve as <a href="/wiki/Landammann" title="Landammann">Landammann</a> (governor) of his canton.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_mystic">Political mystic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Political mystic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After receiving a mystical vision of a lily eaten by a horse,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> which he recognized as indicating that the cares of his worldly life (the draft horse pulling a plough) were swallowing up his spiritual life (the lily, a symbol of purity), he decided to devote himself entirely to the contemplative life. In 1467, he left his wife and his ten children with her consent<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> rescinded all his political duties and aimed to join a mystic brotherhood near Basel.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A few miles away in <a href="/wiki/Waldenburg,_Switzerland" title="Waldenburg, Switzerland">Waldenburg</a>, he saw three visions that made him understand his aim was not the one of gods and made him return towards the <a href="/wiki/Melchtal" title="Melchtal">Melchtal</a>, near his former home as he didn't dare to return home.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Discovered a few days after his arrival by some hunters,<sup id="cite_ref-:3_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he eventually set himself up a hermit in the Ranft <a href="/wiki/Chine" title="Chine">chine</a> in Switzerland, establishing a <a href="/wiki/Chantry" title="Chantry">chantry</a> for a priest from his own funds so that he could assist at <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">mass</a> daily. Having arrived in the Ranft, he began to fast and after having received the consent of Oswald Yssner, the priest in Kerns he didn't eat anymore.<sup id="cite_ref-:9_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:9-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Upon Yssners doubt and insistence for a clarification, Niklaus explained that he only from assisting a mass in which a priest enjoys the <a href="/wiki/Sacramental_bread" title="Sacramental bread">sacramental bread</a>, received enough nourishment.<sup id="cite_ref-:9_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:9-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Symbolic visions continued to be a feature of his contemplation, and he became a spiritual guide whose advice was widely sought and followed.<sup id="cite_ref-coulson_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coulson-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> His reputation for wisdom and piety was such that notables and clergy from across <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> came to seek advice from him.<sup id="cite_ref-:8_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:8-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The benedictine <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sponheim" title="Sponheim">Sponheim</a> <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Trithemius" title="Johannes Trithemius">Johannes Trithemius</a> convinced by the reports he heard from people who met Niklaus, compared him with <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Saint Anthony</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:10_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:10-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1470, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_II" title="Pope Paul II">Pope Paul II</a> granted the first <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgence</a> to the sanctuary at Ranft and it became a pilgrimage site on the <a href="/wiki/Camino_de_Santiago" title="Camino de Santiago">Way of Saint James</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> a pilgrims' route to <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a> in Spain. His counsel prevented a civil war between the cantons meeting at the <a href="/wiki/Tagsatzung" title="Tagsatzung">Diet</a> of <a href="/wiki/Stans" title="Stans">Stans</a> in 1481, when their antagonism grew.<sup id="cite_ref-coulson_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coulson-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Despite being illiterate and having limited experience with the world, he is honored among both Protestants and Catholics with the permanent national unity of Switzerland. The Archduke Sigismund sent him a <a href="/wiki/Gilding" title="Gilding">gilded</a> chalice in 1473 and 100 <a href="/wiki/Guilder" title="Guilder">Guilders</a> in 1481.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Letters of thanks to him from Berne and Soleure still survive. When he died, on 21 March 1487, he was surrounded by his wife and children.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prayer_citation">Prayer citation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Prayer citation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The new <a href="/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church" title="Catechism of the Catholic Church">Catechism of the Catholic Church</a> cites a brief personal prayer of Nicholas of Flue in paragraph #226<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> of <i>Chapter 1</i> of <i>Part 1, Section 2 "The Profession of the Christian Faith"</i> under subheading <i>IV</i> <small><b>"The implications of faith in one God"</b></small>, an aspect of which is making good use of created things.
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>My Lord and my God, take from me everything that distances me from you.<br />
    My Lord and my God, give me everything that brings me closer to you.<br />
    My Lord and my God, detach me from myself to give my all to you. <br />
</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:352px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg/350px-Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="556" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg/525px-Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg/700px-Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1059" data-file-height="1683" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tagsatzung_Stans_1481.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A plate from the Amtliche Luzerner Chronik of 1513 of <a href="/wiki/Diebold_Schilling_the_Younger" title="Diebold Schilling the Younger">Diebold Schilling the Younger</a>, illustrating the events of the Tagsatzung at Stans in 1481. <b>Top:</b> A priest named Heini am Grund visits Niklaus von Flüe to ask him for his advice to save the failing Tagsatzung at Stans, where the delegates of the rural and urban cantons of the Old Swiss Confederacy could not agree and threatened civil war. <b>Bottom:</b> Am Grund returned to the Tagsatzung and related Niklaus' advice, whereupon the delegates compromised. Am Grund is shown holding back a bailiff who wants to go and spread the good news already: Niklaus' advice remains secret to this day.</div></div></div><p>During his lifetime pilgrims who had visited the <a href="/wiki/Einsiedeln_Abbey" title="Einsiedeln Abbey">Einsiedeln abbey</a> often also went to the nearby Ranft.<sup id="cite_ref-:10_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:10-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> After Nichoas of Flüe died in 1487, his funeral was attended by a large number of people<sup id="cite_ref-:0_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and he was buried in the chapel in Sachseln.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The Austrian Archduke Sigismund organized a memorial service for Niklaus with a hundred priests in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His tomb would become a prominent site of pilgrimage and by 1518 the epitaph with a depiction of him had to be renewed.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In In 1492 he was painted on the interior of his burial chapel.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1669. After his beatification, the municipality of Sachseln built a church in his honour, where his body was interred. During World War II he was the spiritual saviour of Switzerland, and in August 1941 the Swiss bishops promised to go on a pilgrimage in his honour if the country was spared from the effects of war.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1947 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>. His feast day in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> is 21 March, except in Switzerland and Germany, where it is 25 September. In June 1984, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> held a mass in Flüeli Ranft and a prayer at the tomb of Niklaus von Flüe in Sachseln during his visit to Switzerland.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a layman with family responsibilities who took his civic duties as an ancestral landowner seriously, Brother Klaus is a model of heroic manhood for many concerned with the flourishing of local communities and sustainable use of open land. He is the patron saint of the German-language association KLB (Katholischen Landvolkbewegung), the Catholic Rural Communities Movement.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Wölflins biography was translated into German in 1947 by Josef Konrad Scheuber.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Biographies_on_Nicholas_of_Fl.C3.BCe"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Biographies_on_Nicholas_of_Flüe">Biographies on Nicholas of Flüe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Biographies on Nicholas of Flüe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The abbot of the Einsiedeln abbey <a href="/wiki/Albrecht_von_Bonstetten" title="Albrecht von Bonstetten">Albrecht von Bonstetten</a> wrote the first known report on his life in 1479, while Nicholas still alive.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In his report <i>Historia fratris Nicholae</i> the abbot distinguished between witnesses who saw, heard or heard someone say something on the life of Nicholas.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In 1485 the report was translated from latin into german on request by the clergy and mayor of <a href="/wiki/Nuremberg" title="Nuremberg">Nuremberg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>A second biography was written a year after Nicholas died by Heinrich of Gundelfingen.<sup id="cite_ref-:5_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:5-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> It was called <i>Historia Nicholae Unterwadensis eremitae</i> and presented to the authorities of Lucerne.<sup id="cite_ref-:5_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:5-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> He alluded that Nicholas aimed at re-establishing the ascetic life of the early christian Saints. He acknowledged his ascetic life but was skeptical on his yearlong fasting.<sup id="cite_ref-:5_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:5-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The Government of Obwalden requested from Heinrich Wölflin, a noted historian of the time to write a biography of Niklaus von Flüe in 1493.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Wölflin then recollected reports of witnesses for several years and published the first state sponsored biography in 1501 in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Visionary_images">Visionary images</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Visionary images">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Of the many spiritual insights Nicholas received in his visions, one, in particular, is reproduced often in a reduced logographic format, as a mystical wheel.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Nicholas described his vision of the Holy Face at the center of a circle with the tips of three swords touching the two eyes and mouth, while three others radiate outwards in a sixfold symmetry reminiscent of the <a href="/wiki/Seal_of_Solomon" title="Seal of Solomon">Seal of Solomon</a>. A cloth painted with the image, known as the meditation prayer cloth<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> associates the symbol with six episodes from the life of Christ: the mouth of God at the Annunciation, the eyes spying Creation both in its prelapsarian innocence and redemption from the Fall at Calvary, while in the inward direction the betrayal by his disciple <a href="/wiki/Judas_Iscariot" title="Judas Iscariot">Judas</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Gethsemane" title="Gethsemane">Garden of Gethsamene</a> points to the crown of the <a href="/wiki/Pantocrator" class="mw-redirect" title="Pantocrator">Pantocrator</a> sitting in the judgment seat, the glad tidings of the Nativity scene's <i>"Glory to God in the Highest and Peace to his people on Earth"</i> echoes in the ear on the right of the head, while the memorial of the Lord's Supper <i>"This is my body, which will be given for you"</i> at the prayers of consecration in the Divine Liturgy of the Mass echoes to the ear on the left of the head.
</p><p>These six medallions contain additional symbols of acts of Christian kindness:
</p>
<ol><li>two crutches suggest <i>Visiting the sick</i> as a work of mercy</li>
<li>hiker's walking stick with travel pouch suggests <i>Hospitality to strangers</i></li>
<li>a loaf of bread, fish and a pitcher of water and wine represent <i>Feed the hungry, quench the thirsty</i></li>
<li>chains indicate <i>Care for the incarcerated</i></li>
<li>Christ's garments evoke <i>Clothe the naked</i></li>
<li>a coffin reminds us to <i>Bury the dead</i></li></ol>
<p>This visual interpretation encapsulates the personal piety of rural peasants, many illiterate, for whom salvation history was expressed in these crucial aspects of God's loving relationship with us and the Christian duty to the love of neighbor. Sanctifying grace flows from the Paschal Victim on the Cross, an image Nicholas described in his vision by the stream,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> where the Tabernacle sits atop a spring that flows forth covering the earth, echoing the rivers flowing from the Temple in <a href="/wiki/Ezekiel" title="Ezekiel">Ezekiel</a>'s visions. Such profound insights on the allegorical,<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> anagogical and <a href="/wiki/Tropological" class="mw-redirect" title="Tropological">tropological</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianity#Catholic_and_Orthodox_interpretations" title="Christianity">senses of scripture</a> are often lost in modern biblical exegesis that focuses too narrowly on the literal sense, the <a href="/wiki/Historical-grammatical_method" title="Historical-grammatical method">historical-critical method</a>. One vision he had between 1474, the year the monk <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hans_von_Waltheim&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hans von Waltheim (page does not exist)">Hans von Waltheim</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_von_Waltheim" class="extiw" title="de:Hans von Waltheim">de</a>&#93;</span> visited him, and 1478, when <a href="/wiki/Albrecht_von_Bonstetten" title="Albrecht von Bonstetten">Albrecht von Bonstetten</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:8_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:8-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was frightened by the vision of a glowing face and adopted a bewildered appearance which also shocked von Bonstetten.<sup id="cite_ref-:8_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:8-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The medieval biographer Heinrich Wölflin wrote that other visitors were also frightened but there is no other report about this.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>   
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_of_Fl%C3%BCe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1445/1446, when he was around the age of 29, he married <a href="/wiki/Dorothea_Wyss" title="Dorothea Wyss">Dorothea Wyss</a>, a farmer's daughter and at the time fourteen years of age.<sup id="cite_ref-:7_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:7-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The newly weds settled into a house built by Niklaus von Flüe.<sup id="cite_ref-:7_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:7-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The next year the first son Hans was born.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> According to his own account, after his turn to a life as a hermit, he did not feel tempted to return to a earthly life with wife and children.<sup id="cite_ref-:8_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:8-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>   
</p>
