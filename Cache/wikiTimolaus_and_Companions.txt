<p>Saint <b>Timolaus</b> and five companions, according to the historian of the early <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> church <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Palestine" title="Martyrs of Palestine">Martyrs of Palestine</a></i>, were young men who, having heard that the Roman authorities in <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima" title="Caesarea Maritima">Caesarea</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>, in 303 AD, had condemned a number of Christians to die by being thrown to wild beasts in the public arena, came before the governor of their own volition with their hands tied behind their backs and demanded to join their fellow Christians in that <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>. They were not however thrown to wild beasts but decapitated along with two other men who were already in prison.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Timolaus_and_companions&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are two extant versions of Eusebius' <i>Martyrs of Palestine</i> but in only the shorter recension is the story of Timolaus and his companions recounted; in the longer recension Timolaus is not mentioned.  Eusebius was present in Caesarea during the persecutions, part of the empire-wide campaign to suppress <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Persecution_of_Christians_in_Caesarea">Persecution of Christians in Caesarea</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Timolaus_and_companions&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Persecution of Christians in Caesarea">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> had ordered that all in the Empire should perform worship and sacrifices to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a>. Those who refused to do so in Caesarea, according to Eusebius, were interrogated and tortured if necessary to try to force them to <a href="/wiki/Apostasize" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostasize">apostasize</a>, and if nothing would convince them to perform the sacrifices, they were executed.<sup id="cite_ref-Eusebius_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eusebius-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Timolaus">Timolaus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Timolaus_and_companions&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Timolaus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The governor of Caesarea, Urban, had already had two Christians thrown to wild beasts in the public arena for refusing to perform the sacrifices to the Roman gods.<sup id="cite_ref-Eusebius_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eusebius-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> As part of an upcoming festival, among the spectacles planned were more executions of this kind.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Hearing of this, a young man, Timolaus from <a href="/wiki/Pontus_(region)" title="Pontus (region)">Pontus</a> (on the Black Sea, in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>), along with five others, "went in haste" to the governor, having tied their own hands behind their backs, declared themselves Christians, and demanded also to be thrown to the beasts.<sup id="cite_ref-Eusebius_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eusebius-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The "astonished" governor, however, had them imprisoned for two days and then decapitated along with two others.<sup id="cite_ref-Eusebius_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eusebius-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Companions">Companions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Timolaus_and_companions&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Companions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Eusebius, Timolaus' companions in seeking <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> in this way were "Dionysius from Tripolis in Phoenicia, Romulus, a sub-<a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> of the parish of Diospolis, Paesis and Alexander, both Egyptians, and another Alexander from Gaza." They were joined by another man who was already in prison, having previously been tortured, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Agapius_of_Palestine" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Agapius of Palestine">Agapius</a>, and another Dionysius, who had supplied him with food while he was in prison. All eight were beheaded on the same day.<sup id="cite_ref-Eusebius_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eusebius-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
