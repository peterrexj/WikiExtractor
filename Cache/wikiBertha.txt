<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox"><caption class="infobox-title">Bertha</caption><tbody><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Gender</th><td class="infobox-data">Female</td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background:#ADD8E6">Origin</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Meaning</th><td class="infobox-data">"Bright one"</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Bertha</b> is a female <a href="/wiki/Germanic_name" title="Germanic name">Germanic name</a>, from Old High German <i>berhta</i> meaning "bright one". It was usually a short form of <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo Saxon</a> names <i>Beorhtgifu</i> meaning "bright gift" or <i>Beorhtwynn</i> meaning "bright joy". 
</p><p>The name occurs as a <a href="/wiki/Theonym" title="Theonym">theonym</a>, surviving as  <a href="/wiki/Perchta" title="Perchta">Berchta</a>, a figure in <a href="/wiki/Alpine_folklore" class="mw-redirect" title="Alpine folklore">Alpine folklore</a> connected to the <a href="/wiki/Wild_Hunt" title="Wild Hunt">Wild Hunt</a>, probably an epithet of <i>*<a href="/wiki/Frijj%C5%8D" class="mw-redirect" title="Frijjō">Frijjō</a></i> in origin.
</p><p><i>Bertha</i> appears as a <a href="/wiki/Frankish_language" title="Frankish language">Frankish</a> given name from as early as the 6th century.
The monothematic <i>Bertha</i>  as a given name may, however, not originate with the theonym but rather as a short form of dithematic given names including the "bright" element. 
This is notably the case with the mother of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bertrada_of_Laon" title="Bertrada of Laon">Bertrada</a> (properly <i>berht-rada</i> "bright counsel") called "Bertha Broadfoot." Carolingian uses of the name <i>Bertha</i>, as in the case of <a href="/wiki/Bertha,_daughter_of_Charlemagne" title="Bertha, daughter of Charlemagne">Bertha, daughter of Charlemagne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bertha,_daughter_of_Lothair_II" title="Bertha, daughter of Lothair II">Bertha, daughter of Lothair II</a>, are in this tradition.
</p><p>In modern times, the name is associated with an unusually large example of a class of objects. Many large machines are nicknamed Bertha for the World War I howitzer known as <a href="/wiki/Big_Bertha_(howitzer)" title="Big Bertha (howitzer)">Big Bertha</a>.
