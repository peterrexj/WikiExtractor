<p><b>Robert of Newminster</b> ( c. 1100&#8211;1159) was a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. He was born in <a href="/wiki/Gargrave" title="Gargrave">Gargrave</a> in <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, England. He was one of the monks who founded <a href="/wiki/Fountains_Abbey" title="Fountains Abbey">Fountains Abbey</a> and is named from the abbey he founded in <a href="/wiki/Morpeth,_Northumberland" title="Morpeth, Northumberland">Morpeth</a>, Northumberland.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Newminster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert was born in what is now the district of <a href="/wiki/Craven_District" title="Craven District">Craven</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Skipton" title="Skipton">Skipton</a> in <a href="/wiki/North_Yorkshire" title="North Yorkshire">North Yorkshire</a>, probably in the village of <a href="/wiki/Gargrave" title="Gargrave">Gargrave</a>. He studied at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>, where he is said to have composed a commentary - since lost - on the <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a>.  He became a parish priest, returning to serve Gargrave where he was made rector.  He became a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> joining the monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Abbey,_York" title="St Mary&#39;s Abbey, York">Saint Mary's Abbey in York</a>. A group of monks, including Robert were expelled from York and established a monastery in a valley near Skeldale, on land given them by Archbishop Thurstan in winter 1132.  The first two years were difficult and the monks struggled in extreme poverty.  Initially they lived in a makeshift structure on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/River_Skell" title="River Skell">River Skell</a>. Despite the hardships, the monks were known for their holiness, austerity and dedication to the strict Benedictine way of life.  Their fame brought a new novice, Hugh, <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_York" title="Dean of York">Dean of York</a>, who relinquished all his wealth to the community who built more suitable facilities.  Because of the many natural springs in the area, the monastery was called <a href="/wiki/Fountains_Abbey" title="Fountains Abbey">Fountains Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Fountains became affiliated with the <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercian</a> reform which had been introduced by <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sankovitz_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sankovitz-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cistercian_and_abbot">Cistercian and abbot</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Newminster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cistercian and abbot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert was described as a devout, prayerful, and gentle man. He is known for being merciful in his judgment of others and a warm and considerate companion. He was zealous regarding his own vows of poverty.<sup id="cite_ref-sankovitz_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sankovitz-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> About 1138 he headed a group of monks sent out from Fountains to establish <a href="/wiki/Newminster_Abbey" title="Newminster Abbey">Newminster Abbey</a> near the castle of Ralph de Merlay and his wife, Juliana, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Gospatric_II,_Earl_of_Lothian" title="Gospatric II, Earl of Lothian">Gospatric II, Earl of Lothian</a>, west of <a href="/wiki/Morpeth,_Northumberland" title="Morpeth, Northumberland">Morpeth</a> in <a href="/wiki/Northumberland" title="Northumberland">Northumberland</a>. Abbot Robert was said to be was favoured with the gift of prophecy and miracles. During his abbacy three colonies of monks were sent to found new monasteries at <a href="/wiki/Pipewell" title="Pipewell">Pipewell</a> in <a href="/wiki/Northamptonshire" title="Northamptonshire">Northamptonshire</a> (1143), <a href="/wiki/Roche_Abbey" title="Roche Abbey">Roche</a> in <a href="/wiki/South_Yorkshire" title="South Yorkshire">South Yorkshire</a> (1147), and <a href="/wiki/Sawley,_Lancashire" title="Sawley, Lancashire">Sawley</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a> (1148).
</p><p>Capgrave's life tells that an accusation of misconduct was brought against him by his own monks. Robert was said to have had an interest towards a woman in the village. He went to defend himself before Bernard of Clairvaux in 1147–1148. Bernard did not doubt Robert's innocence as he had received a heavenly sign of his virtuous conduct. Doubt has been cast upon the truth of the story, which may have arisen from a desire to associate him personally with the greatest of the Cistercians.<sup id="cite_ref-raymond_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raymond-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Robert ruled and directed the monks at Newminster for 21 years.<sup id="cite_ref-sankovitz_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sankovitz-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The small monastery of only 17 monks was one of the first to be dissolved in 1535 by Henry VIII, and the site has been privately owned since.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Newminster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In one instance, a monk is said to have fallen unhurt from a ladder while working on one of the buildings.  His tomb has become a centre for pilgrimage. Robert was a close spiritual friend of the hermit <a href="/wiki/Godric_of_Finchale" title="Godric of Finchale">Godric of Finchale</a>. On the night Robert died, Godric is said to have seen a vision of Robert's soul, like a ball of fire, being lifted by angels on a pathway of light toward the gates of heaven.  As they approached, Godric heard a voice saying, <i>"Enter now my friends."</i>
</p><p>Robert is often depicted in church art as an abbot holding a church.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Newminster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert's tomb in the church of Newminster became an object of pilgrimage. His feast day is celebrated on 7 June, the day of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-raymond_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raymond-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Newminster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A number of parishes are dedicated to St Robert of Newminster including those in <a href="/wiki/St_Robert_of_Newminster_Church,_Morpeth" title="St Robert of Newminster Church, Morpeth">Morpeth</a>, Northumberland;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Fenham, Newcastle upon Tyne;<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Ada, Michigan<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and Shorewood, Wisconsin.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Robert_of_Newminster_Catholic_School" title="St Robert of Newminster Catholic School">St Robert of Newminster Catholic School</a> in Washington, Tyne and Wear is named after him.
</p><p>St. Robert School in Sacramento, CA is also named after him.
</p><p>There is a St Robert of Newminster Parish and Primary School in Aberkenfig, Bridgend South Wales.
</p><p>St. Robert Catholic School in Ada, MI, is a brand new Catholic school, that opened in August 2018. The school opened with 2, 3, and 4 year old preschool classes as well as a kindergarten class. Each year, the school will add a grade until it is full with preschool through 8th grade students.
</p>
