<p><b>Xenia the Righteous of Rome</b> was a saint of the 5th century, honored by some Christian Churches, including <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Xenia, originally born Eusebia, was the only daughter of a wealthy <a href="/wiki/Roman_senator" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman senator">Senator</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. She and two devoted servants of hers, left to avoid an arranged marriage. She escaped to <a href="/wiki/Mylasa" class="mw-redirect" title="Mylasa">Mylasa</a>, on the island of <a href="/wiki/Kos" title="Kos">Kos</a>, where she accepted  name "Xenia" (stranger). She wanted to hide in a deserted place not to be discovered by her parents. 
</p><p>Upon arrival, Xenia began a church dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen" title="Saint Stephen">Saint Stephen</a> and a woman's monastery. Soon after, she was made a deaconess by Bishop Paul of Mylasa.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-poulos_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poulos-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Of her that is written says that she "helped everyone: for the destitute, she was a benefactress; for the grief-stricken, a comforter; for sinners, a guide to repentance. She possessed a deep humility, accounting herself the worst and most sinful of all."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Feast of St. Xenia is celebrated in the Orthodox church and Catholic church on January 24, the day on which she died. It was alleged that "during her funeral, a luminous wreath of stars surrounding a radiant cross appeared over the monastery in the heavens."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She is said to have foreseen her own death.<sup id="cite_ref-poulos_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-poulos-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
