					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_Forzat%C3%A8&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Jordan Forzatè">Jordan Forzatè</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Giordano Forzatè</b>, anglicized <b>Jordan Forzatè</b> (1158 – 7 August 1248), was a <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Paduan</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> monk and religious leader.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> For his noble background, peacemaking efforts and monastic reforms, the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_the_Trevisan_March" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicle of the Trevisan March">Chronicle of the Trevisan March</a></i> calls him the <i>pater Padue</i>, "father of Padua".<sup id="cite_ref-Kohl_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kohl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monk">Monk</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giordano_Forzat%C3%A8&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Monk">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, Giordano was born at Padua in 1158. His family, the Forzatè branch of the Tanselgardi (or Transelgardi),<sup id="cite_ref-DdlS_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DdlS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> belonged to the upper ranks of the aristocracy of the <a href="/wiki/Trevisan_March" class="mw-redirect" title="Trevisan March">Trevisan March</a>, part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" title="Kingdom of Italy (Holy Roman Empire)">Kingdom of Italy</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a>. They were probably vassals of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Padua">bishop of Padua</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A relative, Forzatè di Tanselgardino, was the lawyer of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Santa_Giustina" title="Abbey of Santa Giustina">Abbey of Santa Giustina</a> in the early 13th century.<sup id="cite_ref-DdlS_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DdlS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Giordano is first mentioned as a monk of <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_di_San_Benedetto_Vecchio" class="extiw" title="it:Chiesa di San Benedetto Vecchio">San Benedetto Vecchio</a> in a document of 1203. This monastery may have been founded by his family.<sup id="cite_ref-DdlS_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DdlS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Documents from the following years show that he received substantial sums of money through inheritance and trusts.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He earned a reputation as a local peacemaker.<sup id="cite_ref-Kohl_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kohl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Giordano was probably a doctor of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> (<i>decretorum doctor</i>). On 7 June 1211, Pope <a href="/wiki/Innocent_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent III">Innocent III</a> nominated him to the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Ferrara" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Ferrara">bishopric of Ferrara</a>, but he declined, preferring to play a larger role in his native Padua and in the Benedictine Order. He served as <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_delegate" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic delegate">apostolic delegate</a> in Padua in 1213–14 (when he oversaw the election of Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_(bishop_of_Padua)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jordan (bishop of Padua) (page does not exist)">Giordano</a> following the death of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerardo_Offreducci&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gerardo Offreducci (page does not exist)">Gerardo Offreducci</a>) and in 1229 (when he oversaw the election of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Giacomo_Corrado&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Giacomo Corrado (page does not exist)">Giacomo Corrado</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prior">Prior</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giordano_Forzat%C3%A8&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Prior">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>fundator pater
<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_reformer">Monastic reformer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giordano_Forzat%C3%A8&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Monastic reformer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>By 1213, Giordano had become prior of San Benedetto. He founded a new movement, called the <i>Albi</i> (whites) or more fully <i>ordo monachorum alborum Sancti Benedicti de Padua</i> (order of white monks of Saint Benedict of Padua), which aimed to combine hospitals with communities of canons, male and female monastics and hermits.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 30 May 1224, with the approval of the bishop, and with the support of six priors of the municipal houses, the first congregation of <i>Albi</i> was founded in Padua. The six other municipal priories also became the seats of communities of <i>Albi</i>. On 7 June 1234 in <a href="/wiki/Rieti" title="Rieti">Rieti</a>, Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a> issued a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bull</a> confirming the new order.<sup id="cite_ref-DdlS_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DdlS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Giordano also reformed the Benedictine monasteries of Santa Maria in Vanzo and Santa Maria di Porciglia.<sup id="cite_ref-Kohl_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kohl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He pioneered the intimate connection of monastery and municipality that has been called "communal monasticism". After his death, the commune of Padua entrusted its books to the safekeeping of the <i>Albi</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Giordano's ideals were not far from those of the <a href="/wiki/Mendicant_orders" title="Mendicant orders">mendicant orders</a>. He was present as a witness at the first donation of land to the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> in Padua in October 1226, and he intervened with the bishop on behalf of the canonization of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">Anthony of Padua</a> in 1232. In 1227, he performed a visitation of Benedictine houses exempt from episcopal jurisdiction, of congregations of <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a> and <a href="/wiki/Humiliati" title="Humiliati">Humiliati</a> and of the hospitals of Padua, <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> and <a href="/wiki/Treviso" title="Treviso">Treviso</a>. Giordano's stature in Padua can be gauged by the great number of testamentary bequests he received. He was said to have convinced <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Beatrice_d%27Este" title="Blessed Beatrice d&#39;Este">Beatrice d'Este</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Azzo_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Azzo VI">Azzo VI</a>, to renounce the world.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Politics">Politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giordano_Forzat%C3%A8&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Giordano undertook several missions as apostolic delegate for Innocent III, <a href="/wiki/Honorius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Honorius III">Honorius III</a> and Gregory IX. In 1216, he was sent to the financially troubled <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Treviso" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Treviso">bishopric of Treviso</a>. In 1217, he investigated the election of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ceneda" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ceneda">bishop of Ceneda</a> and the work of the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_administrator" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic administrator">apostolic administrator</a> in <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Vicenza" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Vicenza">Vicenza</a>. In 1218, he was present to supervise the handover by the bishop of Treviso of his right to levy tolls to the commune of Treviso.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Giordano was a close ally of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Este" title="House of Este">House of Este</a> and in the intercommunal conflicts of the <a href="/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines" title="Guelphs and Ghibellines">Guelphs and Ghibellines</a> aligned with the pro-papal Guelph faction.<sup id="cite_ref-Kohl_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kohl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The hostile pro-imperial Ghibelline chronicler <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerardo_Maurisio&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gerardo Maurisio (page does not exist)">Gerardo Maurisio</a> of <a href="/wiki/Vicenza" title="Vicenza">Vicenza</a> calls him evil, hypocritical, conspiratorial and "the author and prince of all discord in the March" of Treviso (<i>totius discordie Marchie auctor et princeps</i>). Contemporary chronicles are clear, however, that Giordano was one of the most respected political actors in the Trevisan March. According <a href="/wiki/Rolandino_of_Padua" title="Rolandino of Padua">Rolandino of Padua</a>, Padua and Vicenza voluntarily submitted to his arbitration and Padua "entrusted many things" to him.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Giordano regularly attended meetings of the council of Padua, offering advice and acting on their behalf. He was present for the signing of the treaty of peace between Padua and Venice following the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Castle_of_Love" title="War of the Castle of Love">War of the Castle of Love</a> in 1216. On behalf of the commune, he arbitrated a dispute between the commune of Vicenza and <a href="/wiki/Ezzelino_II_da_Romano" title="Ezzelino II da Romano">Ezzelino II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ezzelino_III_da_Romano" title="Ezzelino III da Romano">Ezzelino III da Romano</a> in 1218. He ordered the Ezzelini to hand over to Vicenza the castle of <a href="/wiki/Marostica" title="Marostica">Marostica</a>. He mediated between some Vicenzan aristocrats and the <i><a href="/wiki/Podest%C3%A0" title="Podestà">podestà</a></i> Lorenzo da Brescia in 1222–1223. He was present when Padua conceded the right of toll-free transit of goods to the Venetian monastery of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Santa_Maria_delle_Vergini&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Santa Maria delle Vergini (page does not exist)">Santa Maria delle Vergini</a> on 19 September 1229. In that year, he also strove to prevent open warfare between Padua and Treviso after the latter had, at the instigation of Ezzelino III da Romano, occupied the towns of <a href="/wiki/Feltre" title="Feltre">Feltre</a> and <a href="/wiki/Belluno" title="Belluno">Belluno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Arrest.2C_exile_and_death"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest,_exile_and_death">Arrest, exile and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giordano_Forzat%C3%A8&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Arrest, exile and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg/220px-Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg/330px-Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4f/Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg/440px-Beatogiordanoforzat%C3%A8.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="533" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a> body of Giordano in Padua</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1235, Giordano supported the election of <a href="/wiki/Azzo_VII_d%27Este" title="Azzo VII d&#39;Este">Azzo VII d'Este</a> as <i><a href="/wiki/Podest%C3%A0" title="Podestà">podestà</a></i> of Vicenza. The following year the city was occupied by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a>, who entrusted it to Ezzelino III. On 25 February 1237, Ezzelino also took control of Padua. At first, fearing reprisals, Giordano took refuge in his family's castle at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Montemerlo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Montemerlo (page does not exist)">Montemerlo</a>, but he later returned to the city, where he was arrested in June. The bishop of Padua and Pope Gregory IX intervened on his behalf with the emperor, who ordered his release. He was not allowed to remain in Padua, however, as a concession to Ezzelino. Giordano's later reputation as the "father of Padua" owes much to his showdown with Ezzelino, who became the archetypal tyrant in Paduan historical memory.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In exile, Giordano went first to the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Aquileia" title="Patriarchate of Aquileia">patriarchate of Aquileia</a>, where he is recorded on 26 May 1239. He later decided to leave the Empire entirely and took refuge in the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Celestia" title="Santa Maria della Celestia">Santa Maria della Celestia</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Republic of Venice</a>. He died there on 7 August 1248. His body was returned to Padua in 1260. In the following century, a <a href="/wiki/Hagiographic" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographic">hagiographical</a> tradition developed. His <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> was approved by Pope <a href="/wiki/Clement_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement XIII">Clement XIII</a> in 1767.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His feast is celebrated on 7 August.<sup id="cite_ref-DdlS_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DdlS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
