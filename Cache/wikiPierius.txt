<p><b>Pierius</b> was a Christian priest and probably head of the <a href="/wiki/Catechetical_School_of_Alexandria" title="Catechetical School of Alexandria">Catechetical School of Alexandria</a>, conjointly with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Achillas_of_Alexandria" title="Pope Achillas of Alexandria">Achillas</a>. He flourished while <a href="/wiki/Theonas" class="mw-redirect" title="Theonas">Theonas</a> was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Alexandria">bishop of Alexandria</a>, and died at Rome after 309. The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> commemorates him on 4 November. 
</p><p>His skill as an exegetical writer and as a preacher gained for him the appellation, "Origen the Younger".<sup id="cite_ref-DVI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DVI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Side" title="Philip of Side">Philip of Side</a>, <a href="/wiki/Photios_I_of_Constantinople" title="Photios I of Constantinople">Photius</a>, and others assert that he was a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. However, since <a href="/wiki/St._Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Jerome">St. Jerome</a> assures us that he survived the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> and spent the rest of his life at Rome,<sup id="cite_ref-DVI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DVI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the term "martyr" can only mean that he underwent sufferings, not death, for his faith.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He wrote a work (<i>biblion</i>) comprising twelve treatises or sermons <a href="/wiki/Logos" title="Logos">(<i>logoi</i>)</a>, in some of which he repeats the dogmatic points attributed by some authors to <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>, such as the subordination of the Holy Ghost to the Father and the Son, and the pre-existence of human souls.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His known sermons are: one on the <i><a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_St._Luke" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospel of St. Luke">Gospel of St. Luke</a></i> (<i>eis to kata Loukan</i>); an Easter sermon on <a href="/wiki/Osee" class="mw-redirect" title="Osee">Osee</a> (Hosea) (<i>eis to pascha kai ton Osee</i>); a sermon on the Mother of God (<i>peri tes theotokou</i>); a few other Easter sermons; and a eulogy on <a href="/wiki/St._Pamphilus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Pamphilus">St. Pamphilus</a>, who had been one of his disciples (<i>eis ton bion tou hagiou Pamphilou</i>). 
</p><p>Only fragments of his writings are extant. They were edited by <a href="/wiki/Martin_Joseph_Routh" class="mw-redirect" title="Martin Joseph Routh">Martin Joseph Routh</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and in <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Graeca" title="Patrologia Graeca">Patrologia Graeca</a></i>;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Carl de Boor added some fragments.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
