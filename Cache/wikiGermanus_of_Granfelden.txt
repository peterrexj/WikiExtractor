<p><b>Saint Germanus of Granfelden</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;612</span> in <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> died 675, near <a href="/wiki/Moutier" title="Moutier">Moutier</a>) was the first <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Moutier-Grandval_Abbey" title="Moutier-Grandval Abbey">Moutier-Grandval Abbey</a>. He is venerated as a martyr saint in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Vita"><i>Vita</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Granfelden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Vita">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Codex_Sangallensis_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Codex Sangallensis (disambiguation)">Codex Sangallensis (disambiguation)</a></div>
<p>The "Life" of Saint Germanus is recounted in the eleventh-century "<span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Passio sancti Germani</i></span>", which appears in the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Vitae et passiones diversorum sanctorum</i></span>. It was written about 695 by Bobolène, a priest probably of <a href="/wiki/Moutier-Grandval_Abbey" title="Moutier-Grandval Abbey">Moutier-Grandval Abbey</a> and later at <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil</a>. It was composed at the request of the religious brothers Chadoal and Aridius, contemporaries of Abbot Germanus.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The manuscript of the <i>Vitae</i> is preserved as the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Codex Sangallensis</i></span> 551 ('Codex of Saint-Gall' 551), housed in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_library_of_Saint_Gall" title="Abbey library of Saint Gall">Abbey library of Saint Gall</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-codex_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-codex-4">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Granfelden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Germanus was the second son of Optardus, a wealthy senator in <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>. His older brother became a courtier, while his younger brother Numerianus eventually succeeded <a href="/wiki/Saint_Modoald" title="Saint Modoald">Modoald</a> as Bishop of Trier.
</p><p>Optardus entrusted the young Germanus to Modoald to be educated.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> At the age of seventeen, Germanus decided to take up the monastic life. He left to join <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Metz" title="Arnulf of Metz">Arnulf of Metz</a>, who had retired from the bishopric of Metz to a hermitage at a mountain site in his domains in the Vosges to become a monk.<sup id="cite_ref-Boillon_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boillon-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Germanus stayed for some time with Arnulf who gave him the tonsure and then sent him, with his younger brother Numerian who had come to join him, to the recently founded monastery <a href="/wiki/Remiremont_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Remiremont monastery">Remiremont monastery</a>. Driven by a desire for greater perfection, he left with his brother and some religious for <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil_Abbey" title="Luxeuil Abbey">Luxeuil</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Saint_Waldebert" title="Saint Waldebert">Abbot Waldebert</a> received him, conferred on him the priesthood, then around 640 sent him, to organize and govern the monastery of Granfeld (Monasterium Grandis Vallis) or Moutier-Granval, recently founded by <a href="/wiki/Gundoin,_Duke_of_Alsace" title="Gundoin, Duke of Alsace">Gundoin, Duke of Alsace</a>. Germanus served as abbot for 35 years.<sup id="cite_ref-Boillon_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boillon-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His history with Remiremont, Luxeuil, and Granval show his connection to the network of Columbanian establishments. His contacts with Modoald, Arnulf, and Gundoin suggest he supported the <a href="/wiki/Pippinids" title="Pippinids">Arnulfings</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to legend, Gundoin's successor, <a href="/wiki/Boniface,_Duke_of_Alsace" title="Boniface, Duke of Alsace">Boniface, Duke of Alsace</a> had trouble keeping the people of the Sornegau from revolting. The situation became worse under his successor <a href="/wiki/Adalrich,_Duke_of_Alsace" title="Adalrich, Duke of Alsace">Adalrich, Duke of Alsace</a> (known as "Eticho"), who wanted to subdue the area around <a href="/wiki/Del%C3%A9mont" title="Delémont">Delémont</a>. Germanus and his prior <a href="/wiki/Randoald_of_Grandval" title="Randoald of Grandval">Randoald of Grandval</a> met him for negotiations near <a href="/wiki/Court%C3%A9telle" title="Courtételle">Courtételle</a> (southwest of Delémont). After discussions Germanus and Randoald were slain on their way back by supporters of the Duke.<sup id="cite_ref-Boillon_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boillon-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The following night the religious collected the bodies and buried them first in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Ursanne" title="Saint-Ursanne">Saint-Ursanne</a>, then in Moutier-Granval.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_of_Granfelden&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Germanus and Randoald are regarded as martyrs and became very popular. Miracles reportedly took place at their tomb, which became a center of pilgrimage. The remains of the martyrs were then in 1477 transferred under the high altar. Their cult extended throughout the diocese of Basel and throughout the province of Besancon. The progress of Protestantism and a fire at the abbey which had become a collegiate church, of which nothing remains, led the canons to withdraw and transport the relics of the two saints to Délémont in the canton of Jura, where they continue to be venerated. The cult of Saint Germanus is still alive in the Catholic canton of Jura, where many churches are dedicated to him.
</p><p>His feast day, shared with Randoald, is 21 February. The <a href="/wiki/Crosier" title="Crosier">crosier</a> of Germanus, one of the oldest remaining, is kept at <i>Musée jurassien d'art et d'histoire</i> in Delémont.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
