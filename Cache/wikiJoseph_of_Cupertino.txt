<p><b>Joseph of Cupertino</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Conventual" title="Order of Friars Minor Conventual">OFM Conv.</a> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Giuseppe da <a href="/wiki/Copertino" title="Copertino">Copertino</a></i>; 17 June 1603 – 18 September 1663) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Conventual_Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Conventual Franciscan">Conventual Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">Christian mystic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. According to traditional Franciscan accounts, he was "remarkably unclever", but experienced miraculous <a href="/wiki/Levitation_(paranormal)" title="Levitation (paranormal)">levitation</a> and <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">ecstatic visions</a> throughout his life which made him the object of scorn.<sup id="cite_ref-Pastro_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pastro-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He applied to the Conventual Franciscan friars, but was rejected due to his lack of education. He then pleaded with them to serve in their stables. After several years of working there, he had so impressed the friars with the devotion and simplicity of his life that he was admitted to their Order, destined to become a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a>, in 1625.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Cupertino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born the son of Felice Desa and Frencesca Panara in the village of <a href="/wiki/Copertino" title="Copertino">Cupertino</a>, in the Region of <a href="/wiki/Apulia" title="Apulia">Apulia</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>, now in the Italian <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Lecce" title="Province of Lecce">Province of Lecce</a>. His father having died before his birth, however, the family home was seized to settle the large debts he had left, and his mother was forced to give birth to him in a stable.
</p><p>Joseph began to experience <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">ecstatic visions</a> as a child, which were to  continue throughout his life, and made him the object of scorn. His life was not helped by his frequent outbursts of anger. He was soon apprenticed by his uncle to a shoemaker. Feeling drawn to religious life, in 1620 he applied to the Conventual Franciscan friars, but was rejected due to his lack of education. He then applied to the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin friars</a> in Martino, near <a href="/wiki/Taranto" title="Taranto">Taranto</a>, by whom he was accepted in 1620 as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>, but he was dismissed as his continued ecstasies made him unfit for the duties required of him.
</p><p>After Joseph returned to the scorn of his family, he pleaded with the Conventual friars near Cupertino to be allowed to serve in their stables. After several years of working there, he had so impressed the friars with the devotion and simplicity of his life that he was admitted to their Order, destined to become a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a>, in 1625. He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest on 28 March 1628. He was then sent to the convent of Santa Maria della Grotella, just outside Cupertino, where he spent the next 15 years.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After this point, the occasions of ecstasy in Joseph's life began to multiply. It was claimed that he began to levitate<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> while participating at the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> or joining the community for the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a>, thereby gaining a widespread reputation of holiness among the people of the region and beyond. He was deemed disruptive by his religious superiors and church authorities, however, and eventually was confined to a small cell, forbidden from joining in any public gathering of the community.
</p><p>As the phenomenon of flying or levitation was widely believed to be connected with <a href="/wiki/Witchcraft" title="Witchcraft">witchcraft</a>, Joseph was denounced to the <a href="/wiki/Inquisition" title="Inquisition">Inquisition</a>. At their command, he was transferred from one Franciscan friary in the region to another for observation, first to <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a> (1639–1653), then briefly to <a href="/wiki/Pietrarubbia" title="Pietrarubbia">Pietrarubbia</a> and finally <a href="/wiki/Fossombrone" title="Fossombrone">Fossombrone</a>, where he lived with and under the supervision of the Capuchin friars (1653–1657). He practiced a severe <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> throughout his life, usually eating solid food only twice a week, and adding bitter powders to his meals. He passed 35 years of his life following this regimen.
</p><p>Finally, on 9 July 1657, Joseph was allowed to return to a Conventual community, being sent to the one in <a href="/wiki/Osimo" title="Osimo">Osimo</a>, where he soon died.
</p><p>Joseph was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1753 and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1767.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reception">Reception</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Cupertino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Reception">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It has been pointed out that alleged eyewitness reports of his <a href="/wiki/Levitation_(paranormal)" title="Levitation (paranormal)">levitations</a> are unreliable as they are subject to gross <a href="/wiki/Exaggeration" title="Exaggeration">exaggeration</a>, or written two years after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Nickell_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nickell-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Robert D. Smith in his book <i>Comparative Miracles</i> (1965) suggested that Joseph performed feats similar to a <a href="/wiki/Gymnastics" title="Gymnastics">gymnast</a>. Smith noted that some of his alleged levitations "originate from a leap, and not from a prone or simple standing or kneeling position, the witnesses mistook a leap of a very agile man for levitation."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Skeptical investigator <a href="/wiki/Joe_Nickell" title="Joe Nickell">Joe Nickell</a> concluded that:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Joseph’s most dramatic aerial traverses were launched by a leap—not by a simple slow rising while merely standing or kneeling—but, moreover, I find that they appear to have continued as just the sudden arcing trajectories that would be expected from bounding. They were never circuitous or spiraling flights like a bird’s. Invariably, Joseph’s propulsions began with a shout or scream, suggesting that he was not caused to leap by some force but <i>chose</i> to.<sup id="cite_ref-Nickell_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nickell-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Human poisoning due to the consumption of <a href="/wiki/Rye_bread" title="Rye bread">rye bread</a> made from <a href="/wiki/Ergot" title="Ergot">ergot</a>-infected grain was common in Europe in the Middle Ages. It was known to cause <a href="/wiki/Convulsion" title="Convulsion">convulsion</a> symptoms and <a href="/wiki/Hallucination" title="Hallucination">hallucinations</a>. British academic <a href="/wiki/John_Cornwell_(writer)" title="John Cornwell (writer)">John Cornwell</a> has suggested that Joseph had consumed rye bread (see <a href="/wiki/Ergotism" title="Ergotism">ergot poisoning</a>). According to Cornwell "Here, perhaps, lay the key to his levitations. After sampling his own loaves he evidently believed he was taking off."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
