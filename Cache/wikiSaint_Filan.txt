					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Filan&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Filan">Saint Filan</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For Fillan of Strath Fillan, see <a href="/wiki/Fillan" title="Fillan">Fillan</a>.</div>
<p>
<b>Saint Fillan</b> was a sixth-century Scottish <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> active in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>. His feast day is 20 June.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackinlay_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackinlay-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg/220px-St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg/330px-St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg/440px-St_Fillan%27s_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1419612.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>St Fillan's Church, Aberdour</figcaption></figure>
<p>Fillan of Pittenweem is not to be confused with the later Fillan of Munster, who settled at <a href="/wiki/Strath_Fillan" title="Strath Fillan">Strath Fillan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackinlay_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackinlay-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Scott_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Scott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Fillan of Pittenweem worked in <a href="/wiki/Aberdour" title="Aberdour">Aberdour</a>, (where the parish church bears his name), as well as in <a href="/wiki/Forgan_(Fife)" title="Forgan (Fife)">Forgan</a>. On the top of Dunfillan near <a href="/wiki/Comrie,_Fife" title="Comrie, Fife">Comrie</a> was a rocky seat where, according to tradition, Fillan sat and gave his blessing to the country 'round. Up until the eighteenth century, there was a belief that sitting there could be beneficial for rheumatism of the back. A stone basin at the bottom the hill, was known as "Fillan's Spring", whose water was said to cure sore eyes.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackinlay_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackinlay-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to historian and antiquary <a href="/wiki/William_Forbes_Skene" title="William Forbes Skene">William Forbes Skene</a>, the village of <a href="/wiki/St_Fillans" title="St Fillans">St Fillans</a>, on the eastern end of <a href="/wiki/Loch_Earn" title="Loch Earn">Loch Earn</a>, takes its name from him.<sup id="cite_ref-Mackinlay_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mackinlay-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fillan of Pittenweem died at the <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)"><i>disert</i></a> of Tyrie near <a href="/wiki/Kinghorn" title="Kinghorn">Kinghorn</a><sup id="cite_ref-Scott_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Scott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="St_Fillan.27s_Cave"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St_Fillan's_Cave">St Fillan's Cave</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fillan_of_Pittenweem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: St Fillan&#039;s Cave">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St Fillan's Cave, situated in Cove <a href="/wiki/Wynd" title="Wynd">Wynd</a>, Pittenweem has long been associated with Fillan. The cave has flat rocks that are presumed to have been used as beds and a small spring of "<a href="/wiki/Holy_water" title="Holy water">holy water</a>" at its rear and a well. The cave was a stopping off point for pilgrims on their way to <a href="/wiki/St_Andrews" title="St Andrews">St Andrews</a> or <a href="/wiki/Ethernan" title="Ethernan">St. Ethernan's</a> shrine on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_May" title="Isle of May">Isle of May</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stfillans_stairwell_hi.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/50/Stfillans_stairwell_hi.jpg/220px-Stfillans_stairwell_hi.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="335" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/5/50/Stfillans_stairwell_hi.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="294" data-file-height="448" /></a><figcaption>St Fillan's Cave showing stairwell up to priory grounds</figcaption></figure>
<p>Antiquarian <a href="/wiki/Robert_Sibbald" title="Robert Sibbald">Robert Sibbald</a> says that in 1100, <a href="/wiki/Edgar,_King_of_Scotland" title="Edgar, King of Scotland">Edgar, King of Scotland</a> gave Pittenweem to the <a href="/wiki/Culdees" title="Culdees">Culdees</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Later, 
<a href="/wiki/David_I_of_Scotland" title="David I of Scotland">David I of Scotland</a> granted the monks of the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_May_Priory" title="Isle of May Priory">Priory of St. Mary the Virgin</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_May" title="Isle of May">Isle of May</a> the manor of Pittenweem,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> where they erected the Priory of St. Adrian<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> over the ancient cave associated with Saint Fillan. A stairway was built by the monks of the priory from the cave, ending in a vaulted cellar in the Priory grounds.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The cave was also used by smugglers for some time, and as a store room for local fisherfolk (Pittenweem has been a fishing village since the time of early <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> settlement and later a harbour was constructed). It served as a prison during the witch hunts of the 17-18th centuries and was used as a rubbish tip which probably resulted in its disappearance for some time.
</p><p>The cave was rediscovered about 1900 when a horse ploughing in the Priory garden fell down a hole into it.  It was rededicated as a place of worship by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_St._Andrews" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of St. Andrews">Bishop of St. Andrews</a> in 1935.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It has since been refurbished and opened to visitors as of October 2000, and is owned by the Bishop Low Trust. It is entrusted to St John's Scottish Episcopal Church in Pittenweem,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and is open to the public.
</p>
