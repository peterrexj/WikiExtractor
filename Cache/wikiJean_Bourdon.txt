					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:JeanBourdon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/JeanBourdon.jpg/200px-JeanBourdon.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="174" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/JeanBourdon.jpg/300px-JeanBourdon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/JeanBourdon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="303" data-file-height="263" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:JeanBourdon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Jean Bourdon.</div></div></div>
<p><b>Jean Bourdon </b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1601</span> &#8211; 1668) was the first engineer-in-chief and land-surveyor in the colony of <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a>, and the first attorney-general of the Conseil Superieur.
</p><p>Bourdon came to New France in 1634 and he was designated as the engineer to Governor <a href="/wiki/Charles_de_Montmagny" title="Charles de Montmagny">Charles de Montmagny</a>. In 1639, the governor made a land grant to him of 50 acres (200,000&#160;m<sup>2</sup>) and, later, he built a mill. He also built a chapel on it for his friend, Abbé <a href="/wiki/Jean_Le_Sueur" title="Jean Le Sueur">Jean Le Sueur</a>. This was only one of several seigneuries that Bourdon received.
</p>
