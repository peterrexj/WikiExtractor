<p><b>Marculf</b> (in French <i>Marcoult</i>, <i>Marcouf</i>, <i>Marcoul</i> or <i>Marcou</i>) (d. 558) <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was the abbot at <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Saint-Marcouf" title="Îles Saint-Marcouf">Nantus</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Cotentin" class="mw-redirect" title="Cotentin">Cotentin</a>. He is regarded as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and is associated with the healing of <a href="/wiki/Scrofula" class="mw-redirect" title="Scrofula">scrofula</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marculf&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Marculf was born in the <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxon</a> colony of <a href="/wiki/Bayeux" title="Bayeux">Bayeux</a> around AD 500. The accounts of his life are merged with that of Saint <a href="/wiki/Helier" title="Helier">Helier</a>, whom he sent to convert the inhabitants of <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a> to Christianity. Marcouf also visited Jersey himself, where <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> are ascribed to him.
</p><p>He died on May 1, 558, in the <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Saint-Marcouf" title="Îles Saint-Marcouf">Îles Saint-Marcouf</a> off the east coast of the <a href="/wiki/Cotentin_Peninsula" title="Cotentin Peninsula">Cotentin Peninsula</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were transferred to the abbey of Corbény in <a href="/wiki/Champagne_(province)" title="Champagne (province)">Champagne</a>, where they played a part in the coronation ceremonies of <a href="/wiki/Kings_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Kings of France">kings of France</a>, crowned at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, and in the tradition of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_touch" title="Royal touch">royal touch</a>.
</p><p>The traditional power ascribed to French and English kings to cure scrofula (<a href="/wiki/Tuberculous_cervical_lymphadenitis" class="mw-redirect" title="Tuberculous cervical lymphadenitis">the king's Evil</a>) by the laying on of hands derives from the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/efficacy#English" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:efficacy">efficacy</a> of the relics of Marcouf, according to the chronicle of Joan of Arc, <i><a href="/wiki/Chronique_de_la_Pucelle" title="Chronique de la Pucelle">Chronique de la Pucelle</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The north ambulatory of the <a href="/wiki/Coutances_Cathedral" title="Coutances Cathedral">Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Coutances</a> contains the Chapel of Saint Marcouf, with a stained glass window showing scenes from the saint's life.
</p>
