<p><b>Justus and Pastor</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Iustus et Pastor</i>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 304), venerated as <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>, were two <a href="/wiki/Schoolboy" class="mw-redirect" title="Schoolboy">schoolboy</a> brothers (Justus was 13 years old, Pastor less than 9) who were killed for their faith during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecutions. The boys are today considered the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of Alcalá.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> They are mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Prudentius" title="Prudentius">Prudentius</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justus_and_Pastor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the imperial proclamation was made public in <a href="/wiki/Complutum" class="mw-redirect" title="Complutum">Complutum</a>, Justus and Pastor, were at school. Stimulated by their desire to give their lives for <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>, they chucked the tablets they used for writing and ran to Dacianus’ residence to bear witness of their faith to the point of martyrdom, despite their young age. They confessed to Dacianus that they were Christians and were reluctant to sacrifice to idols. He tried to win them over with gifts, but he was unable to persuade them. They were viciously <a href="/wiki/Flogging" class="mw-redirect" title="Flogging">flogged</a> for denouncing the Roman religion and later <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> outside the <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> city of <a href="/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares" title="Alcalá de Henares">Alcalá de Henares</a> (known in Roman times as <a href="/wiki/Complutum" class="mw-redirect" title="Complutum">Complutum</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justus_and_Pastor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">Relics</a> believed to be those of Justus and Pastor were discovered in the 8th century and taken to <a href="/wiki/Huesca" title="Huesca">Huesca</a>. In 1568, they were brought back to <a href="/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares" title="Alcalá de Henares">Alcalá de Henares</a> and interred beneath the high altar of the city's <a href="/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares_Cathedral" title="Alcalá de Henares Cathedral">Cathedral-Magistral of Saints Justus and Pastor</a> (known familiarly as the Catedral de los Santos Niños). Constructed between 1497 and 1514,the ancient cathedral is still in daily use, and the saints' tomb is accessible to the public.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saints_Justus_and_Pastor" title="Basilica of Saints Justus and Pastor">Basilica of Saints Justus and Pastor</a> in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> was erected in the 16th century in the <a href="/wiki/Gothic_art" title="Gothic art">Gothic style</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Narbonne_Cathedral" title="Narbonne Cathedral">Narbonne Cathedral</a> located in the town of <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a> in southern France is also dedicated to Justus and Pastor.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justus_and_Pastor&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The story of Justus and Pastor is mentioned in the 2001 film <i><a href="/wiki/The_Others_(2001_film)" title="The Others (2001 film)">The Others</a></i>, where it is read by Nicholas (<a href="/wiki/James_Bentley_(actor)" title="James Bentley (actor)">James Bentley</a>), with his sister Anne (Alakina Mann) sitting nearby, under the supervision of their mother Grace Stewart (<a href="/wiki/Nicole_Kidman" title="Nicole Kidman">Nicole Kidman</a>). The children find the story amusing, where Anne criticises Justus and Pastor as being "really stupid" for facing a gruesome martyrdom, when they could have lied to spare their lives. She tells her mother (Kidman), "Inside I would have believed in Him, but I wouldn’t have told the Romans that."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
