<p><b>Godric of Finchale</b> (or <b>St Goderic</b>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1065-1070</span> – 21 May 1170)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, <a href="/wiki/Merchant" title="Merchant">merchant</a> and popular <a href="/wiki/Medieval" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval">medieval</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, although he was never formally <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a>. He was born in <a href="/wiki/Walpole,_Norfolk" title="Walpole, Norfolk">Walpole</a> in <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a> and died in <a href="/wiki/Finchale_Priory" title="Finchale Priory">Finchale</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Durham" title="County Durham">County Durham</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Godric_of_Finchale&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1062260506">.mw-parser-output .quotebox{background-color:#F9F9F9;border:1px solid #aaa;box-sizing:border-box;padding:10px;font-size:88%;max-width:100%}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatleft{margin:.5em 1.4em .8em 0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatright{margin:.5em 0 .8em 1.4em}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.centered{overflow:hidden;position:relative;margin:.5em auto .8em auto}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatleft span,.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatright span{font-style:inherit}.mw-parser-output .quotebox>blockquote{margin:0;padding:0;border-left:0;font-family:inherit;font-size:inherit}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-title{background-color:#F9F9F9;text-align:center;font-size:110%;font-weight:bold}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote>:first-child{margin-top:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote:last-child>:last-child{margin-bottom:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote.quoted:before{font-family:"Times New Roman",serif;font-weight:bold;font-size:large;color:gray;content:" “ ";vertical-align:-45%;line-height:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote.quoted:after{font-family:"Times New Roman",serif;font-weight:bold;font-size:large;color:gray;content:" ” ";line-height:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .left-aligned{text-align:left}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .right-aligned{text-align:right}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .center-aligned{text-align:center}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .quote-title,.mw-parser-output .quotebox .quotebox-quote{display:block}.mw-parser-output .quotebox cite{display:block;font-style:normal}@media screen and (max-width:640px){.mw-parser-output .quotebox{width:100%!important;margin:0 0 .8em!important;float:none!important}}</style><div class="quotebox pullquote floatleft" style="width:25%; ;">
<blockquote class="quotebox-quote left-aligned" style="">
<p>I. Saintë Marië Virginë, <br /> Moder Iesu Cristes Nazarenë,<br /> Onfo, schild, help thin Godric,<br /> Onfong bring hegilich<br /> With the in Godës riche.<br /><br />
II. Saintë Marië Cristes bur,<br /> Maidenës clenhad, moderës flur;<br />Dilie min sinnë, rix in min mod,<br />
Bring me to winnë with the selfd God.
</p>
</blockquote>
<p><cite class="left-aligned" style="">The first two hymns of St Godric, some of the earliest surviving musical settings in <a href="/wiki/Middle_English" title="Middle English">Middle English</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup></cite>
</p>
</div>
<p>Godric's life was recorded by a contemporary of his, a monk named <a href="/wiki/Reginald_of_Durham" title="Reginald of Durham">Reginald of Durham</a>. Several other <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> are also extant. According to these accounts, Godric, who began from humble beginnings as the son of Ailward and Edwenna, "both of slender rank and wealth, but abundant in righteousness and virtue". He began as a peddler and became an entrepreneur. "[H]e was wont to wander with small wares around the villages and farmsteads of his own neighborhood; but, in process of time, he gradually associated himself by compact with city merchants."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then he was a ship's captain and part owner of two ships, one of which may have conveyed <a href="/wiki/Baldwin_I_of_Jerusalem" title="Baldwin I of Jerusalem">Baldwin I of Jerusalem</a> to <a href="/wiki/Jaffa,_Israel" class="mw-redirect" title="Jaffa, Israel">Jaffa</a> in 1102. After many <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimages</a> around the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea" title="Mediterranean Sea">Mediterranean</a>, Godric found himself off the <a href="/wiki/Farne_Islands" title="Farne Islands">Farne Islands</a> near <a href="/wiki/Lindisfarne" title="Lindisfarne">Lindisfarne</a> and there was inspired to changed his life.<sup id="cite_ref-Norman_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norman-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Godric returned to England and lived at <a href="/wiki/Wolsingham" title="Wolsingham">Wolsingham</a> with an elderly hermit named Aelric (†1107) for two years.<sup id="cite_ref-northern_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-northern-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Upon Aelric's death, Godric made one last pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, and then returned home where he convinced <a href="/wiki/Ranulf_Flambard" title="Ranulf Flambard">Ranulf Flambard</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Durham" title="Bishop of Durham">Bishop of Durham</a>, to grant him a place to live as a hermit at Finchale, by the <a href="/wiki/River_Wear" title="River Wear">River Wear</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He had previously served as doorkeeper, the lowest of the <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a>, at the <a href="/wiki/St_Giles_Church,_Durham" title="St Giles Church, Durham">hospital church</a> of nearby <a href="/wiki/Kepier_Hospital" title="Kepier Hospital">St Giles Hospital</a> in <a href="/wiki/Durham,_England" title="Durham, England">Durham</a>. At Finchale he cleared forests to build a wooden oratory dedicated to the Virgin Mary; later he constructed a stone chapel dedicated to St John the Baptist.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is recorded to have lived at Finchale for the final sixty years of his life, occasionally meeting with visitors approved by the local prior. As the years passed, his reputation grew, and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> both reportedly sought Godric's advice as a wise and holy man.<sup id="cite_ref-Norman_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norman-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Song-Godric.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Song-Godric.jpg/220px-Song-Godric.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="269" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Song-Godric.jpg/330px-Song-Godric.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Song-Godric.jpg/440px-Song-Godric.jpg 2x" data-file-width="547" data-file-height="669" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Song-Godric.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>13th-century manuscript of the four hymns of St Godric</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fichale.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Fichale.jpg/220px-Fichale.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="157" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Fichale.jpg/330px-Fichale.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Fichale.jpg/440px-Fichale.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="571" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fichale.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Finchale_Priory" title="Finchale Priory">Finchale Priory</a> on the <a href="/wiki/River_Wear" title="River Wear">River Wear</a> on the site of Godric's hermitage</div></div></div>
<p>Reginald describes Godric's physical attributes:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>For he was vigorous and strenuous in mind, whole of limb and strong in body. He was of middle stature, broad-shouldered and deep-chested, with a long face, grey eyes most clear and piercing, bushy brows, a broad forehead, long and open nostrils, a nose of comely curve, and a pointed chin. His beard was thick, and longer than the ordinary, his mouth well-shaped, with lips of moderate thickness; in youth his hair was black, in age as white as snow; his neck was short and thick, knotted with veins and sinews; his legs were somewhat slender, his instep high, his knees hardened and horny with frequent kneeling; his whole skin rough beyond the ordinary, until all this roughness was softened by old age.</p></blockquote>
<p>St Godric is perhaps best remembered for his kindness toward animals, and many stories recall his protection of the creatures who lived near his forest home. According to one of these, he hid a stag from pursuing hunters; according to another, he even allowed snakes to warm themselves by his fire. Godric lived on a diet of <a href="/wiki/Herb" title="Herb">herbs</a>, wild <a href="/wiki/Honey" title="Honey">honey</a>, acorns, <a href="/wiki/Malus" title="Malus">crab-apples</a> and nuts.<sup id="cite_ref-Clay_1914_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clay_1914-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He slept on the bare ground.<sup id="cite_ref-Clay_1914_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clay_1914-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Reginald of Durham recorded four songs of St Godric's: they are the oldest songs in English for which the original musical settings survive. Reginald describes the circumstances in which Godric learnt the first song.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In a vision the Virgin Mary appeared to Godric with at her side "two maidens of surpassing beauty clad in shining white raiments." They pledged to come to his aid in times of need; and the Virgin herself taught Godric a song of consolation to overcome grief or temptation (<i>Saintë Marië Virginë</i>).
</p><p>The novel <i><a href="/wiki/Godric_(novel)" title="Godric (novel)">Godric</a></i> (1981) by <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Buechner" title="Frederick Buechner">Frederick Buechner</a> is a fictional retelling of his life and travels. It was a finalist for a <a href="/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize" title="Pulitzer Prize">Pulitzer Prize</a>.
</p><p>A tune named for him by its composer, <a href="/wiki/John_Bacchus_Dykes" title="John Bacchus Dykes">John Bacchus Dykes</a>, appears in dozens of hymnals.
</p>
