					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Acheolus and Acius</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 290</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a>, France</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 May</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saints <b>Acheolus</b> (or Acheul) and <b>Acius</b> were early Christian martyrs in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>. They are associated with <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a>, where Acheolus's name was given to an ancient church, later an abbey.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aceolus_and_Acius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2><p>
The martyrologist <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> wrote in his <i>The lives of the fathers, martyrs, and other principal saints</i> (1821),<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>May 1 – SS. Acius and Acheolus, Martyrs of Amiens
</p><p>[Called in French SS. Ach and Acheul.]  THEY seem to have suffered about the year 290<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>, and are honoured in the Gallican Martyrologies, and especially at Amiens, on the 1st of May. See Molanus in Auctario Usuardi, and Henschenius 1st of May, and an old Martyrology under the name of St. Jerom, quoted by him.
</p><p>
The church of St. Acheul, without the walls of Amiens, was originally the cathedral; but this being removed by <a href="/wiki/Salvius_of_Amiens" title="Salvius of Amiens">St. Salvius</a> to our Lady’s in the city, the church of St. Acheul became dependent on it. A community of regular canons was there erected in 1145. It is now a member of the reformed congregation of <a href="/wiki/Genevieve" title="Genevieve">St. Genevieve</a>. In digging foundations for a new church, five very ancient tombs were found, which have been the subject of many dissertations, especially whether one is not that of <a href="/wiki/Fermin" title="Fermin">St. Firminus</a>, bishop and confessor, whose relics are enshrined in the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler182114_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler182114-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miscellaneous">Miscellaneous</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aceolus_and_Acius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Miscellaneous">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Acheolus and Achius are honored on 1 May.
The emblem of Acheolus is an instrument resembling a <a href="/wiki/Wimble" class="mw-redirect" title="Wimble">wimble</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDrakeDrake19162_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDrakeDrake19162-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
According to <a href="/wiki/E._Cobham_Brewer" title="E. Cobham Brewer">E. Cobham Brewer</a> in <i>A Dictionary of Miracles: Imitative, Realistic, and Dogmatic</i> (1884), <link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><i>Christ Himself appears to <a href="/wiki/Honoratus_of_Amiens" title="Honoratus of Amiens">St. Honoré</a> and administers to him the eucharist</i> (sixth century). St. Honoré, on one occasion, went to St. Acheolus to assist in saying mass in the chapel of the Virgin, when Christ Himself appeared to him visibly, in human form, and administered to him the holy elements with His own bands, "lui accordant ainsi la même grâce qu'il avait faite aux Apôtres, le soir de sa Passion." In memory of this event, a divine hand is blazoned in the arms of the abbey of St. Acheolus.—<i>Les Petits Hollandistes</i>, vol. v. p. 676.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrewer188420_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrewer188420-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<p>The saint's name survives in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Acheul" title="Abbey of Saint-Acheul">Abbey of Saint-Acheul</a> in the Saint-Acheul district of Amiens, and in the commune of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Acheul" title="Saint-Acheul">Saint-Acheul</a> to the north of Amiens,
</p>
