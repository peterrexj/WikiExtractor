					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Byzantine Greek holy man and visionary</div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Death_of_Theodora.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Death_of_Theodora.jpg/220px-Death_of_Theodora.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="257" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Death_of_Theodora.jpg/330px-Death_of_Theodora.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Death_of_Theodora.jpg/440px-Death_of_Theodora.jpg 2x" data-file-width="869" data-file-height="1015" /></a><figcaption>19th-century Russian depiction of Gregory's vision of Theodora's death and the <a href="/wiki/Aerial_toll_house" title="Aerial toll house">aerial toll houses</a>. This vision was granted him by Basil's intercession and begins in Basil's house, where Theodora lay dying.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint <b>Basil the Younger</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 26 March 944/952) was a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Greeks" title="Byzantine Greeks">Byzantine Greek</a> holy man and visionary. He is the subject of a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Greek" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Greek">Greek</a> <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographical biography</a>, the <i><b>Vita sancti Basilii iunioris</b></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> written by his pupil Gregory. Although the <i>Vita</i> portrays its subject as historical, there is yet no consensus whether Basil or even Gregory were real persons or fabrications.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMagdalino199989–91_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMagdalino199989–91-3">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFoxhall_Forbes2018_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFoxhall_Forbes2018-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAngelidi201526_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAngelidi201526-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983576_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983576-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Vita"><i>Vita</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Vita">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Manuscripts">Manuscripts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Manuscripts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <i>Vita sancti Basilii iunioris</i> is preserved in whole or in part in a total of 24 manuscripts dating from the 12th to the 19th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETimotin2006406–407_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETimotin2006406–407-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The fullest is the 16th-century Greek MS no. 249 of the Synodal (Patriarchal) Library in Moscow and published in François Halkin, <i><a href="/wiki/Bibliotheca_Hagiographica_Graeca" title="Bibliotheca Hagiographica Graeca">Bibliotheca Hagiographica Graeca</a></i>, 3rd edition (Brussels, 1957).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Two 13th-century manuscripts are also known. <a href="/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_nationale_de_France" title="Bibliothèque nationale de France">Parisinus</a> Gr. 1547 is an abridged text that uses less florid language generally. It has been published by <a href="/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Combefis" title="François Combefis">François Combefis</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i>, March III (1668).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The codex Iviron 478 from <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> contains only the visions and none of the biographical or historical material. It has been published by S. G. Vilinskij in <i>Zapiski Imperatorskogo novorossijskogo universiteta</i> (Odessa, 1911).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Despite the publication of parts of these three manuscripts, no <a href="/wiki/Critical_edition" class="mw-redirect" title="Critical edition">critical edition</a> was produced<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETimotin2006406–407_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETimotin2006406–407-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> before 2014, when a critical edition with an annotated English translation appeared.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESullivanTalbotMcGrath2014_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESullivanTalbotMcGrath2014-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 14th-century, the <i>Vita</i> was translated into <a href="/wiki/Middle_Bulgarian" title="Middle Bulgarian">Middle Bulgarian</a>. This text was only found in 1975 in codex no. 20N from <a href="/wiki/St_Catherine%27s_Monastery,_Sinai" class="mw-redirect" title="St Catherine&#39;s Monastery, Sinai">St Catherine's Monastery, Sinai</a>. The translation was made by a certain Father Peter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEvangelou2009_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvangelou2009-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Date_of_composition">Date of composition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Date of composition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The composition of the <i>Vita</i> is usually dated to between 956 and 959. The <i><a href="/wiki/Terminus_post_quem" title="Terminus post quem">terminus post quem</a></i> is provided by the death of the Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Theophylact_of_Constantinople" title="Theophylact of Constantinople">Theophylaktos</a>, whom Gregory readily criticizes; the <i><a href="/wiki/Terminus_ante_quem" class="mw-redirect" title="Terminus ante quem">terminus ante quem</a></i> by the death of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_VII" title="Constantine VII">Constantine VII</a>, since his joint reign with his son <a href="/wiki/Romanos_II" title="Romanos II">Romanos II</a> is mentioned but there is no reference to Romanos' sole reign. The <i>terminus ante</i> may, however, be pushed back to 961 (the death of Romanos' mother <a href="/wiki/Helena_Lekapene" title="Helena Lekapene">Helena Lekapene</a>) or even 963 (date of composition of the <i>Vision of Kosmas</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Vision_of_Theodora">Vision of Theodora</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Vision of Theodora">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Aerial_toll_house" title="Aerial toll house">Aerial toll house</a></div>
<p>One of the most famous passage's in the <i>Vita</i> concerns the death of Theodora, Basil's servant. Shortly after her death, Gregory asks Basil if Theodora was rewarded in the afterlife and Basil grants Gregory a vision. His vision begins in Basil's house, where Theodora is dying. She is surrounded by <a href="/wiki/Aethiopia" title="Aethiopia">Ethiopians</a> (representative of <a href="/wiki/Demons" class="mw-redirect" title="Demons">demons</a>), who bear documents recording all her sins. As Theodora's soul exits her body, two angels weigh her good deeds against her sins until Basil arrives to provide a scarlet bag full of gold, his <a href="/wiki/Supererogatory" class="mw-redirect" title="Supererogatory">supererogatory</a> good works, to outweigh her sins. The demons leave and Theodora and the angels go up through the air.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729–33_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729–33-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The passage through the air towards <a href="/wiki/Heaven" title="Heaven">heaven</a> is interrupted by 21 toll houses (<i><a href="/wiki/Teloneum" title="Teloneum">telonia</a></i>), each manned by demons and dedicated to exposing a specific sin:<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729–33_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729–33-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Theodora's soul runs out of good deeds to outweigh her sins by the fifth toll house and must rely thereafter on Basil's gold. The angels tell her that at baptism each person receives a <a href="/wiki/Guardian_angel" title="Guardian angel">guardian angel</a> to record her good deeds and a demon to record her sins. If she fails at any toll house, the demons cast her soul into <a href="/wiki/Hades" title="Hades">Hades</a>. The angels inform Theodora, who did not practice confession, that only through <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a> and <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> can one erase one's sins while alive. The toll house of fornication, they add, is the most dangerous one. Theodora successfully passes through the toll houses, enters heaven and <a href="/wiki/Beatific_vision" title="Beatific vision">sees God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729–33_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarinis201729–33-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Of the 301 printed pages of the Moscow version of the <i>Vita</i>, 38 cover the vision of the death of Basil's servant Theodora and 162 cover the visions of the <a href="/wiki/Resurrection_of_the_Dead" class="mw-redirect" title="Resurrection of the Dead">Resurrection of the Dead</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Last_Judgement" class="mw-redirect" title="Last Judgement">Last Judgement</a>. The <i>Vita</i> is as much a piece of <a href="/wiki/Visionary_literature" class="mw-redirect" title="Visionary literature">visionary literature</a> as it is a standard saint's life. "The author ... is evidently less concerned with [Basil's life] as such than with using him as a pretext for recounting numerous other matters of interest to him."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chronologically, the <i>Vita</i> is divided into three parts separated by long gaps. Between the first and second parts there is a gap of 17 years and between the second and third one of about 20 years.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Move_to_Constantinople">Move to Constantinople</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Move to Constantinople">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the start of the <i>Vita</i>, Basil is living in <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a> as a grass-eating hermit or <i>boskos</i>. Arrested on suspicion of espionage, he is brought to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, where he is interrogated, tortured and thrown before a lion by the <i><a href="/wiki/Parakoimomenos" title="Parakoimomenos">parakoimomenos</a></i> <a href="/wiki/Samonas" title="Samonas">Samonas</a>. Unbroken, he does not even reveal his name and is thrown into the sea, where he is rescued by dolphins, who take him to <a href="/wiki/Hebdomon" class="mw-redirect" title="Hebdomon">Hebdomon</a>. From there he returns to Constantinople, where he is sheltered by a poor husband and wife named John and Helena. This episode is said to take place in the tenth year of the joint reign of <a href="/wiki/Leo_VI_the_Wise" title="Leo VI the Wise">Leo VI</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alexander_(Byzantine_emperor)" title="Alexander (Byzantine emperor)">Alexander</a>, which would be 896. Samonas, however, did not become <i>parakoimomenos</i> until 907.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of John and Helena, Basil moves into the house of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Barbaros" title="Constantine Barbaros">Constantine Barbaros</a>, Samonas' successor as <i>parakoimomenos</i>, in the <a href="/wiki/Arkadianai" class="mw-redirect" title="Arkadianai">Arkadianai</a> quarter. There he spent the rest of his life except for a week he spent in the <a href="/wiki/Great_Palace_of_Constantinople" title="Great Palace of Constantinople">Great Palace of Constantinople</a> and a short period he spent as a guest in the house of the Paphlagonian brothers <a href="/w/index.php?title=Anastasios_Gongyles&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Anastasios Gongyles (page does not exist)">Anastasios</a> and <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Gongyles" title="Constantine Gongyles">Constantine Gongylios</a> near the <a href="/wiki/Harbour_of_Eleutherios" title="Harbour of Eleutherios">Harbour of Eleutherios</a>. These brothers, relatives of Barbaros and of the <i><a href="/wiki/Tourmarches" class="mw-redirect" title="Tourmarches">tourmarches</a></i> of Paphlagonia, are said to have been held in high regard by those reigning at the time, which points to the period of the regency of Empress <a href="/wiki/Zoe_Karbonopsina" title="Zoe Karbonopsina">Zoe Karbonopsina</a> in 914–919 for Basil's stay. All three of Basil's hosts—Barbaros and the Gongylioi—were <a href="/wiki/Eunuch" title="Eunuch">eunuchs</a>. Barbaros disappears from the historical record after the failed revolt his relative, <a href="/wiki/Leo_Phokas_the_Elder" title="Leo Phokas the Elder">Leo Phokas</a>, in 919.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his stay in the Great Palace, Basil rebukes Romanos I for his greediness and lechery, a reproach that the emperor, indulgent towards monks, did not mind. Basil also convinces a certain Kosmas, who had ambitions of becoming emperor, to abandon his worldly pursuits and become a hermit near <a href="/wiki/Nicomedeia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicomedeia">Nicomedeia</a>. This story seems to be based on that of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Kosmas_the_Monk&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Kosmas the Monk (page does not exist)">Kosmas the Monk</a>, who had a famous vision in 933.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Predictions_and_visions">Predictions and visions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Predictions and visions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The second part of the <i>Vita</i> recounts in detail the rebellion of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Doukas_(usurper)" title="Constantine Doukas (usurper)">Constantine Doukas</a> in 913, which Basil is said to have predicted. Gregory admits that this part of the work is little concerned with Basil. It may be included because Doukas's widow was forced to retire to an estate in <a href="/wiki/Paphlagonia" title="Paphlagonia">Paphlagonia</a> or as an excuse to criticize the Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Mystikos" title="Nicholas Mystikos">Nicholas Mystikos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The third part of the <i>Vita</i> begins in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Romanos_I_Lekapenos" title="Romanos I Lekapenos">Romanos I</a>, specifically after the death of his son <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Lekapenos" title="Christopher Lekapenos">Christopher</a> (931) and at a time when there were seven persons of imperial rank in the palace. This is probably a reference to the widowed Romanos I, and his sons <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Lekapenos" title="Stephen Lekapenos">Stephen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Lekapenos" title="Constantine Lekapenos">Constantine</a> and their wives, as well as Romanos' daughter Helena Lekapene and her husband, Constantine VII. The date of the marriages of Stephen and Constantine are not known with certainty, but Stephen's wife, Anna Gabalas, was only crowned in 933 and Constantine's first wife is not recorded before 939. Basil, however, prophesies that Helena will give birth to another daughter and then a son named Romanos, Since Romanos II was born in 938, this prophecy could only have been made in the mid-930s.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In this part, Basil also predicts the <a href="/wiki/Rus%27%E2%80%93Byzantine_War_(941)" title="Rus&#39;–Byzantine War (941)">Rus' attack on Constantinople</a> in 941 four months in advance. God also gives him foreknowledge of the planned <i>coup d'état</i> of Romanos I's son-in-law Romanos Saronites. Basil tries to talk Saronites out of it, but is treated cruelly. Saronites then falls ill and dies. This is contradicted by <a href="/wiki/John_Skylitzes" title="John Skylitzes">John Skylitzes</a>, who says that Saronites entered a monastery during the reign of Romanos II. Skylitzes does link his retirement to a rebellion, but does not implicate Saronites in it.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Basil is said to have died on 26 March during <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a>, which corresponds to either 944 or 952.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETimotin2006406–407_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETimotin2006406–407-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The earlier date is more likely.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> According to Gregory, he was buried by Constantine Barbaros in the private church of the Theotokos on the Asian side of the strait across from the capital. This is chronologically implausible, given that Barbaros is not heard of after 919. The likely source for this story is <a href="/wiki/Pseudo-Symeon" class="mw-redirect" title="Pseudo-Symeon">Pseudo-Symeon</a>, who records that Barbaros' father owned "a small suburban estate by the sea" near the capital and that Leo VI turned it into a monastery. The chronological inconsistencies and creative use of sources tell against the general historicity of the <i>Vita</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERydén1983_9-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERydén1983-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
