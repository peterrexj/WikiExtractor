<p><b>Carlos Manuel Cecilio Rodríguez Santiago</b>, also known as "Blessed Charlie" (22 November 22, 1918 – July 13, 1963), was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> catechist and liturgist who was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on April 29, 2001.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is the first <a href="/wiki/Puerto_Rican_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Puerto Rican people">Puerto Rican</a> and the first <a href="/wiki/Caribbean" title="Caribbean">Caribbean</a>-born layperson in history to be beatified.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Rodríguez was born in 1918,<sup id="cite_ref-Childs_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Childs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in Caguas, Puerto Rico, the son of Manuel Baudelio Rodriguez Rodriguez and Herminia Santiago Esteras, both from large, Catholic families. He was baptized at the nearby <a href="/wiki/Catedral_Dulce_Nombre_de_Jes%C3%BAs_(Caguas,_Puerto_Rico)" title="Catedral Dulce Nombre de Jesús (Caguas, Puerto Rico)">Sweet Name of Jesus Church</a> (now the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> of the region) on May 4, 1919. Rodríguez was the second of five brothers and sisters. Two of his sisters married, while another became a <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelite nun</a>. His brother, José (Pepe) Rodriguez became a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and the first Puerto Rican to become <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of his <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanList_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanList-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1925, a fire destroyed the family's residence and business, and they were forced to live with his mother's parents. That same year, Rodríguez was enrolled to study at the <a href="/wiki/Colegio_Cat%C3%B3lico_Notre_Dame" title="Colegio Católico Notre Dame">Colegio Católico Notre Dame</a>, attached to the parish church. After graduating the Catholic elementary school, he began to attend <a href="/wiki/Gautier_Ben%C3%ADtez_High_School" title="Gautier Benítez High School">Gautier Benítez High School</a>. His desire to become a priest was undermined by ill health.<sup id="cite_ref-RCL_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RCL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At that point, he began to develop <a href="/wiki/Ulcerative_colitis" title="Ulcerative colitis">ulcerative colitis</a>. After two years at the local public high school, he transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Academia_del_Perpetuo_Socorro" title="Academia del Perpetuo Socorro">Academy of Our Lady of Perpetual Help</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Juan,_Puerto_Rico" title="San Juan, Puerto Rico">San Juan</a>. His medical problems, however, caused him to leave before graduation. He returned to the family home and continued his high school studies as best he could while working as a clerk, finally receiving his diploma in May 1939.<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanList_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanList-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pastoral_life">Pastoral life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Pastoral life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>While Rodríguez was working as an office clerk in various towns of the region, he dedicated his resources to promote a greater knowledge of the Catholic faith by promoting a greater understanding of the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a>. Using articles on liturgical subjects he had translated and edited, he began publishing <i>Liturgy</i> and <i>Christian Culture</i>, which he dedicated innumerable hours. Rodríguez organized discussion groups in towns across the entire island and worked with Catholic social organizations to disseminate his ideas. He also taught <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to high school students whose study aids he supplied out of his pocket.<sup id="cite_ref-RCL_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RCL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was a <a href="/wiki/Knight_of_Columbus" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of Columbus">Knight of Columbus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-knight_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-knight-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1946 Rodríguez enrolled at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Puerto_Rico" title="University of Puerto Rico">University of Puerto Rico</a> in <a href="/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Piedras,_Puerto_Rico" title="Río Piedras, Puerto Rico">Río Piedras</a>, to pursue higher studies, where his brother José and sister Haydée were already UPR faculty members. As his disciples grew in number, he moved into nearby Catholic University Center and organized another Liturgy Circle (later called the <i>Círculo de Cultura Cristiana</i>). Despite excellent grades and his love for studies, however, illness prevented him from completing his second year. Nonetheless, he was a voracious reader and, with only a year's study, was able to master both the piano and the church organ. In 1948, he assembled along with Father McGlone, the parroquial chorus "Te Deum Laudamus".<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanList_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanList-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Mousolfe1986_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mousolfe1986-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rodríguez zealously promoted a renewal of the Catholic liturgy among bishops, clergy, and laypeople. He professed extreme devotion to the liturgy and worked to repair the loss of liturgical customs that had been abandoned over generations. He advocated for active participation of the laity in prayer, the use of the vernacular, and – most significantly – the observance of his much loved Paschal Vigil in its proper nighttime setting, after centuries of having this service celebrated on the morning of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Saturday" title="Holy Saturday">Holy Saturday</a>. Increasingly convinced that “the liturgy is the life of the Church," he organized a "Liturgy Circle" in Caguas to foment better knowledge among the people. He expressed particular concern over the Easter <a href="/wiki/Vigil" title="Vigil">vigil</a>, saying that it had lost its ancient character as the focal night of the Christian year. To his delight, the Easter vigil was restored to its proper time near midnight by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> in 1952. One of his favorite sayings about this feast was <i><b>Vivimos para esa noche</b></i> (We live for that night). This is now the <a href="/wiki/Motto" title="Motto">motto</a> on his tomb, which is located in the Cathedral of Caguas.<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanList_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanList-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Rodríguez was diagnosed with rectal cancer following an operation in 1963<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanList_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanList-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and died on July 13, 1963, at the age of 44.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Caguas_cathedral.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Caguas_cathedral.jpg/250px-Caguas_cathedral.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="261" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f6/Caguas_cathedral.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="300" data-file-height="313" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Caguas_cathedral.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Caguas cathedral "Dulce Nombre de Jesús" (Sweet Name of Jesus). On the facade an image of Blessed <a href="/wiki/Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez" class="mw-redirect" title="Carlos Manuel Rodríguez">Carlos Manuel Rodríguez</a>, whose relics rest in the cathedral.</div></div></div>
<p>Rodríguez did not care about possessions or money. As an adult, he only owned one pair of shoes. Those shoes are kept at his sister's house, where people leave notes in them, asking for his prayers.<sup id="cite_ref-RCL_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RCL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG/150px-Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG/225px-Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG/300px-Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3240" data-file-height="4320" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Catedral_de_San_Juan_Bautista_de_Puerto_Rico_-_DSC06864.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relics at the Cathedral of San Juan</div></div></div>
<p>In 1991, a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> from Spain, Mauro Meza, was authorized by the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> to take the story to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a>. In Rome, Meza initiated the process of inquiry that could lead to canonization. In 1981, a 42-year-old mother was diagnosed with non-<a href="/wiki/Hodgkin%27s_lymphoma" class="mw-redirect" title="Hodgkin&#39;s lymphoma">Hodgkin's</a> <a href="/wiki/Malignant" class="mw-redirect" title="Malignant">malignant</a> <a href="/wiki/Lymphoma" title="Lymphoma">lymphoma</a>. She and her husband had been friends of Rodríguez during his college years and knew of his death from cancer. She prayed to Rodríguez for <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercession</a> on her behalf. After fully recovering, she publicly attributed her recovery to the miracle of intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-Childs_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Childs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On July 7, 1997, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> decreed Rodríguez's heroic sanctity and service in his life. The process took a major step on April 29, 2001, when Rodríguez was beatified by Pope John Paul.<sup id="cite_ref-Childs_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Childs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-knight_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-knight-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Rodríguez is the first Puerto Rican person and the first <a href="/wiki/Caribbean" title="Caribbean">Caribbean</a>-born layperson in history to be beatified. In the entire <a href="/wiki/Western_Hemisphere" title="Western Hemisphere">Western Hemisphere</a>, Rodríguez is only the second layperson to be beatified, the first being <a href="/wiki/Kateri_Tekakwitha" title="Kateri Tekakwitha">St. Kateri Tekakwitha</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MendezMéndez-Méndez2003_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MendezMéndez-Méndez2003-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The 1983 reform of the Catholic Church's canon law has streamlined the canonization procedure considerably compared to the process carried out previously. Pope John Paul II established the new method, in his <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_constitution" title="Apostolic constitution">apostolic constitution</a> of January 25, 1983, <i>Divinus Perfectionis Magister</i> and by the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Palazzini" title="Pietro Palazzini">Pietro Palazzini</a>, Prefect of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a>. For Rodríguez to pass from Blessed to Saint, one more miracle (confirmed by the Vatican) is necessary.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carlos_Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Santiago&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>A school in Bayamón is named after him, with the blessed title. The school, called Escuela Superior Católica de Bayamón initially, was renamed in 2001: Colegio Beato Carlos Manuel Rodríguez. Staff from the school witnessed the beatification ceremony.</li></ul>
