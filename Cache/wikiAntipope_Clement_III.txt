<p><b>Guibert</b> or <b>Wibert of Ravenna</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1029&#160;&#8211;&#32;8 September 1100) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Ravenna" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Ravenna">archbishop of Ravenna</a>, who was elected pope in 1080 in opposition to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a> and took the name <b>Clement III</b>. Gregory was the leader of the movement in the church which opposed the traditional claim of European monarchs to control <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> appointments, and this was opposed by supporters of monarchical rights led by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a>. This led to the conflict known as the <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">Investiture Controversy</a>. Gregory was felt by many to have gone too far when he <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> the Holy Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a> and supported a rival claimant as emperor, and in 1080 the pro-imperial <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Brixen" title="Synod of Brixen">Synod of Brixen</a> pronounced that Gregory was deposed and replaced as pope by Guibert.
</p><p>Consecrated as Pope Clement III in Rome in March 1084, he commanded a significant following in Rome and elsewhere, especially during the first half of his pontificate, and reigned in opposition to four successive popes in the anti-imperial line: <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Gregory VII</a>, <a href="/wiki/Victor_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Victor III">Victor III</a>, <a href="/wiki/Urban_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Urban II">Urban II</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Paschal_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Paschal II">Paschal II</a>. After his death and burial at <a href="/wiki/Civita_Castellana" title="Civita Castellana">Civita Castellana</a> in 1100 he was celebrated locally as a miracle-working saint, but Paschal II and the anti-imperial party soon subjected him to  <i><a href="/wiki/Damnatio_memoriae" title="Damnatio memoriae">damnatio memoriae</a></i>, which included the exhuming and dumping of his remains in the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is considered an <a href="/wiki/Anti-pope" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-pope">anti-pope</a> by the Roman Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-Dolcini_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dolcini-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Clement_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born into the noble family of the Correggio, probably between 1020 and 1030.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHayes1911_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHayes1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had family connections to the <a href="/wiki/Margrave" title="Margrave">margraves</a> of <a href="/wiki/Canossa" title="Canossa">Canossa</a>.  A cleric, he was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Archchancellor" title="Archchancellor">Imperial chancellorship</a> for <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> by <a href="/wiki/Empress_Agnes" class="mw-redirect" title="Empress Agnes">Empress Agnes</a> in 1058, which position he held until 1063.<sup id="cite_ref-Dolcini_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dolcini-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1058 he participated in the election of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II" title="Pope Nicholas II">Pope Nicholas II</a> but on the death of Nicholas in 1061, Guibert sided with the imperial party to elect Cadalous of Parma as <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Honorius_II" title="Antipope Honorius II">Antipope Honorius II</a> against <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a>. Owing to the active support of Duke Godfrey of Lorraine, of Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Anno_II" title="Anno II">Anno II</a> of Cologne, and especially of <a href="/wiki/Peter_Damian" title="Peter Damian">Peter Damian</a>, Alexander was soon recognized even in Germany and by Empress Agnes which may have been the reason of Guibert's dismissal in 1063 from the chancellorship.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Guibert apparently continued to cultivate his contacts within the German court, for in 1072, <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry IV">Emperor Henry IV</a> named him archbishop of the vacant see of <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoulombe2003218_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoulombe2003218-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> And, although Pope Alexander II was reluctant to confirm this appointment, he was persuaded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Hildebrand</a> to do so, perhaps as a compromise for peace. Guibert then took an oath of allegiance to the pope and his successors and was installed at Ravenna in 1073.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quarrels_with_Pope_Gregory">Quarrels with Pope Gregory</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Clement_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Quarrels with Pope Gregory">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Shortly after Pope Alexander II died Hildebrand was proclaimed the next pope, being installed as Pope Gregory VII on 29 April 1073.  Guibert attended the first <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lenten</a> <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">Synods</a> of Pope Gregory in March 1074 in Rome at which important laws were passed against <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a> and the incontinence of the clergy, and <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">lay investiture</a>. The Imperial appointed German bishops were the most important officials of the empire, and a means to balance the ambitions of the territorial princes.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Guibert soon emerged as one of the most visible leaders of opposition to the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_reforms" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian reforms">Gregorian reforms</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Having attended Gregory's first Lenten Synods, Guibert refused to attend the next, the Lenten Synod of 1075, although he was bound by oath to obey the summons to attend. Guibert was unsympathetic to Gregory's opposition to the Imperial Court, which Guibert had served as Chancellor of Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> By his absence Guibert demonstrated his opposition to Gregory VII, who now suspended him for his refusal to attend the synod.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was in this same year that Emperor Henry IV began his open conflict with Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> At the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Worms_(1076)" title="Synod of Worms (1076)">synod of Worms</a> in January 1076, a resolution was adopted deposing Gregory, and in this decision the pro-imperial bishops of Transalpine Italy joined. Among these must have been Guibert, for he shared in the sentence of excommunication and interdiction which Gregory VII pronounced against the Transalpine bishops at the Lenten Synod of 1076.
</p><p>Shortly after, in April 1076, bishops and abbots of the imperial Transalpine party convened at <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> under the presidency of Guibert and proclaimed the excommunication of Gregory VII; a messenger, bearing a caustic personal letter from Henry, was dispatched with the Pavian reply to the pope. In response to the action of Henry's 1076 Synod of Worms, Gregory excommunicated Henry IV. Gregory excommunicated Guibert by name at the Lenten Synod of February 1078 and with him his main accomplice Archbishop Tebaldo of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reign_as_Imperial_Opposition_Pope">Reign as Imperial Opposition Pope</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Clement_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Reign as Imperial Opposition Pope">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the next four years, the Emperor and the Pope reconciled but then quarreled again, and, facing a rebellion among the German nobles, Emperor Henry threatened to depose Pope Gregory. Carrying out his threats, Henry summoned his German and Transpadine partisans to a Synod at <a href="/wiki/Brixen" title="Brixen">Brixen</a> in June 1080, which drew up a new decree purporting to depose Pope Gregory VII,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> and which Henry himself also signed, and then proceeded to elect Guibert, the excommunicated Archbishop of Ravenna, as pope in opposition to Pope Gregory, whom the Synod considered deposed; Guibert took the name Clement III.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Henry recognized Guibert as pope, swearing that he would lead him to Rome, and there receive from his hands the imperial crown.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With <a href="/wiki/Rudolph_of_Swabia" class="mw-redirect" title="Rudolph of Swabia">Rudolph of Swabia</a>, leader of the rebellious nobles, having fallen mortally wounded at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Mersburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Mersburg">Battle of Mersburg</a> in 1080, Henry could concentrate all his forces against Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In 1081, he marched on Rome, but failed to force his way into the city, which he finally accomplished only in 1084.
</p><p>Henry entered Rome on 21 March 1084, and succeeded in gaining possession of the greater part of the city. Gregory took refuge in <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Castel Sant'Angelo</a>. On 24 March, Guibert was enthroned as pope in the church of <a href="/wiki/St._John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John Lateran">St. John Lateran</a> as Clement III, and on 31 March he crowned Henry IV as Emperor at St. Peter's.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>However, with news of the approach of the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> army of Gregory's  ally, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Guiscard" title="Robert Guiscard">Robert Guiscard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Apulia_and_Calabria" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Apulia and Calabria">Duke of Apulia and Calabria</a>, Henry and Guibert abandoned Rome, and, in revenge for <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Tuscany" title="Matilda of Tuscany">Matilda of Tuscany</a>'s support of Gregory, Henry ravaged her possessions in <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. Guibert withdrew to Ravenna, where he still held the title of archbishop. His influence, after Henry IV's withdrawal from Italy, was largely concentrated in Ravenna and a few other districts of Northern Italy, but he also retained some support in Rome.
</p><p>Gregory was liberated, but the people were incensed by the excesses of his Norman allies, and he was compelled to leave Rome. He withdrew to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>, and later to the castle of <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a> by the sea, in 1084, where he died in the following year, 25 May 1085.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The German <a href="/wiki/Episcopate" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopate">episcopate</a> stood divided. While bishops of Gregory VII's party held a Synod in <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg" title="Quedlinburg">Quedlinburg</a>, at which they denounced and condemned Guibert, partisans of Henry held a rival Synod at <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a> in 1085, where they approved the deposition of Gregory and the elevation of Guibert. This conflict continued even after the death of Gregory, during the entire reigns of whose successors, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_III" title="Pope Victor III">Pope Victor III</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a>, Guibert continued to be regarded as pope by Henry and his party.
</p><p>Victor III, who was elected after a prolonged vacancy caused by the critical position of the Church in Rome, was compelled, eight days after his coronation in St. Peter's on 3 May 1087, to flee Rome before the partisans of Guibert. The latter were in turn assailed by the troops of Countess Matilda, and entrenched themselves in the Pantheon. However, at the threats of the emperor, Victor was obliged to flee once more.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The succeeding pope, Urban II (1088–1099), spent most of the first half of his pontificate in exile, in southern Italy and in France.  Late in 1093 he managed to obtain a foothold in Rome, with help from the Frangipane family, and gradually expanded his power there.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1089, Clement III was back in Rome, where in June, he held a Synod declaring invalid the decree of excommunication launched against Henry, and various charges were made against the supporters of Urban II, the pope of the anti-imperial party.
Still, by the mid-nineties his power and authority began to wane. The greater part of the city of Rome was captured by an army under Count <a href="/wiki/Hugh_I,_Count_of_Vermandois" class="mw-redirect" title="Hugh I, Count of Vermandois">Hugh of Vermandois</a>, brother of the <a href="/wiki/King_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="King of France">King of France</a>. The party of Guibert retained only the Castle of Sant' Angelo, and even this in 1098 fell into the hands of Vermandois.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1099, he repaired to <a href="/wiki/Albano_Laziale" title="Albano Laziale">Albano</a> after the accession of Paschal II (1099–1118), hoping again to become master of Rome, but Norman troops compelled him to withdraw. He reached <a href="/wiki/Civita_Castellana" title="Civita Castellana">Civita Castellana</a>, where he died 8 September 1100.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> His followers elected a successor to Guibert, the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Theodoric" title="Antipope Theodoric">Antipope Theodoric</a>, who, however, was not a serious threat to the popes of the anti-imperial line, now considered canonical.
</p>
