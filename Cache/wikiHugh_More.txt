<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Thomas Holford</b> (sometimes called <b>Thomas Acton</b>) (1541–1588) was an English <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> schoolteacher who became a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> during the reign of Queen <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyred</a> at <a href="/wiki/Clerkenwell" title="Clerkenwell">Clerkenwell</a> in London, and is recognised by the Catholic Church as having the status of <i><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a></i>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Holford&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Holford, the son of a minister,<sup id="cite_ref-Pullan2008_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pullan2008-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was born in 1541 near <a href="/wiki/Nantwich" title="Nantwich">Nantwich</a>, Cheshire, at Aston<sup id="cite_ref-SP_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Acton,_Cheshire" title="Acton, Cheshire">Acton</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Byrne_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Byrne-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was raised as a Protestant and became a schoolteacher. He moved to <a href="/wiki/Herefordshire" title="Herefordshire">Herefordshire</a> to become resident tutor to the children of <a href="/wiki/John_Scudamore_(courtier)" title="John Scudamore (courtier)">Sir James Scudamore</a> of <a href="/wiki/Holme_Lacy" title="Holme Lacy">Holme Lacy</a>. There, through the ministry of a Catholic priest, Richard Davis, around 1579 he became a Catholic himself. On 18 August 1582, Holford entered the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims</a>, where he trained to become a Catholic priest. He was ordained at <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a> 9 April 1583 and was sent to England on 4 May the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-SP_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Holford&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Holford, and his mentor Richard Davis, narrowly avoided capture when staying with the Bellamys of <a href="/wiki/Uxenden_Hall" title="Uxenden Hall">Uxenden Hall</a> near <a href="/wiki/Harrow-on-the-Hill" class="mw-redirect" title="Harrow-on-the-Hill">Harrow-on-the-Hill</a>, <a href="/wiki/Middlesex" title="Middlesex">Middlesex</a>. The Bellamy house was raided on 2 November 1584. Holford returned to his native Cheshire, but was arrested at Nantwich in May 1585. A letter survives, sent by Holford from prison to his brother, in which he asks for some of his clothing to be sent so he can have it sold for funds. In it, he signs his name as <i>Tho: Holforde.</i><sup id="cite_ref-SP_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In captivity, Holford was interrogated by the Anglican Bishop of Chester. The bishop left a description of Thomas as a <i>tall, black (haired), fat, strong man, the crown of his head bald, his beard marquessated (shaven except for a moustache)</i>. Holford refused to accept exile, stating instead his expectation that he would be executed at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> or Boughton. He was sent back to London for trial, but escaped by feigning insanity when his guards were suffering from a hangover.<sup id="cite_ref-SP_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Byrne_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Byrne-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1586, Holford again narrowly escaped capture at the Bellamy house when <a href="/wiki/Sir_Francis_Walsingham" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Francis Walsingham">Sir Francis Walsingham</a> raided London Catholic houses following an unsuccessful <a href="/wiki/Babington_Plot" title="Babington Plot">Catholic plot</a> to replace Elizabeth I with <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots" title="Mary, Queen of Scots">Mary, Queen of Scots</a>. Holford, using the alias <i>Acton</i>, then stayed away from London for some time but returned in 1588 to buy clothes. He was spotted after celebrating a clandestine Mass in <a href="/wiki/Holborn" title="Holborn">Holborn</a>, at the home of <a href="/wiki/Swithin_Wells" class="mw-redirect" title="Swithin Wells">Swithin Wells</a>. He was followed to the tailors, and arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-SP_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Byrne_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Byrne-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom_and_liturgical_cult">Martyrdom and liturgical cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Holford&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Martyrdom and liturgical cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Holford was held in custody at <a href="/wiki/Newgate" title="Newgate">Newgate</a>, where he was condemned on 26 August 1588. He was hanged (though not drawn and quartered) on 28 August 1588, at Clerkenwell.<sup id="cite_ref-UsherwoodUsherwood1987_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-UsherwoodUsherwood1987-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The executions which took place on this day and shortly thereafter were not all at the usual sites for executions in London, but were spread across the city to make an example of those condemned for their treasonous acts. This has been interpreted as a gesture of reprisal following the defeat of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Armada" title="Spanish Armada">Spanish Armada</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SPC_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SPC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 2004 edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, Holford is listed as one of a group of eight martyrs who died in London on 28 August 1588. Following the Roman custom of naming a principal martyr 'and companions', the principal martyr is one <a href="/wiki/William_Dean_(priest)" title="William Dean (priest)">William Dean</a>, a priest. Three other priests are named besides Holford: William Gunter, Robert Morton and James Claxton. The group is completed by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Felton_(martyr)" title="Thomas Felton (martyr)">Thomas Felton</a>, a Franciscan cleric, and the laymen Henry Webley and Hugh More.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Blessed Thomas is the eponymous patron of <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Thomas_Holford_Catholic_College" title="Blessed Thomas Holford Catholic College">Blessed Thomas Holford Catholic College</a> in Altrincham. There is a panel picture of him in St Joseph’s Church, Sale.
</p>
