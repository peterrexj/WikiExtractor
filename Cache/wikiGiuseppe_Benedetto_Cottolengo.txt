<p><b>Giuseppe Benedetto Cottolengo</b> or <b>Joseph Benedict Cottolengo</b> (3 May 1786 &#8211; 30 April 1842) was the <a href="/wiki/Entrepreneur" class="mw-redirect" title="Entrepreneur">founder</a> of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Little_House_of_Divine_Providence&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Little House of Divine Providence (page does not exist)">Little House of Divine Providence</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccola_casa_della_Divina_Provvidenza" class="extiw" title="it:Piccola casa della Divina Provvidenza">it</a>&#93;</span> and is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giuseppe_Benedetto_Cottolengo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Joseph Benedict Cottolengo was born on May 3, 1786, into a middle-class family, in <a href="/wiki/Bra_(CN)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bra (CN)">Bra</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia" title="Kingdom of Sardinia">Kingdom of Sardinia</a>. The eldest of twelve children (six of whom died in infancy), on 2 October 1802 he became a <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Franciscan tertiary</a>. In 1805 he entered the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a>. Two years later it was closed, and he was forced to continue his studies at home. Cottolengo was ordained a priest on June 8, 1811.<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Assigned as a <a href="/wiki/Curate" title="Curate">curate</a> to <a href="/wiki/Corneliano_d%27Alba" title="Corneliano d&#39;Alba">Corneliano D'Alba</a>, he completed his doctorate in theology in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a>, and in 1818 was accepted as a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Corpus_Domini" title="Basilica of Corpus Domini">Basilica of Corpus Domini</a> in Turin.  Cottolengo donated all his gifts, donations, fees for preaching, and Mass stipends to the poor.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Charitable_work">Charitable work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giuseppe_Benedetto_Cottolengo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Charitable work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the time, Turin was still recovering from <a href="/wiki/P%C3%B4_(department)" title="Pô (department)">the French occupation</a> and under the pressure of intense immigration from the countryside, which caused serious social problems and poverty. The city was rife with pauperism and beggary, illiteracy and recurrent epidemics, numerous illegitimate births and high infant mortality.<sup id="cite_ref-Tuninetti_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tuninetti-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the age of forty-one, after reading the life of <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a>, he came to understand that his true vocation was that of charity.<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At this time Cottolengo attended a family traveling from <a href="/wiki/Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Lyons">Lyons</a> to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>. The pregnant mother was ill and was not accepted at the Maggiore Hospital because she had <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>. Neither could she go to the maternity hospital because she was ill with fever, and the regulations proscribed admitting anyone who might be infectious. Cottolengo gave the mother last rites and baptized her child before it died.<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo,_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo%2C_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg/220px-Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo%2C_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="254" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo%2C_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg/330px-Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo%2C_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo%2C_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg/440px-Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo%2C_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1963" data-file-height="2268" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ritratto_di_Giuseppe_Cottolengo,_1826_-_Accademia_delle_Scienze_di_Torino_-_Ritratti_0092_B.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ritratto di Giuseppe Cottolengo, 1826</div></div></div>
<p>Affected by the scene and the cries of her surviving children, Cottolengo went and sold everything he owned, including his cloak, and rented two rooms. He began his new work on January 17, 1828, offering free accommodation to an elderly paralytic. Before long the premises turned into a hospitality center for people who were not accepted in hospitals. He came to be assisted by Doctor Lorenzo Granetti, pharmacist Paul Royal Anglesio and twelve "Ladies of Charity", under the direction of the rich widow Marianna Nasi, who visited the sick.<sup id="cite_ref-Arduino_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arduino-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> broke out in 1831, the small hospital was closed by the authorities as a precaution for fear of contagion. Cottolengo then bought a house in <a href="/wiki/Valdocco" title="Valdocco">Valdocco</a>, on the outskirts of the city, and relocated there with two nuns and a patient suffering from <a href="/wiki/Cancer" title="Cancer">cancer</a>. This was the beginning of the <i>"Little House of the Divine Providence"</i>. Due to the generosity of a number of benefactors, especially the Cavalier Ferrero, he was soon able to establish an orphanage.
</p><p>He also founded monasteries, convents, communities of priests, communities of brothers, and organised groups of <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a> <a href="/wiki/Volunteering" title="Volunteering">volunteers</a>. His legacy of charity stands today at the heart of Turin city as a sign of what it means to love and serve others in an evangelical way.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giuseppe_Benedetto_Cottolengo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Don Cottolengo contracted typhoid while assisting his patients and died in <a href="/wiki/Chieri" title="Chieri">Chieri</a>, <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> on April 30, 1842.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Giuseppe_Benedetto_Cottolengo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The institution that Cottolengo founded, the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Little_House_of_Divine_Providence&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Little House of Divine Providence (page does not exist)">Little House of Divine Providence</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccola_casa_della_Divina_Provvidenza" class="extiw" title="it:Piccola casa della Divina Provvidenza">it</a>&#93;</span>, still exists in Turin.  The Cottolengo <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious community</a> includes Fathers, Sisters, and Brothers who still work together in activities focused on communicating God's love for the poorest. They operate facilities in <a href="/wiki/Ecuador" title="Ecuador">Ecuador</a>, <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kenya" title="Kenya">Kenya</a>, <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tanzania" title="Tanzania">Tanzania</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cottolengo was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> in 1917,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  and was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> in 1934.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 30 April.  Cottolengo is invoked against infectious diseases.  He is also invoked against <a href="/wiki/Cirrhosis" title="Cirrhosis">cirrhosis</a> and other liver diseases, due to a miracle attributed to him where he appeared in a dream to a nun who then miraculously recovered from cirrhosis.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Several other places are named for him.  The parish of Saint Joseph Benedict Cottolengo is located in <a href="/wiki/Grosseto" title="Grosseto">Grosseto</a>, Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  There is a Via San Giuseppe Benedetto Cottolengo in <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>. One of the most important charity institutions in Central Brazil is the Sao Jose Bento Cotollengo Institute, in <a href="/wiki/Trindade,_Goi%C3%A1s" title="Trindade, Goiás">Trindade, Goiás</a>.
</p><p>Joseph Benedict Cottolengo was listed among the saints of charity by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in his 2005 encyclical <i><a href="/wiki/Deus_caritas_est" title="Deus caritas est">Deus caritas est</a></i>.
</p><p>St. Joseph is a character in <a href="/wiki/Bruce_Marshall_(writer)" title="Bruce Marshall (writer)">Bruce Marshall</a>'s 1960 novel <i><a href="/wiki/The_Divided_Lady" title="The Divided Lady">The Divided Lady</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  In the 2006 Italian film <i>Una cosa in mente. Giuseppe Benedetto Cottolengo</i> ("One thing in mind: Giuseppe Benedetto Cottolengo"), Cottolengo is played by <a href="/wiki/Massimo_Wertm%C3%BCller" title="Massimo Wertmüller">Massimo Wertmüller</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
