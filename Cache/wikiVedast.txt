<p><b>Vedast</b> or <b>Vedastus</b>, also known as <b>Saint Vaast</b> (in <a href="/wiki/Flemish" title="Flemish">Flemish</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norman_language" title="Norman language">Norman</a> and <a href="/wiki/Picard_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="Picard (language)">Picard</a>) or <b>Saint Waast</b> (also in Picard and <a href="/wiki/Walloon_language" title="Walloon language">Walloon</a>), <b>Saint Gaston</b> in French, and Foster in <a href="/wiki/English_language" title="English language">English</a> (died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;540</span>) was an early bishop in the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> realm. After the victory of <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Tolbiac" title="Battle of Tolbiac">Tolbiac</a> Vedast helped instruct the Frankish king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis</a> in the Christian faith of his wife, <a href="/wiki/Clotilde" title="Clotilde">Queen Clotilde</a>.
</p><p>Opinions differ as to whether <a href="/wiki/Saint_Remigius" title="Saint Remigius">Remigius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Reims">bishop of Reims</a>, entrusted the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Arras" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Arras">diocese of Arras</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Cambrai">diocese of Cambrai</a> to Vedast as is traditionally held, or if Vedast was more an itinerant bishop without a specific see. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vedast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vedast was probably born in the village of Villae in <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigord" title="Périgord">Périgord</a>. As a young man, he moved to Toul, where the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Toul" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Toul">bishop</a>, taking notice of his many virtues, ordained him to the priesthood.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Clovis, King of Franks, while returning from his <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Tolbiac" title="Battle of Tolbiac">victory over the Alemanni</a>, was on his way to Rheims and contemplating baptism to the faith of his wife, <a href="/wiki/Clotilde" title="Clotilde">Clotilde</a>, and stopped at Toul to request some priest to instruct him on the way. Vedast agreed to accompany the king.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is believed that in 499, Remigius named him the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Arras" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Arras">bishop of Arras</a>, France;<sup id="cite_ref-catholique_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholique-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> around 510, he was also given oversight over Cambrai.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, more modern studies regard Vedast "...as an itinerant bishop who had no clearly defined bishopric."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vedast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Within Christian <a href="/wiki/Sacred_tradition" title="Sacred tradition">sacred tradition</a> extraordinary healings were attributed to his intercession. One account says that while on the road to Rheims, they encountered a blind beggar at the bridge over the river <a href="/wiki/Aisne_(river)" title="Aisne (river)">Aisne</a>. The man besought Vedast's assistance. The priest prayed and blessed the beggar, at which point the man recovered his sight.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This increased Vedast's esteem in the eyes of the king and he became one of the King's advisers.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg/220px-Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="134" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg/330px-Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg/440px-Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="460" data-file-height="280" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sanctus_Vedastus_cum_bestia.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Vedast and the beast</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Jonas_of_Bobbio" title="Jonas of Bobbio">Jonas of Bobbio</a> wrote a <i>Vita Vedastis</i> to promote the cult of Vedast at the cathedral in Arras. He "presents Vedast as the model ascetic-bishop for the new Columbanian monk-bishops who occupied many of the sees of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> after the unification of the kingdom under <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a> in 613."<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Hara_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Hara-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The incident of expelling the bear from the city mirrors Columbanus expelling a bear from his hermitage at Annegray.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Hara_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Hara-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>A <i>Vita</i> of Vedast by <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> recounts a story that on one occasion, having spent the day in instructing a nobleman, his host would see him on his way with a glass of wine to sustain him, but found the cask empty. Vedast bid the servant to bring whatever he should find in the vessel. The servant then found the barrel overflowing with excellent wine.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vedast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG/220px-Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="298" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG/330px-Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG/440px-Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="2031" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wambrechies_St_Vaast.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The statue of St Vedast in the church of St Vedast in <a href="/wiki/Wambrechies" title="Wambrechies">Wambrechies</a></div></div></div>
<p>He died about 540 at <a href="/wiki/Arras" title="Arras">Arras</a>; that night the locals reportedly saw a luminous cloud ascend from his house, apparently carrying away Vedast's soul.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Vedast was buried in the old cathedral in Arras; his relics were later transferred to the new <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Vaast" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Vaast">Abbey of St. Vaast</a> founded in his honour in Arras. <sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vedast was venerated in Belgium as well as England (from the 10th century) where he was known as <i>Saint Foster</i>.  <a href="/wiki/St._Vedast_Church,_Vlamertinge" title="St. Vedast Church, Vlamertinge">St. Vedast Church, Vlamertinge</a> is named for him. The spread of his cult was aided by the presence of <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinians</a> from Arras in England in the 12th century. Three ancient <a href="/wiki/Church_Building" title="Church Building">churches</a> in England – <a href="/wiki/St_Vedast_Foster_Lane" title="St Vedast Foster Lane">St Vedast Foster Lane</a> in London, and in <a href="/wiki/Norwich" title="Norwich">Norwich</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tathwell" title="Tathwell">Tathwell</a> in Lincolnshire – were dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast is on 6 February.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vedast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He is a patron saint invoked against eye trouble.
</p>
