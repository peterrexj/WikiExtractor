<p>
<b>John Carey</b> (died 4 July 1594) was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> at <a href="/wiki/Dorchester,_Dorset" title="Dorchester, Dorset">Dorchester</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dorset" title="Dorset">Dorset</a>, England for adherence to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> faith. His feast day is 4 July. 
</p><p>John (or Terence) Carey was an Irish layman, born in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, and servant of Thomas Bosgrave and was put to death with Thomas Bosgrave, <a href="/wiki/John_Cornelius_(priest)" title="John Cornelius (priest)">John Cornelius</a> (a priest, born of Irish parents in <a href="/wiki/Bodmin" title="Bodmin">Bodmin</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>), and Patrick Salmon, another lay helper also of Dublin birth, at Dorchester in Dorset in 1594. They were all beatified in 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The persecution was part of a crackdown by the <a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_government" title="Elizabethan government">Elizabethan government</a> after the passing of the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits,_etc._Act_1584" title="Jesuits, etc. Act 1584">Jesuits, etc. Act 1584</a>, which made it an offence punishable by death to seek ordination to the priesthood overseas and return to England. See <a href="/wiki/List_of_Catholic_martyrs_of_the_English_Reformation" title="List of Catholic martyrs of the English Reformation">List of Catholic martyrs of the English Reformation</a>. Those apprehended suffered a "traitor's death": partial choking by hanging, then evisceration whilst still alive, and quartering. The authorities hoped that by staging such spectacles the arrival of young, idealistic missionary priests (most of whom were English), inspired by the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a>, would be brought to an end.
</p><p>John Cornelius was <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Arundell_family" title="Arundell family">Arundell family</a>, which service took him to <a href="/wiki/Chideock" title="Chideock">Chideock</a> Castle, where he was arrested. The two lay helpers, <b>John Carey</b> and <b>Patrick Salmon</b>, were servants at the castle.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <b>Thomas Bosgrave</b> was a relative of the Arundells.
</p><p>John Cornelius was accused of <a href="/wiki/High_treason" class="mw-redirect" title="High treason">high treason</a>, by virtue of being a Catholic priest and returning to England. The others were charged with a <a href="/wiki/Felony" title="Felony">felony</a>, for having rendered assistance to one whom they knew to be a priest. Their trial took place in the main hall of what is now Chideock House Hotel; they were condemned to death on 2 July 1594 and executed two days later. The two lay helpers refused the offer of amnesty through the abjuration of their allegiance to Catholicism and conversion to the Protestant faith. All were hanged, drawn, and quartered, at Dorchester, Oxfordshire, England. When he mounted the scaffold, Carey is reported as saying aloud "Oh, precious collar!".<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Arundell family retrieved the bodies for proper burial. The men became known as the Chideock Martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
