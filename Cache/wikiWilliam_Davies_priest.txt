					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">William Davies</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;"><a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">Martyr</a></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 1555<br /><a href="/wiki/North_Wales" title="North Wales">North Wales</a>, probably <a href="/wiki/Croes_yn_Eirias" class="mw-redirect" title="Croes yn Eirias">Croes yn Eirias</a>, <a href="/wiki/Denbighshire" title="Denbighshire">Denbighshire</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">27 July 1593 (aged 37 - 38)<br /><a href="/wiki/Beaumaris_Castle" title="Beaumaris Castle">Beaumaris Castle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Beaumaris" title="Beaumaris">Beaumaris</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglesey" title="Anglesey">Anglesey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">22 November 1987 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">27 July, 22 November (with the <a href="/wiki/Eighty-five_martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Eighty-five martyrs of England and Wales">Martyrs of England and Wales</a>)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>

<b>William Davies</b> (died 27 July 1593) was a Welsh <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1987.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There is a chapel in <a href="/wiki/Anglesey" title="Anglesey">Anglesey</a> built as a memorial to him. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Davies_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Davies was born in <a href="/wiki/North_Wales" title="North Wales">North Wales</a>, probably <a href="/wiki/Croes_yn_Eirias" class="mw-redirect" title="Croes yn Eirias">Croes yn Eirias</a>, Denbighshire, but his date of birth is not known, however one source gives the year 1555. Groes yn Eirias (meaning Torch Cross) is the old name for the area of dwellings between Llanelian and Colwyn Bay, Groes Road Colwyn Bay is a route to Llanelian Church. Eirias Park is in the same area. (It is now in the County of Conwy.) He studied at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, where he arrived on 6 April 1582 just in time to assist at the first Mass of <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Garlick" title="Nicholas Garlick">Nicholas Garlick</a>. He received <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> and <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a> on 23 September 1583, together with seventy-three English students. Ordained as a priest in April 1585, he worked as a missionary in Wales. With his patron Robert Pugh, he secretly produced the book <i>Y Drych Christianogawl</i>, said to be the first book printed in Wales. The press may have been located in a cave above the sea at the Little Orme head between Llandudno and Penrhyn Bay.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In March 1592, he was arrested at <a href="/wiki/Holyhead" title="Holyhead">Holyhead</a>, with four students whom he was sending via Ireland to the English College at <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a>. Pugh escaped arrest.
</p><p>He was imprisoned in a <a href="/wiki/Dungeon" title="Dungeon">dungeon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Beaumaris_Castle" title="Beaumaris Castle">Beaumaris Castle</a> and separated from his companions, having confessed that he was a priest. After a month he was able to join the students for an hour in the day, and even to celebrate <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>. The jailor became lax, and they might have escaped had they so willed. Catholics from all parts came to consult him, and Protestant ministers came to dispute with him.
</p><p>At the assizes he and his companions were condemned to death, on which Davies intoned the <i><a href="/wiki/Te_Deum" title="Te Deum">Te Deum</a></i>, which the others took up. The judge reprieved the condemned till the Queen's pleasure be known.
</p><p>Sent to <a href="/wiki/Ludlow" title="Ludlow">Ludlow</a>, to be examined by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_the_Marches" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of the Marches">Council of the Marches</a>, Davies encountered more Protestant ministers. They took him to church under pretext of a disputation, and then began the Protestant service. He recited the Latin Vespers in a loud voice.
</p><p>From Ludlow he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Bewdley" title="Bewdley">Bewdley</a>, where he had to share his prison with felons, and thence to other jails. He was sent back to Beaumaris, and rejoined his young companions. For some six months he lived with them the life of a religious community, dividing the time between prayer and study.
</p><p>At the summer assizes it was decided that the priest must die as a traitor, though he was offered his life if he would go but once to church. In spite of local opposition, the sentence was carried out and he was <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn,_and_quartered" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanged, drawn, and quartered">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a> at <a href="/wiki/Beaumaris_Castle" title="Beaumaris Castle">Beaumaris Castle</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Davies_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Blessed William Davies' feast is celebrated on 27 July. His <a href="/wiki/Cassock" title="Cassock">cassock</a> was preserved as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> by his companions. They, though condemned to imprisonment for life, managed in time to escape. The youngest found his way to Valladolid, where he recounted the whole story to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bishop_Yepes&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bishop Yepes (page does not exist)">Bishop Yepes</a>, who wrote it in his <i>Historia particular de la Persecucion en Inglaterra</i>. 
</p><p>In 2010, during his papal visit to the UK, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> was presented with an exact facsimile of the book <i>Y Drych Christianogawl</i>, which had been commissioned by Joseph Kelly, Editor of The Universe Catholic weekly as a gift from the people of Wales. The facsimile was produced by renowned book conservator Julian Thomas at the National Library of Wales in Aberystwyth, using one of only two surviving copies of the original book.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
