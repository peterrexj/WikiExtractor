<p><b>Symphorian</b> (<b>Symphorianus</b>, <b>Symphorien</b>), <b>Timotheus</b> (<b>Timothy</b>), and <a href="/wiki/Hippolytus_of_Rome" title="Hippolytus of Rome">Hippolytus of Rome</a> are three <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian martyrs</a> who though they were unrelated and were killed in different places and at different times, shared a common feast day in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> from at least the 1568 <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">Mysterii Paschalis</a>. While still a young man, Symphorian was either beheaded or beaten to death with clubs.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Symphorian">Symphorian</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorian_and_Timotheus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Symphorian">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a legendary <i>passio</i> of St. <a href="/wiki/Benignus_of_Dijon" title="Benignus of Dijon">Benignus of Dijon</a>, Symphorian was a young nobleman who was converted by Benignus at <a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a>. Symphorian was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>, while still a young man, during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was the son of a senator named Faustus. He studied at Autun and was brought before the provincial governor Heraclius for not worshipping the pagan goddess <a href="/wiki/Cybele" title="Cybele">Cybele</a> (Berecynthia), by failing to show due reverence when a procession during her festival was passing by. Symphorian is said to have asked for tools to destroy the statue. He was arrested and flogged and, because he was from a noble family, he was given a chance to <a href="/wiki/Recant" class="mw-redirect" title="Recant">recant</a> but refused.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His mother, the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> Augusta, encouraged him on his way to execution and was present at her son's death, which took place on 22 August 178. In the oldest redaction of manuscripts containing the saint's life we find a Gaulish sentence recorded that she allegedly yelled from the city wall: <i>Nate, Nate, Symphoriane, mentobeto to Diuo</i> which may be read as "<i>gnate, gnate, mentobe to diwo"</i><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> ("Son, Son, O Symphorian, remember your God!").<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Symphorian was beaten to death with clubs.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorian_and_Timotheus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bishop <a href="/wiki/Euphronius_of_Autun" title="Euphronius of Autun">Euphronius of Autun</a> (died 490) built a handsome church over Symphorian's grave, connected with a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Symphorian,_Autun" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Symphorian, Autun">Abbey of St. Symphorian</a>, which belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_St._Genevi%C3%A8ve" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of St. Geneviève">Congregation of Sainte-Geneviève</a> from 1656 until its suppression in 1791. Abbot <a href="/wiki/Germain_of_Paris" title="Germain of Paris">Germanus</a> later became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Paris">Bishop of Paris</a>, where he dedicated a chapel to the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Genesius_of_Clermont" class="mw-redirect" title="Genesius of Clermont">Genesius of Clermont</a> built a church dedicated to him at <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a>.
</p><p>Saint Symphorian is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Autun. His veneration spread at an early date through the empire of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>. His cult was especially popular at <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>; Saint <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> relates a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> performed by the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a St. Symphorian's Church at <a href="/wiki/Veryan" title="Veryan">Veryan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, and <a href="/wiki/St_Symphorian%27s_Church,_Durrington" title="St Symphorian&#39;s Church, Durrington">another</a> at <a href="/wiki/Durrington,_West_Sussex" title="Durrington, West Sussex">Durrington</a> in <a href="/wiki/West_Sussex" title="West Sussex">West Sussex</a>, now a suburb of the town of <a href="/wiki/Worthing" title="Worthing">Worthing</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Timotheus">Timotheus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorian_and_Timotheus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Timotheus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/Pontificate" title="Pontificate">pontificate</a> of <a href="/wiki/Melchiades" class="mw-redirect" title="Melchiades">Melchiades</a> (311–13), St. Timotheus came from <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he preached for fifteen months and lived with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Silvester_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Silvester I">Sylvester</a>, who later became pope. The <a href="/wiki/Prefect_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Prefect of Rome">prefect of the city</a>, Tarquinus Perpenna, threw him into prison, tortured, and finally beheaded him at the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ostiensis" title="Via Ostiensis">Via Ostiense</a> in 311.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  A Christian woman named Theon buried him in her garden. This is related in the legend of Sylvester. The name of Timotheus occurs in the earliest <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hippolytus">Hippolytus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Symphorian_and_Timotheus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hippolytus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The 22 August feast of Saint <a href="/wiki/Hippolytus_of_Rome" title="Hippolytus of Rome">Hippolytus of Rome</a> was a duplicate of his 13 August feast and for that reason was deleted when the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> was revised in 1969.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Earlier editions of the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> referred to the 22 August Hippolytus as Bishop of Porto. <a href="/wiki/Johann_Peter_Kirsch" title="Johann Peter Kirsch">Johann Peter Kirsch</a>, writing in the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a>,</i> sees this as "connected with the confusion regarding the Roman presbyter resulting from the Acts of the Martyrs of Porto. It has not been ascertained whether the memory of the latter was localized at Porto merely in connection with the legend in Prudentius, without further foundation, or whether a person named Hippolytus was really martyred at Porto, and afterwards confounded in legend with Hippolytus of Rome."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This opinion is shared by a Benedictine source.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
