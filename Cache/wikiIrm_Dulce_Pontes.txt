<p><b>Irmã Dulce</b>, also known as Saint Dulce of the Poor (26 May 1914 – 13 March 1992) was a <a href="/wiki/Brazilian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Brazilian people">Brazilian</a> Catholic <a href="/wiki/Nun#Religious_Sister" title="Nun">Franciscan Sister</a> who was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Obras_Sociais_Irm%C3%A3_Dulce" title="Obras Sociais Irmã Dulce">Obras Sociais Irmã Dulce</a> (Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce).
</p><p>In 1949, she started caring for the poorest of the poor in her <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>'s chicken yard in <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia" title="Salvador, Bahia">Salvador, Bahia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Today, more than 3,000 people arrive every day at this same site (where the Santo Antônio Hospital now stands) to receive free medical treatment. She also established CESA, a school for the poor in <a href="/wiki/Sim%C3%B5es_Filho" title="Simões Filho">Simões Filho</a>, one of the most impoverished cities in the state of <a href="/wiki/Bahia" title="Bahia">Bahia</a>.
</p><p>At the time of her death in 1992, Pontes had been nominated for the <a href="/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize" title="Nobel Peace Prize">Nobel Peace Prize</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> she had received two personal audiences with <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, and she had, almost single-handedly, created one of the largest and most respected philanthropic organizations in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>. She was named the most admired woman in the history of Brazil by <i><a href="/wiki/O_Estado_de_S._Paulo" title="O Estado de S. Paulo">O Estado de S. Paulo</a></i> newspaper<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and the most influential religious person in Brazil during the 20th century, by <i><a href="/wiki/ISTO%C3%89" class="mw-redirect" title="ISTOÉ">ISTOÉ</a></i> magazine.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2011, she was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> with papal approval by <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Geraldo_Majella_Agnelo" title="Geraldo Majella Agnelo">Geraldo Majella Agnelo</a>, the penultimate step toward <a href="/wiki/Sainthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Sainthood">sainthood</a>. In May 2019, Pope Francis, during an audience given to the Congregation for the Causes of Saints Prefect <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Angelo_Becciu" title="Giovanni Angelo Becciu">Giovanni Angelo Becciu</a>, recognized the second miracle needed to officially canonize her as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-becciufrancis_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-becciufrancis-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsannppre_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsannppre-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She was formally canonized on 13 October 2019,<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanCanonized_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanCanonized-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> making her the first Brazilian female saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Giuffrida_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Giuffrida-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia" title="Salvador, Bahia">Salvador, Bahia</a>, the second daughter of Augusto Lopes Pontes and Dulce Maria de Souza, as <b>Maria Rita de Souza Pontes</b>, she entered <a href="/wiki/Religious_life" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious life">religious life</a> when she was 18 years old. When she was thirteen years old, her aunt had taken her on a trip to the poor area of the city. The sight of the misery and poverty she encountered there made a deep impression on the young girl, who came from an <a href="/wiki/Upper_middle-class" class="mw-redirect" title="Upper middle-class">upper middle-class</a> background.
</p><p>She began to care for the <a href="/wiki/Homeless" class="mw-redirect" title="Homeless">homeless</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beggars" class="mw-redirect" title="Beggars">beggars</a> in her neighborhood, giving them free haircuts and treating their wounds. By that time, she had already shown interest in following religious life. Her father, however, did not like the idea and insisted that she become a teacher<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2007)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Graduation_and_religious_life">Graduation and religious life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Graduation and religious life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She graduated from high school at the age of 18. She, then, asked her father to allow her to follow her religious <a href="/wiki/Calling_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calling (religious)">calling</a>. He agreed and she joined the Congregation of the <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_of_the_Mother_of_God" title="Missionary Sisters of the Immaculate Conception of the Mother of God">Missionary Sisters of the Immaculate Conception of the Mother of God</a>, in Our Lady of Carmel Convent, in <a href="/wiki/Sergipe" title="Sergipe">Sergipe</a>. A year later, she received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of that Congregation and was given the name <b>Dulce</b>, in memory of her mother (who had died when she was 6 years old).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Social_works">Social works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Social works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the same year, she founded the "São Francisco's Worker's Union", the first Christian worker's movement in Bahia. A year later, she started welfare work in the poor communities of Alagados and Itapagipe. It was then that they started calling her the "Angel of Alagados". In 1937, she transformed the Worker's Union into the Worker's Center of Bahia.
</p><p>Determined to house sick people who came to her for help, in 1939 Pontes started to shelter them in abandoned houses in <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia" title="Salvador, Bahia">Salvador</a>'s 'Ilha dos Ratos' (rats' island) district. Then she would go in search of food, medicine and <a href="/wiki/Medical_care" class="mw-redirect" title="Medical care">medical care</a>. Later, when she and her patients were evicted from the neighborhood, she started housing them in an old fish market, but City Hall denied her the use of the space and told her to leave.
</p><p>Facing a big problem and already taking care of over 70 people, she turned to the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mother_superior" class="extiw" title="wikt:mother superior">Mother Superior</a> of her convent and asked her permission to use the convent's chicken yard as an improvised hostel. The Superior reluctantly agreed, so long as Pontes could take care of the chickens (which she did, by feeding them to her patients). That improvised hostel gave rise to the Hospital Santo Antonio, the center of a medical, social and educational complex which continues to open doors for the poor in Bahia and throughout Brazil.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_foundation_of_OSID">The foundation of OSID</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: The foundation of OSID">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There, in 1960, the Santo Antônio Hospital, consisting of 150 beds, was inaugurated. On 26 May 1959 the <a href="/wiki/Charitable_Works_Foundation_of_Sister_Dulce" class="mw-redirect" title="Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce">Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce</a> was born, a result of the determination of a <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sister (Catholic)">religious sister</a> who was tireless in her attendance to the sick and to the <a href="/wiki/Beggars" class="mw-redirect" title="Beggars">beggars</a> who lived on <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia" title="Salvador, Bahia">Salvador</a>'s streets.
</p><p>Pontes's work impressed the <a href="/wiki/President_of_Brazil" title="President of Brazil">President of Brazil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Sarney" title="José Sarney">José Sarney</a>, who in 1988 nominated her for the <a href="/wiki/Nobel_Peace_Prize" title="Nobel Peace Prize">Nobel Peace Prize</a>, with support of <a href="/wiki/Queen_Silvia_of_Sweden" title="Queen Silvia of Sweden">Queen Silvia of Sweden</a>.
</p><p>The organization she founded, known by its Portuguese acronym as OSID (<a href="/wiki/Obras_Sociais_Irm%C3%A3_Dulce" title="Obras Sociais Irmã Dulce">Obras Sociais Irmã Dulce</a>), is one of the most well-known and respected philanthropic organizations in Brazil.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="OSID">OSID</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: OSID">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Charitable_Works_Foundation_of_Sister_Dulce" class="mw-redirect" title="Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce">Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce</a></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Charitable_Works_Foundation_of_Sister_Dulce" class="mw-redirect" title="Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce">Charitable Works Foundation of Sister Dulce</a> is a private charity chartered under Brazilian law. It is accredited at federal, state and municipal levels and registered by the National Welfare Council and the Federal Ministry of Education.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Obras_Sociais_Irm%C3%A3_Dulce" title="Obras Sociais Irmã Dulce">OSID</a> provides health, welfare and education services, with a strong commitment to medical education and research.
</p><p>The Santo Antônio Hospital is the largest completely free hospital in Brazil, according to the Federal Ministry of Health. It has over 1,000 beds and receives more than 3,000 patients every day.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Obras_Sociais_Irm%C3%A3_Dulce" title="Obras Sociais Irmã Dulce">OSID</a> also established CESA (Santo Antônio Educational Center), a school for the poor in Simões Filho, one of the most impoverished cities in the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Area" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan Area">Metropolitan Area</a> of <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia" title="Salvador, Bahia">Salvador</a> and in the State of <a href="/wiki/Bahia" title="Bahia">Bahia</a>. There, OSID provides free educational programs for approximately 800 children and young people ranging in age from 6 to 19 years old.
</p><p>CESA offers basic education in accordance with the guidelines of the <a href="/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_(Brazil)" title="Ministry of Education (Brazil)">Brazilian Federal Ministry of Education</a>. In addition, CESA has developed a complementary program of educational, physical and professional development activities to help students learn more effectively and enrich their lives.
</p><p>It also operates a commercial bakery and an orthopedic production center, staffed by professional workers, which produce and sell their products in many regions of Brazil and internationally, following the idea of self-sustainability which is part of the work concept created by Pontes.
</p>
<h2><span id="Health_problems.2C_death_and_burial"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Health_problems,_death_and_burial">Health problems, death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Health problems, death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the last 30 years of her life, Pontes's lungs were highly impaired and she had only 30% breathing capacity. In 1990, her respiratory problems began to worsen and she was hospitalized. It was in her sick bed that she received the visit of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> (whom she had met, for the first time, in 1980).
</p><p>After being hospitalized for 16 months, Pontes died on 13 March 1992, at the age of 77, in Santo Antônio's Convent, and she was buried at the Basilica of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception. On 26 May 2000, her body was transferred to the Chapel of Santo Antônio Convent.
</p><p>On 9 June 2010, Pontes was finally buried at the Imaculada Conceição da Madre de Deus church, in <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Bahia" title="Salvador, Bahia">Salvador, Bahia</a>. It was discovered that her body was naturally <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a> and even her clothes were still preserved 18 years after her death.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Irm%C3%A3_Dulce&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pontes received the title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> with the beginning of the cause for her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> in January 2000 under the Archbishop of Salvador da Bahia and Primate of Brazil, <a href="/wiki/Geraldo_Majella_Agnelo" title="Geraldo Majella Agnelo">Geraldo Majella Agnelo</a>. who examined her <a href="/wiki/Virtues" class="mw-redirect" title="Virtues">virtues</a>, fame of <a href="/wiki/Sainthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Sainthood">sainthood</a> and the tireless determination of a life dedicated to the needy.
</p><p>In June 2001 the process continued in the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_the_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of the Saints">Congregation for the Causes of the Saints</a>. In June 2003 the Congregation received the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">positio</a>. At that same time, the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a> formally recognized a miracle performed by the intercession of Pontes.
</p><p>On 10 May 2007, in a meeting with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> during his visit to Brazil, the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Governors of São Paulo">Governor</a> of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a> and former <a href="/wiki/President_of_Brazil" title="President of Brazil">Presidential</a> candidate <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Serra" title="José Serra">José Serra</a> said he would send a letter to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a> in favor of Pontes's beatification.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 20 January 2009, the Congregation for the Causes of Saints unanimously recommended to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> that he proclaim Pontes's heroic virtue. Pope Benedict XVI approved and on 3 April 2009 granted her the title <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Pontes's body was exhumed and examined on 9 July 2010 as part of the beatification process, and was found to be still incorrupt.
</p><p>On 27 October 2010, the Archbishop of Salvador announced that the Congregation for the Causes of Saints had recognized a miracle attributed to her intersession, paving the way for her to be beatified. The pope officially approved on 10 December 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Pontes was beatified in a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> on 22 May 2011, in Salvador, Bahia. The Mass was presided over by the Archbishop of Salvador and the Primate of Brazil, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Geraldo_Majella_Agnelo" title="Geraldo Majella Agnelo">Geraldo Majella Agnelo</a>, who conducted the beatification by mandate of Pope Benedict XVI; the service was attended by about 70,000 people. President <a href="/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff" title="Dilma Rousseff">Dilma Rousseff</a> and Governor <a href="/wiki/Jaques_Wagner" title="Jaques Wagner">Jaques Wagner</a> also attended.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 13 May 2019, Congregation of Saints Prefect Angelo Becciu approved a miracle needed to make her a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-becciufrancis_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-becciufrancis-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsannppre_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsannppre-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The decree recognizing the second miracle was then signed by Pope Francis, thus ensured that Pontes would be canonized.<sup id="cite_ref-becciufrancis_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-becciufrancis-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsannppre_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsannppre-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It was announced on 1 July 2019 that Pontes would be canonized with four others on 13 October 2019,<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanCanonized_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanCanonized-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dulceothers_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dulceothers-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> making her the first Brazilian female saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Giuffrida_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Giuffrida-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
