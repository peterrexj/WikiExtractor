<p>Saint <b>Conleth</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;k&#39; in &#39;kind&#39;">k</span><span title="/ɒ/: &#39;o&#39; in &#39;body&#39;">ɒ</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/θ/: &#39;th&#39; in &#39;thigh&#39;">θ</span></span>/</a></span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Old_Irish_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Irish language">Old Irish</a>: <i lang="sga"><b>Conláed</b></i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="sga-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA" title="Help:IPA">[ˈkonlaið]</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Modern Irish</a>: <i>Naomh Connlaodh</i>; also <i>Conlaeth; Conlaid; Conlaith; Conlath; Conlian, Hugh the Wise</i>)<sup id="cite_ref-heritage_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heritage-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and metalworker, also said to have been a copyist and skilled illuminator of manuscripts. He is believed to have come from the <a href="/wiki/Wicklow" title="Wicklow">Wicklow</a> area.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conleth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Conleth_window,_Doolin.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/St_Conleth_window%2C_Doolin.jpg/220px-St_Conleth_window%2C_Doolin.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="439" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/St_Conleth_window%2C_Doolin.jpg/330px-St_Conleth_window%2C_Doolin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/St_Conleth_window%2C_Doolin.jpg/440px-St_Conleth_window%2C_Doolin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2312" data-file-height="4612" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Conleth_window,_Doolin.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Conleth in <a href="/wiki/Stained_glass" title="Stained glass">stained glass</a>, <a href="/wiki/Doolin" title="Doolin">Doolin</a></div></div></div>
<p>While living in seclusion at <a href="/wiki/Connell_(barony)" title="Connell (barony)">Old Connell</a> on the <a href="/wiki/River_Liffey" title="River Liffey">River Liffey</a> in what is now <a href="/wiki/Newbridge,_County_Kildare" title="Newbridge, County Kildare">Newbridge</a> Conleth was persuaded by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Brigid" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Brigid">Saint Brigid</a> to make sacred vessels for her convent. Conleth, <a href="/wiki/Tassach" class="mw-redirect" title="Tassach">Tassach</a> of Elphin (Saint Patrick's craftsman), and Daigh (craftsman of Kieran of Saigher) were acclaimed the "three chief artisans of Ireland" during their period. Conleth was head of the Kildare school of metal-work and penmanship. According to Brigid's biographer, <a href="/wiki/Cogitosus" title="Cogitosus">Cogitosus</a>, a community of monks grew up which, under his guidance, excelled in the making of beautiful chalices and other metal objects needed in the church, and in the writing and ornamentation of missals, gospels, and psalters. A product of Saint Conleth's metalwork for which he is noted is the <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> that he fashioned for Saint Finbarr of Termonbarry.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Kildare_and_Leighlin" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Kildare and Leighlin">Diocese of Kildare</a> appears to have been founded around 490, by Conleth who, with the assistance of St. Bridget, then presiding over the monastery, erected the cathedral and became first bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Cogitosus" title="Cogitosus">Cogitosus</a>, in his <i>Life of Brigid</i>, calls him "bishop and abbot of the monks of Kildare".<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Conleth died when he was attacked by wolves in the forests of <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a> on pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 4 May 519 and was buried nearby. In 799 his relics were transported and laid beside Brigid's in the great cathedral in <a href="/wiki/Kildare" title="Kildare">Kildare</a>. His relics were finally laid to rest in Connell in 835 to protect the inhabitants from invading Danes.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conleth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Conleth is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the Roman Catholic St Conleth's Parish, which includes Newbridge and the surrounding areas. Old Connell – the site of Conleth's original cell, which is now a graveyard – is within the parish limits.
</p><p>Conleth's feast day is 4 May. Every year on the Sunday after St Conleth's Day a pilgrimage takes place from the parish church in Newbridge to Old Connell, about two miles outside the town.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conleth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Conleth's College is located in Dublin.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His tale and community are briefly described in <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a>'s "A Book of Cornwall"<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> (now in the public domain,) in which his name is spelled "Conlaeth."
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Conleth%27s_Park" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Conleth&#39;s Park">St. Conleth's Park</a> is the name of <a href="/wiki/Kildare_GAA" title="Kildare GAA">Kildare GAA</a>'s home stadium, located in <a href="/wiki/Newbridge,_County_Kildare" title="Newbridge, County Kildare">Newbridge, County Kildare</a>.
</p>
