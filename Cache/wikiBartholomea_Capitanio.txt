<p><b>Bartolomea Capitanio</b> (13 January 1807 – 26 July 1833) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the co-foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Saints_Bartolomea_Capitanio_and_Vincenza_Gerosa_(SCCG)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sisters of Charity of Saints Bartolomea Capitanio and Vincenza Gerosa (SCCG)">Sisters of Charity of Lovere</a> that she established with <a href="/wiki/Vincenza_Gerosa" title="Vincenza Gerosa">Vincenza Gerosa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Capitanio's rather short life was dedicated to the educational needs of children and the poor and she served as a teacher for most of her life while using her order to achieve this aim.<sup id="cite_ref-AG_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> beatified her in 1926 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> canonized her - alongside her old friend Gerosa - in 1950.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolomea_Capitanio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bartolomea Capitanio was born in <a href="/wiki/Bergamo" title="Bergamo">Bergamo</a> in 1807 as the eldest of seven children to the merchant Modesto Capitanio and his second wife Caterina Casnossi; she had two brothers and four sisters. All her siblings died as infants except for Camilla. Her father ran a business dealing in grain and also a small greengrocers. Her mother educated her children with great care and also a deep faith. Her father soon became an <a href="/wiki/Alcoholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Alcoholic">alcoholic</a> and became aggressive at home.<sup id="cite_ref-AG_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Capitanio was educated at the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lovere" title="Lovere">Lovere</a> after her mother sent her there in 1818; she remained there until as a teacher 1825 when she returned home.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The girl would have entered the order of those <a href="/wiki/Nuns" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuns">nuns</a> but her parents would not consent to her request but approached her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a>, the priest Angelo Bosio, for guidance. Reading the life of <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a> enlightened and inspired her and she attempted to emulate his virtues.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After the conclusion of her studies she opened a private school for girls where she encouraged the devotion of the "Six Sundays of Saint Aloysius" that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> had approved back in 1739.
</p><p>The Austrian government - which controlled the northern Italian region at the time - issued her a teacher's diploma upon her passing the requisite examination in 1822; she began teaching first grade schoolgirls at her old school though left on 18 July 1824 to go back home and teach at the local school.<sup id="cite_ref-AG_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1824 she first became acquainted with <a href="/wiki/Vincenza_Gerosa" title="Vincenza Gerosa">Vincenza Gerosa</a> who also happened to hail from Lovere. The two became close friends and together they embarked on a mission to start a hospital to care for those who could not afford medical care. They co-founded their own religious order as a means to teach children and nurse the sick. The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">Bishop of Brescia</a> allowed for Bosio and Father Rusticiano Barboglio to purchase a house for the two women to found their order at.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Presentation" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Presentation">Feast of the Presentation</a> - on 21 February 1832 - the pair dedicated themselves to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> (in Bosio and Barboglio's presence at San Giorgio in Casa Gaia) and began to live a communal life in accordance with the order the two founded.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The formal founding was on 21 November 1832 after the women began their work for the founding the previous 26 April 1831.
</p><p>Bartolomea Capitanio died from <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> in 1833 in Lovere.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="School">School</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolomea_Capitanio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: School">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>Bartolomea Capitanio is also the name of a school in the <a href="/wiki/Amap%C3%A1" title="Amapá">Amapá</a> state in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolomea_Capitanio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process started under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> on 8 March 1866 and she became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> while the confirmation of her <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> to name her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 6 January 1902; <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> beatified her on 30 May 1926 while <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> later canonized her as a saint - alongside Vincenza Gerosa - on 18 May 1950.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
