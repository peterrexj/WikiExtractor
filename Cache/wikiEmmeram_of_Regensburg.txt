<p><b>Saint Emmeram of Regensburg</b> (also <i>Emeram(m)us</i>, <i>Emmeran</i>, <i>Emmerano</i>, <i>Emeran</i>, <i>Heimrammi</i>, <i>Haimeran</i>, or <i>Heimeran</i>) was a Christian <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> born in <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a>. Having heard of idolatry in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, Emmeram travelled to Ratisbon (Regensburg) some time after the year 649 to the court of <a href="/wiki/Theodo_I_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodo I of Bavaria">Theodo I</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bavaria">Duke of Bavaria</a>. He supposedly travelled up the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a>, crossed through the <a href="/wiki/Black_Forest" title="Black Forest">Black Forest</a> and then followed the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> to Regensburg. Theodo welcomed Emmeram to his court, where he laboured for three years carrying out <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> work. During this time, he gained a reputation as a pious man. He died circa 652 and is buried in <a href="/wiki/St._Emmeram%27s_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Emmeram&#39;s Abbey">St. Emmeram's</a> in <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a>, Germany. His feast day in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Roman_Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Roman Catholic)">Calendar of saints</a> is September 22.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Emmeram_of_Regensburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Arbeo_of_Freising" title="Arbeo of Freising">Arbeo of Freising</a> wrote a biography of Emmeram in 750, the <i>Vita Sancti Emmerami</i>, about 100 years after the saint's death. The literature tells the story of Emmeram, born to a noble family in <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a>. According to <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> and others, he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Poitiers">Bishop of Poitiers</a>,although this cannot be verified.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There is speculation that he held the office briefly between the death of Dido and the accession of <a href="/wiki/Ansoaldus" class="mw-redirect" title="Ansoaldus">Ansoaldus</a>.
</p><p>Having heard of idolatry in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, he decided to travel to Ratisbon (Regensburg) some time after the year 649 to the court of <a href="/wiki/Agilofing" class="mw-redirect" title="Agilofing">Agilofing</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theodo_I_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodo I of Bavaria">Theodo I</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bavaria">Duke of Bavaria</a>. He supposedly travelled up the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a>, crossed through the <a href="/wiki/Black_Forest" title="Black Forest">Black Forest</a> and then followed the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> to Regensburg. Theodo welcomed Emmeram to his court, where he laboured for three years carrying out <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> work. During this time, he gained a reputation as a pious man. He founded the monastery that later bore his name.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He then went on a pilgrimage to Rome, but after a five days' journey, at a place now called Kleinhelfendorf, south of Munich, he was set upon by envoys of the Duke of Bavaria who tortured him cruelly. He died shortly afterwards at Ascheim, about fifteen miles distant. The cause of this attack and the circumstances attending his death are not known.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Emmeram_of_Regensburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As the story goes, Uta (or Ota), the daughter of the duke, confided to Emmeram that she was expecting a child out of wedlock. According to Arbeo, the father was one Sigipaldus from her father's own court. Moved with compassion, Emmeram advised her to name himself, whom everyone respected, as the father hoping to mitigate some of her shame.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Shortly thereafter, the legend goes, Emmeram abruptly went on a pilgrimage to Rome. At this point, Uta named Emmeram as the father.
</p><p>When Duke Theodo and his son <a href="/wiki/Lantpert" class="mw-redirect" title="Lantpert">Lantpert</a> learned of Uta's pregnancy, Lantpert went after the bishop. Lantpert caught up with Emmeram in Helfendorf (now part of the Munich suburb of <a href="/wiki/Aying" title="Aying">Aying</a>) on the old Roman road between <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augsburg" title="Augsburg">Augsburg</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Julia_Augusta" title="Via Julia Augusta">Via Julia Augusta</a> and greeted him as "bishop and brother-in-law". According to popular tradition, wanting to protect the real culprit, Emmeram did not defend himself, and received numerous wounds.<sup id="cite_ref-van_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Lantpert and his followers tied Emmeram to a ladder and proceeded to torture him; he was then beheaded.
</p><p>His companions, Vitalis and Wolflete, found him still alive, lying in his own blood, and tried to bring him quickly back to <a href="/wiki/Munich_(district)#Towns_and_municipalities" title="Munich (district)">Aschheim</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-van_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> where a walled church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Apostle Peter</a> stood.
</p><p>The improbability of the tale, the details of the saint's martyrdom, which are certainly untrue, and the fantastic account of the prodigies attending his death show that the writer, infected by the pious mania of his time, simply added to the facts imaginary details supposed to redound to the glory of the martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Emmeram_of_Regensburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Emmeram3.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Emmeram3.jpg/220px-Emmeram3.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="291" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/Emmeram3.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="285" data-file-height="377" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Emmeram3.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Martyrdom of Saint Emmeram</i> (<a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>), from the Cathedral Treasury and Diocese Museum, <a href="/wiki/Eichst%C3%A4tt" title="Eichstätt">Eichstätt</a></div></div></div>
<p>A text printed in Munich in 1743, <i>Officium oder Tageszeiten des wunderthätigen Bayerischen Apostels und Blutzeugen Christi St. Emmerami, zu täglichen und andächtigen Gebrauch in allen Anliegen und Widerwärtigkeiten etc.</i>, states that the cart was accompanied by
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>men and women of two hundred persons with great <a href="/wiki/Sympathy" title="Sympathy">sympathy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prayer" title="Prayer">prayer</a>. A half hour before reaching Aschheim, the saint called for a halt, as within the hour his reward of heaven was before him. Then it happened that they lifted him down from the cart and laid him upon a beautiful <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/sward" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:sward">sward</a>, where he gave up his ghost at once. ...The place where this happened remained fresh and green for all time until finally the <a href="/wiki/Alms" title="Alms">alms</a> of travelers (because <a href="/wiki/Crossroads_(culture)" class="mw-redirect" title="Crossroads (culture)">all four roads come together</a> there) and other good-hearted <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> had a church built, where even today many wonders still occur!</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="/wiki/Arbeo_of_Freising" title="Arbeo of Freising">Arbeo of Freising</a> depicted the place of his death as a "lovely, ever spring-green place, upon which a spring appeared and the local people later built a little church."
</p><p>When the misunderstanding of Emmeram's relationship to Uta was revealed, he was entombed in Aschheim, whereupon legend states that it rained for forty days. Emmeram was exhumed and put upon a raft in the <a href="/wiki/Isar" title="Isar">Isar</a>. When the raft reached the Danube, it miraculously floated upstream to Regensburg, where Emmeram was interred in the church of <a href="/wiki/St._George" class="mw-redirect" title="St. George">St. George</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-van_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>(A somewhat similar tale is told of <a href="/wiki/Lubentius" title="Lubentius">Lubentius</a> of Dietkirchen.)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His remains were later moved to a church dedicated to the martyr. This church burned down in 1642. Emmeram's bones were found under the altar in 1645 and moved to <a href="/wiki/St._Emmeram%27s_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Emmeram&#39;s Abbey">St. Emmeram's Abbey</a>. The church, now a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica minor</a>, houses his <a href="/wiki/Femur" title="Femur">leg bones</a> in a <a href="/wiki/Silver" title="Silver">silver</a> <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in the eastern portion under the altar.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the spot Saint Emmeram died in the year 652, a small chapel was erected in the year 1842. The church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lawrence" title="Saint Lawrence">St. Lorenz</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bogenhausen" title="Bogenhausen">Oberföhring</a> has a side altar dedicated to St. Emmeram. In the church of Saints Peter and <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul</a> in Aschheim, a <a href="/wiki/Commemorative_plaque" title="Commemorative plaque">plaque</a> memorializes the first grave of Emmeram with an inscription.
</p><p>The day of his <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> is also his feast day, 22 September.
</p>
