<p><b>Jan Beyzym</b>, SJ (15 May 1850 – 2 October 1912) was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" title="Polish people">Polish</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> priest and a professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-JC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He served as an educator in Jesuit boarding schools for a while after his <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> though later left <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> to work alongside lepers in <a href="/wiki/Madagascar" title="Madagascar">Madagascar</a> where he remained until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification cause started in 1985 and in 1992 he was titled as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>, upon the confirmation of his life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified him while in Poland on 18 August 2002.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Beyzym&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jan Beyzym was born in Stari Beizymy, <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>)  on 15 May 1850 as the eldest of five children to Jan Beyzym and Olga. His father served as a freedom fighter and in 1863 was sentenced to death in absentia for his activities.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He moved alongside his mother and siblings to <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a> and studied there from 1864 until 1871.
</p><p>He completed his education in Kiev before deciding to pursue the religious life; he had thought of being a diocesan <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> but his late father had moved him to learn about the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> and pursue a path with them - he decided to join them after a long internal struggle. Beyzym joined the Jesuits for his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> period on 10 December 1872 at <a href="/wiki/Stara_Wie%C5%9B,_Podkarpackie_Voivodeship" title="Stara Wieś, Podkarpackie Voivodeship">Stara Wies</a> and this concluded in 1874.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During his novitiate there was a <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> epidemic and he received the permission of his superior to go out into the streets to tend to the ailing victims.<sup id="cite_ref-JC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Beyzym received his <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> as a priest on 26 July 1881 in Kraków from Bishop <a href="/wiki/Albin_Dunajewski" title="Albin Dunajewski">Albin Dunajewski</a>.
</p><p>He served as a teacher after his ordination until 1898 at Jesuit boarding schools in both <a href="/wiki/Tarnopol" class="mw-redirect" title="Tarnopol">Tarnopol</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jesuit_School_in_Chyr%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit School in Chyrów">Chyrów</a> where he taught the French and Russian languages.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His students knew him for his sense of humor. In 1898 he left his native land to join the Jesuit missions to lepers near Tananariwa in <a href="/wiki/Madagascar" title="Madagascar">Madagascar</a> with the permission of his superiors.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Father Beyzym left Poland on 17 October 1898 and arrived on the following 30 December at Red Island before being posted to Ambahivoraka near <a href="/wiki/Antananarivo" title="Antananarivo">Antananarivo</a>. He fainted several times while tending to the lepers due to the horrible smells. In October 1902 he began to see the construction of a leper hospital at Marana and it was finished and inaugurated on 16 August 1911.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He became a noted figure for his activism and collaboration with the lepers and became known for his intense devotion to both the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">Mother of God</a> while being known also as a lover of all nature.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Beyzym died on 2 October 1912; his health had declined and he suffered both <a href="/wiki/Arteriosclerosis" title="Arteriosclerosis">arteriosclerosis</a> and sores which confined him to bed. His remains were exhumed and relocated back to his native Poland on 8 December 1993 at a Jesuit church.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Beyzym&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:O._Jan_Beyzym.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/O._Jan_Beyzym.jpeg/190px-O._Jan_Beyzym.jpeg" decoding="async" width="190" height="214" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/O._Jan_Beyzym.jpeg 1.5x" data-file-width="210" data-file-height="236" /></a><figcaption>The Polish priest around 1904 while in Madagascar.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The beatification process opened in Kraków from 1984 until 1986 while a second diocesan process opened and closed in <a href="/wiki/Fianarantsoa" title="Fianarantsoa">Fianarantsoa</a> in 1987; the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> later validated the processes on 27 October 1989. The official start to the process came on 27 September 1985 after the C.C.S. issued the official "<a href="/wiki/Nihil_obstat" title="Nihil obstat">nihil obstat</a>" to the cause and titled her as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The C.C.S. later received the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> in 1990. Theologians approved this on 28 April 1992 as did the C.C.S. on 3 November 1992; <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed his life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and titled the late priest as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 21 December 1992.
</p><p>John Paul II decreed that a healing credited to the late priest was a miracle on 5 July 2002 and beatified Beyzym two months later on 18 August 2002 while on an apostolic visit to Poland.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for this cause is the Jesuit priest Anton Witwer.
</p>
