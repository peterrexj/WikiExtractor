<p><b>Theodore the Martyr</b> refers to the two saints <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Amasea" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodore of Amasea">Theodore of Amasea</a> (Theodore the Recruit) and <a href="/wiki/Theodore_Stratelates" title="Theodore Stratelates">Theodore Stratelates</a> (Theodore the General), two important military saints of the Byzantine period.
The two saints are likely identical in origin, the veneration of Theodore of Amasea is ascertained for the late 4th century, while the tradition of Theodore Stratelates develops from that of Theodore of Amasea by the 9th century.
There is a Coptic Life of Theodore the General, which places his martyrdom in Egypt. In Coptic tradition, the saint is also known as Saint Theodore of Shwtp.
</p><p>There are other martyrs called Theodore, see <a href="/wiki/Theodore_and_Pausilippus" title="Theodore and Pausilippus">Theodore and Pausilippus</a> (2nd c.), <a href="/wiki/Theodore,_Philippa_and_companions" title="Theodore, Philippa and companions">Theodore, Philippa and companions</a> (3rd c.), <a href="/wiki/Teodor_Komogovinski" title="Teodor Komogovinski">Teodor Komogovinski</a> (1788).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Accounts">Accounts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_the_Martyr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Accounts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:StratilatesandTyron.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/StratilatesandTyron.jpg/220px-StratilatesandTyron.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="183" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/StratilatesandTyron.jpg/330px-StratilatesandTyron.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/StratilatesandTyron.jpg/440px-StratilatesandTyron.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2410" data-file-height="2008" /></a><figcaption>Saints Theodore Tyron and Theodore Stratelates in a fresco from Rila Monastery, Bulgaria</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to the <i>Book of the Saints of the Ethiopian Church</i>, Saint Theodore the Martyr was a captain of the soldiers of the city of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Setb&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Setb (page does not exist)">Setb</a>, in the province of <a href="/wiki/Asyut" title="Asyut">Asyut</a> in Upper Egypt. A church was dedicated to him, and great signs and wonders took place through his body.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBook_of_the_Saints_of_the_Ethiopian_Church210_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBook_of_the_Saints_of_the_Ethiopian_Church210-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
<a href="/wiki/Abu_al-Makarim" title="Abu al-Makarim">Abu al-Makarim</a>, writing in 1348, says there was a church named after him in <a href="/wiki/Babylon,_Egypt" class="mw-redirect" title="Babylon, Egypt">Babylon, Egypt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEArmenian2001328_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmenian2001328-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa" title="Gregory of Nyssa">Gregory of Nyssa</a> (c. 335 – c. 395) gave an <i>Oration on Theodore the Martyr</i> in Amasia in 383, in the church where the saint's remains were said to be located.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBitton-Ashkelony200535_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBitton-Ashkelony200535-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
This Theodore was a Roman soldier martyred around 303 in the time of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> (c. 250 – 310).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBorromeo2010235_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorromeo2010235-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
He was among a surge of soldiers who were martyred in the 4th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBitton-Ashkelony200535_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBitton-Ashkelony200535-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
He is described as "a poor soldier, newly conscripted."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGreer2008110_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreer2008110-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
Gregory said, "This, as we believe, is he who during the past year calmed the stormy fury of the barbarians, and stopped the terrible war of the savage <a href="/wiki/Scythians" title="Scythians">Scythians</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESmithWace1887_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESmithWace1887-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
He called on Theodore for assistance, saying "gather together the troops of thy brother martyrs, and thou with them beseech God to stay the invasion of the barbarians."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFreeman1839306_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFreeman1839306-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
Gregory wrote, "The hues of the ornamentation in the church are veritably like a book that speaks, for painting even if silent knows how to speak from the wall."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBiale2012200_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBiale2012200-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
This speech is often used in defense of the use of religious images.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBorromeo2010235_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorromeo2010235-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Emperor <a href="/wiki/John_I_Tzimiskes" title="John I Tzimiskes">John I Tzimiskes</a> fought a successful battle against the <a href="/wiki/Russians" title="Russians">Russians</a> in 970 in which the whole Greek army saw a champion on a white horse fighting before the first ranks. He disappeared after the battle was won.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEUniversal_History178098_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEUniversal_History178098-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
The Russian prince <a href="/wiki/Sviatoslav_I_of_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Sviatoslav I of Kiev">Sviatoslav I of Kiev</a> was forced to abandon <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a> and retreat to Russia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKelly185425_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKelly185425-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
The victory occurred on the anniversary of Saint Theodore the Martyr, and the army thought that the knight on the white horse was the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEUniversal_History178098_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEUniversal_History178098-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
The emperor repaired the church of Theodore in <a href="/wiki/Euchaneia" title="Euchaneia">Euchaneia</a>, and changed the name of that city to Theodoropolis.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEUniversal_History178098_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEUniversal_History178098-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Theodore was the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchins</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMant184477_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMant184477-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> 
An observer in Rome in 1844 of a Capuchin's panegyric of Saint Theodore at the church dedicated to the martyr, on the saint's day, recorded as a sample, 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>But he hath left us the memory of his battles as a lesson, assembling the faithful in crowds, instructing the Church, driving away evil spirits, bringing down good angels among us, procuring all suitable gifts for us from God, converting this place into a hospital for every variety of disease, a haven for those who are tossed in a sea of affliction, a store-house for the needy poor, a secure home for the wayfarer, a never-failing place of meeting for those who celebrate the festival.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMant184477_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMant184477-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_the_Martyr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Italy">Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_the_Martyr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_(Venice).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_%28Venice%29.jpg/220px-Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_%28Venice%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="253" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_%28Venice%29.jpg/330px-Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_%28Venice%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_%28Venice%29.jpg/440px-Column_of_San_Teodoro_in_Piazzetta_San_Marco_%28Venice%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2372" data-file-height="2727" /></a><figcaption>Statue of St Theodore on western column in Piazzetta in Venice</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1100 the remains of Saint Theodore the Martyr were stolen from <a href="/wiki/Myra" title="Myra">Myra</a> in Asia Minor and taken to Venice, along with the body of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas" title="Saint Nicholas">Saint Nicholas</a> of Myra and his uncle, also Nicholas.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENicol199225_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENicol199225-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
This occurred during an expedition to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> of about 200 ships  dispatched by the Doge <a href="/wiki/Vitale_Michiel" class="mw-redirect" title="Vitale Michiel">Vitale Michiel</a> of Venice under the spiritual leadership of <a href="/wiki/Enrico_Contarini" title="Enrico Contarini">Enrico Contarini</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Olivolo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Olivolo">Bishop of Olivolo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENicol199271_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENicol199271-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
Theodore the Martyr became one of the four tutelary guardians of Venice, along with the angel Gabriel, the Virgin Mary and Saint <a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist" title="Mark the Evangelist">Mark the Evangelist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBatty1820169_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatty1820169-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> However, Saint Theodore was often used as a symbol of Venice's affiliation to Byzantium and as Venice became more independent, Saint Theodore's presence waned in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1257 Jacopo Dauro raided <a href="/wiki/Mesembria_(Pontus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mesembria (Pontus)">Mesembria</a> on the Bulgarian coast of the <a href="/wiki/Black_Sea" title="Black Sea">Black Sea</a>, and took the headless body of Saint Theodore the Martyr from its church of Saint Sophia. The saint was headless because the Emperor Licinius had decapitated him after he had slain a dragon. A relative of Jacopo, Marco Dauro, took the body to Venice where he placed it in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Salvador,_Venice" title="San Salvador, Venice">San Salvador</a>. Saint Theodore and the dragon were later commemorated in a statue on top of his column in Venice.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENicol1992186_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENicol1992186-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
The Irish friar Simon Fitz-Simon passed through Venice in 1323 on his way to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, and listed Theodore the Martyr among the bodies of saints that lay in the city, "whole and uncorrupted".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDavisMarvin200418_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavisMarvin200418-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Jesuit Richa in his <i>History of the Churches of Florence</i> recorded that at the Convent of our Lady of the Murate there was a rib of St. Theodore the Martyr, which had been mislaid and forgotten, but which the saint revealed in a vision to Sister Maria of Scarperia. 
The chronicle of the convent included many wonderful events associated with this relic.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETrollope1856363-4_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETrollope1856363-4-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
An 1872 account tells of a small church of Theodore the Martyr at the foot of <a href="/wiki/Palatine_Hill" title="Palatine Hill">Palatine Hill</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. 
Mothers would bring their children to the church to kiss the saint's relic.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEA.W.1872102_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEA.W.1872102-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Elsewhere">Elsewhere</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_the_Martyr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Elsewhere">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Glastonburyabbey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Glastonburyabbey.jpg/220px-Glastonburyabbey.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="151" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Glastonburyabbey.jpg/330px-Glastonburyabbey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Glastonburyabbey.jpg/440px-Glastonburyabbey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="581" data-file-height="399" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a> once held relics of Theodore</figcaption></figure>
<p>At <a href="/wiki/Thorney,_Cambridgeshire" title="Thorney, Cambridgeshire">Thorney, Cambridgeshire</a>, England, a new church was built by Abbott Gunther in 1069. In 1105 relics of Theodore the Martyr were translated to the church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrickHouts2011309_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrickHouts2011309-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
John of Glastonbury's <i>Cronica sive Antiquitates Glastoniensis Ecclesie</i>, written in the mid-14th century, recorded that <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a> had relics of Theodore the Martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECarley198527_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECarley198527-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Crusade" title="Fourth Crusade">Fourth Crusade</a> (1202–1204) the German monk <a href="/wiki/Gunther_of_Pairis" title="Gunther of Pairis">Gunther of Pairis</a> obtained a variety of relics, including a trace of the blood of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBirdPetersPowell201361_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBirdPetersPowell201361-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
The collection included a piece of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a>, a piece of Saint <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a> and the arm of Saint <a href="/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee" class="mw-redirect" title="James, son of Zebedee">James the Great</a>. 
There were relics of other saints including Theodore the Martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBirdPetersPowell201362_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBirdPetersPowell201362-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Clairvaux_Abbey" title="Clairvaux Abbey">Clairvaux</a> inventory of 1504 included a number of relics including the shoulder blade of Saint Theodore the Martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESchenk2012204_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESchenk2012204-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
The <i>Dames de l'Assomption</i> of <a href="/wiki/Rue_Saint-Honor%C3%A9" title="Rue Saint-Honoré">rue Saint-Honoré</a> in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, who rebuilt their church in 1670 at their own expense, preserved the body of Saint Theodore the Martyr with great veneration. It had been given to them by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Barberini" title="Antonio Barberini">Antonio Barberini</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rheims">Archbishop of Rheims</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThe_History_of_Paris_1825314_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThe_History_of_Paris_1825314-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a> (1527–1598) was a great collector of relics, and accumulated a huge collection. It included 290 teeth of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Apollonia" title="Saint Apollonia">Saint Apollonia</a>, the patron saint of toothache.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFuentes1999164_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFuentes1999164-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
His successors continued to add to the collection.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENoone199830_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENoone199830-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
In 1762 it was reported that the body of Saint Theodore the Martyr was lying in a magnificent shrine of silver or brass gilt, adorned with crystals and gems and enameled with gold, in  the monastery of <a href="/wiki/El_Escorial" title="El Escorial">El Escorial</a> near <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThompson1762227_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson1762227-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
