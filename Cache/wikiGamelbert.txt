					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">8th century Bavarian Christian priest</div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg/220px-Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="294" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg/330px-Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg/440px-Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2134" data-file-height="2848" /></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure> 
<p>The Blessed <b>Gamelbert</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, who worked in the 8th century in what is now <a href="/wiki/Stephansposching" title="Stephansposching">Stephansposching</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>. He is commemorated on January 17.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamelbert_of_Michaelsbuch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gamelbert of Michaelsbuch was born in 720 in Michaelsbuch in Bavaria. He was the son of wealthy aristocrats, who planned for him a career in the military. However, he preferred to herd his father's flocks, because in the peaceful stillness of the fields and forests he could turn his mind to prayer and meditation. A local priest began to teach him, and he was ordained a priest.
</p><p>Once he made a pilgrimage to Rome. On the return journey he found hospitality in a house where a mother had just given birth. She asked Gamelbert to christen her newborn son. The child's name was <a href="/wiki/Utto" title="Utto">Utto</a>. After the death of his parents, he used his inheritance to establish a parish on property he owned in Michaelsbuch.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He worked there for over 50 years as a parish priest in Michaelsbuch.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Gentle by nature, he was unfailingly generous to the poor. 
</p><p>Gamelbert acquired from Duke <a href="/wiki/Tassilo_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Tassilo III">Tassilo III</a> a piece of woodland on the opposite bank of the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> between <a href="/wiki/Mariaposching" title="Mariaposching">Mariaposching</a> and Deggendorf, for which he had to pay a tax known as the <i>Medema</i>. From this was derived the name of <a href="/wiki/Metten" title="Metten">Metten</a> both for the place itself and for the monastery he founded there.
</p><p>In 766 he cleared the land and founded the Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Metten_Abbey" title="Metten Abbey">Metten Abbey</a> in Lower Bavaria, not far from Michaelsbuch.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Gamelbert had prepared his godson Utto for the priesthood. He brought, Utto, now a monk at <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Abbey" title="Reichenau Abbey">Reichenau</a>, to be its first abbot. (Other sources say that Utto himself founded the monastery in 766 on a piece of land he owned.)<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamelbert_of_Michaelsbuch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gamelbert died on 17 January around 802. Many pilgrims and needy people made pilgrimages to his tomb. His <i>vita</i> was written abut 1000 by a monk from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Emmeram%27s_Abbey" title="Saint Emmeram&#39;s Abbey">Saint Emmeram's Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gamelbert was depicted in 1414 on the cover of a Bible in Metten Abbey.
</p><p>He was beatified by the confirmation of his cult on 25 August 1909 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>. His memorial day is January 17. At the same time, the cult of blessed Utto of Metten was confirmed. In art he is depicted as a priest in an oratory surrounded by sheep.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2017 five graves of Christians from about the 8th to 12th century were discovered during an archaeological dig at a construction site in Michaelsbuch. Also found were post pits suggesting the site of an early church. The five skeletons have been referred for further study.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
