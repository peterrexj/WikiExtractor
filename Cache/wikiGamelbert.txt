					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg/220px-Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="294" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg/330px-Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg/440px-Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2134" data-file-height="2848" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Michaelsbuch-Gamelbert-Gedenkstein.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div></div></div></div> 
<p>The Blessed <b>Gamelbert</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Christian</a> priest, who worked in the 8th century in the area of the present <a href="/wiki/Deggendorf" title="Deggendorf">Deggendorf</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamelbert_of_Michaelsbuch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gamelbert is said to have been of noble descent and a lord of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Michaelsbuch&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Michaelsbuch (page does not exist)">Michaelsbuch</a>. In the mid-8th century he acquired from Duke <a href="/wiki/Tassilo_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Tassilo III">Tassilo III</a> a piece of woodland on the opposite bank of the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> between <a href="/wiki/Mariaposching" title="Mariaposching">Mariaposching</a> and <a href="/wiki/Deggendorf" title="Deggendorf">Deggendorf</a>, for which he had to pay a tax known as the <i>Medema</i>. From this was derived the name of <a href="/wiki/Metten" title="Metten">Metten</a> both for the place itself and for the monastery, <a href="/wiki/Metten_Abbey" title="Metten Abbey">Metten Abbey</a>, that was founded there.
</p><p>The first abbot was Gamelbert's godson <b><a href="/wiki/Utto" title="Utto">Utto</a></b>, who directed the construction of the monastery from his hermitage (the present <a href="/w/index.php?title=Uttobrunn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Uttobrunn (page does not exist)">Uttobrunn</a>). In 766 twelve monks arrived from <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Abbey" title="Reichenau Abbey">Reichenau Abbey</a> as the first official occupants, although the place was well settled by then
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other">Other</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamelbert_of_Michaelsbuch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Other">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In art, Gamelbert is represented as a priest or as a pilgrim surrounded by birds. His feast is celebrated on 17 January.
</p><p>Grave finds from <b>Uttenkofen</b> near Michaelsbuch have been dated to the late 7th or early 8th century and have been associated with the founding family of Metten Abbey.
</p>
