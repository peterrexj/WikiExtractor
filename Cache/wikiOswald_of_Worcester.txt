<p><b>Oswald of Worcester</b> (died 29 February 992) was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a> from 972 to his death in 992. He was of Danish ancestry, but brought up by his uncle, <a href="/wiki/Oda_of_Canterbury" title="Oda of Canterbury">Oda</a>, who sent him to France to the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Fleury_Abbey" title="Fleury Abbey">Fleury</a> to become a monk. After a number of years at Fleury, Oswald returned to England at the request of his uncle, who died before Oswald returned. With his uncle's death, Oswald needed a patron and turned to another kinsman, <a href="/wiki/Oscytel" title="Oscytel">Oskytel</a>, who had recently become Archbishop of York. His activity for Oskytel attracted the notice of Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a> who had Oswald consecrated as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a> in 961. In 972, Oswald was promoted to the see of York, although he continued to hold Worcester also.
</p><p>As bishop and archbishop, Oswald was a supporter and one of the leading promoters (together with <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelwold_of_Winchester" title="Æthelwold of Winchester">Æthelwold</a>) of Dunstan's reforms of the church, including monastic reforms.<sup id="cite_ref-Monasticism101_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monasticism101-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Oswald founded a number of monasteries, including <a href="/wiki/Ramsey_Abbey" title="Ramsey Abbey">Ramsey Abbey</a>, and reformed another seven, including <a href="/wiki/Winchcombe_Abbey" title="Winchcombe Abbey">Winchcombe</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gloucestershire" title="Gloucestershire">Gloucestershire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pershore_Abbey" title="Pershore Abbey">Pershore</a> and <a href="/wiki/Evesham_Abbey" title="Evesham Abbey">Evesham</a> in <a href="/wiki/Worcestershire" title="Worcestershire">Worcestershire</a>. Oswald also switched the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral chapter</a> of Worcester from <a href="/wiki/Secular_clergy" title="Secular clergy">secular clergy</a> to monks. While archbishop, he brought the scholar <a href="/wiki/Abbo_of_Fleury" title="Abbo of Fleury">Abbo of Fleury</a> to teach, and he spent two years in England, mostly at Ramsey. Oswald died in 992, while <a href="/wiki/Maundy_(foot_washing)" title="Maundy (foot washing)">washing the feet</a> of the poor. A hagiographical life was written shortly after his death, and he was quickly hailed as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oswald_of_Worcester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oswald, of <a href="/wiki/Danes" title="Danes">Danish</a> parentage, was brought up by his uncle Oda, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, and was also related to Oskytel, later Archbishop of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic40_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic40-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was also related to the <i><a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">cniht</a></i> Osulf, who received land while Oswald was bishop of Worcester.<sup id="cite_ref-Govern57_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Govern57-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Oswald was instructed by a Frankish scholar <a href="/wiki/Frithegod" title="Frithegod">Frithegod</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He held the office of <a href="/wiki/Dean_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dean (religion)">dean</a> of <a href="/wiki/Winchester_Cathedral" title="Winchester Cathedral">Winchester</a>, but he was sent by his uncle to France and entered the monastery of Fleury about 950,<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic40_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic40-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where he was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> in 959. While at Fleury he met <a href="/wiki/Osgar" title="Osgar">Osgar of Abingdon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Germanus_of_Winchester" title="Germanus of Winchester">Germanus of Winchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic40_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic40-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The influence of Fleury was to be evident later in Oswald's life, when it was one of the inspirations for the <i><a href="/wiki/Regularis_Concordia_(Winchester)" title="Regularis Concordia (Winchester)">Regularis Concordia</a></i>, the English code of monastic conduct agreed to in 970.<sup id="cite_ref-Monasticism102_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monasticism102-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_England">Return to England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oswald_of_Worcester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Return to England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oswald returned to England in 958 at the behest of his uncle, but Oda died before Oswald returned. Lacking a patron, Oswald turned to Oskytel, recently named Archbishop of York. It is possible that Oswald along with Oskytel travelled to Rome for Oskytel's <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>, but this story is only contained in a 12th-century Ramsey Abbey chronicle, so it may not be authentic.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Even if he did not travel to Rome, Oswald was active in ecclesiastical affairs at <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> until Dunstan obtained Oswald's appointment to the see, or bishopric, of <a href="/wiki/Worcester,_England" title="Worcester, England">Worcester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic40_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic40-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was consecrated as Bishop of Worcester in 961.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook224_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook224-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Soon after his consecration, he persuaded Germanus to come back to England and made him head of a small religious community near <a href="/wiki/Westbury-on-Trym" title="Westbury-on-Trym">Westbury-on-Trym</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic40_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic40-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After the establishment of this group about 962, Oswald grew worried that because the monastery was located on lands owned by the see of Worcester, his successors in the see might disrupt the community. He was offered the site of Ramsey Abbey in <a href="/wiki/Huntingdonshire" title="Huntingdonshire">Huntingdonshire</a> by <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelwine,_Ealdorman_of_East_Anglia" title="Æthelwine, Ealdorman of East Anglia">Æthelwine</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelstan_Half-King" title="Æthelstan Half-King">Æthelstan Half-King</a>, and Oswald established a monastery there about 971 that attracted most of the members of the community at Westbury. This foundation at Ramsey went on to become Ramsey Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic51_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic51-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Ramsey was Oswald's most famous foundation,<sup id="cite_ref-ASE450_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE450-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> with its church dedicated in 974. Later, Oswald invited Abbo of Fleury to come and teach at Ramsey.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic488_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic488-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Oswald directed the affairs of Ramsey Abbey until his death, when the dean Eadnoth became the first abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He gave a magnificent Bible to Ramsey, which was important enough to merit a mention in Oswald's <i>Life</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Dodwell95_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dodwell95-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Alongside the gift of the book, Oswald also contributed wall hangings and other textiles to the abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Dodwell129_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dodwell129-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg/220px-Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="252" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg/330px-Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg/440px-Tract_by_Abbo_of_Fleury.jpg 2x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="515" /></a><figcaption>A medieval manuscript of Abbo of Fleury's work</figcaption></figure>
<p>Oswald supported Dunstan and Æthelwold, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>, in their efforts to purify the Church from <a href="/wiki/Secularism" title="Secularism">secularism</a>. Aided by King <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceful" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceful">Edgar</a>, he took a prominent part in the revival of monastic discipline along the precepts of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Rule of Saint Benedict</a>. His methods differed from Æthelwold's, who often violently ejected secular clergy from churches and replaced them with monks.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic55_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic55-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Oswald also organised the estates of his see into administrative <a href="/wiki/Hundred_(country_subdivision)" class="mw-redirect" title="Hundred (country subdivision)">hundreds</a> known as the <a href="/wiki/Oswaldslow" title="Oswaldslow">Oswaldslow</a>, which helped stabilise the ecclesiastical revenues.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic488_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic488-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He constantly visited the monasteries he founded, and was long remembered as father of his people both as bishop and archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic55_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic55-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> It was Oswald who changed the cathedral chapter of Worcester from priests to monks,<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic621_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic621-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> although the exact method that he employed is unclear. One tradition says that Oswald used a slow approach in building up a new church of monks next to the cathedral, allowing the cathedral's priests to continue performing services in the cathedral until the monastic foundation was strong enough to take over the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE450_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE450-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Another tradition claims that, instead, Oswald expelled any of the clergy in the cathedral that would not give up their wives and replaced them with monks immediately. Oswald also reformed Winchcombe Abbey, along with the monasteries of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Church,_Westbury_on_Trym" title="Holy Trinity Church, Westbury on Trym">Westbury Priory</a>, Pershore Abbey, and Evesham Abbey. It is also possible that monasteries were established in Gloucester and <a href="/wiki/Deerhurst" title="Deerhurst">Deerhurst</a>, but evidence is lacking for their exact foundation dates.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_York">Archbishop of York</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oswald_of_Worcester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop of York">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 972 Oswald was made Archbishop of York<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook224_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook224-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and journeyed to Rome to receive a pallium from Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XIII" title="Pope John XIII">John XIII</a>. It is possible that he also travelled on Edgar's behalf to the court of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto I</a>, and that these two journeys had been combined.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He continued to hold the see of Worcester in addition to York.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook224_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook224-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The holding of Worcester in addition to York became traditional for almost the next fifty years. Although it was uncanonical, it had many advantages for York in that it added a much richer diocese to their holdings, and one which was more peaceful as well.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE436_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE436-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> When Edgar died in 975, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfhere,_Ealdorman_of_Mercia" title="Ælfhere, Ealdorman of Mercia">Ælfhere</a>, Ealdorman of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>, broke up many monastic communities, some of which were Oswald's foundations.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic53_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic53-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Ramsey, however, was not disturbed, probably due to the patronage of Æthelwine, Ealdorman of <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a>, son of Æthelstan Half-King. Ælfhere was a supporter of <a href="/wiki/Ethelred_the_Unready" class="mw-redirect" title="Ethelred the Unready">Ethelred the Unready</a>, the son of Edgar's third marriage, while Oswald supported the son of Edgar's first marriage, <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Martyr" title="Edward the Martyr">Edward the Martyr</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> in the dispute over who would succeed King Edgar.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams9_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams9-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 985, Oswald invited Abbo of Fleury to come to Ramsey to help found the monastic school there. Abbo was at Ramsey from 985 to 987, where he taught <a href="/wiki/Computus" class="mw-redirect" title="Computus">computus</a>, or the methods for calculating <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>. It was also often used in trying to calculate the date of the <a href="/wiki/Last_Judgment" title="Last Judgment">Last Judgment</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher92_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher92-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> A surviving manuscript gives a list compiled by Oswald, setting forth estates that had been taken from the diocese of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Wormald186_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wormald186-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_sainthood">Death and sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Oswald_of_Worcester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oswald died on 29 February 992 in the act of washing the feet of the poor at Worcester,<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic55_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic55-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> as was his daily custom during <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a>, and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Worcester_Cathedral" title="Worcester Cathedral">Church of St Mary at Worcester</a>. He promoted the education of the clergy and persuaded scholars to come from Fleury and teach in England.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic53_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic53-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> A <i>Life of Oswald</i> was written after his death, probably by <a href="/wiki/Byrhtferth" title="Byrhtferth">Byrhtferth</a>, a monk of Ramsey Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Monastic494_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monastic494-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Two manuscripts, a <a href="/wiki/Psalter" title="Psalter">psalter</a> (Harley MS 2904 in the British Library) and a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Pontifical" title="Roman Pontifical">pontifical</a> (MS 100, part 2 from Sidney Sussex College of Cambridge University), probably belonged to Oswald and would have been used in his daily devotions.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Almost immediately after his death miracles were reported at his funeral and at his tomb. His remains were translated to a different burial spot in <a href="/wiki/Worcester_Cathedral" title="Worcester Cathedral">Worcester Cathedral</a> ten years after his death. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 28 February<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh459_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh459-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> or on 19 May in the <a href="/wiki/Personal_Ordinariate_of_the_Chair_of_Saint_Peter" title="Personal Ordinariate of the Chair of Saint Peter">Personal Ordinariate of the Chair of Saint Peter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
