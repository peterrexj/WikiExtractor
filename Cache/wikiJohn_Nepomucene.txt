<p><b>John of Nepomuk</b> (or <b>John Nepomucene</b>) (<a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Jan Nepomucký</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Johannes Nepomuk</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ioannes Nepomucenus</i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1345 – 20 March 1393)<sup id="cite_ref-JBohemia_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JBohemia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
was the <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>) who was drowned in the <a href="/wiki/Vltava" title="Vltava">Vltava</a> river at the behest of <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_IV_of_Bohemia" title="Wenceslaus IV of Bohemia">Wenceslaus IV of Bohemia</a>. Later accounts state that he was the confessor of the queen of Bohemia and refused to divulge the secrets of the confessional. On the basis of this account, John of Nepomuk is considered the first <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Seal_of_the_Confessional_and_the_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Seal of the Confessional and the Catholic Church">Seal of the Confessional</a>, a patron against <a href="/wiki/Defamation" title="Defamation">calumnies</a> and, because of the manner of his death, a protector from <a href="/wiki/Flood" title="Flood">floods</a> and drowning.<sup id="cite_ref-JBohemia_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JBohemia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Basic_biographical_information">Basic biographical information</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Nepomuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Basic biographical information">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jan z Pomuku came from the small market town of Pomuk (later renamed <a href="/wiki/Nepomuk" title="Nepomuk">Nepomuk</a>) in Bohemia, now in the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>, which belonged to the nearby <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> abbey.
</p><p>Born in the 1340s, his father was a certain Velflín (Welflin, Wölflin) and his mother is unknown. His father's name is probably a derivative of the German name Wolfgang.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jan first studied at the <a href="/wiki/Charles_University_in_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles University in Prague">University of Prague</a>, then furthered his studies in <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon law</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Padua" title="University of Padua">University of Padua</a> from 1383 to 1387. In 1393 he was made the vicar-general of <a href="/wiki/St._Giles%27_Church_(Prague)" title="St. Giles&#39; Church (Prague)">Saint Giles Cathedral</a> by <a href="/wiki/Jan_of_Jen%C5%A1tejn" title="Jan of Jenštejn">Jan of Jenštejn</a> (1348–1400), who was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Prague">Archbishop of Prague</a> from 1378 to 1396. In the same year, on 20 March, he was tortured and thrown into the river <a href="/wiki/Vltava" title="Vltava">Vltava</a> from <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> by order of King Wenceslaus IV.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Czechowicz_St._John_Nepomuk.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img Xaver Palko</a>.</figcaption></figure><p> At issue was the appointment of a new <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> for the rich and powerful <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Kladruby_(Tachov_District)" title="Kladruby (Tachov District)">Abbey of Kladruby</a>; its abbot was a territorial magnate whose resources would be crucial to Wenceslaus in his struggles with nobles. Wenceslaus at the same time was backing the <a href="/wiki/Avignon_papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Avignon papacy">Avignon papacy</a>, whereas the Archbishop of Prague followed its rival, the pope at Rome. Contrary to the wishes of Wenceslaus, John confirmed the archbishop's candidate for Abbot of Kladruby, and was drowned on the king's orders on 20 March 1393.
</p><p>This account is based on four contemporary documents. The first is the accusation of the king, presented to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> on 23 April 1393, by Archbishop Jan of Jenštejn, who immediately went to Rome together with the new abbot of Kladruby.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A few years later Abbott Ladolf of Sagan listed John of Nepomuk in the catalog of Sagan abbots, completed in 1398,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> as well as in the treatise "De longævo schismate", lib. VII, c. xix.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A further document is the "Chronik des Deutschordens"/<i>Chronik des Landes Preussen</i>, a chronicle of the <a href="/wiki/Teutonic_Order" title="Teutonic Order">Teutonic Order</a> compiled by <a href="/wiki/John_of_Posilge" class="mw-redirect" title="John of Posilge">John of Posilge</a>, who died in 1405.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the above accusation, Jan of Jenštejn already calls John of Nepomuk a "saint martyr". The biography of the bishop (written by his chaplain) describes John of Nepomuk as "gloriosum Christi martyrem miraculisque coruscum" (in English: "a glorious martyr of Christ and sparkling with miracles").
</p><p>Thus, the vicar put to death for defending the laws and the autonomy of the Catholic Church became revered as a saint directly after his death.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_accounts">Later accounts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Nepomuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Later accounts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg/220px-Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="299" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg/330px-Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg/440px-Jan_Nepomucky_na_Karlove_moste_detail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1821" data-file-height="2478" /></a><figcaption>The prototypical statue of John of Nepomuk at <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, at the site where the saint was thrown into Vltava. Made by <a href="/wiki/Jan_Brokoff" title="Jan Brokoff">Jan Brokoff</a> upon a model by <a href="/wiki/Matthias_Rauchmiller" title="Matthias Rauchmiller">Matthias Rauchmiller</a> in 1683, on the supposed 300th anniversary of the saint's death, which was until the mid-18th century presumed to have happened in 1383. It was the basis for a number of statues of the saint all across the Europe.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge,_PRAGUE.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge%2C_PRAGUE.jpg/220px-3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge%2C_PRAGUE.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="148" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge%2C_PRAGUE.jpg/330px-3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge%2C_PRAGUE.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge%2C_PRAGUE.jpg/440px-3_of_10_-_Charles_Bridge%2C_PRAGUE.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2896" data-file-height="1944" /></a><figcaption>Cross on the bridge of St. John of Nepomuk</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg/220px-St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg/330px-St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg/440px-St._Ian_Nepomuk._Buchach_3.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="4000" /></a><figcaption>St. John of Nepomuk in <a href="/wiki/Buchach" title="Buchach">Buchach</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>. Made by <a href="/wiki/Johann_Georg_Pinsel" title="Johann Georg Pinsel">Johann Georg Pinsel</a>, 1750 (copy)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Much additional biographical information comes from Bohemian annalists who wrote 60 or more years after the events they recount. Although they may have taken advantage of sources not available today, their contribution is considered legendary by many historians, particularly by the Protestant ones.
</p>
<ul><li>In his chronicle <i>Chronica regum Romanorum</i>, completed in 1459, Thomas Ebendorfer (d. 1464) states that King Wenceslaus had drowned the <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> (the priest who heard the confession) of his wife, indicated as <i>Magister Jan</i>, because he had stated that <i>only the one who rules properly deserves the name of king</i> and had refused to betray the <a href="/wiki/Seal_of_Confession" class="mw-redirect" title="Seal of Confession">seal of Confession</a>. This is the first source to mention this refusal as a motivation of the condemnation of John of Nepomuk.</li>
<li>In his <i>Instructions for the King</i>, completed in 1471, Paul Zidek provides further details.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> King Wenceslaus was afraid that his wife had a lover. As she was used to confessing to <i>Magister Jan</i>, King Wenceslaus ordered him to reveal the name of the lover, but to no avail. Therefore, the king ordered John to be drowned. Note that in these chronicles neither the date of the events nor the name of the queen is mentioned.</li>
<li>In 1483 John of Krumlov, dean of St. Vitus cathedral, states that John died in 1383 (one decade earlier than the recognized date, perhaps due to a transcription error).<sup id="cite_ref-off_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-off-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> As the first wife of Wenceslaus died in 1386, this change of date also causes uncertainty about the name of the queen.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>The mistake of John of Krumlov crept into the <i><a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_Hajek" title="Wenceslaus Hajek">Annales Bohemorum</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> of <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_Hajek_of_Liboczan" class="mw-redirect" title="Wenceslaus Hajek of Liboczan">Wenceslaus Hajek of Liboczan</a> (Václav Hájek z Libočan), the "Bohemian Livy". He suggested that two Jan di Nepomuks may have existed and have both been killed by King Wenceslaus. The first one is the queen's confessor, who died in 1383; the other the vicar of the archbishop, who disagreed with the king on the election of the abbot of Kladruby and was drowned in 1393. As Hajek's annals enjoyed a wide success, they influenced all subsequent historians for two centuries, up to the Latin edition, critically annotated by the translator, which considerably reduced Hajek's credit as a reliable historian.
</p><p>Further and less reliable details about John of Nepomuk come from the annalists of the 17th and 18th centuries. <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_Balbinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus Balbinus">Boleslaus Balbinus</a>, S.J., in his <i>Vita b. Joannis Nepomuceni martyris</i><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> gives the most rich account.
</p><p>Although the theory of Hajek of Liboczan has no credit today, some historians believe the vicar's refusal to betray the seal of the confessional might have been the secret reason why Wenceslaus took vengeance on John of Nepomuk as soon as a credible excuse provided the opportunity.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="A_controversial_figure">A controversial figure</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Nepomuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: A controversial figure">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg/170px-Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg/255px-Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg/340px-Wroclaw_Nepomucen_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2112" data-file-height="2816" /></a><figcaption>1732 monument on Cathedral Island in <a href="/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw" title="Wrocław">Wrocław</a>, by Jan Jiři Urbansky<sup id="cite_ref-Więcek1964_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Więcek1964-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>Catholics see John of Nepomuk as a martyr to the cause of defending the <a href="/wiki/Seal_of_the_Confessional_and_the_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Seal of the Confessional and the Catholic Church">Seal of the Confessional</a>. <a href="/wiki/Romantic_Nationalism" class="mw-redirect" title="Romantic Nationalism">Romantic nationalists</a> regard him as a Czech martyr to imperial interference. Most historians present him as a victim of a late version of the inveterate <a href="/wiki/Investiture_controversy" class="mw-redirect" title="Investiture controversy">investiture controversy</a> between secular rulers and the Catholic hierarchy.
</p><p>The connection of John of Nepomuk with the <a href="/wiki/Priest-penitent_privilege" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest-penitent privilege">inviolability of the confessional</a> is part of the transformation of an historical figure into a legend, which can be traced through successive stages. The <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a>, who hastened to Rome soon after the crime, in his charge against Wenceslaus, called the victim a martyr; in the <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> written a few years later miracles are already recorded, by which the drowned man was discovered. About the middle of the 15th century the statement appears for the first time that the refusal to violate the seal of confession was the cause of John's death. Two decades later (1471), the dean of Prague, Paul Zidek, makes John the queen's confessor. The chronicler Wenceslaus Hajek speaks in 1541 (perhaps due to an incorrect reading of his sources) of <i>two</i> Johns of Nepomuk being drowned; the first as confessor, the second for his confirmation of the abbot.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_of_Nepomuk2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/John_of_Nepomuk2.jpg/220px-John_of_Nepomuk2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="148" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/John_of_Nepomuk2.jpg/330px-John_of_Nepomuk2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/57/John_of_Nepomuk2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="380" data-file-height="256" /></a><figcaption>The place on the bridge parapet where John of Nepomuk was thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Vltava" title="Vltava">Vltava</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The legend is especially indebted for its growth to the Jesuit historiographer <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_Balbinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus Balbinus">Boleslaus Balbinus</a> the "Bohemian Pliny", whose <i>Vita beati Joannis Nepomuceni martyris</i> was published in Prague, 1670. Although the <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> metropolitan chapter did not accept the biography dedicated to it, "as being frequently destitute of historical foundation and erroneous, a bungling work of mythological rhetoric", Balbinus stuck to it. In 1683 the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a> was adorned with a statue of the saint, which has had numerous successors; in 1708 the first church was dedicated to him at <a href="/wiki/Hradec_Kr%C3%A1lov%C3%A9" title="Hradec Králové">Hradec Králové</a>; a more famous <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage_Church_of_Saint_John_of_Nepomuk" title="Pilgrimage Church of Saint John of Nepomuk">Pilgrimage Church of Saint John of Nepomuk</a> was founded in 1719.
</p><p>Meanwhile, in spite of the objection of the Jesuits, the process was inaugurated which ended with his <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">canonisation</a>. On 31 May 1721, he was beatified, and on 19 March 1729, he was canonised under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a>. The acts of the process, comprising 500 pages, distinguish two Johns of Nepomuk and sanction the <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cult (religion)">cult</a></i> of the one who was drowned in 1383 as a martyr of the sacrament of penance.
</p><p>According to some Protestant sources, the figure of John Nepomuk is a legend due to <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> and that its historical kernel is really <a href="/wiki/Jan_Hus" title="Jan Hus">Jan Hus</a>, who was metamorphosed from a Bohemian Reformer into a Roman Catholic saint: the Nepomuk story would be based on Wenceslaus Hajek's blending of the Jan who was drowned in 1393 and the <a href="/wiki/Jan_Hus" title="Jan Hus">Jan</a> who was burned in 1415.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The resemblances are certainly striking, extending to the manner of celebrating their commemorations. But when the Jesuits came to <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, the Nepomuk veneration had long been widespread; and the idea of canonization originated in opposition not to the <a href="/wiki/Hussites" title="Hussites">Hussites</a>, but to <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestantism</a>, as a weapon of the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a>. In the image of the saint which gradually arose is reflected the religious history of Bohemia.
</p><p>A coincidental drought in the region a year later helped the legend along; the church convinced the peasants that the drought represented God's punishment for the killing of Jan Nepomucký.  Building on that success, they attempted to paint the king as even blacker, with certain clerical circles spreading reports of John's courage, saying that as confessor to the Queen he had refused to reveal her secrets, and that was why he had been murdered.  Belief in John's supernatural powers culminated with the discovery of his supposed tongue when three centuries later his tomb was opened and a piece of reddened tissue fell out of his skull.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Regional_significance">Regional significance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Nepomuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Regional significance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk,_Prague.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk%2C_Prague.JPG/220px-Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk%2C_Prague.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk%2C_Prague.JPG/330px-Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk%2C_Prague.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk%2C_Prague.JPG/440px-Tomb_of_John_of_Nepomuk%2C_Prague.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>St. John of Nepomuk's Tomb in <a href="/wiki/St_Vitus_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="St Vitus Cathedral">St Vitus Cathedral</a>, Prague</figcaption></figure>
<p>Statues of Saint John of Nepomuk are often encountered in Central Europe, including the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Austria, Croatia, Slovenia, Germany, Italy, Poland, Lithuania, Romania (Sibiu), <a href="/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Alsace-Lorraine">Alsace-Lorraine</a> and, although rarely, <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>. He is usually portrayed with a <a href="/wiki/Halo_(religious_iconography)" title="Halo (religious iconography)">halo</a> with five stars, reminding to the stars that hovered over the <a href="/wiki/Vltava" title="Vltava">Vltava</a> River on the night of his murder. His tomb, a <a href="/wiki/Baroque" title="Baroque">Baroque</a> monument cast in silver and silver-gilt that was designed by <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Emanuel_Fischer_von_Erlach" title="Joseph Emanuel Fischer von Erlach">Josef Emanuel Fischer of Erlach</a>, stands in <a href="/wiki/St_Vitus_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="St Vitus Cathedral">St Vitus Cathedral</a>, Prague.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Statue_usage">Statue usage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Nepomuk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Statue usage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A statue of Saint John of Nepomuk has often been erected on bridges in many countries, such as on the <a href="/wiki/Ponte_Milvio" title="Ponte Milvio">Ponte Milvio</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. The one in Prague was extremely popular as late as the 19th Century, when people traveled from <a href="/wiki/Tyrol" title="Tyrol">Tyrol</a>, Hungary, and particularly Bohemia to Prague to celebrate his feast day, May 16.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> There is a white <a href="/wiki/Statue_of_St._John_of_Nepomuk_in_Divina" title="Statue of St. John of Nepomuk in Divina">statue of St. John of Nepomuk</a> in the small village of <a href="/wiki/Divina" title="Divina">Divina</a>, in Slovakia.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> There is also a commemorative plaque on a bridge leading out of <a href="/wiki/Obergurgl" title="Obergurgl">Obergurgl, Austria</a> depicting Nepomuk holding a finger to his lips, as if protecting a secret.
</p><p>There is a statue of St. John of Nepomuk in Riedstadt, a small town near Frankfurt am Main, Germany. The statue was first put at the riverside in Gernsheim where it stayed from 1933–1998. In May 1999, it was moved 10 kilometers North and placed at its new location by then newly built bridge.
</p>
