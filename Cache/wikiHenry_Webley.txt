<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Blessed William Dean</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Grassington" title="Grassington">Grassington</a>, <a href="/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="West Riding of Yorkshire">West Riding of Yorkshire</a>, England</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">28 August 1588<br /><a href="/wiki/Mile_End" title="Mile End">Mile End</a>, London, England</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">15 December 1929 by Pope Pius XI</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>William Dean</b> or <b>Deane</b> (died 28 August 1588) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is one of the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Catholic_martyrs_of_the_English_Reformation" title="List of Catholic martyrs of the English Reformation">Catholic martyrs</a>, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Venn_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venn-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Dean_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Son of Thomas B. of <a href="/wiki/Grassington" title="Grassington">Grassington</a> <a href="/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="West Riding of Yorkshire">West Riding of Yorkshire</a> William Dean attended schools in <a href="/wiki/Leeds" title="Leeds">Leeds</a> and <a href="/wiki/Clitheroe" title="Clitheroe">Clitheroe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Venn_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venn-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dean was matriculated <a href="/wiki/Sizar" title="Sizar">sizar</a> from <a href="/wiki/Magdalene_College,_Cambridge" title="Magdalene College, Cambridge">Magdalene College, Cambridge</a> in 1575 and was admitted <a href="/wiki/Pensioner" title="Pensioner">pensioner</a> at <a href="/wiki/Caius_College,_Cambridge" class="mw-redirect" title="Caius College, Cambridge">Caius College, Cambridge</a> in November 1577, aged 20.<sup id="cite_ref-Venn_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venn-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He then became a minister. In 1581, he was reconciled to the Catholic faith by the seminary priest <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aufield" title="Thomas Aufield">Thomas Alfield</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Dunn_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dunn-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That same year he studied at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> and was ordained priest at <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>, 21 December 1581, together with the martyrs <a href="/wiki/George_Haydock" title="George Haydock">George Haydock</a> and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Nutter" title="Robert Nutter">Robert Nutter</a>. Their ordination coincided with the time that the news of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a>'s death reached the college.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dean said his first Mass 9 January and left for England 25 January 1582. He was probably being tracked the whole time and was arrested 21 February and sent to <a href="/wiki/Newgate_prison" class="mw-redirect" title="Newgate prison">Newgate prison</a> and subjected to torture. In April 1584 he was moved to <a href="/wiki/The_Clink" title="The Clink">The Clink</a>. He was banished with a number of other priests in early 1585, put ashore on the coast of <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, and threatened with death if he dared to go back to England. Nevertheless, in November he returned to his mission work there and was again arrested and confined to <a href="/wiki/Gatehouse_Prison" title="Gatehouse Prison">Gatehouse Prison</a>. He was tried, and condemned for his priesthood on 22 August 1588.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The failure of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Armada" title="Spanish Armada">Spanish Armada</a> brought about a fierce anti-Catholic persecution and some twenty-seven Catholics were executed that year. Six new <a href="/wiki/Gibbet" class="mw-redirect" title="Gibbet">gibbets</a> were erected in London, it is said at the <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Leicester" title="Earl of Leicester">Earl of Leicester</a>'s instigation, and Dean, who had been condemned with five other priests and four laymen, was the first to suffer on the gallows erected at <a href="/wiki/Mile_End" title="Mile End">Mile End</a>. With him died a layman, Henry Webley, for relieving and assisting him.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At his execution Dean tried to speak to the people, but he was struck on the head and gagged.<sup id="cite_ref-Dunn_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dunn-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Henry_Webley">Henry Webley</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Dean_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Henry Webley">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Henry Webley was born in Gloucester around 1558. From there he went to London, where he assisted William Dean during the priest's brief mission in the city. By 1586, the ports were being closely watched by both government officials and government spies. In April of that year, Webley was seized onboard a ship at <a href="/wiki/Chichester" title="Chichester">Chichester</a> bound for France and sent to the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>, where he remained for two years. His crimes included being reconciled to the Catholic religion, making his confession to Fr. Dean, and aiding and assisting the priest. His trial was held in the latter part of August 1588 at the Old Bailey, but appears to have been merely a formality since those sent for trial were those selected for execution.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDunn,_p.360_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDunn,_p.360-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Webley was hanged, along with Dean, at Mile End Green on 28 August 1588.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Edward_Shelley">Edward Shelley</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Dean_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Edward Shelley">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Edward Shelley of <a href="/wiki/Warminghurst" title="Warminghurst">Warminghurst</a>, Sussex, and East Smithfield, London (son of Edward Shelley, of Warminghurst, a Master of the Household of the sovereign, and the settlor in <a href="/wiki/Rule_in_Shelley%27s_Case" title="Rule in Shelley&#39;s Case">"Shelley's case"</a>, and Joan, daughter of Paul Eden, of Penshurst, Kent). His grandfather was Sir John Shelley of Michelgrove near Arundel. He was apparently uncle by marriage to Benjamin Norton, afterwards one of the seven vicars of <a href="/wiki/Richard_Smith_(bishop)" title="Richard Smith (bishop)">Dr. Richard Smith</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aged 50 or 60, Shelley was already in <a href="/wiki/The_Clink" title="The Clink">The Clink</a> for his religion in April, 1584. He was condemned for keeping a book called <a href="/wiki/Leicester%27s_Commonwealth" title="Leicester&#39;s Commonwealth"><i>My Lord Leicester's Commonwealth</i></a> and for having assisted the Venerable William Dean. He was hanged at Tyburn 30 August 1588.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
