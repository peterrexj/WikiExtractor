<p><b>Saint Reverianus of Autun</b> (French: <i lang="fr">Révérien, Rirand</i>, also <i>Revenerius, Rivianus, Reverentianus</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-heiligen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <i>Reveriano, Reverie</i><sup id="cite_ref-cath_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) (died June 1, 273 AD) was a 3rd-century <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Autun" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Autun">bishop of Autun</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Reverianus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, Reverianus was of Italian origin, he was sent by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Felix_I" title="Pope Felix I">Pope Felix I</a><sup id="cite_ref-heiligen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to evangelize the <a href="/wiki/Aedui" title="Aedui">Aedui</a>, a Gallic people of <a href="/wiki/Gallia_Lugdunensis" title="Gallia Lugdunensis">Gallia Lugdunensis</a> and is considered the "apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Morvan" title="Morvan">Morvan</a>.”
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Usuard" title="Usuard">Usuard</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Holmes_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holmes-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> he was persecuted during the reign of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Aurelian" title="Aurelian">Aurelian</a>.  According to tradition, Reverianus' preaching and conversions came to the attention of Emperor Aurelian, who was visiting the province of <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a> at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-crosnier_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crosnier-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
Also executed with Reverianus were his companion, named St. <b>Paul</b> or <b>Paulus of Autun</b>, along with 10 other followers.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-crosnier_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crosnier-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to one source, “the name [Reverianus] is not mentioned by earlier writers, and the first bishop of Autun seems to have been <a href="/wiki/Reticius" title="Reticius">Reticius</a>, who was present at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Rome" title="Synod of Rome">Synod of Rome</a> A.D. 313, and at <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Arles_(314)" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Arles (314)">Arles</a> in the following year."<sup id="cite_ref-Holmes_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holmes-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Reverianus may have been revered as a bishop because he was the leader of this group of preachers.<sup id="cite_ref-heiligen_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He may not have been executed at Autun itself but at the site of <a href="/wiki/Saint-R%C3%A9v%C3%A9rien" title="Saint-Révérien">the town named after him</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-heiligen_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to a tradition at <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a>, Reverianus was executed outside of the city walls of that city.<sup id="cite_ref-crosnier_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crosnier-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Reverianus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the 4th century, with freedom of worship granted to Christians by the <a href="/wiki/Edict_of_Milan" title="Edict of Milan">Edict of Milan</a> (313 AD), a monastic cell was built over the graves of these martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This monastic cell became in 866 the oratory of St. Révérien, which was granted by <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Fat" title="Charles the Fat">Charles the Fat</a> to the chapter of Nevers.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It had been previously under the jurisdiction of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictines</a> of the Abbey of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Martin_d%27Autun&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Martin d&#39;Autun (page does not exist)">Saint Martin d'Autun</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1076, this monastery became affiliated with <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Cluny" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Cluny">Abbey of Cluny</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In the 13th century, the present church of St. Révérien was built according to the Cluniac style.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another tradition celebrated by the Abbey of Notre-Dame de Nevers held that Reverianus died with his companions near this site,<sup id="cite_ref-crosnier_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crosnier-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> near the spring that still bears his name.
</p><p>Of his relics, only a piece of Reverianus' head have been preserved.<sup id="cite_ref-heiligen_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The relic was kept at <a href="/wiki/Villy-le-Moutier" title="Villy-le-Moutier">Villy-le-Moutier</a> (in the Canton of <a href="/wiki/Nuits-Saint-Georges" title="Nuits-Saint-Georges">Nuits-Saint-Georges</a>), a village to the east of the city of <a href="/wiki/Beaune" title="Beaune">Beaune</a>.  Until 1836, Reverianus' head was carried in procession to the church of Saint-Nicolas de Beaune during times of great drought.  Crowds from the city and the surrounding areas would pray for rain.<sup id="cite_ref-memoires_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-memoires-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
