<p><b>John of Parma</b> (c. 1209 – 19 March 1289) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, who served as one of the first <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Ministers General</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Order of Friars Minor</a> (1247–1257). He was also a noted <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theologian</a> of the period.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Parma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was born about 1209<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in the <a href="/wiki/Medieval_commune" title="Medieval commune">medieval commune</a> of <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a> in the northern Italian region of <a href="/wiki/Emilia-Romagna" title="Emilia-Romagna">Emilia-Romagna</a>; his family name was probably Buralli. Educated by an uncle, <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> of the Church of St. Lazarus at Parma, his progress in learning was such that he quickly became a teacher of philosophy (<i>magister logicæ</i>). When and where he entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> (commonly called the "Franciscans"), the old sources do not say. <a href="/wiki/Ireneo_Aff%C3%B2" title="Ireneo Affò">Affò</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> assigns 1233 as the year, and Parma as the probable place. <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, he taught <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">University of Bologna</a> and the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Naples">University of Naples</a>, and finally taught the <i><a href="/wiki/Sentences" title="Sentences">Sentences</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Peter_Lombard" title="Peter Lombard">Peter Lombard</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>. He assisted at the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="First Council of Lyons">First Council of Lyons</a> in 1245, representing the current Minister General, <a href="/wiki/Crescentius_of_Jesi" title="Crescentius of Jesi">Crescentius of Jesi</a>, who was too ill to attend.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Minister_General">Minister General</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Parma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Minister General">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a> of the Order held at Lyons in July 1247, John was elected Minister General, at the suggestion of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a>, who had been impressed by him during his service at the Council of Lyons two years earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was elected with the support of the rigorist branch of the Order (known as the <a href="/wiki/Fraticelli" title="Fraticelli">Fraticelli</a>), which office he held till 2 February 1257. The desire for the original fervor of the Order animated the new Minister General and of his purposes for the full observance of the <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">Rule</a> of St. Francis, reflects from the joy recorded by <a href="/wiki/Angelus_Clarenus" class="mw-redirect" title="Angelus Clarenus">Angelus Clarenus</a> among the survivors of Francis's first companions at his election—though <a href="/wiki/Giles_of_Assisi" title="Giles of Assisi">Giles of Assisi</a>'s words sound somewhat pessimistic: "Welcome, Father, but you come late".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>John set to work immediately. Wishing to know personally the state of the Order, he began visiting every community of friars. His first visit was to England, where he was extremely satisfied, and where he was received by King <a href="/wiki/Henry_III_of_England" title="Henry III of England">Henry III of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> At <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a> in France, <a href="/wiki/King_Louis_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="King Louis IX">King Louis IX</a> (later a member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>) honored with his presence the Provincial Chapter held by John. 
</p><p>Having visited the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">Provinces</a> of <a href="/wiki/Burgundy_(historical_region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy (historical region)">Burgundy</a> and of <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a>, he set out in September 1248, for Spain, whence Pope Innocent recalled him to entrust him with an embassy to the East. Before departing, John appears to have held the General Chapter of Metz in 1249 (others put it after the embassy, 1251). It was at this Chapter that John refused to draw up new statutes to avoid overburdening the friars.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Only some new rubrics were promulgated, which in a later chapter in Genoa (1254) were included in the official ceremonial of the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The object of John's embassy to the East was reunion with the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, whose representatives he met at <a href="/wiki/Nice" title="Nice">Nice</a>, and who saluted him as an "angel of peace". John's mission bore no immediate fruit, though it may have prepared the way for the union decreed at the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyon" title="Second Council of Lyon">Council of Lyons</a> in 1274. 
</p><p>In his generalate occurred also the famous dispute between the <a href="/wiki/Mendicants" class="mw-redirect" title="Mendicants">mendicants</a> and the <a href="/wiki/College_of_Sorbonne" title="College of Sorbonne">Sorbonne University of Paris</a>. According to Salimbene,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> John went to Paris (probably in 1253), and, by his mild yet strenuous arguments, strove to secure peace. It was in connection with this attack on the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a> and the Franciscans that John of Parma and <a href="/wiki/Humbert_of_Romans" title="Humbert of Romans">Humbert of Romans</a>, <a href="/wiki/Master_General_of_the_Dominicans" class="mw-redirect" title="Master General of the Dominicans">Master General of the Dominicans</a>, published at Milan in 1255 a letter recommending peace and harmony between the two Orders (text in Wadding, 111, 380). In the "Introductorius in Evangelium Æternum" of <a href="/wiki/Gerard_of_S._Donnino" class="mw-redirect" title="Gerard of S. Donnino">Gerard of S. Donnino</a> (1254), John's friend, Humbert, was denounced by the professors of Paris and condemned by a commission at Anagni in 1256;<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> John himself was in some way compromised—a circumstance which, combined with others, finally brought about the end of his generalate. He convened a General chapter at Rome on 2 February 1257. If Peregrinus of Bologna<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> is correct, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a> secretly intimated to John that he should resign, and decline reelection should it be offered him, while Salimbene<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> insists that John resigned of his own free will. The pope may have exerted some pressure on John, who was only too glad to resign, seeing himself unable to promote henceforth the good of the Order. Questioned as to the choice of a successor, he proposed <a href="/wiki/Bonaventure" title="Bonaventure">Bonaventure</a>, who had succeeded him as professor at Paris. 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Parma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>John retired to the <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> at the famed village of <a href="/wiki/Greccio" title="Greccio">Greccio</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Rieti" title="Rieti">Rieti</a>, memorable for the <a href="/wiki/Nativity_scene" title="Nativity scene">Nativity scene</a> first introduced there by <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>. There he lived in voluntary exile and complete solitude; his cell near a rock is still shown. But another trial awaited him. Accused of <a href="/wiki/Joachimism" class="mw-redirect" title="Joachimism">Joachimism</a>, he was submitted to a canonical process at <a href="/wiki/Citt%C3%A1_della_Pieve" class="mw-redirect" title="Cittá della Pieve">Cittá della Pieve</a> (in Umbria), reportedly presided over by <a href="/wiki/Bonaventure" title="Bonaventure">Bonaventure</a> and Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Gaetano_Orsini_(died_1335)" title="Giovanni Gaetano Orsini (died 1335)">Giovanni Gaetano Orsini</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_protector" title="Cardinal protector">Cardinal protector</a> of the Order. The mention of this cardinal as protector brings us to a chronological difficulty, overlooked by writers who assign the process against John to 1257; for Alexander IV (1254–61) retained the protectorship<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and Orsini became protector, at the earliest, at the end of 1261.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Angelus_Clarenus" class="mw-redirect" title="Angelus Clarenus">Angelus Clarenus</a> tells us that the concealed motive of this process was John's attachment to the literal observance of the Rule; the accusation of <a href="/wiki/Joachimism" class="mw-redirect" title="Joachimism">Joachimism</a>, against which he professed his Catholic faith, being only a pretext. Other sources, however,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> speak of retractation. Clarenus relates that John would have been condemned had it not been for the powerful intervention of Innocent IV's nephew, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Ottoboni_Fioschi" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottoboni Fioschi">Ottoboni Fioschi</a>, later Pope Hadrian V.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> John certainly did not profess the <a href="/wiki/Dogma" title="Dogma">dogmatic</a> errors of Joachimism, though he may have held some of its <a href="/wiki/Apocalypticism" title="Apocalypticism">apocalyptic</a> ideas. 
</p><p>Upon his acquittal, he returned to Greccio and continued his life of prayer and work. It was there, it is said, that an angel once served his Mass,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and that in 1285 he received the visit of <a href="/wiki/Ubertin_of_Casale" class="mw-redirect" title="Ubertin of Casale">Ubertin of Casale</a>, who has left an account of this meeting.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Hearing that the Orthodox were abandoning the union agreed upon in 1274, John, now 80 years old, desired to use his last energies in the cause of Christian unity. He obtained the permission of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicolas_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Nicolas IV">Pope Nicolas IV</a> to go to Greece, but reached only as far as <a href="/wiki/Camerino" title="Camerino">Camerino</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Ancona" title="March of Ancona">March of Ancona</a>, where he died in the local friary on 19 March 1289. 
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> in 1777; his <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated by the Friars Minor on 20 March.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Parma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>With the exception of his letters, scarcely any literary work can, with surety, be attributed to John. 
</p><p>He is certainly not the author of the "Introductorius in Evangel. Æternum", nor of the "Visio Fratris Johannis de Parma".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>With more probability we can attribute to John the "Dialogus de vitia SS. Fratrum Minorum", partly edited by L. Lemmens, O.F.M. (Rome, 1902). The "Chronicle of the XXIV Generals"<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> ascribes to John the allegoric treatise on poverty: "Sacrum Commercium B. Francisci cum Domina Paupertate" (ed. Milan, 1539), edited by Ed. d'Alençon (Paris and Rome, 1900), who ascribes it (without sufficient reason) to John Parent. Carmichael has translated this edition: "The Lady Poverty, a thirteenth-century allegory" (London, 1901); another English translation is by Rawnsly (London, 1904); a good introduction and abridged version is given by Macdonell, "Sons of Francis", 189-213. 
</p><p>Other works are mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Sbaralea" class="mw-redirect" title="Sbaralea">Sbaralea</a>, "Suppl. ad Script." (Rome, 1806), 398.
</p>
