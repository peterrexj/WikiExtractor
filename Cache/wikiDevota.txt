<p><b>Devota</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Sainte Dévote</i>; died ca. 303 AD) is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Corsica" title="Corsica">Corsica</a> and <a href="/wiki/Monaco" title="Monaco">Monaco</a>.  She was killed during the persecutions of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperors</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>. She is sometimes identified with another Corsican saint named <a href="/wiki/Julia_of_Corsica" title="Julia of Corsica">Julia</a>, who was described in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> as <i>Deo devota</i> ("devoted to God").  The description was misinterpreted as a <a href="/wiki/Proper_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Proper name">proper name</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The legend connected with her is similar to those told of other saints of the region, such as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Reparata" title="Saint Reparata">Reparata</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Torpes_of_Pisa" title="Torpes of Pisa">Torpes of Pisa</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition holds that she was a <a href="/wiki/Corsican_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Corsican people">Corsican</a> woman born around 283 AD at <a href="/wiki/Mariana,_Corsica" title="Mariana, Corsica">Mariana</a>.  A young virgin, she had decided to devote herself fully to the service of God. Devota was part the household of senator Eutychius. During the Diocletian persecution, the prefect Barbarus arrived in Corsica with a fleet and when he learned that the senator was harboring a Christian in his  house, demanded that she be given up and compelled to perform the requisite sacrifice to the imperial cult. Eutychius refused, and not wishing to confront him directly, Barbarus arranged to have him poisoned. Devota was imprisoned and tortured for her faith.  Her mouth was crushed, and her body was dragged through rocks and <a href="/wiki/Brambles" class="mw-redirect" title="Brambles">brambles</a>.  She was martyred at Mariana by being <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">racked</a> or <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoned</a> to death.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her death, the governor of the province ordered for her body to be burnt to prevent its veneration.  However, it was saved from the flames by Christians.  Her body was placed on a boat bound for <a href="/wiki/Africa_(Roman_province)" title="Africa (Roman province)">Africa</a>.  Gratianus (Graziano), the boat's <a href="/wiki/Harbour_pilot" class="mw-redirect" title="Harbour pilot">pilot</a>; Benedict (Benenato), a priest; and Apollinaris, his deacon; believed it would receive proper Christian burial there.  However, a storm overtook the boat. A dove appeared and guided the boat to present-day Les Gaumates, today part of the Principality of Monaco, where a chapel dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a> stood.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In her honor a chapel was built, which stands in Monaco still.  Traditionally, flowers are said to bloom before their season on January 27, the saint's feast day.
</p>
<h2><span id="Sainte-D.C3.A9vote_Chapel"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainte-Dévote_Chapel">Sainte-Dévote Chapel</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainte-Dévote Chapel">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Sainte-D%C3%A9vote_Chapel" title="Sainte-Dévote Chapel">Sainte-Dévote Chapel</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg/220px-%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg/330px-%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg/440px-%C3%89glise_de_Saint-D%C3%A9vote_Monaco_IMG_1204.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3718" data-file-height="3718" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Sainte-D%C3%A9vote_Chapel" title="Sainte-Dévote Chapel">Sainte-Dévote Chapel</a> in <a href="/wiki/Monaco" title="Monaco">Monaco</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Sainte-D%C3%A9vote_Chapel" title="Sainte-Dévote Chapel">Sainte-Dévote Chapel</a> is first mentioned about 1070, belonging to the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Pontius_of_Cimiez" title="Pontius of Cimiez">Saint Pons</a>. The chapel was rebuilt and expanded several times.  It became a priory in the 13th century and in 1536 was acquired by <a href="/wiki/Honor%C3%A9_I,_Lord_of_Monaco" title="Honoré I, Lord of Monaco">Honoré I, Lord of Monaco</a>. The chapel became the parish church in 1887.<sup id="cite_ref-palais_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-palais-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_in_Monaco">Veneration in Monaco</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration in Monaco">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The "Legend of Saint Devota" is one of the Principality of Monaco's oldest traditions – it has influenced national culture in fields as diverse as religion, folklore and popular beliefs, history, literature, the arts, painting, music, coins and stamps. This legend, passed on through a medieval document, holds a special place in the heart of Monaco's people and over the centuries has been awarded a permanent place in the city's history.<sup id="cite_ref-Prince_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prince-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
Reports of miracles soon sprung in connection with the tomb.  During incursions by <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moors</a>, Devota's relics were translated to safety at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Cimiez" title="Cimiez">Cimiez</a>.  They were returned to Monaco, to a church restored much later by <a href="/wiki/Antonio_I_of_Monaco" class="mw-redirect" title="Antonio I of Monaco">Antonio I</a>, prince of Monaco.
</p><p>In 1070, Antinope, the captain of a <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florentine</a> ship, attempted to steal the reliquary containing Devota's relics.  The legend states that a violent wind impeded him from escaping with the relics.  Arrested, Ugo Grimaldi ordered his ears and nose to be cut off.  Antinope's boat was then burnt on the beach of Monaco.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 16th century, Devota was invoked for protection against the <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoese</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisans</a>.  Their ultimate failure to take away the independence of Monaco was attributed to the divine protection of Devota.  An end to the outbreak of the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">plague</a> in 1631 was attributed to Devota, as well as the expulsion of the <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> on the night of 17 November 1641 -a <a href="/wiki/Galley" title="Galley">galley</a> taken from the Spanish was rebaptized "Devota."  Monégasque <a href="/wiki/Coin" title="Coin">coins</a> bearing the effigy of Devota were issued in the 16th century, beginning in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Honor%C3%A9_II,_Prince_of_Monaco" title="Honoré II, Prince of Monaco">Honoré II, Prince of Monaco</a>. During his reign, Devota became the patroness of Monaco.<sup id="cite_ref-Prince_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prince-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A series of postage stamps bearing Devota's likeness were issued in 1944 as well as 1992.
</p><p>However, as the <a href="/wiki/Sainte-D%C3%A9vote_Chapel" title="Sainte-Dévote Chapel">Sainte-Dévote Chapel</a> lends its name to the first corner of the <a href="/wiki/Circuit_de_Monaco" title="Circuit de Monaco">Circuit de Monaco</a>, one source has commented that "Saint Devota's name is unfortunately heard on the radio waves solely for the <a href="/wiki/Monaco_Grand_Prix" title="Monaco Grand Prix">Monaco's Grand Prix Formula 1</a> for the famous bend of Saint Devota."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Joseph_M%C3%A9ry" title="Joseph Méry">Joseph Méry</a> put the legend of Saint Devota into verse.  The first book written in <a href="/wiki/Monegasque_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Monegasque language">Monegasque language</a> by the Monegasque poet, <a href="/wiki/Louis_Notari" title="Louis Notari">Louis Notari</a>, is entitled <i>A legenda de Santa Devota</i> (The Legend of Saint Devota). It was composed in 1927 and is important because until then the <a href="/wiki/Mon%C3%A9gasque_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Monégasque language">Monégasque language</a>, had been utilized for speech.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg/220px-Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg/330px-Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg/440px-Reliques_de_Sainte_D%C3%A9vote.jpg 2x" data-file-width="850" data-file-height="622" /></a><figcaption>Reliques of Saint Devota.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Her cult became important to Monaco and the Grimaldis.  Since 1874 it has been the custom that on the evening before her feast day a symbolic fishing boat is brought in procession into Port-Hercule and set alight outside the Église Sainte Dévote. This is followed by a fireworks display that lights up Port-Hercule.<sup id="cite_ref-Prince_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prince-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On January 27, Mass is celebrated in the Cathedral of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception attended by Prince Albert II and Princess Charlene. It is followed by a Solemn Procession of the Relics, to request protection for the Royal Family and the Principality. Receptions in the <a href="/wiki/Monte_Carlo_Opera_House" class="mw-redirect" title="Monte Carlo Opera House">Monte Carlo Opera House</a> also take place.  <a href="/wiki/Albert_II,_Prince_of_Monaco" title="Albert II, Prince of Monaco">Albert II, Prince of Monaco</a> participates in this ceremony.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 2014 the Monegasque Rugby Federation, in association with the Princess Charlene of Monaco Foundation, organized the Saint Devota Rugby Challenge. Including teams from Spain and Italy, the free program includes educational workshops and circuits for disabled children, to introduce them to the game.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Diffusion_of_cult_in_Corsica">Diffusion of cult in Corsica</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Diffusion of cult in Corsica">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the 1687, some of Devota's relics were transported from Monaco to the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> church of Sant'Ignazio on the island. Between 1727 and 1751, attempts were made to receive from the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a> official recognition of Devota as special patroness of Corsica. These were refused because there was scant evidence of her existence. However, the diffusion of her cult continued and <a href="/wiki/Pasquale_Paoli" title="Pasquale Paoli">Pasquale Paoli</a> created a short-lived "Order of Saint Devota" in 1757 during the movement for Corsican independence.
</p><p>In 1820, the first bishop of the diocese of Corsica proclaimed both Saint Devota and Saint Julia to be the principal patron saints of Corsica.  In 1893, a church was dedicated to Saint Devota on the island.  Her cult was officially approved in 1984 by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ajaccio" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ajaccio">bishop of Ajaccio</a>.  The new <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> commemorates her on January 27, with the description, "At Mariana, on the island of Corsica, in memory of Saint Devota, virgin and martyr."<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Devota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Devota was recently selected as the main motif of a high value collector's coin; the €5 silver <a href="/wiki/Euro_gold_and_silver_commemorative_coins_(Monaco)#2004_Coinage" title="Euro gold and silver commemorative coins (Monaco)">1700th anniversary of Saint Devota's death commemorative coin</a>, minted in 2004. On the reverse of the coin, a statue of the saint can be seen. Next to it, a representation of the dove guiding the boat to the coast of Monaco, as recorded in the legend, can be observed.
</p>
