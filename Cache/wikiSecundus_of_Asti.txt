<p><b>Secundus of Asti</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Secondo di Asti</i>) (died c. 119) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is generally celebrated on March 29. Until the 15th century it was celebrated at <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a> on March 30, but it is now celebrated there on the first Tuesday in May.  He was a historical figure who was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> at Asti under <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a>.  He is said to have been a <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(post-Roman_Europe)" title="Patrician (post-Roman Europe)">patrician</a> of Asti and a <a href="/wiki/Subaltern_(military)" title="Subaltern (military)">subaltern</a> officer in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Army">imperial army</a>.  It is known that a church was dedicated to him in the area as early as the 9th century.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>   
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Secundus_of_Asti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Later legends made Secundus a member of the <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a>.  A more elaborate legend states that he was a young man of noble lineage who visited the jails of Asti.  Secundus was a friend of Sapricius (Saprizio), <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> of the city.  They traveled together to the city of <a href="/wiki/Tortona" title="Tortona">Tortona</a>, where Secundus met the city's first bishop, <a href="/wiki/Marcian_of_Tortona" title="Marcian of Tortona">Marcian</a>, who was later martyred under Hadrian.  Secundus' meeting with Marcian influenced his decision to become a Christian; his meeting with <a href="/wiki/Faustinus_and_Jovita" title="Faustinus and Jovita">Faustinus and Jovita</a> further influenced his conversion.  His friend Sapricius attempted to make him abjure his newfound faith.  Secundus refused, and was tortured and decapitated.
</p>
