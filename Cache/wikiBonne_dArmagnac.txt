<p><b>Bonne of Armagnac</b> (19 February 1399 – 1430/35) was the eldest daughter of <a href="/wiki/Bernard_VII,_Count_of_Armagnac" title="Bernard VII, Count of Armagnac">Bernard VII, Count of Armagnac</a> and <a href="/wiki/Constable_of_France" title="Constable of France">Constable of France</a>, and his wife <a href="/wiki/Bonne_of_Berry" title="Bonne of Berry">Bonne of Berry</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bonne_of_Armagnac&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 15 April 1410 at the age of 11, she married <a href="/wiki/Charles,_Duke_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Charles, Duke of Orléans">Charles, Duke of Orléans</a> (left an orphan by his father Louis's <a href="/wiki/Civil_war_between_the_Armagnacs_and_the_Burgundians#Outbreak" class="mw-redirect" title="Civil war between the Armagnacs and the Burgundians">assassination</a> in 1407).<sup id="cite_ref-N196_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N196-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This marriage made the constable not only Charles's father-in-law but also his natural defender. The <a href="/wiki/Armagnac_(party)" title="Armagnac (party)">Orléans</a> party, left without a leader by Louis's death, thus became the Armagnac party, the name it held up to the treaty of Arras in 1435.<sup id="cite_ref-N196_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N196-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following the French defeat at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt" title="Battle of Agincourt">Battle of Agincourt</a> on 25 October 1415, Charles was taken prisoner by the English. Bonne had not borne any children prior to his imprisonment. She died sometime between 1430 and 1435 while her husband was still in captivity.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_literature_and_art">In literature and art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bonne_of_Armagnac&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: In literature and art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bonne appears in the critically acclaimed historical novel <i>Het woud der verwachting</i> (1949) by <a href="/wiki/Hella_Haasse" title="Hella Haasse">Hella Haasse</a>, (translated into English in 1989 under the title "<i><a href="/wiki/In_a_Dark_Wood_Wandering" title="In a Dark Wood Wandering">In a Dark Wood Wandering</a></i>"). The novel portrays the life of Bonne's husband Charles. 
</p><p>Charles and Bonne's marriage at the <a href="/wiki/Chateau_de_Dourdan" class="mw-redirect" title="Chateau de Dourdan">Chateau de Dourdan</a> is thought<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span title="The material near this tag may use weasel words or too-vague attribution. (April 2019)">by whom?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> to be depicted in the elaborate <a href="/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript" title="Illuminated manuscript">illuminated manuscript</a> entitled <i><a href="/wiki/Tr%C3%A8s_Riches_Heures_du_duc_de_Berry" class="mw-redirect" title="Très Riches Heures du duc de Berry">Très Riches Heures du duc de Berry</a></i> (<i>Very Rich Hours of the Duke of Berry</i>) in the illustration for April. 
</p>
