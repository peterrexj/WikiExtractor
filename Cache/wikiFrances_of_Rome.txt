<p><b>Frances of Rome</b>, Obl.S.B. (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it"><b>Santa Francesca Romana</b></i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la"><b>Sāncta Francisca Rōmāna</b></i>) (1384 – March 9, 1440) was an Italian Catholic <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a>, organizer of charitable services and a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Oblate" title="Oblate">oblate</a> who founded a religious community of oblates, who share a common life without <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>. She was canonized in 1608.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Frances was born in 1384 in Rome to a wealthy and <a href="/wiki/Aristocracy_(class)" title="Aristocracy (class)">aristocratic</a> couple, Paolo Bussa and Iacobella dei Roffredeschi, in the up-and-coming <a href="/wiki/Rioni_of_Rome" title="Rioni of Rome">district</a> of <a href="/wiki/Parione" title="Parione">Parione</a> and <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">christened</a> in the nearby <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Agnese_in_Agone" title="Sant&#39;Agnese in Agone">Church of St. Agnes</a> on the famed <a href="/wiki/Piazza_Navona" title="Piazza Navona">Piazza Navona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When she was eleven years old, she wanted to be a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, but, at about the age of twelve, her parents forced her to marry Lorenzo Ponziani, commander of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">papal</a> troops of Rome and member of an extremely wealthy family. Although the marriage had been arranged, it was a happy one, lasting for forty years.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg/220px-Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="193" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg/330px-Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg/440px-Antoniazzo_Romano_%E2%80%93_Santa_Francesca_Romana_e_il_miracolo_del_grano.jpg 2x" data-file-width="778" data-file-height="684" /></a><figcaption>Santa Francesca Romana e il miracolo del grano</figcaption></figure>
<p>With her sister-in-law Vannozza, Frances visited the poor and took care of the sick, inspiring other wealthy women of the city to do the same. Soon after her marriage, Frances fell seriously ill. Her husband called a man in who dabbled in magic, but Frances drove him away, saying, “Begone, thou servant of Satan, nor ever venture into these walls again!” and later recounted to Vannozza that St. Alexis had appeared to her and cured her.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When her mother-in-law died, Frances became the mistress of the household. During a time of flood and famine, she turned part of the family's country estate into a hospital<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and distributed food and clothing to the poor. According to one account, her father-in-law was so angry that he took away from her the keys to the supply rooms; but gave them back when he saw that the corn bin and wine barrel were replenished after Frances finished praying.
</p><p>During the wars between the pope in Rome and various <a href="/wiki/Anti-popes" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-popes">anti-popes</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> of the Catholic Church, Lorenzo served the former. According to one story, their son Battista was to be delivered as a hostage to the commander of the Neapolitan troops. Obeying this order on the command of her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a>, Frances took her son to the <a href="/wiki/Campidoglio" class="mw-redirect" title="Campidoglio">Campidoglio</a>. On the way, she stopped in the Church of the <a href="/wiki/Aracoeli" class="mw-redirect" title="Aracoeli">Aracoeli</a> located there and entrusted her son's life to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Mother" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Mother">Blessed Mother</a>. When they arrived at the appointed site, the soldiers tried to put her son on a horse to transport him to captivity. However, the horse refused to move despite heavy whipping. The soldiers saw the hand of God in this and returned the boy to his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-lady_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lady-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg/220px-Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg/330px-Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg/440px-Confessio_of_Santa_Francesca_Romana_05.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4864" data-file-height="3648" /></a><figcaption>Sculpture by Giosuè Meli (1866)</figcaption></figure>
<p>During a period of forced exile, much of Lorenzo's property and possessions were destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-paoli_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paoli-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In the course of one occupation of Rome by <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Neapolitan</a> forces in the early part of the century, he was wounded so severely that he never fully recovered. Frances nursed him throughout the rest of his life.
</p><p>Frances experienced other sorrows during her marriage to Lorenzo. They lost two children to the plague. Chaos ruled the city in that period of neglect by the pope and the ongoing warfare between him and the various forces competing for power on the Italian peninsula devastated the city. The city of Rome was largely in ruins, and wolves were known to enter the streets. Frances again opened her home as a hospital and drove her wagon through the countryside to collect wood for fire and herbs for medicine.<sup id="cite_ref-Loyola_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loyola-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It is said she had the gift of healing, and over 60 cases were attested to during the canonization proceedings.<sup id="cite_ref-lady_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lady-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a>, "With her husband's consent St. Frances practiced <a href="/wiki/Josephite_marriage" title="Josephite marriage">continence</a>, and advanced in a life of <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplation</a>. Her visions often assumed the form of drama enacted for her by heavenly personages. She had the <a href="/wiki/Gift_of_miracles" title="Gift of miracles">gift of miracles</a> and ecstasy, as well as the bodily vision of her <a href="/wiki/Guardian_angel_(spirit)" class="mw-redirect" title="Guardian angel (spirit)">guardian angel</a>, had revelations concerning <a href="/wiki/Purgatory" title="Purgatory">Purgatory</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hell" title="Hell">Hell</a>, and foretold the ending of the Western Schism. She could read the secrets of consciences and detect plots of <a href="/wiki/Evil" title="Evil">diabolical</a> origin. She was remarkable for her humility and detachment, her obedience and patience".<sup id="cite_ref-paoli_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paoli-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On August 15, 1425, the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption of Mary</a>, she founded the <a href="/wiki/Olivetan" class="mw-redirect" title="Olivetan">Olivetan</a> <a href="/wiki/Oblate" title="Oblate">Oblates</a> of <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Mary</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Confraternity" title="Confraternity">confraternity</a> of pious women, under the authority of the Olivetan <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> of the Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Francesca_Romana,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Francesca Romana, Rome">Santa Maria Nova</a> in Rome but neither cloistered nor bound by formal vows, so they could follow her pattern of combining a life of prayer with answering the needs of their society.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Untitled_(St._Francesca_Romana),_ca._1650.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Untitled_%28St._Francesca_Romana%29%2C_ca._1650.jpg/220px-Untitled_%28St._Francesca_Romana%29%2C_ca._1650.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="180" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Untitled_%28St._Francesca_Romana%29%2C_ca._1650.jpg/330px-Untitled_%28St._Francesca_Romana%29%2C_ca._1650.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Untitled_%28St._Francesca_Romana%29%2C_ca._1650.jpg/440px-Untitled_%28St._Francesca_Romana%29%2C_ca._1650.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="2459" /></a><figcaption>Untitled (St. Francesca Romana), c.&#160;1650</figcaption></figure>
<p>In March 1433, she founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Tor_de_Specchi_Monastery&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Tor de Specchi Monastery (page does not exist)">Tor de' Specchi</a>, near the Campidoglio, to allow for a common life by those members of the confraternity who felt so-called.<sup id="cite_ref-lady_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lady-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This monastery remains the only house of the Institute. That July 4, they received the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> of oblates with private <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>. The community later became known simply as the Oblates of St. Frances of Rome.
</p><p>Frances herself remained in her own home, nursing her husband for the last seven years of his life from wounds he had received in battle. When he died in 1436, she moved into the monastery and became the superior.<sup id="cite_ref-paoli_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paoli-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She died in 1440 and was buried in Santa Maria Nova.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:FrancesRome.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/f/f9/FrancesRome.jpg" decoding="async" width="213" height="361" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="213" data-file-height="361" /></a><figcaption>Frances of Rome accompanied by her guardian angel</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On May 9, 1608, she was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and in the following decades a diligent search was made for her remains, which had been hidden due to the troubled times in which she lived. Her body was found incorrupt some months after her death. Her grave was identified on April 2, 1638, (but this time only the bones remained), and her remains were reburied in the Church of Santa Maria Nova on March 9, 1649, which since then has been her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>. Again, in 1869, her body was <a href="/wiki/Exhumation" class="mw-redirect" title="Exhumation">exhumed</a> and has since then been displayed in a glass coffin for the <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> of the faithful. The Church of Santa Maria Nova is now usually referred to as the Church of St. Frances.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1925, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> declared her the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of automobile drivers because of a legend that an angel used to light the road before her with a lantern when she traveled, keeping her safe from hazards. Within the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine Order</a>, she is honored as a patron saint of all oblates. She is also a patron saint of widows.
</p>
