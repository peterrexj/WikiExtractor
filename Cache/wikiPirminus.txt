<p>Saint <b>Pirmin</b> (latinized <i>Pirminius</i>, born before 700 (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;670</span> according to many sources<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>), died November 3, 753 in <a href="/wiki/Hornbach,_Germany" title="Hornbach, Germany">Hornbach</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-old_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-old-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian-era</a> monk and missionary.
He founded or restored numerous monasteries in <a href="/wiki/Alemannia" class="mw-redirect" title="Alemannia">Alemannia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia" title="Duchy of Swabia">Swabia</a>),  especially in the <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>, along the <a href="/wiki/Upper_Rhine" title="Upper Rhine">Upper Rhine</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Lake_Constance" title="Lake Constance">Lake Constance</a> region.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pirmin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pirmin was probably from the area of <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>, possibly of <a href="/wiki/Visigoths" title="Visigoths">Visigothic</a> origin.<sup id="cite_ref-old_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-old-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Many Visigoths fled to <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Arab_conquest_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Arab conquest of Spain">Arab conquest of Spain</a> at the beginning of the 8th century.<sup id="cite_ref-fletcher_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fletcher-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 718 onwards, he was abbot of the monastery <i>Quortolodora</i> in <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a> (<a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and, together with its pupils, served the church inside the <a href="/wiki/Broch" title="Broch">broch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Het_Steen" title="Het Steen">Het Steen</a>. (In the 12th century, this church was dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Walpurga" title="Saint Walpurga">Saint Walpurga</a>.) After a while Pirmin was invited by count Rohingus to stay at his <i><a href="/wiki/Villa" title="Villa">villa</a></i> in Thommen, near <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Vith" class="mw-redirect" title="Sankt Vith">Sankt Vith</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ardennes" title="Ardennes">Ardennes</a>.
</p><p>Pirmin gained the favour of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a>, mayor of the palace of Francia. He was sent to help rebuild <a href="/wiki/Disentis_Abbey" title="Disentis Abbey">Disentis Abbey</a> in what is today Switzerland.  In 724, he was appointed abbot of Mittelzell Abbey on <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Island" title="Reichenau Island">Reichenau Island</a>, which had earlier founded.<sup id="cite_ref-old_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-old-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Later, for political reasons, he was banished to <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Alsace" title="Duchy of Alsace">Alsace</a>. In 753, he died in <a href="/wiki/Hornbach_Abbey" title="Hornbach Abbey">the abbey at Hornbach</a>, where his body is entombed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_and_other_activities">Missionary and other activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pirmin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Missionary and other activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pirmin's missionary work mainly took place in the <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a> and the upper area of the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a>. Besides actively preaching and converting, he also founded or reformed many monasteries, such as those at <a href="/wiki/Amorbach_Abbey" title="Amorbach Abbey">Amorbach</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gengenbach" title="Gengenbach">Gengenbach</a>, <a href="/wiki/Murbach_Abbey" title="Murbach Abbey">Murbach</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wissembourg" title="Wissembourg">Wissembourg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marmoutier_Abbey_(Alsace)" class="mw-redirect" title="Marmoutier Abbey (Alsace)">Marmoutier</a>, <a href="/wiki/Neuweiler" title="Neuweiler">Neuweiler</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Island" title="Reichenau Island">Reichenau</a>. Pirmin secured endowments from area nobility: <a href="/wiki/Odilo_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Odilo of Bavaria">Odilo of Bavaria</a> financed the foundation of <a href="/wiki/Niederaltaich_Abbey" title="Niederaltaich Abbey">Niederaltaich Abbey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-fletcher_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fletcher-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Werner I of what became the <a href="/wiki/Salian_dynasty" title="Salian dynasty">Salian dynasty</a> endowed the new abbey at <a href="/wiki/Hornbach,_Germany" title="Hornbach, Germany">Hornbach</a>.
</p><p>The most important of Pirmin's books is <i>Dicta Abbatis Pirminii, de Singulis Libris Canonicis Scarapsus</i> ("Words of Abbot Pirminius, extracts from the Single Canonical Books").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The book collects quotations from Church Fathers and scriptures, presumably for use by missionaries,<sup id="cite_ref-old_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-old-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or reading during monastic meals. Written between 710-724, it contains the earliest appearance of the present text of the <a href="/wiki/Apostles%27_Creed" title="Apostles&#39; Creed">Apostles' Creed</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
