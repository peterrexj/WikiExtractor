<p><b>Himerius</b> (<b>Imier</b>, <b>Imerio</b>) <b>of Cremona</b> (d. June 17, c. 560), also known as <b>Himerius of Amelia</b> or <b>Irnerius</b>, was an Italian bishop. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and Christian communities of <a href="/wiki/Western_Rite_Orthodoxy" title="Western Rite Orthodoxy">Western Rite Orthodoxy</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Himerius_of_Cremona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano,_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano%2C_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg/220px-Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano%2C_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="143" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano%2C_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg/330px-Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano%2C_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano%2C_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg/440px-Abbazia_di_San_Giuliano%2C_Monteluco_di_Spoleto.jpg 2x" data-file-width="608" data-file-height="396" /></a><figcaption>Abbazia di San Giuliano, Monteluco di Spoleto</figcaption></figure>
<p>A native of <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>, Himerius was a hermit. He then became a monk, probably in the monastery of San Giuliano in <a href="/wiki/Monteluco" title="Monteluco">Monteluco</a> near Spoleto or in the monastery of San Pietro in Valle in the Valnerina Valley near <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Terni</a> before being elected bishop of <a href="/wiki/Amelia_(Italy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Amelia (Italy)">Amelia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-crypt_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crypt-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is described as having been an extremely austere, ascetic person.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Himerius_of_Cremona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Himerius' name does not appear in the ancient <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a>.  However, his name appears in the <i>Roman Martyrology</i>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sant_imerio_ripalta_vecchia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Sant_imerio_ripalta_vecchia.jpg/200px-Sant_imerio_ripalta_vecchia.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="139" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Sant_imerio_ripalta_vecchia.jpg/300px-Sant_imerio_ripalta_vecchia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ef/Sant_imerio_ripalta_vecchia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="277" /></a><figcaption>The parish church of Sant'Imerio (<a href="/wiki/Madignano" title="Madignano">Madignano</a>)</figcaption></figure>
<p>An abbot named Ambrose (Ambrogio), who lived around the twelfth century, wrote a <i>Vita</i> of Himerius, but only its <a href="/wiki/Prologue" title="Prologue">prologue</a> remains.  A later <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Amelia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Amelia">bishop of Amelia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Maria_Graziani" title="Antonio Maria Graziani">Antonio Maria Graziani</a> (1592–1611), also wrote a biography of the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 965, Himerius' relics were moved from Amelia to <a href="/wiki/Cremona" title="Cremona">Cremona</a> by <a href="/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona" title="Liutprand of Cremona">Liutprand</a> (Liutprando, Luizo), <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cremona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cremona">bishop of Cremona</a> from 962 to 972. <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They were interred in a church that was later destroyed in the earthquake of 1117.  Rediscovered in 1129, they were placed in a <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Miracles were reported at his tomb. A monk named John (Giovanni) wrote, in the 12th century, a collection of miracles performed by the saint after this rediscovery of the relics.  This was composed during the episcopate of the Cremonese bishop Offredo (1168–1185).
</p><p>In 1196, <a href="/wiki/Sicard_of_Cremona" title="Sicard of Cremona">Sicardo</a>, another bishop of Cremona, placed the relics of Himerius in an archway of stone along with those of a martyr named Archelaus (Archelao) and consecrated an altar in their honor.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a relief of "Bishop Himerius distributing alms" by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Amadeo" title="Giovanni Antonio Amadeo">Giovanni Antonio Amadeo</a> in the presbytery of <a href="/wiki/Cremona_Cathedral" title="Cremona Cathedral">Cremona Cathedral</a> at the entrance on the right.<sup id="cite_ref-crypt_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crypt-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The monastery of Sant'Imerio was built in Cremona in 1606 to house members of the <a href="/wiki/Discalced_Carmelites" title="Discalced Carmelites">Order of the Discalced Carmelites</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
