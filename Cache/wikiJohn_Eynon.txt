<p><b>John Eynon</b>, OSB († 1539) was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Order of Saint Benedict</a> who acted as the pastor of the parish of <a href="/wiki/St_Giles%27_Church,_Reading" title="St Giles&#39; Church, Reading">St Giles</a> in <a href="/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire" title="Reading, Berkshire">Reading</a>, England. Copies of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Aske_(political_leader)" title="Robert Aske (political leader)">Robert Aske</a>'s proclamation setting forth the reasons behind the <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage_of_Grace" title="Pilgrimage of Grace">Pilgrimage of Grace</a> had circulated at Reading. Eynon was one of those who had made a copy.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">dissolution of the monasteries</a> under <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">King Henry VIII</a>, Eynon refused to surrender the parish to the King's authorities and was accused of <a href="/wiki/High_treason" class="mw-redirect" title="High treason">high treason</a>. He was executed on 15 November 1539 at the gateway to <a href="/wiki/Reading_Abbey" title="Reading Abbey">Reading Abbey</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Cook_alias_Faringdon" class="mw-redirect" title="Hugh Cook alias Faringdon">Hugh Faringdon</a> and John Rugg. Rugg was a prebendary at Chichester, but had retired to live at Reading Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> All three men were declared to be <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1895.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John Eynon is commemorated by a carved wooden plaque in <a href="/wiki/St_Giles%27_Church,_Reading" title="St Giles&#39; Church, Reading">St Giles' Church</a>, now a <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> parish church, and by a stained glass window in <a href="/wiki/St_James%27_Church,_Reading" class="mw-redirect" title="St James&#39; Church, Reading">St James' Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> parish church that occupies part of the footprint of the now ruined Reading Abbey. He is also depicted on <i>The Martyrdom of Hugh Faringdon, last Abbot of Reading</i>, painted by <a href="/wiki/Harry_Morley" title="Harry Morley">Harry Morley</a> in 1917, and now in the collection of the <a href="/wiki/Museum_of_Reading" class="mw-redirect" title="Museum of Reading">Museum of Reading</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
