<p><b>Jan Wojciech Balicki</b> (25 January 1869 – 15 March 1948) was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" title="Polish people">Polish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> who served as a confessor and <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a> to seminarians in <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> around the time of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>; he also acted in various leadership positions in the education of new priests and was noted for his intellectual gifts.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was beatified during the apostolic visit of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to Poland on 18 August 2002. The pope - before his pontifical elevation - had even written to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1975 beseeching the latter to hold Balicki up as a model for priests of the modern era.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Wojciech_Balicki&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg/160px-Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="213" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg/240px-Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg/320px-Bazylika_archikatedralna_Przemysl_Balicki.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="2048" /></a><figcaption>Tomb.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Jan Wojciech Balicki was born in 1869 in <a href="/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w" title="Rzeszów">Rzeszów</a> to Mikołaja Balicki. During his childhood he underwent studies at school in Rzeszow from 1876 to 1888.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He commenced his studies for the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> in September 1888.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the priesthood on 20 July 1892 and was then positioned in Polna where he was stationed for fifteen months - garnering a reputation as a gifted preacher<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> - until being sent for further studies at the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Gregorian_University" title="Pontifical Gregorian University">Pontifical Gregorian University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> from 1893 until 1897.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It was there that he graduated in philosophical studies and in <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> in addition to receiving a <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorate</a> in his theological studies.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It was also there that he studied and focused on <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a> and during his free time went to the churches of the saints and the <a href="/wiki/12_Disciples" class="mw-redirect" title="12 Disciples">Apostles</a> - he also came to the realization that science could in fact also lead a man to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His return home saw him go back to where he studied as a priest and he taught dogmatic theological studies from 1897. He was made its prefect of studies and held the post until 1897 to 1900 and then later its vice-rector in 1927 until he was made the rector in 1928; he resigned all posts after a bout of ill health in 1934 but continued to live there.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However Balicki continued to serve as a confessor and as a <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a> to seminarians. Among those that he had taught were the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Bb.</a> Władysław Findysz (also serving as his spiritual aide) and Stanislaus Kolodziej.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the second half of February 1948 he was diagnosed with bilateral <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> in its advanced stage. Balicki died in hospital on 15 March 1948 of both tuberculosis and pneumonia and is buried in the <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl_Cathedral" title="Przemyśl Cathedral">Cathedral of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary and St. John the Baptist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Wojciech_Balicki&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 22 December 1975 the Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Krakow" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Krakow">Archbishop of Krakow</a> Karol Józef Wojtyła - the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> - wrote to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> asking the pontiff to hold Balicki up as a model for priests in the modern era.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultivation_of_the_spiritual_life">Cultivation of the spiritual life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Wojciech_Balicki&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cultivation of the spiritual life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Balicki wrote an extensive treatise of mystical prayer that consisted of four degrees:
</p>
<ul><li>Prayer of quiet</li>
<li>Prayer of simple union</li>
<li>Ecstatic union</li>
<li>Perfect union<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>He also provided a list of seven steps for the progress of all people in the spiritual life:
</p>
<ul><li>A serious approach to life</li>
<li>A readiness to be critical of self</li>
<li>Unshakable confidence in prayer</li>
<li>Joy of spirit</li>
<li>Love for suffering</li>
<li>Praise of <a href="/wiki/Divine_mercy" title="Divine mercy">divine mercy</a></li>
<li>Continuous self amendment<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jan_Wojciech_Balicki&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The process for Balicki's beatification commenced in a diocesan process that opened on 22 July 1959 and continued its business until 22 November 1963; the process saw the accumulation of documentation pertaining to his life and also heard from a range of witnesses that could attest to the beatification in the affirmative. His writings received full approval on 3 May 1974 - thus incorporated into the cause - as a means of evaluating his beliefs and to ensure his writings did not conflict with the magisterium of the Universal Church.
</p><p>These processes took place despite the fact that the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> did not grant its formal approval to the initiation of the cause until 19 June 1982 - this act designated Balicki as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. Another process was then held to continue the work of the first and both processes received ratification as having done their work on 21 June 1991 in Rome.
</p><p>The postulation then compiled the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> - containing biographical details and also stating the affirmative reasons for beatification - to Rome for their own investigation in 1992. The theologians in Rome approved the cause on 24 June 1994 and the Congregation for the Causes of Saints followed suit on 8 November 1994. On 15 December 1994 he was proclaimed to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> after Pope John Paul II decreed that Balicki had lived a model life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The approval of a single miracle attributed to Balicki's intercession allowed for the same pontiff to preside over the beatification on 18 August 2002 in Poland.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> assigned to the cause is Father Roman Chowaniec.
</p>
