<p><b> Galdino della Sala</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1096</span> – 18 April 1176), <b>Galdinus</b> or <b>Galdimus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Milanes" class="mw-redirect" title="Milanes">Milanese</a>: <i lang="lmo">Galdin</i>), was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> from <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. He was a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> elevated in 1165 and he also served as <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> from 1166 to his death in 1176.<sup id="cite_ref-Miranda_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miranda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a staunch supporter both of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>, and of Milan and its neighbours in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, in their joint and parallel struggles against the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159%E2%80%931164)" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159–1164)">Antipope Victor IV</a>, supported by <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I_Barbarossa" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I Barbarossa">Frederick I Barbarossa</a>.
</p><p>He is remembered also for his charity in Milan to the poor and to those imprisoned for debt. Alexander III canonized him as a saint of the Roman Catholic Church, and he is a patron of both Lombardy and his old archdiocese.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Galdino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in Milan around 1096<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> into the della Sala family which was considered to be minor nobility of the city.
</p><p>He was a strong supporter of the Roman <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Schism_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Schism (religion)">schism</a> that erupted in 1159 after the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_IV" title="Pope Adrian IV">Pope Adrian IV</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> was the Roman candidate, while <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159-1164)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159-1164)">Antipope Victor IV</a> was supported by <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa" title="Frederick Barbarossa">Frederick Barbarossa</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinals</a>. Galdino's Milanese church supported Alexander III, and Galdino, as archdeacon of the church, took a very public stand. Frederick came to besiege Milan and reduced it within six months.
</p><p>Galdino joined Alexander III in <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> and followed him to <a href="/wiki/Maguelonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Maguelonne">Maguelonne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a>.  He later followed him to <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> upon his return in 1165. When Alexander returned to the papacy in 1165, he named Galdino in the consistory of 15 December as the Cardinal Priest of the <a href="/wiki/Titular_church" title="Titular church">titular church</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Santa Sabina</a>, and a year later made him the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a>.  The year after that, Alexander III made Galdino the apostolic legate for <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>.
</p><p>When the <a href="/wiki/Lombard_League" title="Lombard League">Lombard League</a> expelled Barbarossa, Galdino took possession of his see and began deposing any Lombard priests who were faithful to Victor IV. He consecrated new bishops at <a href="/wiki/Lodi,_Lombardy" title="Lodi, Lombardy">Lodi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alba,_Piedmont" title="Alba, Piedmont">Alba</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cremona" title="Cremona">Cremona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vercelli" title="Vercelli">Vercelli</a>, <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Novara" title="Novara">Novara</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Alessandria" title="Alessandria">Alessandria</a>.
</p><p>On 18 April 1176 Galdino della Sala died in his <a href="/wiki/Pulpit" title="Pulpit">pulpit</a>, having just completed a sermon against the <a href="/wiki/Cathars" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathars">Cathars</a>, who were seen by orthodox Catholics as ‘<a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretics</a>’.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Galdino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pope Alexander III canonized Galdino as a saint of the Roman Catholic Church during his pontificate.
</p><p>His liturgical <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, celebrated particularly in churches which follow the <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite" title="Ambrosian Rite">Ambrosian Rite</a>, is 18 April (the anniversary of his death).
</p>
