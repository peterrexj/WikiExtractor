<p><b>Maximianus of Ravenna</b>, or Maximian (499 &#8211; February 22, 556; feast day formerly February 21) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ravenna" title="Bishop of Ravenna">bishop of Ravenna</a> in Italy.  <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> was then the capital of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>'s territories in Italy, and Maximianus's role may have included secular political functions.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximianus_of_Ravenna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in the <a href="/wiki/Istrian" class="mw-redirect" title="Istrian">Istrian</a> town of Vistar (now Veštar) near Pola (<a href="/wiki/Pula" title="Pula">Pula</a>) in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, Maximianus was consecrated bishop of Ravenna in 546 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">Pope Vigilius</a> in <a href="/wiki/Patras" title="Patras">Patras</a>, Greece. Maximianus was a forty-eight-year-old <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> from Pola when he became the twenty-sixth bishop of Ravenna. According to the ninth-century Ravennate priest <a href="/wiki/Andreas_Agnellus" title="Andreas Agnellus">Andreas Agnellus</a>, Maximianus' flock initially refused his leadership, because he was selected by the emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a> and was not their initial candidate. To a modern art historian <a href="/wiki/Meyer_Schapiro" title="Meyer Schapiro">Meyer Shapiro</a>, "Maximian was "a poor deacon of Pola who rose to a high position through his political adroitness" as a protégé of Justinian I.  He had not been wanted as archbishop by the people of Ravenna, but "by shrewd maneuvers he overcame their opposition, and won their respect by his discretion, generosity, and great enterprises of church building and decoration".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He completed the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Vitale" title="Basilica of San Vitale">Basilica of San Vitale</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Apollinare_in_Classe" class="mw-redirect" title="Sant&#39;Apollinare in Classe">Sant'Apollinare in Classe</a> in Ravenna, and built several other churches, including <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Canneto_(Pula,_Croatia)" title="Santa Maria del Canneto (Pula, Croatia)">Santa Maria del Canneto</a> in his native Istria.
</p><p>Maximianus devoted himself to the revision of <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_book" title="Liturgical book">liturgical books</a> and to the emendation of the Latin text of the Bible, and commissioned a large number of illuminated manuscripts. For the high altar in Ravenna he had a hanging made of the most costly cloth, which was embroidered with a portrayal of the entire life of Jesus. In another hanging he had portraits of all his predecessors embroidered on <a href="/wiki/Gold_ground" title="Gold ground">gold ground</a>.
</p><p>Maximian's most remarkable episcopal furnishing is the <a href="/wiki/Throne_of_Maximian" title="Throne of Maximian">Throne of Maximian</a>, the <i>cathedra</i> of the bishop which was constructed entirely of ivory panels. It was probably carved in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and shipped to Ravenna. It consists of decorative floral panels framing various figured panels, including one with the complex monogram of the bishop.
</p><p>In a famous 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Mosaic" title="Mosaic">mosaic</a> in San Vitale, Maximianus (named above the figure) is with Emperor Justinian and his retinue. The saint holds a <a href="/wiki/Crux_gemmata" title="Crux gemmata">jewelled cross</a> and wears early versions of an <a href="/wiki/Alb" title="Alb">alb</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chasuble" title="Chasuble">chasuble</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>.
</p><p>He is regarded as a saint by both the Catholic and Orthodox churches, though essentially local to Ravenna, where there is a church dedicated to him at Piazza S. Massimiano, Punta Marina, Ravenna, 48020.
</p>
