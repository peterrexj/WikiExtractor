<p><b>Eochaid mac Colla</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 560 – 640), better known as <b>Saint Dallán</b> or <b>Dallán Forgaill</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_Irish_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Irish language">Old Irish</a>: <i lang="sga">Dallán Forchella</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Dallanus Forcellius</i>; <a href="/wiki/Primitive_Irish" title="Primitive Irish">Primitive Irish</a>: <i>Dallagnas Worgēllas</i>), was an early Christian <a href="/wiki/Irish_poet" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish poet">Irish poet</a> and saint known as the writer of the "<i><a href="/wiki/Amra_Coluim_Chille" class="mw-redirect" title="Amra Coluim Chille">Amra Coluim Chille</a></i>" ("Elegy of Saint Columba") and, traditionally, "<i>Rop Tú Mo Baile</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> ("<a href="/wiki/Be_Thou_My_Vision" title="Be Thou My Vision">Be Thou My Vision</a>").
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_history">Personal history</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dall%C3%A1n_Forgaill&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Personal history">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Dallan's given name was <a href="/wiki/Eochaidh" class="mw-redirect" title="Eochaidh">Eochaidh</a> (<a href="/wiki/Old_Irish_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Irish language">Old Irish</a>: <i lang="sga">Eochaid</i>); his father was Colla, a descendant of the legendary High King <a href="/wiki/Colla_Uais" class="mw-redirect" title="Colla Uais">Colla Uais</a>, and his mother was Forgall (Old Irish: <i>Forchella</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His nickname, <i>Dallán</i> ("little blind one"), was earned after he lost his sight,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> reputedly as a result of studying intensively.
</p><p>He was born in Maigen (now <a href="/wiki/Ballyconnell" title="Ballyconnell">Ballyconnell</a>), at the eastern edge of the territory of the <a href="/wiki/Masraige" title="Masraige">Masraige</a> of <a href="/wiki/Magh_Sl%C3%A9cht" title="Magh Slécht">Magh Slécht</a> in the north-west of modern <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a>. He was not a member of the <a href="/wiki/Masraige" title="Masraige">Masraige</a> but belonged to a branch of the <a href="/wiki/Airg%C3%ADalla" title="Airgíalla">Airgíalla</a> called the Fir Lurg, who were in the process of spreading southwards into modern-day <a href="/wiki/County_Fermanagh" title="County Fermanagh">County Fermanagh</a> and <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a>. (The <a href="/wiki/Barony_(Ireland)" title="Barony (Ireland)">Barony</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lurg" title="Lurg">Lurg</a> in the north of County Fermanagh was named after them.)<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was a first cousin of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Mogue" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Mogue">Saint Mogue</a>. (The <i>Life of Máedóc of Ferns</i> says in ch. 72 that Dallán and Máedóc were sons of two brothers and he lived in <a href="/wiki/Kildallan_townland" class="mw-redirect" title="Kildallan townland">Kildallan townland</a>.)<sup id="cite_ref-Leverhulme_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leverhulme-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was also a fourth cousin of <a href="/wiki/Tigernach_of_Clones" title="Tigernach of Clones">Tigernach of Clones</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Amhra Coluim Cille</i>, a <a href="/wiki/Panegyric" title="Panegyric">panegyric</a> on <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a>, written shortly after Columba's death in 597, is his best known work<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and considered "one of the most important poems we have from the early medieval Gaelic world".<sup id="cite_ref-Leverhulme_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leverhulme-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It is reported that after completing the work, Dallan regained his sight. It was claimed that those who recited the praises of Columba from memory would receive the gift of a happy death,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> a custom that was widely abused by those who attempted to rely on their memory rather than a virtuous life.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Donnell_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Donnell-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The "<i>Amhra Coluim Cille</i>" became a popular text for students in Irish monasteries.
</p><p>The "<i>Amra Senáin</i>",<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> a funeral oration in praise of <a href="/wiki/Sen%C3%A1n_mac_Geirrcinn" title="Senán mac Geirrcinn">Senán mac Geirrcinn</a> (Senán of Iniscattery), was said to preserve from blindness those who recited it with devotion.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Donnell_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Donnell-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In c.640 Dallan was visiting his friend Saint Conall Cael at his monastery on <a href="/wiki/Inishkeel" title="Inishkeel">Inishkeel</a> when pirates raided the island monastery. Dallan was reportedly beheaded, and it is said that God reattached his head to his body after he was martyred.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was buried on Iniskeel; his friend Canall Cael was later laid to rest in the same grave.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Donnell_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Donnell-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was acclaimed a saint in the early 11th century, during the reign of the High King of Ireland <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A1el_Sechnaill_mac_Domnaill" title="Máel Sechnaill mac Domnaill">Máel Sechnaill mac Domnaill</a> but was already listed as a saint in the earlier 9th century martyrologies compiled by <a href="/wiki/%C3%93engus_of_Tallaght" title="Óengus of Tallaght">Óengus of Tallaght</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A medieval poem entitled "On the breaking up of a School" composed by Tadhg Og O Huiginn, c.1400, refers to the death of Dallán which caused his school to break up and the students to disperse as they would accept no other master.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In a list of ancient Irish authors contained in the Book of Ballymote, Dallán is called "grandson of testimony".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dall%C3%A1n_Forgaill&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Dallan was a poet, <a href="/wiki/Chief_Ollam_of_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Chief Ollam of Ireland">Chief Ollam of Ireland</a>, as well as a scholar of Latin scriptural learning.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> He helped to reform the Bardic Order at the Convention of Drumceat.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In addition to <i>"<a href="/wiki/Amra" title="Amra">Amra</a> Choluim Chille"</i> and "<i>Amra Senáin"</i>, the following works are attributed to Dallán, although some may be later works by other poets who credited Dallan with authorship in order to make their poems more famous.
</p><p>1. <i>Amra Conall Coel</i> – in praise of St. Conall Coel, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Inishkeel" title="Inishkeel">Inishkeel</a>
</p><p>2. <i>Dubgilla dub-airm n-aisse</i><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>3. <i>Fo réir Coluim cén ad-fías</i><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Churches">Churches</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dall%C3%A1n_Forgaill&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Churches">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although he was not a priest, Dallán founded several churches throughout Ireland, such as <a href="/wiki/Kildallan" title="Kildallan">Kildallan</a> in County Cavan, <a href="/wiki/Disert,_Tullyhunco" title="Disert, Tullyhunco">Disert, Tullyhunco</a> in County Cavan, <a href="/wiki/Kildallan,_Westmeath" title="Kildallan, Westmeath">Kildallan, Westmeath</a>, <a href="/wiki/Burnchurch" title="Burnchurch">Burnchurch</a> in County Kilkenny, <a href="/wiki/Killallon" title="Killallon">Killallon</a> in County Meath, <a href="/wiki/Clonallan" title="Clonallan">Clonallan</a> in County Down and <a href="/wiki/Tullygallan" title="Tullygallan">Tullygallan</a> in County Donegal. He probably did this out of his friendship for the clergy and perhaps to ensure Masses for his soul. Because of this he was known as <i>Forgaill Cille</i> in medieval texts, meaning 'Forgaill of the Churches'.
