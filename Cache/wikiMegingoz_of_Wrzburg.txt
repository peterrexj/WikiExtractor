					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Megingoz</b> (or Megingod, Megingaud, Megingold, Mengold) was the second <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_W%C3%BCrzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Würzburg">bishop of Würzburg</a> from 753 until his retirement in 768.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005274_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005274-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Three manuscripts emanating from the <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg_Cathedral" title="Würzburg Cathedral">scriptorium of Würzburg</a> can be dated to his episcopate. At that time, Würzburg was still part of the circle of German monasteries founded by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick1983204_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick1983204-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His successor was <a href="/wiki/Berowulf" title="Berowulf">Berowulf</a>, who brought the diocese into closer relations with the ruling <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty" title="Carolingian dynasty">Carolingian dynasty</a>.
</p><p>Megingoz and Boniface's successor, <a href="/wiki/Lullus" title="Lullus">Lullus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">Archbishop of Mainz</a>, together commissioned <a href="/wiki/Willibald" title="Willibald">Willibald</a> to write the <i>Vita Bonifatii</i> ("Life of Boniface"), the earliest biography of the missionary bishop. Willibald claims in his preface to have relied in part on Megingoz's firsthand testimony. Boniface's mentions a deacon named Megingoz in a letter of 737/738 concerning the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Fritzlar" title="Fritzlar">Fritzlar</a>, where Megingoz was probably a monk.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBoniface197667_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoniface197667-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Megingoz was also a witness, along with Lullus, of the founding of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Fulda" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Fulda">abbey of Fulda</a>.
</p><p>Three letters of Megingoz to Lullus survive. They show him to have been concerned particularly with the permissibility of divorce in cases of adultery or forced marriage. He apparently found <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> inconsistent on the matter, but Lullus' response is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005262_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005262-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In one letter he even asked Lullus to help discourage a relative of his from entering a monastery, because he believed his relative did not have the required constancy (<i>stabilitas</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005263_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005263-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Liudger" class="mw-redirect" title="Liudger">Liudger</a>'s <i>Vita Gregorii</i> of <i>c.</i> 800, Megingoz is described as <i>venerabilis pater</i>, "venerable father".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005273_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmer2005273-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
