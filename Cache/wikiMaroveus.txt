					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Maroveus</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Marovée</i>) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Poitiers">bishop of Poitiers</a> in the late sixth century, between 565×573 and 590×594.
</p><p>Maroveus's predecessor, Pascentius, took over around 561 during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Charibert_I" title="Charibert I">Charibert I</a>, but the date of the end of his episcopate is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFavreau1988341_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFavreau1988341-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPon198820–21_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPon198820–21-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Pascentius was still bishop in 565, when <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a> dedicated his <i>Life</i> of <a href="/wiki/Hilary_of_Poitiers" title="Hilary of Poitiers">Hilary of Poitiers</a> to him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuchesne191083_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuchesne191083-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Maroveus became bishop before 573, while <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_I" title="Sigebert I">Sigebert I</a> was king.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFavreau1988341_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFavreau1988341-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPon198820–21_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPon198820–21-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His episcopate for the period 585–590 is well known because <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>'s <i>History of the Franks</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuchesne191083_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuchesne191083-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Gregory of Tours, in 585, King <a href="/wiki/Guntram" title="Guntram">Guntram</a> demanded the submission of Poitiers, which he claimed had broken its oath to him. Maroveus declined on behalf of the people, whereupon the region was ravaged. When Guntram's envoys again demanded submission, Maroveus melted down a chalice to make coin to pay them off.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;VII.24_(pp._406–407)_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;VII.24_(pp._406–407)-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMacGonagle29_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacGonagle29-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maroveus was on poor terms with Queen <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Radegund</a> and her religious foundation in Poitiers, <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey_(Poitiers)" title="Holy Cross Abbey (Poitiers)">Holy Cross Abbey</a>. He refused to <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">formally deposit</a> the relic of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a> which she had acquired in the abbey and did not attend the ceremony when it was performed by <a href="/wiki/Eufronius" title="Eufronius">Eufronius</a>. He refused to officiate or even attend her funeral in 587, which was officiated instead by Gregory of Tours.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.40_(pp._529–531)_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.40_(pp._529–531)-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood199473–74,_138_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood199473–74,_138-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His attitude probably stemmed from a belief that the abbey and its relic challenged his authority as bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood199473–74,_138_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood199473–74,_138-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Radegund's death, according to Gregory of Tours, Maroveus had his authority over the abbey confirmed by King <a href="/wiki/Childebert_II" title="Childebert II">Childebert II</a>, but in 589, led by two royal women, <a href="/wiki/Basina,_daughter_of_Chilperic_I" title="Basina, daughter of Chilperic I">Basina</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Clotilda,_daughter_of_Charibert_I&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Clotilda, daughter of Charibert I (page does not exist)">Clotild</a>, the nuns rebelled.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.40_(pp._529–531)_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.40_(pp._529–531)-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> When Maroveus gathered his fellow bishops <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gundegisel&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gundegisel (page does not exist)">Gundegisel of Bordeaux</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicasius_(bishop_of_Angoul%C3%AAme)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Nicasius (bishop of Angoulême) (page does not exist)">Nicasius of Angoulême</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Safarius&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Safarius (page does not exist)">Safarius of Périgueux</a> in the <a href="/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Hilaire-le-Grand" title="Église Saint-Hilaire-le-Grand">church of Saint Hilary</a> in Poitiers in order to censure the nuns, the bishops were attacked by a mob and forced to disperse.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.41_(p._532)_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.41_(p._532)-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> When Clotild imprisoned the Abbess Leubovera in 590, Maroveus threatened to "rouse the townsfolk and free" her, but in the event she was rescued by a royal official, Flavianus.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;X.15_(p._568)_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;X.15_(p._568)-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Gregory of Tours, around 589, when the burden of taxation at Poitiers was falling heavily on the poor because of outdated tax registers, Maroveus successfully pleaded for a reassessment from Childebert II, bringing much relief on the poor of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.30_(p._515)_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.30_(p._515)-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood199462_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood199462-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Around the same time, Gregory and Maroveus were charged by Childebert to mediate between <a href="/w/index.php?title=Berthegund&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Berthegund (page does not exist)">Berthegund</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingitrude&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ingitrude (page does not exist)">Ingitrude</a>, but while the former came before the bishops in <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>, the latter refused.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.33_(p._521)_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTE&#39;&#39;HF&#39;&#39;IX.33_(p._521)-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maroveus seem to have been succeeded as bishop around 590 by Plato. He was certainly gone by 594.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFavreau1988341_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFavreau1988341-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPon198820–21_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPon198820–21-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
