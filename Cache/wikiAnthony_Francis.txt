<p>The <b>Cuncolim Massacre</b> or <b>Cuncolim Revolt</b> was an incident that involved the massacre and <a href="/wiki/Mutilation" title="Mutilation">mutilation</a> of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">priests</a> and civilians by <a href="/wiki/Hindu" class="mw-redirect" title="Hindu">Hindu</a> chieftains in the <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_Goa" class="mw-redirect" title="Portuguese Goa">Portuguese Goa</a> village of <a href="/wiki/Cuncolim" title="Cuncolim">Cuncolim</a> on Monday, 15 July 1583.
</p><p>Five <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> priests along with one <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_people" title="Portuguese people">Portuguese</a> civilian and 14 <a href="/wiki/Goan_Catholics" title="Goan Catholics">Goan Catholics</a> were killed in the incident.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The local Portuguese garrison retaliated by executing the village chieftains involved, and destroying the economic infrastructure of Cuncolim.<sup id="cite_ref-merinews_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merinews-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_(1635)_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_%281635%29_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png/250px-Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_%281635%29_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png" decoding="async" width="250" height="239" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_%281635%29_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png/375px-Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_%281635%29_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_%281635%29_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png/500px-Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_%281635%29_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png 2x" data-file-width="1340" data-file-height="1280" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Francis_Xavier_taking_leave_of_King_John_III_(1635)_-_Jos%C3%A9_Avelar_Rebelo.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting showing Jesuit missionary <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a> taking leave of <a href="/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal" title="John III of Portugal">John III of Portugal</a> before his departure to Goa in 1541, by <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_Avelar_Rebelo" title="José de Avelar Rebelo">Avelar Rebelo</a> (1635)</div></div></div>
<p>Following the <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_conquest_of_Goa" title="Portuguese conquest of Goa">Portuguese conquest of Goa</a> by <a href="/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque" title="Afonso de Albuquerque">Afonso de Albuquerque</a> in 1510, missionaries of various religious orders (<a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>, <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinians</a>, etc.) were sent from Portugal to Goa with the goal of fulfilling the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Romanus_Pontifex" title="Romanus Pontifex">Romanus Pontifex</a></i>, which granted the <a href="/wiki/Patronage_of_the_propagation_of_the_Christian_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Patronage of the propagation of the Christian faith">patronage of the propagation of the Christian faith</a> in Asia to the Portuguese. To promote assimilation of the native Goans with the Portuguese people, the Portuguese authorities in Goa supported these missionaries.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg/220px-Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="156" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg/330px-Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg/440px-Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1896" data-file-height="1342" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Codice_Casanatense_Portuguese_Nobleman_and_Christian_Indian.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Christian maidens of Goa meeting a Portuguese nobleman seeking a wife, from the <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3dice_Casanatense" title="Códice Casanatense">Códice Casanatense</a> (c. 1540)</div></div></div>
<p>Almost half of Salcete (present-day <a href="/wiki/Salcete" title="Salcete">Salcete</a> and <a href="/wiki/Morumug%C3%A3o_Sub-District" title="Morumugão Sub-District">Morumugão Sub-District</a>) remained Hindu till 1575, but the <a href="/wiki/Velha_Goa" class="mw-redirect" title="Velha Goa">city of Goa</a> was almost completely Christian by this time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMendonça2002405_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendonça2002405-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Cuncolim" title="Cuncolim">Cuncolim</a> was a border village, inhabited by a majority Hindu population. It was prosperous compared to neighboring areas due to its fertile land, with abundant and fresh water from rivers descending from the hinterland of Goa.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Surplus agricultural production had enabled this village to develop crafts of a very skilled order and it was known for its metal work. As Afonso de Albuquerque wrote in his letters back to Portugal, guns of good quality were manufactured in Cuncolim, which he found comparable to those made in Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There were 12 <i>Vangodds</i> (<a href="/wiki/Konkani_language" title="Konkani language">Konkani</a>: clans) of <i>Gaonkars</i> (landlords) in Cuncolim. Their names, in order of precedence, were Mhal, Shetkar, Naik, Mangro, <a href="/wiki/Shett" title="Shett">Shet</a>, Tombdo, Parab, Sidakalo, Lokakalo, Bandekar, Rounom and Benklo. The <i>Gaonkars</i>, who held common ownership of the village and paid all taxes, were also the founders and caretakers of the main village temple.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cuncolim depended on a permanent bazaar at the end of more than one caravan route, connecting it with the mainland through the Ghats of Ashthagrahar province.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In keeping with the traditional fairs and religious festivities, the economy of Cuncolim depended upon its temple and religious celebrations. Due to this, there was an angry reaction from the Brahmin caste towards the attempts of the Jesuits who sought to establish Christianity in Cuncolim and its satellite villages of <a href="/wiki/Assolna" title="Assolna">Assolna</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Veroda&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Veroda (page does not exist)">Veroda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Velim,_Goa" title="Velim, Goa">Velim</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ambelim,_Goa" class="mw-redirect" title="Ambelim, Goa">Ambelim</a> in 1583. The abandonment of local temples by <a href="/wiki/Goan_Catholics" title="Goan Catholics">Goan Catholics</a> would lead to the financial deprivation of the Brahmins.<sup id="cite_ref-goacom.com_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goacom.com-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Gauncars</i> of Cuncolim openly rebelled by refusing to pay taxes to the Portuguese authorities. They built temples illegally on lands owned by Christian converts. They also rebuilt temples that had been demolished by the <a href="/wiki/Bahamani_Sultanate" class="mw-redirect" title="Bahamani Sultanate">Bahamani Sultanate</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bijapur_Sultanate" class="mw-redirect" title="Bijapur Sultanate">Bijapur Sultanate</a> before the arrival of the Portuguese. Hence the Portuguese <a href="/wiki/Christian_missionary" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian missionary">missionaries</a> found it difficult to convert them.<sup id="cite_ref-Cuncolim_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cuncolim-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMendonça2002272–73_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendonça2002272–73-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Portuguese chronicler <a href="/wiki/Diogo_do_Couto" title="Diogo do Couto">Diogo do Couto</a> described Cuncolim as "The leader of rebellions" and its people as "The dangerous of all villages of Salcete".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Jesuit priest  <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Valignano" title="Alessandro Valignano">Alessandro Valignano</a> described Cuncolim as 'rigid and obstinate' in its adherence to idolatory.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This was due to their refusal to give up their native religions and customs.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Massacre">The Massacre</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Massacre">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Five Jesuits, including <a href="/wiki/Rodolfo_Acquaviva" title="Rodolfo Acquaviva">Rodolfo Acquaviva</a>, met in the Church at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Orlim&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Orlim (page does not exist)">Orlim</a> on 15 July 1583 and then proceeded to Cuncolim. They were accompanied by one Portuguese layman (Gonçalo Rodrigues) and 14 native converts, with the objective of erecting a cross and selecting ground for building a church. Meanwhile, several villagers in Cuncolim, after holding a council, advanced in large numbers, armed with swords, lances, and other weapons, towards the spot where the Christians were.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Anthony D'Souza, writing in the <i>Catholic Encyclopaedia</i>, Gonçalo Rodrigues leveled his gun at the advancing crowd, but was stopped by Fr. Alfonso Pacheco who said: "We are not here to fight." Then, he addressed the crowd in Konkani, their native language, he said "Do not be afraid". Following this, the villagers attacked the party.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Father Rodolfo received five cuts from a <a href="/wiki/Scimitar" title="Scimitar">scimitar</a> and a spear and was killed on the spot. According to D'Souza, he died praying to God to forgive the assailants. Next, the crowd turned on Fr. Peter Berno who was horribly mutilated, and Fr. Pacheco who, wounded with a spear, fell on his knees extending his arms in the form of a cross. Fr. António Francisco was shot with arrows, and his head was split open with a sword. Br. Francisco Aranha, wounded at the outset by a scimitar and a lance, fell down a deep declivity into the thick crop of a rice-field, where he lay until he was discovered. He was then carried to a Hindu idol, to which he was bidden to bow his head. Upon his refusal to do this, he was tied to a tree and was shot to death with arrows. The spot where this tree stood is marked with an octagonal monument surmounted by a cross, which was repaired by the Patriarch of Goa in 1885.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Along with the five priests, Gonçalo Rodrigues and the 14 <a href="/wiki/Goan_Catholics" title="Goan Catholics">Goan Catholics</a> were also killed. Of the latter, one was Domingo, a boy of Cuncolim, who was a student at <a href="/wiki/Rachol_Seminary" title="Rachol Seminary">Rachol Seminary</a>, and had accompanied the priests on their expeditions to Cuncolim and pointed out to them the Hindu temples. He was killed by his own Hindu uncle for assisting the priests.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Afonso - a native of Margão or Verna, was an altar-boy of Fr. Pacheco and followed him closely, carrying his <a href="/wiki/Breviary" title="Breviary">breviary</a>. His hands were cut off on his refusal to part with the breviary and he was cut through his knee-joints to prevent his escape. He survived in this condition until the next day when he was found and killed. He was later buried in the Church of the Holy Ghost at <a href="/wiki/Margao" title="Margao">Margao</a> in South Goa. Several of the victims, including Francisco Rodrigues and Paolo da Costa had earlier affirmed their desire to be martyred for the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_of_the_Priests">Beatification of the Priests</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification of the Priests">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Following the massacre the bodies of the five priests were thrown into a well, water of which was afterwards sought by people from all parts of Goa for its miraculous healing. The well still stands today inside the St. Francis Xavier chapel situated at Maddicotto in Cuncolim and is opened for people to view once a year on the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">St. Francis Xavier</a>, celebrated on 3rd December.<sup id="cite_ref-merinews_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merinews-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Christian chroniclers state that the bodies themselves, when found, after two and a half days, allowed no signs of decomposition. They were solemnly buried in the <a href="/wiki/Igreja_de_Nossa_Senhora_das_Neves" title="Igreja de Nossa Senhora das Neves">Church of Our Lady of the Snows</a> at <a href="/wiki/Rachol" title="Rachol">Rachol</a>, and remained there until 1597, when they were removed to the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paul%27s_College,_Goa" title="Saint Paul&#39;s College, Goa">Saint Paul's College, Goa</a>, and in 1862 to the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Goa" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Goa">cathedral in Velha Goa</a>. Some of these <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> have been sent to Europe at various times. All the bones of the entire right arm of Blessed Rodolfo were taken to Rome in 1600, and his left arm was sent from Goa as a present to the Jesuit College at <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In accordance with the request of the Pacheco family, an arm and leg of Blessed Alfonso were sent to Europe in 1609. The process of canonisation began in 1600, but it was only in 1741 that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a> declared the martyrdom proved. On the 16 April 1893, the five martyrs were <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> at St. Peter's in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Portuguese_retaliation_and_aftermath">Portuguese retaliation and aftermath</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Portuguese retaliation and aftermath">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The captain-major of <a href="/wiki/Rachol" title="Rachol">Rachol</a> in charge of the Portuguese Army garrison at the (now extinct) <a href="/w/index.php?title=Assolna_Fort&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Assolna Fort (page does not exist)">Assolna Fort</a>, Gomes Eanes de Figueiredo, was determined to punish those responsible for the deaths of the victims.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Hence the Portuguese army raided and destroyed orchards and fields surrounding the village.<sup id="cite_ref-merinews_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merinews-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Hindu Kshatriya chieftains of Cuncolim, who had led the massacre, were then summoned to the Assolna fort situated on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Sal_River_(India)" title="Sal River (India)">River Sal</a>. (The Church of Regina Martyrum, built in memory of the martyred Christians, now stands at this location.) Charged with <a href="/wiki/Treason" title="Treason">treason</a>, sixteen of them were sentenced to death by the Portuguese authorities. One escaped execution by jumping into the Assolna River through a toilet hole and fleeing to distant <a href="/wiki/Karwar" title="Karwar">Karwar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following the execution of their leaders, the Hindu landlords of Cuncolim and neighbouring villages (Velim, Assolna, Ambelim and Veroda) refused to pay taxes on the produce generated from their fields and orchards to the Portuguese government. As a result, their lands were confiscated by the Portuguese Crown and entrusted to the <a href="/wiki/County" title="County">Condado</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Marquis_of_Fronteira" title="Marquis of Fronteira">Marquis of Fronteira</a> in 1585.<sup id="cite_ref-merinews_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merinews-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-goacom.com_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goacom.com-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Most of Cuncolim's villagers converted to Christianity in the years following the massacre. The Church of Nossa Senhora de Saúde was constructed by the Portuguese at the site of the massacre.<sup id="cite_ref-Machado_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Machado-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
The conversion of almost all of the villagers to Christianity forced the few remaining Hindus in Cuncolim to move their places of worship. The temple of the goddess Shantadurga Cuncolikarian was moved to the neighboring village of <a href="/wiki/Fatorpa" title="Fatorpa">Fatorpa</a> to the south.<sup id="cite_ref-merinews_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merinews-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biographies_of_the_Jesuits_killed_in_the_murders">Biographies of the Jesuits killed in the murders</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Biographies of the Jesuits killed in the murders">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fr._Rodolfo_Acquaviva">Fr. Rodolfo Acquaviva</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Fr. Rodolfo Acquaviva">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Rodolfo_Acquaviva" title="Rodolfo Acquaviva">Rodolfo Acquaviva</a> was born on 2 October, 1550, at <a href="/wiki/Atri,_Abruzzo" title="Atri, Abruzzo">Atri</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>. He was the fifth child of the Duke of Atri and nephew of <a href="/wiki/Claudio_Acquaviva" title="Claudio Acquaviva">Claudio Acquaviva</a>, the fifth General of the Society of Jesus, while on his mother's side he was a cousin of <a href="/wiki/St._Aloysius_Gonzaga" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Aloysius Gonzaga">St. Aloysius Gonzaga</a>. Admitted into the Society of Jesus on 2 April 1568, he landed in Goa on 13 September 1578. Shortly after his arrival he was selected for an important mission to the court of the emperor <a href="/wiki/Akbar_the_great" class="mw-redirect" title="Akbar the great">Akbar the great</a>, who had sent an <a href="/wiki/Ambassador" title="Ambassador">emissary</a> to <a href="/wiki/Velha_Goa" class="mw-redirect" title="Velha Goa">Velha Goa</a> requesting that two learned missionaries might be sent to <a href="/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri" title="Fatehpur Sikri">Fatehpur Sikri</a>, the capital of the <a href="/wiki/Moghul_empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Moghul empire">Moghul empire</a>. After spending three years at the Mughal court, he returned to Goa, much to the regret of the whole court and especially of the emperor. On his return to Goa, he was appointed superior of the <a href="/wiki/Salcette" class="mw-redirect" title="Salcette">Salcette</a> mission, a post he held until his martyrdom. After hearing of Fr Acquaviva's death, Emperor Akbar is believed to have grieved; "Alas, father, my advice was good that you should not go, but you would not follow it."<sup id="cite_ref-Machado_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Machado-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fr._Alphonsus_Pacheco">Fr. Alphonsus Pacheco</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Fr. Alphonsus Pacheco">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Alphonsus Pacheco was born about 1551, of a noble family of <a href="/wiki/New_Castile_(Spain)" title="New Castile (Spain)">New Castile</a>, and entered the Society on 8 September 1567. In September 1574, he arrived in Goa, where he so distinguished himself by his rare prudence and virtue that in 1578; he was sent to Europe on important business. Returning to India in 1581, he was made rector of <a href="/wiki/Rachol_Seminary" title="Rachol Seminary">Rachol Seminary</a>. He accompanied two punitive expeditions of the Portuguese to the village of Cuncolim, and was instrumental in destroying the pagodas there.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fr._Peter_Berno">Fr. Peter Berno</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Fr. Peter Berno">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Peter Berno (or Berna) was born of humble parents in 1550 at <a href="/wiki/Ascona" title="Ascona">Ascona</a>, a Swiss village at the foot of the Alps. After being ordained priest in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, he entered the Society of Jesus in 1577, arrived in Goa in 1579, and was soon appointed to Salcete. He accompanied the expeditions to Cuncolim, and assisted in destroying the Hindu temples, destroyed an ant-hill which was deemed very sacred, and killed a cow which was also an object of Hindu worship. He used to say constantly that no fruit would be gathered from Cuncolim and the hamlets around it till they were bathed in blood shed for the Faith. His superiors declared that he had converted more pagans than all the other fathers put together.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fr._Anthony_Francis">Fr. Anthony Francis</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Fr. Anthony Francis">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Anthony Francis, born in 1553, was a poor student of <a href="/wiki/Coimbra" title="Coimbra">Coimbra</a> in Portugal. He joined the Society in 1571, accompanied Father Pacheco to India in 1581, and was shortly afterwards ordained priest in Goa. It is said that whenever he said Mass, he prayed, at the Elevation, for the grace of martyrdom; and that on the day before his death, when he was saying Mass at the church of Orlim, a miracle prefigured the granting of this prayer.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Br._Francis_Aranha">Br. Francis Aranha</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Br. Francis Aranha">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Brother Francis Aranha was born of a wealthy and noble family of <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a> in Portugal, about 1551, and went to India with his uncle, the first Archbishop of Goa, <a href="/wiki/Gaspar_de_Le%C3%A3o_Pereira" class="mw-redirect" title="Gaspar de Leão Pereira">Dom Gaspar</a>. There he joined the Society of Jesus on 1 November  1571. Being a skilled draughtsman and architect, he built several fine chapels in Goa.
</p><p>This <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> was celebrated in Goa in 1894, and the feast has ever since then been kept with great solemnity at Cuncolim, even by the descendants of those who participated in the murders. The Calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Goa" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Goa">Archdiocese of Goa</a> has fixed 27 July as their feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Controversies">Controversies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuncolim_Massacre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Controversies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The native Goans and the Portuguese layman who were killed along with the five Jesuits were excluded from the list of the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_the_Faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrs of the Faith">Martyrs of the Faith</a>, when the Church opened its <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatification</a> process. According to writer Délio de Mendonça, this was due to the then prevailing attitude among the missionaries that the resident Catholics were by nature incapable of performing spiritual feats.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMendonça2002335_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMendonça2002335-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2003, a memorial to the Hindu chieftains was constructed in Cuncolim, initiative of Prof Vermissio Coutinho, head of the Cuncolim Chieftains Memorial Trust.<sup id="cite_ref-merinews_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-merinews-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Prior to its construction however, the memorial met with strong opposition from the local Catholic parish, on the grounds of its proximity to another memorial built 102 years ago in memory of the five slain Jesuit priests. They instead argued that the memorial should have been built in Assolna, where the Hindu chieftains were executed.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
