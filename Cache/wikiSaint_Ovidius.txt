<p><b>Ovidius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Santo Ovídio</i>), also <i>Saint Auditus</i>, was the third <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Braga" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Braga">Bishop of Braga</a>; he is a <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portuguese</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Auditus_of_Braga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> of the 16th century, Ovidius was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> citizen of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicilian</a> origin. Tradition states that he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Clement I">Pope Clement I</a>, where he served as <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Braga#List_of_Bishops_of_Braga" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Braga">the city's third bishop</a> around 95.<sup id="cite_ref-Borelli_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Borelli-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is said to have <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> <a href="/wiki/Quiteria" title="Quiteria">Saint Quiteria</a> and her sisters after they were abandoned by their mother. He was <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyred</a> for his <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> faith in 135.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Auditus_of_Braga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Portuguese call him <i>Santo Ovídio</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Borelli_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Borelli-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and sometimes, by the folkloric <i>São Ouvido</i> (literally "Saint that is heard" or "Saint Ear"), a <a href="/wiki/Folk_etymology" title="Folk etymology">folk-etymological</a> translation of the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> name <i>Auditus</i>; this name was then rendered as <i>Ovídio</i>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>His relics were elevated in 1527, thus giving official recognition to his cult.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Accounts of the 17th century assert that in <a href="/wiki/Lusitania" title="Lusitania">Lusitanian</a> archaeological sites, Saint Ovidius was depicted in episcopal robes or in the garments of a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>; these depictions testify to an ancient veneration.  His <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> can be found in the <a href="/wiki/Braga_Cathedral" title="Braga Cathedral">cathedral of Braga</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Because of his name, Saint Auditus or Ovidius was traditionally invoked against <a href="/wiki/Auditory_system" title="Auditory system">auditory</a> diseases.  Under the base of his sepulcher in the cathedral, there were two holes into which the <a href="/wiki/Deaf" class="mw-redirect" title="Deaf">deaf</a> would slip their fingers.  They would then place their fingers into their ears by way of intercession with this saint.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>His feast day is June 3.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
