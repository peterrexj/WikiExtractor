					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Saint Faro</b> (or <b>Burgundofaro</b>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 675 AD), Count of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Gu%C3%AEnes" title="County of Guînes">Guînes</a>, was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Meaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Meaux">bishop of Meaux</a>. The family to which Faro belonged is known as the <a href="/wiki/Burgundofara" title="Burgundofara">Faronids</a> and is named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is canonized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Faro&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Burgundofaro was of an ancient noble Burgundian family. His father, Ageneric, was one of the principal lords at the Court of Theodebert II.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
His brothers were <a href="/wiki/Saint_Waldebert" title="Saint Waldebert">Saint Waldebert</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gu%C3%AEnes" title="Guînes">count of Guines</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ponthieu" title="Ponthieu">Ponthieu</a> and <a href="/wiki/County_of_Saint-Pol" title="County of Saint-Pol">Saint-Pol</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> who became abbot of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil" class="mw-redirect" title="Luxeuil">Luxeuil</a>, and  <a href="/wiki/Chagnoald" title="Chagnoald">Chagnoald</a>, who was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Laon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Laon">bishop of Laon</a>, while his sister was <a href="/wiki/Saint_Burgundofara" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Burgundofara">Saint Burgundofara</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who founded the convent of <a href="/wiki/Faremoutiers_Abbey" title="Faremoutiers Abbey">Faremoûtiers</a>.  They were the children of Chagnoric, <a href="/wiki/Chancellor" title="Chancellor">chancellor</a> to <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Faro spent his youth at the court of King <a href="/wiki/Theodobert_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodobert II">Theodobert II</a>. He served his successor, Theodoric, and then <a href="/wiki/Clotaire_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotaire II">Clotaire II</a>. At court he employed his credit with the king to protect the innocent, the orphan, and the widow; and to relieve and comfort all that were in distress. On one occasion, when provoked at the insolent speeches of certain Saxon ambassadors, Clothaire had them cast them into prison, and swore he would cause them to be put to death. Faro first prevailed on him to defer the execution twenty-four hours, and afterwards not only to pardon them, but also to send them home loaded with presents.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His sister, Burgundafara, had become an abbess, and in speaking with her, Faro formed the idea of giving up court life. Blidechilde, his wife, whose consent he asked, was in the same dispositions; and they parted by mutual consent. She took the religious veil, and retired to a solitary place upon one of her own estates. Faro received the tonsure and joined the clergy of Meaux.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Faro, who inherited lands in Guines from his brother, count Waldebert,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELambert2007ch._3.6._6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELambert2007ch._3.6.-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> succeeded <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gundoald,_Bishop_of_Meaux&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gundoald, Bishop of Meaux (page does not exist)">Gundoald</a>, probably a kinsman of his, as bishop of Meaux at some time between 625 and 637. He built a monastery at Estrouanne, near the English channel port of <a href="/wiki/Wissant" title="Wissant">Wissant</a>, destroyed and burnt by <a href="/wiki/Gormond_et_Isembart" title="Gormond et Isembart">Gormond and Isembart</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELambert2007ch._3.6._6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELambert2007ch._3.6.-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Fiacre" title="Saint Fiacre">Saint Fiacre</a> approached Bishop Faro, as he had a desire to live a life of solitude in the forest. Faro assigned him a site at Breuil, in the region of Brie.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Here Fiacre built an oratory in honour of the Virgin Mary, a hospice in which he received strangers, and a cell in which he himself lived apart.
</p>
