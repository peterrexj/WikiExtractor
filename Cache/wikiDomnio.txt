<p><b>Saint Domnius</b> (also known as <b>Saint Dujam</b> or <b>Saint Duje</b>, <b>Saint Domnio</b>, <b>Saint Doimus</b>, or <b>Saint Domninus</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Salona">Bishop of Salona</a> (today's <a href="/wiki/Solin" title="Solin">Solin</a>) around the year 300, and is venerated as the patron of the nearby city of <a href="/wiki/Split_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Split (city)">Split</a> in modern <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>. Salona was a large <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> city serving as capital of the <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia_(Roman_province)" title="Dalmatia (Roman province)">Province of Dalmatia</a>. Saint Domnius  was martyred with seven other Christians in the persecutions of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. He was born in <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a> but historically in <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>, and beheaded in 304 at Salona.
</p><p>He was more likely a martyr of the 4th century, but Christian tradition also states that he was one of the <a href="/wiki/Seventy_Disciples" class="mw-redirect" title="Seventy Disciples">Seventy Disciples</a> of the 1st century.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This tradition holds that Domnio came to Rome with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and was then sent by Peter to evangelize Dalmatia, where he was martyred along with eight soldiers he had converted.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Domnius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were later moved to the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Domnius" title="Cathedral of Saint Domnius">Cathedral of Saint Domnius</a> in Split. When Salona was sacked by the <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Avars" title="Pannonian Avars">Avars</a> and <a href="/wiki/South_Slavs" title="South Slavs">Slavs</a> in the 6th century, the population eventually moved to the nearby <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Diocletian" class="mw-redirect" title="Palace of Diocletian">Palace of Diocletian</a>, enlarging the nearby city of <a href="/wiki/Split_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Split (city)">Split</a> (<i>Spalatum</i>), and establishing it as the successor to Salona. Saint Domnius became the city's patron saint, and the city's <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Domnius" title="Cathedral of Saint Domnius">Cathedral of Saint Domnius</a> was built in the mausoleum of Diocletian itself, the emperor who martyred him.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. John Lateran">Basilica of St. John Lateran</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> claims to own some of Domnio's relics, since <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_IV" title="Pope John IV">Pope John IV</a>, in the 7th century, had requested that relics of a martyr named Domnio be brought to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
