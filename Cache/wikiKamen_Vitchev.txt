<p><b>Peter Vitchev</b>, also known as <b>Kamen Vitchev</b>, was a Bulgarian <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Catholic">Eastern Catholic</a> and an <a href="/wiki/Assumptionists" title="Assumptionists">Assumptionist</a> priest who was martyred by the Bulgarian communist regime. He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 2002.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kamen_Vitchev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_priesthood">Early life and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kamen_Vitchev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Vitchev was born on May 23, 1893 at Srem, near <a href="/wiki/Topolovgrad" title="Topolovgrad">Topolovgrad</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a> and came from a peasant Orthodox family. In 1910, h joined the <a href="/wiki/Assumptionists" title="Assumptionists">Congregation of the Augustinians of the Assumption</a>, beginning his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> in Gempe, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, and taking the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> <i>Kamen</i>. He pursued his studies of philosophy and theology in <a href="/wiki/Leuven" title="Leuven">Louvain</a>, Belgium. He was ordained a priest in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> on 22 December 1921. After a brief period teaching at St. Augustine College in <a href="/wiki/Plovdiv,_Bulgaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Plovdiv, Bulgaria">Plovdiv, Bulgaria</a> and at a high school seminary in <a href="/wiki/Kumkap%C4%B1" title="Kumkapı">Kumkapı</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>, he returned to <a href="/wiki/Strasbourg" title="Strasbourg">Strasbourg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, to complete his studies and obtained a doctorate in theology in 1929.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bulgarian_communist_regime">Bulgarian communist regime</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kamen_Vitchev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bulgarian communist regime">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Very knowledgeable in the history of the Bulgarian church, Vitchev published several articles in the review known as <i>Échos d'Orient</i>. In 1930 he was appointed professor of philosophy and Dean of Studies at St. Augustine College in Plovdiv and maintained this position until the school was closed by the Communist regime on August 2, 1948.
</p><p>After this prestigious institution founded and maintained by the Assumptionists was closed, Vitchev became superior of the Assumptionist seminary in Plovdiv which housed a small number of students. That same year all foreign members of religious orders were expelled and Vitchev was named Vicar-Provincial of the remaining Bulgarian Assumptionists. They numbered 20 and staffed 5 Oriental and 4 Latin rite parishes.
</p><p>As a <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> satellite, Bulgaria suffered from the wave of anti-Church legislation that swept the bloc in the years after <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> (e.g. the arrest of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Stepinac" title="Aloysius Stepinac">Aloysius Stepinac</a> in <a href="/wiki/Yugoslavia" title="Yugoslavia">Yugoslavia</a> in 1946, of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/J%C3%B3zsef_Mindszenty" title="József Mindszenty">József Mindszenty</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> in 1948, of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Josef_Beran" title="Josef Beran">Josef Beran</a> in <a href="/wiki/Czechoslovakia" title="Czechoslovakia">Czechoslovakia</a> in 1950, and of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Wyszy%C5%84ski" title="Stefan Wyszyński">Stefan Wyszyński</a> in <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> in 1953).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kamen_Vitchev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Highly esteemed and respected by the influential young graduates of St. Augustine College, Vitchev posed a threat to the Communist authorities in Bulgaria and was arrested on July 4, 1952. After what international organizations universally considered a show trial which began on September 29, 1952 and ended with a guilty verdict and a death sentence on October 3, Vitchev, two of his Assumptionists companions, <a href="/wiki/Josaphat_Chichkov" title="Josaphat Chichkov">Josaphat Chichkov</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pavel_Djidjov" title="Pavel Djidjov">Pavel Djidjov</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Passionist" class="mw-redirect" title="Passionist">Passionist</a> bishop, <a href="/wiki/Eugene_Bossilkov" title="Eugene Bossilkov">Eugene Bossilkov</a>, were shot to death, without public notice, at approximately 11:30 PM the evening of November 11, 1952.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kamen_Vitchev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vitchev was declared a martyr for the faith and <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> in Plovdiv on May 26, 2002. On July 28, 2010, the Bulgarian parliament passed a law officially rehabilitating all of those who had been condemned by the People's Republic of Bulgaria in 1952, including Vitchev.
</p>
