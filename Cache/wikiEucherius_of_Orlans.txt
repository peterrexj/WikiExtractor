<p><b>Saint Eucherius of Orléans</b> (c. 687 in <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a> – February 20, 743 <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>), nephew of <a href="/wiki/Savaric_of_Auxerre" title="Savaric of Auxerre">Suavaric</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Auxerre" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Auxerre">bishop of Auxerre</a>, was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Orléans">Bishop of Orléans</a>.
</p><p>Reading the letters of <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> led Eucherius to seek the monastic life in 714, when he retired to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Jumi%C3%A8ges" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Jumièges">Abbey of Jumièges</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Rouen">Diocese of Rouen</a>. After seven years his uncle, Suavaric, Bishop of Orléans, died. The reputation of his virtue must have been very great, for a deputation was sent to <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_palace" title="Mayor of the palace">mayor of the palace</a>, who practically governed the <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Kingdom">Frankish Kingdom</a>, to beg that Eucherius might be elected to the vacant <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a>. Despite his dislike of the idea he consented to their request and became bishop in 721.
</p><p>Having opposed the elevation of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a> and the latter's confiscation of church property to fund his war efforts against the <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moorish</a> invasions from <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">Al-Andalus</a>, Eucherius found himself out of favor with the new <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> dynasty. When Charles Martel returned from his victory at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Tours" title="Battle of Tours">Battle of Tours</a>, he stopped in Orléans and exiled Eucherius to <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>. Eucherius retired to the <a href="/wiki/Sint-Truiden#Origins_and_Golden_Age" title="Sint-Truiden">abbey of Sint-Truiden</a>, where he spent the remainder of his life in prayer and contemplation until his death in 743.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Vision_of_St._Eucherius">Vision of St. Eucherius</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eucherius_of_Orl%C3%A9ans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Vision of St. Eucherius">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Hincmar_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Hincmar of Reims">Hincmar of Reims</a> reported to a Council of <a href="/wiki/Quierzy" title="Quierzy">Quierzy</a> in 858, a vision that Bishop Eucherius of Orléans had seen during the reign of King <a href="/wiki/Pepin_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Pepin III">Pepin III</a> over a century before. While at prayer, Eucherius had been taken up and shown, among other things, the sufferings of those in hell, among whom he saw Charles Martel. When the vision ended, he called Boniface and Fulrad, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis" title="Saint-Denis, Seine-Saint-Denis">Saint-Denis</a>, and sent to them to see whether Charles was in his tomb. When the two opened the tomb a dragon rushed out, and they found the tomb's interior blackened as though burned. These two signs were taken as evidence that the vision had been accurate and that Charles had been condemned to hell for his despoliation of Church property.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
