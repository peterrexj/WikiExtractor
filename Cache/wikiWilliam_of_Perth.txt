<p><b>Saint William of Perth</b> (died c. 1201), also known as <b>Saint William of Rochester</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scottish</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in England. He is the patron saint of adopted children. Following his death, he gained local acclaim and was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a> in 1256.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_Perth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Little is known about the life of William, and practically all information regarding him comes from the <i>Nova Legenda Anglie</i>. He was born in <a href="/wiki/Perth,_Scotland" title="Perth, Scotland">Perth</a>, at that time one of the principal towns of <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. In youth, he had been somewhat wild, but on reaching manhood he devoted himself wholly to the service of God. A baker by trade (some sources say he was a <a href="/wiki/Fisherman" title="Fisherman">fisherman</a>), he was accustomed to setting aside every tenth loaf for the poor.
</p><p>He went to <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> daily, and one morning, before it was light, found on the threshold of the church an <a href="/wiki/Child_abandonment" title="Child abandonment">abandoned child</a>, whom he adopted and to whom he taught his trade. Later he took a vow to visit the Holy Places, and, having received the consecrated wallet and staff as a <a href="/wiki/Palmer_(pilgrim)" title="Palmer (pilgrim)">Palmer</a>, set out with his adopted son, whose name is given as "Cockermay Doucri", which is said to be <a href="/wiki/Scots_language" title="Scots language">Scots</a> for "David the <a href="/wiki/Child_abandonment" title="Child abandonment">Foundling</a>". They stayed three days at <a href="/wiki/Rochester,_Kent" title="Rochester, Kent">Rochester</a>, and proceeded to <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> the next day (and perhaps thence to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>). However, David willfully misled his benefactor with a short-cut and, with robbery in view, felled him with a blow on the head and cut his throat.
</p><p>The body was discovered by a <a href="/wiki/Lunatic" title="Lunatic">mad</a> woman, who plaited a <a href="/wiki/Garland" title="Garland">garland</a> of <a href="/wiki/Honeysuckle" title="Honeysuckle">honeysuckle</a> and placed it first on the head of the <a href="/wiki/Corpse" class="mw-redirect" title="Corpse">corpse</a> and then her own, whereupon the madness left her. On learning her tale the <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> of Rochester carried the body to the cathedral and there buried it. He was honoured as a martyr because he was on a pilgrimage to holy places. As a result of the miracle involving the madwoman as well as other miracles wrought at his intercession after death, he was acclaimed a saint by the people.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_Perth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1256 <a href="/wiki/Lawrence_of_St_Martin" title="Lawrence of St Martin">Lawrence of St Martin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester" title="Bishop of Rochester">Bishop of Rochester</a>, obtained the canonisation of William from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a>, which was situated first in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>, then in the northeast <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">transept</a>, was begun at once and attracted crowds of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a>. At the same time a small chapel was built at the place of the murder, which was thereafter called Palmersdene. Remains of this chapel can still be seen near the site of the old St William's Hospital, on the road leading by Horsted Farm to <a href="/wiki/Maidstone" title="Maidstone">Maidstone</a>.
</p><p>The shrine of St William of Perth became a place of pilgrimage second only to Canterbury's shrine of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Becket" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Thomas Becket">Saint Thomas Becket</a>, bringing many thousands of medieval pilgrims to the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Their footsteps wore down the original stone Pilgrim Steps, and nowadays they are covered with wooden steps.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Margaret Darcy, of Essex, in her will, expressed the wish that her servant, Margaret Staunford, should go on a pilgrimage to "Seint Willyam of Rowchester".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 18 and 19 February 1300, <a href="/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England">King Edward I</a> gave two donations of seven <a href="/wiki/Shillings" class="mw-redirect" title="Shillings">shillings</a> to the shrine. Offerings at the shrine were also recorded from <a href="/wiki/Philippa_of_Hainault" title="Philippa of Hainault">Queen Philippa</a> (1352). On 29 November 1399, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> granted an <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgence</a> to those who visited and gave <a href="/wiki/Alms" title="Alms">alms</a> to the shrine on certain specified days. The local people continued to make bequests through the 15th and 16th centuries.
</p><p>The coat of arms of the Bishop of Rochester consists of <a href="/wiki/Saltire" title="Saltire">Saint Andrew's cross</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Scallop" title="Scallop">scallop shell</a> in its centre, which is said to represent William; Andrew being the patron saint of Scotland and scallops being the symbol of pilgrimage. St William is represented in a wall-painting, which was discovered in 1883 in <a href="/wiki/Frindsbury" title="Frindsbury">Frindsbury</a> church, near Rochester, which is supposed to have been painted about 1256–1266.
</p><p>His official feast day is 23 May,<sup id="cite_ref-ScotEpiscCal_archived2021_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ScotEpiscCal_archived2021-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> though there is another feast day celebrated on 22 April. He is the patron saint of adopted children. In the film <a href="/wiki/St._Vincent_(film)" title="St. Vincent (film)"><i>St. Vincent</i></a>, St William is featured in a school report by one of the main characters, Oliver, who finds him interesting primarily because he himself is adopted.
</p><p>St William of Perth Primary School, Rochester, is named after him.
</p>
