<p><b>Pancras</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Sanctus Pancratius</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> citizen who converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and was beheaded for his faith at the age of fourteen, around the year 304. His name is <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> (Παγκράτιος) and means "the one that holds everything".
</p><p>From an early stage, Pancras was venerated together with <a href="/wiki/Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus" title="Saints Nereus and Achilleus">Nereus and Achilleus</a> in a shared feast day and <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> formula on 12 May. In 1595, 25 years after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> promulgated the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Mass" title="Tridentine Mass">Tridentine Missal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Domitilla_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavia Domitilla (saint)">Domitilla</a> was also added.
</p><p>Since 1969, Pancras has been venerated separately, still on 12 May. He is, traditionally, the second of the <a href="/wiki/Ice_Saints" title="Ice Saints">Ice Saints</a>.
In the Syriac traditions he is known as Mor Izozoel (Mar Azazael) remembered on 12 May and 12 August. He is the saint of children.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> district of <a href="/wiki/St_Pancras,_London" title="St Pancras, London">St Pancras</a>, and by extension, the <a href="/wiki/St_Pancras_railway_station" title="St Pancras railway station">railway station</a> of the same name, is named after Pancras.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pancras_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_(Vranov)42.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_%28Vranov%2942.JPG/220px-Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_%28Vranov%2942.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_%28Vranov%2942.JPG/330px-Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_%28Vranov%2942.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_%28Vranov%2942.JPG/440px-Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_%28Vranov%2942.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="3072" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Church_of_the_Nativity_of_the_Virgin_Mary_(Vranov)42.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Pancras in a church at <a href="/wiki/Vranov_nad_Dyj%C3%AD" title="Vranov nad Dyjí">Vranov</a>, Moravia</div></div></div>
<p>Because he was said to have been martyred at the age of fourteen during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">persecution under Diocletian</a>, Pancras would have been born around 289, at a place designated as near <a href="/wiki/Synnada_in_Phrygia" class="mw-redirect" title="Synnada in Phrygia">Synnada</a>, a city of <a href="/wiki/Phrygia_Salutaris" class="mw-redirect" title="Phrygia Salutaris">Phrygia Salutaris</a>, to parents of <a href="/wiki/Roman_citizenship" title="Roman citizenship">Roman citizenship</a>. His mother Cyriada died during childbirth, while his father Cleonius died when Pancras was eight years old.  Pancras was entrusted to his uncle Dionysius' care. They both moved to Rome to live in a <a href="/wiki/Villa" title="Villa">villa</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Caelian_Hill" title="Caelian Hill">Caelian Hill</a>. They converted to Christianity, and Pancras became a zealous adherent of the religion.
</p><p>During the persecution of Christians by <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Emperor Diocletian</a>, around 303 AD, he was brought before the authorities and asked to perform a sacrifice to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a>. Diocletian, impressed with the boy's determination to resist, promised him wealth and power, but Pancras refused, and finally the emperor ordered him to be beheaded on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Aurelia" title="Via Aurelia">Via Aurelia</a>, on 12 May 303 AD; this traditional year of his martyrdom cannot be squared with the saint's defiance of Diocletian in Rome, which the emperor had not visited since 286, nor with the mention of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Cornelius" title="Pope Cornelius">Cornelius</a> (251–253) as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a> at the time of the martyrdom, as the most recent monograph on Pancras' texts and cult has pointed out.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A Roman matron named Ottavilla recovered Pancras' body, covered it with <a href="/wiki/Balsam_of_Mecca" class="mw-redirect" title="Balsam of Mecca">balsam</a>, wrapped it in precious linens, and buried it in a newly built sepulchre dug in the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome" title="Catacombs of Rome">Catacombs of Rome</a>. Pancras' head was placed in the <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> that still exists today in the <a href="/wiki/San_Pancrazio" title="San Pancrazio">Basilica of Saint Pancras</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Pancrazio_Martire_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Pancrazio_Martire-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pancras_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG/220px-1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="492" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG/330px-1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG/440px-1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2123" data-file-height="4752" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:1300_Hl._Pankratius_Bodemuseum_anagoria.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Shrine to St Pancras, made in northern Germany, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1300</span></div></div></div>
<p>Devotion to Pancras existed from the fifth century onwards, for the <a href="/wiki/San_Pancrazio" title="San Pancrazio">basilica of Saint Pancras</a> was built by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Symmachus" title="Pope Symmachus">Pope Symmachus</a> (498–514), on the place where the body of the young martyr had been buried; his earliest <i><a href="/wiki/Passio" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio">passio</a></i> seems to have been written during this time.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Pancrazio_Martire_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Pancrazio_Martire-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Gregory the Great">Pope Gregory the Great</a> gave impetus to the cult of Pancras, sending <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Canterbury" title="Augustine of Canterbury">Augustine</a> to England carrying relics of that saint and including his legend in <i>Liber in gloria martyrum</i> (for this reason, many English churches are dedicated to Pancras; <a href="/wiki/St_Pancras_Old_Church" title="St Pancras Old Church">St Pancras Old Church</a> in London is one of the oldest sites of Christian worship in England). In medieval iconography, Pancras was depicted as a young soldier, due to his association with the paired <a href="/wiki/Soldier_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Soldier saint">soldier saints</a> Nereus and Achilleus.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> By the mid-nineteenth century, pious embroidery set Pancras's martyrdom in the arena among wild beasts, where the panther refrains from attacking and killing him until the martyr gives the beast permission.
</p><p>Pancras is popularly venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of children, jobs and health. His name is also invoked against cramps, false witnesses, headaches and perjury. His image in statue form can be found in many bars, restaurants and other businesses. He is also the patron saint of the Italian city <a href="/wiki/San_Pancrazio_Salentino" title="San Pancrazio Salentino">San Pancrazio Salentino</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> had on 12 May a joint feast (semidouble rank) of Nereus, Achilleus and Pancras. The name of <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Domitilla_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavia Domitilla (saint)">Domitilla</a> was added in 1595.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The joint celebration of Nereus, Achilleus, Domitilla and Pancras continued with that ranking (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1954" title="General Roman Calendar of 1954">General Roman Calendar of 1954</a>) until the revision of 1960, when it was reclassified as a third-class feast (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>).
</p><p>In the present <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, revised in 1969, Saints <a href="/wiki/Nereus_and_Achilleus" class="mw-redirect" title="Nereus and Achilleus">Nereus and Achilleus</a> (together) and Saint Pancras have distinct celebrations (optional memorials) on 12 May. Saint Domitilla is not included in the revised calendar, because the liturgical honours once paid to her "have no basis in tradition".<sup id="cite_ref-CR_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
