<p><b>Benignus of Armagh</b> (died 467) was the son of Sesenen, an <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> chieftain in the part of Ireland that is now called as <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a>. He was baptised into the <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> faith by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>, and became his favourite disciple and his <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_bishop" title="Coadjutor bishop">coadjutor</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh_(Roman_Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Armagh (Roman Catholic)">Diocese of Armagh</a> around AD 450. His gentle disposition suggested the name Benen, which was Latinised as Benignus.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He followed his master in his travels and assisted him in his <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> labours, helping in the formation of choral services. His family may have belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Bardic" class="mw-redirect" title="Bardic">bardic</a> order.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> From his musical achievements he was known as "Patrick's psalm-singer". As Benignus had been trained by Patrick in sacred learning from his youth and was well versed in the language and learning of his native land, he was appointed secretary to the Commission of Nine, which a few years before had been directed to compile the <a href="/wiki/Early_Irish_law" title="Early Irish law">Brehon Laws</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benignus is said to have contributed materials for the <i><a href="/wiki/Psalter_of_Cashel" title="Psalter of Cashel">Psalter of Cashel</a></i>, and the <a href="/wiki/Lebor_na_Cert" title="Lebor na Cert"><i>Book of Rights</i></a>. He succeeded St. Patrick's nephew Sechnall as coadjutor and became the first rector of the Cathedral School of Armagh.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was present at the <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> that passed the canon recognising "the See Of the Apostle Peter" as the final court of appeals in difficult cases. This canon is to be found in the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Armagh" title="Book of Armagh">Book of Armagh</a>. Benignus resigned his coadjutorship in 467 and died the same year. His feast is celebrated on 9 November.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 433, Patrick clashed with King <a href="/wiki/Laoghaire" class="mw-redirect" title="Laoghaire">Laoghaire</a> at <a href="/wiki/Hill_of_Tara" title="Hill of Tara">Tara</a> over religion. Legend reports that a trial by fire was proposed. A <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> <a href="/wiki/Druid" title="Druid">druid</a> and Benignus were tied inside a burning timber building, the former was reduced to ash while Benignus was untouched, at this turning point, Christian teaching was established.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Most authorities identified Patrick's psalm-singer with the Benignus who founded Kilbannon, near <a href="/wiki/Tuam" title="Tuam">Tuam</a>. However, Tirechán's collections in the Book of Armagh states that Benignus of Kilbannon was the son of Lugni of Connaught. Benignus of Kilbannon had a famous monastery, where <a href="/wiki/St._Jarlath" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Jarlath">Jarlath</a> was educated, and he presided over Drumlease. His sister Mathona served as abbess of <a href="/wiki/Tawney" title="Tawney">Tawney</a>, in Tirerrill.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Cavan" title="Cavan">Cavan</a>, he established a monastery on Drom Benen (hill of Benan), today's Drumbannon. Other monasteries are in <i>cill benen</i> (church of Benan), today's Kilbonane, <a href="/wiki/West_Cork" title="West Cork">West Cork</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
