<p><b>Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">O.S.B.</a></span> (<small>Italian pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="it-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian" title="Help:IPA/Italian">[alˈfreːdo ildeˈfɔnso ʃˈʃuster]</a></span>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <small>German:&#32;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="de-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Standard_German" title="Help:IPA/Standard German">[ˈʃuːstɐ]</a></span>; born <b>Alfredo Ludovico Schuster</b>; 18 January 1880 – 30 August 1954) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> and professed member from the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictines</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> from 1929 until his death. He became known as <b>Ildefonso</b> as a Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and served as an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> prior to his elevation to the <a href="/wiki/Cardinalate" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinalate">cardinalate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He led the Milanese archdiocese during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> and was known to have supported <a href="/wiki/Italian_Fascism" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian Fascism">Fascism</a> at first. But his views changed to opposition after the <a href="/wiki/Annexation_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Annexation of Austria">annexation of Austria</a> and the introduction of <a href="/wiki/Italian_Racial_Laws" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian Racial Laws">racial laws</a> prompting vocal criticisms of anti-Christian aspects of the <a href="/wiki/Benito_Mussolini" title="Benito Mussolini">Mussolini</a> regime.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification was celebrated in mid-1996 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_priesthood">Childhood and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Alfredo Ludovico Schuster was born in 1880 in the Ospedale Santissimo Salvatore in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to Johann Schuster (a <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavarian</a> <a href="/wiki/Tailor" title="Tailor">tailor</a> and double widower) and Maria Anna Tutzer (who hailed from <a href="/wiki/Bolzano" title="Bolzano">Bolzano</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Johann was three decades older than Tutzer. His sister Giulia entered the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Vincentians</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>. He also had three half-siblings from his father's second marriage. Schuster was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 20 January as "Alfredo Ludovico Luigi". In his childhood he was <a href="/wiki/Kidnapping" title="Kidnapping">kidnapped</a> for a brief period but the kidnapper was arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Schuster received his Confirmation on 2 April 1887 from <a href="/wiki/Monsignor" title="Monsignor">Monsignor</a> Giulio Lenti and made his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a> 1890 in the Santa Anna in Porta Angelica church. His father Johann died on 18 September 1889.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He served as an <a href="/wiki/Altar_boy" class="mw-redirect" title="Altar boy">altar server</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Piet%C3%A0_in_Camposanto_dei_Teutonici" title="Santa Maria della Pietà in Camposanto dei Teutonici">Santa Maria della Pietà in Camposanto dei Teutonici</a> church next to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a>. Schuster completed his high school studies (<i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ginnasiale" class="extiw" title="wikt:ginnasiale">ginnasiali</a></i> and <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/liceale" class="extiw" title="wikt:liceale">liceali</a></i>) at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls</a> in November 1891. On 13 November 1898 he joined the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Order of Saint Benedict</a> at their <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at Saint Paul Outside the Walls when he took the name <b>Ildefonso</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He later <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">professed</a> his <a href="/wiki/Monastic_vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic vows">monastic vows</a> on 13 November 1900. He graduated with a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy" title="Doctor of Philosophy">Doctor of Philosophy</a> on 14 June 1903 and later received a doctorate in theology from the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Atheneum_of_St._Anselm" class="mw-redirect" title="Pontifical Atheneum of St. Anselm">Pontifical Atheneum of Saint Anselm</a> in Rome.
</p><p>Schuster received his <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordination</a> as a priest on 19 March 1904 at the patriarchal <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Lateran Basilica">Lateran Basilica</a> in Rome from Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Respighi" title="Pietro Respighi">Pietro Respighi</a> (its <a href="/wiki/Archpriest" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a>). He returned to the Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls in 1904. His two mentors during his time of education were Father Bonifacio Oslander and <a href="/wiki/Tommaso_Riccardi" title="Tommaso Riccardi">Tommaso Riccardi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbotship">Abbotship</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Abbotship">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He became the <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">novice master</a> in 1908 and <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> in 1916 before he was elected as the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> for Saint Paul Outside the Walls on 6 April 1918. He also received the abbatial blessing from Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Basilio_Pompili" title="Basilio Pompili">Basilio Pompili</a> on 14 April there.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He served as the <a href="/wiki/Procurator_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Procurator (Catholic Church)">Procurator General</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Cassinese_Congregation" class="mw-redirect" title="Cassinese Congregation">Cassinese Congregation</a> from 1914 to 1929 and also served as the President of the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Oriental_Institute" title="Pontifical Oriental Institute">Pontifical Oriental Institute</a> from 1919 to 1922. He visited the seminaries of the northern Lombard region as well as those in the southern regions of Campania and Calabria from 1924 to 1928.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Either in November or December 1926 he preached the spiritual exercises to Angelo Giuseppe Roncalli (the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>) at Saint Paul Outside the Walls. While abbot he was made a consulter to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Oriental_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Oriental Churches">Congregation for the Oriental Churches</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate_and_cardinalate">Episcopate and cardinalate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Episcopate and cardinalate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Schuster was named <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> on 26 June 1929 to succeed <a href="/wiki/Eugenio_Tosi" title="Eugenio Tosi">Eugenio Tosi</a>. On the following 13 July he took the oath of allegiance to the Italian state in front of <a href="/wiki/Victor_Emmanuel_III_of_Italy" title="Victor Emmanuel III of Italy">King Vittorio Emanuele III</a>; he was the first Italian bishop to do this since the new <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Treaty" title="Lateran Treaty">Lateran Concordat</a> required it according to Article 20 of the concordat.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> elevated Schuster to the <a href="/wiki/Cardinalate" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinalate">cardinalate</a> in 1929 as the <a href="/wiki/San_Martino_ai_Monti" title="San Martino ai Monti">Cardinal-Priest of Santi Silvestro e Martino ai Monti</a>. <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Cremonesi" title="Carlo Cremonesi">Carlo Cremonesi</a> and Agostino Zampini served as the <a href="/wiki/Co-consecrators" class="mw-redirect" title="Co-consecrators">co-consecrators</a>. In 1933 he was conferred as a bailiff grand cross of honor and devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Malta" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Malta">Order of Malta</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Schuster ordained 1265 priests and consecrated 22 bishops during his tenure as archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He also made five pastoral visits during his episcopate and selected <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Saint Carlo Borromeo</a> as his model as an archbishop. He emphasized the importance of <a href="/wiki/Catechetics" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechetics">catechetics</a> and promoted the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Action" title="Catholic Action">Catholic Action</a> movement for the faithful. He also believed that the goal of all Christians was <a href="/wiki/Universal_call_to_holiness" title="Universal call to holiness">holiness</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He served as a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> on several occasions. On 15 August 1932 he was appointed as the legate to the celebration of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Caravaggio" title="Our Lady of Caravaggio">Nostra Signora di Caravaggio</a>; on 21 March 1934 to the <a href="/wiki/Millennium" title="Millennium">millennial</a> commemorations of the <a href="/wiki/Einsiedeln_Abbey" title="Einsiedeln Abbey">Einsiedeln convent</a> in <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>; on 15 September 1937 to the inauguration of the new facade of the cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Desio" title="Desio">Desio</a>; and on 2 August 1951 to the <a href="/wiki/National_Eucharistic_Conference" class="mw-redirect" title="National Eucharistic Conference">National Eucharistic Conference</a> in <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He participated in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1939" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1939">papal conclave in 1939</a> which elected <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on the eve of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> and was even considered as a <i><a href="/wiki/Papabile" title="Papabile">papabile</a></i> candidate for those seeking a more pastoral pope.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg/220px-Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="131" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg/330px-Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg/440px-Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1545" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Ildefonso_Schuster_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Schuster's tomb in the Cathedral of Milan.</div></div></div>
<p>He died on 30 August 1954 at 4:15am of a heart ailment in at <a href="/wiki/Venegono_Inferiore" title="Venegono Inferiore">Venegono Inferiore</a> near Milan. Cardinal Angelo Giuseppe Roncalli (the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>) celebrated his funeral. He was buried on 2 September 1954 in the metropolitan cathedral next to his two immediate predecessors.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His tomb was opened on 28 January 1985 and his remains were found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">intact</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerry2010_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerry2010-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Honours">Honours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Honours">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/File:Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg" class="image"><img alt="Equestrian order of the Holy Sepulcher of Jerusalem BAR.svg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg/50px-Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="14" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg/75px-Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg/100px-Equestrian_order_of_the_Holy_Sepulcher_of_Jerusalem_BAR.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="218" data-file-height="60" /></a> Schuster was honoured with the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Order of the Holy Sepulchre">Order of the Holy Sepulchre</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/File:SMOM-gcs.svg" class="image"><img alt="SMOM-gcs.svg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/SMOM-gcs.svg/50px-SMOM-gcs.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="14" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/SMOM-gcs.svg/75px-SMOM-gcs.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/SMOM-gcs.svg/100px-SMOM-gcs.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="436" data-file-height="120" /></a> In 1933 the cardinal was conferred the honour of Bailiff Knight Grand Cross of Honor and Devotion of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Malta" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Malta">Order of Malta</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_Fascism">Relations with Fascism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Relations with Fascism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There were claims during the process for Schuster's <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> that he was supportive to Italian Fascism.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerry2010_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerry2010-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> While there is evidence of some support for fascist ambitions, there is also evidence that he denounced the anti-Christian element of fascism. It was said that he refused to participate in ceremonies involving Mussolini and also condemned racist legislation during the fascist period.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerry2010_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerry2010-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Schuster was an enthusiastic supporter of the Italian <a href="/wiki/Second_Italo-Abyssinian_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Italo-Abyssinian War">invasion of Ethiopia</a> in 1935 and compared it to the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a> and viewed it as a potential source of converts.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELee2000126_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELee2000126-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChadwick19888_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChadwick19888-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> On 28 October 1935, while celebrating Mass in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Milan">Cathedral of Milan</a>, he asked God to protect the Italian troops as "they open the door of Ethiopia to the Catholic faith and Roman civilisation"<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> before he blessed the banners of the departing troops.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In a speech at the School of Fascist Mystique in Rome in 1937, he spelled out a fanciful direct link between Imperial Rome and Christian Rome to fascism: "God has chosen to reward the Duce by drawing his historical figure closer to the great spirits of Constantine and Augustus, through the work of Benito Mussolini reconnecting Rome and its King to a shining new imperial crown of Roman peace".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarris2007218_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarris2007218-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1938, his views changed by Germany <a href="/wiki/Anschluss" title="Anschluss">annexing Austria</a> and introducing <a href="/wiki/Racial_policy_of_Nazi_Germany" title="Racial policy of Nazi Germany">German racial doctrines</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Italian_Racial_Laws_(1938-45)" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian Racial Laws (1938-45)">Italian Racial Laws</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relationship_with_Mussolini">Relationship with Mussolini</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Relationship with Mussolini">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Italian_Fascism" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian Fascism">Fascist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Nazi" class="mw-redirect" title="Nazi">Nazi</a> press attacked Schuster during the war, without his suffering loss of esteem among his own flock in Milan. On 25 April 1945, the cardinal hosted in the archbishop's palace in Milan a meeting between <a href="/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement" title="Italian resistance movement">Italian partisans</a> and Mussolini in an attempt to obtain a truce between the two parties. However, Mussolini did not accept the demand for unconditional surrender that Marazza and <a href="/wiki/Sandro_Pertini" title="Sandro Pertini">Pertini</a>, the partisan delegates, made. Mussolini arrived on time at 4:00pm without the other side being present. The delegates <a href="/wiki/Raffaele_Cadorna,_Jr." class="mw-redirect" title="Raffaele Cadorna, Jr.">Cadorna</a> and Lombardi as well as Marazza arrived an hour later. Mussolini had a talk with Schuster in the meantime, who gave him a glass of <a href="/wiki/Rosolio" title="Rosolio">rosolio</a> to drink and a copy of a book he had written about the life of a saint. Schuster made an effort to preach humility to Mussolini. Once Graziani and the other Fascist leaders arrived (according to the versions given according to all those present including Schuster), events that occurred differ according to the individuals versions of events.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although the cardinal sought Mussolini out on 25 April 1945 and urged him to make his peace with God and his fellow man, Mussolini spurned the admonition and was killed within a week.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Following the end of the war, cardinal made frequent attempts to emphasize the danger of <a href="/wiki/Totalitarianism" title="Totalitarianism">totalitarianism</a> that <a href="/wiki/Communism" title="Communism">communism</a> and fascism inspired.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Idelfuns.jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Idelfuns.jpeg/180px-Idelfuns.jpeg" decoding="async" width="180" height="225" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Idelfuns.jpeg/270px-Idelfuns.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Idelfuns.jpeg/360px-Idelfuns.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="1280" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Idelfuns.jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Monument in <a href="/wiki/Verano_Brianza" title="Verano Brianza">Verano Brianza</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>The process for his beatification opened in Milan in a diocesan process that his successor inaugurated on 30 August 1957; the process was closed on 31 October 1963 after a process was held in Rome from 21 November 1959 to 13 July 1961 to collect additional evidence and documentation.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> His writings were approved on 5 March 1970 as having adhered to traditional doctrine. The cause remained dormant for some time until 18 July 1986 when the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> validated the diocesan phase while later receiving the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> for assessment in 1989.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Theologians approved the cause on 12 October 1993 as did the C.C.S. members later on 11 January 1994. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed that Schuster had led a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and named him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 26 March 1994.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Schuster's beatification now depended upon one confirmed miracle. One such case was investigated with the evidence collected sent to Rome with the C.C.S. validating this process on 5 July 1985. Medical experts confirmed this miracle as such a decade later on 17 November 1994 while theologians confirmed the assessment on 21 February 1995; the C.C.S. also approved it on 2 May 1995. John Paul II confirmed that the healing in question was a miracle on 11 July 1995 and confirmed Schuster's beatification. The beatification was celebrated on 12 May 1996 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiranda1998-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerry2010_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerry2010-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The miracle that led to his beatification was the cure of the nun Maria Emilia Brusati from a severe <a href="/wiki/Glaucoma" title="Glaucoma">glaucoma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
