<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>

<p><b>Rodolfo Acquaviva</b> (2 October 1550 – 25 July 1583) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> missionary and priest in India who served the court of <a href="/wiki/Akbar_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Akbar the Great">Akbar the Great</a> from 1580 to 1583. He was killed in 1583 and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1893.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodolfo_Acquaviva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rodolfo Acquaviva was the son of Giangirolamo Acquaviva, the 10th Duke of Atri. He was the great-grandson of <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Matteo_Acquaviva" title="Andrea Matteo Acquaviva">Andrea Matteo Acquaviva</a>, <a href="/wiki/Condottiere" class="mw-redirect" title="Condottiere">condottiere</a> and <a href="/wiki/Man_of_letters" class="mw-redirect" title="Man of letters">man of letters</a>. Rodolfo (also known as Rudolfo) belonged to a powerful and illustrious family of Germanic origin settled in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a> since the twelfth century.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His mother was Marguerite Pio where on his mother's side, he was a cousin of <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dsouza_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dsouza-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodolfo_Acquaviva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In April 1568, inspired by the example of his uncle <a href="/wiki/Claudio_Acquaviva" title="Claudio Acquaviva">Claudio Acquaviva</a> who later became the 5th <a href="/wiki/General_of_the_Jesuits" class="mw-redirect" title="General of the Jesuits">General of the Jesuits</a>, he too joined the Society of Jesus. He became a novice at <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Andrea_al_Quirinale" title="Sant&#39;Andrea al Quirinale">Sant'Andrea al Quirinale</a> in Rome together with <a href="/wiki/Stanislas_Kostka" class="mw-redirect" title="Stanislas Kostka">Stanislas Kostka</a>. After completing his studies, Acquaviva was chosen by his superiors for the prestigious and challenging Indian missions, begun by <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a> in the Portuguese territory of <a href="/wiki/Goa" title="Goa">Goa</a>. He then travelled to <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a>, the starting point for the eastern voyage where he was ordained as a priest and sailed for India in 1578.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="At_Akbar.27s_Court"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="At_Akbar's_Court">At Akbar's Court</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodolfo_Acquaviva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: At Akbar&#039;s Court">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At first Acquaviva taught at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paul%27s_College,_Goa" title="Saint Paul&#39;s College, Goa">Saint Paul's College, Goa</a> but was then assigned as the leader of a mission to the court of the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Mughal" title="Grand Mughal">Grand Mughal</a> <a href="/wiki/Akbar" title="Akbar">Akbar</a> (1542–1605) who had requested missionaries be sent.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In his new palace in <a href="/wiki/Fatehpur_Sikri" title="Fatehpur Sikri">Fatehpur Sikri</a> Akbar built the <a href="/wiki/Ibadat_Khana" title="Ibadat Khana">Ibadat Khana</a> (House of Worship) where he invited leaders of the Muslim, Hindu and other religions to debate points of religious truth, including Acquaviva and his companion Jesuit António de Monserrate (Antoni de Montserrat in his native Catalan), and their young translator, Francisco Henriques, who spoke Persian. Akbar was interested in founding a new pantheistic religion with elements from different traditions and his new faith was called <a href="/wiki/Din-i-Ilahi" class="mw-redirect" title="Din-i-Ilahi">Din-i-Ilahi</a> ("Faith of the Divine").<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although Acquaviva came equipped with the Bible translated into many different languages, (though not yet Persian) and was the object of Akbar's sympathetic personal attention, the Jesuit felt his efforts were fruitless, one obstacle being the ruler's repugnance to monogamy, and after three years, decided to withdraw, though other Jesuits maintained the mission at the courts of the <a href="/wiki/Mughal_Emperors" class="mw-redirect" title="Mughal Emperors">Mughal Emperors</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Agra" title="Agra">Agra</a> for the next two centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-dsouza_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dsouza-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodolfo_Acquaviva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cuncolim_Martyrs.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Cuncolim_Martyrs.jpg/290px-Cuncolim_Martyrs.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="385" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b5/Cuncolim_Martyrs.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="325" data-file-height="431" /></a><figcaption>A 17th-century painting<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Accuracy_dispute#Disputed_statement" title="Wikipedia:Accuracy dispute"><span title="The material near this tag is possibly inaccurate or nonfactual. (April 2023)">dubious</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Rodolfo_Acquaviva#Dubious" title="Talk:Rodolfo Acquaviva">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup> in a church in Colva depicting the massacre of the five Jesuits in Cuncolim, Goa on 25 July 1583. Acquaviva in the center uncovering his neck.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Upon his return to Goa as part of his missionary commitments, Acquaviva led a mission to the <a href="/wiki/Hindu" class="mw-redirect" title="Hindu">Hindu</a> <a href="/wiki/Kshatriyas" class="mw-redirect" title="Kshatriyas">Kshatriyas</a> of <a href="/wiki/Salcette" class="mw-redirect" title="Salcette">Salcette</a>, south of Goa. This was seen as a provocation by the locals, inciting the <a href="/wiki/Cuncolim_Revolt" class="mw-redirect" title="Cuncolim Revolt">Cuncolim Revolt</a> of July 1583. Acquaviva had his throat cut while praying to God.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rodolfo_Acquaviva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Acquaviva and his four Jesuit companions were <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1893. He is commemorated with the Jesuit martyrs of the missions on 4 February by the order and its institutions.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
