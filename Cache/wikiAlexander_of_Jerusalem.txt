<p><b>Alexander of Jerusalem</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Αλέξανδρος Ιεροσολύμων</span>; died 251 AD) was a third century bishop who is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodox churches">Oriental Orthodox churches</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.  He died during the persecution of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DGRBM_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DGRBM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alexander was originally from Cappadocia and became Cappadocia's first bishop.  Afterwards he was associated as <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor" title="Coadjutor">coadjutor</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Jerusalem" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Jerusalem">Bishop of Jerusalem</a>, <a href="/wiki/Narcissus_of_Jerusalem" title="Narcissus of Jerusalem">Narcissus</a>, who was, at that time, very old. Alexander had been imprisoned for his faith in the time of Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Severus" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexander Severus">Alexander Severus</a>. After his release, he came to <a href="/wiki/Aelia_Capitolina" title="Aelia Capitolina">Jerusalem</a>, where the aged Bishop Narcissus prevailed on Alexander to remain and assist him in the government of that see.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>It was Alexander who permitted <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>, despite being a layman, to speak in the churches. For this concession he was taken to task, but he defended himself by examples of other permissions of the same kind given even to Origen himself elsewhere, although then quite young. <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> says that they had studied together in the great Christian school of Alexandria. Alexander ordained him a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alexander is praised for the library he built at Jerusalem.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Though at his time Jerusalem was officially known as <a href="/wiki/Aelia_Capitolina" title="Aelia Capitolina">Aelia Capitolina</a>, the name used by the Roman authorities since the city was rebuilt by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a>, Christian tradition persisted in using the original name.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Persecution_and_death">Persecution and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Persecution and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Finally, in spite of his years, he, with several other bishops, was carried off a prisoner to <a href="/wiki/Caesarea" title="Caesarea">Caesarea</a>, and as the historians say, "The glory of his white hairs and great sanctity formed a double crown for him in captivity".<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  His <i>vita</i> states that he suffered many tortures, but survived them all. When the wild beasts were brought to devour him, some licked his feet, and others their impress on the sand of the arena.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His feast is kept by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> on March 18, by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Churches">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a> on May 16/29<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and December 12/25.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
