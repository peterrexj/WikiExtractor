<p><b>Emmelia of Caesarea</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Εμμέλεια) was born in Cappadocia, a province of the Roman Empire (nowadays Central Anatolia, Turkey). She died on 30 May 375 AD. She was born in the late third to early fourth century, a period in time when Christianity was becoming more widespread, posing a challenge to the Roman government and its pagan rule.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
She was the wife of <a href="/wiki/Basil_the_Elder" title="Basil the Elder">Basil the Elder</a> and bore nine or ten children,<sup id="cite_ref-Velimirovic_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Velimirovic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> including <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a> (born circa 330<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>), <a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Younger" title="Macrina the Younger">Macrina the Younger</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Sebaste" title="Peter of Sebaste">Peter of Sebaste</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa" title="Gregory of Nyssa">Gregory of Nyssa</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Naucratius" title="Naucratius">Naucratius</a>.
</p><p>Emmelia&#8212;also known as Emilia or Emily&#8212;is venerated as a saint in both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and is said to have died on 30 May 375.<sup id="cite_ref-Velimirovic_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Velimirovic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, she is not the only woman in her family to be venerated as a saint. Both her mother-in-law, <a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Elder" title="Macrina the Elder">Macrina the Elder</a>, as well as her daughters, <a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Younger" title="Macrina the Younger">Macrina the Younger</a> and <a href="/wiki/Theosebia" title="Theosebia">Theosebia</a> are recognized as saints in the Catholic Church (except for Theosebia) and Eastern Orthodox Church.
</p><p>Emmelia spent much of her later years living with her eldest daughter, Macrina the Younger. Macrina the Younger had a profound impact on her mother. With her husband no longer around, Emmelia and her daughter lived a life dedicated to Christianity, surrounded by servants whom they treated as equals, at Macrina the Younger's insistence. Their ascetic way of life attracted a following of women which created a convent-like atmosphere, where one was considered rich if she lived a pure and devout Christian life and disregarded the materialistic lure of earthly pleasures and possessions.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
