<p><b>Maria Elizabeth Hesselblad</b>, <a href="/wiki/Bridgettines" title="Bridgettines">OSsS</a> (4 June 1870 – 24 April 1957), was a <a href="/wiki/Swedes" title="Swedes">Swedish</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sister</a> who founded a new, active, branch of the <a href="/wiki/Bridgettines" title="Bridgettines">Bridgettine</a> order in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, known as the "Bridgettine Sisters". Hesselblad is recognised as a <a href="/wiki/Righteous_Among_the_Nations" title="Righteous Among the Nations">Righteous Among the Nations</a> due to her efforts in <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> saving the lives of <a href="/wiki/Jews" title="Jews">Jews</a> during the genocide of <a href="/wiki/The_Holocaust" title="The Holocaust">the Holocaust</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified her on 9 April 2000 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> canonised her 5 June 2016 at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_conversion">Early life and conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg/220px-The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="128" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg/330px-The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg/440px-The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3925" data-file-height="2292" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:The_birth_house_of_Elisabeth_Hesselblad.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The birth house of Elisabeth Hesselblad</div></div></div>
<p>Maria Elizabeth Hesselblad born on 4 June 1870 as the fifth of thirteen children born to August Robert Hesselblad and Cajsa Petersdotter Dag – <a href="/wiki/Lutheran" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran">Lutheran</a> parents from <a href="/wiki/F%C3%A5glavik" title="Fåglavik">Fåglavik</a> in <a href="/wiki/V%C3%A4stra_G%C3%B6taland_County" title="Västra Götaland County">Västra Götaland County</a>; she had a brother named Thur. She was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> the following month and received into the Lutheran Church of Sweden in her parish of Hudene.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1886, she had to work to help them make ends meet. At first she looked for work in Sweden, but eventually emigrated to the <a href="/wiki/United_States_of_America" class="mw-redirect" title="United States of America">United States of America</a> in 1888, where she studied <a href="/wiki/Nursing" title="Nursing">nursing</a> at <a href="/wiki/Roosevelt_Hospital" class="mw-redirect" title="Roosevelt Hospital">Roosevelt Hospital</a> in <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>. While there, she did home nursing, which brought her into contact with the Catholic faith of many of the poor for whom she cared. She developed an interest in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> while deep prayer and personal study led her down the path of conversion, and on 15 August 1902, the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Feast of the Assumption</a>, she received <a href="/wiki/Conditional_baptism" title="Conditional baptism">conditional baptism</a> from a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Johann_Georg_Hagen" title="Johann Georg Hagen">Johann Georg Hagen</a>, in the chapel of the <a href="/wiki/Georgetown_Visitation_Preparatory_School" title="Georgetown Visitation Preparatory School">Georgetown Visitation Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Washington,_D.C." title="Washington, D.C.">Washington, D.C.</a><sup id="cite_ref-VNS_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Hagen also became her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As Elisabeth Hasselblad reflected on that moment, she wrote: "In an instant the love of <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> was poured over me. I understood that I could respond to that love only through sacrifice and a love prepared to suffer for His glory and for the Church. Without hesitation I offered Him my life, and my will to follow Him on the <a href="/wiki/Way_of_the_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Way of the Cross">Way of the Cross</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-CO_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Two days later, she received her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> and would depart for <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>.
</p><p>Hesselblad approached Hagen and asked that she be received into the Church at once to which Hagen said: "My dear daughter, how could I do that? I have just met you". She replied: "My father, forgive me, but I have fought in darkness for twenty years; for many years I have studied the Catholic faith and have prayed for a strong faith … I now possess this faith, and I am ready to submit to an examination on all the points of doctrine". Hagen questioned this, and after great consideration told her: "I see no reason not to receive you unto the Church. Today is August 12, and the 15th will be the Feast of the Assumption of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>. That day, I will receive you into the Catholic Church; the following Sunday, the 17th, you will be able to receive Holy Communion. Spend these few days in retreat and come to see me twice a day for lessons".<sup id="cite_ref-CV_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CV-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was in that same year that her brother, Thur, also converted to Catholicism.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_experiences">Religious experiences</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious experiences">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:402px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg/400px-Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg" decoding="async" width="400" height="91" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg/600px-Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg/800px-Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg 2x" data-file-width="16382" data-file-height="3726" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hesselblad-Papcyznski-Canonization.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Canonization Mass on 5 June 2016</div></div></div>
<p>Hesselblad then made a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where she received the sacrament of <a href="/wiki/Confirmation" title="Confirmation">Confirmation</a>. She also visited the house of <a href="/wiki/Bridget_of_Sweden" title="Bridget of Sweden">Bridget of Sweden</a> there, where Bridget had spent the last half of her life, which made a deep impression upon her. At that point Hasselblad felt called to dedicate her life to the work of <a href="/wiki/Ecumenism" title="Ecumenism">Christian unity</a>. She returned to New York City briefly, only to go back to Rome, where, on 25 March 1904, she was welcomed as a guest by the nuns of the <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> monastery housed there; the prioress welcomed her after hesitating to accept her due to her weak health. However, she allowed Hasselblad on the condition of a period of probation.<sup id="cite_ref-VNS_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Yet it was at this point she fell gravely ill and even had to receive the <a href="/wiki/Extreme_Unction" class="mw-redirect" title="Extreme Unction">Extreme Unction</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CV_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CV-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She slowly recovered and held out against her family's pleas to return to Sweden.
</p><p>She petitioned the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> to be able to make <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> under the Rule of the Order which Bridget had founded, and had been a prominent presence in the Church in Sweden before the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> had taken hold there. Hasselblad received special permission for this from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> in 1906, at which time she assumed the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> of the Bridgettines, including its distinctive element of a veil with a symbolic crown.<sup id="cite_ref-SPQN_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SPQN-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She professed into the hands of Hagen on 22 June 1906, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Feast of the Sacred Heart">Feast of the Sacred Heart</a>.
</p><p>Hesselblad attempted to revive interest in the order and its founder in both Sweden and Rome. Her proposal to establish a monastery of the order on the site where Bridget had lived received no volunteers from the few monasteries of the order still in existence. Giving up on the intention of following the established way of life in the order, she proposed one which included the care of the sick. To this end she was joined by three young women from England, whom she received on 9 November 1911, with which the new congregation was established. Their particular mission was to pray and work, especially for the conversion of the Scandinavian people to the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-VNS_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hesselblad returned to her homeland of Sweden in 1923, where she was able to establish a community in <a href="/wiki/Djursholm" title="Djursholm">Djursholm</a>, while she worked nursing the sick poor. The new congregation was established in England in 1931 after receiving the approval of the Holy See.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> That same year, Hesselblad obtained the House of Saint Bridget in Rome for her new congregation. A foundation was made in India in 1937 which drew many new members.<sup id="cite_ref-SPQN_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SPQN-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her order received canonical approval on 7 July 1940.
</p><p>Hesselblad became known as "the second Bridget". During <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> – and after – she performed many charitable works on the behalf of the poor and those that suffered due to racial laws and also promoted a movement for peace that involved Christians and non-Christians, and she became part of their journey to faith and to the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The war also saw her save the lives of Jews who otherwise would have been murdered in <a href="/wiki/The_Holocaust" title="The Holocaust">the Holocaust</a> had it not been for her direct intervention.
</p><p>Her apostolic zeal contributed to the conversion of the Baptist minister Piero Chuminelli – author of a biographical account of Bridget of Sweden – and she also had close ties to the former Chief Rabbi of Rome <a href="/wiki/Israel_Zolli" title="Israel Zolli">Israel Zolli</a> (Eugenio) who converted to the faith in 1946.<sup id="cite_ref-CV_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CV-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Hesselblad's health declined around the time when officials prepared the canonical visit of her order. On 23 April 1957 she gave her blessing to the sisters and held her raised hands in a solemn gesture in which she murmured: "Go to Heaven with hands full of love and virtues". She received the sacraments thereafter and died in Rome on 24 April 1957 (<a href="/wiki/Easter_Wednesday" class="mw-redirect" title="Easter Wednesday">Easter Wednesday</a>) in the first hours of the morning.<sup id="cite_ref-CV_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CV-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Process_and_beatification">Process and beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Process and beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The sainthood process commenced in Rome in 1987 and concluded its work in 1990. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaimed Elisabeth Hasselblad to be a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Venerable Servant of God</a> on 26 March 1999 after he recognised that she had lived a model Christian life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. The miracle requested for her beatification was investigated in 1996 and received ratification in Rome on 17 October 1998. The pope approved it at the beginning of 2000 allowing for her beatification on the following 9 April 2000.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Elizabeth_Hesselblad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved the second miracle attributed to her on 14 December 2015 which would allow for her future canonization; the date was decided at an ordinary consistory of cardinals on 15 March 2016 and was celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 5 June 2016.<sup id="cite_ref-5June_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5June-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The miracle was investigated in the diocese of its origin in <a href="/wiki/Cuba" title="Cuba">Cuba</a> in February 2014 – the miracle in question occurred in the late 2000s – and was validated in Rome on 20 June 2014. A medical board approved the miracle in April 2015.
</p>
