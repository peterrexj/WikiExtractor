					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:BadIburgBennoII.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/BadIburgBennoII.JPG/220px-BadIburgBennoII.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/BadIburgBennoII.JPG/330px-BadIburgBennoII.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/BadIburgBennoII.JPG/440px-BadIburgBennoII.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="3072" /></a><figcaption>Modern statue of Bishop Benno II, Bad Iburg</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Benno II</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1020</span> – 27 July 1088) was <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Osnabr%C3%BCck" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Osnabrück">Bishop of Osnabrück</a> from 1068 until his death. He served as a close advisor and architect of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a>. In 1080 he founded the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> abbey of <a href="/wiki/Iburg_Castle" title="Iburg Castle">Iburg Castle</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benno_II_of_Osnabr%C3%BCck&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born at Löhningen (today part of <a href="/wiki/%C3%9Chlingen-Birkendorf" title="Ühlingen-Birkendorf">Ühlingen-Birkendorf</a>) in <a href="/wiki/Klettgau" title="Klettgau">Klettgau</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia" title="Duchy of Swabia">Swabia</a>, the son of a <a href="/wiki/Ministerialis" title="Ministerialis">ministerial</a> family. His parents sent him at an early age to the <a href="/wiki/Monastic_school" title="Monastic school">monastic schools</a> of <a href="/wiki/Strasbourg" title="Strasbourg">Straßburg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Island" title="Reichenau Island">Reichenau</a> where the learned <a href="/wiki/Hermann_of_Reichenau" title="Hermann of Reichenau">Herman Contractus</a> was then teaching.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Together with William I, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Strasbourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Strasbourg">Archbishop of Strasbourg</a>, he went on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> about 1040.
</p><p>Having completed his education, he taught for some time at the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_school" title="Cathedral school">cathedral school</a> of <a href="/wiki/Speyer_Cathedral" title="Speyer Cathedral">Speyer</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rhenish_Franconia" title="Rhenish Franconia">Rhenish Franconia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1047 he became teacher at the Benedictine school of <a href="/wiki/Goslar" title="Goslar">Goslar</a> in <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony" title="Duchy of Saxony">Saxony</a> and, shortly after, was made headmaster of the cathedral school at <a href="/wiki/Hildesheim_Cathedral" title="Hildesheim Cathedral">Hildesheim</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> where he reformed the education at the behest of Bishop <a href="/wiki/Azelin" title="Azelin">Azelin</a>. In Speyer, he had contacted the imperial court of the <a href="/wiki/Salian_dynasty" title="Salian dynasty">Salian</a> emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry III</a>. On account of his skill in architecture he was appointed imperial architect and, as such, supervised the construction of numerous castles and churches in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>, which flowed close to the Cathedral of Speyer, threatened to undermine its foundations, Benno saved the structure by changing the course of the river.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He and Bishop Azelin of Hildesheim accompanied Henry on a 1051 campaign against King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_I_of_Hungary" title="Andrew I of Hungary">Andrew I of Hungary</a>, whereby Benno distinguished himself in providing the forces' catering. Upon his return he was made <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a> of Hildesheim, <a href="/wiki/Archpriest" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a> at <a href="/wiki/Goslar_Cathedral" title="Goslar Cathedral">Goslar Cathedral</a> and royal <i>vicedominus</i> at the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Palace_of_Goslar" title="Imperial Palace of Goslar">Imperial Palace</a>.
</p><p>Benno's great talent was also recognized by the emperor's son and successor King <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a>. Stuck in the rising <a href="/wiki/Saxon_revolt_of_1073%E2%80%931075" title="Saxon revolt of 1073–1075">Saxon conflict</a>, Henry IV strongly relyed on his abilities as a master builder of several castles in the mainland of Saxony, such as the <a href="/wiki/Harzburg" title="Harzburg">Harzburg</a> finished in 1068. When the ambitious <a href="/wiki/Electorate_of_Cologne" title="Electorate of Cologne">Cologne</a> archbishop <a href="/wiki/Anno_II" title="Anno II">Anno II</a> tried to bring him over to his side, the emperor designated him the successor of Bishop Benno I of Osnabrück who had died the past year. Benno did a lot to promote the economic and agricultural development in his diocese, however, when in 1073 Henry's quarrels with the Saxon nobility culminated in open revolt, he had to seek protection at the royal court. He and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Liemar" title="Liemar">Liemar of Bremen</a> remained close companions of the king, when Henry fled from Saxony and had to consent to the humiliating <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Gerstungen" title="Treaty of Gerstungen">Treaty of Gerstungen</a>.
</p><p>During the fierce <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">Investiture Controversy</a> between <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a> and Henry IV, Benno for a long time sided with the emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He were among the clerics who, led by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Siegfried_I_(archbishop_of_Mainz)" title="Siegfried I (archbishop of Mainz)">Siegfried of Mainz</a>, declared Gregory deposed at the 1076 <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Worms_(1076)" title="Synod of Worms (1076)">Synod of Worms</a>. Benno, like numerous other <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Germany" title="Kingdom of Germany">German</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" title="Kingdom of Italy (Holy Roman Empire)">Italian</a> bishops, signed the formula of deposition and incurred ecclesiastical excommunication<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> a few weeks later. With some other excommunicated bishops, Benno hastened to Italy, where the pope freed them from the ban at <a href="/wiki/Canossa_Castle" title="Canossa Castle">Canossa Castle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He successfully arbitrated between the adversaries, before Henry himself arrived here to do penance on his <a href="/wiki/Walk_to_Canossa" class="mw-redirect" title="Walk to Canossa">Walk to Canossa</a>.
</p><p>However, the tranquility did not last long. In March 1077 several <a href="/wiki/Princes_of_the_Holy_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Princes of the Holy Roman Empire">princes</a> elected <a href="/wiki/Rudolf_of_Rheinfelden" title="Rudolf of Rheinfelden">Rudolf of Rheinfelden</a> antiking and in 1080 Pope Gregory again excommunicated Henry and all his supporters. While Rudolf was killed in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_on_the_Elster" title="Battle on the Elster">Battle on the Elster</a>, Benno and several bishops met in a synod at <a href="/wiki/Brixen" title="Brixen">Brixen</a>, where Gregory was again declared deposed and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Guibert of Ravenna</a> was elected <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a>. In turn, <a href="/wiki/Hermann_of_Salm" title="Hermann of Salm">Hermann of Salm</a> was elected antiking and Benno's diocesan territories were devastated by the insurgents. With Archbishop Liemar of Bremen he commissioned the anti-papal polemic by the Osnabrück canon <a href="/w/index.php?title=Wido_of_Osnabr%C3%BCck&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Wido of Osnabrück (page does not exist)">Wido</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> around 1085. (However, he did not author the <i>Gesta Romanae ecclesiae contra Hildebrandum</i> of <a href="/wiki/Beno_of_Santi_Martino_e_Silvestro" title="Beno of Santi Martino e Silvestro">Cardinal Beno</a>.) On the other hand, Benno tried to bring about a reconciliation,<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> winning over rebellious nobles such as Margrave <a href="/wiki/Egbert_II,_Margrave_of_Meissen" title="Egbert II, Margrave of Meissen">Egbert II of Meissen</a> and even negotiating with the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">Roman Curia</a> while the troops of Henry IV laid siege to the pope in the <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Castel Sant'Angelo</a>.
</p><p>Upon Gregory's death at <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a> in 1085, Benno retired to the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Iburg_Castle" title="Iburg Castle">Iburg Castle</a> near <a href="/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck" title="Osnabrück">Osnabrück</a>, which he had founded in 1080. In a little house near the monastery he lived according to the rule of the monks during the week, while on Sundays and holidays he assisted at his cathedral in Osnabrück.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He died at Iburg three years later.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benno_II_of_Osnabr%C3%BCck&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From humble origins, Benno made a remarkable career, not only because his abilities were needed and appreciated, but also due to his social skills and his beneficial relations. However, his close ties with ecclesiastical and secular authorities imposed considerable difficulties during the fierce conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Church_and_state_in_medieval_Europe" title="Church and state in medieval Europe">church and state</a> that overshadowed his lifetime.
</p><p>Strunck<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and Heitemeyer<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> include him in the list of saints. Kerler<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> says that he is invoked against <a href="/wiki/Grasshopper" title="Grasshopper">grasshoppers</a>, because he once dispersed them by his prayers.
</p>
