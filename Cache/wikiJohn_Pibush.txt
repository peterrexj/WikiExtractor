					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">English Catholic priest and martyr</div>
<p>
<b>John Pibush</b> (born at <a href="/wiki/Thirsk" title="Thirsk">Thirsk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, England; died at <a href="/w/index.php?title=St_Thomas%27s_Waterings&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St Thomas&#39;s Waterings (page does not exist)">St Thomas's Waterings</a>, <a href="/wiki/Camberwell" title="Camberwell">Camberwell</a>, 18 February 1601) was an English Catholic priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Pibush&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Joseph Gillow he was probably a son of Thomas Pibush, of <a href="/wiki/Great_Fencote" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Fencote">Great Fencote</a>, and Jane, sister to Peter Danby of <a href="/wiki/Scotton,_Richmondshire" title="Scotton, Richmondshire">Scotton</a>. He came to <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> on 4 August 1580, received <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a> and <a href="/wiki/Subdiaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Subdiaconate">subdiaconate</a> in September, and <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> in December, 1586, and was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> on 14 March 1587.
</p><p>He was sent on the English mission on 3 January 1588-9, arrested at <a href="/wiki/Moreton-in-Marsh" title="Moreton-in-Marsh">Moreton-in-Marsh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gloucestershire" title="Gloucestershire">Gloucestershire</a>, in 1593, and sent to London, where he arrived before 24 July. The <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council">Privy Council</a> committed him to the <a href="/wiki/Gatehouse" title="Gatehouse">Gatehouse</a> at Westminster, where he remained a year. He was then tried at the Gloucester Assizes under 27 Eliz., c. 2, for being a priest, but not sentenced, and was returned to Gloucester gaol, whence he escaped on 19 February (1594-5). The next day he was recaptured at <a href="/wiki/Matson,_Gloucester" title="Matson, Gloucester">Matson</a> and taken back to Gloucester gaol, whence he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>, London, and again tried under the same statute at Westminster on 1 July 1595.
</p><p>He was sentenced to suffer the penalties of <a href="/wiki/High_treason" class="mw-redirect" title="High treason">high treason</a> at St. Thomas's Waterings, and in the meantime was to be returned to the Marshalsea. However, by the end of the year he was in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Queen%27s_Bench_prison&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Queen&#39;s Bench prison (page does not exist)">Queen's Bench prison</a>, where he remained for more than five years. The sentence was carried out after one year's notice.
</p>
