<p><b>Serenidus of Saulges</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Cénéré de Saulges</i>, also variously spelled <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Sénéré</i></span>, <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Céneré</i></span>, <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Sérène</i></span>, or <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Sérenède</i></span>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;600</span>&#160;– c.<span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;680</span>) was a 7th-century Italian <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monk" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monk">Benedictine monk</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on May 7, with his brother <a href="/wiki/Serenicus" title="Serenicus">Serenicus</a>, or locally on August 16.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serenidus_of_Saulges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born into a noble family in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, a contemporary of fellow <a href="/wiki/Umbrians" class="mw-redirect" title="Umbrians">Umbrian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_I" title="Pope Martin I">Martin the Confessor</a>, his family's status could have had him appointed a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-deacon" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-deacon">cardinal-deacon</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Deciding upon a different path, he travelled to the <a href="/wiki/Maine_(province)" title="Maine (province)">province of Maine</a> in 649 during the reign of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> with his brother, <a href="/wiki/Serenicus" title="Serenicus">Serenicus</a>. His goal was to live as a life of secluded prayer-focused life <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and to preach to the local populations. He was one of a number of evangelizing hermits in Maine (e.g. Saint Longis, Saint Ernier, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fraimbaut&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Fraimbaut (page does not exist)">Saint Fraimbaut</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraimbault_de_Lassay" class="extiw" title="fr:Fraimbault de Lassay">fr</a>&#93;</span>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Tr%C3%A8che&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Trèche (page does not exist)">Saint Trèche</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Tr%C3%A8che" class="extiw" title="fr:Saint Trèche">fr</a>&#93;</span>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Contantien&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Contantien (page does not exist)">Saint Contantien</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantien_de_Javron" class="extiw" title="fr:Constantien de Javron">fr</a>&#93;</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is said to have caused a spring to well up on the site of his hermitage. After he died. the spring was venerated and became a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage site</a>. The chapel was rebuilt in 1849 and called the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Saint_C%C3%A9n%C3%A9r%C3%A9" title="Oratory of Saint Cénéré">Oratory of Saint Cénéré</a> near <a href="/wiki/Saulges" title="Saulges">Saulges</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Le Mans">diocese of Le Mans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Grosse_Dupéron_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grosse_Dupéron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His relics are kept at the oratory.
</p><p>He died in 680 following an illness and was initially buried in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Peter%27s_Church,_Saulges&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Church, Saulges (page does not exist)">Saint Peter's Church, Saulges</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Pierre_de_Saulges" class="extiw" title="fr:Église Saint-Pierre de Saulges">fr</a>&#93;</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-oratoire-saint-cenere_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oratoire-saint-cenere-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/8th_century" title="8th century">8th century</a> his relics were moved to the <a href="/wiki/Angers_Cathedral" title="Angers Cathedral">Cathedral Saint-Maurice</a> in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>; and were later returned and placed in the church at <a href="/wiki/Saint-C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9" title="Saint-Céneré">Saint-Céneré</a>. a reliquary containing a small part of his body is exposed in the north transept of the Saint Peter's church.<sup id="cite_ref-Girard_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Girard-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends_and_miracles">Legends and miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serenidus_of_Saulges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legends and miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:102px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG/100px-Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG" decoding="async" width="100" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG/150px-Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG/200px-Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2000" data-file-height="3008" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_C%C3%A9ner%C3%A9.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Source of the stream in the oratory</div></div></div>
<p>The source of the spring has been venerated, and a pilgrimage site, for more than a millennium. An annual pilgrimage takes place every August around the time of his <a href="/wiki/Saint%27s_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint&#39;s day">saint's day</a>. Many pilgrims come to venerate the relics of the saint in <a href="/wiki/Saulges" title="Saulges">Saulges</a> and see the miraculous <a href="/wiki/Spring_(hydrology)" title="Spring (hydrology)">spring</a> that Serenidus is said to have caused to flow.<sup id="cite_ref-stpierredumaine_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stpierredumaine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are legends about the spring: in one of them, a young pagan girl going to offer a <a href="/wiki/Libation" title="Libation">libation</a> to a water deity stopped to talk to Serenidus; as he prayed for her a tear from his eye fell on the ground, creating a hole that brought forth the spring.<sup id="cite_ref-Grosse_Dupéron_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grosse_Dupéron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In another legend, an unbeliever blocked the flow of the spring with a stick. Upon returning home he was seized with violent pains and prevented from urinating; his illness ceased when returning to the hermitage he unblocked the source. From these legends the spring is nicknamed "the little pissing saint."<sup id="cite_ref-Grosse_Dupéron_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grosse_Dupéron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-skyrock_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-skyrock-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Serenidus had a reputation as a <a href="/wiki/Miracle_worker#Thaumaturgy#Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracle worker">miracle worker</a>, he healed a <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leper</a> with prayer, he gave sight to a blind man with the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">sign of the cross</a>. His hagiography also reports that at the request of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Mans">Bishop of Le Mans</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Berecharius&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Berecharius (page does not exist)">Berecharius</a>, he saved the countryside from a drought, a famine and an epidemic.<sup id="cite_ref-Girard_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Girard-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Impact">Impact</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serenidus_of_Saulges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Impact">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg/220px-Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="179" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg/330px-Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg/440px-Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4181" data-file-height="3397" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saulges_-_%C3%89glise_Notre-Dame_-_Plaque_comm%C3%A9morative.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Commemorative plaque of the soldiers of Saulges who died during the world wars</div></div></div>
<p>Serenidus is influential locally. Many representations of the saint are seen throughout <a href="/wiki/Mayenne_(commune)" title="Mayenne (commune)">Mayenne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sarthe" title="Sarthe">Sarthe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maine-et-Loire" title="Maine-et-Loire">Maine-et-Loire</a>, both as statues or paintings. Serenidus is portrayed wearing a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal%27s_habit" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal&#39;s habit">cardinal's habit</a>. There is bas-relief in the <a href="/wiki/Angers_Cathedral" title="Angers Cathedral">Cathedral Saint-Maurice</a> in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a> where his relics were once housed.<sup id="cite_ref-angot_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-angot-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is also found in a commemorative plaque of the soldiers of Saulges who died during the world wars found in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre_Dame_de_Saulges_church&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre Dame de Saulges church (page does not exist)">Notre Dame de Saulges church</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q33111200#sitelinks-wikipedia" class="extiw" title="d:Special:EntityPage/Q33111200"><span title="&quot;Notre Dame de Saulges church&quot; in other languages">Wikidata</span></a>&#93;</span> (pictured).
</p>
