<p><b>Arnold of Selenhofen</b> (c. 1095/1100 – 24 June 1160) was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">archbishop of Mainz</a> from 1153 to his assassination in the Benedictine abbey St. Jakob, where he took shelter from the raging crowd.
</p><p>He was born to a wealthy Mainz family. He studied at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a> and became the treasurer of the archdiocese of Mainz, then provost of the <a href="/wiki/Mainz_Cathedral" title="Mainz Cathedral">cathedral</a>. <a href="/wiki/Conrad_III_of_Germany" title="Conrad III of Germany">Conrad III</a> made him <a href="/wiki/Archchancellor" title="Archchancellor">archchancellor</a> of Germany in 1151 and <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa" title="Frederick Barbarossa">Frederick Barbarossa</a> made him archbishop in 1153.
</p><p>His administration of justice was unforgiving. While he was away in Italy working for the recognition of the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159-1164)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159-1164)">Antipope Victor IV</a> in 1159, the leading citizens rebelled. When he returned, he was murdered in front of the monastery of St. Jakob. He was buried in the church of <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Maria_ad_gradus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Maria ad gradus (page does not exist)">St. Maria ad gradus</a> (<a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">St. Mary</a> of the Steps).
</p>
