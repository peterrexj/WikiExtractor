<p><b>Andronicus, Probus (Provos), and Tarachus (Tharacus, Tarachos)</b> were <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecution (about 304 AD). According to tradition, Tarachus was beaten with stones. Probus was thrashed with <a href="/wiki/Whip" title="Whip">whips</a>, his back and sides were pierced with heated <a href="/wiki/Spit_(cooking_aide)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spit (cooking aide)">spits</a>; finally he also was cut up with <a href="/wiki/Knives" class="mw-redirect" title="Knives">knives</a>. Andronicus was also cut to pieces with knives.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andronicus,_Probus,_and_Tarachus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the <i>Acts</i>, <b>Tarachus</b> (ca. 239- 304), a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> who was a native of Claudiopolis in <a href="/wiki/Isauria" title="Isauria">Isauria</a> and a former soldier,<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the plebeian <b>Probus</b> of <a href="/wiki/Side,_Turkey" title="Side, Turkey">Side</a> in <a href="/wiki/Pamphylia" title="Pamphylia">Pamphylia</a>, and the patrician <b>Andronicus</b>, who belonged to a prominent family of <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a>, were tried by the governor Numerian Maximus and horribly tortured three times in various cities, including <a href="/wiki/Tarsus_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Tarsus (city)">Tarsus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mopsuestia" title="Mopsuestia">Mopsuestia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Anazarbus" title="Anazarbus">Anazarbus</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg/200px-Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="137" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg/300px-Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg/400px-Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1744" data-file-height="1191" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_059.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Martyrdom of Andronicus, Probus, and Tarachus</div></div></div>
<p>According to tradition, Tarachus was beaten with stones. Probus was thrashed with <a href="/wiki/Whip" title="Whip">whips</a>, his feet were burned with red hot irons, his back and sides were pierced with heated <a href="/wiki/Spit_(cooking_aide)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spit (cooking aide)">spits</a>; finally he also was cut up with <a href="/wiki/Knives" class="mw-redirect" title="Knives">knives</a>. Andronicus was also cut to pieces with knives.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They were then condemned to death by wild beasts, and when the animals would not touch them in the <a href="/wiki/Amphitheatre" title="Amphitheatre">amphitheatre</a> they were put to death with the sword. Three men, named Marcian, Felix, and Verus, witnessed their martyrdom and added an <a href="/wiki/Epilogue" title="Epilogue">epilogue</a> to the saints' <i>Acts</i>.  They retrieved the bodies of the three saints, buried them, and watched over them the rest of their lives, requesting that they be buried in the same vault as the martyrs at the end of theirs.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are two accounts of their martyrdom, the first account being held by <a href="/wiki/Thierry_Ruinart" title="Thierry Ruinart">Thierry Ruinart</a> to be entirely authentic. Harnack, however, expressed doubts as to the genuineness of the account, and <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> puts the martyrdom in the class of legends of martyrs that he calls "historical romances".<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>Their feast is celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> on October 11, and in the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox Church</a> on October 12.
</p>
