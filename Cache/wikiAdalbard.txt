<p><b>Adalbert I of Ostrevent</b> (died 652) was a 7th-century Frankish nobleman of the court of King <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> of France.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and is commemorated on both 2 February (his <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a>) and 2 May (<a href="/wiki/Translation" title="Translation">translation</a> of his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> to <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> in 1221).  
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adalbald was the son of Gerberga, daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Magister_militum" title="Magister militum">magister militum</a> <a href="/wiki/Richomeres" title="Richomeres">Richomer</a> and Gertrude of Hamage (died 649), who founded a nunnery at <a href="/wiki/Wandignies-Hamage" title="Wandignies-Hamage">Wandignies-Hamage</a> near Douai. According to <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, Adalbald had two brothers, Sigefrid, count of Ponthieu, and <a href="/wiki/Erchinoald" title="Erchinoald">Archenald</a>, Mayor of the Palace to Clovis II, son of <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert</a>, to whom they were related.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His relationship with Merovingian King Dagobert has been proposed to have been through his mother Gerberga, and her putative sister <a href="/wiki/Bertrude" title="Bertrude">Bertrude</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> (or perhaps Haldetrude, Clothar's first wife).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> However, Karine Ugé argues that the connection between Adalbald and Erchinoald is a fiction developed by the canons of Saint-Amé (Saint Amatus) at Douai to enhance a connection with Rictrude.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbald was a leading noble and claimed lordship of Douai. He was a disciple of <a href="/wiki/Amand_of_Maastricht" class="mw-redirect" title="Amand of Maastricht">Amand of Maastricht</a>. In 630, Adalbald founded <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes_Abbey" title="Marchiennes Abbey">Marchiennes Abbey</a>. During a military expedition in Gascony, he met and married <a href="/wiki/Rictrude" title="Rictrude">Rictrude</a> of <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes" title="Marchiennes">Marchiennes</a>, daughter of Ernoldo, lord of Toulouse,<sup id="cite_ref-santi_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> despite the opposition of both families. Although her parents approved, others opposed a marriage to a Frank. The marriage was said to be happy.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> They had four children: <a href="/wiki/Maurontius_of_Douai" title="Maurontius of Douai">Maurant</a>, abbot of Breuil (died 702); <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bie_(sainte)" class="extiw" title="fr:Eusébie (sainte)">Eusebia of Douai</a> (also known as Ysoie) (died c. 680), abbess of Hamage Abbey near Arras; <a href="/wiki/Adalsinda" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalsinda">Adalsinda</a>, a nun at Hamage (died 714); and <a href="/wiki/Clotsinda" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotsinda">Clotsinda</a> (died 714). All are venerated as saints. 
</p><p>The couple opened their castle to the poor and disadvantaged. The hermit-monk <a href="/wiki/Richarius" title="Richarius">Richarius</a> was a family friend.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  Adalbald's wife made Marchiennes Abbey a <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> in 643.
</p><p>Adalbard was assassinated in obscure circumstances in 652, near Périgueux during a subsequent expedition to Aquitaine, probably by his wife's relatives still bitter about the marriage to an enemy of her people. His body was returned to <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a> and buried at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Amand_Abbey" title="Saint-Amand Abbey">Saint-Amand Abbey</a> where he was venerated as a martyr, as his death had taken place in a region which largely had not yet adopted Christianity. His name, however, is not currently mentioned in the liturgical calendars of Cambrai and Lille.
</p><p>After the death of Adalbald, his brother Archenald (Erkenwald) rebuilt the castle of Douai (which gave rise to the town) and founded the church of Our Lady, now dedicated to  Saint Amatus.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
