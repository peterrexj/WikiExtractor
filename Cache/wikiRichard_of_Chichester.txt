<p><b>Richard of Chichester</b> (1197&#160;– 3 April 1253), also known as <b>Richard de Wych</b>, is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> (<a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> 1262) who was Bishop of Chichester.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Chichester_Cathedral" title="Chichester Cathedral">Chichester Cathedral</a> a <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> dedicated to Richard had become a richly decorated centre of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. In 1538, during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>, the shrine was plundered and destroyed by order of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Thomas Cromwell</a>. Richard of Chichester is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Sussex" title="Sussex">Sussex</a> in southern England; since 2007, his translated saint's day of 16 June has been celebrated as <a href="/wiki/Sussex_Day" title="Sussex Day">Sussex Day</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_of_Chichester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Richard was born in Burford, near the town of Wyche (modern <a href="/wiki/Droitwich" class="mw-redirect" title="Droitwich">Droitwich</a>, <a href="/wiki/Worcestershire" title="Worcestershire">Worcestershire</a>) and was an orphan member of a gentry family.<sup id="cite_ref-BHO_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On the death of their parents Richard's elder brother was heir to the estates but he was not old enough to inherit, so the lands were subject to a <a href="/wiki/Tenant-in-chief" title="Tenant-in-chief">feudal wardship</a>. On coming of age his brother took possession of his lands, but was required to pay a <a href="/wiki/Feudal_relief" title="Feudal relief">medieval form of death duty</a> that left the family so impoverished that Richard had to work for him on the farm.<sup id="cite_ref-lower242_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower242-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His brother also made Richard heir to the estate.<sup id="cite_ref-lower242_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower242-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to Richard's biographers, friends tried to arrange a match with a <i>certain noble lady</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-lower242_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower242-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However Richard rejected the proposed match, suggesting that his brother might marry her instead; he also reconveyed the estates back to his brother, preferring a life of study and the church.<sup id="cite_ref-lower243_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower243-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Educated at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">University of Oxford</a>, Richard soon began to teach in the university.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> From there he proceeded to <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">Paris</a> and then <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">Bologna</a>, where he distinguished himself by his proficiency in canon law. On returning to England in 1235, Richard was elected Oxford's chancellor.<sup id="cite_ref-stephens84_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens84-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His former tutor, <a href="/wiki/Edmund_of_Abingdon" title="Edmund of Abingdon">Edmund of Abingdon</a>, had become <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">archbishop of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Richard shared Edmund's ideals of clerical reform and supported papal rights even against the king.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  In 1237, Archbishop Edmund appointed Richard chancellor of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Canterbury" title="Diocese of Canterbury">diocese of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Richard joined the archbishop during his exile at <a href="/wiki/Pontigny" title="Pontigny">Pontigny</a>, and was with him when the archbishop died circa 1240.<sup id="cite_ref-stephens84_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens84-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Richard then decided to become a priest and studied theology for two years with the Dominicans at <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Upon returning to England, Richard became the parish priest at <a href="/wiki/Charing" title="Charing">Charing</a> and at <a href="/wiki/Deal,_Kent" title="Deal, Kent">Deal</a>, but soon was reappointed chancellor of Canterbury by the new archbishop <a href="/wiki/Boniface_of_Savoy_(bishop)" title="Boniface of Savoy (bishop)">Boniface of Savoy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1244 Richard was elected Bishop of Chichester. <a href="/wiki/Henry_III_of_England" title="Henry III of England">Henry III</a> and part of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">chapter</a> refused to accept him, the king favouring the candidature of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Passelewe" title="Robert Passelewe">Robert Passelewe</a> (d. 1252).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Archbishop Boniface refused to confirm Passelew, so both sides appealed to the pope.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The king confiscated the see's properties and revenues, but <a href="/wiki/Innocent_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent IV">Innocent IV</a> confirmed Richard's election and consecrated him bishop at Lyons in March 1245.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Handbook239_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook239-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Richard then returned to Chichester, but the king refused to restore the see's properties for two years, and then did so only after being threatened with <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunication</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Henry III forbade anyone to house or feed Richard.<sup id="cite_ref-Seward_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seward-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>   At first, Richard lived at <a href="/wiki/Tarring,_West_Sussex" title="Tarring, West Sussex">Tarring</a> in the house of his friend Simon, the parish priest of Tarring, visited his entire diocese on foot, and cultivated figs in his spare time.<sup id="cite_ref-oup1_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oup1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Seward_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seward-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard's private life was supposed to have displayed rigid frugality and temperance.<sup id="cite_ref-stephens88_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens88-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Richard was an ascetic who wore a <a href="/wiki/Hair-shirt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hair-shirt">hair-shirt</a> and refused to eat off silver.<sup id="cite_ref-Seward_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seward-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  He kept his diet simple and rigorously excluded animal flesh; having been a <a href="/wiki/Christian_vegetarianism" title="Christian vegetarianism">vegetarian</a> since his days at Oxford.<sup id="cite_ref-stephens88_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens88-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard was merciless to <a href="/wiki/Usury" title="Usury">usurers</a>, corrupt clergy and priests who mumbled the Mass.  He was also a stickler for clerical privilege.<sup id="cite_ref-Seward_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Seward-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard's <a href="/wiki/Episcopate" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopate">episcopate</a> was marked by the favour which he showed to the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a>, a house of this order at Orléans having sheltered him during his stay in France, and by his earnestness in preaching a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  After dedicating <a href="/wiki/St_Edmund%27s_Chapel" title="St Edmund&#39;s Chapel">St Edmund's Chapel</a> at <a href="/wiki/Dover" title="Dover">Dover</a>, he died aged 56 at the <a href="/wiki/Maison_Dieu,_Dover" title="Maison Dieu, Dover">Maison Dieu, Dover</a> at midnight on 3 April 1253, where the Pope had ordered him to preach a crusade.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook239_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook239-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> His internal organs were removed and placed in that chapel's altar. Richard's body was then carried to Chichester and buried, according to his wishes, in the chapel on the north side of the nave, dedicated to his patron St. Edmund.<sup id="cite_ref-atkinson18_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atkinson18-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> His remains were translated to a new shrine in 1276.<sup id="cite_ref-atkinson18_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atkinson18-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopal_statutes">Episcopal statutes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_of_Chichester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Episcopal statutes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stone_head,_Rottingdean.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Stone_head%2C_Rottingdean.jpg/220px-Stone_head%2C_Rottingdean.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Stone_head%2C_Rottingdean.jpg/330px-Stone_head%2C_Rottingdean.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Stone_head%2C_Rottingdean.jpg/440px-Stone_head%2C_Rottingdean.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a><figcaption>Sculpture of Richard of Chichester outside <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret%27s_Church,_Rottingdean" title="St Margaret&#39;s Church, Rottingdean">St Margaret's Church, Rottingdean</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>After the full rights of the see and its revenues were returned to him in 1246, the new bishop showed much eagerness to reform the manners and morals of his clergy, and also to introduce greater order and reverence into the services of the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-stephens88_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens88-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
Richard overruled Henry on several occasions.  Richard defrocked a priest who had seduced a nun out of her convent, turning aside a petition from the king in the priest's favour.<sup id="cite_ref-lower244_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower244-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard was militant in protecting the clergy from abuse. The townsmen of <a href="/wiki/Lewes" title="Lewes">Lewes</a> violated the right of <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary" title="Sanctuary">sanctuary</a> by seizing a criminal in church and <a href="/wiki/Lynching" title="Lynching">lynching</a> him, and Richard made them exhume the body and give it a proper burial in consecrated ground.<sup id="cite_ref-stephens88_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens88-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He also imposed severe penance on <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knights</a> who attacked priests.<sup id="cite_ref-lower244_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower244-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard produced a body of statutes with the aid of his chapter, for the organisation of the church in his diocese and the expected conduct of its clergy. It seems that many of the clergy still secretly married, though such alliances were not recognised by canon law, and as such their women's status was that of a mistress or concubine. The Bishop endeavoured to suppress the practice in his diocese with relentless austerity.
</p><p>By Richard's statutes [the following is a close paraphrase not an exact quote]:<sup id="cite_ref-stephens88_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stephens88-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote><p>It was decreed that married clergy should be deprived of their <a href="/wiki/Benefice" title="Benefice">benefices</a>; their <a href="/wiki/Concubinage" title="Concubinage">concubines</a> were to be denied the privileges of the church during their lives and also after death; they were pronounced incapable of inheriting any property from their husbands, and any such bequests would be donated for the upkeep of the cathedral. A vow of chastity was to be required of candidates for ordination. Rectors were expected to reside in their parishes, to be hospitable and charitable and tithes were to be paid on all annual crops. Anyone who did not pay their tithe would not be granted penance until they did.<br />
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Vicars" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicars">Vicars</a> were to be priests and have only one freehold to live on, they were not allowed to have another parish held under an assumed name.<br />
<a href="/wiki/Deacons" class="mw-redirect" title="Deacons">Deacons</a> were not to be allowed to receive confessions or to provide penances, or to baptise except in the absence of a priest. Children had to be confirmed within a year of baptism. The <a href="/wiki/Apostles%27_Creed" title="Apostles&#39; Creed">Apostles' Creed</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Lord%27s_Prayer" title="Lord&#39;s Prayer">Lord's Prayer</a> were to be learned in the mother tongue; priests were to celebrate mass in clean robes, to use a silver or golden <a href="/wiki/Chalice" title="Chalice">chalice</a>; thoroughly clean <a href="/wiki/Altar_cloth" title="Altar cloth">corporals</a> and at least two consecrated <a href="/wiki/Altar_cloth" title="Altar cloth">palls</a> were to be placed on the altar; the cross was to be planted in front of the <a href="/wiki/Officiant" title="Officiant">celebrant</a>; the bread was to be of the purest wheaten flour, the wine mixed with water. The elements were not to be kept more than seven days; when carried to a sick person to be enclosed in a <a href="/wiki/Pyx" title="Pyx">pyx</a>, and the priest to be preceded by a cross; a candle, holy water and bell.<br />
Practices such as gambling at baptisms and marriages is strictly forbidden.<br />
<a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">Archdeacons</a> were to administer justice for their proper fees, not demanding more either for rushing or delaying the business. They were to visit the churches regularly, to see that the services were duly ministered, the vessels and vestments are in proper order, the canon of the mass correctly observed and distinctly read, as also the <i>hours</i>. Priests who clipped or slurred the words by rushing were to be suspended.<br />
The clergy should wear their proper dress and not imitate what the lay people wore. They were not allowed to wear their hair long or have romantic entanglements. The names of excommunicated persons to be read out four times a year in the parish churches.<br />
</p><p>
A copy of these statutes was to be kept by every priest in the diocese and be brought by him to the episcopal synod.</p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrine">Shrine</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_of_Chichester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Shrine">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Altar designed by Robert Potter. Tapestry designed by John Piper. Icon of St Richard (bottom right) Sergei Fyodorov." src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg/220px-Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg/330px-Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg/440px-Tapestry_in_Chichester_Cathedral_-_geograph.org.uk_-_291431.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="469" /></a><figcaption>The modern day site of the shrine of St Richard in Chichester Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-atkinson18_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atkinson18-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Richard.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Saint_Richard.jpg/220px-Saint_Richard.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Saint_Richard.jpg/330px-Saint_Richard.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Saint_Richard.jpg/440px-Saint_Richard.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2644" data-file-height="3525" /></a><figcaption>Icon of St Richard in Chichester Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<p>It was generally believed that miracles were wrought at Richard's tomb in Chichester cathedral, which was long a popular place of pilgrimage, and in 1262, just 9 years after his death, he was canonized at <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_IV" title="Pope Urban IV">Pope Urban IV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-odnb1_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odnb1-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Richard's feast day is on 3 April in the West, but because this date generally falls within Lent or Eastertide this is normally translated to 16 June in some provinces of the Anglican Communion (the Anglican Church of Canada, for example, commemorates Richard on 3 April), which venerates St. Richard more widely than does the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-odnb1_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odnb1-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Richard is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/June_16" title="June 16">16 June</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Richard furnished the chronicler, <a href="/wiki/Matthew_Paris" title="Matthew Paris">Matthew Paris</a>, with material for the life of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Rich" class="mw-redirect" title="Edmund Rich">St. Edmund Rich</a>, and instituted the offerings for the cathedral at Chichester which were known later as "St. Richard's pence".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911293-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the episcopate of the first <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> bishop of Chichester, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Sampson" title="Richard Sampson">Richard Sampson</a>, King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a>, through his <a href="/wiki/Vicar-General" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicar-General">Vicar-General</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Thomas Cromwell</a> ordered the destruction of Richard's shrine in <a href="/wiki/Chichester" title="Chichester">Chichester</a> cathedral in 1538.<sup id="cite_ref-tatton143_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tatton143-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Forasmuch as we have lately been informed that in our cathedral church of Chichester there hath been used long heretofore, and yet at this day is used, much superstition and a certain kind of idolatry about the shrine and bones of a certain bishop of the same, whom they call Saint Richard, and a certain resort there of common people, which being men of simplicity are seduced by the instigation of some of the clergy, who take advantage of their credulity to ascribe miracles of healing and other virtues to the said bones, that God only hath authority to grant. . . . . We have appointed you, with all convenient diligence to repair unto the said cathedral church, and to take away the shrine and bones of that bishop called Saint Richard, with all ornaments to the said shrine belonging, and all other the reliques and reliquaries, the silver, the gold, and all the jewels belonging to said shrine, and that not only shall you see them to be safely and surely conveyed unto our Tower of London there to be bestowed and placed at your arrival, but also ye shall see both the place where the shrine was kept, destroyed even to the ground and all such other images of the said church, where about any notable superstition is used, to be carried and conveyed away, so that our subjects shall by them in no ways be deceived hereafter, but that they,pay to Almighty God and to no earthly creature such honour as is due unto him the Creator. . . . . Given under our privy seal at our manor of Hampton Court, the 14th day of Dec., in the 30th year of our reign (1538). <br />
<small><i>Document issued by Thomas Cromwell on behalf of Henry VIII. </i><sup id="cite_ref-lower249_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lower249-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup></small>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The document ordering the destruction of the shrine was issued to a Sir William Goring of Burton and a William <a href="/wiki/Ernley" class="mw-redirect" title="Ernley">Ernley</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hobbs61_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbs61-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> They received £40 for carrying out the commission on 20 December 1538.<sup id="cite_ref-hobbs61_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hobbs61-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Breviary,_circa_1300.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Breviary%2C_circa_1300.jpg/220px-Breviary%2C_circa_1300.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="313" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Breviary%2C_circa_1300.jpg/330px-Breviary%2C_circa_1300.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/Breviary%2C_circa_1300.jpg/440px-Breviary%2C_circa_1300.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1078" data-file-height="1536" /></a><figcaption>A breviary in Latin probably written for use in the neighborhood of Chichester. The Sarum Breviary contains a translation from St Richard of Chichester present at f.380v.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The Shrine of St. Richard had, up to this point, enjoyed a level of popularity approaching that accorded to <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a> at Canterbury. It seems that someone associated with the parish of <a href="/wiki/West_Wittering" title="West Wittering">West Wittering</a> in Sussex, possibly William Ernley, using his position as royal commissioner for the destruction of St. Richard's Shrine, may have spirited away the relics and bones of St. Richard and hidden them in their own parish church,  as there are persistent legends of the presence there, of the remains of the saint:
</p>
<blockquote><p>The Lady Chapel not only contains the Saxon Cross but also an ancient broken marble slab engraved with a Bishop's pastoral staff and a Greek cross believed to have come from a reliquary containing the relics of St. Richard of Chichester, a 13th century bishop who often visited West Wittering. Part of his story is shown in the beautiful red, white and gold altar frontal presented by Yvonne Rusbridge in 1976. On the left St Richard is shown feeding the hungry in Cakeham and on the right leading his followers from the church, his candle miraculously alight despite the gust of wind which blew out all the other candles.<br />
<small>Extract from the description of the parish church of St Peter and St Paul, West Wittering.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup></small></p></blockquote>
<p>The modern St Richard's Shrine is located in the retro-quire of Chichester cathedral and was re-established in 1930 by <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Stuart_Duncan_Jones" class="mw-redirect" title="Arthur Stuart Duncan Jones">Dean Duncan Jones</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-foster65_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foster65-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In 1987 during the restoration of the <a href="/wiki/La_Lucerne_Abbey" title="La Lucerne Abbey">Abbey of La Lucerne</a>, in Normandy, the lower part of a man's arm was discovered in a reliquary, the relic was thought to be Richard's.<sup id="cite_ref-foster70_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foster70-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> After examination, to establish its provenance, the relic was offered to Bishop <a href="/wiki/Eric_Kemp" title="Eric Kemp">Eric Kemp</a> and received into the cathedral on 15 June 1990.<sup id="cite_ref-foster70_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foster70-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The relic was buried in 1991 below the St Richard altar.<sup id="cite_ref-foster70_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foster70-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> A further relic, together with an authentication certificate, was offered from Rome at the same time and is now housed at the bishop's chapel in Chichester.<sup id="cite_ref-foster70_22-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foster70-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The modern shrine of Richard contains an altar that was designed by Robert Potter, a tapestry designed by Ursula Benker-Schirmer (partly woven in her studio in Bavaria and partly at the <a href="/wiki/West_Dean_College" title="West Dean College">West Dean College</a>) and an icon designed by <a href="/wiki/Sergei_Fyodorov_(painter)" title="Sergei Fyodorov (painter)">Sergei Fyodorov</a> that shows St Richard in episcopal vestments, his hand raised in blessing towards the viewer, but also in supplication to the figure of Christ who appears to him from heaven .<sup id="cite_ref-atkinson18_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-atkinson18-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-foster70_22-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foster70-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prayer">Prayer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_of_Chichester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Prayer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Richard is widely remembered today for the popular <a href="/wiki/Prayer" title="Prayer">prayer</a> ascribed to him:
</p>
<blockquote><p><i>Thanks be to Thee, my Lord Jesus Christ</i><br />
</p><p><i>For all the benefits Thou hast given me,</i><br />
<i>For all the pains and insults Thou hast borne for me.</i><br />
<i>O most merciful Redeemer, friend and brother,</i><br />
<i>May I know Thee more clearly,</i><br />
<i>Love Thee more dearly,</i><br />
<i>Follow Thee more nearly.</i><sup id="cite_ref-bullock48_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bullock48-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Richard is supposed to have recited the prayer on his deathbed, surrounded by the clergy of the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-stone78_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stone78-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The words were transcribed, in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, by his confessor <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Bocking" title="Ralph Bocking">Ralph Bocking</a>, a Dominican friar, and were eventually published in the <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i>, an encyclopedic text in 68 folio volumes of documents examining the lives of Christian saints. The <a href="/wiki/British_Library" title="British Library">British Library</a> copy, contains what is believed to be Bocking's transcription of the prayer:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p><i>Gratias tibi ago, Domine Jesu Christe, de omnibus beneficiis quae mihi praestitisti;</i><br />
<i>pro poenis et opprobriis, quae pro me pertulisti;</i><br />
<i>propter quae planctus ille lamentabilis vere tibi competebat.</i><br />
<i>Non est dolor similis sicut dolor meus.</i><sup id="cite_ref-stone78_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stone78-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-acta10_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-acta10-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
<br />
</p>
</blockquote>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg/170px-Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg/255px-Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg/340px-Chichester_Cathedral_and_St_Richard_-_geograph.org.uk_-_229843.jpg 2x" data-file-width="480" data-file-height="640" /></a><figcaption>The statue of St Richard near the west door of Chichester Cathedral.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Whoever translated the Latin into English was obviously skilled in his craft as he managed to produce a rhyming triplet, namely <i>"clearly, dearly, nearly"</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-stone78_24-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stone78-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> However, versions of St Richard's prayer, before the 20th century, did not contain the triplet and it is thought that the first version that did was published in <i>"The Churchmans Prayer Manual"</i> by G.R.Bullock-Webster in 1913.<sup id="cite_ref-bullock48_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bullock48-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>  The first use of the rhyming triplet in a hymn was in the <i>"Mirfield Mission Hymnbook"</i> of 1922, and the first use of the phrase <i>"Day by Day"</i> was in the  <i>"Songs of Praise, Enlarged Edition"</i> published in 1931.<sup id="cite_ref-stone78_24-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stone78-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-williams_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The author who is credited with translating the prayer from the original <i>Acta Sanctorum</i> and bringing it to public notice, was <a href="/wiki/Cecil_Headlam" title="Cecil Headlam">Cecil Headlam</a> in 1898.<sup id="cite_ref-headlamv_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-headlamv-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> The following version in the  <i>"Prayers of Saints"</i> is quite different from the one that is familiar today&#160;:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p><i>THE DYING PRAYER OF S. RICHARD, </i><br />
<small><i>Bishop of Chichester.</i></small><br />
<i>LORD JESU CHRIST, I thank Thee for </i><br />
<i>all the blessings Thou hast given me, </i><br />
<i>and for all the sufferings and shame Thou </i><br />
<i>didst endure for me, on which account that </i><br />
<i>pitiable cry of sorrow was Thine&#160;: " Behold and </i><br />
<i>see, if there was any sorrow like unto My </i><br />
<i>sorrow&#160;! " Thou knowest, Lord, how willing </i><br />
<i>I should be to bear insult, and pain, and death </i><br />
<i>for Thee&#160;; therefore have mercy on me,  for to </i><br />
<i>Thee do I commend my spirit. Amen</i><sup id="cite_ref-headlam33_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-headlam33-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
<br />
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The prayer was adapted for the song <a href="/wiki/Day_by_Day_(Godspell_song)" title="Day by Day (Godspell song)"><i>"Day by Day"</i></a> in the musical <a href="/wiki/Godspell" title="Godspell"><i>Godspell (1971)</i></a>, with music by <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Schwartz_(composer)" title="Stephen Schwartz (composer)">Stephen Schwartz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The words used, with a few embellishments, were based on the following from  <i>"Songs of Praise, Enlarged Edition"</i>:<sup id="cite_ref-stone78_24-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stone78-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote>
<p><i>Day by day,</i><br />
<i>Dear Lord, of thee three things I pray:</i><br />
<i>To see thee more clearly,</i><br />
<i>Love thee more dearly,</i><br />
<i>Follow thee more nearly,</i><br />
<i>Day by Day.</i><sup id="cite_ref-williams_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
<br />
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>A portion of the prayer has been incorporated into the liturgy of the Roman Catholic Church for use by the <a href="/wiki/Personal_ordinariate" title="Personal ordinariate">personal ordinariates</a> established to bring the Anglican patrimony into the Church.  Observance of the memorial of Saint Richard of Chichester on 16 June is inscribed in the calendar of <a href="/wiki/Divine_Worship:_The_Missal" title="Divine Worship: The Missal">Divine Worship: The Missal</a> and the following <a href="/wiki/Collect" title="Collect">Collect</a> is provided:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p><i>MOST merciful Redeemer,</i><br />
<i>who gavest to thy Bishop Richard a love of learning,</i><br />
<i>a zeal for souls, and a devotion to the poor:</i><br />
<i>grant that, encouraged by his example,</i><br />
<i>and aided by his prayers,</i><br />
<i>we may know thee more clearly,</i><br />
<i>love thee more dearly,</i><br />
<i>and follow thee more nearly,</i><br />
<i>day by day;</i><br />
<i>who livest and reignest with the Father</i><br />
<i>in the unity of the Holy Spirit, ever one God,</i><br />
<i>world without end. <b>Amen.</b></i><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
<br />
</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Current_patronage_and_festivals">Current patronage and festivals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_of_Chichester&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Current patronage and festivals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg/220px-Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="131" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg/330px-Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg/440px-Flag_of_Saint_Richard_of_Chichester.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="794" data-file-height="474" /></a><figcaption>Flag attributed to Saint Richard, which is flown on <a href="/wiki/Sussex_Day" title="Sussex Day">Sussex Day</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Richard is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the county of <a href="/wiki/Sussex" title="Sussex">Sussex</a> in England. Since 2007, his translated <a href="/wiki/Saint%27s_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint&#39;s day">saint's day</a>, 16 June, has been celebrated as Sussex Day.<sup id="cite_ref-WSCC-SxDay_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WSCC-SxDay-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
Richard is honoured with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">liturgical calendar of the Episcopal Church (USA)</a> on 3 April,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> which is also the date for his commemoration in the new Roman Martyrology of 2004 for the Roman Catholic Church.
</p>
