<p><b>Pope Nicholas IV</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Nicolaus IV</i>; 30 September 1227 – 4 April 1292), born <b>Girolamo Masci</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 22 February 1288 to his death, on 4 April 1292. He was the first Franciscan to be elected pope.<sup id="cite_ref-McBrien_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McBrien-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jerome Masci (Girolamo Masci) was born on 30 September 1227 at Lisciano, near <a href="/wiki/Ascoli_Piceno" title="Ascoli Piceno">Ascoli Piceno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was a pious, peace-loving man whose goals as a Franciscan friar were to protect the Church, promote the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">crusades</a>, and root out <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a>. According to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Heinrich_of_Rebdorf&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Heinrich of Rebdorf (page does not exist)">Heinrich of Rebdorf</a>, he was a Doctor of Theology.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, he had been elected the Order's superior (minister) for Dalmatia during the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> general chapter held at Pisa in 1272.   <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a> (1271-1276), was sending a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legate</a> to the Byzantine emperor, <a href="/wiki/Michael_VIII_Palaiologos" title="Michael VIII Palaiologos">Michael VIII Palaiologos</a>, in 1272, to invite the participation of Byzantine prelates in the Second Council of Lyons. The pope's ambition was to achieve a reunion of Eastern and Western Christendom.  St <a href="/wiki/Bonaventure" title="Bonaventure">Bonaventure</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Minister_general" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister general">minister general</a> of the Order of Friars Minor (<a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscans</a>), was asked to select four Franciscans to accompany the legation as nuncios. He chose Friar Jerome Masci as one of the four.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> When <a href="/wiki/Bonaventure" title="Bonaventure">Bonaventure</a> died suddenly during the fifth session of the Order's General Chapter at Lyons on 15 July 1274, Friar Jerome Masci was elected to succeed him as the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> minister general, even though he was absent at the time, only then returning with the Byzantine delegates from the embassy to Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jerome was the associate of <a href="/wiki/John_of_Vercelli" title="John of Vercelli">John of Vercelli</a>, <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">master general</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>, when the latter was sent by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_III" title="Pope Nicholas III">Pope Nicholas III</a> (Giovanni Caetani Orsini) on 15 October 1277, to arrange a peace between <a href="/wiki/Philip_IV_of_France" title="Philip IV of France">Philip IV of France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Aragon" title="Alfonso III of Aragon">Alfonso III of Aragon</a>.  Jerome and <a href="/wiki/John_of_Vercelli" title="John of Vercelli">John of Vercelli</a> were again appointed to the same task on 4 April 1278.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  At the same time, Jerome was ordered to continue for the time being as the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1278 Jerome was made <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal priest">cardinal priest</a> by Pope Nicholas III, and at some point after 16 May 1279 was assigned the titular church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Pudenziana" title="Santa Pudenziana">Santa Pudenziana</a>. Even after his appointment as a cardinal, he was allowed to remain as minister general of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscans</a> until the next general chapter. In the event, however, he was unable to attend the chapter for reasons of ill health, as a letter of apology of Pope Nicholas III, written in May 1279, indicates.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  On 12 April 1281 he was promoted to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal bishop">cardinal bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_IV" title="Pope Martin IV">Pope Martin IV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_conclave">Papal conclave</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Papal conclave">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_IV" title="Pope Honorius IV">Pope Honorius IV</a> on 3 April 1287, <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1287-1288" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1287-1288">a conclave</a> was held in Rome, at the papal palace next to Santa Sabina on the Aventine Hill, where Pope Honorius had died.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This was in accordance with the Constitution "<a href="/wiki/Ubi_Periculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Ubi Periculum">Ubi Periculum</a>" of Pope Gregory X. At the beginning, in April, there were thirteen cardinals in Rome; three cardinals—Gerardo Bianchi, Giovanni Boccamati, and Jean Cholet—did not attend at all. The Sacred College was for a time hopelessly divided in its selection of a successor. When six of the electors died during the year 1287 (Ancher Pantaleon, Geoffrey de Bar, Hugh of Evesham, Giordano Orsini, Comes de Casanate, and Goffredo of Alatri—some, at least, carried off by fever), the others, with the sole exception of Jerome Masci, left the conclave and returned to their homes. It was not until the following year that they reassembled. The electors at that time were seven in number: Jerome Masci, along with Latino Malabranca, Bentivenga de Bentivengis, Bernard de Languissel, Matteo Rosso Orsini, Giacomo Colonna, and Benedetto Caetani. On 15 February 1288, the survivors unanimously elected Jerome Masci, to the papacy on the first scrutiny. It is said that the cardinals were impressed by his steadfastness in remaining at the papal palace, but there is no real documentation as to their motives. As he admitted in his electoral manifesto, Cardinal Masci was extremely reluctant to accept,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and indeed he persisted in his refusal for an entire week. Finally, on 22 February, he gave in and agreed.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> He became the first <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> pope and chose the name Nicholas IV in remembrance of Nicholas III, who had made him a cardinal.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="New_cardinals">New cardinals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: New cardinals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Given the considerable losses to the numbers of the Sacred College in 1286 and 1287, it is not surprising that Nicholas IV quickly proceeded to fill vacancies. What is surprising is that he did not even reach the number of cardinals who were alive under Honorius IV, let alone exceed it.  On 16 May 1288, he named six new cardinals: Bernardus Calliensis, Bishop of Osimo (who died in 1291), Hugues Aiscelin (Seguin) de Billon, OP, of the diocese of Clermont in the Auvergne;<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Matthew of Aquasparta in Tuscany, minister general of the Franciscans since 1287; Pietro Peregrosso of Milan, the vice-chancellor of the Holy Roman Church; Napoleone Orsini; and Pietro Colonna.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nicholas IV issued an important constitution on 18 July 1289, which granted to the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinals</a> one-half of all income accruing to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> and a share in the financial management, thereby paving the way for that independence of the <a href="/wiki/College_of_Cardinals" title="College of Cardinals">College of Cardinals</a> which, in the following century, was to be of detriment to the papacy.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Actions">Actions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Actions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Rabban_Bar_Sauma" title="Rabban Bar Sauma">Rabban Bar Sauma</a>, <a href="/wiki/Franco-Mongol_alliance" title="Franco-Mongol alliance">Franco-Mongol alliance</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Europeans_in_Medieval_China#Diplomatic_missions_to_Europe" title="Europeans in Medieval China">Europeans in Medieval China §&#160;Diplomatic missions to Europe</a></div>
<p>In regard to the question of the Sicilian succession, as feudal suzerain of the kingdom, Nicholas annulled the treaty, concluded in 1288 through the mediation of <a href="/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England">Edward I of England</a>, which confirmed <a href="/wiki/James_II_of_Aragon" title="James II of Aragon">James II of Aragon</a> in the possession of the island of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>.  This treaty had not properly seen to papal interests. In May 1289 he crowned <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_Naples" title="Charles II of Naples">Charles II</a> as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Sicily" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Sicily">king of Sicily</a> after the latter had expressly recognized papal <a href="/wiki/Suzerainty" title="Suzerainty">suzerainty</a>, and in February 1291 concluded a treaty with Kings <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Aragon" title="Alfonso III of Aragon">Alfonso III of Aragon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Philip_IV_of_France" title="Philip IV of France">Philip IV of France</a> looking toward the expulsion of James from Sicily.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1288 Nicholas met with the Nestorian Christian <a href="/wiki/Rabban_Bar_Sauma" title="Rabban Bar Sauma">Rabban Bar Sauma</a> from China.
</p><p>In August 1290 he granted the status of <i><a href="/wiki/Studium_generale" title="Studium generale">studium generale</a></i> to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Coimbra" title="University of Coimbra">university</a> that King <a href="/wiki/Denis_of_Portugal" title="Denis of Portugal">Denis of Portugal</a> has just founded a few months earlier in the city of <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The loss of <a href="/wiki/Akko" class="mw-redirect" title="Akko">Acre</a> in 1291 stirred Nicholas IV to renewed enthusiasm for a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a>.  He sent missionaries, among them the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/John_of_Monte_Corvino" class="mw-redirect" title="John of Monte Corvino">John of Monte Corvino</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-McBrien_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McBrien-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to labour among the <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgarians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ethiopia" title="Ethiopia">Ethiopians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mongols" title="Mongols">Mongols</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tatars" title="Tatars">Tatars</a> and <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">Chinese</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nicholas IV died in Rome on 4 April 1292, in the palace which he had built next to the Liberian Basilica (S. Maria Maggiore). He was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_Maggiore" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore">Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> His epitaph reads: "Here lies Nicolas IV son of St. Francis" (<i>Hic requiescit / Nicolaus PP Quartus / Filius Beati Francisci</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Taxatio"><i>Taxatio</i><span class="anchor" id="&#39;&#39;Taxatio&#39;&#39;"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Nicholas_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Taxatio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The 1291–92 <i><a href="/wiki/Taxatio" class="mw-redirect" title="Taxatio">Taxatio</a></i> he initiated, which was a detailed valuation for <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> taxation of English and Welsh parish churches and <a href="/wiki/Prebendary" title="Prebendary">prebends</a>, remains an important source document for the medieval period. An edition was reprinted by the Record Commission in 1802 as <i>Taxatio Ecclesiastica Angliae et Walliae Auctoritate</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
