<p><b>John Eudes</b>, CIM (French: <i lang="fr">Jean Eudes</i>; 14 November 1601 – 19 August 1680) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and the founder of both the Order of Our Lady of Charity in 1641 and <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Jesus_and_Mary" title="Congregation of Jesus and Mary">Congregation of Jesus and Mary</a>, also known as The Eudists, in 1643.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was also a professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Jesus" title="Oratory of Jesus">Oratory of Jesus</a> until 1643 and the author of the <a href="/wiki/Proper_(liturgy)" title="Proper (liturgy)">proper</a> for the Mass and Divine Office of the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Hearts</a> of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary" title="Immaculate Heart of Mary">Blessed Virgin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-GS_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Eudes was an ardent proponent of the Sacred Hearts and dedicated himself to its promotion and celebration; the Masses he compiled for both Sacred Hearts were both first celebrated within his lifetime. He preached missions across France, including <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/Versailles" class="mw-redirect" title="Versailles">Versailles</a>, while earning recognition as a popular evangelist and confessor.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Eudes was also a prolific writer and wrote on the Sacred Hearts despite opposition from the <a href="/wiki/Jansenists" class="mw-redirect" title="Jansenists">Jansenists</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FS_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FS-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FM_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FM-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eudes was canonized as a saint in mid-1925 and his supporters are now petitioning to have him named a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Jean Eudes was born on 14 November 1601 on a farm close to the village of <a href="/wiki/Ri,_Orne" title="Ri, Orne">Ri</a> to Isaac Eudes (born circa 1566) and Martha Corbin; he had four sisters and two brothers,<sup id="cite_ref-FM_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FM-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> including the historian <a href="/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Eudes_de_M%C3%A9zeray" title="François Eudes de Mézeray">François Eudes de Mézeray</a> (1610-10 July 1683).<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He made his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on 26 May 1613 (<a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>) and at age 14 took a private vow to remain <a href="/wiki/Chastity" title="Chastity">chaste</a>.
</p><p>Eudes studied under the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> at <a href="/wiki/Caen" title="Caen">Caen</a> before he decided to join the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Jesus" title="Oratory of Jesus">Oratorians</a> on 25 March 1623. His masters and models in the spiritual life were <a href="/wiki/Pierre_de_B%C3%A9rulle" title="Pierre de Bérulle">Pierre de Bérulle</a> (who welcomed him into the order) and the contemplative and ascetic <a href="/wiki/Charles_de_Condren" title="Charles de Condren">Charles de Condren</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As a student of de Bérulle, he became a member of the <a href="/wiki/French_school_of_spirituality" title="French school of spirituality">French school</a> that promoted a Christocentric approach to spiritual affairs. This was characterized by a strong sense of adoration, plus pursuit of a personal relationship with <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> which extended to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit" title="Holy Spirit">Holy Spirit</a>. Bishop Jacques Camus de Pontcarré ordained Eudes to the subdiaconate on 21 December 1624.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Eudes was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> on 20 December 1625 and he celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> at Christmas. Almost immediately after his ordination, he came down with an illness that kept him bedridden until 1626. He was sent to <a href="/wiki/Aubervilliers" title="Aubervilliers">Aubervilliers</a> for his theological studies and returned to <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9es" title="Sées">Séez</a> in 1627.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> During severe plagues in 1627 and 1631, he volunteered to care for the stricken in his own diocese, administering the sacraments and ensuring the dead received a proper burial. He did this with the permission of his Oratorian superiors.<sup id="cite_ref-FS_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FS-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> To avoid infecting his colleagues during this time, he lived in a huge cask in the middle of a field. 
</p><p>In 1633 he began preaching parish missions, eventually preaching over a hundred missions throughout his own region as well as in <a href="/wiki/Ile-de-France" class="mw-redirect" title="Ile-de-France">Ile-de-France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Burgundy,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy, France">Burgundy</a> and also in <a href="/wiki/Brittany_(administrative_region)" title="Brittany (administrative region)">Brittany</a>. <a href="/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Olier" title="Jean-Jacques Olier">Jean-Jacques Olier</a> referred to Eudes as "the prodigy of his age." Eudes became a noted preacher and confessor with a flair for evangelization and his missions often lasted from several weeks to several months; he preached three in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> and one in <a href="/wiki/Versailles" class="mw-redirect" title="Versailles">Versailles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Eudes even preached once for <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Austria" title="Anne of Austria">Anne of Austria</a>, though her son <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIV_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XIV of France">King Louis XIV</a> suspected that Eudes was hostile towards his <a href="/wiki/Gallicanism" title="Gallicanism">Gallican</a> policies. He was also quite concerned about the spiritual improvement of priests and realized that the seminaries needed improvement.<sup id="cite_ref-FM_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FM-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He founded several seminaries in the region including in <a href="/wiki/Rennes" title="Rennes">Rennes</a>. In 1674 he received six <a href="/wiki/Papal_bulls" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal bulls">papal bulls</a> of <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgences</a> from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a> for <a href="/wiki/Confraternities" class="mw-redirect" title="Confraternities">confraternities</a> and seminaries dedicated to the Sacred Hearts.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_congregations">Religious congregations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Religious congregations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In his work he became disturbed when he saw the inadequate shelters for those prostitutes who sought to escape that life. Madeleine Lamy – who had cared for some of those women – came up to him on one occasion and challenged Eudes to address the issue. In 1641 he founded the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity" title="Order of Our Lady of Charity">Order of Our Lady of Charity of the Refuge</a> in Caen to provide a refuge for prostitutes who wished to do penance. Three <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Visitation_of_Holy_Mary" title="Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary">Visitation</a> nuns came to his aid for a brief period and in 1644 a house was opened at Caen.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FS_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FS-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Other ladies joined them and on 8 February 1651 the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bayeux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bayeux">Bishop of Bayeux</a> gave the institute his diocesan approbation. The congregation received papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VII" title="Pope Alexander VII">Pope Alexander VII</a> on 2 January 1666. It later also included a convent from which <a href="/wiki/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier" title="Mary Euphrasia Pelletier">Mary Euphrasia Pelletier</a> established the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity_of_the_Good_Shepherd" title="Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd">Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd</a>, in 1829.
</p><p>With the support of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Richelieu" title="Cardinal Richelieu">Cardinal Richelieu</a> and a number of individual bishops, he severed his connection with the Oratorians to establish the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Jesus_and_Mary" title="Congregation of Jesus and Mary">Eudists</a> for the education of priests and for parish missions. This congregation was founded at Caen on 25 March 1643. Eudes also founded the Society of the Most Admirable Mother which acted as a sort of <a href="/wiki/Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order">Third Order</a>, and would later count among its members <a href="/wiki/Jeanne_Jugan" title="Jeanne Jugan">Jeanne Jugan</a> and Amelie Fristel.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eudes was influenced by the teachings of the French school, <a href="/wiki/Francis_de_Sales" title="Francis de Sales">Francis de Sales</a>, and the revelations of <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_the_Great" title="Gertrude the Great">Gertrude the Great</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mechtilde" title="Mechtilde">Mechtilde</a>. Bérulle's devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Incarnate_Word" class="mw-redirect" title="Incarnate Word">Incarnate Word</a> won him over and he combined with it the gentleness and devotional warmth of Francis de Sales. He changed the somewhat individual and private character of the devotion into a devotion for the whole church when he wrote for the benefit of his communities an Office and a Mass which later received approval from several bishops before spreading throughout the church. For this reason <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> – in proclaiming Eudes' heroic virtues later in 1903 – gave him the title of "Author of the Liturgical Worship of the Sacred Heart of Jesus and Holy Heart of Mary". Eudes dedicated the chapels of the Caen and Coutances seminaries to the Sacred Heart. The feast of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary" title="Immaculate Heart of Mary">Immaculate Heart of the Mother of God</a> was celebrated for the first time on 8 February 1648 and that of the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred Heart of Jesus">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> on 20 October 1672 each as a double of the first class with an octave.<sup id="cite_ref-GS_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GS-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FS_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FS-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He composed various <a href="/wiki/Rosaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Rosaries">rosaries</a> and prayers dedicated to the Sacred Hearts. His book <i>Le Cœur Admirable de la Très Sainte Mère de Dieu</i> is the first book ever written on the devotion to the Sacred Hearts.
</p><p>In 1671 there was a rumor that Eudes would be named as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Evreux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Evreux">Coadjutor Bishop of Evreux</a> and that the king Louis XIV would support the nomination based on Eudes' reputation.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Eudes died at Caen on 19 August 1680. He had drafted up his <a href="/wiki/Last_will" class="mw-redirect" title="Last will">last will</a> in April 1671 when his health started to decline. Eudes' remains were exhumed and transferred in 1810 and again for the last time on 6 March 1884.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>The cause for canonization commenced in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_S%C3%A9ez" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Séez">Diocese of Séez</a> in an informative process that commenced on 19 August 1868. The formal introduction to the cause came on 7 February 1874 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>, who titled Eudes as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. Pope Leo XIII later confirmed, on 6 January 1903, that Eudes had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and thus named him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> later approved two miracles attributed to Eudes' intercession and beatified him on 25 April 1909 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a>. The ratification of two more miracles enabled <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> to preside over Eudes' canonization on 31 May 1925.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Doctor_of_the_Church">Doctor of the Church</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Doctor of the Church">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:JeanEudesVat.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/JeanEudesVat.jpg/180px-JeanEudesVat.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="270" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/JeanEudesVat.jpg/270px-JeanEudesVat.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/JeanEudesVat.jpg/360px-JeanEudesVat.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="2250" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:JeanEudesVat.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Eudes in the Vatican which has been in place since 1932.</div></div></div>
<p>His supporters first proposed the idea to have Eudes named as a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a> around the time of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, though the conflict and then the course of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a> caused the suspension of inquiries into such a path. But in January 2012, the General Assembly of the Eudists conceded that it was time for that path to be pursued once more, since there existed no impediments to pursuing such a path. In the course of 2014–15, the episcopal conferences of France, <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>, <a href="/wiki/Venezuela" title="Venezuela">Venezuela</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ecuador" title="Ecuador">Ecuador</a>, <a href="/wiki/Benin" title="Benin">Benin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Honduras" title="Honduras">Honduras</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Colombia" title="Colombia">Colombia</a> gave their full support to this proposal. The plenary session of the <a href="/wiki/French_Episcopal_Conference" class="mw-redirect" title="French Episcopal Conference">French Episcopal Conference</a> on 8 November 2014 confirmed its support for the cause of Eudes as a Doctor of the Church. On 3 December 2016 the then-Superior General of the Eudists Camilo Bernal Hadad and the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Puy-en-Velay" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Puy-en-Velay">Bishop of Le Puy-en-Velay</a> Luc Crépy met with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> to discuss the idea.
</p><p>Those managing the process collected evidence needed to support the idea and compiled it into a <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier, which included the reasons for the proposal as well as those spiritual works that would support it. This dossier was submitted to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in April 2017 for them to evaluate.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spirituality">Spirituality</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Spirituality">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eudes taught about the mystical unity of the Sacred Hearts of Jesus and Mary and wrote:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>You must never separate what God has so perfectly united. So closely are Jesus and Mary bound up with each other that whoever beholds Jesus sees Mary; whoever loves Jesus, loves Mary; whoever has devotion to Jesus, has devotion to Mary.</p></blockquote>
<p>The most striking characteristic of Eudes' teaching on Devotion to the Sacred Heart – as indeed of his whole teaching on the spiritual life – is that Christ is its centre at all times.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Views_of_the_Popes">Views of the Popes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Views of the Popes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> – in his General Audience <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechesis</a> on 19 August 2009 – praised Eudes as a "tireless apostle of the devotion of the Sacred Hearts" noting that Eudes was an example for priests during the Year for Priests. The pope went on to describe Eudes' "apostolic zeal" in the formation of seminarians into priests as well as the fact that Eudes was a model for evangelization and witness to the "love for Christ's Heart and Mary's Heart".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Statue">Statue</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Statue">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Peter's Basilica features a statue portraying (and dedicated to) Eudes constructed by Silvio Silva in 1932. It is located on the right side of the central nave.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Eudes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eudes wrote a number of books. His principal works are:
</p>
<ul><li><i>La Vie et le Royaume de Jésus</i> (The Life and Kingdom of Jesus – 1637)</li>
<li><i>Le contrat de l'homme avec Dieu par le Saint Baptême</i> (Contract of Man with God Through Holy Baptism – 1654)</li>
<li><i>Le Bon Confesseur</i> (The Good Confessor – 1666)</li>
<li><i>Le Mémorial de la vie Ecclésiastique</i></li>
<li><i>Le Prédicateur Apostolique</i></li>
<li><i>Le Cœur Admirable de la Très Sainte Mère de Dieu</i> (the first book ever written on the devotion to the Sacred Hearts)</li></ul>
