<p><b>Gaius Sollius Modestus Apollinaris Sidonius</b>, better known as <b>Sidonius Apollinaris</b> (5 November<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> of an unknown year, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 430 – 481/490 AD), was a <a href="/wiki/Poet" title="Poet">poet</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diplomat" title="Diplomat">diplomat</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. Sidonius is "the single most important surviving author from 5th-century Gaul" according to Eric Goldberg.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was one of four <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> aristocrats of the 5th- to 6th-century whose letters survive in quantity; the others are <a href="/wiki/Ruricius" title="Ruricius">Ruricius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Limoges" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Limoges">bishop of Limoges</a> (died 507), <a href="/wiki/Avitus_of_Vienne" title="Avitus of Vienne">Alcimus Ecdicius Avitus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Vienne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Vienne">bishop of Vienne</a> (died 518) and <a href="/wiki/Magnus_Felix_Ennodius" title="Magnus Felix Ennodius">Magnus Felix Ennodius</a> of Arles, bishop of Ticinum (died 534). All of them were linked in the tightly bound aristocratic <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> network that provided the bishops of Catholic Gaul.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is 21 August.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sidonius_Apollinaris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sidonius was born in <a href="/wiki/Lugdunum" title="Lugdunum">Lugdunum</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>). His father, whose name is unknown, was <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">Prefect</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> under <a href="/wiki/Valentinian_III" title="Valentinian III">Valentinian III</a> (Sidonius recalls with pride being present with his father at the installation of <a href="/wiki/Astyrius" title="Astyrius">Astyrius</a> as <a href="/wiki/Roman_consul" title="Roman consul">consul</a> for the year 449.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>) Sidonius' grandfather was <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Prefect" class="mw-redirect" title="Praetorian Prefect">Praetorian Prefect</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> sometime prior to 409 and a friend of his successor <a href="/wiki/Decimus_Rusticus" title="Decimus Rusticus">Decimus Rusticus</a>. Sidonius may be a descendant of another Apollinaris who was Prefect of Gaul under <a href="/wiki/Constantine_II_(emperor)" title="Constantine II (emperor)">Constantine II</a> between 337 and 340.
</p><p>Sidonius married <a href="/wiki/Papianilla,_Wife_of_Sidonius_Apollinaris" class="mw-redirect" title="Papianilla, Wife of Sidonius Apollinaris">Papianilla</a>, the daughter of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Avitus" title="Avitus">Avitus</a>, around 452.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This union produced one son, <a href="/wiki/Apollinaris_of_Clermont" title="Apollinaris of Clermont">Apollinaris</a>, and at least two daughters, Severina and Roscia, whom Sidonius mentions in his letters. A daughter Alcima is mentioned much later by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Theodor_Mommsen" title="Theodor Mommsen">Theodor Mommsen</a> speculated that Alcima may be another name for one of his other daughters.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His known acquaintances include bishop <a href="/wiki/Faustus_of_Riez" title="Faustus of Riez">Faustus of Riez</a> and his theological adversary <a href="/wiki/Claudianus_Mamertus" title="Claudianus Mamertus">Claudianus Mamertus</a>; his life and friendships put him in the center of 5th-century Roman affairs.
</p><p>In 457 <a href="/wiki/Majorian" title="Majorian">Majorian</a> deprived Avitus of the empire and seized the city of Lyons; Sidonius fell into his hands. However, the reputation of Sidonius's learning led Majorian to treat him with the greatest respect. In return Sidonius composed a panegyric in his honour (as he had previously done for Avitus), which won for him a statue at Rome and the title of <i><a href="/wiki/Comes" title="Comes">comes</a></i>. In 467 or 468 the emperor <a href="/wiki/Anthemius" title="Anthemius">Anthemius</a> rewarded him for the panegyric which he had written in honour of him by raising him to the post of <a href="/wiki/Praefectus_urbi" title="Praefectus urbi">Urban Prefect of Rome</a>, which he held until 469, and afterwards to the dignity of <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(ancient_Rome)" title="Patrician (ancient Rome)">Patrician</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Senator" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Senator">Senator</a>. In 470 or 472, he was elected to succeed <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eparchius_(bishop)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Eparchius (bishop) (page does not exist)">Eparchius</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Clermont" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Clermont">bishopric of Averna (Clermont)</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the Goths captured Clermont in 474 he was imprisoned, as he had taken an active part in its defense, but he was afterwards released from captivity by <a href="/wiki/Euric" title="Euric">Euric</a>, king of the Goths, and continued to serve as bishop until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sidonius's relations have been traced over several generations as a narrative of a family's fortunes, from the prominence of his paternal grandfather's time into later decline in the 6th century under the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>. Sidonius's son Apollinaris, who was a correspondent of <a href="/wiki/Ruricius" title="Ruricius">Ruricius</a> of <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a>, commanded a unit raised in <a href="/wiki/Auvergne_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Auvergne (province)">Auvergne</a> on the losing side of the decisive <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Vouille" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Vouille">Battle of Vouille</a>, and also was bishop of Clermont for four months until he died.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Sidonius's grandson Arcadius, on hearing a rumor that the Frankish king <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_I" title="Theuderic I">Theuderic I</a> had died, betrayed Clermont to <a href="/wiki/Childebert_I" title="Childebert I">Childebert I</a>, only to abandon his wife and mother when Theuderic appeared; his other appearance in the history of Gregory of Tours is as a servant of king Childebert.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> speaks of Sidonius as a man who could celebrate <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> from memory (without a <a href="/wiki/Sacramentary" title="Sacramentary">sacramentary</a>) and give unprepared speeches without any hesitation.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sidonius was still living in 481.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarries2018_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarries2018-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was dead before 490, when his successor as bishop, Aprunculus, died. His date of death was 21 or 23 August.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartindale1980118_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartindale1980118-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sidonius_Apollinaris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif/lossy-page1-220px-Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="325" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif/lossy-page1-330px-Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif/lossy-page1-440px-Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2294" data-file-height="3384" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Caii_Sollii_Apollinaris_Sidonii_Opera.tif" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Opera</i> (1598)</div></div></div>
<p>His extant works are his <i><a href="/wiki/Panegyric" title="Panegyric">Panegyrics</a></i> on different emperors (in which he draws largely upon <a href="/wiki/Statius" title="Statius">Statius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ausonius" title="Ausonius">Ausonius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Claudian" title="Claudian">Claudian</a>), which document several important political events.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Carmen 7 is a panegyric to his father-in-law Avitus on his inauguration as emperor. Carmen 5 is a panegyric to <a href="/wiki/Majorian" title="Majorian">Majorian</a>, which offers evidence that Sidonius was able to overcome the natural suspicion and hostility towards the man who was responsible for the death of his father-in-law. Carmen 2 is a panegyric to the emperor <a href="/wiki/Anthemius" title="Anthemius">Anthemius</a>, part of Sidonius' efforts to be appointed Urban Prefect of Rome; several samples of <a href="/wiki/Occasional_verse" class="mw-redirect" title="Occasional verse">occasional verse</a>; and nine books of <i>Letters</i>, about which <a href="/wiki/W.B._Anderson" class="mw-redirect" title="W.B. Anderson">W.B. Anderson</a> notes, "Whatever one may think about their style and diction, the letters of Sidonius are an invaluable source of information on many aspects of the life of his time."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> While very stilted in diction, these <i>Letters</i> reveal Sidonius as a man of genial temper, fond of good living and of pleasure. A letter of Sidonius's addressed to <a href="/wiki/Riothamus" title="Riothamus">Riothamus</a>, "King of the Brittones" (c. 470) is of particular interest, since it provides evidence that a king or military leader with ties to Britain lived around the time frame of <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">King Arthur</a>. An English translation of his poetry and letters by W.B. Anderson, with accompanying Latin text, have been published by the <a href="/wiki/Loeb_Classical_Library" title="Loeb Classical Library">Loeb Classical Library</a> (volume 1, containing his poems and books 1-2 of his letters, 1939;<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> remainder of letters, 1965). Among his lost works, is the one on <a href="/wiki/Apollonius_of_Tyana" title="Apollonius of Tyana">Apollonius of Tyana</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Manuscript_tradition">Manuscript tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sidonius_Apollinaris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Manuscript tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Although Sidonius' works may have been published in part during his lifetime (5th century), there is no textual evidence of this and all manuscripts can be traced back to a single archetype, which is estimated as dating to roughly the 7th century. The oldest witness dates to the 9th century and is likely a fourth-generation copy. Although the archetype contained poems, they were omitted in most copies, and most extant manuscripts contain only his letters, often jumbled together with a garbled transcription of another writer, <a href="/wiki/Ausonius" title="Ausonius">Ausonius</a>. Most of the work on textual variants was done by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Christian_L%C3%BCtjohann&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Christian Lütjohann (page does not exist)">Christian Lütjohann</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_L%C3%BCtjohann" class="extiw" title="de:Christian Lütjohann">de</a>&#93;</span> in the 1870s, but Lütjohann died prematurely before he had developed the <a href="/wiki/Stemmatics" class="mw-redirect" title="Stemmatics">stemmatics</a>, which are crucial for reconstructing Sidonius' idiosyncratic Latin. Lütjohann's work is published in the 1887 <i><a href="/wiki/Monumenta_Germaniae_Historica" title="Monumenta Germaniae Historica">Monumenta Germaniae Historica</a></i> with inferior stemmatics provided by other scholars. Franz Dolveck provided a partial new stemma, including only those editions complete with poetry, in 2020.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
