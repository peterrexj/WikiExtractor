<p><b>Saint Apollonia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el"><b>Αγία Απολλωνία</b></span>, <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop"><b>Ϯⲁⲅⲓⲁ Ⲁⲡⲟⲗⲗⲟⲛⲓⲁ</b></span>) was one of a group of virgin martyrs who suffered in <a href="/wiki/Alexandria,_Egypt" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexandria, Egypt">Alexandria</a> during a local uprising against the <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> prior to the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a>. According to church tradition, her <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">torture</a> included having all of her <a href="/wiki/Teeth" class="mw-redirect" title="Teeth">teeth</a> violently pulled out or shattered. For this reason, she is popularly regarded as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patroness</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dentistry" title="Dentistry">dentistry</a> and those suffering from <a href="/wiki/Toothache" title="Toothache">toothache</a> or other dental problems. French court painter <a href="/wiki/Jehan_Fouquet" class="mw-redirect" title="Jehan Fouquet">Jehan Fouquet</a> painted the scene of St. Apollonia's torture in <i>The Martyrdom of St. Apollonia</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Apollonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg/220px-Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg/330px-Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg/440px-Heilsbronn_M%C3%BCnster_-_11000_Jungfrauen-Altar_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a><figcaption>Torture of Saint Apollonia (1513, <a href="/wiki/Heilsbronn" title="Heilsbronn">Heilsbronn</a> Cathedral, <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>).</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_historians" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecclesiastical historians">Ecclesiastical historians</a> have claimed that in the last years of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Philip_the_Arabian" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip the Arabian">Philip the Arabian</a> (reigned 244–249), during otherwise undocumented festivities to commemorate the millennium of the founding of Rome (traditionally in 753 BC, putting the date about 248), the fury of the Alexandrian mob rose to a great height, and when one of their <a href="/wiki/Poet" title="Poet">poets</a> prophesied a calamity, they committed bloody outrages on the Christians, whom the authorities made no effort to protect.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jacopo_Zucchi,_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia,_NGA_62616.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Pen and brown in drawing of Saint Apollonia being restrained, her mouth open, and teeth being broken by a tool" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Jacopo_Zucchi%2C_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia%2C_NGA_62616.jpg/220px-Jacopo_Zucchi%2C_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia%2C_NGA_62616.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="408" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Jacopo_Zucchi%2C_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia%2C_NGA_62616.jpg/330px-Jacopo_Zucchi%2C_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia%2C_NGA_62616.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Jacopo_Zucchi%2C_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia%2C_NGA_62616.jpg/440px-Jacopo_Zucchi%2C_The_Martyrdom_of_Saint_Apollonia%2C_NGA_62616.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2159" data-file-height="4000" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Jacopo_Zucchi" title="Jacopo Zucchi">Jacopo Zucchi</a>, The Martyrdom of Saint Apollonia, pen and brown ink with brown wash heightened with white over black chalk on laid paper, overall (approximate): 25.2 x 12.9 cm (9 15/16 x 5 1/16 in.), Julius S. Held Collection, Ailsa Mellon Bruce Fund, 1983.74.20, NGA 62616</figcaption></figure>
<p>Dionysius, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Alexandria">Bishop of Alexandria</a> (247–265), relates the sufferings of his people in a letter addressed to Fabius, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Antioch">Bishop of Antioch</a>, of which long extracts have been preserved in <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius</a>' <i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Historia Ecclesiae</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After describing how a Christian man and woman, Metras and Quinta, were seized and killed by the mob, and how the houses of several other Christians were pillaged, Dionysius continues:
</p>
<blockquote><p>At that time Apollonia, <i>parthénos presbytis</i> (mostly likely meaning a <a href="/wiki/Deaconess" title="Deaconess">deaconess</a>) was held in high esteem. These men seized her also and by repeated blows broke all her teeth. They then erected outside the city gates a pile of wood and threatened to burn her alive if she refused to repeat after them impious words (either a blasphemy against Christ, or an invocation of the heathen gods). Given, at her own request, a little freedom, she sprang quickly into the fire and was burned to death.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>This brief tale was extended and moralized in <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i> (c. 1260).
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Nikolaikirche_Stralsund,_Heilige_Apollonia_(2007-06-11).JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Nikolaikirche_Stralsund%2C_Heilige_Apollonia_%282007-06-11%29.JPG/220px-Nikolaikirche_Stralsund%2C_Heilige_Apollonia_%282007-06-11%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Nikolaikirche_Stralsund%2C_Heilige_Apollonia_%282007-06-11%29.JPG/330px-Nikolaikirche_Stralsund%2C_Heilige_Apollonia_%282007-06-11%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Nikolaikirche_Stralsund%2C_Heilige_Apollonia_%282007-06-11%29.JPG/440px-Nikolaikirche_Stralsund%2C_Heilige_Apollonia_%282007-06-11%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="480" data-file-height="640" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">Fresco</a> of Saint Apollonia<br />(<a href="/wiki/St._Nicholas_Church,_Stralsund" title="St. Nicholas Church, Stralsund">St. Nicholas Church, Stralsund</a>).</figcaption></figure>
<p>Apollonia and a whole group of early martyrs did not await the death they were threatened with, but either to preserve their chastity or because they were confronted with the alternative of renouncing their faith or suffering death, voluntarily embraced the death prepared for them, an action that runs perilously close to <a href="/wiki/Suicide" title="Suicide">suicide</a>, some<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span title="The material near this tag possibly uses too-vague attribution or weasel words. (November 2021)">who?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> thought. <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> touches on this question in the first book of <i><a href="/wiki/City_of_God_(book)" class="mw-redirect" title="City of God (book)">The City of God</a></i>, apropos suicide:
</p>
<blockquote><figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Apolline.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Saint_Apolline.jpg/253px-Saint_Apolline.jpg" decoding="async" width="253" height="346" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Saint_Apolline.jpg/379px-Saint_Apolline.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Saint_Apolline.jpg/505px-Saint_Apolline.jpg 2x" data-file-width="561" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>Jean Bein after Raphael, <i><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://library.nga.gov/permalink/01NGA_INST/1p5jkvq/alma991640133804896">Saint Apolline</a></i>, 1842, steel engraving, <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.nga.gov/research/library/imagecollections.html">Department of Image Collections, National Gallery of Art Library, Washington, DC</a></figcaption></figure><p>But, they say, during the time of persecution certain holy women plunged into the water with the intention of being swept away by the waves and drowned, and thus preserve their threatened chastity. Although they quitted life in this wise, nevertheless they receive high honour as martyrs in the Catholic Church and their feasts are observed with great ceremony. This is a matter on which I dare not pass judgment lightly. For I know not but that the Church was divinely authorized through trustworthy revelations to honour thus the memory of these Christians. It may be that such is the case. May it not be, too, that these acted in such a manner, not through human caprice but on the command of God, not erroneously but through obedience, as we must believe in the case of <a href="/wiki/Samson" title="Samson">Samson</a>? When, however, God gives a command and makes it clearly known, who would account obedience there to a crime or condemn such pious devotion and ready service?"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The narrative of Dionysius does not suggest the slightest reproach as to this act of St. Apollonia; in his eyes she was as much a martyr as the others, and as such she was revered in the Alexandrian Church. In time, her feast was also popular in the West. A later narrative mistakenly duplicated Apollonia, making her a Christian virgin of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Julian_(emperor)" title="Julian (emperor)">Julian the Apostate</a>, suffering the same dental fate.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Apollonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg/220px-Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="345" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg/330px-Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg/440px-Tooth-of-saint-apollonia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="609" data-file-height="956" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">Reliquary</a> containing a tooth, reputedly that of Saint Apollonia, in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Porto" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Porto">Cathedral of Porto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> (<a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">OCA</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Churches celebrate the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of St. Apollonia on 9 February, and she is popularly invoked against the toothache because of the torments she had to endure. She is represented in art with pincers in which a tooth is held. Saint Apollonia is one of the two <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a>.
</p><p>William S. Walsh noted that, though the major part of her relics were preserved in the former church of St. Apollonia at Rome, her head at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_in_Trastevere" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere">Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere</a>, her arms at the <a href="/wiki/San_Lorenzo_fuori_le_Mura" class="mw-redirect" title="San Lorenzo fuori le Mura">Basilica di San Lorenzo fuori le Mura</a>, parts of her jaw in St. Basil's, and other relics are in the Jesuit church at <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a>, in St. Augustine's at <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> church at Mechlin, in St. Cross at Liège, in the treasury of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Porto" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Porto">cathedral of Porto</a>, and in several churches at <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> These relics consist in some cases of a solitary tooth or a splinter of bone. In the Middle Ages, objects claimed to be her teeth were sold as toothache cures.
</p><p>There was a church dedicated to her in Rome, near the Basilica di Santa Maria in <a href="/wiki/Trastevere" title="Trastevere">Trastevere</a>, but it no longer exists. Only its little square, the <i>Piazza Sant'Apollonia</i> remains. One of the principal train stations of <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a> is also named for this saint. There is a statue of Saint Apollonia in the church at <a href="/wiki/Locronan" title="Locronan">Locronan</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. The island of <a href="/wiki/History_of_Mauritius" title="History of Mauritius">Mauritius</a> was originally named <i>Santa Apolónia</i> in her honor in 1507 by Portuguese navigators.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2012)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> A parish church in <a href="/wiki/Eilendorf_(Aachen)" title="Eilendorf (Aachen)">Eilendorf</a>, a suburb of <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a>, Germany, is named in honor of Saint Apollonia.
</p><p>In some areas of Italy, Saint Apollonia is cast in the role of the <a href="/wiki/Tooth_fairy" title="Tooth fairy">tooth fairy</a>, collecting children's fallen <a href="/wiki/Deciduous_teeth" title="Deciduous teeth">baby teeth</a> while they sleep and leaving a gift in exchange.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Madonna_Della_Strada_Chapel" title="Madonna Della Strada Chapel">Madonna Della Strada Chapel</a> at <a href="/wiki/Loyola_University_Chicago" title="Loyola University Chicago">Loyola University Chicago</a> contains a stained glass window on the north wall depicting St. Apollonia.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The windows along this wall correspond with the colleges of the university at the time the chapel was built. The Loyola University School of Dentistry closed in 1993, but the window in the chapel remains.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Presence_in_England">Presence in England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Apollonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Presence in England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG/170px-Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="283" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG/255px-Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG/340px-Saint_Apollonia_-_Kingskerswell_Church.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1568" data-file-height="2607" /></a><figcaption>The stained glass image in Kingskerswell church</figcaption></figure>
<p>There are 52 known images of her in various English churches that survived the ravages of the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">16th-century Commissioners</a>. These are concentrated in <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> and <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a>. Most of these images are on the panels of <a href="/wiki/Rood" title="Rood">rood</a> screens or featured in <a href="/wiki/Stained_glass" title="Stained glass">stained glass</a> with only one being a stone capital (<a href="/wiki/Stokeinteignhead" title="Stokeinteignhead">Stokeinteignhead</a>, Devon). She is also depicted in a tapestry of circa 1499 at <a href="/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Guildhall" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mary&#39;s Guildhall">St. Mary's Guildhall</a>, Coventry.
</p><p>By county, some of the locations are:
</p>

<li><a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>: <a href="/wiki/Poundstock" title="Poundstock">Poundstock</a></li>
<li>Devon: <a href="/wiki/Alphington,_Devon" title="Alphington, Devon">Alphington</a> (now gone), <a href="/wiki/Ashton,_Devon" title="Ashton, Devon">Ashton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Combe_Martin" title="Combe Martin">Combe Martin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Exeter_Cathedral" title="Exeter Cathedral">Exeter Cathedral</a> (tapestry in St. Gabriel's chapel), <a href="/wiki/Holne" title="Holne">Holne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kenn,_Devon" title="Kenn, Devon">Kenn</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kenton,_Devon" title="Kenton, Devon">Kenton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingskerswell" title="Kingskerswell">Kingskerswell</a> (see photo), <a href="/wiki/Manaton" title="Manaton">Manaton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Payhembury" title="Payhembury">Payhembury</a>, <a href="/wiki/South_Milton" title="South Milton">South Milton</a>, Stoke-in-Teignhead, <a href="/wiki/Torbryan" title="Torbryan">Torbryan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ugborough" title="Ugborough">Ugborough</a>, <a href="/wiki/Whimple" title="Whimple">Whimple</a> (now gone), <a href="/wiki/Widecombe-in-the-Moor" class="mw-redirect" title="Widecombe-in-the-Moor">Widecombe-in-the-Moor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wolborough" title="Wolborough">Wolborough</a> (Newton Abbot)</li>
