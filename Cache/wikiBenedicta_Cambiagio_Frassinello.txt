<p><b>Benedetta Cambiagio Frassinello</b> was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the founder of the Benedictine Sisters of Providence. Frassinello married to appease her parents in 1816 but the couple decided to lead a chaste life and both pursued a call to the religious life with Frassinello joining the <a href="/wiki/Ursulines" title="Ursulines">Ursulines</a> in <a href="/wiki/Capriolo" title="Capriolo">Capriolo</a> at <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>. But husband and wife later reunited after setting out to establish schools for the education of girls and the pair moved back to <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> where she founded her order in 1838 based on the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine charism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Frassinello's beatification was celebrated in 1987 and she was canonized as a saint in 2002.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedetta_Cambiagio_Frassinello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benedetta Cambiagio was born on 2 October 1791 in Langasco in <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> as the last of six children to Giuseppe Cambiagio and Francesca Ghiglione.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Due to political discord, the family settled in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> in 1804. In 1811 she had a spiritual experience that gave her a profound desire for a life of <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> and of total consecration to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>. However, in obedience to the wishes of her parents, on 7 February 1816 she married the farmer and <a href="/wiki/Carpentry" title="Carpentry">carpenter</a> Giovanni Battista Frassinello in the Basilica of San Michele.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1818 the couple decided each to live a celibate life "as brother and sister" more so because he was so impressed with her holiness and her desire for the religious life. The couple took in Benedetta's sister Maria who was suffering from <a href="/wiki/Intestinal_cancer" class="mw-redirect" title="Intestinal cancer">intestinal cancer</a> and whose husband had left her. The pair cared for her until she died in July 1825.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her husband Giovanni Battista entered the <a href="/wiki/Somaschi_Fathers" title="Somaschi Fathers">Somaschi Fathers</a> and Benedetta joined the <a href="/wiki/Ursulines" title="Ursulines">Ursuline</a> congregation at <a href="/wiki/Capriolo" title="Capriolo">Capriolo</a> after having failed to join the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Poor_Clares" title="Capuchin Poor Clares">Capuchin Poor Clares</a> in Genoa. In 1826 she was forced to leave because of ill health and returned to <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> but had a vision of <a href="/wiki/Jerome_Emiliani" class="mw-redirect" title="Jerome Emiliani">Girolamo Emiliani</a> who healed her. Once she regained her health - with the approval of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Pavia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Pavia">Bishop of Pavia</a> Luigi Tosi - she dedicated herself to the education of girls. Benedetta needed help in handling such a great task and so Bishop Tosi asked Giovanni Battista to leave the <a href="/wiki/Somaschi_Fathers" title="Somaschi Fathers">Somaschi Fathers</a> <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> and help Benedetta in her <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/apostolic" class="extiw" title="wikt:apostolic">apostolic</a> work.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The pair made a vow to remain <a href="/wiki/Chastity" title="Chastity">chaste</a> in the hands of Bishop Tosi and then began their common work to promote the human and religious formation of poor and abandoned girls; their educational work proved to be of great benefit to Pavia and a school was opened in 1827. The <a href="/wiki/Austrian_government" class="mw-redirect" title="Austrian government">Austrian government</a> recognized her as a "Promoter of <a href="/wiki/Public_Education" class="mw-redirect" title="Public Education">Public Education</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Young female volunteers aided her, in due time to whom she them gave a rule of life that later received <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> approval from the competent authorities. Frassinello also joined formation in <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechesis</a> to in domestic skills like <a href="/wiki/Cooking" title="Cooking">cooking</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sewing" title="Sewing">sewing</a> aiming to transform her students into "models of Christian life" and so assure the formation of families. Her work was considered pioneering for those times.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Although Giovanni had left the Somaschi Fathers as the bishop's request, and both had made a vow of chastity before the bishop, that did not stop many from gossiping. In 1838, in order that she might not be a hindrance to the work, she turned over the institution to the Bishop of Pavia, and withdrew to another town.<sup id="cite_ref-FaithND_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FaithND-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>Her former husband and five companions joined her in moving to <a href="/wiki/Ronco_Scrivia" title="Ronco Scrivia">Ronco Scrivia</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> region on 16 July 1838. The group opened a school for girls there and that was a refinement on what had been done in Pavia.<sup id="cite_ref-FaithND_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FaithND-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Benedetta founded the Benedictine Sisters of Providence on 28 October 1838; it received diocesan approval in 1858, the papal decree of praise on 24 June 1917 from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> and papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 2 March 1937. In her rule she stressed the education of girls and instilled the spirit of unlimited confidence and abandonment to <a href="/wiki/Divine_providence" title="Divine providence">Divine providence</a> and to the love of God through chief <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> principles.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The order grew at a rapid pace since it performed a much-needed service. Benedetta was able to guide the development of the order until her death. On 21 March 1858 she died in <a href="/wiki/Ronco_Scrivia" title="Ronco Scrivia">Ronco Scrivia</a>. In 1944 <a href="/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II" title="Allies of World War II">Allied</a> bombings devastated the region and her remains were lost in the destruction.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her order is now present in places such as <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Burundi" title="Burundi">Burundi</a> and in 2005 there were 113 religious in 22 houses.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedetta_Cambiagio_Frassinello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process commenced in an informative process that Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Minoretti" title="Carlo Minoretti">Carlo Minoretti</a> oversaw from 1927 until closing it at a special Mass in 1932 while theologians later approved her writings on 20 November 1940 having deemed them to be in line with the faith. The postulation submitted the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in 1979 while their historical consultants approved the cause on 29 April 1980. Theological experts also voiced their assent to the cause on 23 April 1985 as did the C.C.S. members later on 4 June 1985; <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed that Frassinello possessed <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and so on 6 July 1985 titled her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.
</p><p>The process for the miracle needed for beatification was investigated where it occurred in and it received C.C.S. validation on 17 January 1986. Medical experts believed this healing to be a miracle on 10 July 1986 as did theologians on 7 November 1986 and the C.C.S. on 23 December 1986. John Paul II confirmed the healing was a miracle on 3 January 1987 and later beatified Frassinello on 10 May 1987 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>. The second and final miracle needed for full sainthood was investigated much like the first and was validated on 24 September 1999; medical experts approved this on 20 December 2000 with the theologians and C.C.S. following suit. John Paul II approved this healing as a miracle on 20 December 2001 and on 26 February 2002 - at 11:00am in the <a href="/wiki/Clementine_Hall" title="Clementine Hall">Clementine Hall</a> - confirmed the date for sainthood.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> John Paul II canonized Frassinello on 19 May 2002 in Saint Peter's Square.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
