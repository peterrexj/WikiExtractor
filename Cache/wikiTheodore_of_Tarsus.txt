<p><b>Theodore of Tarsus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Θεόδωρος Ταρσοῦ</span>; 602&#160;&#8211;&#32;19 September 690)<sup id="cite_ref-ODS496_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS496-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> from 668 to 690. Theodore grew up in <a href="/wiki/Tarsus,_Mersin" title="Tarsus, Mersin">Tarsus</a>, but fled to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> after the Persian Empire conquered Tarsus and other cities. After studying there, he relocated to Rome and was later installed as the Archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> on the orders of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vitalian" title="Pope Vitalian">Pope Vitalian</a>. Accounts of his life appear in two 8th-century texts. Theodore is best known for his reform of the English Church and establishment of a school in Canterbury.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_of_Tarsus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theodore's life can be divided into the time before his arrival in Britain as Archbishop of Canterbury, and his archiepiscopate. Until recently, scholarship on Theodore had focused on only the latter period since it is attested in <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Ecclesiastical History of the English</a></i> (<i>c</i> 731), and also in <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_Ripon" title="Stephen of Ripon">Stephen of Ripon</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Sancti_Wilfrithi" title="Vita Sancti Wilfrithi">Vita Sancti Wilfrithi</a></i> (early 700s), whereas no source directly mentions Theodore's earlier activities. However, <a href="/wiki/Michael_Lapidge" title="Michael Lapidge">Michael Lapidge</a> and Bernard Bischoff have reconstructed his earlier life based on a study of texts produced by his Canterbury School.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_of_Tarsus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theodore was of <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> descent, born in <a href="/wiki/Tarsus_in_Cilicia" class="mw-redirect" title="Tarsus in Cilicia">Tarsus in Cilicia</a>, a Greek-speaking diocese of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Theodore's childhood saw <a href="/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sasanian_War_of_602%E2%80%93628" title="Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628">devastating wars</a> between Byzantium and the Persian <a href="/wiki/Sassanid_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Sassanid Empire">Sassanid Empire</a>, which resulted in the capture of <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> in 613–614. Persian forces captured Tarsus when Theodore was 11 or 12 years old, and evidence exists that Theodore had experience of Persian culture.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It is most likely that he studied at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, the historic home of a distinctive school of <a href="/wiki/Exegesis" title="Exegesis">exegesis</a>, of which he was a proponent.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Theodore also knew Syriac culture, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">language</a> and <a href="/wiki/Syriac_literature" title="Syriac literature">literature</a>, and may even have travelled to <a href="/wiki/Edessa,_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Edessa, Mesopotamia">Edessa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Syriac <i>Acts</i> of Saint <a href="/wiki/Miles_(bishop_of_Susa)" title="Miles (bishop of Susa)">Milus of Persia</a>, which was incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Old_English_Martyrology" title="Old English Martyrology">Old English Martyrology</a>, was probably brought to England by Theodore.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStevenson1998256_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStevenson1998256-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Though a Greek could live under Persian rule, the <a href="/wiki/Muslim_conquests" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim conquests">Muslim conquests</a>, which reached Tarsus in 637, certainly drove Theodore from Tarsus; if he did not flee earlier, Theodore would have been 35 years old when he left his birthplace.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Having returned to the Eastern Roman Empire, he studied in the Byzantine capital of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, including the subjects of astronomy, ecclesiastical <a href="/wiki/Computus" class="mw-redirect" title="Computus">computus</a> (calculation of the date of Easter), astrology, medicine, Roman civil law, Greek rhetoric and philosophy, and the use of the horoscope.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At some time before the 660s, Theodore had travelled west to Rome, where he lived with a community of Eastern monks, probably at the monastery of St. Anastasius.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> At this time, in addition to his already profound Greek intellectual inheritance, he became learned in Latin literature, both sacred and secular.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a> (664) having confirmed the decision in the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon Church">Anglo-Saxon Church</a> to follow Rome, in 667, when Theodore was aged 66, the see of Canterbury happened to fall vacant. <a href="/wiki/Wighard" title="Wighard">Wighard</a>, the man chosen to fill the post, unexpectedly died. Wighard had been sent to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vitalian" title="Pope Vitalian">Pope Vitalian</a> by <a href="/wiki/Ecgberht_of_Kent" title="Ecgberht of Kent">Ecgberht</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kent" title="Kingdom of Kent">Kent</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Oswiu_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Oswiu of Northumbria">Oswy</a>, king of Northumbria, for consecration as archbishop. Following Wighard's death, Theodore was chosen by Vitalian upon the recommendation of <a href="/wiki/Adrian_of_Canterbury" title="Adrian of Canterbury">Hadrian</a> (later abbot of St. Peter's, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a>). Theodore was consecrated archbishop of Canterbury in Rome on 26 March 668, and sent to England with Hadrian, arriving on 27 May 669.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_of_Tarsus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Canterbury">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theodore conducted a survey of the English church, appointed various bishops to sees that had lain vacant for some time,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and then called the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Hertford" title="Council of Hertford">Synod of Hertford</a> (673) to institute reforms concerning the proper calculation of <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>, episcopal authority, itinerant monks, the regular convening of subsequent synods, marriage and prohibitions of consanguinity, and other matters.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
He also proposed dividing the large diocese of Northumbria into smaller sections, a policy which brought him into conflict with <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>, who had become Bishop of York in 664. Theodore deposed and expelled Wilfrid in 678, dividing his dioceses in the aftermath. The conflict with Wilfrid continued until its settlement in 686–687.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 679 <a href="/wiki/Aelfwine_of_Deira" class="mw-redirect" title="Aelfwine of Deira">Aelfwine</a>, the brother of King <a href="/wiki/Ecgfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Ecgfrith of Northumbria">Ecgfrith of Northumbria</a>, died in battle against the Mercians. Theodore's intervention prevented the escalation of the war and resulted in peace between the two kingdoms,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> with King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_of_Mercia" title="Æthelred of Mercia">Æthelred of Mercia</a> paying <i><a href="/wiki/Weregild" title="Weregild">weregild</a></i> compensation for Aelfwine's death.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canterbury_School">Canterbury School</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_of_Tarsus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Canterbury School">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg/200px-Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="300" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg/300px-Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg/400px-Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3888" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Staugustinescanterburygravetheodore.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gravesite of Theodore at St. Augustine's Abbey, Canterbury.</div></div></div>
<p>Theodore and Hadrian established a school in Canterbury, providing instruction in both Greek and Latin, resulting in a "golden age" of Anglo-Saxon scholarship:<sup id="cite_ref-Bede_1896_loc=4.2_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede_1896_loc=4.2-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd>They attracted a large number of students, into whose minds they poured the waters of wholesome knowledge day by day. In addition to instructing them in the Holy Scriptures, they also taught their pupils poetry, <a href="/wiki/Astronomy" title="Astronomy">astronomy</a>, and the calculation of the church calendar&#160;... Never had there been such happy times as these since the English settled Britain.</dd></dl>
<p>Theodore also taught sacred music,<sup id="cite_ref-Bede_1896_loc=4.2_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede_1896_loc=4.2-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> introduced various texts, knowledge of Eastern saints, and may even have been responsible for the introduction of the <a href="/wiki/Litany_of_the_Saints" title="Litany of the Saints">Litany of the Saints</a>, a major liturgical innovation, into the West.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Some of his thoughts are accessible in the Biblical Commentaries, notes compiled by his students at the Canterbury School.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Of immense interest is the text, recently attributed to him, called <i><a href="/wiki/Laterculus_Malalianus" class="mw-redirect" title="Laterculus Malalianus">Laterculus Malalianus</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Overlooked for many years, it was rediscovered in the 1990s, and has since been shown to contain numerous interesting elements reflecting Theodore's trans-Mediterranean formation.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> A record of the teaching of Theodore and Adrian is preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Leiden_Glossary" title="Leiden Glossary">Leiden Glossary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELapidge200633,_87–88_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELapidge200633,_87–88-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pupils from the school at Canterbury were sent out as <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbots in southern England, disseminating the curriculum of Theodore.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Theodore called other synods, in September 680 at <a href="/wiki/Hatfield,_Hertfordshire" title="Hatfield, Hertfordshire">Hatfield, Hertfordshire</a>, confirming English orthodoxy in the <a href="/wiki/Monothelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Monothelite">Monothelite</a> controversy,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> and circa 684 at Twyford, near <a href="/wiki/Alnwick" title="Alnwick">Alnwick</a> in Northumbria. Lastly, a <a href="/wiki/Paenitentiale_Theodori" title="Paenitentiale Theodori">penitential</a> composed under his direction is still extant.
</p><p>Theodore died in 690 at the age of 88, having held the archbishopric for twenty-two years. He was buried in Canterbury at the church known today as <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a>; at the time of his death it was called St. Peter's church.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodore_of_Tarsus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Like the archbishops of Canterbury before him, Theodore is venerated as a saint. His saint's day is 19 September in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar#England" title="General Roman Calendar">Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_liturgical_calendar" title="Eastern Orthodox liturgical calendar">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">Episcopal Church (USA)</a><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>. He is also recorded on this day in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>. Canterbury also recognises a feast of his ordination on 26 March.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS496_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS496-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
