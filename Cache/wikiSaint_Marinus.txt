<p><b>Saint Marinus</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;r&#39; in &#39;rye&#39;">r</span><span title="/iː/: &#39;ee&#39; in &#39;fleece&#39;">iː</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Marino</i>) was an <a href="/wiki/History_of_Christianity#Early_Christianity_(c._31/33–324)" title="History of Christianity">Early Christian</a> and the founder of a chapel and monastery in 301 from whose initial community the state of <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">San Marino</a> later grew.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Marinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition holds that he was a <a href="/wiki/Stonemason" class="mw-redirect" title="Stonemason">stonemason</a> by trade who came from the island of Arba (today <a href="/wiki/Rab" title="Rab">Rab</a>), on the other side of the <a href="/wiki/Adriatic_Sea" title="Adriatic Sea">Adriatic Sea</a> (in what is now part of modern-day <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>), fleeing persecution for his <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> beliefs in the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>. 
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Marino_als_steinhauer.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Marino_als_steinhauer.png/220px-Marino_als_steinhauer.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="178" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Marino_als_steinhauer.png/330px-Marino_als_steinhauer.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Marino_als_steinhauer.png/440px-Marino_als_steinhauer.png 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="972" /></a><figcaption>Illustration of Saint Marinus working at the chapel-monastery</figcaption></figure>
<p>Known only by the single name <i>Marinus</i> (lit. of the sea), he became a Deacon, and was ordained by <a href="/wiki/Gaudentius_of_Rimini" title="Gaudentius of Rimini">Gaudentius</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Rimini" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Rimini">Bishop of Rimini</a>; later, he was recognised and accused by an insane woman of being her estranged husband, so he quickly fled to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Titano" title="Monte Titano">Monte Titano</a> to build a chapel-monastery and live as a hermit.<sup id="cite_ref-Radovinovič_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Radovinovič-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Another version of the story says that hearing that the town of Rimini (Italy) was being rebuilt, he travelled there and was astonished to find among the workmen many Christians of formerly high position who had been sentenced to hard labour because of their refusal to sacrifice to the gods. He sought to comfort them and to alleviate their sufferings, so far as was in his power.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> In his old age Marinus withdrew to a hermitage and decided to seclude himself on Mount Titano, living the life of a hermit in holy contemplation. As his reputation for his sanctity grew, others started to follow him there, until finally a lady from Rimini and the owner of Mount Titano decided to gift him the mountain.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg/350px-San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="159" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg/525px-San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg/700px-San_Marino_Palazzo_Pubblico_painting.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2655" data-file-height="1205" /></a><figcaption>Painting of Saint Marinus descending from heaven surrounded by the people of San Marino, painting located within the <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Pubblico_(San_Marino)" title="Palazzo Pubblico (San Marino)">Palazzo Pubblico</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Marinus was canonised as a saint, and later, the State of San Marino grew up from the centre created by the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-Radovinovič_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Radovinovič-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His feast day/memorial day is 3 September, commemorating the day, in 301, when he founded what became known as <a href="/wiki/San_Marino" title="San Marino">San Marino</a>, which is also the state's national holiday. He is venerated in the Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox faiths.
</p><p>According to legend, he died in the winter of 366 and his last words were: "<i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Relinquo vos liberos ab utroque homine</i></span></i>" ("I leave you free from both men"). This somewhat mysterious phrase is most likely to refer to the two "men" from whose oppressive power Saint Marinus had decided to separate himself, becoming a hermit on Mount Titano: respectively the <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Emperor</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a>. This affirmation of freedom (first and foremost fiscal franchise) from both the <a href="/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire" title="Western Roman Empire">Empire</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, however legendary, has always been the inspiration of the tiny republic.<sup id="cite_ref-Miller1901_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miller1901-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

</p>
