<p><b>Augustine Fangi</b> (also, <b>Augustine of Biella</b>; 1430 – 22 July 1493) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a>. He was commonly regarded in his time as a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle worker</a>, and, in serving as the <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of several of his Order's monasteries, was concerned with restoring and maintaining a faithful observance of the Rule of Saint Dominic.<sup id="cite_ref-marisstella_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marisstella-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1878.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustine_Fangi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The life of Augustine Fangi was marked by piety and regularity, rather than by spiritual ambitions. Fangi was born in 1430 in <a href="/wiki/Biella" title="Biella">Biella</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> region of northern Italy, to a wealthy family who had planned a secular career for him. Instead, young Augustine was impressed by the newly arrived Dominican friars in Biella and joined the monastery there.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One remarkable characteristic noted of Fangi was his equanimity and ability to <a href="/wiki/Christian_meditation" title="Christian meditation">concentrate intensely</a> on spiritual matters. One incident recorded involves a <a href="/wiki/Surgery" title="Surgery">surgical procedure</a> which he was required to undergo without <a href="/wiki/Anesthetic" title="Anesthetic">anesthetic</a>, as such an aid was not available in the fifteenth century. He did so without crying out at all. Afterwards, he simply stated that his mind was so intensely focused on something else that he hardly noticed what was being done.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It is claimed that in prayer he was often seen levitating in ecstasy.<sup id="cite_ref-short_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-short-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1464, Fangi was made <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">prior</a> of the priory at <a href="/wiki/Soncino,_Lombardy" title="Soncino, Lombardy">Soncino, Lombardy</a>. He is said to have performed several miracles there. One involved a <a href="/wiki/Disability" title="Disability">deformed child</a> who had died without <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> yet was said to have been restored to life by his prayer.<sup id="cite_ref-short_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-short-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Another purported miracle involved a boy who was crying bitterly because he had broken a jug of wine. Fangi gathered up the shards and put them back together again. The account continues to state that with a <a href="/wiki/Prayer_in_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Prayer in Christianity">prayer</a>, he refilled the jug, and handed it back to the startled child.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The last ten years of Fangi's life were spent in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, where he died on 22 July 1493, the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Saint Mary Magdalene</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustine_Fangi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Augustine Fangi was buried in a <a href="/wiki/Humidity" title="Humidity">damp</a> <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>, subject to flooding like much of Venice.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In the 1530s, workmen doing repairs on the church where he was interred found his <a href="/wiki/Coffin" title="Coffin">coffin</a> floating in water which had seeped into the burial chamber. However, when opened, Fangi's body and clothing were reportedly found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This did much to increase devotion to his cause for canonization. Even so, three centuries had passed by the time he was beatified, and he had been forgotten by everyone except the residents of Biella and the Dominican Order.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1872, Fangi's <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)#Roman_Catholic_&#39;&#39;cultus&#39;&#39;" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a></i> was confirmed, and in 1878 he was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-shep_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shep-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
