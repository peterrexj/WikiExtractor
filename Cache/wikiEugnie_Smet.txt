					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eug%C3%A9nie_Smet&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Eugénie Smet">Eugénie Smet</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>The <b>Society of Helpers</b>, formerly known as the <b>Society of the Helpers of the Holy Souls</b>, is a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> of women founded in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, France, in 1856, with the objective of assisting the <a href="/wiki/Souls" class="mw-redirect" title="Souls">souls</a> in <a href="/wiki/Purgatory" title="Purgatory">Purgatory</a> through their service to the needy of the world. The Sisters use the <a href="/wiki/Postnominal" class="mw-redirect" title="Postnominal">postnominal</a> initials of "A.P." (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Auxiliatrices des âmes du purgatoire</i>) in Europe, or, alternately, "H.H.S" in English-speaking countries.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Eug.C3.A9nie_Smet"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Eugénie_Smet">Eugénie Smet</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Eugénie Smet">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Eugénie Smet</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Foundress</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">25 March 1825<br /><a href="/wiki/Lille" title="Lille">Lille</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">7 February 1871<br /><a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">26 May 1957, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">February 7</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Eugénie Smet (1825&#8211;1871), also known as <b>sœur Marie de la Providence</b>, was a French nun who founded the Society of the Helpers of the Holy Souls in 1856. Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 7 February.
</p><p>She was born in <a href="/wiki/Lille" title="Lille">Lille</a> on 25 March 1825, the daughter of Henri Édouard Joseph and Marie Pauline Joseph Taverne Smet. Her father was a trader from Lille. From 1836 to 1843 she was educated at the Convent of the Sacred Heart in Lille, where she demonstrated a particular devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Guardian_Angels" title="Guardian Angels">Guardian Angels</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Garside_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Garside-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In November 1853, she discovered her mission in the Church. In 1855, the <a href="/wiki/John_Vianney" title="John Vianney">Curé d'Ars</a> confirmed her in her mission. She went to Paris on 19 January 1856 (the society dates its foundation from this day); three days later, Smet obtained permission of <a href="/wiki/Marie-Dominique-Auguste_Sibour" title="Marie-Dominique-Auguste Sibour">Archbishop Sibour</a> to establish her congregation in Paris. The community Mademoiselle Smet had gathered round her took possession of No. 16, Rue de la Barouillère, on 1 July 1856.<sup id="cite_ref-society_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 27 December 1857, Smet, with five of her first companions, pronounced her first <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>. A <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> was appointed <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a>, and the Rule of <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">St. Ignatius</a> was adapted.<sup id="cite_ref-shuk_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shuk-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The congregation was dedicated to Our Lady of Providence.<sup id="cite_ref-Garside_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Garside-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eugénie Smet died on 7 February 1871,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> of breast cancer, at the age of 45. She is buried in the Montparnasse cemetery. She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on May 26, 1957 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>. Her feast is kept on February 7, the anniversary of her death.<sup id="cite_ref-society_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Georgiana_Fullerton" title="Georgiana Fullerton">Lady Georgiana Fullerton</a> wrote a biography of Eugénie Smet, <i>Mere Marie de la Providence</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-shr_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Communities">Communities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Communities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The first branch house was established at Nantes in July 1864. In 1867 six nuns were conducted by <a href="/wiki/Adrien_Languillat" title="Adrien Languillat">Bishop Adrien Languillat</a> to Shanghai to take charge of an orphanage. In December, 1869, a house was established in Brussels. The Helpers did good work in the ambulances for the wounded of both nations during the <a href="/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War" title="Franco-Prussian War">Franco-Prussian War</a>. On 25 June 1878, the constitutions of the order were approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>. From 1874 to 1880 communities were established at Cannes, Orléans, Tourcoing, and Montmartre.<sup id="cite_ref-society_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Present_day">Present day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Present day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Helpers profess vows of poverty, chastity and obedience, and follow an <a href="/wiki/Ignatian_spirituality" title="Ignatian spirituality">Ignatian spirituality</a>. They have worked with the poor and the marginal of their societies since their founding. They work in a variety of ministries, with the goal of working  for peace and justice in a lifestyle based in <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplation</a>. Rather than focus on a particular ministry, Smet decided to respond to such needs as presented themselves.<sup id="cite_ref-shuk_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shuk-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As of 2015 they numbered some 500 Sisters in twenty four countries.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_United_Kingdom">In the United Kingdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In the United Kingdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The first foundation in the UK was in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Westminster" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Westminster">Archdiocese of Westminster</a>, at 23 Queen Anne Street, Cavendish Square. They removed to Gloucester Avenue, Regent's Park, in 1882.<sup id="cite_ref-society_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As of 2019, sisters serve in London, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Liverpool and Burnley.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="United_States_province">United States province</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: United States province">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In May, 1892, seven "Helpers" sailed to <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>, and were heartily welcomed by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Michael_Corrigan" title="Michael Corrigan">Michael Corrigan</a>. The first convent was a small house in Seventh Avenue; there they laboured for nearly three years, when they removed to 114 East 86th Street. During 1905 a course of lectures on hygiene and first aid to the injured was given. In 1906, they had five houses in the same neighbourhood. Children from the public schools came to the convents for religious instruction and preparation for First Communion and Confirmation,<sup id="cite_ref-shr_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and there were sewing classes for girls.
</p><p>In May 1903, some Helpers were sent to St. Louis, Missouri, led by Mother Mary St. Bernard. Archbishop <a href="/wiki/John_J._Glennon" title="John J. Glennon">John J. Glennon</a> asked them to work among the African-American community. Home visitation was a major part of the Sisters’ work in North St. Louis.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1905, the Sisters went to San Francisco, where they settled in a house in Howard Street, which was destroyed in the earthquake of 1906.<sup id="cite_ref-society_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The leadership team of the U.S. Province is located in Chicago. The province produces an annual publication, <i>Voices of Hope</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
