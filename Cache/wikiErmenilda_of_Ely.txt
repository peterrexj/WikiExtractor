<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Ermenilda of Ely</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Abbess</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Eormenhild</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 700/703</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">13 February</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Eormenhild</b> (or <i>Ermenilda</i>, <i>Ermenildis</i>, <i>Ermengild</i>, all meaning "battle-great", from eormen- "great", hild- "battle") (d. about 700/703) is a 7th-century <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon">Anglo-Saxon</a> saint venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> churches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ermenilda_of_Ely&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She features in the genealogies of various 11th and 12th century versions of the <a href="/wiki/Kentish_Royal_Legend" title="Kentish Royal Legend">Kentish Royal Legend</a>. These describe her as the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Eorcenberht_of_Kent" title="Eorcenberht of Kent">Eorcenberht of Kent</a> and St. <a href="/wiki/Seaxburh_of_Ely" title="Seaxburh of Ely">Seaxburh of Ely</a>, and wife to <a href="/wiki/Wulfhere_of_Mercia" title="Wulfhere of Mercia">Wulfhere of Mercia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Casanova_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Casanova-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> with whom she had a daughter, St. <a href="/wiki/Werburgh" title="Werburgh">Wærburh</a>, and a son, <a href="/wiki/Cenred_of_Mercia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenred of Mercia">Coenred</a>. Eormenhild became a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> after her husband died in 675, and eventually became <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of <a href="/wiki/Minster,_Swale" title="Minster, Swale">Minster-in-Sheppey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ely_Cathedral#List_of_abbots_and_abbesses_of_the_first_monastery_(673-870)" title="Ely Cathedral">Ely</a> consecutively.
</p><p>There are almost no contemporary records for her life. When discussing Wulfhere, <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> mentions neither she nor her daughter Wærburh. However, her name is mentioned as an abbess in a (copy of a) <a href="/wiki/Charter" title="Charter">charter</a> of King <a href="/wiki/Wihtred_of_Kent" title="Wihtred of Kent">Wihtred of Kent</a>, dated 699, along with three other abbesses present at the occasion when the charter was issued: "Irminburga, Aeaba et Nerienda".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 13 February.
</p>
