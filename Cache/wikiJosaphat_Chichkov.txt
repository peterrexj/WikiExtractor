					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Bulgarian priest</div>
<p><b>Robert-Matthew Chichkov</b> (9 February 1884 – 11 November 1952) in <a href="/wiki/Plovdiv" title="Plovdiv">Plovdiv</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>, was a priest, rector and teacher who was <a href="/wiki/Capital_punishment" title="Capital punishment">sentenced to death</a> in the early 1950s.
</p><p>He came from a large and fervent <a href="/wiki/Latin_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Rite">Latin rite</a> Catholic family. He entered the <a href="/wiki/Assumptionist" class="mw-redirect" title="Assumptionist">Assumptionist</a> high school seminary in Adrianopolis and entered the Assumptionist novitiate in Phanaraki, Turkey in 1900. His name in religion was Josaphat. He was ordained a priest in the Latin rite in Malines, Belgium, in 1909 after studying philosophy and theology at <a href="/wiki/Katholieke_Universiteit_Leuven" class="mw-redirect" title="Katholieke Universiteit Leuven">Louvain University</a>.
</p><p>Once back in Bulgaria, he taught at St. Augustine College in Plovdiv and at St. Michael College in Varna. Later he moved to Yambol where he served as superior and rector of the high school seminary of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints Cyril and Methodius">Saints Cyril and Methodius</a>, as pastor of the local Latin rite parish, and as chaplain to the Oblate Sisters of the Assumption. In 1937 he returned to the college in Varna where he served as rector and teacher until he was arrested in 1951.
</p><p>He was responsible for enlarging the Yambol seminary to include seminarians of both rites, Latin and Byzantine-Slav, and found ways to integrate students into one community. He organized <a href="/wiki/Fundraising" title="Fundraising">fundraising</a> activities for the institution and taught French to teachers, <a href="/wiki/Civil_service" title="Civil service">civil servants</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Military_of_Bulgaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Military of Bulgaria">Bulgarian army</a> officers.
</p><p>He had a particular interest in the latest technology and introduced a <a href="/wiki/Amateur_radio" title="Amateur radio">ham radio</a> and <a href="/wiki/Movie_projector" title="Movie projector">movie projector</a> at the seminary.
</p><p>Josaphat Chichkov was arrested in December 1951. After what <a href="/wiki/International_organization" title="International organization">international organizations</a> universally considered a <a href="/wiki/Show_trial" title="Show trial">show trial</a> which began on 29 September 1952 and ended with a <a href="/wiki/Guilt_(law)" title="Guilt (law)">guilty verdict</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Death_sentence" class="mw-redirect" title="Death sentence">death sentence</a> on 3 October, Fr. Chickov, two of his Assumptionists companions, Fr. <a href="/wiki/Kamen_Vitchev" title="Kamen Vitchev">Kamen Vitchev</a> and Fr. <a href="/wiki/Pavel_Djidjov" title="Pavel Djidjov">Pavel Djidjov</a>, and a Passionist bishop, Most Rev. <a href="/wiki/Eugene_Bossilkov" title="Eugene Bossilkov">Eugene Bossilkov</a>, were shot to death, without <a href="/wiki/Public_notice" title="Public notice">public notice</a>, at 11:30 PM the evening of 11 November 1952.
</p><p>Fr. Chichkov was declared a martyr for the faith and beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in Plovdiv on 26 May 2002.
</p><p>On 28 July 2010 the Bulgarian parliament passed a law officially rehabilitating all of those who had been condemned by the People's Republic of Bulgaria in 1952, including Fr. Chichkov.
</p>
