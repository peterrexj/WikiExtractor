<p><b>Hugh Faringdon</b>, OSB (died 14 November 1539), earlier known as <b>Hugh Cook</b>, later as <b>Hugh Cook alias Faringdon</b> and <b>Hugh Cook of Faringdon</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> who presided as the last <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">Abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Reading_Abbey" title="Reading Abbey">Reading Abbey</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">English</a> town of <a href="/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire" title="Reading, Berkshire">Reading</a>. At the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">dissolution of the monasteries</a> under King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a>, Faringdon was accused of <a href="/wiki/High_treason" class="mw-redirect" title="High treason">high treason</a> and executed. He was declared a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in 1895.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_Faringdon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:265px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hugh_Faringdon_plaque,_English_Martyrs_Church,_Liebenhood_Road,_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Hugh_Faringdon_plaque%2C_English_Martyrs_Church%2C_Liebenhood_Road%2C_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg/263px-Hugh_Faringdon_plaque%2C_English_Martyrs_Church%2C_Liebenhood_Road%2C_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg" decoding="async" width="263" height="197" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Hugh_Faringdon_plaque%2C_English_Martyrs_Church%2C_Liebenhood_Road%2C_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg/395px-Hugh_Faringdon_plaque%2C_English_Martyrs_Church%2C_Liebenhood_Road%2C_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d3/Hugh_Faringdon_plaque%2C_English_Martyrs_Church%2C_Liebenhood_Road%2C_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg/526px-Hugh_Faringdon_plaque%2C_English_Martyrs_Church%2C_Liebenhood_Road%2C_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hugh_Faringdon_plaque,_English_Martyrs_Church,_Liebenhood_Road,_Reading_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1769778.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Hugh Faringdon plaque, English Martyrs Church, Liebenrood Road, Reading</div></div></div><p>Born Hugh Cook, he adopted the surname of Faringdon when he became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, sometime prior to 1500. The use of this surname suggests that he came from <a href="/wiki/Faringdon" title="Faringdon">Faringdon</a>, a town some 30 miles (48&#160;km) northwest of Reading. However it is also significant that he subsequently used the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">arms</a> of the Cook family of <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>, suggesting that he had connections there.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is believed to have been educated within the abbey, and later served as the <a href="/wiki/Cellarium" title="Cellarium">sub-cellarer</a> of the abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hugh Faringdon was elected Abbot of Reading Abbey in 1520, upon the death of Abbot Thomas Worcester. As well as his spiritual duties, he also took up the civil duties expected at that time of a <a href="/wiki/Mitre" title="Mitre">mitred</a> abbot, being appointed as <a href="/wiki/Justice_of_the_Peace" class="mw-redirect" title="Justice of the Peace">Justice of the Peace</a> and to various governmental Commissions for <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a> from 1526 to 1538.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At first Faringdon's relationship with King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a> seems to have been supportive. King Henry was his guest on 30 January 1521, and he later became one of the royal chaplains. Among Henry's New Year gifts in 1532 was £20 in a white leather purse to the Abbot of Reading.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When the king was hunting in the neighborhood, the abbot would take the opportunity of sending him presents of Kennet trout or hunting knives.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Faringdon seems to have taken the king's side during the divorce controversy. While Henry was searching for authorities to support his views on matrimonial laws, Faringdon sent him books which he thought would serve the purpose.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He sat in <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_England" title="Parliament of England">Parliament</a> from 1523 to 1539 and, in 1530, he signed, with other members of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>, a letter to the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> pointing out the evils likely to result from delaying the divorce desired by the King; and, again in 1536, he signed the Articles of Faith drawn up in Convocation which virtually acknowledged the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Supremacy" title="Acts of Supremacy">supremacy of the Crown</a> over the Church. On Sunday, 4 November 1537, he sang the requiem and dirge for Queen <a href="/wiki/Jane_Seymour" title="Jane Seymour">Jane Seymour</a> at St. George's Chapel, Windsor, and was present at the burial on 12 November. As late as March 1538, he was in favour, being placed in the commission of the peace for Berkshire.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the commissioners arrived to take the surrender of <a href="/wiki/Reading_Abbey" title="Reading Abbey">Reading Abbey</a>, they reported favourably of the abbot's willingness to conform, but the surrender of the abbey does not survive, and it is not therefore known whether or not Faringdon actually signed it.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:265px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Reading_Abbey,_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Reading_Abbey%2C_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg/263px-Reading_Abbey%2C_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg" decoding="async" width="263" height="351" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Reading_Abbey%2C_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg/395px-Reading_Abbey%2C_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Reading_Abbey%2C_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg/526px-Reading_Abbey%2C_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3120" data-file-height="4160" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Reading_Abbey,_Martyrdom_Bas-relief.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bas-relief of execution of Hugh Faringdon, at Reading Abbey ruins.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1539, Faringdon was indicted for high treason, being accused of having assisted the Northern rebels with money. He was tracked down at Bere Court, his manor at <a href="/wiki/Pangbourne" title="Pangbourne">Pangbourne</a>, and taken back to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>, where he spent two months.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As a mitred abbot he was entitled to be tried by Parliament, but no scruples troubled the <a href="/wiki/Chancellor" title="Chancellor">chancellor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Thomas Cromwell</a>. His death sentence was passed before his trial began.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Along with John Rugg, a known associate, and <a href="/wiki/John_Eynon" title="John Eynon">John Eynon</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a> of <a href="/wiki/St_Giles%27_Church,_Reading" title="St Giles&#39; Church, Reading">St Giles' Church</a> in Reading, he was found guilty and <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> before the inner Abbey <a href="/wiki/Gatehouse" title="Gatehouse">gatehouse</a> on 14 November 1539.
</p><p>John Rugg had been charged with taking and concealing one of the Abbey's celebrated relics, being the purported hand of St. Anastasius. The monks of Reading, not under suspicion of complicity in the Abbot's alleged treason, were given pensions normally set upon monks and nuns at the dissolution of their monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-dnb_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dnb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_Faringdon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hugh Faringdon was declared a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1895. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 15 November.
</p><p>There are stained glass windows of Blessed Hugh in the following churches: 
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St_James%27_Church,_Reading" class="mw-redirect" title="St James&#39; Church, Reading">St James' Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> parish church that occupies part of the footprint of the now ruined Reading Abbey<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Meinrad_Archabbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Meinrad Archabbey">Saint Meinrad Archabbey</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Meinrad,_Indiana" title="Saint Meinrad, Indiana">Saint Meinrad, Indiana</a>, along with beatified companions <a href="/wiki/Richard_Whiting_(abbot)" title="Richard Whiting (abbot)">Whiting</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Marshall_(Abbot_of_Colchester)" title="Thomas Marshall (Abbot of Colchester)">Beche</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Belmont_Abbey,_Herefordshire" title="Belmont Abbey, Herefordshire">Belmont Abbey</a> outside <a href="/wiki/Hereford" title="Hereford">Hereford</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St_James%27s_Church,_Reading" title="St James&#39;s Church, Reading">Saint James Roman Catholic Church</a> in Reading, along with his two martyred companions.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>A plaque at the English Martyrs Church in Liebenrood Road, Reading commemorates him.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a panel painting of him in the Our Lady and Saint Anne Church in Caversham, Reading.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is also depicted on <i>The Martyrdom of Hugh Faringdon, last Abbot of Reading</i>, painted by <a href="/wiki/Harry_Morley" title="Harry Morley">Harry Morley</a> in 1917, and now in the collection of the <a href="/wiki/Museum_of_Reading" class="mw-redirect" title="Museum of Reading">Museum of Reading</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Hugh_Faringdon_Catholic_School" title="Blessed Hugh Faringdon Catholic School">Blessed Hugh Faringdon Catholic School</a>, a specialist performing arts college in Reading, is named after him,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> as also the Blessed Hugh Catholic Church in Faringdon.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
