					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Flora_and_Mary&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Flora and Mary">Flora and Mary</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Flora and María</b> were the first two of nine female <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Martyrs of Córdoba">Martyrs of Córdoba</a>. After denouncing <a href="/wiki/Islam_in_Spain" title="Islam in Spain">Islam</a> before an <a href="/wiki/Qadi" title="Qadi">Islamic judge</a>, they were imprisoned. Though threatened "with being thrown upon the streets as <a href="/wiki/Prostitute" class="mw-redirect" title="Prostitute">prostitutes</a>", they were eventually <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-women_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-women-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  They are commemorated on 24 November.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Flora">Flora</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flora_and_Maria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Flora">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flora was born to a Christian mother and a Muslim father.  Her father died when she was still very young, and Flora was brought up as a Christian. Her Muslim older brother tried in vain to convert Flora to Islam, eventually surrendering his sister to the authorities.  Flora was imprisoned and a Muslim scholar was appointed to teach her about Islam, but she managed to escape and fled towards the Christian settlements.
</p>
<h2><span id="Mar.C3.ADa"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="María">María</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flora_and_Maria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: María">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>María was born to a Christian father and <a href="/wiki/Muslim" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim">Muslim</a> mother. Her mother was <a href="/wiki/Baptised" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptised">baptised</a> some time after she got married. Fearing charges of <a href="/wiki/Apostasy_in_Islam" title="Apostasy in Islam">apostasy</a>, the couple and their two children left their home in the town of <a href="/wiki/Elche" title="Elche">Elche</a> and moved to the village of Fronianus. María lost her mother at a young age, and her father sent her to <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> in <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuteclara&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cuteclara (page does not exist)">Cuteclara</a>. The convent's <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>, Artemia, told María how she witnessed the execution of her two sons by the Muslim rulers thirty years prior. The story had a long-lasting impression on the girl.
</p><p>María's older brother, Walabonsus, also received a religious education at the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of Saint Felix. The siblings, who were separated when María was sent to the convent and Walabonsus to the monastery, met again when Walabonsus was appointed one of the supervisors of María's convent. Walabonsus was executed by Muslim authorities on 16 July 851, and his martyrdom along with Artemia's story drove María to follow in her brother's footsteps.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Christians_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christians-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_martyrdom">Imprisonment and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flora_and_Maria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flora and María met at the church of Saint <a href="/wiki/Acisclus" title="Acisclus">Acisclus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Christians_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christians-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and decided to denounce <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a> together.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Christians_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christians-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They did so before a <i><a href="/wiki/Qadi" title="Qadi">Qadi</a></i> (Islamic judge) and were subsequently imprisoned.<sup id="cite_ref-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In jail, Flora was offered a full <a href="/wiki/Pardon" title="Pardon">pardon</a> if she "returned" to Islam, but she explained that the only beliefs she had ever held were Christian. This explanation was not accepted because according to <a href="/wiki/Islamic_law" class="mw-redirect" title="Islamic law">Islamic law</a>, a child born to even one Muslim parent should have adopted Islam as their religion.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Christians_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christians-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabigotho&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sabigotho (page does not exist)">Sabigotho</a> used to visit Flora and María in jail and once spent a night in their cell "as if she herself were shackled, not only to console the two soldiers, but to confide in them her own intention to die".<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-women_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-women-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In accordance with Shari'a law, Flora and María were found guilty of two different crimes: Flora was executed for <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostasy</a>, and María for <a href="/wiki/Islam_and_blasphemy" title="Islam and blasphemy">blasphemy</a>.  Before the executions, the young women were threatened "with being thrown upon the streets as prostitutes", which was an unbearable punishment for <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgins</a>.  They were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on 24 November 851.<sup id="cite_ref-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their bodies were left in the open for a day then thrown into the river.  The body of Mary was recovered and taken to the convent, that of Flora was never recovered.  Their heads were buried at the church of St Acisilus in Cordoba.<sup id="cite_ref-Fell_1750_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fell_1750-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Flora_and_Maria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flora and María were the first two of nine female <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Martyrs of Córdoba">Martyrs of Córdoba</a> described by <a href="/wiki/Eulogius_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Eulogius of Córdoba">Eulogius of Córdoba</a> in his <i>Memorial of the Saints</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christianity_and_Islam_in_Spain-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Their example inspired other Christians to become martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-women_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-women-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
