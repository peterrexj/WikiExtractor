<p><b>Adomnán</b> or <b>Adamnán of Iona</b> (<small>Old Irish pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="sga-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA" title="Help:IPA">[ˈaðəṽˌnaːn]</a></span>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Adamnanus, Adomnanus</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 624&#160;– 704), also known as <b>Eunan</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/j/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;yes&#39;">j</span><span title="/uː/: &#39;oo&#39; in &#39;goose&#39;">uː</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">YOO</span>-nən</i></a>; from <span title="Irish-language text"><i lang="ga">Naomh Adhamhnán</i></span>), was an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Iona Abbey</a> (<abbr title="Reigned">r.</abbr>&#160;679–704), <a href="/wiki/Hagiographer" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographer">hagiographer</a>, statesman, canon jurist, and <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">saint</a>. He was the author of the <i><a href="/wiki/Life_of_Columba" title="Life of Columba">Life of Columba</a></i> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vita Columbae</i>), probably written between 697 and 700. This biography is by far the most important surviving work written in early-medieval Scotland, and is a vital source for our knowledge of the <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a>, and an insight into the life of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> and the early-medieval <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Gaelic</a> monk.
</p><p>Adomnán promulgated the <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A1in_Adomn%C3%A1in" title="Cáin Adomnáin">Law of Adomnán</a> or "Law of Innocents" (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Lex Innocentium</i>). He also wrote the treatise <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/De_Locis_Sanctis" class="mw-redirect" title="De Locis Sanctis">De Locis Sanctis</a></i></span> ('On Holy Places'), an account of the great Christian holy places and centres of pilgrimage. Adomnán got much of his information from a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> bishop called <a href="/wiki/Arculf" title="Arculf">Arculf</a>, who had personally visited <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, and visited Iona afterwards.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adomn%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adomnán was born about 624, a relative on his father's side of Columba.<sup id="cite_ref-Wetherill_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wetherill-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Northern_U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Northern Uí Néill">Northern Uí Néill</a> lineage <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_Conaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenél Conaill">Cenél Conaill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was the son of Rónán mac Tinne by Ronat, a woman from another <a href="/wiki/Northern_U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Northern Uí Néill">Northern Uí Néill</a> lineage known as the Cenél nÉnda. Adomnán's birthplace was probably in or near <a href="/wiki/Raphoe" title="Raphoe">Raphoe</a>, a town in what later became <a href="/wiki/T%C3%ADr_Chonaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Tír Chonaill">Tír Chonaill</a> (now mainly <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">County Donegal</a>), in <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a> in the north of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. Some of Adomnán's childhood anecdotes seem to confirm at least an upbringing in this fertile eastern part of present-day County Donegal, not far from the modern city of <a href="/wiki/Derry" title="Derry">Derry</a>.
</p><p>It is thought that Adomnán may have begun his monastic career at a Columban monastery called Druim Tuamma, but any Columban foundation in northern <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> or <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_Riata" title="Dál Riata">Dál Riata</a> is a possibility, although Durrow is a stronger possibility than most. He probably joined the Columban <i>familia</i> (i.e. the federation of monasteries under the leadership of <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Iona Abbey</a>) around the year 640. Some modern commentators believe that he could not have come to Iona until sometime after the year 669, the year of the accession of <a href="/wiki/F%C3%A1ilbe_mac_P%C3%ADp%C3%A1in" title="Fáilbe mac Pípáin">Fáilbe mac Pípáin</a>, the first abbot of whom Adomnán gives any information. However, Richard Sharpe argues that he probably came to Iona during the abbacy of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9g%C3%A9ne_of_Iona" class="mw-redirect" title="Ségéne of Iona">Ségéne</a> (d. 652). Whenever or wherever Adomnán received his education, Adomnán attained a level of learning rare in <a href="/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages" title="Early Middle Ages">early-medieval</a> Northern Europe. It has been suggested by Alfred Smyth that Adomnán spent some years teaching and studying at Durrow,<sup id="cite_ref-smyth_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smyth-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and while this is not accepted by all scholars, it remains a strong possibility.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Iona_Abbey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Iona_Abbey.jpg/305px-Iona_Abbey.jpg" decoding="async" width="305" height="202" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Iona_Abbey.jpg/458px-Iona_Abbey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ca/Iona_Abbey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="579" data-file-height="384" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Iona Abbey</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 679, Adomnán became the ninth abbot of Iona after Columba.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Abbot Adomnán enjoyed a friendship with King <a href="/wiki/Aldfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Aldfrith of Northumbria">Aldfrith of Northumbria</a>. In 684, Aldfrith had been staying with Adomnán in Iona. In 686, after the death of Aldfrith's brother King <a href="/wiki/Ecgfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Ecgfrith of Northumbria">Ecgfrith of Northumbria</a> and Aldfrith's succession to the kingship, Adomnán was in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Northumbria">Kingdom of Northumbria</a> on the request of King <a href="/wiki/F%C3%ADnsnechta_Fledach" title="Fínsnechta Fledach">Fínsnechta Fledach</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kings_of_Brega" title="Kings of Brega">Brega</a> in order to gain the freedom of sixty <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Gaels</a> who had been captured in a Northumbrian raid two years before.<sup id="cite_ref-smyth_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smyth-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adomnán, in keeping with Ionan tradition, made several more trips to the lands of the English during his abbacy, including one the following year. It is sometimes thought, after the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_History_of_the_English_People" title="Ecclesiastical History of the English People">account</a> given by <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, that it was during his visits to Northumbria, under the influence of Abbot <a href="/wiki/Ceolfrith" title="Ceolfrith">Ceolfrith</a>, that Adomnán decided to adopt the Roman dating of <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> that had been agreed some years before at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a>. Bede implies that this led to a schism at Iona, whereby Adomnán became alienated from the Iona brethren and went to Ireland to convince the Irish of the Roman dating. Jeffrey Wetherill sees Adomnán's long absences from Iona as having led to something of an undermining of his authority; he was thus unable to persuade the monks to adopt the Roman dating of Easter, let alone the tonsure.<sup id="cite_ref-Wetherill_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wetherill-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It is clear that Adomnán did adopt that Roman dating and, moreover, probably did argue the case for it in Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For many years, the people of Leinster made a triennial tribute payment, called the <i>Borumha</i>, to the Uí Néill. The payment was made largely in cattle. Around 692, the King of Leinster was prepared to go to war over the payment, but first sent a delegation led by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Moling" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Moling">Saint Moling</a> to ask for its remittance. <a href="/wiki/F%C3%ADnsnechta_Fledach" title="Fínsnechta Fledach">Fínsnechta Fledach</a> agreed to remit payment "for a day and a night". Moling argued that "All time is day and night; thou canst never reimpose this tax." Although this was not the king's intention, Moling held him to his word, promising him heaven if he kept it, and the reverse if he did not. When Adomnán learned of this he left Iona to see the king and scolded him soundly for yielding the rights of the Uí Néill.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="C.C3.A1in_Adomn.C3.A1in"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Cáin_Adomnáin"><i>Cáin Adomnáin</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adomn%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cáin Adomnáin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A1in_Adomn%C3%A1in" title="Cáin Adomnáin">Cáin Adomnáin</a></div>
<p>It is generally believed that in 697, Adomnán promulgated the <i><a href="/wiki/C%C3%A1in_Adomn%C3%A1in" title="Cáin Adomnáin">Cáin Adomnáin</a></i>, meaning literally the "Canons" or "Law of Adomnán". The <i>Cáin Adomnáin</i> was promulgated amongst a gathering of <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a>, Dál Riatan and <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Pictish</a> notables at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Birr" title="Synod of Birr">Synod of Birr</a> in 697.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It is a set of laws designed, among other things, to guarantee the safety and immunity of various types of non-combatants in warfare. For this reason it is also known as the <i>Lex Innocentium</i> (Law of Innocents).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adomn%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adomnán's most important work, and the one for which he is best known, is the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Columbae" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita Columbae">Vita Columbae</a></i> ("Life of Columba"), a hagiography of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>'s founder, <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-butler_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> probably written between 697 and 700. The format borrows to some extent from <a href="/wiki/Sulpicius_Severus" title="Sulpicius Severus">Sulpicius Severus</a>' <i>Life of Saint <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ellis_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ellis-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Adomnán adapted traditional forms of Christian biography to group stories about Columba thematically rather than chronologically,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and present Columba as comparable to a hero in Gaelic mythology.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Wetherill suggests that one of the motivations for writing the <i>Vita</i> was to offer Columba as a model for the monks, and thereby improve Adomnán's standing as abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-Wetherill_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wetherill-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The biography is by far the most important surviving work written in early-medieval Scotland, and is a vital source for our knowledge of the Picts, as well as a great insight into the life of Iona and the early-medieval Gaelic monk.
</p><p>However, the <i>Vita</i> was not his only work. Adomnán also wrote the treatise <i><a href="/wiki/De_Locis_Sanctis" class="mw-redirect" title="De Locis Sanctis">De Locis Sanctis</a></i> (i.e. "On Holy Places"), an account of the great Christian holy places and centres of pilgrimage. Adomnán got much of his information from a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> bishop called <a href="/wiki/Arculf" title="Arculf">Arculf</a>, who had personally visited the <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, and visited Iona afterwards. Adomnán gave a copy to the scholar-king <a href="/wiki/Aldfrith" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldfrith">Aldfrith</a> of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> (685–704).<sup id="cite_ref-smyth_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smyth-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Also attributed to him is a good deal of Gaelic poetry, including a celebration of the Pictish King <a href="/wiki/Bridei_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Bridei III">Bridei</a>'s (671–93) victory over the Northumbrians at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Dun_Nechtain" title="Battle of Dun Nechtain">Battle of Dun Nechtain</a> (685).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adomn%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adomnán died in 704, and became a saint in Scottish and Irish tradition, as well as one of the most important figures in either Scottish or Irish history. His death and <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> are commemorated on 23 September. Along with <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a>, he is joint patron of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Raphoe" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Raphoe">Roman Catholic Diocese of Raphoe</a>, which encompasses the bulk of <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">County Donegal</a> in the north-west of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_St_Eunan_and_St_Columba" title="Cathedral of St Eunan and St Columba">Cathedral of St Eunan and St Columba</a> (popularly known as St. Eunan's Cathedral), the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> in that diocese, is in <a href="/wiki/Letterkenny" title="Letterkenny">Letterkenny</a>. In 727 the relics of Adomnán were brought to Ireland to renew the <i>Cáin Adomnáin</i><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and they were returned to Iona in 730.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adomn%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In his native Donegal, Adomnán has given his name to several institutions and buildings including:
</p>
<ul><li>The <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._Eunan_and_St_Columba" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of St. Eunan and St Columba">Cathedral of St. Eunan and St Columba</a> in <a href="/wiki/Letterkenny" title="Letterkenny">Letterkenny</a>, County Donegal; the current seat of the Bishop of Raphoe,</li>
<li>The Anglican <a href="/wiki/St_Eunan%27s_Cathedral,_Raphoe" title="St Eunan&#39;s Cathedral, Raphoe">St Eunan's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Raphoe" title="Raphoe">Raphoe</a>, County Donegal,</li>
<li>St Eunan's NS, a national school in Raphoe,</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St_Eunan%27s_College" title="St Eunan&#39;s College">St Eunan's College</a>, a secondary school in Letterkenny,</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St_Eunan%27s_NS" class="mw-redirect" title="St Eunan&#39;s NS">St Eunan's NS</a>, a national school in the small village of <a href="/wiki/Laghey" class="mw-redirect" title="Laghey">Laghey</a>, just south of <a href="/wiki/Donegal_(town)" title="Donegal (town)">Donegal Town</a>,</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St_Eunan%27s_GAA" title="St Eunan&#39;s GAA">St Eunan's GAA</a>, a GAA club in Letterkenny</li></ul>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/County_Sligo" title="County Sligo">County Sligo</a>, just to the south, he is venerated as the founder of Skreen Abbey, now the site of the Church of Ireland church of Skreen Parish.
</p>
