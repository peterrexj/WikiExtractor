<p><b>Agnes of Bohemia</b>, <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">O.S.C.</a> (<a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Svatá Anežka Česká</i>, 20 January 1211 – 2 March 1282), also known as <b>Agnes of Prague</b>, was a medieval <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemian</a> princess who opted for a life of charity, <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification of the flesh</a> and piety over a life of luxury and comfort. Although she was venerated soon after her death, Agnes was not <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> or <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> for over 700 years.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood">Childhood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Agnes was the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Ottokar_I_of_Bohemia" title="Ottokar I of Bohemia">Ottokar I of Bohemia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer2004_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer2004-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> making her a descendant of <a href="/wiki/Ludmila_of_Bohemia" title="Ludmila of Bohemia">Ludmila of Bohemia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" title="Wenceslaus I, Duke of Bohemia">Wenceslaus I</a>, <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia" title="Kingdom of Bohemia">Bohemia</a>. Agnes's mother was <a href="/wiki/Constance_of_Hungary" title="Constance of Hungary">Constance of Hungary</a>, who was the sister of King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_II_of_Hungary" title="Andrew II of Hungary">Andrew II of Hungary</a>, so Agnes was a first cousin to <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth of Hungary</a>.
</p><p>When she was three years old, Agnes was entrusted to the care of <a href="/wiki/Hedwig_of_Andechs" class="mw-redirect" title="Hedwig of Andechs">Hedwig of Andechs</a>, the wife of Duke <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_the_Bearded" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry I the Bearded">Henry I the Bearded</a> of <a href="/wiki/Silesia" title="Silesia">Silesia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Hedwig placed her to be educated by a community of <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> in a monastery that she herself had founded in <a href="/wiki/Trzebnica" title="Trzebnica">Trzebnica</a>. Upon her return to <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, Agnes was entrusted to a <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> of <a href="/wiki/Premonstratensian" class="mw-redirect" title="Premonstratensian">Premonstratensian</a> <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular#Canonesses_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">Canonesses</a> to continue her education.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Arranged_marriages">Arranged marriages</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Arranged marriages">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the age of eight, Agnes was betrothed to <a href="/wiki/Henry_(VII)_of_Germany" title="Henry (VII) of Germany">Henry</a>, son of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Henry was ten years old and had just been crowned <a href="/wiki/King_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Germany">King of Germany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to custom, Agnes should have spent her childhood at her future husband's court. Emperor Frederick, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily" title="Kingdom of Sicily">King of Sicily</a>, had his court in <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>, but his son Henry, King of Germany, lived in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> at the palace of Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Engelbert_II_of_Berg" title="Engelbert II of Berg">Engelbert</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>.
</p><p>Agnes was sent to the court of Duke <a href="/wiki/Leopold_VI,_Duke_of_Austria" title="Leopold VI, Duke of Austria">Leopold VI of Babenberg</a>. Leopold, however, wanted the young Henry to marry his daughter, <a href="/wiki/Margaret,_Duchess_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret, Duchess of Austria">Margaret</a>. After being betrothed for six years, Henry and Agnes's betrothal contract  was cancelled. Like other noble women of her time, Agnes was a valuable political pawn. In 1226, Agnes's father Ottokar went to war against the Babenbergs as a result of the cancelled betrothal. Ottokar then planned for Agnes to marry <a href="/wiki/Henry_III_of_England" title="Henry III of England">Henry III of England</a>, but this was vetoed by the Emperor, who wanted to marry Agnes himself.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundress">Foundress</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Foundress">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg/170px-St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="236" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg/255px-St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg/340px-St_Agnes_tending_the_sick.jpg 2x" data-file-width="577" data-file-height="800" /></a><figcaption><i>Agnes of Bohemia Tending the Sick</i> by the Bohemian Master, 1482</figcaption></figure>
<p>Agnes refused to play any more part in a politically arranged marriage. She decided to devote her life to prayer and spiritual works, for which she sought the help of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a>. Emperor Frederick is said to have remarked: "If she had left me for a mortal man, I would have taken vengeance with the sword, but I cannot take offence because in preference to me she has chosen the King of Heaven."<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On land donated by her brother, <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_I,_King_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Wenceslaus I, King of Bohemia">Wenceslaus I, King of Bohemia</a>, she founded the <a href="/wiki/History_of_hospitals#Late_medieval_European_hospitals" title="History of hospitals">Hospital</a> of St. Francis (<i>circa</i> 1232–33)<sup id="cite_ref-catholicsaints_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicsaints-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and two friaries for the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> friars, who had just come to Bohemia at her brother's invitation. Through them, Agnes learned of <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a> and her <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Poor_Ladies" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Poor Ladies">Order of Poor Ladies</a>, the monastic counterpart of the friars. She began a correspondence with Clare (which lasted for over two decades).
</p><p>Agnes built a monastery and friary complex attached to the hospital. It housed the Franciscan friars and the Poor Clare nuns who worked at the hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> This religious complex was one of the first <a href="/wiki/Gothic_architecture" title="Gothic architecture">Gothic</a> buildings in Prague. This was the first Poor Clare community north of the <a href="/wiki/Alps" title="Alps">Alps</a>. In 1235, Agnes gave the property of the <a href="/wiki/Teutonic_Knights" class="mw-redirect" title="Teutonic Knights">Teutonic Knights</a> in Bohemia to the hospital. She herself became a member of what became known as the Franciscan Poor Clares in 1234.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> As a nun, she cooked for and mended the clothes of <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">lepers</a> and paupers, even after becoming <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of the Prague Clares the following year.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicsaints_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicsaints-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> As can be seen in their correspondence, Clare wrote with deep maternal feelings toward Agnes, though they never met.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A lay group working at the hospital was organized by Agnes in 1238 as a new <a href="/wiki/Military_order_(society)" class="mw-redirect" title="Military order (society)">military order</a>, dedicated primarily to nursing, known as the <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_the_Cross_with_the_Red_Star" title="Knights of the Cross with the Red Star">Knights of the Cross with the Red Star</a>, following the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Augustine" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Augustine">Rule of St. Augustine</a>. That next year, Agnes handed over all authority over the hospital she had founded to these monastic knights. They were recognized as an order by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a> in 1236–37.
</p><p>Agnes lived out her life in the cloister, leading the monastery as abbess, until her death on 2 March 1282.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Monastery of the Holy Savior, renamed the <a href="/wiki/Convent_of_Saint_Agnes_in_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Convent of Saint Agnes in Prague">Convent of Saint Agnes</a>, (<a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Klášter sv. Anežky</i>) began to fall into decline after the <a href="/wiki/Hussite_Wars" title="Hussite Wars">Hussite Wars</a> of the 15th century. The community was abolished in 1782. Restored in the 1960s, the building is now a branch of the <a href="/wiki/National_Gallery_in_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="National Gallery in Prague">National Gallery in Prague</a>, featuring the medieval Central European and Bohemian collections.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1874, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> Agnes.<sup id="cite_ref-Mueller130_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mueller130-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Blessed Agnes on 12 November 1989.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Mueller130_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mueller130-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> While she was known by her contemporaries because of her supposed visions and healing, such as her prophecy that King Wenceslaus would be victorious in his battle against the Austrians, her canonization was based on her practice of the Christian virtues of faith, hope, and charity to an extraordinary degree, and the church's view is confirmed either through a miracle granted by God in answer to the saint's prayers or as in this case, by the continuing devotion of the Christian faithful to a saint's example across centuries.
</p><p>Though Agnes died in 1282, she is still venerated by Christians around the world more than 700 years later. She was honored in 2011, the 800th anniversary of her birth, as the Saint of the Overthrow of Communism,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> with a year dedicated to her by Catholics in the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_reference">Cultural reference</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Cultural reference">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On the occasion of the eight-hundredth anniversary of Agnes' birth, the Prague Archbishopric in cooperation with the National Gallery in Prague organized an exhibition called "Saint Agnes of Bohemia – Princess and Nun"  at the national cultural heritage site, the <a href="/wiki/Convent_of_Saint_Agnes_in_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Convent of Saint Agnes in Prague">Convent of Saint Agnes in Prague</a> (Old Town). The exhibition was held from November 25, 2011, to March 25, 2012. A similar exhibition, also honoring Agnes of Bohemia, took place some 80 years ago. The exposition held about 300 exhibits. Contributing partners included are the <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_the_Cross_with_the_Red_Star" title="Knights of the Cross with the Red Star">Knights of the Cross with the Red Star</a>, the National Archives and <a href="/wiki/Charles_University" title="Charles University">Charles University</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
