<p><b>Amadeus IX</b> (1 February 1435 &#8211; 30 March 1472), nicknamed <b>the Happy</b>, was the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Savoy" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Savoy">Duke of Savoy</a> from 1465 to 1472. The Catholic Church venerates him with a liturgical feast on March 30.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_IX,_Duke_of_Savoy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born at <a href="/wiki/Thonon-les-Bains" title="Thonon-les-Bains">Thonon-les-Bains</a>, the son of <a href="/wiki/Louis,_Duke_of_Savoy" title="Louis, Duke of Savoy">Louis, Duke of Savoy</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Anne_de_Lusignan" class="mw-redirect" title="Anne de Lusignan">Anne de Lusignan</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Janus_of_Cyprus" class="mw-redirect" title="Janus of Cyprus">Janus of Cyprus</a>, King of Cyprus. In 1452, his mother arranged a political marriage to <a href="/wiki/Yolande_of_Valois" title="Yolande of Valois">Yolande of Valois</a> (1434&#8211;1478), sister of <a href="/wiki/Louis_XI_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XI of France">Louis XI of France</a> and daughter of <a href="/wiki/Charles_VII_of_France" title="Charles VII of France">Charles VII of France</a>. Because of his epilepsy and retirement, she was left in control of the state.<sup id="cite_ref-bunson_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bunson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and the Holy Roman Empire competed to gain control of Savoy's strategically important <a href="/wiki/Alps" title="Alps">Alpine</a>'s mountain passes and trade routes.<sup id="cite_ref-savoy_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-savoy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His sister, <a href="/wiki/Charlotte_of_Savoy" title="Charlotte of Savoy">Charlotte of Savoy</a>, became the second wife of <a href="/wiki/Louis_XI_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XI of France">Louis XI of France</a>. French influence increased in Savoy and involved the country in the wars between France and the emperors.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Castle of Moncalleri in Piedmont, Italy was initially built around 1100 as a fortress on a hill, to command the main southern access to Turin. In the mid-15th century Yolande turned it into a Renaissance Royal Palace.
</p><p>Amadeus was a particular protector of Franciscan friars and endowed other religious houses as well as homes for the care of the poor and suffering.<sup id="cite_ref-savoy_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-savoy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He made a pilgrimage to Saint-Claude in 1471.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Amadeus died in 1472.<sup id="cite_ref-savoy_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-savoy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was also an avid collector of <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscripts</a>, adding over sixty items to the ducal library started by his great-grandfather <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus VIII">Amadeus VIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_IX,_Duke_of_Savoy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Amadeus IX had ten children with Yolande of Valois:
</p>
<ol><li>Louis (1453)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Savoy" title="Anne of Savoy">Anne</a> (1455&#8211;1480), married <a href="/wiki/Frederick_IV_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick IV of Naples">Frederick IV of Naples</a> (1452&#8211;1504), prince of <a href="/wiki/Altamura" title="Altamura">Altamura</a></li>
<li>Charles (1456&#8211;1471), <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Piedmont" title="Prince of Piedmont">Prince of Piedmont</a></li>
<li>Maria (1460&#8211;1511) married <a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Hachberg-Sausenberg" title="Philip of Hachberg-Sausenberg">Philip of Hachberg-Sausenberg</a> (1454–1503)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Louise_of_Savoy_(nun)" title="Louise of Savoy (nun)">Louise</a> (1461&#8211;1503), married Hugh, Prince of <a href="/wiki/House_of_Ch%C3%A2lon-Arlay" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Châlon-Arlay">Chalon</a> and, later, became a <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clare">Poor Clare</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Philibert_I,_Duke_of_Savoy" title="Philibert I, Duke of Savoy">Philibert</a> (1465&#8211;1482), oldest surviving son</li>
<li>Bernard (1467)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Charles_I_of_Savoy" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles I of Savoy">Charles</a> (1468&#8211;1490)</li>
<li>James Louis (1470&#8211;1485), Count of <a href="/wiki/Genevois_(province)" title="Genevois (province)">Genevois</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a></li>
<li>Gian Claudio Galeazzo (1472)</li></ol>
<p>His daughter Louise became a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a> after being widowed at a young age. She was also beatified.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_IX,_Duke_of_Savoy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A painting of Amadeus, made in 1474 was housed in the Dominican church in Turin and acquired a miraculous reputation.
In 1612 a brief text was published in the same city, by Girolamo Cordieri, canon of the cathedral chapter of Mondovi, extolling the holy Amadeus. Cordieri was later appointed theologian to <a href="/wiki/Charles_Emmanuel_I,_Duke_of_Savoy" title="Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy">Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy</a>. Also that year, a canon from Vercelli published a compendium of miracles attributed to the intercession of Amadeus IX. The cultus of Amadeus was actively promoted by Charles Emanuel's son, <a href="/wiki/Prince_Maurice_of_Savoy" title="Prince Maurice of Savoy">Prince Maurice of Savoy</a>, Cardinal of Vercelli.<sup id="cite_ref-Merle_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Merle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1613, an <i>Historia del Beato Amedeo terzo duca di Savoia</i> was composed by Fr. Pietro-Francisco Malletta. Six years later, the Duke of Savoy issued nine-florin coins depicting Amadeus IX on one side. These appear to have been used as religious medals, particularly in the <a href="/wiki/Chablais" title="Chablais">Chablais</a>, where they were distributed by <a href="/wiki/Francis_de_Sales" title="Francis de Sales">Francis de Sales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Merle_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Merle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Michel Merle suggests that the revival of the cult of Amadeus IX was part of a decades long effort on the part of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Savoy" title="House of Savoy">House of Savoy</a> to enhance its political status. Presented as a holy prince known for his charity and concern for the poor, Amadeus IX was beatified on 3 March 1677 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Merle_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Merle-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
