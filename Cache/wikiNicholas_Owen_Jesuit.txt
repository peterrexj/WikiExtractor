<p><b>Nicholas Owen</b>, <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">S.J.</a>, (c. 1562 &#8211; 1/2 March 1606) was an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> who was the principal builder of <a href="/wiki/Priest_hole" title="Priest hole">priest holes</a> during the reigns of <a href="/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Elizabeth I">Queen Elizabeth I</a> and <a href="/wiki/James_VI_and_I" title="James VI and I">James I</a> of England.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Owen built many priest holes in the buildings of English Catholics from 1588 until his final arrest in 1606, when he was tortured to death by prison authorities in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>. Owen is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1970.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Owen_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nicholas Owen was born around 1562 in <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a>, England, into a devoutly <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> family and grew up during the <a href="/wiki/Penal_law_(Britain)" class="mw-redirect" title="Penal law (Britain)">Penal Laws</a>. His father, Walter Owen, was a <a href="/wiki/Carpenter" class="mw-redirect" title="Carpenter">carpenter</a> and Nicholas was apprenticed as a <a href="/wiki/Joiner" class="mw-redirect" title="Joiner">joiner</a> in February 1577, acquiring the skills that he would use to build hiding places. Two of his older brothers became <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priests</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Owen served as a servant of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a>, who was arrested by <a href="/wiki/Priest_hunter" title="Priest hunter">priest hunters</a> in 1581, and was himself arrested for protesting Campion's innocence.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Upon his release, he entered the service of <a href="/wiki/Henry_Garnet" title="Henry Garnet">Henry Garnet</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a>, around 1588. For the next 18 years, Owen built hiding places for Catholic priests in the homes of Catholic families. He frequently travelled from one house to another under the name of "Little John" and accepted only the necessities of life as payment before he started off for a new project. He also used the aliases "Little Michael", "Andrewes" and "Draper". During the daytime, he would work as a travelling carpenter to deflect suspicion.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Owen was of very short stature, and suffered from a <a href="/wiki/Hernia" title="Hernia">hernia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> as well as a crippled leg from a horse falling on him. Nevertheless, his work often involved breaking through thick stonework, and to minimise the likelihood of betrayal, he often worked at night and always alone. Sometimes, he built an easily discovered outer hiding place, which concealed an inner hiding place. The location of the secret room was known to only himself and the owner of the house. Examples of his work survive at <a href="/wiki/Sawston" title="Sawston">Sawston Hall</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cambridgeshire" title="Cambridgeshire">Cambridgeshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oxburgh_Hall" title="Oxburgh Hall">Oxburgh Hall</a> in <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Huddington_Court" title="Huddington Court">Huddington Court</a> in <a href="/wiki/Worcestershire" title="Worcestershire">Worcestershire</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Coughton_Court" title="Coughton Court">Coughton Court</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warwickshire" title="Warwickshire">Warwickshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Harvington_Hall" title="Harvington Hall">Harvington Hall</a> in Worcestershire has seven "priest holes".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Due to the ingenuity of his craftsmanship, some may still be undiscovered.
</p>

<p>For many years, Owen worked in the service of the Jesuit priest <a href="/wiki/Henry_Garnet" title="Henry Garnet">Henry Garnet</a> and was admitted into the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>. He was arrested in 1594 and tortured at the <a href="/wiki/Poultry_Compter" title="Poultry Compter">Poultry Compter</a> but revealed nothing. He was released after a wealthy Catholic family paid a fine on his behalf, the jailers believing that he was merely the insignificant friend of some priests. He resumed his work and is believed to have masterminded the famous escape of <a href="/wiki/John_Gerard_(Jesuit)" title="John Gerard (Jesuit)">John Gerard</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> in 1597.
</p><p>In early 1606, Owen was arrested a final time at <a href="/wiki/Hindlip_Hall" title="Hindlip Hall">Hindlip Hall</a> in Worcestershire,<sup id="cite_ref-lives_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> giving himself up voluntarily in the hope of distracting attention from his master Garnet, who was hiding nearby with another priest. Realising just whom they had caught, and his value, Secretary of State <a href="/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_1st_Earl_of_Salisbury" title="Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury">Robert Cecil</a> exulted: "It is incredible, how great was the joy caused by his arrest... knowing the great skill of Owen in constructing hiding places, and the innumerable quantity of dark holes which he had schemed for hiding priests all through England".<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Owen_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2><p>
After being committed to the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>, a prison on the southern bank of the <a href="/wiki/Thames" class="mw-redirect" title="Thames">Thames</a>, Owen was then removed to the Tower of London. He was submitted to torture on the <a href="/wiki/Richard_Topcliffe" title="Richard Topcliffe">Topcliffe</a> rack, dangling from a wall with both wrists held fast in iron <a href="/wiki/Gauntlet_(glove)" title="Gauntlet (glove)">gauntlets</a> and his body hanging. As his hernia allowed his intestines to bulge out during this procedure, the rack master strapped a circular plate of iron to his stomach. When he remained stubborn, it is believed that he was transferred to the rack, where the greater power of the <a href="/wiki/Windlass" title="Windlass">windlass</a> forced out his hernia, which was then slashed by the plate, resulting in his death. Owen revealed nothing to his inquisitors,<sup id="cite_ref-gerardsj_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gerardsj-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and died in the night between 1 and 2 March 1606. Gerard wrote of him: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I verily think no man can be said to have done more good of all those who laboured in the English vineyard. He was the immediate occasion of saving the lives of many hundreds of persons, both ecclesiastical and secular.<sup id="cite_ref-gerardsj_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gerardsj-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Owen_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Owen was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 25 October 1970. Their joint <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> was initially celebrated on the anniversary of the canonization. That feast has been moved in England to 4 May. His individual feast day is on 22 March.<sup id="cite_ref-ketter_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ketter-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Catholic stage magicians who practice <a href="/wiki/Gospel_Magic" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospel Magic">Gospel Magic</a> consider St. Nicholas Owen the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Illusionists" class="mw-redirect" title="Illusionists">illusionists</a> and <a href="/wiki/Escapology" title="Escapology">escapologists</a>, due to his facility at using <i><a href="/wiki/Trompe-l%27%C5%93il" title="Trompe-l&#39;œil">trompe-l'œil</a></i> when creating his hideouts.
</p><p>There are Roman Catholic churches dedicated to Saint Nicholas Owen in Little Thornton, Lancashire and Burton Latimer, Northamptonshire.
</p><p>The Roman Catholic school academy company that serves the Kidderminster, Hagley and Stourbridge areas near Harvington Hall is named in his honour.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Portrayals_in_fiction">Portrayals in fiction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Owen_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Portrayals in fiction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Owen is portrayed, as a minor character, in <a href="/wiki/Robert_Hugh_Benson" title="Robert Hugh Benson">Robert Hugh Benson</a>'s novel <i><a href="/wiki/Come_Rack!_Come_Rope!" title="Come Rack! Come Rope!">Come Rack! Come Rope!</a></i> (1912), where he is named "Hugh Owen".</li>
<li>One of his priest holes plays a key role in the <a href="/wiki/Catherine_Aird" title="Catherine Aird">Catherine Aird</a> mystery novel <i>A Most Contagious Game</i> (1967). A priest hole attributed to him is also part of <a href="/wiki/Peter_Carey_(novelist)" title="Peter Carey (novelist)">Peter Carey</a>'s novel <i><a href="/wiki/Parrot_and_Olivier_in_America" title="Parrot and Olivier in America">Parrot and Olivier in America</a></i> (2010).</li>
<li>Owen and his work play a key role in <i>The House of a Hundred Whispers</i> by Graham Masterton, published in 2020 by Head of Zeus Ltd.</li>
<li>Owen is also a minor character in Leonard Tourney's novel <i>Catesby's Ghost: A Mystery of Shakespeare</i> (2022).</li></ul>
