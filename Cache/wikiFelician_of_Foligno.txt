<p><b>Felician(us) of Foligno</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Feliciano di Foligno</i>) (c. 160 – c. 250) is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Foligno" title="Foligno">Foligno</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_of_Foligno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> tradition, he was born in <i>Forum Flaminii</i> (present-day <a href="/wiki/San_Giovanni_Profiamma" title="San Giovanni Profiamma">San Giovanni Profiamma</a>), on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Flaminia" title="Via Flaminia">Via Flaminia</a>, of a Christian family, around 160.  He was the spiritual student of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eleuterus" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Eleuterus">Pope Eleuterus</a> and evangelized in Foligno, <a href="/wiki/Spello" title="Spello">Spello</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bevagna" title="Bevagna">Bevagna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norcia" title="Norcia">Norcia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Plestia" title="Plestia">Plestia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trevi,_Umbria" title="Trevi, Umbria">Trevi</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was later <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Foligno" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Foligno">bishop of Foligno</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_I" title="Pope Victor I">Pope Victor I</a> around 204 (he was the first bishop to receive the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> as a symbol of his office).<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He ordained <a href="/wiki/Valentine_of_Terni" class="mw-redirect" title="Valentine of Terni">Valentine of Terni</a> as a priest.  His episcopate lasted for more than 50 years; he was one of the first Christian bishops of northern Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He was arrested at the age of 94 for refusing to sacrifice to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a> during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a>. He was tortured and scourged, and died outside Foligno while being conveyed to Rome for his execution.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Messalina">Saint Messalina</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_of_Foligno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Saint Messalina">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Foligno085.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Foligno085.jpg/220px-Foligno085.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="342" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Foligno085.jpg/330px-Foligno085.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Foligno085.jpg/440px-Foligno085.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1524" data-file-height="2368" /></a><figcaption>Saint Messalina</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Messalina was a <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">consecrated virgin</a> who had received the religious veil from Felician. She cared for him during his imprisonment, and for this she was also arrested and clubbed to death when she refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felician_of_Foligno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A church was built over his grave at Foligno. His <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a> on October 4, 970.  In 965 some relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/Minden" title="Minden">Minden</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>; Felician was thus erroneously considered a bishop of that German city (and he had a separate feast day of October 20), an error that entered the <i>Roman Martyrology</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  Some of his relics were later returned to Foligno in 1673–4.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Foligno_Cathedral" title="Foligno Cathedral">Foligno Cathedral</a> preserves a statue of the saint, of silver and bronze, made by the sculptor <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Maini" title="Giovanni Battista Maini">Giovanni Battista Maini</a>.
</p>
