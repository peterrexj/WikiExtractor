<p><b>Gregorio Giovanni Gaspare Barbarigo</b> (16 September 1625 &#8211; 18 June 1697) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">cardinal</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bergamo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bergamo">Bishop of Bergamo</a> and later as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Padua">Bishop of Padua</a>. He was a <a href="/wiki/Papabile" title="Papabile">frontrunner</a> in both the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1689" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1689">1689</a> and <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1691" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1691">1691</a> <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave" title="Papal conclave">papal conclaves</a> as he had distinguished himself for his diplomatic and scholastic service.<sup id="cite_ref-SN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He became noted as a scholar for his distinguished learning and as an able pastor for his careful attention to pastoral initiatives and frequent parish visitations.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-GG_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Barbarigo's beatification was celebrated in 1761 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a>, while <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> canonized the late cardinal in 1960; the latter pope held Barbarigo as a great role model and fostered a devotion to him since the pope had hailed from <a href="/wiki/Bergamo" title="Bergamo">Bergamo</a>. His liturgical feast is on 17 June in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>. On the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1969" title="General Roman Calendar of 1969">General Roman Calendar of 1969</a>, it is observed on 18 June.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Barbarigo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_education">Childhood and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Barbarigo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Gregorio Giovanni Gaspare Barbarigo was born on 16 September 1625 in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> as the eldest of four children to the nobles Giovanni Francesco Barbarigo (a senator) and Lucrezia Leoni (d. 19 March 1631 - plague). His father brought a cousin - Franchesina Lippomani - to look after the children after the death of his wife.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His sister was Elena and his two brothers were Pietro and Antonio. He was a relative of the cardinal <a href="/wiki/Marcantonio_Barbarigo" title="Marcantonio Barbarigo">Marcantonio Barbarigo</a> and the uncle of Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Francesco_Barbarigo" title="Giovanni Francesco Barbarigo">Giovanni Francesco Barbarigo</a>. He was also a relation of Cardinal Angelo Barbarigo. His ancestors included the two <a href="/wiki/Doge_of_Venice" title="Doge of Venice">Venetian doges</a> <a href="/wiki/Marco_Barbarigo" title="Marco Barbarigo">Marco Barbarigo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Agostino_Barbarigo" title="Agostino Barbarigo">Agostino Barbarigo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father instructed him in philosophical studies and in mathematics while preceptors taught him <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>; he also received the rudiments of music.
</p><p>In 1643 he accompanied as secretary the Venetian ambassador Aloise Contarini to <a href="/wiki/M%C3%BCnster" title="Münster">Münster</a> for the negotiations to prepare for the <a href="/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia" title="Peace of Westphalia">Peace of Westphalia</a> which was signed on 24 October 1648.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> There he became acquainted with Archbishop Fabio Chigi (the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VII" title="Pope Alexander VII">Pope Alexander VII</a>) - the nuncio to <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> and a participant in the negotiations.<sup id="cite_ref-GG_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He then went to <a href="/wiki/Holland" title="Holland">Holland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a> and then to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>. In July 1648 he returned to Venice and continued his studies in <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-strong_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strong-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1650 he was elected as a member of the Collegio dei Savi and initiated his political career which he did not find to be good for him. In the winter in 1653 he went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to ask the advice of Cardinal Chigi who recommended that he not retire as a hermit but follow the ecclesiastical career and begin obtaining a doctorate in law.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood_and_Bishop_of_Bergamo">Priesthood and Bishop of Bergamo</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Barbarigo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Priesthood and Bishop of Bergamo">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Barbarigo obtained a doctorate in "utroque iure" both <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> and civil law on 25 September 1655 and received his <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> on 21 December 1655 from the Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice" title="Patriarch of Venice">Patriarch of Venice</a> Gianfrancesco Morosini.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He left for Rome in late February 1656 for Chigi - now Pope Alexander VII - initiated him into the papal service. He was named a <a href="/wiki/Monsignor" title="Monsignor">domestic prelate of His Holiness</a><sup id="cite_ref-strong_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-strong-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and on 21 April 1656 was appointed as the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Signatura" title="Apostolic Signatura">Referendary of the Tribunals of the Apostolic Signature of Justice and of Grace</a>. On 9 June 1665 he was given a canonicate in the cathedral chapter of Padua without the requirement of residence and in 1656 - at the request of the pope - he organized the assistance to the Romans in the Trastevere area who had been stricken with the plague. He oversaw the care of the mothers and their children and the funerals of the deceased in this work. He nursed the sick, buried to dead, and comforted those frightened and in mourning.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 9 July 1657 the pope appointed him as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bergamo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bergamo">Bishop of Bergamo</a> and he received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> as such on 29 July 1657 from <a href="/wiki/Marcantonio_Bragadin" class="mw-redirect" title="Marcantonio Bragadin">Marcantonio Bragadin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> But before he accepted the post he decided to celebrate a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> to discern the will of <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> when he felt the concrete call to take on the see during the Mass. Barbarigo took possession of his new episcopal see on 2 September through his procurators Rodolfo Roncalli the archdeacon and the vice-capitular Giovanni Battista Lavezzali while he himself arrived there on 27 March 1658. He inspected each of the 279 parishes of the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-GG_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinalate">Cardinalate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Barbarigo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cardinalate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was a successful bishop and his fame spread through the ranks so much to the point that his old friend Alexander VII elevated him to the cardinalate on 5 April 1660 at the <a href="/wiki/Quirinale_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Quirinale Palace">Quirinale Palace</a>. He was made the <a href="/wiki/San_Tommaso_in_Parione" title="San Tommaso in Parione">Cardinal-Priest of San Tommaso in Parione</a> on 21 June 1660 but later opted to become the <a href="/wiki/San_Marco,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="San Marco, Rome">Cardinal-Priest of San Marco</a> on 13 September 1677.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1664 he was made <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Padua">Bishop of Padua</a> and upon entrance into his new diocese he strove to model himself upon the example of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a>. His procurator the Archpriest Galeazzo Mussato took possession of the see on Barbarigo's behalf on 24 April before the cardinal entered the see on 22 June. He was a strong supporter of the work of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>. He made the seminaries of Padua and of Bergamo larger and added an archive and printing press in Padua. He celebrated a <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Bishops_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Bishops (Catholic)">diocesan synod</a> from 1–3 September 1683 and wrote the "Regulae Studiorum" in 1690 for ecclesial studies.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He also visited all 320 parishes in his diocese.
</p><p>The cardinal soon learned in 1678 that <a href="/wiki/Elena_Cornaro_Piscopia" title="Elena Cornaro Piscopia">Elena Cornaro Piscopia</a> was pursuing a theological course and he refused this on the basis that she was a woman. But he allowed for her to obtain a philosophical degree which she did.
</p><p>In his role as a cardinal he participated in his first <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave" title="Papal conclave">papal conclave</a> in 1667 and also attended those in 1676 and that of 1689 when he emerged as a potential contender though falling short of the votes needed to become pope. The same was said for 1691 which saw him emerge as a greater threat to other contenders. He did not attend the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1669-1670" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1669-1670">1669-70 conclave</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The election of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a> in 1676 saw the pope ask Barbarigo to remain in Rome until 1679 as his counselor and entrusted Rome's education to him and the reunification of the Eastern Churches. One of his episcopal acts was to consecrate as a bishop <a href="/wiki/Niels_Stensen" class="mw-redirect" title="Niels Stensen">Niels Stensen</a> on 19 September 1677 and he also ordained the <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">convert</a> Thomas Nicholson a priest in Padua.<sup id="cite_ref-GG_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 1689 conclave his candidature received little support from his compatriots with Cardinal Flavio Chigi not supporting his candidature. Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Maria_de%27_Medici" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco Maria de&#39; Medici">Francesco Maria de' Medici</a> had put his name forth as per an elaborate ruse with the intent of never having Barbarigo elected.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Medici's aim was to indeed have a Venetian elected but decided to settle on Pietro Vito Ottoboni who was elected as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a>. But the 1691 conclave shifted Barbarigo's status as a potential contender for he gained greater ground and almost succeeded in becoming pope. Cardinal <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_d%27Estr%C3%A9es" title="César d&#39;Estrées">d'Estrées</a> included him in his list of potential candidates in light of the impending death of Alexander VIII while Cardinal Leandro Colloredo decided to throw his support behind Barbarigo.<sup id="cite_ref-SN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Even Chigi - who had blocked his candidature back in 1689 - thought well to advocate Barbarigo's name. But the French were not all that enthralled with Barbarigo since he was considered a leading 'creature' of Alexander VII who had not been too lenient with the French. Cardinals <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Ottoboni_(cardinal)" title="Pietro Ottoboni (cardinal)">Pietro Ottoboni</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paluzzo_Paluzzi_Altieri_degli_Albertoni" title="Paluzzo Paluzzi Altieri degli Albertoni">Paluzzo Paluzzi Altieri</a> were opposed to his candidature and in the end was elected Antonio Pignatelli as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cardinal Barbarigo fostered catechetical instruction and he travelled to each village in his diocese in order to teach and to preach to the people. His compassion to the poor was well known for he gave his household goods and his clothes to the poor for their comfort. He even sold his bed on one occasion to help them.
</p><p>Barbarigo died after a brief illness on 18 June 1697 in Padua where he was interred in the <a href="/wiki/Padua_Cathedral" title="Padua Cathedral">diocesan cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-GG_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregorio_Barbarigo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Bishop Barbarigo was distinguished by his piety and heroic patience and miracles quickly bore witness to his sanctity.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  
His holiness was observed in his life enough to the point that the faithful started to press the competent ecclesial authorities to launch the process for sainthood with the informative phase of the investigation ending on 11 July 1716; the formal introduction to the cause came under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XIII" title="Pope Innocent XIII">Pope Innocent XIII</a> on 3 July 1723.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His remains were exhumed on 25 May 1725 and allegedly found to be incorrupt with his nephew Bishop <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Francesco_Barbarigo" title="Giovanni Francesco Barbarigo">Giovanni Francesco Barbarigo</a> leading the exhumation initiative. However, the report of the examining physicians was actually equivocal. While medical professionalism prevented them form saying the body was incorrupt, popular opinion and ecclesiastical expectations were otherwise. The report stated that the condition of the body was "wondrous" which fell short of miraculous, a distinction largely lost upon those interested.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a> beatified Barbarigo in 1761 while <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> canonized Barbarigo in mid-1960. The beatification seemed all the more fitting for Clement XIII since his mother - Vittoria Barbarigo - was a distant relative of the late cardinal.
</p><p>That latter pope - while he was a simple priest - signed a petition in 1911 addressed to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> requesting Barbarigo's cause go forward for the late cardinal to become a saint; Pius X did so on 28 February 1912 when he issued the decree to resume the cause.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John XXIII is said to have felt a close kinship with the late cardinal and was also said to have manifested a lifelong devotion to him and his work as a model and so included him in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a> while assigning 17 June as the liturgical feast since 18 June was assigned to another. But the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> to the calendar saw the removal of his name which left his feast to a celebration in local calendars. His liturgical feast remains affixed at present to 18 June.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The Church of <a href="/wiki/San_Gregorio_Barbarigo_alle_Tre_Fontane" title="San Gregorio Barbarigo alle Tre Fontane">San Gregorio Barbarigo alle Tre Fontane</a> in Rome is dedicated to him.
</p>
