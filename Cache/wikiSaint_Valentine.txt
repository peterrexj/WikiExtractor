<p><b>Saint Valentine</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Valentino</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Valentinus</i>) was a 3rd-century Roman <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, commemorated in Western <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> on February 14 and in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Eastern Orthodoxy</a> on July 6. From the <a href="/wiki/High_Middle_Ages" title="High Middle Ages">High Middle Ages</a>, <a href="/wiki/Valentine%27s_Day" title="Valentine&#39;s Day">his Saints' Day</a> has been associated with a tradition of <a href="/wiki/Courtly_love" title="Courtly love">courtly love</a>. He is also a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Terni</a>, <a href="/wiki/Epilepsy" title="Epilepsy">epilepsy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beekeeping" title="Beekeeping">beekeepers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
Saint Valentine was a clergyman&#160;&#8211;&#32;either a priest or a <a href="/wiki/Bishops_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Bishops in the Catholic Church">bishop</a>&#160;&#8211;&#32;in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a> who ministered to <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" title="Persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire">persecuted Christians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cooper2013_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cooper2013-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> and his body buried on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Flaminia" title="Via Flaminia">Via Flaminia</a> on February 14, which has been observed as the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saint_Valentine" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of Saint Valentine">Feast of Saint Valentine</a> (Saint Valentine's Day) since at least the eighth century.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Relics of him were kept in the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_San_Valentino" title="Catacombs of San Valentino">Church and Catacombs of San Valentino</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, which "remained an important pilgrim site throughout the Middle Ages until the relics of St. Valentine were transferred to the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a> during the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV" title="Pope Nicholas IV">Nicholas IV</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-Webb2001_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webb2001-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His skull, crowned with flowers, is exhibited in the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin" title="Santa Maria in Cosmedin">Santa Maria in Cosmedin</a>, Rome. Other relics of him are in <a href="/wiki/Whitefriar_Street_Carmelite_Church" title="Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church">Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, Ireland, a popular place of pilgrimage, especially on Saint Valentine's Day, for those seeking love.<sup id="cite_ref-Hecker2010_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hecker2010-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> At least two different Saint Valentines are mentioned in the early <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> For <b>Saint Valentine of Rome</b>, along with <b>Saint Valentine of Terni</b>, "abstracts of the acts of the two saints were in nearly every church and monastery of Europe", according to Professor Jack B. Oruch of the University of Kansas.<sup id="cite_ref-Chapman2013_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chapman2013-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Valentine is commemorated in the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a><sup id="cite_ref-Anglican_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anglican-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Lutheranism" title="Lutheranism">Lutheran Churches</a> on February 14.<sup id="cite_ref-Pfatteicher2008_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pfatteicher2008-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, he is recognised on <a href="/wiki/July_6_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="July 6 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">July 6</a>; in addition, the Eastern Orthodox Church observes the feast of <a href="/wiki/Hieromartyr" title="Hieromartyr">Hieromartyr</a> Valentine, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Interamna" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Interamna">Bishop of Interamna</a>, on <a href="/wiki/July_30_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="July 30 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">July 30</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Valentine_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Valentine-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-stmarina.org_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stmarina.org-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In 1969, the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> removed his name from the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, leaving his liturgical celebration to local calendars, though use of the pre-1970 liturgical calendar is also authorised under the conditions indicated in the motu proprio <i><a href="/wiki/Summorum_Pontificum" title="Summorum Pontificum">Summorum Pontificum</a></i> of 2007.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic Church continues to recognise him as a saint, listing him as such in the February 14 entry in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and authorising liturgical veneration of him on February 14 in any place where that day is not devoted to some other obligatory celebration, in accordance with the rule that on such a day the Mass may be that of any saint listed in the Martyrology for that day.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Identification">Identification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Valentine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Identification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Valentine does not occur in the earliest list of Roman <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronography_of_354" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronography of 354">Chronography of 354</a></i>, although the patron of the Chronography's compilation was a wealthy Roman Christian named Valentinus.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> However, it is found in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> which was compiled between 460 and 544 from earlier local sources. The feast of St. Valentine of February 14 was first established in 496 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Pope Gelasius I</a>, who included Valentine among all those "... whose names are justly reverenced among men, but whose acts are known only to God."
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and other <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographical</a> sources<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> speak of three Saints Valentine that appear in connection with February 14. One was a Roman priest, another the bishop of Interamna (modern <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Terni</a>, Italy) both buried along the <a href="/wiki/Via_Flaminia" title="Via Flaminia">Via Flaminia</a> outside Rome, at different distances from the city. The third was said to be a saint who suffered on the same day with a number of companions in the <a href="/wiki/Africa_(Roman_province)" title="Africa (Roman province)">Roman province of Africa</a>, of whom nothing else is known.
</p><p>Though the extant accounts of the martyrdoms of the first two listed saints are of a late date and contain legendary elements, "a common nucleus of fact" may underlie the two accounts and they may refer to "a single person".<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> According to the official biography of the Diocese of Terni, Bishop Valentine was born and lived in Interamna and while on a temporary stay in Rome he was imprisoned, tortured, and martyred there on February 14, 269. His body was hastily buried at a nearby cemetery and a few nights later his disciples retrieved his body and returned him home.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>, the Catholic Church's official list of recognised saints, for February 14 gives only one Saint Valentine: a martyr who died on the Via Flaminia.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The name "Valentine" derived from <i>valens</i> (worthy, strong, powerful), was popular in <a href="/wiki/Late_Antiquity" class="mw-redirect" title="Late Antiquity">Late Antiquity</a>. About eleven other saints with the name Valentine are commemorated in the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Some Eastern Churches of the Western rite may provide still other different lists of Saint Valentines.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The Roman martyrology lists only seven who  died on days <i>other</i> than February 14: a priest from <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a> (November 3); <a href="/wiki/Valentine_of_Passau" title="Valentine of Passau">Valentine of Passau</a>, papal missionary bishop to <a href="/wiki/Raetia" title="Raetia">Raetia</a>, among first patrons of <a href="/wiki/Passau" title="Passau">Passau</a>, and later hermit in Zenoburg, near Mais, <a href="/wiki/South_Tyrol" title="South Tyrol">South Tyrol</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, where he died in 475 (January 7); a 5th-century priest and hermit (July 4); <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fructus" title="Saint Fructus">a Spanish hermit who died in about 715</a> (October 25); Valentine Berrio Ochoa, martyred in 1861 (November 24); and Valentine Jaunzarás Gómez, martyred in 1936 (September 18). It also lists a virgin, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Valentina" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Valentina">Saint Valentina</a>, who was martyred in 308 (July 25) in Caesarea, Palestine.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography_and_testimony">Hagiography and testimony</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Valentine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography and testimony">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Valentineanddisciples.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Valentineanddisciples.jpg/220px-Valentineanddisciples.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="202" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Valentineanddisciples.jpg/330px-Valentineanddisciples.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Valentineanddisciples.jpg/440px-Valentineanddisciples.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="459" /></a><figcaption>Saint Valentine of Terni oversees the construction of his <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> at <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Terni</a>, from a 14th-century French manuscript (<a href="/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_National" class="mw-redirect" title="Bibliothèque National">BN</a>, Mss fr. 185)</figcaption></figure>
<p>The inconsistency in the identification of the saint is replicated in the various <a href="/wiki/Curriculum_vitae" title="Curriculum vitae">vitae</a> that are ascribed to him.
</p><p>A common hagiography describes Saint Valentine as a priest of Rome or as the former Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Terni</a>, an important town of <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, in central <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. While under house arrest of Judge Asterius, and discussing his faith with him, Valentinus (the Latin version of his name) was discussing the validity of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>. The judge put Valentinus to the test and brought to him the judge's adopted blind daughter. If Valentinus succeeded in restoring the girl's sight, Asterius would do whatever he asked. Valentinus, praying to God, <a href="/wiki/Christian_laying_on_of_hands" title="Christian laying on of hands">laid his hands</a> on her eyes and the child's vision was restored.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Immediately humbled, the judge asked Valentinus what he should do. Valentinus replied that all of the <a href="/wiki/Idolatry" title="Idolatry">idols</a> around the judge's house should be broken, and that the judge should fast for three days and then undergo the Christian sacrament of <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>. The judge obeyed and, as a result of his fasting and prayer, freed all the <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> inmates under his authority. The judge, his family, and his forty-four member <a href="/wiki/Household" title="Household">household</a> of adult family members and servants were baptised.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Valentinus was later arrested again for continuing to <a href="/wiki/Evangelism" title="Evangelism">evangelise</a>. He was sent to the prefect of Rome, to the emperor <a href="/wiki/Claudius_Gothicus" title="Claudius Gothicus">Claudius Gothicus</a> (Claudius II) himself. Claudius took a liking to him until Valentinus tried to convince Claudius to <a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity" title="Conversion to Christianity">embrace Christianity</a>. Claudius refused and condemned Valentinus to death, commanding that Valentinus either renounce his faith or he would be beaten with clubs and beheaded. Valentinus refused and was executed outside the <a href="/wiki/Piazza_del_Popolo" title="Piazza del Popolo">Flaminian Gate</a> on February 14, 269.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg/276px-Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg" decoding="async" width="276" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg/414px-Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg/552px-Jean-L%C3%A9on_G%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_-_The_Christian_Martyrs%27_Last_Prayer_-_Walters_37113.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1800" data-file-height="1050" /></a><figcaption>Saint Valentine is said to have ministered to the faithful amidst the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" title="Persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire">persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cooper2013_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cooper2013-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>An embellishment to this account states that before his execution, Saint Valentine wrote a note to Asterius's daughter signed "from your Valentine", which is said to have "inspired today's romantic missives".<sup id="cite_ref-Kithcart2013_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kithcart2013-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Legenda Aurea</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a>, compiled in about 1260 and one of the most-read books of the <a href="/wiki/High_Middle_Ages" title="High Middle Ages">High Middle Ages</a>, gives sufficient details of the saints for each day of the liturgical year to inspire a homily on each occasion. The very brief <i>vita</i> of St Valentine states that he was executed for refusing to deny Christ by the order of the "Emperor Claudius"<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> in the year 269. Before his head was cut off, this Valentine restored sight and hearing to the daughter of his jailer. Jacobus makes a play with the etymology of "Valentine", "as containing valor".
</p><p>A popularly ascribed hagiographical identity appears in the <a href="/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle" title="Nuremberg Chronicle">Nuremberg Chronicle</a> (1493). Alongside a woodcut portrait of Valentine, the text states that he was a Roman priest of exceptional learning who converted the daughter of Asterius and forty-nine others to Christianity before being martyred during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Claudius_Gothicus" title="Claudius Gothicus">Claudius Gothicus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are many other legends behind Saint Valentine. One is that in the 3rd century AD<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> it is said that Valentine, who was a priest, defied the order of the emperor Claudius and secretly performed <a href="/wiki/Christian_views_on_marriage" title="Christian views on marriage">Christian weddings</a> for couples, allowing the husbands involved to escape conscription into the pagan army. This legend claims that soldiers were sparse at this time so this was a big inconvenience to the emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-Christensen1997_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christensen1997-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> The account mentions that in order "to remind these men of their vows and God’s love, Saint Valentine is said to have cut hearts from parchment", giving them to these <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" title="Persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire">persecuted Christians</a>, a possible origin of the widespread use of hearts on St. Valentine's Day.<sup id="cite_ref-Frank_Staff_1969_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Frank_Staff_1969-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Churches_named_after_Saint_Valentine">Churches named after Saint Valentine</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Valentine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Churches named after Saint Valentine">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St-Valentine-Kneeling-In-Supplication.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/St-Valentine-Kneeling-In-Supplication.jpg/224px-St-Valentine-Kneeling-In-Supplication.jpg" decoding="async" width="224" height="283" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/St-Valentine-Kneeling-In-Supplication.jpg/336px-St-Valentine-Kneeling-In-Supplication.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d6/St-Valentine-Kneeling-In-Supplication.jpg 2x" data-file-width="370" data-file-height="468" /></a><figcaption><i>St Valentine Kneeling in Supplication</i> (<a href="/wiki/David_Teniers_III" title="David Teniers III">David Teniers III</a>, 1600s) – Valentine kneels to receive a <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Virgin Mary</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>There are many churches <a href="/wiki/Dedication_(ritual)" title="Dedication (ritual)">dedicated</a> to Saint Valentine in countries such as Italy. Saint Valentine was venerated no more than other <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyrs</a> and saints.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A 5th- or 6th-century work called <i>Passio Marii et Marthae</i> made up a legend about Saint Valentine's Basilica being dedicated to Saint Valentine in Rome. A later <i>Passio</i> repeated the legend and added the adornment that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_I" title="Pope Julius I">Pope Julius I</a> (357–352) had built the ancient basilica <i>S. Valentini extra Portam</i> on top of his sepulchre, in the Via Flaminia.<sup id="cite_ref-ansgar49_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ansgar49-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> This church was really named after a 4th-century tribune called Valentino, who donated the land on which it is built.<sup id="cite_ref-ansgar49_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ansgar49-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> It hosted the martyr's relics until the 13th century, when they were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a>, and the ancient basilica decayed.<sup id="cite_ref-hulsen_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hulsen-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Valentine's Church in Rome, built in 1960 for the needs of the <a href="/wiki/Olympic_Village" title="Olympic Village">Olympic Village</a>, continues as a modern, well-visited parish church.
</p>
<h2><span id="Saint_Valentine.27s_Day"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Valentine's_Day">Saint Valentine's Day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Valentine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Saint Valentine&#039;s Day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Saint_Valentine%27s_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Valentine&#39;s Day">Saint Valentine's Day</a></div>
<p>Saint Valentine of Rome was martyred on February 14 in AD 269.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler1981_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler1981-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> The Feast of Saint Valentine, also known as Saint Valentine's Day, was established by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Pope Gelasius I</a> in AD 496 to be celebrated on February 14 in honour of the <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ChanchreekJain2007_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ChanchreekJain2007-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St-valentine_110921-01.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/St-valentine_110921-01.jpg/170px-St-valentine_110921-01.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/St-valentine_110921-01.jpg/255px-St-valentine_110921-01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/St-valentine_110921-01.jpg/340px-St-valentine_110921-01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="764" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>A shrine of Saint Valentine in <a href="/wiki/Whitefriar_Street_Carmelite_Church" title="Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church">Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, Ireland</figcaption></figure>
<p>February 14 is Saint Valentine's Day in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Lutheran)" title="Calendar of saints (Lutheran)">Lutheran calendar of saints</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pfatteicher2008_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pfatteicher2008-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Valentine <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">is remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on February 14.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> The Church of England had him in its pre-Reformation calendars, and restored his mention as bishop and martyr in its 1661–62 <i><a href="/wiki/Book_of_Common_Prayer" title="Book of Common Prayer">Book of Common Prayer</a></i>, and most provinces of the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> celebrate his feast.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic Church includes him in its official list of saints, the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>. 
</p><p>Saint Valentine was also in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration as a <a href="/wiki/Ranking_of_liturgical_days_in_the_Roman_Rite" title="Ranking of liturgical days in the Roman Rite">simple feast</a> until 1955, when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> reduced all such feasts to just a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">commemoration</a> within another celebration. The 1969 revision of the General Roman Calendar removed this mention, leaving it for inclusion only in local calendars such as that of <a href="/wiki/Balzan" title="Balzan">Balzan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a>. His commemoration was still in the 1962 <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a> and is thus observed also by those who, in the circumstances indicated in <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>'s 2007 <a href="/wiki/Motu_proprio" title="Motu proprio">motu proprio</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Summorum_Pontificum" title="Summorum Pontificum">Summorum Pontificum</a></i>, use that edition.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/July_6_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="July 6 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">July 6</a> is the date on which the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> celebrates the Roman presbyter Valentine; on <a href="/wiki/July_30_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="July 30 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">July 30</a> it observes the feast of the Hieromartyr Valentine, Bishop of Interamna.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Valentine_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Valentine-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-stmarina.org_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stmarina.org-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Members of the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox Church</a> named Valentinos (male) or Valentina (female) may observe their name day on the Western ecclesiastical calendar date of February 14.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>English 18th-century antiquarians <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> and <a href="/wiki/Francis_Douce" title="Francis Douce">Francis Douce</a>, noting the obscurity of Saint Valentine's identity, suggested that Saint Valentine's Day was created as an attempt to supersede the pagan holiday of <a href="/wiki/Lupercalia" title="Lupercalia">Lupercalia</a> (mid-February in Rome). This idea has lately been dismissed by academics and researchers, such as Jack B. Oruch of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Kansas" title="University of Kansas">University of Kansas</a>, Henry Ansgar Kelly of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_California,_Los_Angeles" title="University of California, Los Angeles">University of California, Los Angeles</a><sup id="cite_ref-Kelly1986_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly1986-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> and Michael Matthew Kaylor of <a href="/wiki/Masaryk_University" title="Masaryk University">Masaryk University</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Many of the current legends that characterize Saint Valentine were invented in the 14th century in England, notably by <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="Geoffrey Chaucer">Geoffrey Chaucer</a> and his circle, when the feast day of February 14 first became associated with <a href="/wiki/Romantic_love" class="mw-redirect" title="Romantic love">romantic love</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oruch charges that the traditions associated with "Valentine's Day", documented in <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="Geoffrey Chaucer">Geoffrey Chaucer</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Parlement_of_Foules" title="Parlement of Foules">Parlement of Foules</a></i> and set in the fictional context of an old tradition, did not exist before Chaucer.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> He argues that the speculative explanation of sentimental customs, posing as historical fact, had their origins among 18th-century <a href="/wiki/Antiquary" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiquary">antiquaries</a>, notably <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, the author of <i>Butler's Lives of Saints</i>, and have been perpetuated even by respectable modern scholars. In the French 14th-century manuscript illumination from a <i>Vies des Saints</i> (<i>illustration above</i>), Saint Valentine, bishop of Terni, oversees the construction of his basilica at <a href="/wiki/Terni" title="Terni">Terni</a>; there is no suggestion here that the bishop was a patron of lovers.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the Middle Ages, it was believed that birds paired in mid-February. This was then associated with the romance of Valentine. Although these legends differ, Valentine's Day is widely recognised as a day for <a href="/wiki/Romance_(love)" title="Romance (love)">romance</a> and devotion.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Associated_Christian_relics">Associated Christian relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Valentine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Associated Christian relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Rom,_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin,_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Rom%2C_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin%2C_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg/220px-Rom%2C_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin%2C_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Rom%2C_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin%2C_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg/330px-Rom%2C_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin%2C_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Rom%2C_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin%2C_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg/440px-Rom%2C_Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin%2C_Reliquien_des_Hl._Valentin_von_Terni.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2672" data-file-height="2280" /></a><figcaption>A <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of Saint Valentine in the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin" title="Santa Maria in Cosmedin">Santa Maria in Cosmedin</a>, Rome</figcaption></figure>
<p>The flower-crowned alleged skull of St. Valentine is exhibited in the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin" title="Santa Maria in Cosmedin">Santa Maria in Cosmedin</a>, Rome.
</p><p>St. Valentine's remains are deposited in St Anton's Church, <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, where they have lain since the late 1700s. They were a present from the Pope to King <a href="/wiki/Carlos_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Carlos IV">Carlos IV</a>, who entrusted them to the Order of Poor Clerics Regular of the Mother of God of the Pious Schools (<a href="/wiki/Piarists" title="Piarists">Piarists</a>). The relics have been displayed publicly since 1984, in a foundation open to the public at all times in order to help people in need.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Whitefriar_Street_Carmelite_Church" title="Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church">Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, also houses some relics of St Valentine. On 27 December 1835 the Very Reverend Father John Spratt, Master of Sacred Theology to the Carmelite order in Dublin, was sent the partial remains of St Valentine by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Odescalchi" title="Carlo Odescalchi">Carlo Odescalchi</a>, under the auspices of Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory XVI">Gregory XVI</a>. The relics and the accompanying letter from Cardinal Odescalchi have remained in the church ever since.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> The remains, which include "a small vessel tinged with his blood", were sent as a token of esteem following an eloquent sermon Fr Spratt had delivered in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>On Saint Valentine's Day in Ireland, many individuals who seek true love make a <a href="/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage" title="Christian pilgrimage">Christian pilgrimage</a> to the Shrine of St. Valentine in Whitefriar Street Carmelite Church in Dublin, which is said to house relics of Saint Valentine of Rome; they <a href="/wiki/Christian_prayer" title="Christian prayer">pray</a> at the shrine in hope of finding romance.<sup id="cite_ref-IrishCentral2017_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IrishCentral2017-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> There lies a book in which foreigners and locals have written their prayer requests for love.<sup id="cite_ref-Hecker2010_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hecker2010-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another relic was found in 2003 in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Peter_and_St._Paul,_Prague" title="Basilica of St. Peter and St. Paul, Prague">Basilica of St. Peter and St. Paul</a> at <a href="/wiki/Vy%C5%A1ehrad" title="Vyšehrad">Vyšehrad</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Valentine's relics can also be found in Slovakia in two cities. The first is Šaštín, where the relic is placed in the Immaculate Conception (placed in 1720).<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> The second is Nováky, which they had in the church of St. Nicholas and the rare statue of Saint Valentine, which was stolen in the 90s (according to one saved original part of the statue - the head, a new copy was created, which was ceremoniously placed in the church in 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A silver reliquary containing a fragment of St. Valentine's skull is found in the parish church of St. Mary's Assumption in <a href="/wiki/Che%C5%82mno" title="Chełmno">Chełmno</a>, Poland.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Relics can also be found in <a href="/wiki/Mytilene" title="Mytilene">Mytilene</a> on the Greek island of <a href="/wiki/Lesbos" title="Lesbos">Lesbos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another set of relics can also be found in <a href="/wiki/Savona" title="Savona">Savona</a>, in the Cathedral of Santa Maria Assunta.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alleged relics of St. Valentine also lie at the reliquary of <a href="/wiki/Roquemaure,_Gard" title="Roquemaure, Gard">Roquemaure, Gard</a>, France, in the <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen%27s_Cathedral,_Vienna" title="St. Stephen&#39;s Cathedral, Vienna">St. Stephen's Cathedral, Vienna</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Balzan" title="Balzan">Balzan</a> in <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a> and also in <a href="/wiki/Blessed_John_Duns_Scotus_Church,_Glasgow" title="Blessed John Duns Scotus Church, Glasgow">Blessed John Duns Scotus Church</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Gorbals" title="Gorbals">Gorbals</a> area of <a href="/wiki/Glasgow" title="Glasgow">Glasgow</a>, Scotland. There is also a gold reliquary bearing the words "Corpus St. Valentin, M" (Body of St. Valentine, Martyr) at <a href="/wiki/Birmingham_Oratory" title="Birmingham Oratory">Birmingham Oratory</a>, UK, in one of the side altars in the main church.
</p>
