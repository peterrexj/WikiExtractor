<p><b>Philip Howard, 13th Earl of Arundel</b> (28 June 1557&#160;&#8211;&#32;19 October 1595) was an English <a href="/wiki/Nobleman" class="mw-redirect" title="Nobleman">nobleman</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1970, as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>. Howard lived mainly during the reign of Queen <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>; he was charged with being a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a>, quitting England without leave, and sharing in <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> plots. For this, he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> in 1585. Howard spent ten years in the Tower, until his death from <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a>.
</p>

<p>Philip was born at <a href="/wiki/Arundel_House" title="Arundel House">Arundel House</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Strand,_London" title="Strand, London">Strand</a>, <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> in 1557, during the upheaval of the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>. He was the only son of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_4th_Duke_of_Norfolk" title="Thomas Howard, 4th Duke of Norfolk">Thomas Howard, 4th Duke of Norfolk</a> by his first wife <a href="/wiki/Mary_FitzAlan" title="Mary FitzAlan">Mary FitzAlan</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_FitzAlan,_12th_Earl_of_Arundel" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry FitzAlan, 12th Earl of Arundel">Henry FitzAlan, 12th Earl of Arundel</a> and his first wife, Lady Katherine Grey. He was baptised by the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor" title="Lord Chancellor">Lord Chancellor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a> <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Heath" title="Nicholas Heath">Nicholas Heath</a> at <a href="/wiki/Whitehall_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Whitehall Palace">Whitehall Palace</a> with the royal family in attendance, and was named after his co-godfather, King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a>, husband of the ruling monarch, <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Mary I</a>. The baby's other godfather was his grandfather, the 12th Earl of Arundel. His godmother was his great-grandmother, <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Howard,_Duchess_of_Norfolk" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth Howard, Duchess of Norfolk">Elizabeth Stafford</a>, widow of the 3rd Duke, who held the child over a gold baptismal font which was kept in the Treasury and normally used only for the baptism of royal children.<sup id="cite_ref-pollen_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after his birth, his mother became seriously ill, possibly from <a href="/wiki/Postpartum_infections" title="Postpartum infections">puerperal infection</a> and died at Arundel House in August of that year. His home from the age of seven was a former <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-cormac_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cormac-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Being the eldest son and heir to the Duke of Norfolk, Philip was destined to become the future 5th Duke. From the moment of his birth, he bore the <a href="/wiki/Courtesy_title" title="Courtesy title">courtesy title</a> of <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Surrey" title="Earl of Surrey">Earl of Surrey</a>, a subsidiary title of the dukes of Norfolk.
</p><p>In 1569 his father arranged the marriage of Philip to his step-sister <a href="/wiki/Anne_Howard,_Countess_of_Arundel" title="Anne Howard, Countess of Arundel">Anne Dacre</a>, daughter of Norfolk's third wife, <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Leyburne" title="Elizabeth Leyburne">Elizabeth Leyburne</a>, by the latter's previous marriage. Since both children were only 12 years old at the time, the ceremony was repeated two years later, after both parties had attained the age of consent. Philip's half-brothers <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_1st_Earl_of_Suffolk" title="Thomas Howard, 1st Earl of Suffolk">Thomas</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lord_William_Howard" title="Lord William Howard">William</a>, sons of their father by his second wife <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Howard,_Duchess_of_Norfolk" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret Howard, Duchess of Norfolk">Margaret Audley</a>, subsequently married Anne's sisters Mary and Elizabeth respectively.
</p><p>Philip's father, a Roman Catholic with a Protestant education, was arrested in 1569 for being involved in intrigues against <a href="/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Elizabeth I">Queen Elizabeth I</a>, mainly because of the Duke's intention to marry <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots" title="Mary, Queen of Scots">Mary, Queen of Scots</a>. Although he was released in August 1570, a few months later he became involved in the <a href="/wiki/Ridolfi_Plot" class="mw-redirect" title="Ridolfi Plot">Ridolfi Plot</a>, being arrested again in September 1571, when his participation in the plot was discovered. He was executed in June 1572, when Philip was nearly fifteen years old. After Norfolk's death, Philip and his surviving half-siblings Thomas, <a href="/wiki/Lady_Margaret_Sackville_(1562-1591)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lady Margaret Sackville (1562-1591)">Margaret</a> and William were left in the care of their uncle, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Howard,_Earl_of_Northampton" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry Howard, Earl of Northampton">Henry Howard</a>, who also took charge of their education. During that time, the Howard children lived with their uncle at <a href="/wiki/Audley_End" class="mw-redirect" title="Audley End">Audley End</a>, one of his family's properties. Due to his father's execution, Philip lost much of his paternal family's estates and the title of Earl of Surrey, as the dukedom of Norfolk was <a href="/wiki/Forfeiture_(law)" title="Forfeiture (law)">forfeit</a>. Although Howard did not manage to recover the dukedom, a few years later he and his younger half-siblings were able to recover part of the forfeited properties.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was during this time that Philip was sent to study at <a href="/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Cambridge" title="St John&#39;s College, Cambridge">St John's College, Cambridge</a>. While Howard was studying there, his wife came under the protection of <a href="/wiki/Henry_FitzAlan,_12th_Earl_of_Arundel" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry FitzAlan, 12th Earl of Arundel">Henry FitzAlan, 12th Earl of Arundel</a>, Philip's maternal grandfather.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Philip graduated in 1574, aged 17. He began attending Elizabeth I's court by the time he turned eighteen; notably, this was only a few years after his father had been executed for treason against the Queen.<sup id="cite_ref-cathedral_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathedral-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His life had been frivolous both at Cambridge and remained so at court, where he nevertheless became a favourite of the Queen, despite Philip's troubled family past.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg/220px-George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="274" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg/330px-George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg/440px-George_Gower_Philip_Howard_Earl_of_Arundel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="481" data-file-height="600" /></a><figcaption>Philip Howard at 18 years of age by <a href="/wiki/George_Gower" title="George Gower">George Gower</a>, c. 1575.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In July 1578, his maternal aunt <a href="/wiki/Jane_Lumley,_Baroness_Lumley" title="Jane Lumley, Baroness Lumley">Jane FitzAlan</a> died without living descendants, as the only three children she had from her marriage to <a href="/wiki/John_Lumley,_1st_Baron_Lumley" title="John Lumley, 1st Baron Lumley">John Lumley, 1st Baron Lumley</a> all died during infancy. With the death of his aunt, Philip became the sole surviving descendant of his maternal grandfather, and was heir to the <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Arundel" title="Earl of Arundel">earldom of Arundel</a> and its subsidiary titles, and all of FitzAlan's extensive properties in Sussex, including <a href="/wiki/Arundel_Castle" title="Arundel Castle">Arundel Castle</a>, the main residence of the FitzAlan family, which later became the principal home of Philip's descendants. After his grandfather's death in February 1580, Howard received the entire inheritance of his maternal family, and the Queen made him <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Arundel" title="Earl of Arundel">Earl of Arundel</a>. Since then, the earldom of Arundel has remained in the Howard family, and with the restoration of the dukedom of Norfolk in 1660, remains as a subsidiary title of the dukes.<sup id="cite_ref-pollen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Howard is variously numbered as 1st, 20th or 13th <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Arundel" title="Earl of Arundel">Earl of Arundel</a>. This is due to forfeiture losses and subsequent recreations of the title. If taken in a continuous line from the creation of the Earldom in 1138 by <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen of England">King Stephen</a> for <a href="/wiki/William_d%27Aubigny,_1st_Earl_of_Arundel" title="William d&#39;Aubigny, 1st Earl of Arundel">William d'Aubigny</a> (d. 1176), Philip's direct ancestor, Howard would be the 20th Earl.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Received_again_into_Catholic_Church_and_later_imprisonment">Received again into Catholic Church and later imprisonment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Howard,_13th_Earl_of_Arundel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Received again into Catholic Church and later imprisonment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg/220px-Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="274" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg/330px-Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg/440px-Thomas-howard-rubensportrait.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3394" data-file-height="4226" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_14th_Earl_of_Arundel" title="Thomas Howard, 14th Earl of Arundel">Thomas Howard, 14th Earl of Arundel</a>, only son of Philip and Anne Dacre</figcaption></figure>
<p>Howard was present at a debate in the Tower of London in 1581 between a group of Protestant theologians and the Catholic priests <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Sherwin" title="Ralph Sherwin">Ralph Sherwin</a>. This led Philip to return to Arundel to think about reconciliation with the Catholic Church, despite the <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">dangers</a> that would entail.<sup id="cite_ref-web.archive.org_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-web.archive.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Philip was originally baptised as a Catholic and although he received a Protestant education, a vein of Catholicism was never far below the surface, mostly due to the fact that his close relations had remained loyal to the Roman Church during the Reformation. His maternal grandfather was a staunch Catholic<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and his father, who had also been educated as a Protestant but was a Catholic, was disgraced for having conspired against Queen Elizabeth with the intention of replacing her with <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots" title="Mary, Queen of Scots">Mary, Queen of Scots</a> and thus restore Catholicism in England. His paternal grandfather, the <a href="/wiki/Henry_Howard,_Earl_of_Surrey" title="Henry Howard, Earl of Surrey">Earl of Surrey</a>, also fell out of favour and was executed by <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII" title="Henry VIII">Henry VIII</a>, partly because he was a Catholic.
</p><p>In 1583, the Earl was suspected of complicity in the <a href="/wiki/Throckmorton_Plot" title="Throckmorton Plot">Throckmorton Plot</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He prepared to escape to the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Netherlands" title="Spanish Netherlands">Spanish Netherlands</a>, but his plans were interrupted by a visit from Elizabeth at his house in London and she ordered that he confine himself there.
</p><p>That same year, the Countess of Arundel, without her husband's knowledge and in great fear of his displeasure, secretly returned to the illegal and underground <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Church in England">Catholic Church in England</a>. After much effort, she successfully regained her husband's affection.
</p><p>On 30 September 1584,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> the Earl was secretly received again into the Catholic Church by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest Father <a href="/wiki/William_Weston_(Jesuit)" title="William Weston (Jesuit)">William Weston</a>. At the same time, the Earl's younger half-brother, Lord William Howard, was also received into Catholicism.<sup id="cite_ref-joh_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-joh-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Arundel, while still attending Elizabeth's court, successfully hid his adherence to Catholicism for a time,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> before withdrawing to his home and attempting to focus on being a better husband and father. The next year, Howard acted against Father Weston's cautions,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> by attempting to flee to mainland Europe in order to live openly as a Catholic with his wife and children.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His flight abroad was recommended, planned, and betrayed to Sir <a href="/wiki/Francis_Walsingham" title="Francis Walsingham">Francis Walsingham</a> by a trusted servant, whom Father Philip Caraman identifies as the Earl's chaplain, underground Catholic priest and <i><a href="/wiki/Agent_provocateur" title="Agent provocateur">agent provocateur</a></i> Father Edward Grately.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> While many other <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusants</a> had been able to successfully flee England, the Earl of Arundel, through his kinship to the late <a href="/wiki/Anne_Boleyn" title="Anne Boleyn">Anne Boleyn</a>, was a second cousin once removed of the Queen. He was widely considered by persecuted Roman Catholics who were plotting <a href="/wiki/Regime_change" title="Regime change">regime change</a> to be a possible <a href="/wiki/Heir_presumptive" title="Heir presumptive">heir presumptive</a> to the English throne.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911707–708_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911707–708-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The Earl's ship was accordingly boarded by the <a href="/wiki/Tudor_Navy" class="mw-redirect" title="Tudor Navy">Tudor Navy</a> while setting sail from <a href="/wiki/Littlehampton" title="Littlehampton">Littlehampton</a>, where he was arrested and committed to the Tower of London on 25 April 1585.<sup id="cite_ref-hume_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-pollen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was charged before the <a href="/wiki/Star_Chamber" title="Star Chamber">Star Chamber</a> with being a Catholic, quitting England without leave, sharing in Jesuit plots, and claiming the title <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Norfolk" title="Duke of Norfolk">Duke of Norfolk</a> in defiance of his father's <a href="/wiki/Attainder" title="Attainder">attainder</a>. On 17 May 1586, he was fined £10,000 and sentenced to imprisonment at the Queen's pleasure.<sup id="cite_ref-William_Weston_1955_Page_20_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Weston_1955_Page_20-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In July 1586 he was offered his freedom if he would carry the sword of state before the Queen to church; he refused. In 1588 he was accused of praying, together with other Catholics, for the victory of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Armada" title="Spanish Armada">Spanish Armada</a>. He was tried for <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a> on 14 April 1589 and was found guilty. He was immediately condemned to <a href="/wiki/Death_penalty" class="mw-redirect" title="Death penalty">death</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Attainder" title="Attainder">attainted</a>, with all his titles and property declared forfeit to the Crown.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In a letter dated 1 May 1589 to <a href="/wiki/Claudio_Aquaviva" class="mw-redirect" title="Claudio Aquaviva">Claudio Aquaviva</a>, Father <a href="/wiki/Henry_Garnet" title="Henry Garnet">Henry Garnet</a> recalled, "When the sentence was pronounced and the crowd saw the Earl coming out of the hall with the axe-edge turned in towards him -- in the trial of nobles this is the sign that the prisoner has been condemned -- suddenly there was a great uproar that was carried miles along the river bank, some people demanding what had come of the Queen's clemency that such a splendid and gallant gentleman should suffer condemnation, others passionately indignant that a man who had prayed to God should be executed for that alone. For among the accusations brought against him, the principal charge and the one on which the whole case turned, was this -- he had asked a certain priest to pray for the success of the Spanish fleet; whereas in fact, all his enemies could prove against him and all he had done was this, that he sought that prayers should be said every day and night in the Tower of London and in other prisons at that time, chiefly, when everyone was expecting a general massacre [of Catholics]."<sup id="cite_ref-William_Weston_1955_Page_20_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Weston_1955_Page_20-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Queen Elizabeth did not sign his <a href="/wiki/Death_warrant" class="mw-redirect" title="Death warrant">death warrant</a>, but Howard was never told this.<sup id="cite_ref-hume_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> He was kept constantly in fear of execution, although comforted by the companionship of a dog, which served as a go-between by which Howard and other prisoners, most notably the priest <a href="/wiki/Robert_Southwell_(Jesuit)" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Southwell (Jesuit)">Robert Southwell</a>, could smuggle messages to each other. Although these two men never met, Howard's dog helped them to deepen their friendship and exchange encouragement in each other's plight. Philip Howard loved his pet, which is remembered along with him in a statue at <a href="/wiki/Arundel_Cathedral" title="Arundel Cathedral">Arundel Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cathedral_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathedral-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One day Howard scratched into a wall of his cell the words, still visible today: (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Quanto plus afflictiones pro Christo in hoc saeculo, tanto plus gloriae cum Christo in futuro</i>) ("The more affliction [we endure] for Christ in this world, the more glory [we shall obtain] with Christ in the next") (cf. Romans, chapter 8).<sup id="cite_ref-hume_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> That same phrase is also found today on one of the steps of Howard's shrine in Arundel.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Each day he spent several hours in prayer and meditation; he was noted for his patience in suffering and courtesy to unkind keepers.<sup id="cite_ref-web.archive.org_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-web.archive.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Howard spent more than ten years in the Tower. Father Weston later recalled, "While he was enduring these cruel sufferings an offer of liberation was made him in the Queen's name, but on condition that he held a disputation with the so-called Archbishop of Canterbury. This he refused to do. He preferred to be afflicted in the company of God's people than to possess the passing pleasures of temporal freedom. Indeed, he reckoned persecution a greater happiness than the frail and shallow satisfaction of worldly well-being."<sup id="cite_ref-William_Weston_1955_Page_15_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Weston_1955_Page_15-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Howard,_13th_Earl_of_Arundel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the autumn of 1595, while dying of <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a>, the Earl petitioned the Queen to be allowed to see his wife and his son, who had been born after his imprisonment. The Queen responded, "If he will but once attend the Protestant Service, he shall not only see his wife and children, but be restored to his honours and estates with every mark of my royal favour". To this, Howard is said to have replied: "Tell Her Majesty if my religion be the cause for which I suffer, sorry I am that I have but one life to lose". He remained in the Tower, never seeing his wife or son again, and died alone on Sunday 19 October 1595.<sup id="cite_ref-hume_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hume-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Father Weston later recalled, "There were some who thought he was carried off by poison. I, however, made careful inquiries of a certain Catholic who had served him as a page at that time in the Tower, but I was never able to get any confirmation of this. As he lay dying he bequeathed to me the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Breviary" title="Roman Breviary">breviary</a> which he used: but Father Garnet decided to keep it himself for posterity like some religious object. He did not dare to entrust it to me, for everything I had was likely to be seized at any moment, and he did not think it right to expose to such manifold risk a possession which, he declared, was more precious than gold."<sup id="cite_ref-William_Weston_1955_Page_15_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Weston_1955_Page_15-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Father Philip Caraman, Garnet also kept the breviary which Robert Southwell had used in the Tower of London. Both, however, were lost in a raid by <a href="/wiki/Priest_hunter" title="Priest hunter">priest hunters</a> on Garnet's London safe house.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Howard was buried beneath the floor of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Peter_ad_Vincula" title="Church of St Peter ad Vincula">church of St Peter ad Vincula</a>, inside the walls of the Tower, where his father's remains also lay. The Earl's funeral and burial, according to Father Caraman, "cost his frugal Sovereign two pounds."<sup id="cite_ref-William_Weston_1955_Page_21_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Weston_1955_Page_21-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Countess of Arundel took a vow of <a href="/wiki/Chastity" title="Chastity">chastity</a> after being widowed, and never remarried. She spent her remaining days writing <a href="/wiki/Christian_poetry" title="Christian poetry">Christian poetry</a>, attending mass, and making other religious observances. She had a passion for helping those in need, especially the sick.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1624, the dowager Countess and their son obtained permission from  <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="James I of England">King James I</a> to move Howard's remains, first to the residence of the dowager Countess at <a href="/wiki/West_Horsley" title="West Horsley">West Horsley</a>, <a href="/wiki/Surrey" title="Surrey">Surrey</a>, and finally to the <a href="/wiki/Fitzalan_Chapel" title="Fitzalan Chapel">Fitzalan Chapel</a> at <a href="/wiki/Arundel_Castle" title="Arundel Castle">Arundel Castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sussex" title="Sussex">Sussex</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-William_Weston_1955_Page_21_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Weston_1955_Page_21-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Dowager Countess died of natural causes on 19 April 1630 at <a href="/wiki/Shifnal" title="Shifnal">Shifnal Manor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Shropshire" title="Shropshire">Shropshire</a> aged 73, and was buried next to her husband inside the Fitzalan Chapel.
</p><p>Even though Howard had been attainted at his trial in 1589, in 1603, shortly after the accession of the Scottish king <a href="/wiki/James_VI_and_I" title="James VI and I">James VI and I</a> to the English throne and thanks to the intercession of Philip's uncle, the <a href="/wiki/Henry_Howard,_Earl_of_Northampton" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry Howard, Earl of Northampton">Earl of Northampton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_14th_Earl_of_Arundel" title="Thomas Howard, 14th Earl of Arundel">Thomas Howard, Lord Maltravers</a>, Arundel's son, was able to regain royal favour. In 1604, Thomas recovered the earldom of Arundel and estates.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Along with <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Assumption" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of the Assumption">Our Lady of the Assumption</a>, St. Philip Howard is co-<a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Arundel_and_Brighton" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Arundel and Brighton">Roman Catholic Diocese of Arundel and Brighton</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cormac_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cormac-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Arundel_Cathedral" title="Arundel Cathedral">Arundel Cathedral</a>, originally dedicated to St. <a href="/wiki/Philip_Neri" title="Philip Neri">Philip Neri</a>, was commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Henry_Fitzalan-Howard,_15th_Duke_of_Norfolk" title="Henry Fitzalan-Howard, 15th Duke of Norfolk">the 15th Duke of Norfolk</a> in 1868. It was elevated to the status of a cathedral in 1965 and its dedication changed to Our Lady and Saint Philip Howard just after Pope <a href="/wiki/Paul_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul VI">Paul VI</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> the Earl as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a> in October 1970. On 10 March 1971 in the presence of Monsignor <a href="/wiki/Michael_Bowen_(bishop)" title="Michael Bowen (bishop)">Michael Bowen</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_bishop" title="Coadjutor bishop">Coadjutor bishop</a> of Arundel and Brighton, and the Chapter of Canons, Howard's remains were moved from the Fitzalan Chapel to the new shrine erected in the Cathedral, where the consecration ceremony was held.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cathedral_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathedral-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Since then, the shrine has become a place of pilgrimage.
</p><p>While imprisoned, Howard spent much of his time writing and translating Catholic poetry and devotional literature. The manuscripts, according to Father Caraman, were routinely smuggled out of the Tower of London to be edited and corrected by Father William Weston,<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> before in at least one case, being smuggled to the Spanish Netherlands for publication by exiled English <a href="/wiki/Recusant" class="mw-redirect" title="Recusant">recusant</a> <a href="/wiki/Richard_Verstegan" class="mw-redirect" title="Richard Verstegan">Richard Verstegan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For example, the Earl made a Latin-<a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_English" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabethan English">Elizabethan English</a> translation of <i>An Epistle in the Person of Jesus Christ to the Faithful Soule</i> by <a href="/wiki/John_Justus_of_Landsberg" class="mw-redirect" title="John Justus of Landsberg">John Justus of Landsberg</a>, which was posthumously published at <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a> (1595, reprinted 1871). Howard's verse translation of <a href="/wiki/Marko_Maruli%C4%87" title="Marko Marulić">Marko Marulić</a>'s poem <i>Carmen de doctrina Domini nostri Iesu Christi pendentis in cruce</i> ("A Dialogue Betwixt a Christian and Christ Hanging on the Crosse"), served in lieu of an introduction in the Antwerp edition.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> Howard's poetry translation of Marulić was published again, with updated <a href="/wiki/English_orthography" title="English orthography">English orthography</a>, in the March/April 2022 issue of the <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholic">Traditionalist Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Literary_magazine" title="Literary magazine">literary magazine</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/St._Austin_Review" title="St. Austin Review">St. Austin Review</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Howard also authored three manuscript treatises <i>On the Excellence and Utility of Virtue</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911708-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Further detailed research about the Earl's life, as well as several of his works of <a href="/wiki/Christian_poetry" title="Christian poetry">Christian poetry</a>, was collected by the poet <a href="/wiki/Louise_Imogen_Guiney" title="Louise Imogen Guiney">Louise Imogen Guiney</a> and published as part of her <a href="/wiki/1939_in_poetry" title="1939 in poetry">1939</a> collection <i><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015004720150&amp;view=1up&amp;seq=7">The Recusant Poets</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
