<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the French hermit and saint who died 430 AD, see <a href="/wiki/Caprasius_of_L%C3%A9rins" title="Caprasius of Lérins">Caprasius of Lérins</a>.</div>

<p><b>Saint Caprasius of Agen</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Caprais</i>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the fourth century.  <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">Relics</a> associated with him were discovered at <a href="/wiki/Agen" title="Agen">Agen</a> in the fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_2000_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_2000-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Local legends dating from the 14th century make him the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Agen" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Agen">bishop of Agen</a>, though, as <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> writes, the only evidence to support his existence is the dedication of a church to him in the 6th century.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_2000_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_2000-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the 9th century, his cult was fused with that of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Faith" title="Saint Faith">Saint Faith</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alberta_of_Agen" title="Alberta of Agen">Alberta of Agen</a>, also associated with Agen.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_2000_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_2000-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>   His cult was also fused with that of <a href="/wiki/Primus_and_Felician" title="Primus and Felician">Primus and Felician</a>, who are called Caprasius' brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:(Agen)_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/%28Agen%29_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg/220px-%28Agen%29_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="167" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/%28Agen%29_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg/330px-%28Agen%29_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/%28Agen%29_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg/440px-%28Agen%29_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg 2x" data-file-width="7346" data-file-height="5587" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:(Agen)_Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Caprais_-_Vue_de_la_Place_du_Mar%C3%A9chal_Foch.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Cathédrale Saint-Caprais d'Agen</div></div></div>
<p>In the year 866, Faith's remains had been transferred to <a href="/wiki/Conques" title="Conques">Conques</a>, which was along the pilgrimage route to <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Compostela</a>.  Her cult, centered at the Abbatiale Sainte-Foy de Conques, spread along the pilgrim routes on the <a href="/wiki/Way_of_St._James" class="mw-redirect" title="Way of St. James">Way of St. James</a>.  The Church of San Caprasio, built in the beginning of the 11th century, is a <a href="/wiki/First_Romanesque" title="First Romanesque">First Romanesque</a> church located at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_la_Ser%C3%B3s" title="Santa Cruz de la Serós">Santa Cruz de la Serós</a>, which was on the Way of St. James.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Caprasius_of_Agen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the persecutions of Christians by the <a href="/wiki/Dacian_(prefect)" title="Dacian (prefect)">prefect Dacian</a>, Caprasius fled to Mont-Saint-Vincent, near Agen.  He witnessed the execution of Faith from atop the hill.  Caprasius was condemned to death, and was joined on his way to execution by Alberta, Faith's sister (also identified as Caprasius' mother<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>), and two brothers, named Primus and Felician.  All four were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.
</p>
