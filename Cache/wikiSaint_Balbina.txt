<p><b>Balbina of Rome</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">bahl-BEE-nə</i>), sometimes called <b>Saint Balbina</b> and <b>Balbina the Virgin</b> is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Virgin_martyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin martyr">virgin martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Balbina_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:183px;"><a href="/wiki/File:QuirinusBalbina.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/17/QuirinusBalbina.jpg" decoding="async" width="181" height="253" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="181" data-file-height="253" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:QuirinusBalbina.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Balbina and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quirinus_of_Neuss" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Quirinus of Neuss">Saint Quirinus of Neuss</a>, from an 18th-century print.</div></div></div>
<p>The story of Balbina is introduced in the legendary Acts of Sts. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_I" title="Pope Alexander I">Alexander</a> and Balbina,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> where it is said that she was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Quirinus_of_Neuss" title="Quirinus of Neuss">Quirinus</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Tribune" title="Tribune">Tribune</a> in the Roman Army.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Usuardus" class="mw-redirect" title="Usuardus">Usuardus</a> speaks of her in his martyrology; his account of Balbina rests on the record of the martyrdom of Alexander I.
</p><p>Legend has it that Quirinus was ordered to hold <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_I" title="Pope Alexander I">Pope Alexander I</a> and a man named Hermes in prison because of their Christian faith. They were held in separate prisons that were a great distance apart, and both were shackled and well guarded. Quirinus was trying to convert Hermes back to the old gods, but promised to become a Christian if Hermes could prove that there was an after life. Hermes explained that Pope Alexander could make a better argument than he, and asked several times to visit his prison. At first Quirinus agreed to this, but after a while he became angry, convinced that these visits were frivolous excursions. He tightened the guard so that the two would not be able to speak with one another. That night Hermes prayed, and an angel appeared to Pope Alexander, releasing his chains and bringing him to Hermes' prison cell.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The next morning Quirinus came to Hermes cell as usual, and he was shocked to find both men inside. His heart now open to what the two Christians had to say, he stayed and talked with the men for a while. Hermes shared his story and told of how the Pope with the help of God had raised his son from the dead. Hearing this, Quirinus said that his daughter Balbina had a large <a href="/wiki/Goiter" class="mw-redirect" title="Goiter">goiter</a>, and if the Pope could heal her, he would believe and become a Christian. 
The Pope asked him to bring her to prison where he was held originally. <i>"How can she find you there, when you're here with Hermes?"</i> Quirinus asked. <i>"He who brought me here, will also bring me back"</i>, replied the Pope.
So Quirinus quickly returned home and brought his daughter to the prison where he had originally locked up Pope Alexander. There, they found Alexander patiently waiting for them, tightly shackled in the way he had been before. Seeing this, they knelt before him in reverence.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg/250px-Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="257" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg/375px-Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg/500px-Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2080" data-file-height="2140" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Roma_san_pietro_in_vincoli_catene.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Reliquary containing the chains of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">St. Peter</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Pietro_in_Vincoli" title="San Pietro in Vincoli">St. Peter in Chains</a> in Rome, Italy</div></div></div>
<p>Full of devotion, Balbina started to kiss the links of the chains that the Pope was attached to. But he said to her: <i>"You shall not kiss these chains, but go out and find St. Peter's chains. Once you've found them, kiss them with devotion and you will soon be well."</i><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Knowing where <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> had been held before his martyrdom, Quirinus jumped up immediately and took Balbina with him, and there he found the chains. Balbina kissed them and was soon well. Quirinus immediately had Pope Alexander and Hermes pardoned and released. Along with his wife and daughter he was baptized by the Pope. Pope Alexander stipulated that the miracle of the chains should be celebrated from that day forward, and he built the church of the Apostle Peter, where the chains have been held ever since. The church he called "ad Vincula" ("in chains"), is today <a href="/wiki/San_Pietro_in_Vincoli" title="San Pietro in Vincoli">St. Peter in Chains</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another account has Quirinus placed as the one ordered to execute Alexander and two others, Eventius, and Theodolus, who had been arrested by order of <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a>. Quirinus converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, however, after witnessing miracles performed by these three saints, and he was baptized along with his wife, and daughter Balbina.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In both accounts, Quirinus was then arrested as a Christian and martyred through decapitation on 30 March 116. He was buried in the catacomb of Prætextatus on the Via Appia. His grave was later regarded with great veneration and is referred to in the old itineraries (guides for pilgrims) of the Roman <a href="/wiki/Catacombs" title="Catacombs">catacombs</a>.
</p><p>It is not fully known what happened to Balbina after the death of her father, but some accounts list her as living as a virgin recluse nun until her death in 130 A.D.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Balbina_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The general consensus is that in 130 A.D., Balbina was found guilty of being a Christian and sentenced to death by <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Emperor Hadrian</a>. Whether she was drowned or buried alive is a matter of dispute among historians.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is one alternate account that Balbina was arrested along with her father in 116, and beheaded in the same manner.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her death, she was buried next to her father in the catacomb of Praetextatus on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Appia" class="mw-redirect" title="Via Appia">Via Appia</a>. At a later date, the bones and relics of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quirinus_of_Neuss" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Quirinus of Neuss">Quirinus</a> and Balbina were brought to the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Balbina" title="Santa Balbina">church</a> built in her honor in the 4th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Balbina_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>St. Balbina is honored as one of the 140 saints atop the colonnade of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a>.</li>
<li>Some of her <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were brought to <a href="/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral" title="Cologne Cathedral">Cologne Cathedral</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, most likely around the same time that Quirinus' body was donated in 1050 by Pope Leo IX to an abbess of <a href="/wiki/Neuss" title="Neuss">Neuss</a> named Gepa (who is called a sister of the pope).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The majority of her relics are said to still be in the altar of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Balbina" title="Santa Balbina">Santa Balbina</a>.</li>
<li>At some point in the late 15th or early 16th century, Balbina's skull was removed from her body and placed inside an ornate reliquary. This reliquary now resides in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York City.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Basilica_of_Santa_Balbina">Basilica of Santa Balbina</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Balbina_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Basilica of Santa Balbina">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_balbina.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Santa_balbina.JPG/250px-Santa_balbina.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Santa_balbina.JPG/375px-Santa_balbina.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Santa_balbina.JPG/500px-Santa_balbina.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_balbina.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Santa Balbina Basilica in Rome</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Santa_Balbina" title="Santa Balbina">Santa Balbina</a> is a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> <a href="/wiki/Churches_of_Rome" title="Churches of Rome">church in</a> <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, devoted to St. Balbina. It was built in the 4th century over the house of <a href="/wiki/Consul" title="Consul">consul</a> <a href="/wiki/Lucius_Fabius_Cilo" title="Lucius Fabius Cilo">Lucius Fabius Cilo</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Aventine_Hill" title="Aventine Hill">Aventine Hill</a>, behind the <a href="/wiki/Baths_of_Caracalla" title="Baths of Caracalla">Baths of Caracalla</a>.
</p><p>The site should not be confused with the location of a catacomb named after her (<i>coem. Balbinae</i>) which lay between the Via Appia and the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a> not far from the little church called Domine quo vadis. Over this cemetery another, now lost <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> was erected in the fourth century by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Mark" title="Pope Mark">Pope Mark</a>.
</p><p>Tradition has it that it was at this church that the <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Emperor Constantine</a> bade farewell to the Pope on his departure for Constantinople.
</p>
