<p><b>Jean-Baptiste de La Salle</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span><span title="/æ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;bad&#39;">æ</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span></span>/</a></span></span>) (<small>French pronunciation:&#160;&#8203;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[lasal]</a></span>; 1651 – 7 April 1719) was a French <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, educational reformer, and founder of the <a href="/wiki/De_La_Salle_Brothers" title="De La Salle Brothers">Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools</a>. He is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> for teachers of youth. He is referred to both as <b>La Salle</b> and as <b>De La Salle</b>.
</p><p>La Salle dedicated much of his life to the <a href="/wiki/Education" title="Education">education</a> of poor children in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>; in doing so, he started many lasting educational practices.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Baptiste_de_La_Salle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>La Salle was born to a wealthy family in <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, France, on 30 April 1651. He was the eldest child of Louis de La Salle and Nicolle Moet de Brouillet. Nicolle's family was a noble one and ran a successful winery business; she was a relative of <a href="/wiki/Claude_Mo%C3%ABt" title="Claude Moët">Claude Moët</a>, founder of <a href="/wiki/Mo%C3%ABt_%26_Chandon" title="Moët &amp; Chandon">Moët &amp; Chandon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>La Salle was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> at age eleven on 11 March 1662,<sup id="cite_ref-DeLaSalle_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DeLaSalle-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-graham_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-graham-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in an official ceremony that marked a boy's intention, and his parents offer of their young sons, to the service of God.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was named <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Reims_Cathedral" title="Reims Cathedral">Reims Cathedral</a> when he was sixteen,<sup id="cite_ref-Lasalle_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lasalle-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and at seventeen received minor orders.<sup id="cite_ref-DeLaSalle_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DeLaSalle-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was sent to the College des Bons Enfants, where he pursued higher studies and on 10 July 1669 he took the degree of Master of Arts. When De La Salle had completed his classical, literary, and philosophical courses, he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> to enter the <a href="/wiki/Seminary_of_Saint-Sulpice" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminary of Saint-Sulpice">Seminary of Saint-Sulpice</a> on 18 October 1670. His mother died on 19 July 1671 and his father on 9 April 1672. This circumstance obliged him to leave Saint-Sulpice on 19 April 1672. He was now twenty-one, the head of the family, and as such had the responsibility of educating his four brothers and two sisters. In 1672 he received the minor order of subdeacon, was ordained a deacon in 1676, and he then finally completed his theological studies and was ordained to the priesthood at the age of 26 on 9 April 1678.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Two years later he received a <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-graham_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-graham-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sisters_of_the_Child_Jesus">Sisters of the Child Jesus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Baptiste_de_La_Salle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sisters of the Child Jesus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Child_Jesus" title="Sisters of the Child Jesus">Sisters of the Child Jesus</a> were a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> whose work was the care of the sick and education of poor girls. The young priest had helped them in becoming established, and then served as their chaplain and confessor. It was through his work with the Sisters that in 1679 he met <a href="/wiki/Adrian_Nyel" title="Adrian Nyel">Adrian Nyel</a>. With De La Salle's help, a school was soon opened. Shortly thereafter, a wealthy woman in <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> told Nyel that she also would endow a school, but only if La Salle would help. What began as an effort to help Adrian Nyel establish a school for the poor in La Salle's home town gradually became his life's work.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Institute_of_the_Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools">Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Baptiste_de_La_Salle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_(Paris)_15.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_%28Paris%29_15.jpg/200px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_%28Paris%29_15.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_%28Paris%29_15.jpg/300px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_%28Paris%29_15.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_%28Paris%29_15.jpg/400px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Jean-Baptiste-de-la-Salle_%28Paris%29_15.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1368" data-file-height="1824" /></a><figcaption>Statue in the Church of Saint Jean-Baptiste de La Salle, Paris, France</figcaption></figure>
<p>At that time, most children had little <a href="/wiki/Life_chances" title="Life chances">hope</a> for <a href="/wiki/Social_mobility" title="Social mobility">social</a> and <a href="/wiki/Economic_mobility" title="Economic mobility">economic advancement</a>. Moved by the plight of the poor who seemed so "far from salvation" either in this world or the next, he determined to put his own talents and advanced education at the service of the children "often left to themselves and badly brought up".<sup id="cite_ref-Lasalle_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lasalle-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>La Salle knew that the teachers in Reims were struggling, lacking leadership, purpose, and training, and he found himself taking increasingly deliberate steps to help this small group of men with their work. First, in 1680, he invited them to take their meals in his home, as much to teach them <a href="/wiki/Table_manners" title="Table manners">table manners</a> as to inspire and instruct them in their work. This crossing of social boundaries was one that his relatives found difficult to bear. In 1681, De La Salle decided that he would take a further step – he brought the teachers into his own home to live with him. De La Salle's relatives were deeply disturbed; his social peers were scandalized. A year later, when his family home was lost at <a href="/wiki/Auction" title="Auction">auction</a> because of a family lawsuit, De La Salle rented a house into which he and the handful of teachers moved.<sup id="cite_ref-institute_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-institute-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>La Salle decided to resign his canonry to devote his full attention to the establishment of schools and training of teachers. He had inherited a considerable fortune, which he could have been used to further his aims, but on the advice of a Father Barre of Paris, he sold what he had and sent the money to the poor of the province of Champagne, where a famine was causing great hardship.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>La Salle thereby began a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a>, the first one with no priests whatsoever among its members: the Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools,<sup id="cite_ref-Lasalle_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lasalle-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> also known as the Christian Brothers in the United States and as the De La Salle Brothers in the United Kingdom, Ireland, Malta, Australasia, Mexico, and Asia. The institute is sometimes confused with a different congregation of the same name, founded by <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Ignatius_Rice" title="Edmund Ignatius Rice">Edmund Ignatius Rice</a> in Ireland and known in the USA as the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Christian_Brothers" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Christian Brothers">Irish Christian Brothers</a>.
</p><p>
One decision led to another, and La Salle found himself doing something he had never anticipated. La Salle wrote:<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><i>I had imagined that the care which I assumed of the schools and the masters would amount only to a marginal involvement committing me to no more than providing for the subsistence of the masters and assuring that they acquitted themselves of their tasks with piety and devotion ...<sup id="cite_ref-graham_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-graham-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Indeed, if I had ever thought that the care I was taking of the schoolmasters out of pure charity would ever have made it my duty to live with them, I would have dropped the whole project. ... God, who guides all things with wisdom and serenity, whose way it is not to force the inclinations of persons, willed to commit me entirely to the development of the schools. He did this in an imperceptible way and over a long period of time so that one commitment led to another in a way that I did not foresee in the beginning.</i><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>La Salle's enterprise met with opposition from <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> authorities who resisted the creation of a new form of <a href="/wiki/Religious_life" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious life">religious life</a>, a community of <a href="/wiki/Consecrated" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated">consecrated</a> <a href="/wiki/Laymen" class="mw-redirect" title="Laymen">laymen</a> to conduct free schools "together and by association". The educational establishment resented his innovative methods.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Nevertheless, La Salle and his small group of free teachers set up the institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools which is, according to the La Salle Web site, entirely dedicated to the Christian education of the "children of artisans and the poor", in a life close to that of the <a href="/wiki/Religious_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious (Catholicism)">Catholic religious</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1685 La Salle founded what is generally considered the first <a href="/wiki/Normal_school" title="Normal school">normal school</a> &#8212; that is, a school whose purpose is to train teachers &#8212; in Reims, France.<sup id="cite_ref-jefferson_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jefferson-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Worn out by austerity and exhausting labor, La Salle died at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Yon" title="Saint Yon">Saint Yon</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a>, early in 1719 on <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a>, three weeks before his 68th birthday.<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Baptiste_de_La_Salle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg/200px-John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg/300px-John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg/400px-John_baptist_de_la_salle-relics.jpg 2x" data-file-width="639" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>Relics of John Baptist de La Salle in the Casa Generaliza in Rome, Italy</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> La Salle on 24 May 1900 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> inserted his feast in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1904 for celebration on 15 May. Because of his life and inspirational writings, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> proclaimed him <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patron Saint</a> of All Teachers of Youth on 15 May 1950.<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the Church calendar, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> moved his feast day to 7 April, the day of his death or "birth to heaven", his <i>dies natalis</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Baptiste_de_La_Salle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Glasallejf.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Glasallejf.JPG/140px-Glasallejf.JPG" decoding="async" width="140" height="249" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Glasallejf.JPG/210px-Glasallejf.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Glasallejf.JPG/280px-Glasallejf.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1080" data-file-height="1920" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Jean-Baptiste de La Salle, <a href="/wiki/De_La_Salle_University" title="De La Salle University">De La Salle University</a>, Philippines</figcaption></figure>
<p>La Salle was a pioneer in programs for training lay teachers. Of his writings on education, Matthew Arnold remarked: "Later works on the same subject have little improved the precepts, while they entirely lack the unction."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> His educational innovations include Sunday courses for working young men, one of the first institutions in France for the care of delinquents, technical schools, and secondary schools for modern languages, arts, and sciences. The LaSalle University says that his writings influenced educational practice, school management, and teacher preparation for more than 300 years.<sup id="cite_ref-univ_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-univ-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Lasallian_educational_institutions" title="Lasallian educational institutions">Lasallian schools</a> form a 300-year-old network<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> following La Salle's principles. Many of the schools are named after La Salle, and several streets, often near a Lasallian School, are named after him.
</p><p>In 2021 the International Lasallian Mission Web site stated that the Lasallian order consists of about 3,000 Brothers, who help in running over 1,100 education centers in 80 countries with more than a million students, together with 90,000 teachers and <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a> associates.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Asteroid" title="Asteroid">Asteroid</a> <a href="/wiki/3002_Delasalle" class="mw-redirect" title="3002 Delasalle">3002 Delasalle</a> was discovered in 1982 and was named after De La Salle.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
