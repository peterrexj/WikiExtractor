					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Monk and abbot</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Cathróe" redirects here. For the Bishop of St. Andrews, see <a href="/wiki/Cathr%C3%B3e_(bishop_of_the_Scots)" title="Cathróe (bishop of the Scots)">Cathróe (bishop of the Scots)</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Cathróe</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">6 March</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Cathróe</b> (<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/circa" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:circa">circa</a> 900&#8211;971) was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>. His life is recorded in a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> written soon after his death by a monk at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Saint-Cl%C3%A9ment_de_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Saint-Clément de Metz (page does not exist)">monastery of Saint Felix</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbaye_Saint-Cl%C3%A9ment_de_Metz" class="extiw" title="fr:Abbaye Saint-Clément de Metz">fr</a>&#93;</span> at <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>, where Cathróe was abbot. <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">Miracles</a> of healing were attributed to Cathróe during his life, and he was considered a saint after his death.
</p><p>As well as the information it contains on events in <a href="/wiki/Lotharingia" title="Lotharingia">Lotharingia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, and on attitudes of the time, Cathróe's life is of particular interest to historians for the light it sheds on southern <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> in the 10th century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origins">Origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cathr%C3%B3e_of_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>One clue to Cathróe's background, his name, has also proved to be a stumbling block. In his <i>Life</i>, it is commonly spelt as <i>Cathroe</i>, but other forms are given such as <i>Cadroe</i> (heading) and <i>Kaddroe</i> (3x), which come closer to those used later at Metz (<i>Cadroe</i>, <i>Kadroe</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His biographer explains it as meaning "a soldier in the Lord's camp(s)" (<i>bellator in castris Dominis</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some scholars have proposed that what the continental monks heard and transcribed was a <a href="/wiki/Brythonic_languages" class="mw-redirect" title="Brythonic languages">Brythonic</a>, more specifically <a href="/wiki/Cumbric" title="Cumbric">Old Cumbric</a> name, the first element of which represents Cumbric <i>Cat</i> "battle".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, following John Colgan's lead, David Dumville favours a <a href="/wiki/Goidelic_languages" title="Goidelic languages">Goidelic</a> etymology. He points out that the personal name Cathróe is attested in Old and Middle Irish and can be explained as a compound meaning "battle-field" (<i>Cath</i>, cognate with Welsh <i>cat</i>, + <i>róe</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cathróe was born <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/circa" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:circa">circa</a> 900. Neil McGuigan suggests that he grew up in or near <a href="/wiki/Dunkeld" title="Dunkeld">Dunkeld</a> in Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His father's name is given as Fochereach, a nobleman, his mother was Bania, who came from a similar background. After the birth of a brother with the Goidelic name Mattadán, Cathróe was fostered with his paternal uncle Beanus (Saint Bean; there were several Gaelic saints of this name).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pilgrimage">Pilgrimage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cathr%C3%B3e_of_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pilgrimage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bean sent his nephew to study in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Armagh" title="Armagh">Armagh</a>, where he seems to have learned both <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Greek</a>. He returned to Scotland, to teach in his uncle's monastery. Visions persuaded Cathróe to leave Scotland as a pilgrim. The hagiographer tells us that "the king that ruled the land, <a href="/wiki/Constantine_II_of_Scotland" title="Constantine II of Scotland">Constantine</a> by name, hastened to hold back [Cathróe]". Cathróe entered the "house of the blessed Brigit", presumed to be the monastery dedicated to Saint <a href="/wiki/Brigid_of_Kildare" title="Brigid of Kildare">Brigid of Kildare</a> at <a href="/wiki/Abernethy,_Perth_and_Kinross" title="Abernethy, Perth and Kinross">Abernethy</a>. "A certain abbot, called Maelodair [Máel Odran]" persuaded King Constantine to allow Catroe to leave, and to help him on his journey. "Then all emulously rendered assistance with gold and silver, with raiment and horses' and they sped [Cathróe] with God's blessing; and conducted by the king himself he came to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Strathclyde" title="Kingdom of Strathclyde">Cumbrians' land</a>." The writer tells us that King <a href="/wiki/Dyfnwal_ab_Owain" title="Dyfnwal ab Owain">Dovenaldus</a> ruled the Cumbrians, and that he was Cathróe's kinsman. The king escorted Catroe to <i>Loidam Civitatem</i> (read as <a href="/wiki/Leeds" title="Leeds">Leeds</a> or <a href="/wiki/Carlisle,_Cumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Carlisle, Cumbria">Carlisle</a>), "which is the boundary between the Cumbrians and the Northmen".
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>, Cathróe was welcomed, the writer claims, by King <a href="/wiki/Eric_Bloodaxe" title="Eric Bloodaxe">Eric</a>, whose wife was a relative of Cathróe's. This is problematic as Eric Bloodaxe is not thought to have ruled York at the time of the journey, and Eric's wife <a href="/wiki/Gunnhild_Mother_of_Kings" class="mw-redirect" title="Gunnhild Mother of Kings">Gunnhild</a> was said to be Norwegian.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_career">Later career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cathr%C3%B3e_of_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cathróe was abbot of <a href="/wiki/Waulsort_Abbey" title="Waulsort Abbey">Waulsort</a> for several years, until <a href="/wiki/List_of_bishops_of_Metz" title="List of bishops of Metz">Adelbero, Bishop of Metz</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> gave him the administration of <a href="/w/index.php?title=St_Felix%27s_Abbey,_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St Felix&#39;s Abbey, Metz (page does not exist)">St Felix's Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
