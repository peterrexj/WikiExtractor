<p><b>Constantius of Perugia</b> (also known as Costantius, Constance or Costanzo) (died c. 170 AD) is one of the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron saints">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Constantius_of_Perugia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to his legend, of which four versions exist, he was arrested during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Antoninus_Pius" title="Antoninus Pius">Antoninus</a> (some sources say <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>) and whipped, and then forced into a stove along with his companions, from which all escaped unharmed.  He was jailed and set free by his guards, whom he had converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>.  He sought refuge in a house owned by a Christian named Anastasius.  But he, along with Anastasius, were arrested again and after being tortured in prisons at <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spello" title="Spello">Spello</a>, were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a> near <a href="/wiki/Foligno" title="Foligno">Foligno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Local tradition makes him the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Perugia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Perugia">bishop of Perugia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  This tradition states that he became the first bishop of the city at the age of 30. He was active in evangelism and care for the poor.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Constantius_of_Perugia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His cult was diffused beyond <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>. He was listed in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> under the feast day of January 29.
</p><p>The four variations of his legend are consistent in regards to the place of his martyrdom (Foligno).  A church in Perugia dedicated to him was demolished in 1527.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The accounts of his martyrdom state that his body was carried to Perugia and buried near the site of the present-day <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> there.  His relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> in 1825 with great solemnity to a new altar at the present-day church of San Costanzo.
</p><p>On his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>, "torcolo", a ring-shaped cake made of <a href="/wiki/Pine_nuts" class="mw-redirect" title="Pine nuts">pine nuts</a>, <a href="/wiki/Raisins" class="mw-redirect" title="Raisins">raisins</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Dried_fruit" title="Dried fruit">dried fruit</a>, is a traditional food in Perugia.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In art, Costantius is often represented as a bishop wearing a <a href="/wiki/Mitre" title="Mitre">mitre</a> and robe and bearing a <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a>. He frequently appears in the company of another Perugian <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>, Sant'Ercolano (<a href="/wiki/Herculanus_of_Perugia" title="Herculanus of Perugia">Herculanus of Perugia</a>). His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 29 January.
</p>
