<p><b>Savina Petrilli</b> (29 August 1851 - 18 April 1923) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> who founded the Sisters of the Poor of Saint Catherine of Siena upon receiving the encouragement of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Petrilli devoted her order to the alleviation of the poor while catering to those people who came to the congregation seeking help. She opened her first international house in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> after her order was established though managed the house in <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a> where she lived until her death from <a href="/wiki/Cancer" title="Cancer">cancer</a> in 1923.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her beatification was celebrated on 24 April 1988. Her feast in the liturgical calendar is assigned to 18 April.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Savina_Petrilli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Savina Petrilli was born on 29 August 1851 in <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a> as the second daughter of Celso Petrilli and Matilde Venturini; her sister was Emilia.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the age of ten - in 1861 - she read an account of the life of <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a> that instilled in her a strong devotion to her. Petrilli had her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> at the age of twelve in 1863.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Petrilli later made a vow to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> in which she pledged to remain a virgin at the age of seventeen in 1868. She served as part of the Marian movement <a href="/w/index.php?title=Figlie_di_Maria&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Figlie di Maria (page does not exist)">Daughters of Mary</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figlie_di_Maria" class="extiw" title="it:Figlie di Maria">it</a>&#93;</span> from the age of fifteen and was appointed its president in 1873.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1869 she was received in a private audience with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> who invited her to establish a religious congregation devoted to Catherine of Siena upon learning that she was from Siena.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Petrilli then confided her plan to establish an order to her sister Emilia who was on the verge of death.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She - alongside five others - made her solemn profession as a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> on 15 August 1873 - the date of her order's establishment. The new order received the approval of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Siena">Archbishop of Siena</a> Enrico Bindi. She and her new professed sisters moved to a new house in the name of the Sisters of the Poor of Saint Catherine of Siena on 7 September 1874 with the approval of the archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The congregation opened their first house in <a href="/wiki/Onano" title="Onano">Onano</a> in 1881 and their first mission in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> in 1903. The papal decree of praise of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> for the order was issued in 1891.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The constitution of the order was approved as being implemented for a brief period on September 5, 1899 and approved in full after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> approved it in a decree on 17 June 1906.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Petrilli died on 18 April 1923 at 5:20pm in Siena due to <a href="/wiki/Cancer" title="Cancer">cancer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her order now operates in <a href="/wiki/Latin_America" title="Latin America">Latin America</a> in both <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> and <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> while it spread and operates in the <a href="/wiki/United_States_of_America" class="mw-redirect" title="United States of America">United States of America</a> and <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>. As of 2005 there were 589 religious in a total of 87 houses.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Savina_Petrilli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process for Petrilli commenced with a diocesan process in Siena that was tasked with compiling evidence of her deeds during her life as well as compiling a comprehensive account of her life. The process also took possession of her writings so that theologians could ascertain if the writings adhered to the magisterium of the Roman Catholic Church - the writings were approved in a decree of 21 December 1968.
</p><p>These processes commenced despite the fact that the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> did not grant their formal and full approval to the cause's initiation until 15 October 1981 in a move that also granted Petrilli the posthumous title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> - the first stage in the process. The second diocesan process opened not long after this while both were granted a decree of ratification following the closure of that process. The ratification asserted that the process completed all the work assigned to it.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> - a large dossier on her deeds as well as a biographical account - was compiled and submitted to officials at the C.C.S. in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for their own evaluation in 1985. It led to <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaiming Petrilli to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 16 November 1985.
</p><p>The single miracle needed for Petrilli's beatification was investigated in the diocese of its origin and was ratified in 1986 before being sent to Rome for extensive evaluation. John Paul II approved the healing to be a credible miracle in 1987 and beatified her on 24 April 1988.
</p>
