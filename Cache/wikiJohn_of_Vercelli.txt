<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was born in 1205 to the Garbella family in <a href="/wiki/Mosso,_Piedmont" title="Mosso, Piedmont">Mosso Santa Maria</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Biella" title="Province of Biella">Province of Biella</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> region of Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He did his initial studies in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> (one could not graduate in the Arts before the age of 21, and only after a minimum of six years of study),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and then studied <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon law</a> in Paris,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Pavia, and Vercelli<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> before he joined the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican friars</a> during the 1240s.<sup id="cite_ref-nahns_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nahns-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II</a>, that <i>stupor mundi</i> and "malleus Italiae Regionis",  died on December 13, 1250.  Pope Innocent IV's exile was over.  He left Lyons on April 19, 1251, and arrived in his home town, Genoa, on May 18.  From Genoa he began the difficult task of getting back the territories of the Catholic Church lost to the Emperor Frederick, and reconstructing the effective operation of the church hierarchy in northern and central Italy.  One of his problems was the rise and flourishing of heresy in the Po Valley.  On June 11, 1251, the pope issued instructions from Genoa to Vincentius of Milan and Joannes of Vercelli, to undertake the office of inquisitor, jointly or severally, in Venice and other parts of Lombardy.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  These were not the only inquisitors appointed.  On the same day, and with the same form letter ("Misericors et Miserator"), the pope also appointed Peter of Verona and Vivianus of Bergamo to the same task in the area of Cremona and other cities of Lombardy.  No doubt there were others, now unattested.  The pope was also prepared to take on Frederick's sons <a href="/wiki/Manfred,_King_of_Sicily" title="Manfred, King of Sicily">Manfred</a> and <a href="/wiki/Enzo_of_Sardinia" title="Enzo of Sardinia">Enzo (Ezolino) of Sardinia</a>, as he indicated in a letter to the Dominican Bishop of Treviso and the Prior of Mantua.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Umberto de Romans, the fifth Master General, elected in 1253, held the General Chapter in Buda, Hungary, on May 31, 1254.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  At the conclusion of the meeting, he appointed John of Vercelli as the Master General's Vicar to Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Later (1255-1257) he was appointed  <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">Prior</a> of the Dominican monastery in Bologna.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prior_of_Lombardy">Prior of Lombardy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Prior of Lombardy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1257, at the Provincial Chapter for Lombardy held at Novara, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Prior Provincial</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>. At that time the province contained thirty convents of the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> During his administration three new convents were founded, at Turin, Chieri<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and Tortona. He served as Prior of Lombardy for seven years.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  One of the major and continuing problems in his province was the rise and spread of heresy, especially Catharism.  The inquisitorial machine was being constructed, as John's own service in Venice a few years earlier indicates.  These new inquisitors were directing one question after another to Rome, and were overwhelming the Curia with their concerns.  Alexander replied, urging them to act boldly and independently, against any manner or quality of person, but to continue to consult the Holy See in difficult cases.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  On March 23, 1262, the new pope, Urban IV, sent a mandate to John of Vercelli, authorizing him, in consultation with other discreet members of his Order, to appoint up to eight Dominican friars as inquisitors in the Province of Lombardy and the March of Genoa.  He was also authorized to remove inquisitors from office who proved inadequate and to replace them; he could delegate this task to his vicar.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Prior of Lombardy he was expected to see to the election of a delegate (<i>diffinitor</i>) to the annual General Chapter, and to preside over the Provincial Chapter in Lombardy. In 1258, the General Chapter was held at Toulouse, and the Provincial Chapter at Milan.  He took part in the General Chapter at Valenciennes, on April 13, 1259, and held the Provincial Chapter in Bologna.  In 1260 the General Chapter was in Strasbourg, and John presided over the provincial General Chapter, which was held at Ferrara. In 1261 the General Chapter was held at Barcelona, and the Provincial Chapter at Milan.  In 1262, both meetings took place in Bologna.  In 1263 the General Chapter was held in London, and the Provincial Chapter at Venice.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Master_General">Master General</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Master General">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On June 7, 1264 he was elected as Master General by the <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a>, held in Paris, under the presidency of Pierre of Tarentaise, the future Pope Innocent V.  John held the post of Master General until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Known for his tireless energy and his commitment to simplicity, John made personal visits—typically on foot—to almost all the Dominican houses, urging his fellow friars to observe faithfully the <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">Rule</a> and <a href="/wiki/Constitutions" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitutions">Constitutions</a> of the Order.
</p><p>In 1267, an event of paramount importance for the Dominican Order took place in Bologna.  Already in 1262, under the fifth Master General, the decision had been taken by the General Chapter to provide a more imposing resting place for their founder, Dominic Guzman, than his remains currently enjoyed.  John of Vercelli and the members of the Order carried that plan to completion, providing a new shrine for Dominic's body in the apse of their church.  Already on March 15, 1267, Pope Clement IV provided a bull, granting a series of indulgences for those visiting the shrine during the week centered on Dominic's feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>  On May 27, 1267, Pope Innocent sent his blessing with his best wishes for an agreeable assembly to the General Congregation which was about to meet.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>  On June 5, 1267, the translation actually took place in a grand ceremony, with John and the delegates assembled for the General Chapter taking part, with the attendance of  Archbishop Filippo of Ravenna, Bishop Ottaviano de' Ubaldinis of Bologna, Bishop Tommaso de' Ubaldinis of Imola, and other bishops.  Before being placed in the shrine, the head and relics were shown publicly.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John is known to have consulted Thomas Aquinas officially on several occasions on matters pertaining to theology and the teaching of Pierre de Tarantaise.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his administration, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a> entrusted the Dominican Order with the task of trying to establish peace among the warring States of the Italian peninsula. Additionally, John was also given the task of preparing a framework for the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Council of Lyons">Second Council of Lyons</a>, held in 1274 in an attempt to unify the Eastern and Western Churches. In the course of this work, he met and worked with the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Friars Minor</a>, Jerome of Ascoli (who would later become <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV" title="Pope Nicholas IV">Pope Nicholas IV</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Both were later sent by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> to negotiate a disagreement with King <a href="/wiki/Philip_III_of_France" title="Philip III of France">Philip III of France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nahns_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nahns-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="1276-1277.E2.80.94Five_popes"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="1276-1277—Five_popes">1276-1277—Five popes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: 1276-1277—Five popes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following the Council, Pope Gregory again looked to the Dominican friars, this time to spread devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Name_of_Jesus" title="Holy Name of Jesus">Holy Name of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> John took the task to heart, requiring that every Dominican church contain an altar dedicated to the Holy Name. The <a href="/wiki/Society_of_the_Holy_Name" title="Society of the Holy Name">Society of the Holy Name</a> was formed to combat <a href="/wiki/Blasphemy" title="Blasphemy">blasphemy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Profanation" class="mw-redirect" title="Profanation">profanation</a> of this name.<sup id="cite_ref-americancatholic_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-americancatholic-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>  Pope Gregory returned to Italy at the end of 1275, but illness ensured that he never reached Rome.  He died at Arezzo on January 10, 1276.  His new regulations for conclaves "<a href="/wiki/Ubi_Periculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Ubi Periculum">Ubi Periculum</a>", promulgated at the Council of Lyons, were applied for the first time, and a one-day Conclave (January 20–21, 1276), produced a new pope, the Dominican Innocent V.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  The General Chapter that year met in Pisa in May, and the pope sent his greetings to his brethren, but he died suddenly on June 22, 1276. The Conclave to elect his successor began on July 2, and lasted ten days.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>  Cardinal Ottobono Fieschi was elected on July 11, 1276, and took the name Adrian V.  In August, even before his consecration and coronation, he travelled to Viterbo, partially to escape the Roman summer, but also because he wished to meet with the Emperor-elect Rudolf.  He died in Viterbo only thirty-seven days after his election, on August 18.  At some point during this whirlwind of disasters, John of Vercelli reached the Papal Curia.  This was not surprising, since, after a General Chapter, and this one in Pisa, the leaders of the Order of Preachers would have business of all sorts to do with the Curia;<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> the decision to go to Rome was much happier, since one of their own had been elected pope. After his death, they had no alternative but to wait until the new pope was willing and able to do business with them.  The death of Adrian V at Viterbo meant that the Dominican leaders were in Viterbo for the third Conclave of 1276.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>  It should have begun on August 29 or 30, but there were disorders in the town, caused, it seems by curial agitators who wanted a quick election.  The cardinals were forced to remonstrate with the disorderly mob, and they sent three Dominicans, the Archbishop of Corinth (Petrus de Confluentia), the Master General of the Dominicans (John of Vercelli), and the Procurator General (Ioannes Vereschi), to carry their reproof to the citizens of Viterbo and the unruly Curia.  Insults were hurled at the messengers, and stones were thrown.  Once order was restored, however, the Conclave began, and in one day, on September 8, produced a new pope, Cardinal Peter Julian of Lisbon, who chose to be called John XXI.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On October 15, Pope John XXI appointed John of Vercellae, as well as Hieronymus, the Minister General of the Franciscans, as Apostolic Legates to go to France to arrange a peace between King Philip and King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile" title="Alfonso X of Castile">Alfonso X of Castile</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>  The urgency of their mission was repeated in a letter from the College of Cardinals, written during the Sede Vacante<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> following the death of Pope John XXI on May 16, 1277.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>  Pope Nicholas III repeated the same urgent wish for peace directly to  King Philip III of France and  King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile" title="Alfonso X of Castile">Alfonso X of Castile</a>, with a recommendation for John of Vercellae and Hieronymus Masci.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>  On March 12, 1278, Hieronymus Masci was named a cardinal,<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> but the legates had their orders reinforced by a letter from Nicholas III dated April 4, 1278.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>  Another letter was sent to Masci on April 23, urging him to conclude the embassy as soon as possible, since his services were urgently needed in the Roman Curia.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Later_Years.2C_1278.E2.80.931283"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_Years,_1278–1283">Later Years, 1278–1283</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Later Years, 1278–1283">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Late in his life, on May 15, 1278, John was appointed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_III" title="Pope Nicholas III">Pope Nicholas III</a> to the position of <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem">Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> It was a promotion to the prelacy which he did not welcome and which he wished to decline.  After consideration and with considerable reluctance, the pope wrote John a long letter (October 1, 1278) rehearsing the reasons why he should not ask to be released from the episcopal office, addressing him in the letter as <i>Joannes electus Hierosolymitanus, quondam Ordinis Fratrum Praedicatorum Magister</i>  (John, Bishop-Elect of Jerusalem and former Master of the Order of Preachers).<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>  The Pope was still firm in his refusal to release Bishop-elect John, in letters to King Philip of France and King Alfonso of Castile on November 29, 1278.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>  It was finally after the intervention and persuasion of Nicholas III's nephew, Cardinal Latino Malabranca Orsini, that the pope finally relented and restored John of Vercelli to the office of Master General of his Order.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The General Chapter of the Order of Preachers was held in Montpellier in 1283.  The Chapter decided that the next Chapter would be held in Bologna. But John of Vercelli died on 30 November 1283 in the convent of the Dominicans in <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>, France.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="General_Chapters_held_by_John_of_Vercelli_as_Master_General">General Chapters held by John of Vercelli as Master General</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: General Chapters held by John of Vercelli as Master General">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John of Vercelli insisted on the rule instituted by Dominic himself that Friars of the Order should travel on foot, never using a horse or a wheeled vehicle.  The table below demonstrates the peripatetic nature of John's life as Master General, never staying in one convent for very long, but constantly pursuing his visitations of one province after another, one convent after another.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> The site of each General Chapter<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> had been decided at the General Chapter the year before (with the proviso that emergency circumstances might allow the Master General to choose another site),<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> and therefore John's itinerary every year would include the goal of reaching the chosen site at the appropriate time each Spring.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Vercelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John of Vercelli is the patron of the Confraternity of the most Holy Names of God and Jesus ('The Holy Name Society').
</p>

<li id="cite_note-5"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-5">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">The <i>studium generale</i> was allegedly founded in 1228.  Tommaso Vallauri, <i>Storia delle università degli studi del Piemonte</i> (Torino 1875),  pp. 15-25.  It is imagined, without specific sources, that John of Vercelli left Paris in 1229, as a result of a strike on the part of all of the professors: Mortier, p. 6 and n. 1.  So too Perotta. Such an early date for his departure from Paris would be impossible if he were a <i>Doctor in utroque iure.</i> His study at Pavia and at Vercelli is conjecture and inference. Before adopting the unlikely idea of a university at Vercelli, consult William A. Hinnebusch, <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.dominicans.ca/Documents/articles/Hinnebusch1.html">"The Dominican Order and Learning,"</a>  History of the Dominican Order II, pp. 3-18, where S. Dominic's idea is explained—that each house of the Order should have a Doctor and a Lector for the education of the members.  The ideal, occasionally realized, was that each convent would become a <i>studium</i>, if not a <i>studium generale</i>.</span>
