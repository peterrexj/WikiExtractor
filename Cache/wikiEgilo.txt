					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Egilo&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Egilo">Egilo</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Egilona</b> (or <b>Egilo</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Visigoth" class="mw-redirect" title="Visigoth">Visigothic</a> noblewoman and the last known <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_the_Visigoths" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen of the Visigoths">queen of the Visigoths</a>. She was the wife first of <a href="/wiki/Roderic" title="Roderic">Roderic</a>, the Visigothic king (710–11), and then of <a href="/wiki/Abd_al-Aziz_ibn_Musa" title="Abd al-Aziz ibn Musa">ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz</a>, Muslim governor (<i><a href="/wiki/W%C4%81l%C4%AB" class="mw-redirect" title="Wālī">wālī</a></i>) of <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">al-Andalus</a> (714–16). Her name is rendered <b>Aylū</b> by <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a> writers, who also give her the <i><a href="/wiki/Kunya_(Arabic)" title="Kunya (Arabic)">kunya</a></i> <b>Umm ʿAṣim</b> ("mother of ʿAṣim").<sup id="cite_ref-Barton_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was independently wealthy.<sup id="cite_ref-Hitchcock_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hitchcock-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Egilona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Egilona may have been related to Kings <a href="/wiki/Egica" title="Egica">Egica</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wittiza" title="Wittiza">Wittiza</a>. Since the succession of 710 was contested, Roderic may have married Egilona to strengthen his position. Their marriage did not last long, as he died at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Guadalete" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of the Guadalete">Battle of the Guadalete</a> the following year.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The date of Egilona's second marriage is uncertain. She may have married ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz before he became governor, when he was still merely the son of the conquering general, <a href="/wiki/Musa_ibn_Nusayr" title="Musa ibn Nusayr">Mūsā bin Nuṣayr</a>, who had overthrown the Visigothic kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-Hitchcock_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hitchcock-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There is no reference in the sources to Egilona converting to Islam, and scholars are divided on whether it is likely that she did.<sup id="cite_ref-Orlandis_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orlandis-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Although both Arabic and Latin sources depict Egilona and her second husband as communicating directly, it is not clear whether they could have done so directly, since Egilona would have spoken a <a href="/wiki/Vulgar_Latin" title="Vulgar Latin">vulgar Latin</a> dialect and did not likely have time or reason to learn Arabic quickly. Likewise, ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz could have had little reason to learn Latin, although he was criticised for certain foreign (<i><a href="/wiki/Ajam" title="Ajam">ʿajamī</a></i>) practices.<sup id="cite_ref-Hitchcock_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hitchcock-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Both Christian and Muslims sources make her responsible for the governor's assassination, and there is no reason to doubt the general portrayal of her role.<sup id="cite_ref-Collins_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Collins-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Christian <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_754" title="Chronicle of 754">Chronicle of 754</a></i> records that "on the advice of Queen Egilona, wife of the late king Roderic, whom he had joined to himself, [ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz] tried to throw off the Arab yoke from his neck and retain the conquered kingdom of Iberia for himself."<sup id="cite_ref-Barton_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The ninth-century Muslim historian <a href="/wiki/%CA%BFAbd_al-%E1%B8%A4akam" class="mw-redirect" title="ʿAbd al-Ḥakam">ʿAbd al-Ḥakam</a>, on the other hand, says that he ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz was killed because Egilona "had made him a Christian". This seems unlikely, since he took refuge in a mosque and recited from the Koran when attacked. Al-Ḥakam does corroborate the <i>Chronicle of 754</i>'s claim that Egilona stoked his royal ambitions, urging him to act so as to attain the respect her first husband had.<sup id="cite_ref-Hitchcock_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hitchcock-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Akhb%C4%81r_majm%C5%AB%CA%BFa" title="Akhbār majmūʿa">Akhbār majmūʿa</a></i> of 858 even claims that Egilona had a crown made out of her own jewels and forced her husband to wear it on the grounds that "a king without a crown is a king without a kingdom".<sup id="cite_ref-Orlandis_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orlandis-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She also tried to have his men perform acts of obeisance to him in <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Collins_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Collins-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Egilona's life and legend have been given dramatic treatment several times in the modern era. In 1760, <a href="/w/index.php?title=C%C3%A1ndido_Mar%C3%ADa_Trigueros&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cándido María Trigueros (page does not exist)">Cándido María Trigueros</a> published the play <i>La Egilona, viuda del rey don Rodrigo</i>. In 1785, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Antontio_Valladares_de_Sotomayor&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Antontio Valladares de Sotomayor (page does not exist)">Antontio Valladares de Sotomayor</a> staged a similar play that has been called by the same title, <i>La Egilona, viuda del rey don Rodrigo</i>. In 1788, an anonymous play entitled <i>La Egilona, drama heroica en prosa</i> was put on during the coronation ceremonies of <a href="/wiki/Charles_IV_of_Spain" title="Charles IV of Spain">Charles IV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1845, the Cuban playwright <a href="/wiki/Gertrudis_G%C3%B3mez_de_Avellaneda" title="Gertrudis Gómez de Avellaneda">Gertrudis Gómez de Avellaneda</a> published a play titled <i>Egilona</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
