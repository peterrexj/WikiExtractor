<p><b>Saint Afan of Builth</b> (<a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Sant Afan Buellt</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Avanus</i>) was an early 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>, <a href="/wiki/Martyr_of_the_Faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyr of the Faith">martyr</a>, and <a href="/wiki/List_of_Welsh_saints" title="List of Welsh saints">saint</a>. His <a href="/wiki/G%C5%B5yl_Mabsant" title="Gŵyl Mabsant">feast day</a> is generally placed on 17 November, although the <a href="/wiki/Demetia" class="mw-redirect" title="Demetia">Demetian</a> Calendar formerly used in southern <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> placed it on the 16th;<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> it is no longer observed by either the <a href="/wiki/Church_in_Wales" title="Church in Wales">Anglican</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England_and_Wales" title="Catholic Church in England and Wales">Catholic</a> church in <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Afan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Afan as a <a href="/wiki/Masculine_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Masculine name">man's name</a> in <a href="/wiki/Welsh_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Welsh name">Wales</a> is probably a loan from the Latin <i><a href="/wiki/Amandus" title="Amandus">Amandus</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In Welsh, he is sometimes known as <i><span title="Welsh-language text"><i lang="cy">Esgob Afan</i></span></i> ("Bishop Afan") from his title and as <i><span title="Welsh-language text"><i lang="cy">Afan Buellt</i></span></i> or <i><span title="Welsh-language text"><i lang="cy">Buallt</i></span></i> from his diocese around <a href="/wiki/Builth" class="mw-redirect" title="Builth">Builth</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brycheiniog" title="Brycheiniog">Brycheiniog</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Afan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Afan was the son of Cedig ap <a href="/wiki/Ceredig" title="Ceredig">Ceredig</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Cunedda_Wledig" class="mw-redirect" title="Cunedda Wledig">Cunedda Wledig</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Gwynedd" title="Kingdom of Gwynedd">Gwynedd</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Through this line, he was a cousin of <a href="/wiki/Saint_David" title="Saint David">Saint David</a>, <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>. Afan's mother is variously given as Dwywai,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Degfed ("Tenth"),<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Tegfedd, or Tegwedd,<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> all said to have been daughters of <a href="/wiki/Tegid_Foel" title="Tegid Foel">Tegid the Bald</a>, a lord of <a href="/wiki/Penllyn_(cantref)" title="Penllyn (cantref)">Penllyn</a> in <a href="/wiki/Meirionnydd" title="Meirionnydd">Meirionnydd</a> who was the husband of the sorceress <a href="/wiki/Ceridwen" title="Ceridwen">Ceridwen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Welsh_mythology" title="Welsh mythology">Welsh legend</a>.
</p><p>Afan was the founder of a <a href="/wiki/Llanafan_(Ceredigion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Llanafan (Ceredigion)">Llanafan</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a> and two others (<a href="/wiki/Llanafan_Fawr" title="Llanafan Fawr">Llanafan Fawr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Llanfechan" title="Llanfechan">Llanafan Fechan</a>) in <a href="/wiki/Brecknockshire" title="Brecknockshire">Brecknockshire</a>. He is recorded as a bishop, although his diocese remains unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He may have been the third bishop of <a href="/wiki/Llanbadarn_Fawr,_Ceredigion" title="Llanbadarn Fawr, Ceredigion">Llanbadarn</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> bishop over <a href="/wiki/Builth" class="mw-redirect" title="Builth">Builth</a> with his seat at <a href="/wiki/Llanafan_Fawr" title="Llanafan Fawr">Llanafan Fawr</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cathon_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathon-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> or held the title without any purview beyond his own parish.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His death was credited to <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyrdom</a> at the hand of Irish or Danish pirates on the banks of the River Chwefru.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was claimed as an ancestor of the 10th-century bishop Ieuan who was also martyred by Viking marauders.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Afan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg/300px-Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="225" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg/450px-Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg/600px-Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Llanfechan_Church_-_geograph.org.uk_-_230355.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Afan's Church at <a href="/wiki/Llanfechan" title="Llanfechan">Llanfechan</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>A miracle recounted by <a href="/wiki/Gerald_of_Wales" title="Gerald of Wales">Gerald of Wales</a> claimed that the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Normans" title="Anglo-Normans">Anglo-Norman</a> lord <a href="/wiki/Philip_de_Braose" title="Philip de Braose">Philip de Braose</a> was struck <a href="/wiki/Blindness" class="mw-redirect" title="Blindness">blind</a> and saw his <a href="/wiki/Hunting_dog" title="Hunting dog">hunting dogs</a> go mad when he disrespectfully used <span class="nowrap">St Afan's</span> church in Brecknockshire as a makeshift hostel one night.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He was told that his vision would only return if he resolved to leave his estates and fight in the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a>. Some say his sight was restored upon his pledge<sup id="cite_ref-patron_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> but Gerald records that he traveled to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> and fought blind, where he was "immediately struck down by a blow from a sword and so ended his life with honour".<sup id="cite_ref-description_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-description-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Afan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Afan was said to have founded the parish of Llanafan in Ceredigion (now <a href="/wiki/Llanafan_y_Trawsgoed" class="mw-redirect" title="Llanafan y Trawsgoed">Llanafan y Trawsgoed</a>), whose <a href="/wiki/St_Afan%27s_Church,_Llanafan" title="St Afan&#39;s Church, Llanafan">present church</a> serves as the chapel for the village of <a href="/wiki/Llanafan_(Ceredigion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Llanafan (Ceredigion)">Llanafan</a> and the nearby <a href="/wiki/Trawsgoed_Estate" class="mw-redirect" title="Trawsgoed Estate">Trawsgoed Estate</a>. Its grounds house the family crypt of the <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Lisburne" title="Earl of Lisburne">earls of Lisburne</a>.
</p><p>Two churches were dedicated to him in the <a href="/wiki/Deanery" title="Deanery">deanery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Builth" class="mw-redirect" title="Builth">Builth</a>: <a href="/wiki/Llanafan_Fawr" title="Llanafan Fawr">Llanafan Fawr</a> ("Great Llanafan") and Llanafan Fechan or Fach ("Lesser Llanafan"), which eventually became known as <a href="/wiki/Llanfechan" title="Llanfechan">Llanfechan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> are claimed by Llanafan Fawr, which served as a <a href="/wiki/List_of_Christian_pilgrimage_sites" title="List of Christian pilgrimage sites">pilgrimage site</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Wales" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval Wales">Middle Ages</a>. Saint Afan's grave in the churchyard there is inscribed <span class="smallcaps"><span style="font-variant: small-caps; text-transform: lowercase;">HIC IACET SANCTUS AVANUS EPISCOPUS</span></span> ("Here lies Saint Avan, bishop") in deeply cut, slightly ornamented <a href="/wiki/Beneventan_script" title="Beneventan script">Lombardic script</a>. The present tomb, however, is not older than the late 13th century.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Browne Willis also considered the "Saint Afran" honored at <a href="/wiki/Llantrisant,_Anglesey" title="Llantrisant, Anglesey">Llantrisant</a> on <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Anglesey" class="mw-redirect" title="Isle of Anglesey">Anglesey</a> to be a corruption of Afan.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
