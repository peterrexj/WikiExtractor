<p><b>Ambrose of Sienna</b> was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> teacher, missionary and diplomat.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ambrose_of_Siena&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ambrose was born at <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a> on 16 April 1220, to the noble family of Sansedoni.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When he was around a year old, Ambrose was cured of a congenital deformity, in the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> church of St. <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>. As a child and youth he was noted for his love of charity, exercised especially towards pilgrims, the sick in hospitals, and prisoners. He entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the Dominican convent in his native city at the age of seventeen, was sent to Paris to continue his philosophical and theological studies under <a href="/wiki/Albert_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Albert the Great">Albert the Great</a> and had for a fellow-student there, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1248 he was sent with Thomas to <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>, where he taught in the Dominican schools. In 1260 he was one of the band of missionaries who evangelized <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>. Six years later Sienna was put under an <a href="/wiki/Interdict" title="Interdict">interdict</a> for having espoused the cause of the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Frederick_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Frederick II">Emperor Frederick II</a>, then at enmity with the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. The Siennese petitioned Ambrose to plead their cause before the Sovereign Pontiff, and so successfully did he do this that he obtained for his native city full pardon and a renewal of all her privileges.<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The Siennese soon cast off their allegiance; a second time Ambrose obtained pardon for them. He brought about a reconciliation between King <a href="/wiki/Conradin" title="Conradin">Conradin</a> of Germany and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_IV" title="Pope Clement IV">Pope Clement IV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around this time he was chosen bishop of his native city, but he declined the office. For a time, he devoted himself to preaching the <a href="/wiki/Eighth_Crusade" title="Eighth Crusade">Eighth Crusade</a>; and later, at the request of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a>, caused the studies which the late wars had practically suspended to be resumed in the Dominican convent at Rome. After the death of Pope Gregory X, he retired to one of the convents of his order, whence he was summoned by <a href="/wiki/Innocent_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent V">Innocent V</a> and sent as <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> to <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. He restored peace there between <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a><sup id="cite_ref-ncd_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and also between the <a href="/wiki/Doge" title="Doge">dogal</a> republics of <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> and <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, another pair of commercial rivals within Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died at Sienna, in 1286. His name was inserted in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> in 1577. His biographers exhibit his life as one of perfect humility. He loved poetry.
</p><p>He was a renowned <a href="/wiki/Preacher" title="Preacher">preacher</a>. His oratory, simple rather than elegant, was most convincing and effective. His sermons, although once collected, are not extant.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
