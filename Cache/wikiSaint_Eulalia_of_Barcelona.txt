<p><b>Eulalia</b> (c. 289 – February 12, 303), co-<a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>, was a 13-year-old <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" title="Early Christianity">Roman Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in Barcelona during the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians#Persecution_from_the_2nd_century_to_Constantine" title="Persecution of Christians">persecution of Christians</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">reign of emperor Diocletian</a> (although the <a href="/wiki/Sequence_of_Saint_Eulalia" title="Sequence of Saint Eulalia">Sequence of Saint Eulalia</a> mentions the "pagan king" <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>). There is some dispute as to whether she is the same person as <a href="/wiki/Eulalia_of_M%C3%A9rida" title="Eulalia of Mérida">Eulalia of Mérida</a>, whose story is similar.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eulalia_of_Barcelona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg/220px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="366" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg/330px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg/440px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Saint_Eulalia_of_Barcelona_by_Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Benavarre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4593" data-file-height="7643" /></a><figcaption>Painting of Eulalia with the X-shaped "cross saltire" in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona_Cathedral" title="Barcelona Cathedral">Barcelona Cathedral</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg/325px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg" decoding="async" width="325" height="268" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg/488px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg/650px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crucifixion_of_St._Eulalia_by_Pedro_Villar.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5314" data-file-height="4379" /></a><figcaption>Relief of Eulalia in Barcelona Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<p>Eulalia, age thirteen, was the daughter of a noble family that lived near the city of Barcelona. Amid the persecutions under Diocletian, governor <a href="/wiki/Dacian_(prefect)" title="Dacian (prefect)">Dacian</a> arrived in the city intent on enforcing the decrees. Sometime later, Eulalia left her home, entered the city and confronted the governor for his merciless persecution of Christians. Unable to dismiss the eloquent arguments of a young girl, Dacian soon had Eulalia stripped and <a href="/wiki/Flagellation" title="Flagellation">flagellated</a>, which was followed by bloodier <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortures</a> that were not to cease unless she admitted the error of her ways. Resisting to the end, she prayed that God would take her to Heaven, and died of her wounds.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A dove is supposed to have flown forth from her mouth following her death. Then, a sudden snowstorm covered her body like a garment.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is traditionally believed that her tortures culminated in her crucifixion on an <a href="/wiki/X-shaped_cross" class="mw-redirect" title="X-shaped cross">X-shaped cross</a>, and she is depicted with this cross as the instrument of her martyrdom. However, it has been posited that she was instead publicly tortured to death on an X frame and her body left on display, artistic depictions of this leading to the later belief that she was crucified.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg/220px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="178" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg/330px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg/440px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Crypt_of_Santa_Eulalia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="8410" data-file-height="6793" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Saint Eulalia in the crypt of Barcelona Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eulalia_of_Barcelona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are a number of similarities with the description of the martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Eulalia_of_M%C3%A9rida" title="Eulalia of Mérida">Eulalia of Mérida</a>.
</p><p>Her body was originally interred in the church of Santa Maria de les Arenes (St. Mary of the Sands; now <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_del_Mar_(Barcelona)" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Maria del Mar (Barcelona)">Santa Maria del Mar</a>, St. Mary of the Sea). It was hidden in 713 during the <a href="/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania" class="mw-redirect" title="Umayyad conquest of Hispania">Moorish invasion</a>, and only recovered in 878. In 1339, it was relocated to an <a href="/wiki/Alabaster" title="Alabaster">alabaster</a> <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> of the newly built <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Santa_Eulalia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Santa Eulalia">Cathedral of Santa Eulalia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The festival of Saint Eulalia is held in Barcelona for a week around her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on February 12.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eulalia is commemorated with statues and street names throughout Barcelona.<sup id="cite_ref-vazquez_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vazquez-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> For example, Eulalia is traditionally believed to have been placed in a barrel with shards of glass and rolled down the street named <i>Baixada de Santa Eulàlia</i> ("Saint Eulalia's descent").<sup id="cite_ref-vazquez_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vazquez-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
