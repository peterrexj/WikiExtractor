<p><b>Saint Hybald</b> (<a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl.</a> c. 664 &#8211; c. 690),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;nb 1&#93;</a></sup> also known as <b>Higbald</b>, <b>Hibald</b> or <b>Hygbald</b>, was a 7th-century <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Namesdays" class="mw-redirect" title="Namesdays">feastdays</a> are 18 September and 14 December (Orthodox).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_legacy">Life and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hybald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Venerable_Bede" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable Bede">Venerable Bede</a>, in his <i>Ecclesiastical History</i>, describes St Hybald as a "most holy and continent man who was an abbot in Lindsey".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It is conjectured, in the <i>Dictionary of Christian Biography</i> (1877–87), that this is the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbey at <a href="/wiki/Bardney_Abbey" title="Bardney Abbey">Bardney</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer2011p219_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer2011p219-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> then in the old <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lindsey" title="Kingdom of Lindsey">Kingdom of Lindsey</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>.
</p><p>In 679, <a href="/wiki/Osthryth" title="Osthryth">Osthryth</a>, queen of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>, sought to move the remains of her uncle, <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria" title="Oswald of Northumbria">St Oswald</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Bardney_Abbey" title="Bardney Abbey">Bardney</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but the monks refused to accept the body because Oswald, as king of Northumbria, had once conquered Lindsey. The remains were locked outside the abbey but the appearance of a mysterious beam of light, that night, led the monks to reconsider.<sup id="cite_ref-Bede_iii.11_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede_iii.11-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hybald was also a friend of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Chad" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Chad">Saint Chad</a>, and, had a prophetic vision of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer2011p219_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer2011p219-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He later, followed Chad's example and became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>.
</p><p>Hybald died around 690,<sup id="cite_ref-BunsonBunson2003_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BunsonBunson2003-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in the village of <a href="/wiki/Hibaldstow" title="Hibaldstow">Hibaldstow</a>, whose name means <i>place where St Hygbald is buried</i>. Following his <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">canonisation</a>, a shrine was built near his grave to hold his relics, and became a place of pilgrimage. This continued until the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a> when the shrine was destroyed. Hybald's body remained undisturbed until it was rediscovered in 1864, when the, then, dilapidated church was rebuilt.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;nb 2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In addition to Hibaldstow, three Lincolnshire churches are dedicated to Hybald at <a href="/wiki/Ashby_de_la_Launde" title="Ashby de la Launde">Ashby de la Launde</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manton,_North_Lincolnshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Manton, North Lincolnshire">Manton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Scawby" title="Scawby">Scawby</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer2011_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer2011-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
