					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_(Bishop_of_M%C3%A9rida)&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Fidelis (Bishop of Mérida)">Fidelis (Bishop of Mérida)</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG/220px-CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="176" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG/330px-CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG/440px-CatedraldeM%C3%A9rida.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3064" data-file-height="2448" /></a><figcaption>Fidelis (bishop of Mérida)</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Fidelis</b> was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_M%C3%A9rida" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Mérida">Bishop of Mérida</a> flourishing probably in the 550s and 560s.
</p><p>Fidelis was a <a href="/wiki/Greek_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek people">Greek</a> who was hired out by his parents to a merchant venture to Spain in the mid sixth century, arriving in <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain" title="Mérida, Spain">Mérida</a>, where his mother's brother, <a href="/wiki/Paul,_Bishop_of_M%C3%A9rida" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul, Bishop of Mérida">Paul</a>, was bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> As it was customary of merchants to greet the bishop with gifts upon their arrival, it is not surprising that Paul discovered his nephew on one of these trading missions.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paul had amassed a large fortune which by law he was obligated to give to the diocese, but which he kept for himself. He consecrated Fidelis as his successor and forced the clergy to accept this by threatening to bequeath his wealth to others if Fidelis was not his successor.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Fidelis was willed the private wealth and when public opposition tried to force him to abandon taking up his see, he threatened to remove his inherited wealth. He was consequently accepted as bishop.
</p><p>With the immense wealth he had at his disposal and Mérida the richest diocese in Spain, Fidelis went about repairing the buildings of the city, restoring the collapsed episcopal palace &#8212; and decorating lavishly with marble &#8212; and reconstructing the basilica of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eulalia_of_M%C3%A9rida" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Eulalia of Mérida">Saint Eulalia</a> with two added towers.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The foundations of Fidelis' church are full preserved and have been uncovered by archaeologists today.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fidelis bequeathed all his inherited wealth to the church on his death,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and he returned any unredeemed bonds to his debtors on his deathbed.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Vitas_Patrum_Emeritensium" title="Vitas Patrum Emeritensium">Vitas Patrum Emeritensium</a></i> records, in a fashion borrowed from the <i>Dialogues</i> of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, the visions Fidelis saw on his deathbed.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The tradition of the bishop as a source of credit as well as the ambitious building programme were continued by Fidelis' successor, <a href="/wiki/Masona" title="Masona">Masona</a>.
</p>
