<p><b>Nicholas</b> is a male <a href="/wiki/Given_name" title="Given name">given name</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Anglophone" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglophone">Anglophone</a> version of an ancient Greek name in use since antiquity, and cognate with the modern Greek <span title="Greek-language text"><span lang="el">Νικόλαος</span></span>, <span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn"><a href="/wiki/Nikolaos" title="Nikolaos">Nikolaos</a></i></span>. It originally derived from a combination of two <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Greek</a> words meaning 'victory' and 'people'. 
</p><p>The name has been widely used in countries with significant Christian populations, owing in part to the veneration of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas" title="Saint Nicholas">Saint Nicholas</a>, which became increasingly prominent in Western Europe from the 11th century. Revered as a saint in many Christian denominations, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican Church">Anglican Churches</a> all celebrate <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas_Day" title="Saint Nicholas Day">Saint Nicholas Day</a> on December 6. In maritime regions throughout Europe, the name and its derivatives have been especially popular, as St Nicholas is considered the protector saint of seafarers. This remains particularly so in <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>, where St Nicholas is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Hellenic_Navy" title="Hellenic Navy">Hellenic Navy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origins">Origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name derives from the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Νῑκόλᾱος</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek" class="mw-redirect" title="Romanization of Ancient Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Ancient Greek-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">Nikólaos</i></span>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It is understood to mean 'victory of the people', being a compound of two Greek words, <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">νίκη</span></span>, <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><i lang="grc-Latn">nī́kē</i></span> 'victory'<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">λᾱός</span></span>, <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><i lang="grc-Latn">lāós</i></span> 'people'.<sup id="cite_ref-laos_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-laos-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> An ancient<sup id="cite_ref-laos_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-laos-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Paretymology" class="mw-redirect" title="Paretymology">paretymology</a> (a false etymology) of the latter element, <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">λᾱός</span></span>, is that it originates from <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">λᾶς</span></span>, <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">lâs</i></span> (a <a href="/wiki/Synaeresis" title="Synaeresis">contracted form</a> of <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">λᾶας</span></span>, <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">lâas</i></span>), meaning 'stone' or 'rock'.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This is in reference to the story of <a href="/wiki/Deucalion" title="Deucalion">Deucalion</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pyrrha" title="Pyrrha">Pyrrha</a> from <a href="/wiki/Greek_mythology" title="Greek mythology">Greek mythology</a>. As the sole survivors of <a href="/wiki/Deluge_myth" class="mw-redirect" title="Deluge myth">a catastrophic deluge</a>, they were able to repopulate the world by throwing stones behind them, over their shoulders, while they kept marching on. The stones formed men and women where they landed.
</p><p>The name became popular through <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas" title="Saint Nicholas">Saint Nicholas</a>, Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Myra" title="Myra">Myra</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lycia" title="Lycia">Lycia</a>, the inspiration for <a href="/wiki/Santa_Claus" title="Santa Claus">Santa Claus</a>, but it predates said bishop by several centuries: the Athenian historian <a href="/wiki/Thucydides" title="Thucydides">Thucydides</a> for example, mentions that in the second year of the <a href="/wiki/Peloponnesian_War" title="Peloponnesian War">Peloponnesian War</a> (431–404 BC) between Sparta and Athens, the Spartans sent a delegation to the Persian king to ask for his help to fight the Athenians; a certain Nikolaos was one of the delegates.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The customary English spelling <i>Nicholas</i>, using a <i><a href="/wiki/Ch_(digraph)" title="Ch (digraph)">ch</a></i>, as though the word were spelled in Greek with a <a href="/wiki/Chi_(letter)" title="Chi (letter)">chi</a>, first came into use in the 12th century and has been firmly established since the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a>, although the spelling <i>Nicolas</i> is occasionally used.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>In Scotland, until the late nineteenth century, fishing communities used Nicholas as a female name.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
