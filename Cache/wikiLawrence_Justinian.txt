<p><b>Lawrence Justinian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Lorenzo Giustiniani</i>, 1 July 1381 – 8 January 1456) was a <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Venetian</a> Catholic priest and <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> who became the first <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice" title="Patriarch of Venice">Patriarch of Venice</a>. He is venerated as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lawrence_Justinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lawrence Justinian was a member of the well-known <a href="/wiki/Giustiniani" title="Giustiniani">Giustiniani</a> family, which includes several saints. The piety of his mother seems to have served as an inspiration for his own spirituality, as he chose a life of prayer and service. In 1404, after he had been <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, at the suggestion of an uncle who was a priest, he joined a community of <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a> following a monastic form of life on the island of <a href="/wiki/San_Giorgio_in_Alga" title="San Giorgio in Alga">San Giorgio in Alga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
He was admired by his fellows for his poverty, mortification, and fervency of prayer. Two years after his ordination to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priesthood">Catholic priesthood</a> in 1407, the community accepted the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Augustine" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Augustine">Rule of St. Augustine</a>. He was chosen to be the first <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lawrence promoted the Constitutions which had been established for the <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular">Canons Regular</a> of St. George, which was embraced by other communities of Canons in the region and shortly thereafter he became the <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">Prior</a> General of a Congregation. He was so zealous in spreading it that he was looked upon as if he were the actual founder of the Order.
</p><p>In 1433, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a>, one of the founders of the Monastery of San Giorgio,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> named Gustiniani as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Castello,_Venice" title="Castello, Venice">Castello</a>. He found a diocese in shambles and his administration was marked by considerable growth and reform. In 1451, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V" title="Pope Nicholas V">Pope Nicholas V</a> united the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Castello" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Castello">Diocese of Castello</a> with the Patriarchate of <a href="/wiki/Grado,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Grado, Italy">Grado</a>, and the seat of the patriarchate was moved to Venice, making Giustiniani the first Patriarch of Venice, a post that he held for over four years.
</p><p>It was during Lawrence's rule that <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> fell to <a href="/wiki/Muslim" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim">Muslim</a> forces. Due to their centuries of close trading partnerships with <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>, the people of Venice were in panic as to their future. He took a leading role in helping the Republic to deal with the crisis, working with the Senate to help chart its future, as well as with the clergy and people to calm them.<sup id="cite_ref-SB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>He died on January 8, 1456, and was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a> (1689–1691). His works, consisting of <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a>, letters and <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> treatises, have been frequently reprinted.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was proclaimed saint on October 16, 1690 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a>, at the presence of Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Ottoboni_(cardinal)" title="Pietro Ottoboni (cardinal)">Pietro Ottoboni</a> as procurator, assisted by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Gaspare_Carpegna" title="Gaspare Carpegna">Carpegna</a>, and of Abbot Pier Santi Fonti in quality of <a href="/wiki/Master_of_ceremonies" title="Master of ceremonies">master of ceremonies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a> (1691–1700) inserted his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration on 5 September, the anniversary of his elevation to the episcopate. Because of the saint's limited importance worldwide, his liturgical celebration is no longer included in the General Roman Calendar,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and his feast was moved to 8 January, the day of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
