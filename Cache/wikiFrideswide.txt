<p><b>Frithuswith</b>, commonly <b>Frideswide</b> (c. 650&#160;&#8211;&#32;19 October 727; <a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Friðuswīþ</i></span>), was an English princess and <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She is credited as the foundress of a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> later incorporated into <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair1988_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair1988-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was the daughter of a sub-king of a <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a> named <a href="/wiki/Dida_of_Eynsham" title="Dida of Eynsham">Dida of Eynsham</a> whose lands occupied western <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a> and the upper reaches of the <a href="/wiki/River_Thames" title="River Thames">River Thames</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair1987_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair1987-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frithuswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The earliest narrative of the saint's life is the <i>Life of Saint Frideswide</i> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vita sancte Frideswide</i>) preserved in a manuscript from the early twelfth century, copied in the hand of <a href="/wiki/John_of_Worcester" title="John of Worcester">John of Worcester</a>. A longer adaptation of this work is attributed to <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Cricklade" title="Robert of Cricklade">Robert of Cricklade</a>, head of the <a href="/wiki/Priory_of_St_Frideswide,_Oxford" class="mw-redirect" title="Priory of St Frideswide, Oxford">Priory of St Frideswide, Oxford</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair1987_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair1987-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The story recounts that Frideswide was born to <a href="/wiki/Dida_of_Eynsham" title="Dida of Eynsham">King Didan</a> and his wife Safrida. She founds a monastery with her father's assistance while still young. Her parents die soon after. Algar, king of <a href="/wiki/Leicester" title="Leicester">Leicester</a> (<a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelbald_of_Mercia" title="Æthelbald of Mercia">Æthelbald of Mercia</a>) seeks to marry her in spite of her vow of <a href="/wiki/Celibacy" title="Celibacy">celibacy</a>. When she refuses him, Algar attempts to abduct her, and Frideswide flees into the wilderness. On fleeing, she finds a ship sent by God which takes her to <a href="/wiki/Bampton,_Oxfordshire" title="Bampton, Oxfordshire">Bampton, Oxfordshire</a>. Algar searches for her in Oxford, but the people refuse to tell him where she is, and he is struck blind.
</p><p>Frideswide later seeks greater solitude and migrates to <a href="/wiki/Binsey,_Oxfordshire" title="Binsey, Oxfordshire">Binsey, Oxfordshire</a>. To avoid having to fetch water from the distant <a href="/wiki/River_Thames" title="River Thames">River Thames</a>, she prays to God and a well springs up. The well water has healing properties and many people come to seek it out. A nineteenth-century reconstruction of this well can be found at the Church of <a href="/wiki/Margaret_the_Virgin" title="Margaret the Virgin">Saint Margaret</a> in Binsey. She later returns to Oxford and remains abbess until her death.
</p><p>Two <a href="/wiki/Middle_English" title="Middle English">Middle English</a> adaptations of the Life of Frideswide are included in the <i><a href="/wiki/South_English_legendaries" title="South English legendaries">South English legendaries</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> These include several minor variants on the narrative.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_priory">The priory</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frithuswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The priory">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Priory_of_St_Frideswide,_Oxford" class="mw-redirect" title="Priory of St Frideswide, Oxford">Priory of St Frideswide, Oxford</a></div>
<p>St Frideswide's Priory, a medieval <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> house (some of the buildings of which were incorporated into <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a> following the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries" title="Dissolution of the monasteries">dissolution of the monasteries</a>) is claimed to be the site of her abbey and <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>. From early times the abbey appears to have been an important landowner in the area; however, it was destroyed in 1002 during the events of the <a href="/wiki/St._Brice%27s_Day_massacre" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Brice&#39;s Day massacre">St. Brice's Day massacre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair1988_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair1988-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A shrine was kept at the abbey in Frithuswith's honour; later a monastery was built there for <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1180, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> <a href="/wiki/Richard_of_Dover" title="Richard of Dover">Richard of Dover</a> translated Frithuswith's remains to a new shrine in the monastery church, an event that was attended by King <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II of England</a>.  The later history of the monastery was chequered, but it remained sufficiently prominent that <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Aragon" title="Catherine of Aragon">Catherine of Aragon</a> visited the shrine during her final pregnancy.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The priory seal, designed in the late 1180s, depicts Frideswide with a <a href="/wiki/Lily" class="mw-redirect" title="Lily">lily</a> and a set of <a href="/wiki/Wax_tablets" class="mw-redirect" title="Wax tablets">wax tablets</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Henry_Chichele" title="Henry Chichele">Henry Chichele</a>, the archbishop of Canterbury, officially declared Frideswide the patron saint of Oxford and the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">University of Oxford</a> in 1440. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 19 October, the traditional day of her death; the date of her translation is commemorated on 12 February; and the invention (discovery) of her relics on 15 May.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair1987_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair1987-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The shrine was repeatedly vandalized during the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">Dissolution of the Monasteries</a> and beyond.  In 1546 the monastery church became (and still remains) the cathedral church for the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Oxford" title="Diocese of Oxford">diocese of Oxford</a>.  Her shrine was reinstated by <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Queen Mary</a> in 1558, but was later desecrated by <a href="/wiki/James_Calfhill" title="James Calfhill">James Calfhill</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinist</a> canon of the church, who was intent on suppressing her cult.  As a result, Frithuswith's remains were mixed with those of <a href="/wiki/Catherine_Dammartin" title="Catherine Dammartin">Catherine Dammartin</a>, wife of <a href="/wiki/Peter_Martyr_Vermigli" title="Peter Martyr Vermigli">Peter Martyr Vermigli</a>, and they remain so to this day.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_modern_tradition">In modern tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frithuswith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In modern tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Frideswide remains the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Oxford and its university, and there is a revived tradition of pilgrimages to Christ Church.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In later art, she is depicted holding the pastoral staff of an <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> with a fountain springing up near her and an ox at her feet. She appears in medieval stained glass, and in Pre-Raphaelite stained glass by <a href="/wiki/Edward_Burne-Jones" title="Edward Burne-Jones">Edward Burne-Jones</a> in Christ Church Cathedral, Oxford, in the chapel where her shrine is also located.
</p>
