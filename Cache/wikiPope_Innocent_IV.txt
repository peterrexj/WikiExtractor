<p><b>Pope Innocent IV</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Innocentius IV</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1195</span> – 7 December 1254), born <b>Sinibaldo Fieschi</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 25 June 1243 to his death in 1254.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fieschi was born in Genoa and studied at the universities of <a href="/wiki/University_of_Parma" title="University of Parma">Parma</a> and <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">Bologna</a>. He was considered in his own day and by posterity as a fine canonist. On the strength of this reputation, he was called to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">Roman Curia</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a> made him a cardinal and appointed him governor of the Ancona in 1235. Fieschi was elected pope in 1243 and took the name Innocent IV. He inherited an ongoing dispute over lands seized by the Holy Roman Emperor, and the following year he traveled to France to escape imperial plots against him in Rome. He returned to Rome in 1250 after the death of the <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Frederick II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg/220px-Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg/330px-Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg/440px-Plaque_pope_Innocentius_IV_in_Manarola.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2816" data-file-height="2112" /></a><figcaption>Square in Manarola named for Innocent IV</figcaption></figure>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> (although some sources say <a href="/wiki/Manarola" title="Manarola">Manarola</a>) in an unknown year, Sinibaldo was the son of Beatrice Grillo and Ugo Fieschi, Count of <a href="/wiki/Lavagna" title="Lavagna">Lavagna</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Fieschi" class="mw-redirect" title="Fieschi">Fieschi</a> were a noble merchant family of <a href="/wiki/Liguria" title="Liguria">Liguria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Sinibaldo received his education at the universities of <a href="/wiki/University_of_Parma" title="University of Parma">Parma</a> and <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">Bologna</a> and may have taught <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a>, for a time, at Bologna.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The fact is disputed, though, as others pointed out, there is no documentary evidence of his teaching position.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> From 1216 to 1227 he was a canon of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Parma" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Parma">Cathedral of Parma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was considered one of the best <a href="/wiki/Canon_lawyer" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon lawyer">canonists</a> of his time,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He wrote the <i>Apparatus in quinque libros decretalium</i>, a commentary on papal decrees. He was called to serve <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">Roman Curia</a> where he rapidly rose though the hierarchy. He was  <i>Auditor causarum</i>, from 11 November 1226 to 30 May 1227.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was then quickly promoted to the office of <a href="/wiki/Vice-Chancellor_of_the_Holy_Roman_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Vice-Chancellor of the Holy Roman Church">Vice-Chancellor of the Holy Roman Church</a> (from 31 May to 23 September 1227), though he retained the office and the title for a time after he was named Cardinal.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinal">Cardinal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cardinal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While vice-Chancellor, Fieschi was soon created <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Priest">Cardinal-Priest</a> of <a href="/wiki/San_Lorenzo_in_Lucina" title="San Lorenzo in Lucina">San Lorenzo in Lucina</a> on 18 September 1227 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a>  (1227–1241).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He later served as papal governor of the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Ancona" title="March of Ancona">March of Ancona</a>, from 17 October 1235<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> until 1240.
</p><p>Sources from the 17th century onwards reported that he became <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Albenga" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Albenga">Bishop of Albenga</a> in 1235,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> but  later sources disputed this claim. There is no attestation of this in any of the contemporary sources while there is evidence that the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Albenga" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Albenga">see of Albenga</a> was occupied by a certain Bishop Simon from 1230 until 1255.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Innocent's immediate predecessor was <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_IV" title="Pope Celestine IV">Pope Celestine IV</a>, elected on 25 October 1241, whose reign lasted only fifteen days. The events of Innocent IV's pontificate are therefore inextricably linked to the policies dominating the reigns of popes <a href="/wiki/Innocent_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent III">Innocent III</a>, <a href="/wiki/Honorius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Honorius III">Honorius III</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a> had demanded the return of lands belonging to the <a href="/wiki/Papal_State" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal State">Papal States</a> which had been seized by the <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Frederick II</a>. The Pope had called a general council to seek the deposing of the emperor with the support of Europe's Church leaders. However, hoping to intimidate the Curia, Frederick had seized two cardinals traveling to the council. Being incarcerated, the two missed the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave" title="Papal conclave">conclave</a> which quickly elected <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_IV" title="Pope Celestine IV">Celestine IV</a>. The conclave reconvened after Celestine's death split into factions supporting contrasting policies about how to treat the Emperor.
</p>
<h2><span id="New_pope.2C_same_emperor"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="New_pope,_same_emperor">New pope, same emperor</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: New pope, same emperor">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After a year and a half of contentious debate and coercion, the <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1243" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1243">papal conclave</a> finally reached a unanimous decision. The choice fell upon Cardinal Sinibaldo  de' Fieschi, who very reluctantly accepted election as Pope on 25 June 1243, taking the name of Innocent IV.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> As a cardinal, Sinibaldo had been on friendly terms with Frederick, even after the latter's excommunication. The Emperor greatly admired the cardinal's wisdom, having enjoyed discussions with him from time to time.
</p><p>Following the election, the witty Frederick remarked that he had lost the friendship of a cardinal but gained the enmity of a pope.
</p><p>His jest notwithstanding, Frederick's letter to the new pontiff was respectful, offering congratulations to the new Pope and wishing him success. It also expressed hope for an amicable settlement of the differences between the empire and the papacy. Negotiations began shortly afterwards but were not successful. Innocent refused to back down from his demands and Frederick refused to acquiesce.  The dispute continued mostly about the restitution of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Patrimony_of_St_Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrimony of St Peter">Patrimony of St Peter</a>.
</p><p>The Emperor's machinations aroused a good deal of anti-papal feelings in Italy, particularly in the Papal States, and imperial agents encouraged plots against papal rule. Realizing to be increasingly unsafe in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, Innocent IV secretly and hurriedly withdrew, fleeing Rome on 7 June 1244.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Traveling in disguise, he made his way to <a href="/wiki/Sutri" title="Sutri">Sutri</a> and then to the port of <a href="/wiki/Civitavecchia" title="Civitavecchia">Civitavecchia</a>, and from there to <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, his birthplace, where he arrived on 7 July. On 5 October, he fled from there to <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, where he was joyously welcomed. Making his way to <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>, where he arrived on 29 November 1244, Innocent was greeted cordially by the magistrates of the city.
</p><p>Innocent was now safe and out of the reach of Frederick II. In a sermon on 27 December 1244, he summoned as many bishops as could get to Lyon (140 bishops eventually came) to attend what became the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Lyon" title="First Council of Lyon">13th General (Ecumenical) Council</a> of the Church, the first to be held in Lyon.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The bishops met for three public sessions: 28 June, 5 July, and 17 July 1245. Their principal purpose was to win over the <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Frederick II</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="First_Council_of_Lyon">First Council of Lyon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: First Council of Lyon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Excommunication.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Excommunication.jpg/220px-Excommunication.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="157" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Excommunication.jpg/330px-Excommunication.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Excommunication.jpg/440px-Excommunication.jpg 2x" data-file-width="663" data-file-height="473" /></a><figcaption>14th century miniature depicting the excommunication <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Frederick_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Frederick II">Emperor Frederick II</a> by Pope Innocent IV</figcaption></figure>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Ad_Apostolicae_Dignitatis_Apicem" class="mw-redirect" title="Ad Apostolicae Dignitatis Apicem">Ad Apostolicae Dignitatis Apicem</a></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Lyon" title="First Council of Lyon">First Council of Lyon</a> of 1245 had the fewest participants of any previous General Council. However, three patriarchs and the Latin emperor of Constantinople attended, along with about 150 bishops, most of them prelates from France and Spain. They came quickly, and Innocent could rely on their help. Bishops from the rest of Europe outside Spain and France feared retribution from Frederick, while many other bishops were prevented from attending either by the invasions of the <a href="/wiki/Mongols" title="Mongols">Mongols</a> (Tartars) in the Far East or Muslim incursions in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Belgorod" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Belgorod">bishop of Belgorod</a> in Russia, Peter, attended and provided information on the Mongols via the <i><a href="/wiki/Tractatus_de_ortu_Tartarorum" title="Tractatus de ortu Tartarorum">Tractatus de ortu Tartarorum</a></i>.
</p><p>During the session, Frederick II's position was defended by <a href="/wiki/Taddeo_of_Suessa" class="mw-redirect" title="Taddeo of Suessa">Taddeo of Suessa</a>, who renewed in his master's name all the promises made before, but refused to give the guarantees the pope demanded. The council ended on 17 July with the fathers solemnly deposing and excommunicating the Emperor, while absolving all his subjects from their allegiance.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="After_Lyon">After Lyon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: After Lyon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The council's acts inflamed the political conflict across Europe. The tension subsided only with Frederick's death in December 1250: this removed the threat to Innocent's life and allowed his return to Italy. He departed Lyon on 19 April 1251 and arrived in Genoa on 18 May. On 1 July, he was in Milan, accompanied by only three cardinals and the Latin Patriarch of Constantinople. He stayed there until mid-September, when he began an inspection tour of Lombardy, heading for Bologna. On 5 November he reached Perugia. From 1251–53 the Pope stayed at <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> until it was safe for him to bring the papal court back to Rome. He finally saw Rome again in the first week of October, 1253. He left Rome on 27 April 1254, for Assisi and then Anagni. He immediately dealt with the succession to the possessions of Frederick II, both as German Emperor and as King of Sicily. In both instances, Innocent continued Pope Gregory IX's policy of opposition to the Hohenstaufen, supporting whatever opposing party  could be found. This policy embroiled Italy in one conflict after another for the next three decades. Innocent IV himself, following the papal army which was seeking to destroy Frederick's son Manfred, died in Naples on 7 December 1254.
</p><p>While in Perugia, on 15 May 1252, Innocent IV issued the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Ad_extirpanda" title="Ad extirpanda">Ad extirpanda</a></i>, composed of thirty-eight 'laws'. He advised civil authorities in Italy to treat heretics as criminals, and imposed limits on the use of torture to compel disclosures "as thieves and robbers of material goods are made to accuse their accomplices and confess the crimes they have committed."<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ruler_of_princes_and_kings">Ruler of princes and kings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Ruler of princes and kings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As Innocent III had before him, Innocent IV saw himself as the Vicar of Christ, whose power was above earthly kings. Innocent, therefore, had no objection to intervening in purely secular matters. He appointed <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso III of Portugal">Afonso III</a> administrator of Portugal, and lent his protection to <a href="/wiki/Ottokar_II_of_Bohemia" title="Ottokar II of Bohemia">Ottokar</a>, the son of the King of <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>. The Pope even sided with King Henry III against both nobles and bishops of England, despite the king's harassment of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Rich" class="mw-redirect" title="Edmund Rich">Edmund Rich</a>, the Archbishop of Canterbury and Primate of All England, and the royal policy of having the income of a vacant bishopric or benefice delivered to the royal coffers, rather than handed over to a papal Administrator (usually a member of the Curia) or a Papal collector of revenue, or delivered directly to the Pope.
</p><p>In the case of the Mongols, too, Innocent maintained that he, as Vicar of Christ, could make non-Christians accept his dominion and even exact punishment should they violate the non-God centred commands of the Ten Commandments. This policy was held more in theory than in practice and was eventually repudiated centuries later.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Northern_Crusades">Northern Crusades</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Northern Crusades">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Shortly after Innocent IV's election to the papacy, the <a href="/wiki/Teutonic_Order" title="Teutonic Order">Teutonic Order</a> sought his consent for the suppression of the Prussian rebellion and for their struggle against the Lithuanians. In response the Pope issued on 23 September 1243 the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Qui_iustis_causis" title="Qui iustis causis">Qui iustis causis</a></i>,  authorizing <a href="/wiki/Northern_Crusades" title="Northern Crusades">crusades</a> in <a href="/wiki/Livonia" title="Livonia">Livonia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prussia" title="Prussia">Prussia</a>. The bull was reissued by Innocent and his successors in October 1243, March 1256, August 1256 and August 1257.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Vicar_of_Christ">Vicar of Christ</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Vicar of Christ">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Vicar_of_Christ" title="Vicar of Christ">Vicar of Christ</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Medieval_,_Papal_bulla_(FindID_610950).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Medieval_%2C_Papal_bulla_%28FindID_610950%29.jpg/220px-Medieval_%2C_Papal_bulla_%28FindID_610950%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="113" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Medieval_%2C_Papal_bulla_%28FindID_610950%29.jpg/330px-Medieval_%2C_Papal_bulla_%28FindID_610950%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Medieval_%2C_Papal_bulla_%28FindID_610950%29.jpg/440px-Medieval_%2C_Papal_bulla_%28FindID_610950%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3854" data-file-height="1988" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Papal_bulla" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal bulla">Papal bulla</a> of Innocent IV</figcaption></figure>
<p>The papal preoccupation with imperial matters and secular princes caused other matters to suffer. On the one hand, the internal governance of the  <a href="/wiki/Papal_State" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal State">Papal States</a> was neglected. Taxation increased in proportion to the discontent of the inhabitants.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
On the other hand, the spiritual condition of the Church raised concerns. Innocent attempted to give attention to the latter through a number of interventions.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonizations">Canonizations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Canonizations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1246 <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Rich" class="mw-redirect" title="Edmund Rich">Edmund Rich</a>, former <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> (died 1240), was declared a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In 1250 Innocent similarly proclaimed the pious <a href="/wiki/Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland" title="Saint Margaret of Scotland">Queen Margaret</a> (died 1093), wife of King <a href="/wiki/Malcolm_III_of_Scotland" title="Malcolm III of Scotland">Malcolm III of Scotland</a>, a saint. <sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>The Dominican priest <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Verona" title="Peter of Verona">Peter of Verona</a>, martyred by <a href="/wiki/Albigensian" class="mw-redirect" title="Albigensian">Albigensian</a> heretics in 1252, was canonized, as was <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w" title="Stanislaus of Szczepanów">Stanislaus of Szczepanów</a>, the Polish <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cracow" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Cracow">Archbishop of Cracow</a>, both in 1253.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_new_Orders">The new Orders</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: The new Orders">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In August 1253, after much worry about the order's insistence on absolute poverty, Innocent finally approved the rule of the Second Order of the Franciscans, the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> nuns, founded by St. <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a>, the friend of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">St Francis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_concept_of_Persona_ficta">The concept of <i>Persona ficta</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: The concept of Persona ficta">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In a development which undoubtedly had a considerable impact on the emerging religious orders, Innocent IV is often credited with helping to create the idea of <a href="/wiki/Legal_personality" class="mw-redirect" title="Legal personality">legal personality</a>, <i>persona ficta</i> as it was originally written, which has led to the idea of corporate personhood. At the time, this allowed monasteries, universities and other bodies to act as a single legal entity, facilitating continuity in their corporate existence. Monks and friars pledged individually to poverty could be part nonetheless of an organization that could own infrastructure. Such institutions, as "fictive persons", could not be excommunicated or considered guilty of delict, that is, negligence to action that is not contractually required. This meant that punishment of individuals within an organization would reflect less on the organization itself than if the person running such an organization was said to own it rather than be a constituent of it, and hence the concept was meant to provide institutional stability.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Compromise_on_the_Talmud">Compromise on the Talmud</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Compromise on the Talmud">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Possibly prompted by the persistence of heretical movements such as the <a href="/wiki/Albigensian" class="mw-redirect" title="Albigensian">Albigensians</a>, an earlier pope, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a> (1227–1241), had issued letters on 9 June 1239, ordering all the bishops of France to confiscate all <a href="/wiki/Talmud" title="Talmud">Talmuds</a> in the possession of the Jews. Agents were to raid each synagogue on the first Saturday of Lent 1240, and seize the books, placing them in the custody of the Dominicans or the Franciscans.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The Bishop of Paris was ordered to see to it that copies of the Pope's mandate reached all the bishops of France, England, Aragon, Navarre, Castile and León, and Portugal.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> On 20 June 1239, there was another letter, addressed to the Bishop of Paris, the Prior of the Dominicans and the Minister of the Franciscans, calling for the burning of all copies of the Talmud, and any obstructionists were to be visited with ecclesiastical censures. On the same day, the Pope wrote to the King of Portugal ordering him to see to it that all copies of the Talmud be seized and turned over to the Dominicans or Franciscans.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> On account of these letters, King <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">Louis IX of France</a> held <a href="/wiki/Disputation_of_Paris" title="Disputation of Paris">a trial in Paris in 1240</a>, which ultimately found the Talmud guilty of 35 alleged charges; 24 cartloads of copies of the Talmud were burned.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Initially, Innocent IV continued Gregory IX's policy. In a letter of 9 May 1244, he wrote to King Louis IX, ordering the Talmud and any books with Talmudic glosses to be examined by the Regent Doctors of the University of Paris, and if condemned by them, to be burned.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> However, an argument was presented that this policy was a negation of the Church's traditional stance of tolerance toward Judaism. On 5 July 1247, Pope Innocent wrote to the Bishops of France and of Germany to say that because both ecclesiastics and lay persons were lawlessly plundering the property of the Jews, and falsely stating that at Eastertime they sacrificed and ate the hearts of little children, the bishops should see to it that the Jews not be attacked or molested for these or other reasons.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> That same year 1247, in a letter of 2 August to Louis IX,<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> the Pope reversed his stance on the Talmud, ordering that the Talmud should be censored rather than burned. Despite opposition from figures such as <a href="/wiki/Odo_of_Ch%C3%A2teauroux" title="Odo of Châteauroux">Odo of Châteauroux</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Cardinal Bishop of Tusculum and former Chancellor of the University of Paris, Innocent IV's policy was nonetheless continued by subsequent popes.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_the_Jews">Relations with the Jews</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Relations with the Jews">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_Synagogue#Building_Restrictions" title="Córdoba Synagogue">Córdoba Synagogue §&#160;Building Restrictions</a></div>
<p>In April 1250 (5 Iyar<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>), Innocent IV ordered the Bishop of Córdoba to take action against the Jews who were building a synagogue whose height was not acceptable to the local clergy. Documents from the reign of Pope Innocent IV recorded resentment toward a prominent new congregational synagogue:<sup id="cite_ref-Ben-Dov_pp149–161_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ben-Dov_pp149–161-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG/265px-Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG" decoding="async" width="265" height="199" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG/398px-Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG/530px-Patio_of_the_Synagogue_of_C%C3%B3rdoba.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a><figcaption>The courtyard of Córdoba Synagogue.</figcaption></figure><blockquote><p>The Jews of Cordoba are rashly presuming to build a new synagogue of unnecessary height thereby scandalizing faithful Christians, wherefore ... we command [you] ... to enforce the authority of your office against the Jews in this regard....<sup id="cite_ref-Sepharad_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sepharad-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Diplomatic_relations">Diplomatic relations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Diplomatic relations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_the_Portuguese">Relations with the Portuguese</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Relations with the Portuguese">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Innocent IV was responsible for the eventual deposition of King <a href="/wiki/Sancho_II_of_Portugal" title="Sancho II of Portugal">Sancho II of Portugal</a> at the request of his brother Afonso (later King <a href="/w/index.php?title=Afonso_III_of_Portugal)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Afonso III of Portugal) (page does not exist)">Afonso III of Portugal)</a>. One of the arguments he used against Sancho II in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">Bull</a> <i>Grandi non immerito</i> was Sancho's status as a minor upon inheriting the throne from his father Afonso II.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Contacts_with_the_Mongols">Contacts with the Mongols</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Contacts with the Mongols">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:AscelinOfCremone.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/AscelinOfCremone.jpg/220px-AscelinOfCremone.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/AscelinOfCremone.jpg/330px-AscelinOfCremone.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/AscelinOfCremone.jpg/440px-AscelinOfCremone.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1444" data-file-height="1084" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Ascelin_of_Lombardia" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascelin of Lombardia">Ascelin of Lombardia</a> receiving a letter from Pope Innocent IV, and remitting it to the Mongol general <a href="/wiki/Baiju_Noyan" title="Baiju Noyan">Baiju</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg/170px-LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="891" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg/255px-LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg/340px-LetterGuyugToInnocence.jpg 2x" data-file-width="495" data-file-height="2593" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Letter_from_G%C3%BCy%C3%BCk_Khan_to_Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Letter from Güyük Khan to Pope Innocent IV">1246 letter</a> of <a href="/wiki/G%C3%BCy%C3%BCk" class="mw-redirect" title="Güyük">Güyük</a> to Pope Innocent IV</figcaption></figure>
<p>The warlike tendencies of the Mongols also concerned the Pope, and in 1245, he issued bulls and sent a papal <a href="/wiki/Nuncio" title="Nuncio">nuncio</a> in the person of <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_da_Pian_del_Carpine" title="Giovanni da Pian del Carpine">Giovanni da Pian del Carpine</a> (accompanied by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_the_Pole" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict the Pole">Benedict the Pole</a>) to the "Emperor of the Tartars".<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Dei_patris_immensa" title="Dei patris immensa">message</a> asked the <a href="/wiki/Mongol_Empire" title="Mongol Empire">Mongol</a> ruler to become a Christian and stop his aggression against Europe. The Khan <a href="/wiki/G%C3%BCy%C3%BCk" class="mw-redirect" title="Güyük">Güyük</a> replied in 1246 in a letter written in <a href="/wiki/Persian_language" title="Persian language">Persian</a> mixed <a href="/wiki/Turkic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkic language">Turkic</a> that is still preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Library" title="Vatican Library">Vatican Library</a>, demanding the submission of the Pope and the other rulers of Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-webcache.googleusercontent.com_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webcache.googleusercontent.com-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1245 Innocent had sent another mission, through another route, led by <a href="/wiki/Ascelin_of_Lombardia" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascelin of Lombardia">Ascelin of Lombardia</a>, also bearing letters. The mission met with the Mongol ruler <a href="/wiki/Baichu" class="mw-redirect" title="Baichu">Baichu</a> near the <a href="/wiki/Caspian_Sea" title="Caspian Sea">Caspian Sea</a> in 1247. The reply of Baichu was in accordance with that of Güyük, but it was accompanied by two Mongolian envoys to the Papal seat in <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>, <a href="/wiki/A%C3%AFbeg_and_Serkis" title="Aïbeg and Serkis">Aïbeg and Serkis</a>. In the letter, Guyuk demanded that the Pope appear in person at the Mongol imperial headquarters, <a href="/wiki/Karakorum" title="Karakorum">Karakorum</a>, so that “we might cause him to hear every command that there is of the <a href="/wiki/Yassa" title="Yassa">jasaq</a>”.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> In 1248 the envoys met with Innocent, who again issued an appeal to the Mongols to stop their killing of Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-webcache.googleusercontent.com_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webcache.googleusercontent.com-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Innocent IV would also send other missions to the Mongols in 1245, including that of <a href="/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_de_Longjumeau" title="André de Longjumeau">André de Longjumeau</a> and the possibly aborted mission of <a href="/wiki/Laurent_de_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Laurent de Portugal">Laurent de Portugal</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_politics">Later politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: Later politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Despite other concerns, the later years of Innocent's life were largely directed to political schemes for encompassing the overthrow of <a href="/wiki/Manfred_of_Sicily" class="mw-redirect" title="Manfred of Sicily">Manfred of Sicily</a>, the natural son of Frederick II, whom the towns and the nobility had for the most part received as his father's successor. Innocent aimed to incorporate the whole <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily" title="Kingdom of Sicily">Kingdom of Sicily</a> into the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, but he lacked the necessary economic and political power. Therefore, after a failed agreement with <a href="/wiki/Charles_of_Anjou" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles of Anjou">Charles of Anjou</a>, he invested <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Crouchback" title="Edmund Crouchback">Edmund Crouchback</a>, the nine-year-old son of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_III_of_England" title="Henry III of England">Henry III of England</a>, with that kingdom on 14 May 1254.
</p><p>In the same year, Innocent excommunicated Frederick II's other son, <a href="/wiki/Conrad_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Conrad IV">Conrad IV, King of Germany</a>, but the latter died a few days after the investiture of Edmund. Innocent spent the spring of 1254 in Assisi and then, at the beginning of June, moved to <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_the_Popes_in_Anagni" title="Palace of the Popes in Anagni">Anagni</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> where he awaited Manfred's reaction to the event, especially considering that Conrad's heir, <a href="/wiki/Conradin" title="Conradin">Conradin</a>, had been entrusted to Papal tutelage by King Conrad's testament. Manfred submitted, although probably only to gain time and counter the menace from Edmund, and accepted the title of <a href="/wiki/Papal_vicar" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal vicar">papal vicar</a> for southern Italy. Innocent could therefore enjoy a moment in which he was the acknowledged sovereign, in theory at least, of most of the peninsula. Innocent overplayed his hand, however, by accepting the fealty of the city of <a href="/wiki/Amalfi" title="Amalfi">Amalfi</a> directly to the Papacy instead of to the Kingdom of Sicily on 23 October. Manfred immediately, on 26 October, fled from <a href="/wiki/Teano" title="Teano">Teano</a>, where he had established his headquarters, and headed to <a href="/wiki/Lucera" title="Lucera">Lucera</a> to rejoin his Saracen troops.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Manfred had not lost his nerve,<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> and organized resistance to papal aggression. Supported by his faithful Saracen troops, he began using military force to make rebellious barons and towns submit to his authority as Regent for his nephew.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Final_conflict">The Final conflict</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_IV&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: The Final conflict">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Realizing that Manfred had no intention of submitting to the Papacy or to anyone else, Innocent and his papal army headed south from <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_the_Popes_in_Anagni" title="Palace of the Popes in Anagni">his summer residence at Anagni</a> on 8 October, intending to confront Manfred's forces. On 27 October 1254 the Pope entered the city of <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>. It was there, on a sick bed, that Innocent heard of <a href="/wiki/Manfred,_King_of_Sicily#Background" title="Manfred, King of Sicily">Manfred's victory</a> at <a href="/wiki/Foggia" title="Foggia">Foggia</a> on 2 December against the Papal forces, led by the new Papal Legate, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Guglielmo_Fieschi" title="Guglielmo Fieschi">Guglielmo Fieschi</a>, the Pope's nephew.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> The tidings are said to have precipitated Pope Innocent's death on 7 December 1254 in Naples. From triumph to disaster had taken only a few months.
</p><p>Shortly after Innocent's election as pope, his nephew <a href="/wiki/Opizzo_Fieschi" title="Opizzo Fieschi">Opizzo</a> had been appointed <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarchate_of_Antioch" title="Latin Patriarchate of Antioch">Latin Patriarch of Antioch</a>. In December 1251 Innocent IV himself appointed another nephew, <a href="/wiki/Ottobuono_Fieschi" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottobuono Fieschi">Ottobuono</a>, Cardinal Deacon of S. Andriano.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Ottobuono was subsequently elected <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_V" title="Pope Adrian V">Pope Adrian V</a> in 1276.
</p><p>Upon his death, Innocent IV was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a> (Rinaldo de' Conti).
</p>
