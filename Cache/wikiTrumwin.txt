					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Trumwin&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Trumwin">Trumwin</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Bishop of the Northumbrian see of the Picts</div>
<p>

<b>Trumwine</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Trumuinus</i>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was the only ever <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a>, based at <a href="/wiki/Abercorn" title="Abercorn">Abercorn</a>.
</p><p>Trumwine was a contemporary and friend of St. Cuthbert.<sup id="cite_ref-northern_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-northern-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 681, during the reign of King <a href="/wiki/Ecgfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Ecgfrith of Northumbria">Ecgfrith of Northumbria</a>, Trumwine was appointed "Bishop of the Picts"  by <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore of Tarsus</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> ("Bishop of those Picts who were then subject to English rule", i.e. those living north of the River Forth paying tribute to Northumbria).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This was part of a more general division of the Northumbrian church by the Archbishop, who also divided the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hexham" title="Bishop of Hexham">Bishopric of Hexham</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lindisfarne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lindisfarne">Bishopric of Lindisfarne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>After the defeat and death of Ecgfrith at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Dun_Nechtain" title="Battle of Dun Nechtain">Battle of Nechtansmere</a> in 685, Trumwine and his monks fled and dispersed. He retired to the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Whitby" title="Whitby">Whitby</a>, <sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> then ruled by <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lffl%C3%A6d_of_Whitby" title="Ælfflæd of Whitby">Ælflæd</a>, Ecgfrith’s sister and <a href="/wiki/Hilda_of_Whitby" title="Hilda of Whitby">St. Hild's</a> successor.<sup id="cite_ref-northern_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-northern-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is possible that Trumwine was present at the aforementioned battle,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and certainly he would have been a valuable source of advice for Ecgfrith.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Whatever the case here, the Anglo-Saxons were defeated, expelled from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Southern_Pictland&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Southern Pictland (page does not exist)">Southern Pictland</a>, and the episcopal establishment at Abercorn was hence abandoned and the diocese ceased to exist. The territory of modern <a href="/wiki/West_Lothian" title="West Lothian">West Lothian</a> hence probably passed into the hands of the <a href="/wiki/Fortriu" title="Fortriu">Verturian</a> kings, although it is also possible that the British of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Strathclyde" title="Kingdom of Strathclyde">Strathclyde</a> took it over.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his days after 685, it is known that Trumwine interacted with <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, and Bede's <i>Life of Saint Cuthbert</i> tells us that Trumwine was used as one of its sources. Trumwine is said to have related a story about <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cuthbert" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cuthbert">Saint Cuthbert</a>'s childhood, which in turn had supposedly been told to Trumwine by Cuthbert himself.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was buried in Saint Peter's church in Whitby.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
