<p><b>Andrew Zorard</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i>Zoerárd András</i>, <i>Szórád</i>, <i>Zoárd</i>; <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i>Andrzej Świerad</i>, <i>Żurawek</i>, <i>Żórawek</i>, <i>Świrad</i>, and <i>Wszechrad</i>; <a href="/wiki/Slovak_language" title="Slovak language">Slovak</a>: <i>Svorad</i>, <i>Andrej Svorad</i>, <a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i>Sverad</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i>Zoërard</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Zoerardus</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk originating from <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> but active in <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a> (then <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Hungary</a>), who is venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Orthodoxy" title="Orthodoxy">Orthodox Churches</a>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Zorard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Zorard was born around 980 in <a href="/wiki/Opatowiec" title="Opatowiec">Opatowiec</a>, a small village in Poland. A tradition in the small village of <a href="/wiki/Tropie,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship" title="Tropie, Lesser Poland Voivodeship">Tropie</a> holds that in his youth he lived near there as a monk. At around the year 1000, at about the age of 20, he began living as a hermit and a missionary, evangelizing in <a href="/wiki/Olawa" class="mw-redirect" title="Olawa">Olawa</a>, Silesia (modern Poland). At some time, he also traveled to northern Hungary (Slovakia)
</p><p>Around the year 1003 Zorard settled in <a href="/wiki/Zobor_Abbey" title="Zobor Abbey">St. Hippolytus Monastery on Mt. Zobor</a> near <a href="/wiki/Nitra" title="Nitra">Nitra</a> - then part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary_in_the_Middle_Ages" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Hungary in the Middle Ages">Kingdom of Hungary</a>, present day <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a>. He became a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> monk in the Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-erno_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erno-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He took the name "Andrew". There he became the spiritual guide of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Szkalka" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict of Szkalka">Benedict of Skalka</a>. Zorard and Benedict, with the permission of their superior Philip, later left the monastery and became hermits in a cave along the <a href="/wiki/Vah" class="mw-redirect" title="Vah">Váh River</a> near <a href="/wiki/Skalka_nad_V%C3%A1hom" title="Skalka nad Váhom">Skalka nad Váhom</a> not far from <a href="/wiki/Tren%C4%8D%C3%ADn" title="Trenčín">Trenčín</a>. Andrew died of natural causes around 1009. He practiced such severe austerities that, according to legend, the iron chain, which he wore wrapped around the belt, eventually grew into his body.<sup id="cite_ref-erno_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-erno-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benedict continued to live in the cave for three years until he was murdered by a gang of thieves looking for treasure. In 1083 Zorard's relics were transferred to <a href="/wiki/St._Emmeram%27s_Cathedral,_Nitra" title="St. Emmeram&#39;s Cathedral, Nitra">St. Emmeram's Cathedral</a> in Nitra where they remain to this day. A biography of Benedict and Zorard was written by <a href="/wiki/Maurus_of_Nitra" class="mw-redirect" title="Maurus of Nitra">Maurus of Nitra</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_P%C3%A9cs" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Pécs">Bishop of Pécs</a>, in which it says that Zorard led a hermit life living in a small cave near the monastery. The cave has since been called <i>Svoradova</i> (Zorard's).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_Day_and_veneration">Feast Day and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Zorard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Feast Day and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:290px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg/288px-Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg" decoding="async" width="288" height="240" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg/432px-Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg/576px-Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg 2x" data-file-width="719" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Paul_Bril_and_Wenzel_Coebergher_-_Andrew_Zorard_as_a_hermit.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Andrew Zorard</i> by <a href="/wiki/Paul_Bril" title="Paul Bril">Paul Bril</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wenzel_Coebergher" class="mw-redirect" title="Wenzel Coebergher">Wenzel Coebergher</a>, 1601</div></div></div>
<p>Andrew is venerated especially in Slovakia, Hungary, and Poland, but also in diaspora communities of the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>. His feast day is 17 July, but in some calendars he is venerated together with Benedict on 13 June.
</p><p>King <a href="/wiki/G%C3%A9za_I_of_Hungary" title="Géza I of Hungary">Géza I of Hungary</a> declared him one of the patron saints of Hungary. Zorard died around 1009. As early as 1064, Géza took the first steps towards his canonization. The cult of Zorard was officially confirmed in July 1083 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>, thanks to the Hungarian King <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_I_of_Hungary" title="Ladislaus I of Hungary">Ladislaus I</a>. Zorard's remains are located in the <a href="/wiki/St._Emmeram%27s_Cathedral,_Nitra" title="St. Emmeram&#39;s Cathedral, Nitra">St. Emmeram's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nitra" title="Nitra">Nitra</a>.
</p>
