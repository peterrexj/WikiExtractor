<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anastasius_of_Persia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anastasius was born in the city of <a href="/wiki/Ray,_Iran" title="Ray, Iran">Ray</a>. He was the son of a <a href="/wiki/Magi" title="Magi"><i>magus</i></a> named Bau. He had a brother whose name is unknown. He was a cavalryman in the army of <a href="/wiki/Khosrow_II" title="Khosrow II">Khosrow II</a> (r. 590&#8211;628) and participated in the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(614)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Jerusalem (614)">capture</a> of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a> in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, which was carried to the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian" class="mw-redirect" title="Sasanian">Sasanian</a> capital <a href="/wiki/Ctesiphon" title="Ctesiphon">Ctesiphon</a>.
</p><p>The occasion prompted him to ask for information about the Christian religion. He then experienced a conversion of faith, left the army, became a Christian, and afterwards a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Mar_Saba" title="Mar Saba">monastery of Saint Savvas</a> (Mar Saba) in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Accuracy_dispute#Disputed_statement" title="Wikipedia:Accuracy dispute"><span title="The material near this tag is possibly inaccurate or nonfactual. (January 2023)">dubious</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Anastasius_of_Persia#Mar_Saba_isn&#39;t_in_Jerusalem,_but_in_the_Judaen_desert_east_of_Bethlehem._There_was_at_least_one_metochion_of_the_monastery_*near*_Jlm._What_is_meant_here?_Source?" title="Talk:Anastasius of Persia">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup>. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Modestus_of_Jerusalem" title="Modestus of Jerusalem">Modestus</a>, receiving the Christian name Anastasius to honor the <a href="/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Resurrection of Jesus Christ">resurrection of Jesus Christ</a> ("Anástasis" in Greek).
</p><p>After seven years of <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monastic observance</a>, he was moved by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Ghost" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Ghost">Holy Ghost</a> to go in quest of martyrdom and went to <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima" title="Caesarea Maritima">Caesarea</a>, then subject to the Sasanians. There he interrupted and ridiculed the Zoroastrian priests for their religion, and was as a result arrested by the local <i><a href="/wiki/Marzban" title="Marzban">marzban</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPayne2015195_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayne2015195-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> taken prisoner, cruelly tortured to make him <a href="/wiki/Abjuration" title="Abjuration">abjure</a>, and finally carried down near the <a href="/wiki/Euphrates" title="Euphrates">Euphrates</a>, to a place called Barsaloe (or Bethsaloe according to the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a>), where his tortures were continued while at the same time the highest honors in the service of King Khosrow II as a <a href="/wiki/Magi" title="Magi"><i>magus</i></a> were promised him if he would renounce Christianity.
</p><p>Finally, after refusing to abjure, with seventy others, he was strangled to death and decapitated on January 22, 628.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anastasius_of_Persia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His body, which was thrown to the dogs but left untouched by them, was carried from the place of his martyrdom to <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>, then to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, and finally to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> where the relics were venerated at the <a href="/wiki/Tre_Fontane_Abbey" title="Tre Fontane Abbey">Tre Fontane Abbey</a>.
</p><p>A <i><a href="/wiki/Passio" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio">Passio</a></i> <sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> written in Greek was devoted to the saint. An adapted Latin translation, possibly by <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodore of Canterbury">Theodore of Canterbury</a>, was available to the Anglo-Saxon church historian <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, who criticized the result and took it upon himself to 'improve' it. There are no surviving manuscripts of Bede's revision, though one did survive as late as the 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-LK_87_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LK_87-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast day is 22 January.
</p>
