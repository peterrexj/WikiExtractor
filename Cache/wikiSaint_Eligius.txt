<p><b>Saint Eligius</b> (also <b>Eloy</b>, <b>Eloi</b> or <b>Loye</b>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Éloi</i>; 11 June 588 – 1 December 660 AD) is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Goldsmith" title="Goldsmith">goldsmiths</a>, other metalworkers, and <a href="/wiki/Coin_collecting" title="Coin collecting">coin collectors</a>. He is also the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Veterinarian" title="Veterinarian">veterinarians</a>, the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (<a href="/wiki/REME" class="mw-redirect" title="REME">REME</a>), a <a href="/wiki/Corps" title="Corps">corps</a> of the <a href="/wiki/British_Army" title="British Army">British Army</a>, but he is best known for being the patron saint of horses and those who work with them.  Eligius was chief counsellor to <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>, <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> king of France. Appointed the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Noyon-Tournai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Noyon-Tournai">bishop of Noyon-Tournai</a> three years after the king's death in 642, Eligius worked for 20 years to convert the <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> population of <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a> to Christianity.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Eligius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eligius was born at the villa of Captelat, six miles north of <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Aquitaine" title="Aquitaine">Aquitaine</a> (now France), into an educated and influential <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> family. His father, recognising unusual talent in his son, sent him to the goldsmith Abbo, master of the mint at Limoges. Later Eligius went to <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>, the palace of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>, where he worked under Babo, the royal treasurer, on whose recommendation <a href="/wiki/Clotaire_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotaire II">Clotaire II</a>, king of the Franks, is said to have commissioned him to make a throne of gold adorned with precious stones.<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd>"And from that which he had taken for a single piece of work, he was able to make two. Incredibly, he could do it all from the same weight for he had accomplished the work commissioned from him without any fraud or mixture of <i>siliquae</i>, or any other fraudulence. Not claiming fragments bitten off by the file or using the devouring flame of the furnace for an excuse, but filling all faithfully with gems, he happily earned his happy reward."<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Among other goldsmithing work soon entrusted to Eligius were the bas-reliefs for the tomb of <a href="/wiki/Germain_of_Paris" title="Germain of Paris">Saint Germain</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Paris">Bishop of Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Clotaire took Eligius into the royal household and appointed him master of the mint at <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Clotaire in 629, Dagobert appointed his father's friend his chief councillor. Eligius' reputation spread rapidly, to the extent that ambassadors first sought out Eligius for counsel and to pay their respects to him before going to the king. He made some enemies. His success in inducing the Breton prince, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Judicael" title="Saint Judicael">Saint Judicael</a>, to make a pact with Dagobert, at a meeting at the king's villa of Creil (636–37) increased his influence:<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> "Indeed King Dagobert, swift, handsome and famous with no rival among any of the earlier kings of the Franks, loved him so much that he would often take himself out of the crowds of princes, optimates, dukes or bishops around him and seek private counsel from Eligius".<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:272px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg/270px-Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg" decoding="async" width="270" height="340" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg/405px-Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/45/Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg 2x" data-file-width="468" data-file-height="590" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gerard_Seghers_-_Saint_Eligius_at_the_feet_of_the_Virgin_and_Child.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Eligius at the feet of the Virgin and Child</i> by <a href="/wiki/Gerard_Seghers" title="Gerard Seghers">Gerard Seghers</a></div></div></div>
<p>Eligius took advantage of this royal favor to obtain <a href="/wiki/Alms" title="Alms">alms</a> for the poor, and to ransom captive Romans, Gauls, Bretons, Moors, and especially Saxons, who were arriving daily at the <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">slave market</a> in Marseilles.<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His friend recalled him with love:
</p>
<dl><dd>"He was tall with a rosy face. He had a pretty head of hair with curly locks. His hands were honest and his fingers long. He had the face of an angel and a prudent look. At first, he was used to wear gold and gems on his clothes, having belts composed of gold and gems and elegantly jeweled purses, linens covered with red metal and golden sacs hemmed with gold and all of the most precious fabrics including all of silk. But all of this was but fleeting ostentation from the beginning and beneath he wore a <a href="/wiki/Hairshirt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hairshirt">hairshirt</a> next to his flesh and, as he proceeded to perfection, he gave the ornaments for the needs of the poor. Then you would see him, whom you had once seen gleaming with the weight of the gold and gems that covered him, go covered in the vilest clothing with a rope for a belt."<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Besides Eligius's self-mortification, <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Dado</a> recalled his propensity for weeping, "For he had the great grace of tears."<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eligius founded several monasteries, and with the king's consent, sent his servants through towns and villages to take down the bodies of criminals who had been executed and give them decent burial. Eligius was a source of edification at court, where he and his friend Dado lived according to the strict Irish monastic rule that had been introduced into Gaul by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Columbanus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Columbanus">Saint Columbanus</a>. Eligius introduced this rule, either entirely or in part, into the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Solignac" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Solignac">monastery of Solignac</a> near Limoges, which he founded in 632 at a villa he had purchased, and also at the convent he founded at Paris, where three hundred virgins were under the guidance of the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Aurea_of_Paris" title="Saint Aurea of Paris">Abbess Aurea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He also built the basilica of St. Paul, and restored the basilica at Paris that was devoted to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Martial" title="Saint Martial">Saint Martial</a>, the patron bishop-saint of his native Limoges. Eligius also erected several fine tombs in honor of the relics of Saint <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a>, the national saint of the Franks, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris" title="Saint Denis of Paris">Saint Denis</a>, who was chosen patron saint by the king.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the death of Dagobert (639), Queen <a href="/wiki/Nanthild" title="Nanthild">Nanthild</a> took the reins of government, the king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> being a child. During this regency, Eligius launched a campaign against <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a> in the church. On the death of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Acarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Acarius">Acarius</a>, Bishop of Noyon-Tournai, 14 March of Clovis's third year (642), Eligius was made his successor, with the unanimous approbation of clergy and people. "So the unwilling goldsmith was tonsured and constituted guardian of the towns or municipalities of Vermandois which include the metropolis, <a href="/wiki/Tournai" title="Tournai">Tournai</a>, which was once a royal city, and Noyon and <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kortrijk" title="Kortrijk">Kortrijk</a> of <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-ouen_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ouen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The inhabitants of his new diocese were pagans for the most part. He undertook the conversion of the Flemings, Frisians, Suevi, and the other Germanic tribes along the North Sea coast. He made frequent missionary excursions and also founded a great many monasteries and churches. In his own episcopal city of <a href="/wiki/Noyon" title="Noyon">Noyon</a> he built and endowed a nunnery for virgins. After the finding of the body of <a href="/wiki/St._Quentin" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Quentin">St. Quentin</a>, Bishop Eligius erected in his honor a church to which was joined a monastery under the Irish rule. He also discovered the bodies of <a href="/wiki/St._Piatus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Piatus">St. Piatus</a> and his martyred companions, and in 654 removed the remains of <a href="/wiki/Fursey" class="mw-redirect" title="Fursey">Saint Fursey</a>, the celebrated Irish missionary (died 650).<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eligius died on 1 December 660 and was buried at Noyon.<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Several writings of Eligius have survived: a <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermon</a> in which he combats the pagan practices of his time, a <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homily</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Last_Judgment" title="Last Judgment">Last Judgment</a>, and a letter written in 645, in which he begs for the prayers of Bishop <a href="/wiki/Didier_of_Cahors" title="Didier of Cahors">Desiderius of Cahors</a>. There are fourteen other <a href="/wiki/Pseudepigraphy" class="mw-redirect" title="Pseudepigraphy">pseudepigraphical</a> homilies that are no longer attributed to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An important study about the work of Eligius as a goldsmith was contributed by the German scholar <a href="/wiki/Hayo_Vierck" title="Hayo Vierck">Hayo Vierck</a> to the <i><a href="/wiki/Joachim_Werner_(archaeologist)" title="Joachim Werner (archaeologist)">Joachim Werner</a> Festschrift</i> in 1974.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Eligius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:StEloiAveyron.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Statue of St. Eligius" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/c0/StEloiAveyron.jpg/170px-StEloiAveyron.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="372" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/c0/StEloiAveyron.jpg/255px-StEloiAveyron.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/c/c0/StEloiAveyron.jpg 2x" data-file-width="274" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:StEloiAveyron.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of St. Eligius in the church of St. Marcel in St. Marcel (Aveyron), France. At the saint's right foot are the tools of his original trade.</div></div></div>
<p>St Eligius is particularly honored in <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>, in the province of <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a>, and at <a href="/wiki/Tournai" title="Tournai">Tournai</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kortrijk" title="Kortrijk">Kortrijk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bruges" title="Bruges">Bruges</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>. During the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> his relics were the object of special veneration, and were repeatedly divided and transferred to other resting-places, in 881, 1066, 1137, 1255, and 1306. A good deal of legend has gathered round the life of Saint Eloi, who is still very popular with goldsmiths, farriers and car mechanics.<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Eligius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He is the patron of <a href="/wiki/Goldsmith" title="Goldsmith">goldsmiths</a>, <a href="/wiki/Blacksmiths" class="mw-redirect" title="Blacksmiths">blacksmiths</a>, and all workers in metal, although English goldsmiths adopted <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">St. Dunstan</a> as their patron saint. St. Eligius is also the patron saint of cattle and horses.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> St. Eligius is the Patron Saint of the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An annual mass is celebrated around 9 December at <a href="/wiki/Notre_Dame_de_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Notre Dame de Paris">Notre Dame de Paris</a> for members of the <b>Confraternity of St Eloi</b>.  This follows the tradition of the May offering, usually a religious painting, made to the Cathedral between 1630 and 1707 by the goldsmiths of Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The tradition of the Guild Chapel was revived in 1953 by the Paris goldsmiths who provided the altar, crucifix above it and a statue of the Saint.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend_of_the_shoeing_of_the_horse">Legend of the shoeing of the horse</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Eligius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legend of the shoeing of the horse">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is also a legend that St Eloi resolved the problem of a horse reluctant to be shod.  He thought it was possessed by demons, so he cut off the horse's foreleg and, while the horse stood on the remaining three legs and watched, he re-shod the hoof on the amputated leg, before miraculously re-attaching the leg to the horse.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The legend is depicted in a pre-<a href="/wiki/Reformation" title="Reformation">Reformation</a> carving in the <a href="/wiki/Wincanton" title="Wincanton">Wincanton</a> Parish Church, <a href="/wiki/Slapton,_Northamptonshire" title="Slapton, Northamptonshire">Slapton</a> Church Northamptonshire, England,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> a <a href="/wiki/Tapestry" title="Tapestry">tapestry</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Hospices_de_Beaune" title="Hospices de Beaune">Hospices de Beaune</a> (Hotel Dieu) in <a href="/wiki/Beaune" title="Beaune">Beaune</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> France, as a <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a> on the wall of <a href="/wiki/Aarhus_Cathedral" title="Aarhus Cathedral">Aarhus Cathedral</a>, Denmark, as well as in a 14th-century painting attributed to <a href="/wiki/Niccolo_di_Pietro_Gerini" class="mw-redirect" title="Niccolo di Pietro Gerini">Niccolo di Pietro Gerini</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_du_Petit_Palais,_Avignon" title="Musée du Petit Palais, Avignon">Petit Palais</a> in Avignon, France.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The painting was confiscated from an Austrian collector by the Germans during <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">WWII</a>, and was restituted to the heirs of the original owners in March 2013 by the <a href="/wiki/French_Ministry_of_Culture" class="mw-redirect" title="French Ministry of Culture">French Ministry of Culture</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Eligius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The saint is invariably depicted in bishop's garb, holding his emblem, a goldsmith's hammer. The only exceptions are in illustrations to his <i><a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">vita</a></i>, that depict episodes before his investiture as bishop. He is generally represented as a bishop, a crosier in his right hand, holding a miniature church of chased gold on the open palm of his left hand.<sup id="cite_ref-Van_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Petrus_Christus" title="Petrus Christus">Petrus Christus</a> panel of 1449 illustrating this article, since the removal of its overpainted halo in 1993, is now recognised in the Lehman Collection at the Metropolitan Museum of Art as the <i>Vocational Portrait of a Goldsmith</i>, and not as a depiction of Eligius.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
