<p><b>Saint Glyceria</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Γλυκερία</span>; died ca. 177 in <a href="/wiki/Perinthus" title="Perinthus">Heraclea</a>, <a href="/wiki/Propontis" class="mw-redirect" title="Propontis">Propontis</a>) was a Roman <a href="/wiki/Virgin_(title)" title="Virgin (title)">virgin</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Early_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Early church">early church</a>.
</p><p>According to Christian tradition, she was forced to pay tribute to a stone statue of <a href="/wiki/Jupiter_(mythology)" title="Jupiter (mythology)">Jupiter</a> but it was destroyed while she stood before it. The virgin was imprisoned for this, then sentenced to be <a href="/wiki/Damnatio_ad_bestias" title="Damnatio ad bestias">torn apart by wild animals</a>. She, however, was not torn apart. Before the animals could render her any harm, Glyceria died a virgin <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> in Heraclea. Her relics reputedly poured forth the substance known as the <a href="/wiki/Oil_of_Saints" title="Oil of Saints">Oil of Saints</a>, and her name means "sweetness".
</p><p>She is primarily recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">Eastern Christianity</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Her feast day is 13 May in Eastern Orthodox liturgics.
</p>
