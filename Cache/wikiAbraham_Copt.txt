<p><b>Abraham of Farshut</b> was an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Church</a>, and by extension all of the <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox Churches</a>. His feast day in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">calendar of saints</a> of the Coptic Church is February 12.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abraham_of_Farshut&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in Farshut, near the modern city of Hiww.<sup id="cite_ref-Coquin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coquin-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents, who were Christians and locally important figures, died when Abraham was twelve. The next year, Abraham tried unsuccessfully to persuade his sister to retain her virginity.
</p><p>Thereafter, Abraham left to join the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Pachomius" class="mw-redirect" title="Pachomius">Pachomius</a> at <a href="/wiki/Pbow" title="Pbow">Pbow</a>. This monastery was at the time under the direction of Pshintbahse. There Abraham devoted himself to trying to achieve the monastic ideals.
</p><p>Abraham was elected abbot of the monastery on the death of Pshintbahse. Shortly thereafter, <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a> requested that Abraham be brought to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, in an attempt to bring those monks who still rejected the decision of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a> into communion with the greater church. The exact time of this event is unknown, but it is believed to have been between 535 and 548. Abraham brought with him four monks. Upon arrival, Justianian summoned them and informed them that they would either accept the decision of the council or lose their positions. Abraham refused to entertain the idea,<sup id="cite_ref-sams_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sams-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and was removed as <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">archimandrite</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Goehring_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Goehring-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Theodora_(6th_century)" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodora (6th century)">Theodora</a> tried to persuade Justinian to change his mind, seemingly to no avail.  Abraham himself stated in a letter to his monks that he preferred to remain in exile rather than subscribe to a faith contrary to that of <a href="/wiki/Athanasius" class="mw-redirect" title="Athanasius">Athanasius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sams_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sams-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Abraham did return to Egypt, however, possibly due to persuasion from Theodora.
</p><p>Pancharis had been appointed to replace Abraham as archimandrite at <a href="/wiki/Pbow" title="Pbow">Pbow</a>. Abraham first went to the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Shenoute" title="Shenoute">Shenoute</a>, where he made a  copy of the Rule. Then he set up a new monastery at Farshut with two other monks from the monastery of Moses. The new monastery contained a well and a garden. The number of monks grew, however, making it necessary for the buildings to be expanded. He also founded a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of nuns at roughly the same time.<sup id="cite_ref-Goehring_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Goehring-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Late in life, Abraham received a vision in which Pachomius, Petronius, and <a href="/wiki/Shenouda_the_Archimandrite" class="mw-redirect" title="Shenouda the Archimandrite">Shenouda the Archimandrite</a> appeared to him, informing him of his upcoming death.<sup id="cite_ref-Goehring_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Goehring-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some people have suggested that this Abraham might be identical to the <a href="/wiki/Abraham_of_Scetes" title="Abraham of Scetes">Abraham of Scetes</a> commemorated in the same calendar on January 4.
</p>
