<p><b>James Duckett</b> (died 19 April 1602) was an English Catholic layman and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>, executed at Tyburn for printing Catholic devotionals.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Duckett&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James Duckett was born at Gilfortrigs in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Skelsmergh" title="Skelsmergh">Skelsmergh</a> in <a href="/wiki/Westmorland" title="Westmorland">Westmorland</a> at an unknown date. Brought up a Protestant, he was converted by a book: a friend of his, Peter Mauson lent him <i>The Foundation of the Catholic Religion</i> while Duckett was serving his apprenticeship to a book printer in London, and he decided to become a Catholic.<sup id="cite_ref-bassett_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bassett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was twice imprisoned for not attending the Protestant services,<sup id="cite_ref-camm_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> first in <a href="/wiki/Bridewell" class="mw-redirect" title="Bridewell">Bridewell</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Compter" title="Compter">compter</a>. Both times his employer interceded and got him freed. But then the employer asked James to find a job elsewhere.<sup id="cite_ref-pauline_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pauline-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was received into the Catholic Church by an old priest named Weekes who was imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Gatehouse" title="Gatehouse">Gatehouse</a> at Westminster. Two or three years later, about 1590, he married a Catholic widow, Anne Hart. Out of his twelve years of married life, nine were spent in prison for his new faith.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Their son later became a Carthusian monk and recorded much of what we know about his father.<sup id="cite_ref-pauline_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pauline-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Duckett made his living at that time as a tailor, also making garments, vestments, and altar linens for priests.
</p><p>He was active in propagating Catholic literature. His house was searched, and upon finding a press and copies of <i>Our Lady's Psalter</i>, Ducket was confined to <a href="/wiki/The_Clink" title="The Clink">The Clink</a> on St. Thomas' Day before Christmas. He then spent two years in <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a> before being released on bond. Ten weeks later his house was searched again, and although Duckett was able to leave by the back door, he later surrendered to protect those who had posted his bond. He was then sent to The Clink. He was briefly released at the petition of two Protestant midwives when his wife went into labor, but subsequently picked up when it was found that he had sent some English and Latin primers to a bookbinder. He was again released, two knights of the shire going surety for him.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He was finally betrayed by Peter Bullock, a bookbinder, who acted in order to obtain his own release from prison. Duckett's house was searched on 4 March 1602 and he was arrested on a charge of having 25 copies of Fr. Southwell's books on his premises.<sup id="cite_ref-bassett_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bassett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> For this he was at once thrown into <a href="/wiki/Newgate" title="Newgate">Newgate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the trial, Bullock testified that he had bound various Catholic books for Duckett, who admitted this but denied other false accusations in a self-possessed manner. The jury found him not guilty; but the judge, Sir <a href="/wiki/John_Popham_(Lord_Chief_Justice)" class="mw-redirect" title="John Popham (Lord Chief Justice)">John Popham</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chief_Justice" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chief Justice">Lord Chief Justice</a>, browbeat the jury, which reversed its verdict and Duckett was found guilty of <a href="/wiki/Felony" title="Felony">felony</a>. Despite the betrayal of Duckett, Bullock was taken to his death at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> in the same cart as Duckett on 19 April 1601.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>James Duckett's son was the John Duckett who was Prior of the English Carthusians at <a href="/wiki/Nieuwpoort,_Belgium" title="Nieuwpoort, Belgium">Nieuwpoort</a> in <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a> from 1644 to 1647. He related that on the way to <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a> his father was handed a cup of wine, which he drank, and told his wife to drink to Peter Bullock and to forgive him. When she declined, he chided her gently until she did. On arrival at Tyburn Tree James kissed and embraced Bullock, beseeching him to die in the Catholic faith, without success.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the same trial three priests, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Tichborne" title="Thomas Tichborne">Thomas Tichborne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Watkinson" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Watkinson">Robert Watkinson</a>, and Francis Page, were condemned to death. For some reason their execution was remanded to the following day.
</p><p>James Duckett was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 15 December 1929. <a href="/wiki/John_Duckett" title="John Duckett">John Duckett</a>, a later priest and martyr, was probably his grandson.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Duckett&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James Duckett is regarded as a patron intercessor of booksellers and publishers.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
