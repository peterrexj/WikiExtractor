<p><b>Saint Romanus of Rouen</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Romain</i>; reconstructed <a href="/wiki/Frankish_language" title="Frankish language">Frankish</a>: <i>*Hruomann</i>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 640 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Scribe" title="Scribe">scribe</a>, clerical sage, and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rouen">bishop of Rouen</a>. He would have lived under <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> (629–39), though his date of birth is unknown.  His life is known in legend and tradition<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and is shown in the stained glass windows (c. 1521)  and south gate of <a href="/wiki/Rouen_Cathedral" title="Rouen Cathedral">Rouen Cathedral</a> and the stained glass windows of the église Saint-Godard (1555). The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> claims that his legend has little historical value<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> with little authentic information.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He was both <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chancellor of France">Lord Chancellor of France</a> and <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9f%C3%A9rendaire_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Référendaire of France">Référendaire of France</a>. (For Saint Romanus, Martyr, please see <a href="/wiki/Romanus_of_Caesarea" title="Romanus of Caesarea">Romanus of Caesarea</a>).
</p><p>The city's autumn "foire Saint-Romain" was set to his feast day on the "10th day of the Kalends of November" (i.e. 23 October) around 1090, at the same date as his cult was spread to the whole diocese of Rouen.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Rouen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth_and_youth">Birth and youth</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Rouen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Birth and youth">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His mother Félicité lamented her sterility until one night an angel appeared to Romain's father announcing that a child would be born in his house. Very young, Romanus was sent to the king's court. This was common for young aristocrats of the time - those who went were known as <i>nourris</i>, meaning that they effectively carried out domestic duties to the court in return for some education, before being sent back to the provinces as bishops or counts. There Romanus met saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eligius" title="Saint Eligius">Eligius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Audoin</a>. When the episcopal seat of Rouen became vacant, the college of canons voted for Romanus and the king consented to their choice, offering Romain a cross.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legendary_miracles">Legendary miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Rouen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legendary miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG/220px-Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG/330px-Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG/440px-Tableau_sur_la_vie_de_Saint_Romain.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3056" data-file-height="2292" /></a><figcaption>An image from the life of St. Romanus.</figcaption></figure>
<ul><li><b>Destruction of the Temple of Venus</b>: Before becoming bishop, the faithful asked Romanus to make a temple to Venus (with an altar dedicated to her) in the Gallo-Roman amphitheatre in the north of the city disappear. Romanus went to the temple, pulled the dedication off the altar and the temple collapsed.</li>
<li><b>Destruction of the pagan temple</b>: Romanus set out to evangelise the surrounding countryside and one day found himself faced with a pagan temple that looked like a fortress, on which demons were dancing. He cursed the demons, provoked the chief demon and the temple collapsed.</li>
<li><b>Miracle of the holy chrism</b>: One day, Romanus was preparing to consecrate some baptismal fonts but found he had forgotten the holy <a href="/wiki/Chrism" title="Chrism">chrism</a>. The deacon he sent to look for it was in such a rush to return that he dropped and broke the vase containing the chrism, which leaked out onto the earth. Praying all the while, Romanus gathered up the pieces of the vase, which then put itself and its contents back together.</li>
<li><b>Temptation</b>: At the end of his life, he retired to a hermitage to pray and meditate. A demon disguised as a poor woman came to ask his charity and Romanus, though he  hesitated to receive a woman, did not wish to renege on his duties of hospitality. He let in the woman who undressed and untressed her hair. Romanus called on the Lord for help and an angel intervened, throwing the demon into a bottomless pit.</li>
<li><b>Floods</b>: Flooding was frequent at this time, and Romanus intervened when it threatened to be catastrophic, making the river fall.</li>
<li><b>Ecstasy</b>: Shortly before his death, Romanus was saying the Mass when he entered an ecstasy, his body rising off the ground as God announced the date of his death.</li>
<li><b>The dragon or <a href="/wiki/Gargouille" class="mw-redirect" title="Gargouille">Gargouille</a></b><span class="anchor" id="Gargouille"></span>: the Gargouille  (also <i>Garguiem</i>, as <i><a href="/wiki/Gargoyle" title="Gargoyle">gargoyle</a></i> from a word for "throat") is a <a href="/wiki/Dragon" title="Dragon">dragon</a> from the legend of Saint  Romanus of Rouen. The monster does not figure in the older account of the saint's life, being recorded for the first time in 1394.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The account described how, in the wild swamps on the left bank of the Seine a huge serpent or dragon rampaged, who "devoured and destroyed people and beasts of the field".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Romanus decided to hunt in this area but could only find one man to help him, a man condemned to death who had nothing to lose. They arrived in the serpent's land and Romanus drew the sign of the cross on the beast. It then lay down at his feet and let Romanus put his <a href="/wiki/Stole_(vestment)" title="Stole (vestment)">stole</a> on him as a <a href="/wiki/Leash" title="Leash">leash</a>, in which manner he led it into the town to be condemned to death and burned on the parvis of the cathedral (or thrown into the Seine according to other authors). This legend was the origin for the bishops' privilege (lasting until 1790) to pardon one prisoner condemned to death each year, by giving the pardoned man or woman the reliquary holding Romanus's relics in a procession.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Festival">Festival</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Rouen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Festival">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His feast day is traditionally celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Rouen">archdiocese of Rouen</a> on 23 October, as a Triple Feast - First Class. Today they are often transferred to the following Sunday, in contravention of the date decreed for the feast by archbishop <a href="/wiki/Guillaume_Bonne-Ame" class="mw-redirect" title="Guillaume Bonne-Ame">Guillaume Bonne-Ame</a> in around 1090.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Rouen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Four <i>Lives of Saint Romain</i> exist - one is a Latin verse version of the 8th century, another is a prose life addressed to the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">archbishop of Rouen</a> by the doyen of Saint-Médard de <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>. Those two lives are held in the Bibliothèque municipale de Rouen, whilst another <i>Life</i> is held by the <a href="/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_nationale_de_France" title="Bibliothèque nationale de France">Bibliothèque nationale de France</a> in Paris.
</p>
