<p><b>Antoni Julian Nowowiejski</b> (11 February 1858 &#8211; 28 May 1941) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_P%C5%82ock" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Płock">bishop of Płock</a> (1908–1941), <a href="/wiki/Titular_bishop" title="Titular bishop">titular archbishop</a> of Silyum, first secretary of <a href="/wiki/Polish_Episcopal_Conference" title="Polish Episcopal Conference">Polish Episcopal Conference</a> (1918–1919), honorary citizen of <a href="/wiki/P%C5%82ock" title="Płock">Płock</a> and historian. He died at the hands of the <a href="/wiki/Nazi_Germany" title="Nazi Germany">Germans</a> in <a href="/wiki/Soldau_concentration_camp" title="Soldau concentration camp">Soldau concentration camp</a> near <a href="/wiki/Dzia%C5%82dowo" title="Działdowo">Działdowo</a> on 28 May 1941, and was subsequently <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1999 as one of the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Martyrs of World War II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoni_Julian_Nowowiejski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Antoni Julian Nowowiejski was born on 11 February 1858<sup id="cite_ref-cna_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Lubienia" class="mw-redirect" title="Lubienia">Lubienia</a> near <a href="/wiki/Opat%C3%B3w" title="Opatów">Opatów</a>. At sixteen he entered the diocesan seminary at studied <a href="/wiki/P%C5%82ock" title="Płock">Płock</a>. He received <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> on July 10, 1881.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The following year he obtained a degree in theology from the Academy of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petersburg" title="Saint Petersburg">Saint Petersburg</a>. Nowowiejski became a professor and a rector of the Płock Seminary, <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of Płock and in 1902 <a href="/wiki/Vicar_general" title="Vicar general">vicar general</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_P%C5%82ock" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Płock">Płock diocese</a>. He was ordained bishop of Płock on 6 December 1908.
</p><p>As the leader of the Płock diocese he carried out an administrative reform, devoting much attention to catholic education and created a junior seminary.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a>, he was active in charity organizations. He oversaw two diocesans (<a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synods</a>) in 1927 and 1938, and initiated a local chapter of "Akcja Katolicka" (Catholic Action). In November 1930, he became the <a href="/wiki/Titular_bishop" title="Titular bishop">titular archbishop</a> of Silyum.<sup id="cite_ref-Borrelli_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Borrelli-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1931, he was awarded the Commander's Cross with the Star of the Order of Rebirth of Poland by President Mościcki. The <a href="/wiki/University_of_Warsaw" title="University of Warsaw">University of Warsaw</a> awarded him the <a href="/wiki/Honorary_title_(academic)" title="Honorary title (academic)">honorary title</a> of <i><a href="/wiki/Doctor_honoris_causa" class="mw-redirect" title="Doctor honoris causa">doctor honoris causa</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-polonia_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-polonia-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:6_Plock_094.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/6_Plock_094.jpg/220px-6_Plock_094.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/6_Plock_094.jpg/330px-6_Plock_094.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/6_Plock_094.jpg/440px-6_Plock_094.jpg 2x" data-file-width="533" data-file-height="750" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:6_Plock_094.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A tablet to beatified bishop Nowowiejski in <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_P%C5%82ock" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Płock">Cathedral of Płock</a></div></div></div>
<p>On 1 September 1939, the <a href="/wiki/German_invasion_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="German invasion of Poland">German invasion of Poland</a> marked the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">Second World War</a>. One of the Nazis' goals was the elimination of Polish <a href="/wiki/Intelligentsia" title="Intelligentsia">intelligentsia</a>. on 28 February 1940, Bishop Nowowiejski and Płock's <a href="/wiki/Suffragan_bishop" title="Suffragan bishop">suffragan bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Leon_Wetma%C5%84ski" title="Leon Wetmański">Leon Wetmański</a> were arrested by the Germans and imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/S%C5%82uck" class="mw-redirect" title="Słuck">Słuck</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dzia%C5%82dowo" title="Działdowo">Działdowo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He refused the chance to escape, saying,  "How can a pastor abandon their sheep?"<sup id="cite_ref-Borrelli_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Borrelli-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Archbishop Nowowiejski was tortured when he refused to trample on his pectoral cross.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  Although suffering, he imparted his blessing to the tortured and dying. After three months of torture and hunger, he died, at the age of eighty-three, at the Dzialdowo death camp on 28 May 1941.<sup id="cite_ref-Borrelli_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Borrelli-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  The place of burial of his body is unknown, but it is supposed that it was a forest in Malinowo.<sup id="cite_ref-polonia_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-polonia-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 13 June 1999 as one of the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Martyrs of World War II</a>, and is commemorated on June 12.<sup id="cite_ref-polonia_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-polonia-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoni_Julian_Nowowiejski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Antoni Julian Nowowiejski was the author of many works in the realm of history (especially the <a href="/wiki/History_of_P%C5%82ock" class="mw-redirect" title="History of Płock">history of Płock</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_liturgy" title="Catholic liturgy">Catholic liturgy</a>. His <i>Cereminiał parafialny</i> (Parish ceremony) became a standard textbook for parish priests, and went through seven editions before the war. 
</p><p>Selected works:
</p>
<ul><li>Wykład liturgii Kościoła katolickiego</li>
<li>Ceremoniał parafialny</li>
<li>Płock. Monografia historyczna</li></ul>
