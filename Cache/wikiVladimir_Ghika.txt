<p><b>Vladimir Ghika</b> or <b>Ghica</b> (25 December 1873 – 16 May 1954) was a <a href="/wiki/Romanians" title="Romanians">Romanian</a> diplomat and essayist who, after his conversion from <a href="/wiki/Romanian_Orthodox_Church" title="Romanian Orthodox Church">Romanian Orthodoxy</a> to <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholicism</a>, became a priest. He was a member of the princely <a href="/wiki/Ghica_family" title="Ghica family">Ghica family</a>, which ruled <a href="/wiki/Moldavia" title="Moldavia">Moldavia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wallachia" title="Wallachia">Wallachia</a> at various times from the 17th to the 19th century.
</p><p>He <a href="/wiki/Dying_in_prison" class="mw-redirect" title="Dying in prison">died in prison</a> in May 1954 after his arrest by the <a href="/wiki/Communist_Romania" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist Romania">Communist regime</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vladimir_Ghika&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vladimir_Ghika&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Vladimir Ghika was born on <a href="/wiki/Christmas_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Christmas Day">Christmas Day</a> of 1873 in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>). His father was <a href="/wiki/Ioan_Grigore_Ghica" title="Ioan Grigore Ghica">Ioan Grigore Ghica</a>, diplomat, minister plenipotentiary in <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>; his mother Alexandrina was born Moret de Blaremberg (van Blarembergue) in a Flemish-Russian family; he had four brothers and a sister: Gregory, Alexander, George and Ella (who both died at an early age), and <a href="/wiki/Dimitrie_I._Ghika" title="Dimitrie I. Ghika">Dimitrie I. Ghika</a> (future ambassador and minister of foreign affairs). He was the grandson of the last Prince sovereign of <a href="/wiki/Moldavia" title="Moldavia">Moldavia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Grigore_Alexandru_Ghica" title="Grigore Alexandru Ghica">Prince Gregory V Ghika</a>, who ruled from 1849 to 1856.
</p><p>He was raised with the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox faith</a>. In 1878, in order to give a good education to the children, the family moved to <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. There, they frequented the <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> community, because the Orthodox church was not represented in the area. Ghika received his Degree in Law in 1895, after which he attended the Paris Faculty of Political Science. At the same time, he frequented courses of Medicine, Botany, Art, Literature, Philosophy, and History. He returned to <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a> due to an attack of <a href="/wiki/Angina_pectoris" class="mw-redirect" title="Angina pectoris">angina pectoris</a> and continued his studies in Romania.
</p><p>Ghika was an <a href="/wiki/List_of_people_associated_with_the_Pontifical_University_of_St._Thomas_Aquinas" title="List of people associated with the Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas">alumnus</a> of the College of St. Thomas, the future <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_St._Thomas_Aquinas_(Angelicum)" class="mw-redirect" title="Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas (Angelicum)">Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas <i>Angelicum</i></a>, in Rome. In 1898, he enrolled in the Faculty of Philosophy and Theology. At the <i>Angelicum</i>, Ghika completed a licentiate in Philosophy and a Doctorate in Theology in 1905.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Soon after, he converted to the Catholic faith in 1902.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pilgrimage">Pilgrimage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vladimir_Ghika&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Pilgrimage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Vl._Ghika_porte_le_drapeau_%C3%A9cussonn%C3%A9_du_Coeur_Sacr%C3%A9_de_J%C3%A9sus_de_Roumanie_(Agence_Rol).jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Vl._Ghika_porte_le_drapeau_%C3%A9cussonn%C3%A9_du_Coeur_Sacr%C3%A9_de_J%C3%A9sus_de_Roumanie_%28Agence_Rol%29.jpeg/240px-Vl._Ghika_porte_le_drapeau_%C3%A9cussonn%C3%A9_du_Coeur_Sacr%C3%A9_de_J%C3%A9sus_de_Roumanie_%28Agence_Rol%29.jpeg" decoding="async" width="240" height="321" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Vl._Ghika_porte_le_drapeau_%C3%A9cussonn%C3%A9_du_Coeur_Sacr%C3%A9_de_J%C3%A9sus_de_Roumanie_%28Agence_Rol%29.jpeg/360px-Vl._Ghika_porte_le_drapeau_%C3%A9cussonn%C3%A9_du_Coeur_Sacr%C3%A9_de_J%C3%A9sus_de_Roumanie_%28Agence_Rol%29.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2f/Vl._Ghika_porte_le_drapeau_%C3%A9cussonn%C3%A9_du_Coeur_Sacr%C3%A9_de_J%C3%A9sus_de_Roumanie_%28Agence_Rol%29.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="479" data-file-height="640" /></a><figcaption>Prince Ghika at <a href="/wiki/Paray-le-Monial" title="Paray-le-Monial">Paray-le-Monial</a>, holding up the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a> banner of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Romania" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism in Romania">Roman Catholicism in Romania</a> (1917)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ghika wanted to become a priest or monk, but <a href="/wiki/Pius_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius X">Pius X</a> advised him to give up the idea, at least for a while, and to dedicate himself to secular apostolate instead. He became one of the pioneers of the lay apostolate.
</p><p>After returning to Romania, he dedicated himself to works of charity and opened the first free clinic in <a href="/wiki/Bucharest" title="Bucharest">Bucharest</a> called Mariae Bethlehem. He also set the foundation for a great hospital and <a href="/wiki/Sanatorium" title="Sanatorium">sanatorium</a> named after <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a>, founded the first free hospital in Romania and the first ambulance, thereby becoming founder of the first Catholic charity work in Romania. He was dedicated to patient care while participating in health services in the <a href="/wiki/Balkan_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Balkan War">Balkan War</a> in 1913, without the fear of cholera in <a href="/wiki/Zimnicea" title="Zimnicea">Zimnicea</a>. He was also in charge of diplomatic missions among the Avezzano earthquake victims of tuberculosis of Hospice of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Cleanup" title="Wikipedia:Cleanup"><span title="&#39;earthquake victims of tuberculosis of Hospice&#39;? (November 2016)">incomprehensible</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>On 7 October 1923, Ghika was ordained a priest in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> by <a href="/wiki/Louis-Ernest_Dubois" title="Louis-Ernest Dubois">Cardinal Dubois</a>, Archbishop of the city. He served as a priest in France until 1939. Shortly after Ghika was ordained, the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> authorized him to celebrate the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a>. Prince Ghika thereby became the first bi-ritual Romanian priest.
</p><p>On 13 May 1931, the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> appointed Ghika to be an <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Protonotary" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Protonotary">Apostolic Protonotary</a>, but he was reluctant to accept it. He worked worldwide, including <a href="/wiki/Bucharest" title="Bucharest">Bucharest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, <a href="/wiki/Congo_(area)" class="mw-redirect" title="Congo (area)">Congo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tokyo" title="Tokyo">Tokyo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a>, among others. Later, in jest, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> called him an "apostolic vagabond".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_death">Imprisonment and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vladimir_Ghika&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 3 August 1939, he returned to <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a>, where he was caught in the <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">Second World War</a>. He refused to leave Romania at that time so that he could be with the poor and sick. However, he left eventually for the same reason in <a href="/wiki/Bucharest" title="Bucharest">Bucharest</a> when they started Allied bombing. After the Communists came to power, he also refused to leave on the royal train, for the same reasons.
</p><p>He was arrested on 18 November 1952, because of his support for the Catholic Church in communion with Rome and his opposition to the <a href="/wiki/Schism" title="Schism">schismatic</a> church that the regime was creating. He was charged for "high treason" and threatened, beaten, tortured and processed. Eventually, he was imprisoned at <a href="/wiki/Jilava_Prison" title="Jilava Prison">Jilava Prison</a> on 16 May, and he died in 1954 due to the treatment to which he was subjected.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Ghika was proposed for <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Bucharest" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Bucharest">Archdiocese of Bucharest</a>, based on a dossier with his biography submitted to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in the Vatican. On 27 March 2013, Pope Francis declared Ghika to be a martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was beatified on 31 August 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vladimir_Ghika&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although he had culture and capacity, he avoided producing personal writings.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He wrote only because he was forced by circumstances and needs. He did research work in the Vatican archives, publishing some of the results in the <i>Revue Catolique</i>. He also wrote magazine articles in <i>Literary Talk</i>, <i><a href="/wiki/La_Revue_hebdomadaire" title="La Revue hebdomadaire">La Revue Hebdomadaire</a></i>, <i>Les Études</i>, <i>Le Correspondant</i>, <i>La Revue des Jeunes</i>, and <i>La Documentation Catholique</i>. His short personal meditations were subsequently published in various editions as <i>Pensées pour la suite des jours</i>.
</p>
