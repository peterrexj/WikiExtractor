<p><b>Dominic Savio</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Domenico Savio</i>; 2 April 1842 – 9 March 1857) was an Italian student of <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">John Bosco</a>. He was studying to be a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> when he became ill and died at the age of 14, possibly from <a href="/wiki/Pleurisy" title="Pleurisy">pleurisy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was noted for his piety and devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic faith</a>, and was canonized a saint by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> in 1954.
</p><p>Bosco regarded Savio very highly, and wrote a biography of his young student, <i>The Life of Dominic Savio</i>. This volume, along with other accounts of him, were critical factors in his cause for <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">sainthood</a>. Despite the fact that many people considered him to have died at too young an age – fourteen – to be considered for sainthood, he was considered eligible for such singular honour on the basis of his having displayed "<a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>" in his everyday life.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Savio was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> a saint on 12 June 1954, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>, making him the youngest non-<a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> to be canonised in the Catholic Church<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> until the canonisations of <a href="/wiki/Francisco_and_Jacinta_Marto" title="Francisco and Jacinta Marto">Francisco and Jacinta Marto</a>, the pious visionaries of Fatima, in 2017.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The major part of the biographical information known about Dominic Savio comes from his biography written by <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">John Bosco</a>, in addition to the testimonies given by Savio's family and friends.<sup id="cite_ref-Donboscowest_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donboscowest-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 2 April 1842 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Riva_presso_Chieri" title="Riva presso Chieri">Riva</a>, 2 miles (3&#160;km) from the town of <a href="/wiki/Chieri" title="Chieri">Chieri</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>, northern Italy<sup id="cite_ref-Donboscowest_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donboscowest-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> a son was born to Carlo and Brigitta Savio. He was given the name <a href="/wiki/Dominic" title="Dominic">Domenico</a> at <a href="/wiki/Baptism#Catholic_baptism_and_salvation" title="Baptism">baptism</a>. The name <i>Domenico</i> means "of the Lord"<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and the surname <i>Savio</i> means "wise".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> His parents had ten children in all. His father was a blacksmith and his mother, a seamstress. They were poor, hardworking and pious.<sup id="cite_ref-liveschap1_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liveschap1-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When he was two years old, his parents returned to their native place at <a href="/wiki/Murialdo" title="Murialdo">Murialdo</a> on the outskirts of <a href="/wiki/Castelnuovo_Don_Bosco" title="Castelnuovo Don Bosco">Castelnuovo d'Asti</a> and from where they had gone to Riva in 1841. His parents took great care to give him a Christian upbringing. By the age of four, Dominic was able to pray by himself and was occasionally found in solitude, praying.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic1_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic1-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> John Bosco records that Savio's parents recollect how he used to help his mother around the house, welcome his father home, say his prayers without being reminded, (even reminding others when they forgot) and say <a href="/wiki/Grace_(prayer)" title="Grace (prayer)">Grace</a> at mealtimes unfailingly.<sup id="cite_ref-liveschap1_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liveschap1-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="At_the_village_school">At the village school</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: At the village school">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Domenico_Savio.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/San_Domenico_Savio.jpg/220px-San_Domenico_Savio.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d4/San_Domenico_Savio.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="330" data-file-height="440" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Domenico_Savio.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>San Domenico Savio</div></div></div>
<p>Fr. Giovanni Zucca from Murialdo, who was then the chaplain at Murialdo when Dominic was five years old,<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> notes in a statement to John Bosco that he came to notice Dominic due to his regular church attendance with his mother, and his habit of kneeling down outside the church to pray (even in the mud or snow) if he happened to come to church before it had been unlocked in the morning. The chaplain also notes that Savio made good progress at the village school not merely due to his cleverness, but also by working hard. He would not join the other boys in doing something that he believed to be morally wrong and would explain <i>why</i> he thought a particular deed was wrong.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of five, he learned to <a href="/wiki/Altar_server" title="Altar server">serve Mass</a>, and would try to participate at Mass every day as well as go regularly to <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">Confession</a>. Having been permitted to make his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> at an early age, he had much reverence for the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="First_Communion">First Communion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: First Communion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At that time, it was customary for children to receive their <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> at the age of twelve.<sup id="cite_ref-BoscoNet_2006_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BoscoNet_2006-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> (Pope Pius X would later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X#Liturgical_reforms" title="Pope Pius X">lower this age to seven</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> After initial hesitation, and subsequent consultation with other priests, the parish priest agreed to permit Dominic to receive his First Communion at the age of seven, since he knew the catechism and understood something of the Eucharist.<sup id="cite_ref-BoscoNet_2006_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BoscoNet_2006-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He spent much time praying and reading in preparation,<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic.net_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic.net-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> asking his mother's forgiveness for anything he might have done to displease her and then went to church. In his biography of Dominic Savio, John Bosco devotes a chapter to tell of Dominic's First Communion. He says that several years later, whenever Dominic talked of the day of his First Communion, he said with joy: <i>"That was the happiest and most wonderful day of my life."</i><sup id="cite_ref-BoscoNet_2006_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BoscoNet_2006-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
John Bosco records that on the day of his First Communion, Dominic made some promises which he wrote in a "little book", and re-read them many times. John Bosco once looked through Dominic's book, and he quotes from it the promises that he made:<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic.net_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic.net-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd><i>Resolutions made by me, Dominic Savio, in the year 1849, on the day of my First Communion, at the age of seven.</i></dd>
<dd><i>1. I will go to Confession often, and as frequently to Holy Communion as my confessor allows.</i></dd>
<dd><i>2. I wish to sanctify the Sundays and festivals in a special manner.</i></dd>
<dd><i>3. My friends shall be Jesus and Mary.</i></dd>
<dd><i>4. Death rather than sin.</i><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="At_the_county_school">At the county school</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: At the county school">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>For secondary education, Dominic had to go to another school and it was decided that he would go to the County School at <a href="/wiki/Castelnuovo_Don_Bosco" title="Castelnuovo Don Bosco">Castelnuovo</a>, three miles (5&#160;km) from his home.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceB_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceB-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> (Castelnuovo d' Asti, now Castelnuovo John Bosco, was the birthplace of another contemporary of John Bosco, <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Cafasso" title="Joseph Cafasso">Joseph Cafasso</a>, also a saint. He was four years the senior of John Bosco, and was Bosco's mentor and advisor.<sup id="cite_ref-catholictradition.org_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholictradition.org-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>)
</p><p>Now ten years old, Dominic walked daily to and from school. In his biography of Dominic Savio, John Bosco records how a local farmer once asked Dominic, on a hot sunny day, if he was not tired from walking, and received the reply: "Nothing seems tiresome or painful when you are working for a master who pays well."<sup id="cite_ref-TraditionalCatholic_2006_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TraditionalCatholic_2006-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Don Bosco also notes that Dominic refused to go swimming<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> with his friends since Dominic considered that in such a situation, it would be "also easy to offend God",<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceB_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceB-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> he believed that on a previous occasion his friends behaved in, what was to him, a vulgar manner.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> In his biography Bosco records that Fr. Allora, the head of this school, had this to say about Dominic: "...Hence it may very well be said that he was Savio (wise), not only in name, but in fact, viz., in his studies, in <a href="/wiki/Piety" title="Piety">piety</a>, in conversation and his dealing with others, and in all his actions. ..."<sup id="cite_ref-TraditionalCatholic_2006_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TraditionalCatholic_2006-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Under_John_Bosco.27s_mentorship"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Under_John_Bosco's_mentorship">Under John Bosco's mentorship</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Under John Bosco&#039;s mentorship">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Meeting_with_John_Bosco">Meeting with John Bosco</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Meeting with John Bosco">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:162px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Donbosco_furbo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Donbosco_furbo.jpg/160px-Donbosco_furbo.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="178" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ce/Donbosco_furbo.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="212" data-file-height="236" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Donbosco_furbo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. John Bosco (Don Bosco), the spiritual mentor of St. Dominic Savio</div></div></div>
<p>It was Fr. Giuseppe Cugliero,<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceB_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceB-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Dominic's teacher at school, who gave a high account of him to John Bosco and recommended that Bosco meet him during the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary">Feast of the Rosary</a>, when he would take his boys to <a href="/wiki/Murialdo" title="Murialdo">Murialdo</a>. Accordingly, accompanied by his father, Dominic met John Bosco on the first Monday in the month of October 1854.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceC_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceC-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> John Bosco records this conversation in some detail. He notes that Dominic was eager to go to Turin with John Bosco, and that he wished to become a <a href="/wiki/Priest#Roman_Catholic_and_Orthodox" title="Priest">priest</a> after completing his studies in that town.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To test Dominic's intelligence, Don Bosco gave him a copy of <i>The Catholic Readings</i> (pamphlets on the subject of Catholic <a href="/wiki/Apologetics" title="Apologetics">Apologetics</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> asking him to recite a particular page by heart and explain its meaning the next day, and then spoke for a while with Dominic's father. Ten minutes later, he found Dominic was beside him reciting the page and explaining its meaning satisfactorily.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> This meeting was the beginning of their relationship, the result of which was that John Bosco agreed to take Dominic to <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> with him.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceC_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceC-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="At_the_Oratory_of_St._Francis_de_Sales">At the Oratory of St. Francis de Sales</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: At the Oratory of St. Francis de Sales">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco records that when Dominic arrived at the Oratory, he at once placed himself under his guidance.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic2_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic2-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> He also notes that Dominic worked diligently and followed the school rules. He would happily listen to talks and <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">sermons</a> (even if they tended to be lengthy at times), and would, without hesitation, ask for clarification on points that were not clear to him. John Bosco also notes how Dominic was obedient to his teachers and chose his companions carefully.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This happened in 1854, when, in Rome, the <a href="/wiki/Dogma" title="Dogma">dogma</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a> of Mary was being defined. Preparations for the observation of this feast were thus going on at the Oratory. Don Bosco records that, at the advice of his <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>, Dominic renewed his First Communion promises at the altar of <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Mary</a> at the Oratory. Bosco says that, from this point the result of Dominic's attempts towards holy life were so apparent, that he (John Bosco) took to recording the various incidents that occurred for future reference.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic2_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic2-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John Bosco's mother, who was called "Mamma Margaret" remarked to him of Dominic, <i>"You have many good boys, but none can match the good heart and soul of Dominic Savio. I see him so often at prayer, staying in church after the others; every day he slips out of the playground to make a visit to the Blessed Sacrament. When he is in church he is like an angel living in Paradise."</i><sup id="cite_ref-salesiansuk_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-salesiansuk-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Resolve_to_become_a_saint">Resolve to become a saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Resolve to become a saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Around six months after Dominic had come to the Oratory, he had the occasion to listen to a talk on <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">sainthood</a>. John Bosco records that the talk had three main points that impressed Dominic:<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated3_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated3-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ol><li>That it is God's will that each one should become a saint.</li>
<li>That it is easy to become a saint.</li>
<li>That there is a great reward waiting in heaven for those who try to become saints.</li></ol>
<p>This inspired Dominic to take a conscious decision to become a saint. The immediate result of this was that, not being sure how to live a saintly life, and worried about it, he was quiet and worried for the next few days. Noticing this, John Bosco spoke to Dominic and advised him to resume his customary cheerfulness, persevere in his regular life of study and religious practices, and especially not neglect being with his companions in games and recreation.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> On learning that his <a href="/wiki/Dominic" title="Dominic">first name</a> meant "belonging to God", his desire to be a saint intensified.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated3_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated3-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> Dominic's spiritual growth progressed under the guidance of Don Bosco. Clifford Stevens says in his biography of Savio,
<i>"In other circumstances, Dominic might have become a little self-righteous snob, but Don Bosco showed him the heroism of the ordinary and the sanctity of common sense."</i><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Attempts_to_do_penances">Attempts to do penances</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Attempts to do penances">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In his desire to become a saint, Dominic attempted to perform physical <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penances</a>, like making his bed uncomfortable with small stones and pieces of wood, sleeping with a thin covering in winter, wearing a <a href="/wiki/Cilice" title="Cilice">hair shirt</a>, and fasting on bread and water. When his superiors (i.e., John Bosco, or his Rector, or his confessor) learned of this, they forbade him from doing <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">bodily mortification</a>, as it would affect his health.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John Bosco told Dominic that as a schoolboy, the best penance would be to perform all his duties with perfection and humility, and that obedience was the greatest sacrifice.<sup id="cite_ref-Newland_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Newland-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Thus, Dominic formed an important aspect of his philosophy of life, which was, in his words, "I can't do big things but I want everything to be for the glory of God."<sup id="cite_ref-Biography_of_St._Dominic_Savio_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Biography_of_St._Dominic_Savio-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Don Bosco notes that from that time on, Dominic did not complain about the food or the weather, unlike some other boys at the Oratory, bore all suffering cheerfully, and practised custody of his eyes and tongue.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> Eugenio Ceria, a <a href="/wiki/Salesian" class="mw-redirect" title="Salesian">Salesian</a> commentator on the autobiography of John Bosco, (<i>Memoirs of the Oratory of Saint Francis de Sales</i>) notes that by this time, owing to his experience as an educator, John Bosco's ideas on several <a href="/wiki/Pedagogy" title="Pedagogy">pedagogical</a> and spiritual principles were well developed and linked and this led him to associate the fulfillment of daily duties with holiness in his advice to Savio.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Immaculate_Conception_Sodality">The Immaculate Conception Sodality</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: The Immaculate Conception Sodality">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg/150px-Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="216" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg/225px-Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg/300px-Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1888" data-file-height="2717" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Perez_Murillo_021.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/The_Immaculate_Conception_of_El_Escorial" title="The Immaculate Conception of El Escorial">An iconic painting</a> depicting Mary as the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>. The definition of the dogma of the Immaculate Conception had a profound effect on the spirituality of Dominic Savio.</div></div></div>
<p>The definition of the dogma of the Immaculate Conception of Mary influenced Dominic and he was anxious to create at the school a lasting reminder of this event.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic3_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic3-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> He now felt that he had not long to live. With the help of his friends, he started a group called the <i><a href="/wiki/Sodality_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sodality (Catholic Church)">Sodality</a> of Mary Immaculate</i>, the main aim of which was to be to obtain the special protection of Mary during life and at the time of death. The means Dominic proposed to this end were: (1) to honour, and to bring others to honour, Mary by different means, and (2) to encourage frequent Communion. On 8 June, he and his friends read out together before the altar of Mary at the Oratory, the set of rules they had drawn up. There were twenty-one articles (which were recorded by John Bosco in his biography), ending with an appeal to Mary for her assistance. These were submitted to the <a href="/wiki/Rector_(ecclesiastical)" title="Rector (ecclesiastical)">rector</a>, and, after careful perusal, he gave his approval, under certain conditions.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated5_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated5-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
One of the members of this Sodality, Giuseppe Bongioanni,<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated5_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated5-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> (who was later ordained a priest) was later to found the <i>Sodality of the Blessed Sacrament</i>, which became a traditional sodality in Catholic schools.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic3_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic3-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Preparation_for_a_holy_death">Preparation for a holy death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Preparation for a holy death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>All the pupils under John Bosco observed a monthly event called <i>The Exercise of a Happy Death</i>; this practice continues under the name <i>The Monthly Day of Recollection</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donboscowest_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donboscowest-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> This practice was encouraged by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated6_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated6-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Part of this was to make a Confession and Communion as though they were the last ones to be made before death. Bosco notes that Dominic observed this practice devoutly, and that one day, Dominic said that he would be the first amongst the group to die.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated6_40-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated6-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> During the month of May, before his death, the intensity of his spiritual practices increased. John Bosco notes that he said, "Let me do what I can this year; if I am here next year I'll let you know what my plans are."<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Failing_health">Failing health</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Failing health">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Dominic's health was steadily deteriorating, but he spent most of his time with his friends, talking with them, and encouraging those who were experiencing troubles.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated7_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated7-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> He also helped at the school infirmary whenever his companions were admitted. On the recommendation of doctors, Dominic was sent home to recover from his ill health, but a few days later Bosco found him back at the Oratory. Despite his affection for Dominic, and his wish to allow Dominic to remain at the Oratory, John Bosco decided to follow the recommendation of the doctors, especially since Dominic had developed a severe cough and he wrote to Dominic's father, fixing the date of his departure on 1 March 1857. Though Dominic said that he wanted to spend his last days at the Oratory, he accepted this decision and spent the evening before his departure at John Bosco's side, discussing spiritual matters. (Bosco recorded a part of this conversation in his biography of Dominic).<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated7_42-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated7-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> On the morning of his departure, Don Bosco notes that Dominic made the <i>Exercise of a Happy Death</i> with great zeal, even saying that this would be his final such devotion. He said his farewell to John Bosco, asking as a keepsake that Bosco add his name to the list of those who would participate in the <a href="/wiki/Indulgence#The_Indulgence" title="Indulgence">Plenary Indulgence</a> that John Bosco had received from the Pope, to which John Bosco readily agreed.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated7_42-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated7-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> He then took leave of his friends with great affection, which surprised them, for his illness was not considered by many of his companions to be serious.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic4_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic4-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:212px;"><a href="/wiki/File:DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/5b/DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg/210px-DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg" decoding="async" width="210" height="297" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/5b/DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg/315px-DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/5b/DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg/420px-DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3537" data-file-height="4999" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:DominicSavioAltarTurin.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The altar of St. Dominic Savio in <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_Help_of_Christians,_Turin" title="Basilica of Our Lady Help of Christians, Turin">Basilica of Our Lady Help of Christians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a>, under which holds the relic of the saint</div></div></div>
<p>In his first four days at home his appetite decreased and his cough worsened; this prompted his parents to send him to the doctor, who, at once, ordered bed rest.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Inflammation" title="Inflammation">Inflammation</a> was diagnosed, and as was the custom at that time, the doctor decided to perform <a href="/wiki/Bloodletting" title="Bloodletting">bloodletting</a>. The doctor cut Dominic's arm ten times in the space of four days and it is now considered that this probably hastened his death.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Dominic_Savio_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Dominic_Savio-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> In his biography, John Bosco records that Dominic was calm throughout the procedure. The doctor assured his parents that the danger had passed and now it only remained for him to recuperate. Dominic, however, was sure that his death was approaching, and asked that he be allowed to make his Confession and receive Communion. Though they thought it unnecessary, his parents sent for the <a href="/wiki/Parish#Roman_Catholic_Church" title="Parish">parish priest</a> who heard Dominic's confession and administered the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After four days, despite the conviction of the doctor and his parents that he would get better, Dominic asked that he be given the <a href="/wiki/Anointing_of_the_Sick" class="mw-redirect" title="Anointing of the Sick">Anointing of the Sick</a> in preparation for death. Again, his parents agreed, to please him. On 9 March, he was given the <a href="/wiki/Papal_blessing" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal blessing">papal blessing</a> and he said the <i>Confiteor</i>. Don Bosco records that throughout these days, he stayed serene and calm.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated8_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated8-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> On the evening of 9 March 1857, after being visited by his parish priest, he asked his father to read him the prayers for the <i>Exercise of a Happy Death</i> from his book of devotions. Then he slept a while, and shortly awakened and said in a clear voice, "Goodbye, Dad, goodbye ... what was it the parish priest suggested to me ... I don't seem to remember ... Oh, what wonderful things I see ...".<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated8_47-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated8-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> With these words, Dominic died, though, at first, it appeared to his father that he was asleep.<sup id="cite_ref-threelives26_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-threelives26-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> Dominic's father wrote in a letter to John Bosco, conveying the news of the death of his son,
</p>
<blockquote><p>With my heart full of grief I send you this sad news. Dominic, my dear son and your child in God, like a white lily, like <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a>, gave his soul to God on 9 March after having received with the greatest devotion the Last Sacraments and the Papal Blessing.<sup id="cite_ref-threelives26_48-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-threelives26-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notable_incidents_in_the_life_of_Dominic_Savio">Notable incidents in the life of Dominic Savio</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Notable incidents in the life of Dominic Savio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:227px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Pius_xi_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Pius_xi_1.jpg/225px-Pius_xi_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="225" height="176" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Pius_xi_1.jpg/338px-Pius_xi_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Pius_xi_1.jpg/450px-Pius_xi_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="392" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Pius_xi_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> described Dominic Savio as "small in size, but a towering giant in spirit."</div></div></div>
<p>In order to give the reader a well rounded picture of Dominic's personality Don Bosco recorded several incidents from Dominic's life in his biography.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Before_he_joined_the_Oratory">Before he joined the Oratory</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Before he joined the Oratory">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="At_the_school_at_Mondonio">At the school at Mondonio</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: At the school at Mondonio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Don Bosco records this from the testimony of Fr Giuseppe Cugliero.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> One day, in the absence of his teacher, two of Dominic's classmates stuffed the room-heating iron stove with snow and rubbish as a prank. Fearing expulsion, they blamed Dominic. Fr. Cugliero soundly berated Dominic in front of the class and Dominic bore this silently.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> The following day, the true culprits were discovered. On being asked why he had remained silent, Dominic replied that he had thought that he would be let off with a scolding whereas the other boys might have been expelled. Dominic added that Jesus had remained silent when blamed unjustly and that he was trying to imitate him.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Dominic_Savio_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Dominic_Savio-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Mary Reed Newland, in her book, suggests that, since Dominic was yet to meet John Bosco, this incident is indicative of the upbringing his parents had given him.<sup id="cite_ref-Newland_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Newland-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="At_the_Oratory">At the Oratory</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: At the Oratory">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Resolves_a_conflict">Resolves a conflict</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=19" title="Edit section: Resolves a conflict">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>At the Oratory, two of his friends had a disagreement and decided to fight each other by throwing stones. As they were older and stronger than Dominic (he had been promoted from first form to second form
<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup>) physical intervention was not possible. He tried to reason with them but with no positive result. Thus, on the day of the fight, he went with them to the site where the fight was to take place, and just before they could start, he placed himself between them, and holding up his <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a>, requested that they throw their first stones at him. Ashamed, the two boys gave up their fight. Dominic then persuaded them to go to Confession.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Custody_of_the_eyes">Custody of the eyes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=20" title="Edit section: Custody of the eyes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco records that once a boy who was visiting had brought with him a "magazine with bad pictures", and a group of fascinated boys were looking. On finding out, Dominic snatched the magazine and tore it up, saying, "You know well enough that one look is enough to stain your souls, and yet you go feasting your eyes on this."<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated4_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated4-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Influence_over_his_friends">Influence over his friends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=21" title="Edit section: Influence over his friends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco records that Dominic spent a lot of time with his friends, encouraging them in their devotions, discouraging those with a habit of <a href="/wiki/Profanity" title="Profanity">swearing</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> and teaching Catechism at Sunday School.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup> Bosco also records that he would encourage his friends to make frequent use of the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament" title="Sacrament">sacrament</a> of <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a> and take Communion regularly, even giving them encouragement and advice in spiritual practices during games.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> John Bosco makes particular mention of two of Dominic's friends, Camillo Gavio of Tortona,<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> and John Massaglia of Marmorito.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup> (These two friends were dead by the time John Bosco wrote the biography, as he thought it best not to write about the friends of Dominic who were still alive.)
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Devotions_practised_by_Dominic">Devotions practised by Dominic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=22" title="Edit section: Devotions practised by Dominic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco narrates that before he came to the Oratory, Dominic made his Confession and took Communion once a month. After hearing a homily on the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament" title="Sacrament">Sacraments</a>, he chose a priest as his regular <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">Confessor</a>, (to whom Dominic made his Confessions until the end of his stay at the Oratory). The regularity with which Dominic approached the sacraments increased and, at the end of that year, as per the advice of his confessor, Dominic was taking Communion daily.<sup id="cite_ref-conf1_58-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conf1-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup> He had a special intention for the Eucharist each day of the week. John Bosco notes that, whenever permitted, Dominic eagerly accompanied the priest when he took the <a href="/wiki/Viaticum" title="Viaticum">Viaticum</a>, and that he also kept the habit of kneeling down in the street if he encountered the Eucharist being carried by a priest, as was the custom in Catholic countries.<sup id="cite_ref-conf2_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conf2-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Incidents_with_special_spiritual_significance">Incidents with special spiritual significance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=23" title="Edit section: Incidents with special spiritual significance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span id=".22Distractions.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Distractions&quot;">"Distractions"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=24" title="Edit section: &quot;Distractions&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco records that Dominic occasionally had <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">intense experiences</a> during prayer, which Dominic described as such:
<i>"It is silly of me; I get a distraction and lose the thread of my prayers and then I see such wonderful things that the hours pass by like minutes”</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated9_60-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated9-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
On one occasion, he was missing from breakfast and the rector finally found him in the <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a>, standing motionless and gazing at the <a href="/wiki/Church_tabernacle" title="Church tabernacle">tabernacle</a>. He was not aware that the morning <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> had ended.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic5_61-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic5-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> On another occasion, John Bosco records that he saw Dominic in the chapel, speaking to God, and then waiting, as though listening to a reply.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated9_60-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated9-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Special_knowledge">Special knowledge</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=25" title="Edit section: Special knowledge">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco narrates how Dominic came to his room one day and urged him to accompany him. He led Bosco through many streets to a block of flats, rang the doorbell, and at once, went away. When the door opened, John Bosco found that within, there was a dying man who was desperately asking for a priest to make his last <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic5_61-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic5-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> Later, John Bosco asked Dominic how he had known about that man. However, since the question made Dominic uncomfortable, John Bosco did not press the matter.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated9_60-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated9-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:G.P.A.Healy,_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_(1871).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/G.P.A.Healy%2C_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_%281871%29.jpg/150px-G.P.A.Healy%2C_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_%281871%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/G.P.A.Healy%2C_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_%281871%29.jpg/225px-G.P.A.Healy%2C_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_%281871%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/G.P.A.Healy%2C_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_%281871%29.jpg/300px-G.P.A.Healy%2C_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_%281871%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:G.P.A.Healy,_Portrait_of_Pope_Pius_IX_(1871).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> formally defined the dogma of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a> and figured in a <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">vision</a> that occurred to Dominic Savio.</div></div></div>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="The_vision_of_England">The vision of England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=26" title="Edit section: The vision of England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>John Bosco records that Dominic once recounted to him a <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">vision</a> he had:<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated9_60-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated9-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd><i>"... . One morning as I was making my thanksgiving after Communion, a very strong distraction took hold of me. I thought I saw a great plain full of people enveloped in thick fog. They were walking about like people who had lost their way and did not know which way to turn. Someone near me said: 'This is <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>'. I was just going to ask some questions, when I saw <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> just like I have seen him in pictures. He was robed magnificently and carried in his hand a torch alive with flames. As he walked slowly towards that immense gathering of people, the leaping flames from the torch dispelled the fog, and the people stood in the splendour of the noonday sun. 'That torch', said the one beside me, 'is the Catholic Faith, which is going to light up England'".</i></dd></dl>
<p>At his last goodbyes, Dominic requested John Bosco to tell the pope of his vision, which he did in 1858. The pope felt that this confirmed the <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX#England_and_Wales" title="Pope Pius IX">plans he had already made concerning England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-traditionalcatholic5_61-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-traditionalcatholic5-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span id="His_mother.27s_pregnancy"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="His_mother's_pregnancy">His mother's pregnancy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=27" title="Edit section: His mother&#039;s pregnancy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>On 12 September 1856, Dominic asked John Bosco permission to go home, saying that his mother was ill, though he had received no communication. Dominic's mother was then expecting a baby and was in great <a href="/wiki/Labor_pain" class="mw-redirect" title="Labor pain">pain</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> and when Dominic reached the house, he hugged and kissed his mother, and then left. His mother felt her pain leave her and Dominic's baby sister, Catherine, was born. The women assisting at the birth found that Dominic had left a green <a href="/wiki/Scapular" title="Scapular">scapular</a> around his mother's neck. His sister Theresa later wore this same scapular when she was in labour. She testified that it had been passed around to several other pregnant women and was later lost.<sup id="cite_ref-donboscowest.org_63-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donboscowest.org-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span id="Charles_Savio.27s_vision_of_Dominic_after_his_death"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Charles_Savio's_vision_of_Dominic_after_his_death">Charles Savio's vision of Dominic after his death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=28" title="Edit section: Charles Savio&#039;s vision of Dominic after his death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> of Dominic Savio grew with an event narrated by his father:<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<dl><dd><i>"I was in the greatest affliction at the loss of my son, and was consumed by a desire to know what was his position in the other world. God deigned to comfort me. About a month after his death, during a very restless night, I saw, as it were, the ceiling opened, and Dominic appeared in the midst of dazzling light. I was beside myself at this sight, and cried out: "O Dominic, my son, are you already in Paradise?" "Yes," he replied, "I am in Heaven." Then pray for your brothers and sisters, and your mother and father, that we may all come to join you one day in Heaven." "Yes, yes, I will pray," was the answer. "Then he disappeared, and the room became as before."</i></dd></dl>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Life_of_Dominic_Savio"><i>The Life of Dominic Savio</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=29" title="Edit section: The Life of Dominic Savio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Life_of_Dominic_Savio_(front_cover).JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/f/fa/Life_of_Dominic_Savio_%28front_cover%29.JPG/150px-Life_of_Dominic_Savio_%28front_cover%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="150" height="248" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/f/fa/Life_of_Dominic_Savio_%28front_cover%29.JPG 1.5x" data-file-width="172" data-file-height="284" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Life_of_Dominic_Savio_(front_cover).JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Don Bosco's biography of Dominic Savio contributed to his canonisation.</div></div></div>
<p>Soon after the death of Dominic, John Bosco wrote his biography, <i>The Life of Dominic Savio</i>, which contributed to his canonisation.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_of_the_Day_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_of_the_Day-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The original <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a> edition was considered so well written during the time of Don Bosco that, along with his <i>History of Italy</i> and <i>Ecclesiastical History</i>, it was used in many public schools as part of the course materials on the Italian language.<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup> Among the other writings of John Bosco<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup> are the <i>Biography of Fr. Joseph Cafasso</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-catholictradition.org_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholictradition.org-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> <i>The Life of Francis Besucco</i> and <i>The Life of Michael Magone</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg/150px-His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="181" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg/225px-His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg/300px-His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1042" data-file-height="1260" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:His_Holiness_Pope_St._Pius_X_%E2%80%93_edited.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> set in motion the canonisation process for Dominic Savio.</div></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Savio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=30" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Though some were of an opinion that Dominic was too young to be canonised, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> insisted that this was not so, and started the <a href="/wiki/Canonisation#Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">process of his canonisation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup> Dominic Savio was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 1933 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>, was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1950 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>, and declared a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in 1954.<sup id="cite_ref-Biography_of_St._Dominic_Savio_35-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Biography_of_St._Dominic_Savio-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Pope Pius XI described him as "small in size, but a towering giant in spirit."<sup id="cite_ref-salesiansuk_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-salesiansuk-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
