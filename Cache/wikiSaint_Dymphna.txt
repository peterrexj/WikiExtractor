<p><b>Dymphna</b><sup id="cite_ref-nssd_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nssd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> is a <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">Christian saint</a> honoured in <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> traditions.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to tradition, she lived in the 7th century and was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> by her father.
</p><p>The story of Dymphna was first recorded in the 13th century by a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of the Church of <a href="/wiki/Aubert_of_Avranches" title="Aubert of Avranches">Aubert of Avranches</a> at <a href="/wiki/Cambrai" title="Cambrai">Cambrai</a>, France. It was commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Cambrai#Bishops" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Cambrai">Guiard of Laon</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cambrai">Bishop of Cambrai</a> (1238-1248).
</p><p>The author expressly stated that his work was based upon a long-standing oral tradition as well as a persuasive history of miraculous healings of the mentally ill.<sup id="cite_ref-Wagener_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wagener-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dymphna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Dymphna's name (pronounced <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="&#39;f&#39; in &#39;find&#39;">f</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">DIMF</span>-nə</i></a> or <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="&#39;p&#39; in &#39;pie&#39;">p</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">DIMP</span>-nə</i></a>) derives from the <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a> <span title="Irish-language text"><i lang="ga">damh</i></span> ('poet') and suffix <i>-ait</i> ('little' or 'feminine'), therefore meaning 'poetess'. It is also spelled <b>Dimpna</b>, <b> Dymphnart</b>, <b>Dympna</b> or <b>Damnat</b>, this last closer to the Irish spelling <b>Damhnait</b><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> (pronounced <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="ga-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Irish" title="Help:IPA/Irish">[ˈd̪ˠəun̪ˠətʲ]</a></span>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Story_of_her_life_and_death">Story of her life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dymphna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Story of her life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Catholic and Orthodox tradition, Dymphna was born in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> in the 7th century. Dymphna's father Damon was a petty king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Oriel" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Oriel">Oriel</a>. Her mother was a devout Christian.
</p><p>When Dymphna was 14 years old, she consecrated herself to Christ and took a vow of chastity. Shortly thereafter, her mother died. Damon had loved his wife deeply, and in the aftermath of her death his mental health sharply deteriorated. Eventually the king's counsellors pressed him to remarry. Damon agreed, but only on the condition that his bride would be as beautiful as his deceased wife. After searching fruitlessly, Damon began to desire his daughter because of her strong resemblance to her mother.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:287px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_(Attr.)_or_Gerard_Seghers_(Attr.).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_%28Attr.%29_or_Gerard_Seghers_%28Attr.%29.jpg/285px-Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_%28Attr.%29_or_Gerard_Seghers_%28Attr.%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="285" height="242" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_%28Attr.%29_or_Gerard_Seghers_%28Attr.%29.jpg/428px-Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_%28Attr.%29_or_Gerard_Seghers_%28Attr.%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_%28Attr.%29_or_Gerard_Seghers_%28Attr.%29.jpg/570px-Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_%28Attr.%29_or_Gerard_Seghers_%28Attr.%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="847" data-file-height="719" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_of_St_Dymphna_and_St_Gerebernus_by_Jacques_de_l%27Ange_(Attr.)_or_Gerard_Seghers_(Attr.).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Martyrdom of St Dymphna and St Gerebernus</i> by <a href="/wiki/Jacques_de_l%27Ange" title="Jacques de l&#39;Ange">Jacques de l'Ange</a></div></div></div>
<p>When Dymphna learned of her father's intentions, she swore to uphold her vows and fled his court along with her confessor Father <a href="/wiki/Gerebernus" class="mw-redirect" title="Gerebernus">Gerebernus</a>, two trusted servants, and the king's fool. Together they sailed towards the continent, eventually landing in what is present-day Belgium, where they took refuge in the town of <a href="/wiki/Gheel" class="mw-redirect" title="Gheel">Gheel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wagener_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wagener-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One tradition states that once settled in Geel, Dymphna built a hospice for the poor and sick of the region. However, it was through the use of her wealth that her father would eventually ascertain her whereabouts, as some of the coins used enabled her father to trace them to Belgium.<sup id="cite_ref-Wagener_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wagener-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Damon sent his agents to pursue his daughter and her companions. When their hiding place was discovered, Damon travelled to Geel to recover his daughter. Damon ordered his soldiers to kill Gerebernus and tried to force Dymphna to return with him to Ireland, but she resisted. Furious, Damon drew his sword and struck off his daughter's head. She was said to have been 15 years old when she died.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  After Dymphna and Gerebernus were killed, the residents of Geel buried them in a nearby cave. Years later, they decided to move the remains to a more suitable location. 
</p><p>In 1349 a church honouring Dymphna was built in Geel. By 1480, so many pilgrims were coming from all over Europe, seeking treatment for psychiatric disorders that the church housing for them was expanded. Soon the sanctuary for those considered “mad” was again full to overflowing, and the townspeople began taking them into their own homes. Thus began a tradition for the ongoing care of those with psychiatric conditions that has endured for over 500 years and is still studied and admired today. Patients were, and still are, taken into the homes of Geel's inhabitants.  Never called patients, they are called boarders, and are treated as ordinary and useful members of the town.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> They are treated as members of the host family. They work, most often in menial labour, and in return, they become part of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Some stay a few months, some decades, some for their entire lives. At its peak in the 1930s, over 4,000 'boarders' were housed with the town's inhabitants.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dymphna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:272px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St-Dymphna_church.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/St-Dymphna_church.jpg/270px-St-Dymphna_church.jpg" decoding="async" width="270" height="203" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/St-Dymphna_church.jpg/405px-St-Dymphna_church.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/St-Dymphna_church.jpg/540px-St-Dymphna_church.jpg 2x" data-file-width="6732" data-file-height="5064" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St-Dymphna_church.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Dymphna Church, Geel, Belgium</div></div></div>
<p>The remains of Dymphna were later put into a silver <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">reliquary</a> and placed in a church in Geel named in her honour. The remains of Gerebernus were moved to <a href="/wiki/Xanten" title="Xanten">Xanten</a>, Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> During the late 15th century the original St. Dymphna Church in Geel burned down. A second "Church of St. Dymphna" was then built and consecrated in 1532. The church still stands on the site where her body is believed to have first been buried.<sup id="cite_ref-fma_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fma-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to nineteenth-century Irish Catholic priest <a href="/wiki/John_O%27Hanlon_(writer)" title="John O&#39;Hanlon (writer)">John Canon O'Hanlon</a>, the commemoration of Dymphna's and of Gerebern's arrival at Antwerp was celebrated on April 11,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> according to a <a href="/wiki/Carthusians" title="Carthusians">Carthusian</a> Martyrology, however their chief feast day was on May 15,<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> the day of their final martyrdom. According to tradition, <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgy" title="Thaumaturgy">miracles</a> occurred immediately after Dymphna's tomb was discovered.
</p><p>Dymphna is known as the Lily of Éire, due to her spotless virtue. She is traditionally portrayed wearing a crown, dressed in ermine and royal robes, and holding a sword. In modern versions she holds the sword awkwardly, as it symbolises her martyrdom, but in the older versions seen on numerous statues and stained glass images, her sword is pricking the neck of a demon; symbolising her title of Demon Slayer. She is also often portrayed holding a lamp, with the chained devil at her feet.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some modern holy cards portray Dymphna in green and white, holding a book and white lilies.
</p><p>During the 2022 season, the St Dymphna's Church, Geel, Belgium, is hosting an exposition showing the restored altarpiece venerating Dymphna by Goossen Van der Weyden. <sup id="cite_ref-zotvandimpna_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zotvandimpna-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-phoebusfoundation_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-phoebusfoundation-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dymphna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Dymphna is the patron saint of mental illness.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The US National Shrine of St. Dymphna is located inside <a href="/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Catholic_Church_(Massillon,_Ohio)" title="St. Mary&#39;s Catholic Church (Massillon, Ohio)">St. Mary's Catholic Church</a> in Massillon, Ohio.<sup id="cite_ref-nssd_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nssd-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The shrine was destroyed by a fire in 2015, but reopened in December 2016 and is still open to pilgrims and visitors.  St. Dymphna's Special School is located in <a href="/wiki/Ballina,_County_Mayo" title="Ballina, County Mayo">Ballina, County Mayo, Republic of Ireland</a> and operates under the patronage of Western Care Association.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
