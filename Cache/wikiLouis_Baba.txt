<p><b>Ludovicus Baba</b>, also known as Louis Baba or <span title="Japanese-language text"><span lang="ja">ルイス馬場</span></span>, (Unknown – 25 August 1624) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Franciscan Tertiary</a> from Japan. He was beatified in July 1867 by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius IX">Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FRO_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VAT_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VAT-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludovicus_Baba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ludovicus Baba was born in Japan. He was befriended by a young missionary friar, <a href="/wiki/Luis_Sotelo" title="Luis Sotelo">Luis Sotelo</a>, from the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Franciscan Order</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Servant_of_Father_Sotelo">Servant of Father Sotelo</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludovicus_Baba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Servant of Father Sotelo">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Luis_Sotelo" title="Luis Sotelo">Luis Sotelo</a> tried to establish a Franciscan church in the area of Edo. The church was destroyed in 1612, following the interdiction of Christianity in the territories of the <a href="/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate" title="Tokugawa shogunate">Tokugawa</a> <a href="/wiki/Shogunate" class="mw-redirect" title="Shogunate">shogunate</a> on 21 April 1612. After a period of intense missionary activity by the Catholic Church, <a href="/wiki/Tokugawa_Hidetada" title="Tokugawa Hidetada">Tokugawa Hidetada</a>, the second <i><a href="/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgun" class="mw-redirect" title="Shōgun">shōgun</a></i> of the Tokugawa dynasty, issued a decree which banned the practice and teaching of the Christian faith, and under the threat of loss of life, all the missionaries had to leave Japan. This decree started the bloody persecution of Christians, which lasted several decades.<sup id="cite_ref-FRO_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After healing a concubine of the powerful <i><a href="/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D" class="mw-redirect" title="Daimyō">daimyō</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Sendai_Domain" title="Sendai Domain">Sendai</a>, <a href="/wiki/Date_Masamune" title="Date Masamune">Date Masamune</a>, in Edo, Sotelo was invited to the northern part of Japan, in the area controlled by Date, under whom Christianity was still allowed. He returned to Tokyo the following year and constructed and inaugurated a new church on 12 May 1613, in the area of <a href="/wiki/Asakusa" title="Asakusa">Asakusa</a> Torigoe. The Bakufu reacted by arresting the Christians, and Sotelo himself was put in the Kodenma-chō prison. Seven fellow Japanese Christians, who had been arrested with Sotelo, were executed on 1 July, but he was freed following a special request by Date Masamune.<sup id="cite_ref-HRC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HRC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fluent in Japanese, Sotelo planned and acted as translator on a Japanese embassy sent by Date Masamune to <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> on 28 October 1613. Baba accompanied Sotelo and the representatives of Japan, on their voyage to Europe, as his personal servant. The embassy was headed by <a href="/wiki/Hasekura_Rokuemon_Tsunenaga" class="mw-redirect" title="Hasekura Rokuemon Tsunenaga">Hasekura Rokuemon Tsunenaga</a>, and crossed the Pacific Ocean to <a href="/wiki/Acapulco" title="Acapulco">Acapulco</a> on board the Japanese-built galleon <a href="/wiki/Japanese_warship_San_Juan_Bautista" class="mw-redirect" title="Japanese warship San Juan Bautista">San Juan Bautista</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SNA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SNA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Baba cooked and resided with Sotelo during their nine and-a-half months stay in <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a> in 1614.<sup id="cite_ref-SBA_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SBA-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The embassy continued to <a href="/wiki/Veracruz_(city)" title="Veracruz (city)">Vera Cruz</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sanl%C3%BAcar_de_Barrameda" title="Sanlúcar de Barrameda">Sanlúcar de Barrameda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, and Madrid. Sotelo had the Japanese receive <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> while in Madrid, before accompanying them to an audience with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a> in Rome. The embassy was a product of ambitions of Sotelo to increase the spread of the church in Japan and of Date Masamune to provide more priests for the churches of his Christian subjects and to establish trade between Sendai and New Spain, and it had the approval of the shogun, Tokugawa Ieyasu.<sup id="cite_ref-HRC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HRC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Paul V appointed Sotelo to be new Bishop of <a href="/wiki/%C5%8Csh%C5%AB,_Iwate" title="Ōshū, Iwate">Ōshū</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Mutsu_Province" title="Mutsu Province">Northern Honshū</a>). When the party returned to <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a> in June 1618, Bishop-elect Sotelo kept his personal secretary, <a href="/wiki/Ludovicus_Sasada" title="Ludovicus Sasada">Ludovicus Sasada</a>, and his servant, Baba, near.<sup id="cite_ref-SNA_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SNA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_Japan_and_martyrdom">Return to Japan and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludovicus_Baba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Return to Japan and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1622, Sotelo, Sasada and Baba, set sail for Japan, disguised as merchants. As the Chinese captain suspected them for religious missionaries, they were surrendered to governmental authorities at Nagasaki. Within six months, these three prisoners were transferred to the new prison at <a href="/wiki/%C5%8Cmura,_Nagasaki" title="Ōmura, Nagasaki">Ōmura</a>. Their imprisonment almost lasted for two years, however during this time, Baba was received into the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order Secular of Saint Francis</a> by Sotelo. Two other missionary priests, <a href="/wiki/Pedro_V%C3%A1squez" title="Pedro Vásquez">Pedro Vásquez</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">OP</a> and <a href="/wiki/Miguel_de_Carvalho" title="Miguel de Carvalho">Miguel de Carvalho</a>, <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">SJ</a>, eventually joined these three Franciscans in captivity.<sup id="cite_ref-SNA_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SNA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their life in the Ōmura prison was like a friary. These priests performed their religious exercises and celebrated Holy Mass. The imprisoned Christians at the risk of their lives procured what was needed. On 24 August, a death sentence was ordered.<sup id="cite_ref-FRO_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FRO-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SAN_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAN-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> When they received the news they sang the <i><a href="/wiki/Te_Deum" title="Te Deum">Te Deum</a></i>. At ten in the morning, they were taken by boat to the place of execution. They were tied to stakes and a fire was placed around them. Sotelo was placed in the center and the two Japanese were on the outside of the circle. Since the fire burned the ropes of the two Japanese Franciscans, Sasada and Baba prostrated themselves towards the center stake and asked for the Bishop-elect's blessing. Sotelo invoked God's blessing upon them and they returned to their stakes until the fire and smoke overcame them. As Sotelo was the farthest from the flames, the executioners put more wood on the fire closer to him.<sup id="cite_ref-SNA_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SNA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After these martyrs died, the soldiers burned their remains in another fire and took their ashes in a boat to dispose of them at sea, to prevent them being carried away by the Christians, even though a witness was able to collect some of Pedro Vásquez's cremains and had them deposited in a Jesuit Church in the Philippines.<sup id="cite_ref-DOMB_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DOMB-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Baba was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> on 7 July 1867. In the Roman Catholic Church, his feast day is celebrated on 25 August<sup id="cite_ref-SAN_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAN-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>, as well as 10 September, the anniversary of the massacre of 205 <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Japan" title="Martyrs of Japan">Japanese martyrs</a>.
</p>
