					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">French Roman Catholic saint</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Stephen of Obazine</b> also known as <b>Stephen of Vielzot</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Étienne de Vielzot, Étienne d'Obazine</i>; (1085&#160;&#8211;&#32; 1159), was a French priest and <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, famed for his pious nature, even from a young age. He is commemorated on March 8.<sup id="cite_ref-Melleray_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Melleray-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life">Religious life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_of_Obazine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Religious life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Stephen was born in Vielzot, a village in <a href="/wiki/Bassignac-le-Haut" title="Bassignac-le-Haut">Bassignac-le-Haut</a>. According to local tradition, famine was great in the country, the poor went from one door to another. Young Stephen, who was from a fairly good home, suffered to see so much misery outside. One day, when he was left alone, he emptied the bread bin completely in alms, and when the family returned, they were furious, but the future monk smiled calmly as he took them back to the bin and showed them more dough than had been distributed.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He began his religious life in a community of clerics at <a href="/wiki/Pleaux" title="Pleaux">Pleaux</a>, where he became a priest and gained the reputation of holiness, especially when it came to the recitation of the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">divine office</a>, only interrupting these if something of grave necessity arose. He was also known for his love for all things to do with the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>, ensuring the provision of sacred vessels, furnishings and vestments that were perfect for God. Wanting a more austere life, Stephen and a like-minded priest, by the name of Peter, set out at the beginning of <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> one year to locate a place where they could live as <a href="/wiki/Hermits" class="mw-redirect" title="Hermits">hermits</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Obrecht_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Obrecht-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>On <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> they discovered a forest in the region of <a href="/wiki/Obazine" class="mw-redirect" title="Obazine">Obazine</a>. The two priests remained there fasting until <a href="/wiki/Easter_Sunday" class="mw-redirect" title="Easter Sunday">Easter Sunday</a>, after which they found a nearby church to celebrate <a href="/wiki/Eucharist_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Eucharist in the Catholic Church">Mass</a>. There is a well-known story of this time: heading back to their <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a>, the two friends paused to rest on the mountain, exhausted and weak from hunger. A peasant woman offered them half a loaf of bread to eat and a vessel of milk to drink. Stephen would later say that this simple meal was the most delightful he had ever tasted.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founding_of_Obazine_Abbey">Founding of Obazine Abbey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_of_Obazine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Founding of Obazine Abbey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1134, <a href="/wiki/Eustorge_de_Scorailles" title="Eustorge de Scorailles">Eustorge de Scorailles</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Limoges" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Limoges">Bishop of Limoges</a>, approved the establishment of the hermitage as a monastery on a site granted them by the Viscount Archambault. It was known from then on as <a href="/wiki/Obazine_Abbey" title="Obazine Abbey">Obazine Abbey</a>, even though at the time it comprised mainly many small huts in the forest.  Nearby at Coyroux they founded a nunnery for 150 nuns along similar lines. In 1142 Stephen assumed the role of abbot. He affiliated his house with the Cistercians in 1147.<sup id="cite_ref-Obrecht_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Obrecht-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> While on visitation to a daughterhouse, Bonnaigue, he became seriously ill and died there.<sup id="cite_ref-Melleray_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Melleray-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>&#160;
</p><p>The monastery flourished until it was suppressed during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, and its property was seized in 1791. The abbey church survives, and serves as a parish church.<sup id="cite_ref-Obrecht_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Obrecht-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
