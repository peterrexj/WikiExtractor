<p><b>Ralph Milner</b> (born at <a href="/wiki/Hursley" title="Hursley">Slackstead</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hampshire" title="Hampshire">Hampshire</a>, England, early in the sixteenth century; executed at <a href="/wiki/Winchester" title="Winchester">Winchester</a>, 7 July 1591) was an English <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Along with <b>Roger Dicconson</b> <i>(also spelled Dickenson)</i>, a Catholic priest, they were martyred at Winchester. Both were <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ralph_Milner">Ralph Milner</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Milner_and_companion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Ralph Milner">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The greater part of his life was probably passed in his native village, where, being practically illiterate, he supported his wife and eight children by manual labour. He was brought up an <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a>, but became a Catholic convert. On the very day of his first Communion, however, he was arrested for changing his religion and committed to Winchester jail.
</p><p>Here, his good behaviour meant he was frequently allowed out on parole, and was even trusted with the keys of the prison. This leniency enabled him to introduce priests to administer the sacraments to Catholic prisoners. He then acted as escort first to Father <a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Stanney&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Thomas Stanney (page does not exist)">Thomas Stanney</a>, and later to his successor at Winchester, Father <a href="/wiki/Roger_Dicconson" class="mw-redirect" title="Roger Dicconson">Roger Dicconson</a>, conducting them to the different villages to minister to Catholics.
</p>
<h2><span id="Roger_Dicconson_.28Dickenson.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Roger_Dicconson_(Dickenson)">Roger Dicconson (Dickenson)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Milner_and_companion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Roger Dicconson (Dickenson)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nothing much is known of him, except that he was born in <a href="/wiki/Lincoln,_England" title="Lincoln, England">Lincoln</a>, and studied for the priesthood in <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims, France</a>. In 1583, he was sent on a mission to England and was imprisoned soon afterwards, but managed to escape when his guards got drunk. He wasn't very fortunate the second time he was arrested, this time, with Milner, who had been escorting him to other villages.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Execution">Execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Milner_and_companion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Finally seized with Father Dicconson, Milner was with him placed under close confinement in Winchester jail pending the approaching sessions. The judge urged Milner to attend even once the Protestant church and thus escape the gallows. He refused and began to prepare for death. Every effort was made to persuade him to change his purpose and renounce the Catholic faith.
</p><p>When he was approaching the gallows with Father Dicconson, his children were led to him in the hope that he might even then relent. He was unshaken in his resolution, and gave his children his last blessing. There, the two men were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a>.
</p>
