<p><b>James Kisai</b>, SJ, also known as <b>Diego Kisai</b><span style="font-weight: normal"> (<span title="Japanese-language text"><span lang="ja">ディエゴ喜斎</span></span>)</span><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Jacobo Kisai</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> was a Japanese <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/26_Martyrs_of_Japan" title="26 Martyrs of Japan">26 Martyrs of Japan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Out of the 26, Kisai, <a href="/wiki/Paulo_Miki" class="mw-redirect" title="Paulo Miki">Paul Miki</a>, and <a href="/wiki/John_Soan_de_Goto" class="mw-redirect" title="John Soan de Goto">John Soan de Goto</a> were the only Jesuits to be executed in <a href="/wiki/Nagasaki" title="Nagasaki">Nagasaki</a> on February 5, 1597.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Kisai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James Kisai was born as <b>Ichikawa Kisaemon</b> (市川喜佐衛門).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a lay <a href="/wiki/Catholic_catechesis" title="Catholic catechesis">catechist</a> intending to join the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, he was imprisoned along with 23 other Catholics in December 1596 in the aftermath of the pivotal <a href="/wiki/San_Felipe_incident_(1596)" title="San Felipe incident (1596)"><i>San Felipe</i> incident</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Century_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Century-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> While he was in prison, Kisai and a fellow lay catechist John Soan de Goto gave their vows to Jesuit fathers John Rodriguez and Francis Pasia to enter the Jesuit order.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Shortly after, Kisai and the other imprisoned Catholics were forced to take a land journey during the winter time from <a href="/wiki/Sakai" title="Sakai">Sakai</a> to <a href="/wiki/Nagasaki" title="Nagasaki">Nagasaki</a>. Kisai and the others would eventually reach Nishizaka Hill in Nagasaki,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> where they were crucified and <a href="/wiki/Lance" title="Lance">lanced</a> to death on February 5, 1597.<sup id="cite_ref-Century_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Century-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
