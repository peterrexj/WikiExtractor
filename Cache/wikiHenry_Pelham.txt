<p><b>Henry Pelham</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society" title="Fellow of the Royal Society">FRS</a></span> (25 September 1694 – 6 March 1754) was a <a href="/wiki/British_Whig" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig">British Whig</a> statesman who served as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of Great Britain</a> from 1743 until his death in 1754. He was the younger brother of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pelham-Holles,_1st_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle">Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle</a>, who served in Pelham's government and succeeded him as prime minister. Pelham is generally considered to have been Britain's third prime minister, after <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a> and <a href="/wiki/The_Earl_of_Wilmington" class="mw-redirect" title="The Earl of Wilmington">the Earl of Wilmington</a>.
</p><p>Pelham's premiership was relatively uneventful in terms of domestic affairs, although it was during his premiership that <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain" title="Kingdom of Great Britain">Great Britain</a> experienced the tumult of the <a href="/wiki/1745_Jacobite_uprising" class="mw-redirect" title="1745 Jacobite uprising">1745 Jacobite uprising</a>. In foreign affairs, Britain fought in several wars. On Pelham's death, his brother Newcastle took full control of the British government.
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r886046785">.mw-parser-output .toclimit-2 .toclevel-1 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-3 .toclevel-2 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-4 .toclevel-3 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-5 .toclevel-4 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-6 .toclevel-5 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-7 .toclevel-6 ul{display:none}</style><div class="toclimit-3"><meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" /></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pelham, Newcastle's younger brother, was a younger son of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pelham,_1st_Baron_Pelham" title="Thomas Pelham, 1st Baron Pelham">Thomas Pelham, 1st Baron Pelham</a>, and his wife, the former Grace Pelham, Baroness Pelham of Laughton, the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Holles,_3rd_Earl_of_Clare" title="Gilbert Holles, 3rd Earl of Clare">Gilbert Holles, 3rd Earl of Clare</a>, and Grace Pierrepont. He was educated at <a href="/wiki/Westminster_School" title="Westminster School">Westminster School</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Matriculation" title="Matriculation">matriculated</a> at <a href="/wiki/King%27s_College,_Cambridge" title="King&#39;s College, Cambridge">King's College, Cambridge</a> at Easter 1709,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> then migrated to <a href="/wiki/Hart_Hall,_Oxford" class="mw-redirect" title="Hart Hall, Oxford">Hart Hall, Oxford</a> (the present-day Hertford College), matriculating on 6 September 1710,<sup id="cite_ref-ALUM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ALUM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> upon the appointment of his tutor <a href="/wiki/Richard_Newton_(academic)" title="Richard Newton (academic)">Richard Newton</a> as Principal of Hart Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a volunteer he served in Dormer's regiment at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Preston_(1715)" title="Battle of Preston (1715)">Battle of Preston</a> in 1715 and spent some time on the Continent. He was returned as <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament" class="mw-redirect" title="Member of Parliament">Member of Parliament</a> for <a href="/wiki/Seaford,_East_Sussex" title="Seaford, East Sussex">Seaford</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sussex" title="Sussex">Sussex</a> by his brother, the Duke of Newcastle, at a by-election on 28 February 1717 and represented it until 1722.<sup id="cite_ref-HOP_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HOP-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:HenryPelhamYoung.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/HenryPelhamYoung.jpg/170px-HenryPelhamYoung.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="211" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/HenryPelhamYoung.jpg/255px-HenryPelhamYoung.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/HenryPelhamYoung.jpg/340px-HenryPelhamYoung.jpg 2x" data-file-width="486" data-file-height="603" /></a><figcaption>Pelham by <a href="/wiki/Michael_Dahl" title="Michael Dahl">Michael Dahl</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1720</span></figcaption></figure>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Government">Government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Through strong family influence, and the recommendation of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a>, he was chosen in 1721 as <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_the_Treasury" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of the Treasury">Lord of the Treasury</a>. At the <a href="/wiki/1722_British_general_election" title="1722 British general election">1722 general election</a> he was returned as MP for Sussex county.<sup id="cite_ref-HOP_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HOP-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1724 he entered the ministry as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_at_War" title="Secretary at War">Secretary at War</a>, but this office he exchanged in 1730 for the more lucrative one of <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a>. He made himself conspicuous by his support of Walpole on the question of the <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole#Support" title="Robert Walpole">excise</a>. He, Newcastle, and the Prime Minister would often meet at <a href="/wiki/Houghton_Hall" title="Houghton Hall">Houghton Hall</a> in Norfolk, where they would draw up much of the country's policy. These meetings became known as the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Norfolk_Congress&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Norfolk Congress (page does not exist)">Norfolk Congress</a>. With Walpole, he served as a founding governor of the popular charity the <a href="/wiki/Foundling_Hospital" title="Foundling Hospital">Foundling Hospital</a> when it opened its doors in 1739. Like his brother, the Duke of Newcastle, Pelham was an active <a href="/wiki/Freemason" class="mw-redirect" title="Freemason">freemason</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Premier_Grand_Lodge_of_England" title="Premier Grand Lodge of England">Premier Grand Lodge of England</a>, active alongside <a href="/wiki/John_Theophilus_Desaguliers" title="John Theophilus Desaguliers">John Theophilus Desaguliers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1742 a union of parties resulted in the formation of an administration in which Pelham became Prime Minister the following year, succeeding the <a href="/wiki/Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington" title="Spencer Compton, 1st Earl of Wilmington">Earl of Wilmington</a> after his death.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointment">Appointment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Appointment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Henry_Pelham_(1694%E2%80%931754).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Henry_Pelham_%281694%E2%80%931754%29.jpg/170px-Henry_Pelham_%281694%E2%80%931754%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="219" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Henry_Pelham_%281694%E2%80%931754%29.jpg/255px-Henry_Pelham_%281694%E2%80%931754%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Henry_Pelham_%281694%E2%80%931754%29.jpg/340px-Henry_Pelham_%281694%E2%80%931754%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="5146" /></a><figcaption>Pelham, attributed to <a href="/wiki/John_Giles_Eccardt" title="John Giles Eccardt">John Giles Eccardt</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The first year of Pelham's premiership is regarded as a continuation of the <a href="/wiki/Carteret_ministry" title="Carteret ministry">Carteret ministry</a>, with <a href="/wiki/John_Carteret,_1st_Earl_Granville" class="mw-redirect" title="John Carteret, 1st Earl Granville">Lord Carteret</a> continuing as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Northern_Department" title="Secretary of State for the Northern Department">Secretary of State for the Northern Department</a> with responsibility for foreign affairs; Carteret was close to <a href="/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain" title="George II of Great Britain">King George II</a>. Pelham served as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury" title="First Lord of the Treasury">First Lord of the Treasury</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Commons" title="Leader of the House of Commons">Leader of the House of Commons</a>.
</p><p>In November 1744, the Pelhams forced Lord Carteret out of the ministry: Pelham bluntly told the king that either Carteret step down, or the Pelhamites would, leaving His Majesty without a government. Thereafter Pelham shared power with his brother, the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pelham-Holles,_1st_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle">Duke of Newcastle upon Tyne</a>. Pelham was regarded as the leading figure, but rank and influence made his brother very powerful in the Cabinet.  In spite of a genuine attachment, there were occasional disputes between them, which sometimes led to further difficulties.
</p>
<h4><span id=".22The_Broad-Bottomed_Administration.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;The_Broad-Bottomed_Administration&quot;">"The Broad-Bottomed Administration"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: &quot;The Broad-Bottomed Administration&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Broad_Bottom_ministry" title="Broad Bottom ministry">Broad Bottom ministry</a></div>
<p>Being strongly in favour of peace, Pelham carried on the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession" title="War of the Austrian Succession">War of the Austrian Succession</a> with languor and indifferent success, but the country, wearied of the interminable struggle, was disposed to acquiesce in his foreign policy almost without a murmur. King <a href="/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain" title="George II of Great Britain">George II</a>, thwarted in his own favourite schemes, made overtures in February 1746 to <a href="/wiki/William_Pulteney,_1st_Earl_of_Bath" title="William Pulteney, 1st Earl of Bath">Lord Bath</a>, but his purpose was upset by the resignation of the two Pelhams (Henry and Newcastle), who, after a two-day hiatus in which Bath and Carteret (now earl Granville) proved unable to form a ministry, resumed office at the king's request.  One of their terms was to insist that the king should have 'total confidence' in a ministry; rather than partial grudging acceptance of the Whigs.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare,_1743.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare%2C_1743.jpg/220px-Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare%2C_1743.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="359" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare%2C_1743.jpg/330px-Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare%2C_1743.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare%2C_1743.jpg/440px-Henry_Pelham_by_William_Hoare%2C_1743.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2690" data-file-height="4389" /></a><figcaption>Henry Pelham, by <a href="/wiki/William_Hoare" title="William Hoare">William Hoare</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1743</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>The <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Augustan" class="extiw" title="wikt:Augustan">Augustan</a> era was essential to the development of <a href="/wiki/Powers_of_the_prime_minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Powers of the prime minister of the United Kingdom">prime ministerial power</a> as being entirely dependent on a Commons majority, rather than royal prerogative interventions.  While the king struggled with his headstrong son, Frederick, Prince of Wales, his son's uncertain constitutional position was high in the Leicester House party set.  In 1748 Frederick, a Tory, planned to bring down the Pelhamites at a general election due the following year.  The Prime Minister called an early poll in 1748 by asking the king to dissolve parliament in 1747.  The prince and his father, the king grew to hate one another with unspeakable animosity.  But one consequence was a closer relationship between Henry Pelham and the Sovereign.  When he finally died in 1754, the King remarked "Now I shall have no more peace."  The <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Aix-la-Chapelle_(1748)" title="Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748)">Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle</a> had been signed in 1748 leading inexorably to a number of cost-cutting budgetary measures.
</p>
<h5><span class="mw-headline" id="Economic_reform">Economic reform</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Economic reform">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h5>
<p>The Army and Navy spending shrunk from £12 m to £7 million per annum.  Pelham promised to reduce interest rates through introduction of a balancing act measure from 4% to 3% by 1757.  He also assisted a fund to reduce the National Debt. In 1749, the <a href="/wiki/Consolidation_Act" class="mw-redirect" title="Consolidation Act">Consolidation Act</a> was passed, reorganising the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Navy" title="Royal Navy">Royal Navy</a>. On 20 March 1751, the British calendar was reorganised as well (New Year's Day became 1 January); Britain would adopt the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="Gregorian calendar">Gregorian calendar</a> one year later.  In 1752 he was able to reduce the land tax from 4 s. to 2 s in the pound (an effective reduction from 20% to 10%).
</p><p>One social consequence of the press gangs going to sea in an expansive navy fleet was to the growth of industrial processes necessary for warfare.  In the ports the distillation of gin demonstrated clearly by engravers such as Hogarth in "Gin Lane" the depravity issuing forth from the demon drink.  Preachers' "fire and brimstone" in favour of temperance, and drunken soldiers and sailors persuaded the administration to introduce the Gin Acts.  The <a href="/wiki/Gin_Act_1751" title="Gin Act 1751">Gin Act 1751</a> was the last of four that had largely failed to prevent serious social unrest, including riots in London, reduced the number licensed dealers and sellers of liquor.  By restricting supply the consumption dropped and price fell helping to manage the problem.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h5><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h5>
<p>Two of Pelham's final acts were the <a href="/wiki/Jewish_Naturalization_Act_1753" class="mw-redirect" title="Jewish Naturalization Act 1753">Jew Bill of 1753</a>, which allowed Jews to become naturalized by application to <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain" title="Parliament of Great Britain">Parliament</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Marriage_Act_of_1753" class="mw-redirect" title="Marriage Act of 1753">Marriage Act of 1753</a>, which enumerated the minimum age of consent for marriage. Upon his death, his brother (the aforementioned Duke of Newcastle upon Tyne) took over government.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Achievements">Achievements</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Achievements">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His very defects were among the chief elements of Pelham's success, for one with a strong personality, moderate amount self-respect, or haughty conceptions of statesmanship could not have restrained the discordant elements of the cabinet for any length of time the way he did. Moreover, he possessed tact and a thorough acquaintance with the forms of the <a href="/wiki/British_House_of_Commons" class="mw-redirect" title="British House of Commons">House of Commons</a>. Whatever quarrels or insubordination might have existed within the cabinet, they never broke out into open revolt. His foreign policy followed Walpole's model of emphasizing peace and ending wars.  His financial policy was a major success once peace had been signed in 1748 to end the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession" title="War of the Austrian Succession">War of the Austrian Succession</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He demobilized the armed forces, and reduced government spending from £12 million to £7 million.  He refinanced the national debt dropping the interest from 4% to 3%.  In 1752 he reduced the land tax from four shillings to two shillings in the pound; that is, from 20% to 10%.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
According to historians Stephen Brumwell and W.A. Speck, his:
</p>
<dl><dd>subdued manner concealed a shrewd and calculating politician.  He was reserved and cautious, but behind the reserve was steel.  All agreed on his integrity, which was remarkable in a venal age; unlike Walpole, he died relatively poor."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Pelham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Henry_Pelham_(c.1695-1754)_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Henry_Pelham_%28c.1695-1754%29_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG/220px-Henry_Pelham_%28c.1695-1754%29_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Henry_Pelham_%28c.1695-1754%29_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG/330px-Henry_Pelham_%28c.1695-1754%29_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Henry_Pelham_%28c.1695-1754%29_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG/440px-Henry_Pelham_%28c.1695-1754%29_Prime_Minister_lived_here_-_Blue_Plaque.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="2000" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Blue_plaque" title="Blue plaque">Blue plaque</a> located at Henry Pelham’s home in <a href="/wiki/Westminster" title="Westminster">Westminster</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Pelham married Lady Catherine Manners, daughter of the <a href="/wiki/John_Manners,_2nd_Duke_of_Rutland" title="John Manners, 2nd Duke of Rutland">2nd Duke of Rutland</a>, on 31 October 1726 in the <a href="/wiki/St_James%27s" title="St James&#39;s">Parish of St James</a>, <a href="/wiki/City_of_Westminster" title="City of Westminster">City of Westminster</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> They had four daughters:
</p>
<ul><li>Catherine Pelham-Clinton, Countess of Clinton (1727–1760), married 1744 <a href="/wiki/Henry_Pelham-Clinton,_2nd_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Henry Pelham-Clinton, 2nd Duke of Newcastle">Henry Clinton, 9th Earl of Lincoln</a>, who by this marriage subsequently became the 2nd <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Duke of Newcastle">Duke of Newcastle</a> (Newcastle-under-Lyme)</li>
<li>Frances (1728–1775), died unmarried</li>
<li>Grace Watson, Baroness Sondes (1735–1777), married 1752 the <a href="/wiki/Lewis_Watson,_1st_Baron_Sondes" title="Lewis Watson, 1st Baron Sondes">Hon. Lewis Watson</a>, who in 1760 was created <a href="/wiki/Earl_Sondes" title="Earl Sondes">Baron Sondes</a></li>
<li>Mary (1739–1???), died unmarried</li></ul>
<p>When Pelham was elevated to Prime Minister, he began construction of a house located at <a href="/wiki/Wimbourne_House" title="Wimbourne House">22 Arlington Street</a> in St James's, Westminster. He hired the architect <a href="/wiki/William_Kent" title="William Kent">William Kent</a> to build the structure in two phases.<sup id="cite_ref-William_Kent_House_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Kent_House-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Kent died in 1748<sup id="cite_ref-Architect_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Architect-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and the work was completed by Stephen Wright<sup id="cite_ref-Finishing_architect_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Finishing_architect-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> in 1754.<sup id="cite_ref-William_Kent_House_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Kent_House-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pelham died in 1754 and was buried in All Saints' Church, <a href="/wiki/Laughton,_East_Sussex" title="Laughton, East Sussex">Laughton, East Sussex</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-PelODNB_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PelODNB-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His personal papers were inherited by his son-in-law and now form part of the Newcastle (Clumber) Collection held at the department of <a href="/wiki/Manuscripts_and_Special_Collections,_The_University_of_Nottingham" class="mw-redirect" title="Manuscripts and Special Collections, The University of Nottingham">Manuscripts and Special Collections, The University of Nottingham</a>. Pelham was the first British Prime Minister who did not accede to the <a href="/wiki/Peerage" title="Peerage">peerage</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
