<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">These SS Felix and Januarius are not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Felix_and_Januarius" title="Felix and Januarius">SS&#160;Felix and Januarius</a> of Heraclea or with <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Thibiuca" title="Felix of Thibiuca">SS&#160;Felix and Januarius</a> who were martyred in 303 with Audactus, Septimus, and Fortunatus. For the space cat, see <a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9licette" title="Félicette">Félicette</a>.</div>
<p><b>Felicitas of Rome</b> (c. 101 &#8211; 165), also anglicized as <span class="nowrap"><b>Felicity</b>,</span> is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> numbered among the <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian martyrs</a>. Apart from her name, the only thing known for certain about this martyr is that she was buried in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cemetery_of_Maximus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cemetery of Maximus (page does not exist)">Cemetery of Maximus</a>, on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Salaria" title="Via Salaria">Via Salaria</a> on a 23 November.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, a legend presents her as the mother of the seven martyrs whose feast is celebrated on 10 July. The Eastern Orthodox Church celebrates their martyrdom on 25 January.
</p><p>The legend of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Symphorosa" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Symphorosa">Saint Symphorosa</a> is very similar and their acts may have been confused. She was a patron saint of healing. They may even be the same person.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This Felicitas is not the same as the North African Felicitas who was martyred <a href="/wiki/Perpetua_and_Felicity" title="Perpetua and Felicity">with Perpetua</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History_of_Saint_Felicitas">History of Saint Felicitas</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felicitas_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History of Saint Felicitas">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The feast of Saint Felicitas of Rome was first mentioned in the <i>"<a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a>"</i> as celebrated on 25 January. From a very early date her feast as a martyr was solemnly celebrated in the Roman Church on that date, as shown by the fact that on that day <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gregory the Great">Saint Gregory the Great</a> delivered a homily in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">Basilica</a> that rose above her tomb. Her body then rested in the catacomb of Maximus on the Via Salaria; in that cemetery all Roman itineraries, or guides to the burial-places of martyrs, locate her burial-place, specifying that her tomb was in a church above this catacomb.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> where St Felicitas was laid to rest was later enlarged into a subterranean chapel, and was rediscovered in 1885.
</p><p>In the early Middle Ages there was a chapel in honour of St Felicitas (Felicity) in an ancient Roman edifice near the ruins of the <a href="/wiki/Baths_of_Titus" title="Baths of Titus">Baths of Titus</a>.
</p><p>Some of her <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> are in the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> church at <a href="/wiki/Montefiascone" title="Montefiascone">Montefiascone</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. Others are in the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Susanna" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Susanna">Santa Susanna</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Association_with_the_seven_martyrs_venerated_on_10_July">Association with the seven martyrs venerated on 10 July</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felicitas_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Association with the seven martyrs venerated on 10 July">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Seven martyrs who on that day, though perhaps in different years, were buried in four different Roman cemeteries are celebrated jointly on 10 July: 
</p>
<ul><li><b>Saints Alexander, Vitalis, and Martial(is)</b> (Cemetery of the Jordani, on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Salaria" title="Via Salaria">Via Salaria</a>)</li>
<li><b>Saint Januarius</b> (Cemetery of Praetextatus, on the Via Appia)</li>
<li><b>Saints Felix and Philip</b> (Cemetery of Priscilla, on the Via Salaria)</li>
<li><b>Saint Sylvanus</b> or <b>Silvanus</b> (Cemetery of Maximus, on the Via Salaria)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MR_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>The earliest list of the Roman feasts of martyrs, known as the <i>"Depositio Martyrum"</i> and dating from the time of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Liberius" title="Pope Liberius">Pope Liberius</a>, in the middle of the fourth century, already mentions these seven martyrs as celebrated on 10 July in the four different <a href="/wiki/Catacombs" title="Catacombs">catacombs</a> in which their bodies lay. To the name of Silvanus it adds the statement that his body was stolen by the <a href="/wiki/Novatians" class="mw-redirect" title="Novatians">Novatians</a> (<i>hunc Silvanum martyrem Novatiani furati sunt</i>). It does not say that they were brothers.
</p><p>The tomb of St Januarius in the catacomb of Prætextatus belongs to the end of the second century, to which period, therefore, the martyrdoms, if they are in fact associated with one another, must belong, under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>.
</p><p>One of the seven martyrs, Saint Martialis (<i>Martial, Marziale</i>),  is venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Torricella_Peligna" title="Torricella Peligna">Torricella Peligna</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Abruzzo" title="Abruzzo">Abruzzo</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Isca_sullo_Ionio" title="Isca sullo Ionio">Isca sullo Ionio</a> in <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> with his feast day on 10 July.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Until it was <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">revised in 1969</a>, the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> designated these seven martyrs as "The Seven Holy Brothers", and some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholics">traditionalist Catholics</a> continue to celebrate them under this designation.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend_of_Felicitas_and_the_Seven_Holy_Brothers">Legend of Felicitas and the Seven Holy Brothers</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felicitas_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legend of Felicitas and the Seven Holy Brothers">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Felicitas (also known as Felicity) is said<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> to have been a rich and pious <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Widow" title="Widow">widow</a> who had seven sons. She devoted herself to charitable work and converted many to the Christian faith by her example. This aroused the wrath of <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> priests who lodged a complaint against her with Emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>. These priests asserted the ire of the gods and demanded sacrifice from Felicitas and her children. The Emperor acquiesced to their demand and Felicitas was brought before Publius, the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">Prefect</a> of Rome. Taking Felicitas aside, he used various pleas and threats in an unsuccessful attempt to get her to worship the pagan gods. He was equally unsuccessful with her seven sons who followed their mother's example.
</p><p>Before the Prefect Publius they adhered firmly to their religion, and were delivered over to four judges, who condemned them to various modes of death. The division of the martyrs among four judges corresponds to the four places of their burial. She implored God only that she not to be killed before her sons, so that she might be able to encourage them during their torture and death in order that they would not deny Christ.  With joy, she accompanied her sons one by one until she had witnessed the death of all seven. We are not entirely sure as to how each of them died, but it is said that Januarius, the eldest, was <a href="/wiki/Scourged" class="mw-redirect" title="Scourged">scourged</a> to death; Felix and Philip were beaten with clubs until they expired; Silvanus was thrown headlong down a precipice; and the three youngest, Alexander, Vitalis and Martialis were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>. After each execution she was given the chance to denounce her faith. She refused to act against her conscience and so she too suffered martyrdom.  Certain communities around the United States still celebrate San Marziale (Saint Martialis/Saint Marshall) with a San Marziale festival typically held on July 10 or near that date.
</p><p>They suffered and entered into eternal rest in Rome about the year 164 She was buried in the catacomb of Maximus on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Salaria" title="Via Salaria">Via Salaria</a>, beside St Silvanus. It is said that she died eight times. Once with each of her sons, and finally her own, and their feast day is held on January 25.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin_of_the_legend">Origin of the legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felicitas_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Origin of the legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SevenBrothers.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/SevenBrothers.jpg/350px-SevenBrothers.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="191" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c0/SevenBrothers.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="461" data-file-height="251" /></a><figcaption><i>The Seven Holy Brothers</i></figcaption></figure>
<p>The <i>"Acts"</i> that give the above account of the seven martyrs as sons of Felicitas existed, in some form, in the sixth century, since <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Pope Gregory I</a> refers to them in his <i>"Homiliæ super Evangelia, book I, homily iii."</i><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The early twentieth century <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a> reported that <i>"even distinguished modern archæologists have considered them, though not in their present form corresponding entirely to the original, yet in substance based on genuine contemporary records."</i> But it went on to say that investigations had shown this opinion to be hardly tenable. The earliest recension of these <i>"Acts"</i> does not antedate the sixth century, and appears to be based not on a Roman i.e. <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> text, but on a <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> original. Moreover, apart from the existing form of the <i>"Acts,"</i> various details have been called into question. If Felicitas were really the mother of the seven martyrs honoured on 10 July, it is strange that her name does not appear in the well-known fourth-century Roman calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The tomb of St Silvanus, one of the seven martyrs commemorated on 10 July, adjoined that of St Felicitas; it is quite possible, therefore, that tradition soon identified the seven martyrs of 10 July as the sons of St Felicitas, and that this formed the basis for the extant <i>"Acts."</i><sup id="cite_ref-CE_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
