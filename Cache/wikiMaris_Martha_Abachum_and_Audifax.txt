					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Marius, Martha, Audifax, and Abachum</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyrs</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">3rd century<br />Persia</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">270<br />Nymphae Catabassi, near Rome</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data">Rome, <a href="/wiki/Pr%C3%BCm_Abbey" title="Prüm Abbey">Prüm Abbey</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">19 January</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saints Marius, Martha, Audifax, and Abachum</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (died 270) were, according to their largely legendary <i>passio</i> of the 6th century, four saints of the same family (a married couple and their two sons).   They came from <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and were <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in 270 for sympathizing with Christian martyrs and burying their bodies. Some ancient <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a> place the date of their death between 268 and 270, during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Claudius_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Claudius II">Claudius II</a>, although there was no persecution of Christians during this time.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their story relates how the family's assistance to Christians exposed them to persecution.  They were seized and delivered to the judge Muscianus or Marcianus, who, unable to persuade them to <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/abjure" class="extiw" title="wikt:abjure">abjure</a> their faith, condemned them to various tortures.  Despite the torture, the saints refused to abjure.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Marius and his two sons were thus <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Cornelia" title="Via Cornelia">Via Cornelia</a>, and their bodies were burnt. Martha meanwhile was killed at a place called <i>in Nimpha</i> or Nymphae Catabassi (later called Santa Ninfa),<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> thirteen miles from <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.  Tradition states that Martha was cast into a well.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marius,_Martha,_Audifax,_and_Abachum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, a Roman lady named Felicitas secured the half-consumed remains of the father and sons and also the mother's body from the well, and had the sacred <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> secretly interred on her estate at <i>Buxus</i>, today <i>Boccea</i>.  This is said to have occurred on January 20.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  A church arose at Boccea, and during the Middle Ages, it became a place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of the martyrs later suffered various vicissitudes: some were transferred to the churches of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Adriano_al_Foro" title="Sant&#39;Adriano al Foro">Sant'Adriano al Foro</a> and <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and part of these relics were sent to <a href="/wiki/Eginhard" class="mw-redirect" title="Eginhard">Eginhard</a>, biographer of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, who lodged them in the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Seligenstadt" title="Seligenstadt">Seligenstadt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some relics went to <a href="/wiki/Pr%C3%BCm_Abbey" title="Prüm Abbey">Prüm Abbey</a> where their presence was recorded in the early 11th century. The original <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> chest was destroyed during the French occupation at the end of the 18th century. The current chest dates from the 19th century.
</p><p>The martyrs are inscribed in the current <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> on 19 January.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  Their feast or commemoration was included on that date in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> from the 9th century to 1969, when they were excluded because nothing is known with certainty about them except their names, their place of burial (the cemetery <i>Ad Nymphas</i> on the Via Cornelia), and the day of their burial (19 or 20 January).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
