<p><b>Saint Apollonius the Apologist</b> or <b>Saint Apollonius of Rome</b> (died 21 April 185) was a 2nd-century <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Apologetics" title="Apologetics">apologist</a> (not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Apollinaris_Claudius" title="Apollinaris Claudius">Apollinaris Claudius</a>, another contemporary apologist) who was martyred in 185 under the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Commodus" title="Commodus">Commodus</a> (161-192).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Apollonius_the_Apologist&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Four different sources speak of Saint Apollonius of Rome: 
</p>
<ul><li>a record of the trial incorporated into the <i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Ecclesiastical History</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius of Caesarea</a> (265-340);</li>
<li>chapters 40 and 42 of the <i><a href="/wiki/De_Viris_Illustribus_(Jerome)" title="De Viris Illustribus (Jerome)">De Viris Illustribus</a></i> by Saint <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> (347-420),</li>
<li>two versions of the <i>Passio</i> of Apollonius, one <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>, the other <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>, which were discovered in the late 19th century.<sup id="cite_ref-EncSanti_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EncSanti-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Day_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Day-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>These sources present Apollonius as an illustrious Roman, even, it seems, a senator, and an exceedingly talented man, well versed in philosophy. He was denounced as a Christian to the Pretorian Prefect Perennius. Summoned to defend himself, he read to the senate, according to Saint Jerome, "a remarkable volume" in which, instead of recanting, he defended the Christian faith. As a result, he was condemned to death on the basis of the law established by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EncSanti_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EncSanti-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Day_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Day-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sources say he was subjected to two investigations, the first by the Prefect Perennius, the second, three days later, by a group of senators and jurists. The hearings were conducted in a calm and courteous manner. Apollonius was permitted to speak with only rare interruptions, aimed at getting him to tone down his remarks, which were making him liable to punishment.<sup id="cite_ref-EncSanti_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EncSanti-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Apollonius was not afraid to die, because, he said: "There is waiting for me something better: eternal life, given to the person who has lived well on earth." And he argued for the superiority of Christianity's concepts of death and life.<sup id="cite_ref-Day_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Day-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sources disagree on the manner of his death. The Greek <i>Passio</i> says he died after having his legs crushed, a punishment inflicted also on the slave who denounced him; but in the <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenian</a> account he is decapitated.<sup id="cite_ref-EncSanti_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EncSanti-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Day_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Day-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Apollonius_the_Apologist&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Apollonius was not mentioned in the earliest Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>, not being at first the object of individual commemoration. In the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> he was confused with two other saints, Apollo of Alexandria and the Apollonius who was martyred with Saint Valentine and whose feast is on 18 April. As a result, this date was attributed also to Saint Apollonius of Rome, even in editions of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, the latest editions of which have, however, restored the date of 21 April.<sup id="cite_ref-EncSanti_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EncSanti-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The account in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>  (21 April) is as follows:
</p>
<dl><dd>At Rome, commemoration of Saint Apollonius, philosopher and martyr. Under the Emperor Commodus, he defended, before the Prefect Perennius and the Senate, the cause of the Christian faith in a finely argued address, and then, after being condemned to death, confirmed it by the witness of his blood.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
