					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Archbishop of Rouen, France</div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_remi.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Saint_remi.jpg/220px-Saint_remi.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="282" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Saint_remi.jpg/330px-Saint_remi.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Saint_remi.jpg/440px-Saint_remi.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1770" data-file-height="2272" /></a><figcaption>St. Remigius of Rouen.</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Remigius</b> (or <b>Remi</b>) (died 772)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was the illegitimate son of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a> and probably <a href="/wiki/Ruodhaid" title="Ruodhaid">Ruodhaid</a>. He was also the third <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">archbishop of Rouen</a> from 755 to 772.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Remigius_of_Rouen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Remigius was educated at court, but, along with his brothers, (<a href="/wiki/Bernhard,_son_of_Charles_Martel" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernhard, son of Charles Martel">Bernard</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hieronymus,_son_of_Charles_Martel" title="Hieronymus, son of Charles Martel">Hieronymus</a>) was denied any share of his father's patrimony which was apportioned among Charles' legitimate sons. In 737, he was sent with his half brother <a href="/wiki/Pepin_the_Short" title="Pepin the Short">Pepin the Short</a> to strengthen Charles' hold on Burgundy.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 755, Remigius became <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Rouen" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rouen">Archbishop of Rouen</a>. In 760, he accompanied Pepin to Italy, with his two brothers to mediate between <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_I" title="Pope Paul I">Pope Paul I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Desiderius" title="Desiderius">Desiderius</a>. Upon his return, he brought with him an instructor from <a href="/wiki/The_Schola_Cantorum_of_Rome" title="The Schola Cantorum of Rome">The Schola Cantorum of Rome</a> to teach his clergy Roman chants.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Remigius died in 772; in 1090, his relics were translated to the <a href="/wiki/Saint-Ouen_Abbey,_Rouen" title="Saint-Ouen Abbey, Rouen">Abbaye Saint-Ouen de Rouen</a>. During the turmoil of the Reformation in 1562 his reliquary was lost. His feast day is January 19.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was instrumental in implementing the Roman rite and chant in the Gallic church.
</p>
