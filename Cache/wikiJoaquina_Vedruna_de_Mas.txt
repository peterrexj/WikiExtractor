<p><b>Joaquina Vedruna de Mas</b> (or <b>Joaquima</b> in <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>) (16 April 1783 &#8211; 28 August 1854) - born <b>Joaquima de Vedruna Vidal de Mas</b> and in religious <b>Joaquina of Saint Francis of Assisi</b> - was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Carmelite_Sisters_of_Charity" title="Carmelite Sisters of Charity">Carmelite Sisters of Charity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> First she married a nobleman despite her desire to become a nun though she and her husband both desired the religious life; the couple bore nine children but she and her children fled after <a href="/wiki/Napoleon" title="Napoleon">Napoleon</a> invaded the nation to which her husband remained to fight as a volunteer and later died leaving her widowed but free to pursue her religious inclinations.<sup id="cite_ref-SJ_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CN_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> as a saint was celebrated on 12 April 1959.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joaquina_Vedruna_de_Mas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Joaquima Vedruna Vidal de Mas was born on 16 April 1783 in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> to the nobles Lorenzo de Vedruna - who worked for the government - and Teresa Vidal; her <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> was celebrated on the date of her birth in the parish church of Santa Maria del Pi.<sup id="cite_ref-CN_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1795 she expressed a desire to become a <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> but her parents believed she was not mature enough to make such a decision.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her childhood was a pious one and she fostered a special devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Infant_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Infant Jesus">Infant Jesus</a> while being known for her obsessive cleanliness and she made her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> in 1792.<sup id="cite_ref-SJ_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 24 March 1799 she married the barrister and landowner Teodoro de Mas (the firstborn of his own household) with whom she had nine children; both husband and wife later became members of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> and she became known as "Joaquina of Saint Francis of Assisi".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her husband was a friend of her father and was undecided about which of Lorenzo's three daughters to wed: he gave the three a box of almonds and the two older girls rejected it as a childish gift but she accepted and said: "I love almonds" and thus he settled on her.<sup id="cite_ref-SJ_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> But <a href="/wiki/Napoleon" title="Napoleon">Napoleon's</a> invasion saw her flee with her children but her husband insisted that he remain to fight as a volunteer and he died on 6 March 1816; she moved with her children after a few months from Barcelona to their estate of "Manso Escorial" in Vic and she began to wear the habit of the third order on a frequent basis. Here she began her charitable activities with the sick and with women. Her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> - the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> Esteban de Olot - suggested she establish an <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life_(Catholic_Church)#Apostolic_congregations" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated life (Catholic Church)">apostolic congregation</a> devoted to education and to charitable works. Four daughters entered <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convents</a> and two sons married while three others died as children.<sup id="cite_ref-CN_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Vic" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Vic">Bishop of Vic</a> Pablo Jesús Corcuera told her the institute should be of a Carmelite inspiration; she made her vows to the bishop on 6 January 1826. The same bishop wrote the rule for the order on 6 February 1826 and on the morning of 26 February she and another eight women professed their vows while she founded the order at that moment.<sup id="cite_ref-SJ_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> That morning the group attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> at a Capuchin church and then went to her estate to begin their new order. But she also collaborated with <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Mary_Claret" title="Anthony Mary Claret">Anthony Mary Claret</a> for the writing up of the rule. During the <a href="/wiki/First_Carlist_War" title="First Carlist War">First Carlist War</a> she had to flee from Spain because she had founded a hospital in the <a href="/wiki/Carlist" class="mw-redirect" title="Carlist">Carlist</a> town of <a href="/wiki/Berga" title="Berga">Berga</a> that was threatened due to all of the fighting and so she went to <a href="/wiki/Roussillon" title="Roussillon">Roussillon</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and was there from 1836 until 1842. Her congregation received the papal decree of praise from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> on 5 August 1857 while the order was aggregated to the mainstream Carmelites on 14 September 1860; official papal approval came on 20 July 1880 from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>. In spite of serious challenges that the civil war and secular opposition posed the institute she founded soon spread into Catalonia. Thereafter communities were established throughout Spain and <a href="/wiki/Hispanic_America" title="Hispanic America">Hispanic America</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CN_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In due course she was forced to resign as the Superior of her order due to sickness; she died during a <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> epidemic in Barcelona on 28 August 1854 but she fell victim to <a href="/wiki/Paralysis" title="Paralysis">paralysis</a> since 1850. Her first attack of <a href="/wiki/Apoplexy" title="Apoplexy">apoplexy</a> came in September 1849 with more following. Her remains are in the order's motherhouse in Vic. Her order now operates in nations such as <a href="/wiki/Japan" title="Japan">Japan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eritrea" title="Eritrea">Eritrea</a> while in 2008 there were 2012 religious in 280 houses.<sup id="cite_ref-SJ_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SJ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joaquina_Vedruna_de_Mas&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic,_24.08.2004.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic%2C_24.08.2004.jpg/230px-Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic%2C_24.08.2004.jpg" decoding="async" width="230" height="173" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic%2C_24.08.2004.jpg/345px-Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic%2C_24.08.2004.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic%2C_24.08.2004.jpg/460px-Urna_amb_les_restes_de_Sta._Joaquima_al_Mas_Escorial_de_Vic%2C_24.08.2004.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>Remains found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The sainthood cause commenced under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> on 14 January 1920 in a move that titled her as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> while the confirmation of her model life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> to title her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 16 June 1935. The confirmation of two miracles attributed to her intercession saw <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> preside over her beatification on 19 May 1940 and the confirmation of another two allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> to canonize her on 12 April 1959 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a>.
</p>
