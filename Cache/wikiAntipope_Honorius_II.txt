<p><b>Honorius II</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1010 – 1072), born <b>Pietro Cadalo</b> (Latin <i>Petrus Cadalus</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> from 1061 to 1072. He was born in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Verona" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Verona">County of Verona</a>, and became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Parma" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Parma">bishop of Parma</a> in 1045.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He died at <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a> in 1072.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Honorius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cadalo was the son of the Lombard Ingone, the son of Wicard, the son of Atone. The family may have originated at <a href="/wiki/Monselice" title="Monselice">Monselice</a> (39 miles, 63&#160;km east-southeast of Verona), and entered the military service of the Count of Verona. In 992, Wicard is on record as owning the castle of Calmano, when he acquired territory at Lonigo in the County of Verona.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His son Ingone acquired a house and other property in Verona in 1005, and in 1014 he had risen to the post of Vicecomes (vice-count, viscount) of the city of Verona.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ingone was dead by 13 July 1028, on which date his sons and heirs, Cadalo, Erizo and Giovanni, purchased additional property. It is stated in the documents that Cadalo was already in the clerical state ("clericus").<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecclesiastical_career">Ecclesiastical career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Honorius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ecclesiastical career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>By 3 September 1030, Cadalo had been ordained a <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">subdeacon</a>. He was a deacon by 31 July 1034. By 11 April 1041, he held the important post of vicedomino of the Church of Verona, administrator of the possessions of the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 24 April 1046, Bishop Cadalo founded the monastery of S. Giorgio in Braida, on land which he had acquired the previous day in a transaction with Bishop Walter of Verona.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Cadalo attended the diet held by the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry III">Emperor Henry III</a> at Pavia in October 1046; even though he was accused of simony he signed the acts of the diet.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> On 1 May 1047, moreover, the Emperor granted the county of Parma to the Church of Parma and its bishop, Cadalo.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1048, Bishop Cadalo obtained from Marchese Boniface, the Lord of Tuscany, confirmation of the grant of the monastery of S. Bartolomeo in Pistoia to the monastery of S. Giovanni Battista in Parma.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1050, Bishop Cadalo attended the Roman <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> of Pope Leo IX, which was held at the Lateran Basilica from 29 April to 2 May. On 1 May he subscribed Pope Leo's letter to Bishop John, approving his transfer by the pope and synod from the diocese of Tusculum to the diocese of Porto.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the old bishop's house was destroyed by fire, Bishop Cadalo had a new episcopal palace built on a grander scale. It was in use by February 1055.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the spring of 1055, Bishop Cadalo accompanied the Emperor Henry III on his visit to Tuscany, where they met the newly consecrated Pope Victor II, an appointee of the Emperor. Conferences began on 27 May in Florence, and the Emperor and Pope held a joint synod in Florence on June 4. On 6 June the Emperor issued a privilege in favor of the Canons of Parma.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Henry III died on 5 October 1056, leaving his crown to his ten-year-old son, <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry</a>, under the regency of the Empress <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Poitou" title="Agnes of Poitou">Agnes of Poitou</a>. Victor II died on 28 July 1057.
</p><p>The city of Parma was devastated by a major fire on 10 August 1058. The cathedral was so badly damaged that it could not be repaired, and Bishop Cadalo therefore broke ground for a new cathedral in a different place. The residences of the canons of the cathedral were also destroyed, and Cadalo built new ones next to the new cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The building was finally consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a> on 4 November 1106.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pope">Pope</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Honorius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Pope">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II" title="Pope Nicholas II">Pope Nicholas II</a> (1059&#8211;1061) at Florence on 27 July 1061, two different groups initiated plans to choose a new pope.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Beno_of_Santi_Martino_e_Silvestro" title="Beno of Santi Martino e Silvestro">Cardinal Beno</a>, the cardinals were aware of the ambitions of the Archdeacon Hildebrand, and immediately requested the Emperor Henry IV to name Bishop Cadalo of Parma as the new pope.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The reformist <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">cardinals</a> met under the direction of Hildebrand (who later became <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>), and sent a representative, the former monk of Cluny, Cardinal Stephen, to seek permission of the emperor to conduct an election. After a five-day wait during which he was not received in audience, the Cardinal returned to Italy, without having received the <i>congé d'élire</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Three months after the death of Pope Nicholas, the reformists, who were still in Florence, then <a href="/wiki/1061_papal_election" title="1061 papal election">elected</a> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a> (1061&#8211;1073) on 30 September 1061. Alexander II (Anselm the Elder, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lucca" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lucca">Bishop of Lucca</a>) had been one of the leaders of the reform party.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> He was able to enter Rome only through the violent support of <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_Capua" title="Richard I of Capua">Richard of Capua</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointment_to_papacy">Appointment to papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Honorius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Appointment to papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Twenty-eight days after Alexander II's election an assembly of German and <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombard</a> bishops and notables opposed to the reform movement was brought together at <a href="/wiki/Basel" title="Basel">Basel</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Poitou" title="Agnes of Poitou">Empress Agnes</a> as regent for her son, King <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a> (1056&#8211;1105), and was presided over by the Imperial Chancellor Wilbert. The council annulled the electoral decree of Nicholas II, and excommunicated Anselm of Lucca.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> It was argued in the ensuing pamphlet war that a pope whose jurisdiction is universal should be elected not only by the Roman people but also by the Roman emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> With the assent of the assembly, on 28 October 1061 Henry appointed the bishop of Parma, Cadalus, as pope. He had been principally recommended to the German court by Bishop Dionysius of Piacenza and Bishop Gregory of Vercelli.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> He assumed the name of Honorius II.
</p><p>In 1061, after his return from Rome, Pope Honorius II celebrated a diocesan synod in Parma.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In at the beginning of 1062, with the support of the Empress and the nobles, Honorius II and his forces marched towards <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to claim the papal seat by force. Bishop <a href="/wiki/Benzo_of_Alba" title="Benzo of Alba">Benzo of Alba</a> helped his cause as imperial envoy to Rome. The army had advanced as far as <a href="/wiki/Sutri" title="Sutri">Sutri</a> by 25 March. On 14 April a brief but bloody conflict took place near Rome, just north of the <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Castel Sant'Angelo</a> in the "prato Neronis" (prata S. Petri). The forces of Alexander II were defeated, and Honorius II took possession of the Leonine city and the precincts of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>. He did not enter the city of Rome, however, and did not hold his gains for long. A few days later, on the approach of the Duke of Lorraine, he withdrew to the fortress of Flajanum.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Honorius II was recognized as pope by the envoys of the eastern Emperor, <a href="/wiki/Constantine_X_Doukas" title="Constantine X Doukas">Constantine X Doukas</a>. They were in Italy to put together an alliance against the Normans and Alexander II, as part of the emperor's plan to reclaim <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a> for the empire. They had already been treating with the Romans and with the imperial legate Benizo, hoping to include the German emperor in the enterprise.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Duke <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_the_Bearded" title="Godfrey the Bearded">Godfrey of Tuscany and Spoleto</a><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> arrived at the Milvian Bridge, a few miles north of the north gate of Rome in May 1062, thereby frustrating the efforts of the imperial agents. He induced both rivals to submit the decision about their legitimacy to the King's decision. Honorius II withdrew to Parma and Alexander II returned to his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> in Lucca, pending Godfrey's mediation with the German court and the advisers of the young German king, Henry IV.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_change_and_attempted_deposition">Political change and attempted deposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Honorius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Political change and attempted deposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In Germany, meanwhile, a coup-d-état had taken place. <a href="/wiki/Anno_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Anno of Cologne">Anno</a>, the powerful <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">Archbishop of Cologne</a>, had seized the <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">regency</a>, and the Empress Agnes retired to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Fruttuaria" title="Abbey of Fruttuaria">Abbey of Fruttuaria</a> in <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>. The chief authority in Germany passed to Anno, who was hostile to Honorius II.
</p><p>Having declared himself against Cadalus, the new regent summoned the bishops of Germany and Italy to the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Council_of_Augsburg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Council of Augsburg (page does not exist)">Council of Augsburg</a> (Concilium Osboriense) (27 October 1062), and, in the presence of the young King Henry IV, declared Alexander to be the true pope and Cadalo a pretender. He secured the appointment of an envoy to be sent to Rome for the purpose of investigating charges of <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a> against Alexander II. The envoy, who was Anno's nephew <a href="/wiki/Burchard_II_(bishop_of_Halberstadt)" title="Burchard II (bishop of Halberstadt)">Burchard II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Halberstadt" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Halberstadt">Bishop of Halberstadt</a>, found no objection to Alexander II's election. With the political change, Alexander II came to be recognized as the lawful pontiff by the German court. Alexander held a synod in Rome in the spring of 1063, during the Easter season, and had his rival, Cadalo (Honorius II), accused of simony (an old charge, dating back to 1045, and ignored repeatedly by popes and synods), seeking the papacy, attacking Rome, and counselling homicide; he was <a href="/wiki/Excommunicate" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicate">excommunicated</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Honorius did not, however, abandon his claims. At a synod of his own, held at Parma shortly after Alexander's, he defied the excommunication, pointing out that he had been chosen by the German king acting as Roman patrician, while Alexander had been chosen neither by the clergy of Rome nor the Roman people, and had been intruded by the Normans, the enemies of the Roman people; Honorius leveled his own excommunication against Alexander. He gathered an armed force and once more proceeded to Rome, where he established himself in the Castel Sant'Angelo.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The ensuing war between the rival Popes lasted for about a year. After a siege of two months, Honorius II withdrew from Rome and returned to Parma. He continued to act as the pope, however, celebrating masses, conducting ordinations, and issuing <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bulls</a> and apostolic letters.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Formal_deposition_resisted">Formal deposition resisted</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Honorius_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Formal deposition resisted">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The Council of Mantua, on <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>, 31 May 1064, ended the <a href="/wiki/Schism" title="Schism">schism</a> by formally declaring Alexander II to be the legitimate successor of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter">St. Peter</a>. Honorius II, however, maintained his claim to the papal chair to the day of his death. His successor, Evrardus (Heberardus) also rejected Gregory VII and maintained allegiance to Henry IV. He fought for Henry against <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Tuscany" title="Matilda of Tuscany">Matilda of Tuscany</a>, and joined the schism of <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Clement III</a> (Bishop Wibert of Ravenna).<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cadalus died in Parma in 1072, still maintaining that he was pope, "Apostolicus electus". His death is registered by Bonizo of Sutri as occurring just before the death of the bishop of Ravenna, Giovanni Enrico, which took place in 1072.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> His epitaph is preserved by Ireneo Affò.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
