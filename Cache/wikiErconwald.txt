<p><b>Earconwald</b> or <b>Erkenwald</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 693) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_London" title="Bishop of London">Bishop of London</a>  between 675 and 693.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Earconwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Earconwald was born at <a href="/wiki/Lindsey,_Lincolnshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Lindsey, Lincolnshire">Lindsey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh182_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh182-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was supposedly of royal ancestry.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS175_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS175-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 666, he established two <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbeys, <a href="/wiki/Chertsey_Abbey" title="Chertsey Abbey">Chertsey Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Surrey" title="Surrey">Surrey</a><sup id="cite_ref-Kirby83_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby83-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> for men, and <a href="/wiki/Barking_Abbey" title="Barking Abbey">Barking Abbey</a> for women.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh182_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh182-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Courts250_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Courts250-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His sister, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelburg_of_Barking" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelburg of Barking">Æthelburg</a>, was Abbess of Barking,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh182_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh182-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Kirby102_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby102-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> while he served as <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">Abbot</a> of Chertsey.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby95_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby95-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 675, Earconwald became the Bishop of London, after <a href="/wiki/Wine_(bishop)" title="Wine (bishop)">Wine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook219_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook219-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was the choice of Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodore of Canterbury">Theodore of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby95_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby95-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> While bishop, he contributed to King <a href="/wiki/Ine_of_Wessex" title="Ine of Wessex">Ine of Wessex</a>'s law code, and is mentioned specifically in the code as a contributor.<sup id="cite_ref-Conversion235_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Conversion235-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Current historical scholarship credits Earconwald with a large role in the evolution of Anglo-Saxon charters, and it is possible that he drafted the charter of Caedwalla to Farnham.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby102_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby102-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> King Ine of Wessex named Earconwald as an advisor on his laws.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby103_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby103-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Earconwald died in 693<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook219_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook219-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and his remains were buried at <a href="/wiki/Old_St_Paul%27s_Cathedral" title="Old St Paul&#39;s Cathedral">Old St Paul's Cathedral</a>. His grave was a popular place of pilgrimage in the Middle Ages, and was destroyed together with a number of other tombs in the cathedral during the Reformation.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Earconwald's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 30 April, with translations being celebrated on 1 February, 13 May and 14 November.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS175_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS175-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He is a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of London.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
