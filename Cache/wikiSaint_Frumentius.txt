<p><b>Frumentius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ge%27ez_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ge&#39;ez language">Ge'ez</a>: <span lang="gez">ፍሬምናጦስ</span>; died c. 383) was a <a href="/wiki/Phoenicia" title="Phoenicia">Phoenician</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_mission" title="Christian mission">Christian missionary</a> and the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Axum" title="Axum">Axum</a> who brought <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum" title="Kingdom of Aksum">Kingdom of Aksum</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-isbn0-313-32273-2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-isbn0-313-32273-2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is sometimes known by other names, such as <b><a href="/wiki/Abuna" title="Abuna">Abuna</a></b> ("Our Father") and <b>Aba Salama</b> ("Father of Peace").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was ethnically a <a href="/wiki/Phoenicia" title="Phoenicia">Phoenician</a>, according to Rufinus, born in <a href="/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon" title="Tyre, Lebanon">Tyre</a>, modern day Lebanon. As a boy, he was captured with his brother, and they became slaves to the <a href="/wiki/Ousanas" title="Ousanas">King of Axum</a>. He freed them shortly before his death, and they were invited to educate his young heir. They also began to teach Christianity in the region. Later, Frumentius traveled to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, where he appealed to have a bishop appointed and missionary priests sent south to Axum. Thereafter, he was appointed bishop and established the Church in Ethiopia, converting many local people, as well as the king. His appointment began a tradition that the Patriarch of Alexandria appoint the bishops of Ethiopia.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frumentius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the fourth-century historian <a href="/wiki/Tyrannius_Rufinus" title="Tyrannius Rufinus">Tyrannius Rufinus</a> (x.9), who cites Frumentius' brother Edesius as his authority, as children (ca. 316) Frumentius and Edesius accompanied their uncle Meropius from their birthplace of <a href="/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon" title="Tyre, Lebanon">Tyre</a> (now in <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>) on a voyage to Ethiopia.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When their ship stopped at one of the harbors of the <a href="/wiki/Red_Sea" title="Red Sea">Red Sea</a>, local people massacred the whole crew, sparing the two boys, who were <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">taken as slaves</a> to the King of Axum. The two boys soon gained the favor of the king, who raised them to positions of trust. Shortly before his death, the king freed them. The widowed queen, however, prevailed upon them to remain at the court and assist her in the education of the young heir, <a href="/wiki/Ezana_of_Axum" title="Ezana of Axum">Ezana</a>, and in the administration of the kingdom during the prince's minority. They remained and (especially Frumentius) used their influence to spread Christianity. First they encouraged the Christian <a href="/wiki/Merchant" title="Merchant">merchants</a> present in the country to practice their faith openly, and they helped them find places "where they could come together for prayer according to the Roman Rite";<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> later they converted some of the natives.<sup id="cite_ref-isbn0-313-32273-2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-isbn0-313-32273-2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the prince came of age, Edesius returned to <a href="/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon" title="Tyre, Lebanon">Tyre</a>, where he stayed and was ordained a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Frumentius, eager for the conversion of Ethiopia, accompanied his brother as far as <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, where he requested <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch of Alexandria">Patriarch of Alexandria</a>, to send a bishop and some priests as <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionaries</a> to Ethiopia. By Athanasius' own account, he believed Frumentius to be the most suitable person for the job and consecrated him as bishop,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> traditionally in the year 328, or according to others, between 340 and 346.
</p><p>Frumentius returned to Ethiopia, where he erected his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">episcopal see</a> at <a href="/wiki/Axum" title="Axum">Axum</a>, then converted and baptized King <a href="/wiki/Ezana_of_Axum" title="Ezana of Axum">Ezana</a>, who built many churches and spread Christianity throughout Ethiopia. Frumentius established the first monastery of Ethiopia, called <i><a href="/wiki/Degol_Woyane" title="Degol Woyane">Dabba Selama</a></i> in <a href="/wiki/Degua_Tembien" class="mw-redirect" title="Degua Tembien">Dogu'a Tembien</a>. The people called Frumentius <i><b>Kesate Birhan</b></i> (Revealer of Light) and <b>Abba Salama</b> (Father of Peace). He became the first <i>Abune</i>, a title given to the head of the <a href="/wiki/Ethiopian_Orthodox_Tewahedo_Church" title="Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church">Ethiopian Church</a>.
</p><p>In about 356, the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> wrote to King Ezana and his brother <a href="/wiki/Saizana" title="Saizana">Saizana</a>, requesting them to replace Frumentius as bishop with <a href="/wiki/Theophilos_the_Indian" title="Theophilos the Indian">Theophilos the Indian</a>, who supported the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian position</a>, as did the emperor. Frumentius had been appointed by Athanasius, a leading opponent of Arianism. The king refused the request.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ethiopian traditions credit him with the first <a href="/wiki/Ge%27ez" class="mw-redirect" title="Ge&#39;ez">Ge'ez</a> translation of the New Testament, and being involved in the development of <a href="/wiki/Ge%27ez_script" class="mw-redirect" title="Ge&#39;ez script">Ge'ez script</a> from an <a href="/wiki/Abjad" title="Abjad">abjad</a> (consonantal-only) into an <a href="/wiki/Abugida" title="Abugida">abugida</a> (syllabic).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_date">Feast date</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frumentius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Feast date">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and <a href="/wiki/Eritrean_Orthodox_Tewahedo_Church" title="Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church">Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church</a> celebrate the feast of Abba Salama's consecration  on <a href="/wiki/Ethiopian_calendar" title="Ethiopian calendar">Taḫśaś (the 4th month of Ethiopian or Coptic calendar) 18</a> and departure Hamle (the 11th month of Ethiopian or Coptic calendar) 26.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria</a> celebrates the feast of Frumentius on 18 December,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> on 30 November<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> on 20 July.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frumentius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Frumentius is regarded as the patron saint of the former <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aksum" title="Kingdom of Aksum">Kingdom of Aksum</a>, and its contemporary territories.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/St_Frumentius_Theological_College" class="mw-redirect" title="St Frumentius Theological College">St Frumentius Theological College</a>, the Anglican <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in Ethiopia.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
