<p><b>Saint Bibiana</b> (<b>Bibiane</b>, <b>Viviana</b>, or <b>Vivian</b>) is a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Virgin_(title)#Virgin_martyrs" title="Virgin (title)">Virgin martyr</a>. The earliest mention in an authentic historical authority occurs in the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i>, where the biography of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Simplicius" title="Pope Simplicius">Pope Simplicius</a> (468–483) states that this pope "consecrated a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> of the holy martyr Bibiana, which contained her body, near the 'palatium Licinianum'" (ed. Duchesne, I, 249). The <a href="/wiki/Santa_Bibiana" title="Santa Bibiana">Basilica of Santa Bibiana</a> is dedicated to her.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Bibiana&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Bibiana was the daughter of a former prefect, Flavianus, who was banished by <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>. His wife Dafrosa, and two daughters, Demetria and Bibiana, were also persecuted by Julian. Dafrosa and Demetria died a natural death and were buried by Bibiana in their own house; but Bibiana was tortured and died as a result of her sufferings. Two days after her death a priest named John buried Bibiana near her mother and sister in her home, the house being later transformed into a <a href="/wiki/Church_(building)" title="Church (building)">church</a>. It is evident that the legend seeks to explain in this way the origin of the church and the presence in it of the bodies of the above-mentioned confessors. The account contained in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a> of the ninth century is drawn from the legend.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Legnanino_-_The_martyrdom_of_Saint_Bibiana.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Legnanino_-_The_martyrdom_of_Saint_Bibiana.jpg/260px-Legnanino_-_The_martyrdom_of_Saint_Bibiana.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="219" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/94/Legnanino_-_The_martyrdom_of_Saint_Bibiana.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="295" data-file-height="249" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Legnanino_-_The_martyrdom_of_Saint_Bibiana.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Martyrdom of Saint Bibiana</i> by <a href="/wiki/Stefano_Maria_Legnani" title="Stefano Maria Legnani">Legnanino</a></div></div></div>
<p>An alternate account says that in the year 363, Emperor <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian</a> made Apronianus Governor of Rome. Bibiana suffered in the persecution started by him. She was the daughter of Christians, Flavian, a Roman knight, and Dafrosa, his wife. Flavian was tortured and sent into exile, where he died of his wounds. Dafrosa was beheaded, and their two daughters, Bibiana and Demetria, were stripped of their possessions and left to suffer poverty. However, they remained in their house, spending their time in fasting and prayer. Apronianus, seeing that hunger and want had no effect upon them, summoned them. Demetria, after confessing her faith, fell dead at the feet of the tyrant. Bibiana was reserved for greater sufferings. She was placed in the hands of a wicked woman called Rufina, who in vain endeavored to seduce her. She used blows as well as persuasion, but the Christian virgin remained faithful. Enraged at the constancy of this saintly virgin, Apronianus ordered her to be tied to a pillar and beaten with scourges, laden with lead plummets, until she died. The saint endured the torments with joy, and died under the blows inflicted by the hands of the executioner. Her body was then put in the open air to be torn apart by wild animals, yet none would touch it. After two days she was buried.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mistaken_identity">Mistaken identity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Bibiana&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mistaken identity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The above-mentioned Saint Bibiana (of the 4th century) should not be confused with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vibiana" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Vibiana">Saint Vibiana</a> (of the 3rd century), who is the patroness of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Los_Angeles" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Los Angeles</a>.
</p>
