<p><b>Leontius</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Λεόντιος</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Leóntios</i></span>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;660</span> – <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 15 February 706), was <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> from 695 to 698. Little is known of his early life, other than that he was born in <a href="/wiki/Isauria" title="Isauria">Isauria</a> in <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>. He was given the title of <i><a href="/wiki/Patrikios" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrikios">patrikios</a></i>, and made <i><a href="/wiki/Strategos" title="Strategos">strategos</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Anatolic_Theme" title="Anatolic Theme">Anatolic Theme</a> under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_IV" title="Constantine IV">Constantine IV</a>. He led forces against the <a href="/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate" title="Umayyad Caliphate">Umayyads</a> during the early years of <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a>'s reign, securing victory and forcing the Umayyad caliph, <a href="/wiki/Abd_al-Malik_ibn_Marwan" title="Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan">Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan</a>, to sue for peace.
</p><p>In 692, Justinian declared war upon the Umayyads again, and sent Leontius to campaign against them. However, he was defeated decisively at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Sebastopolis" title="Battle of Sebastopolis">Battle of Sebastopolis</a>, and imprisoned by Justinian for his failure. He was released in 695, and given the title of <i>strategos</i> of the Theme of <a href="/wiki/Hellas_(theme)" title="Hellas (theme)">Hellas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Southern_Greece" title="Southern Greece">Southern Greece</a>. After being released, he led a rebellion against Justinian, and seized power, becoming emperor in the same year.
</p><p>He ruled until 697, when he was overthrown by <a href="/wiki/Apsimar" class="mw-redirect" title="Apsimar">Apsimar</a>, a <i><a href="/wiki/Droungarios" title="Droungarios">droungarios</a></i> who had taken part in a failed expedition that had been launched by Leontius to recover <a href="/wiki/Roman_Carthage" title="Roman Carthage">Carthage</a>. After seizing <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, Apsimar took the name <a href="/wiki/Tiberius_III" title="Tiberius III">Tiberius (III)</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> and had Leontius' <a href="/wiki/Rhinotomy" title="Rhinotomy">nose and tongue cut off</a>. He was sent to the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Monastery_of_Dalmatou&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Monastery of Dalmatou (page does not exist)">Monastery of Dalmatou</a>, where he remained until some time between August 705 and February 706. By this time Justinian had retaken the throne. Both Leontius and Tiberius were executed.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin_and_early_life">Origin and early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leontius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origin and early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little of Leontius' early life is known, other than that he was from <a href="/wiki/Isauria" title="Isauria">Isauria</a>, and possibly of <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenian</a> descent.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was appointed as <i><a href="/wiki/Strategos" title="Strategos">strategos</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Anatolic_Theme" title="Anatolic Theme">Anatolic Theme</a>, at the time the most senior military command of the Byzantine Empire,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199189–90_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199189–90-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and <i><a href="/wiki/Patrikios" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrikios">patrikios</a></i> by Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_IV" title="Constantine IV">Constantine IV</a>, possibly <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;682</span> AD.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Starting in 680, the Islamic <a href="/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate" title="Umayyad Caliphate">Umayyad Caliphate</a> erupted into a civil war, known as the <a href="/wiki/Second_Fitna" title="Second Fitna">Second Fitna</a>. Umayyad authority was challenged even in their metropolitan province of <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>, while most of the Caliphate recognized <a href="/wiki/Abdallah_ibn_al-Zubayr" class="mw-redirect" title="Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr">Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr</a> instead. Under <a href="/wiki/Marwan_I" title="Marwan I">Marwan I</a> and his son <a href="/wiki/Abd_al-Malik_ibn_Marwan" title="Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan">Abd al-Malik</a>, however, the Umayyads gained the upper hand, although the Zubayrids were not finally defeated until 692.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDonner2010181–182_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDonner2010181–182-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWellhausen1927168–169_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWellhausen1927168–169-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWellhausen1927182_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWellhausen1927182-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKennedy201680_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKennedy201680-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGibb196077_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGibb196077-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The civil war in the Umayyad Caliphate provided an opportunity for the Byzantine Empire to attack its weakened rival, and, in 686, Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a> sent Leontius to invade Umayyad territory in <a href="/wiki/Armenia_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenia (region)">Armenia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Iberia</a>, where he campaigned successfully, before leading troops into <a href="/wiki/Media_(region)" title="Media (region)">Media</a> and <a href="/wiki/Caucasian_Albania" title="Caucasian Albania">Caucasian Albania</a>; during these campaigns he gathered loot.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011586-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Leontius' successful campaigns compelled the Umayyad Caliph, Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, to sue for peace in 688, agreeing to tender part of the taxes from Umayyad territory in Armenia, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iberia_(antiquity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Iberia (antiquity)">Iberia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>, and to renew a treaty signed originally under Constantine IV, providing for a weekly tribute of 1,000 pieces of gold, one horse, and one slave.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERosser20012_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERosser20012-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Justinian invaded the Caliphate again in 692, feeling that the Umayyads were in a weak position, but was repulsed at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Sebastopolis" title="Battle of Sebastopolis">Battle of Sebastopolis</a>, where a large number of <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavs</a> defected to the Umayyads, ensuring the Byzantine defeat. After this, the Umayyads renewed their invasion of <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>, aimed at taking the city of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Carthage" title="Roman Carthage">Carthage</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Exarchate_of_Africa" title="Exarchate of Africa">Exarchate of Africa</a>, and also invaded <a href="/wiki/Anatolia" title="Anatolia">Anatolia</a>. Around this time, Justinian imprisoned Leontius. Some Byzantine sources, such as <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_I_of_Constantinople" title="Nikephoros I of Constantinople">Nikephoros</a> and <a href="/wiki/Theophanes_the_Confessor" title="Theophanes the Confessor">Theophanes</a>, suggest that Justinian did so because he believed that Leontius was seeking to take the throne,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> but it is possible that the crushing defeat at Sebastopolis played a part in his imprisonment; as <i>strategos</i> of the Anatolic Theme, he likely served in the battle, and may have even been the main Byzantine commander in it.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERosser20012_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERosser20012-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After further setbacks in the war, Justinian released Leontius in 695 because he feared losing control of Carthage, and appointed him <i>strategos</i> of the Theme of <a href="/wiki/Hellas_(theme)" title="Hellas (theme)">Hellas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Southern_Greece" title="Southern Greece">Southern Greece</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaxbyAngelov201627_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaxbyAngelov201627-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAsimov1970120_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAsimov1970120-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> During his captivity, Leontius was cared for by two monks, Gregorios and Paulos, who prophesied his rise to the throne, and encouraged him to rise against Justinian after his release.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Leontius, once free, quickly raised a rebellion against Justinian.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBacharach201015-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Leontius had wide support from the aristocracy, who opposed Justinian's land policies, which restricted the aristocracy's ability to acquire land from peasant freeholders,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and the peasantry, who opposed Justinian's tax policies,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> as well as the <a href="/wiki/Chariot_racing#Byzantine_era" title="Chariot racing">Blue faction</a> (one of the <a href="/wiki/Hippodrome_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Hippodrome of Constantinople">Hippodrome</a> factions), and the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a> <a href="/wiki/Callinicus_I_of_Constantinople" title="Callinicus I of Constantinople">Callinicus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Leontius and his supporters seized Justinian and brought him to the Hippodrome, where Justinian's <a href="/wiki/Rhinotomy" title="Rhinotomy">nose was cut off</a>, a common <a href="/wiki/Political_mutilation_in_Byzantine_culture" title="Political mutilation in Byzantine culture">practice in Byzantine culture</a>, done in order to remove threats to the throne, as mutilated people were traditionally barred from becoming emperor; however, Leontius did not kill Justinian, out of reverence for Constantine IV.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaxbyAngelov201645_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaxbyAngelov201645-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> After Justinian's nose was cut off, Leontius exiled him to <a href="/wiki/Cherson_(theme)" title="Cherson (theme)">Cherson</a>, a Byzantine <a href="/wiki/Exclave" class="mw-redirect" title="Exclave">exclave</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Crimea" title="Crimea">Crimea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaxbyAngelov201627_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaxbyAngelov201627-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOstrogorsky1956116–122-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reign_and_downfall">Reign and downfall</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leontius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Reign and downfall">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon his coronation, Leontius, now known as Leo, adopted a moderate political stance. He restricted the activity of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_army" title="Byzantine army">Byzantine army</a>, allowing small raids against the border of the Byzantine empire to proceed without reprisal, and instead focused upon consolidation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarland20172_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarland20172-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Very little is known of his domestic policy, except that he had the port of <a href="/wiki/Neorion_Harbour" title="Neorion Harbour">Neorion</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> cleared, which allegedly led  to a four-month outbreak of plague.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Umayyads, emboldened by Leontius<span class="nowrap" style="padding-left:0.1em;">&#39;</span> perceived weakness, invaded the Exarchate of Africa in 696, capturing Carthage in 697. Leontius sent the <i>patrikios</i> <a href="/w/index.php?title=John_the_Patrician_(general_of_Leontius)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John the Patrician (general of Leontius) (page does not exist)">John</a> to retake the city. John was able to seize Carthage after a surprise attack on its harbor. However, Umayyad reinforcements soon retook the city, forcing John to retreat to <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a> and regroup. A group of officers, fearing the Emperor's punishment for their failure, revolted and proclaimed Apsimar, a <i><a href="/wiki/Droungarios" title="Droungarios">droungarios</a></i> (mid-level commander) of the <a href="/wiki/Cibyrrhaeot_Theme" title="Cibyrrhaeot Theme">Cibyrrhaeots</a>, emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarland20172_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarland20172-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Apsimar took the regnal name <a href="/wiki/Tiberius_III" title="Tiberius III">Tiberius</a>, gathered a fleet and allied himself with the Green faction, before sailing for Constantinople, which was enduring the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarland20172_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarland20172-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011730_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrubakerHaldon2011730-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> After several months of siege, the city surrendered to Tiberius, in 698. Tiberius captured Leontius, and had <a href="/wiki/Rhinotomy" title="Rhinotomy">his nose slit</a> before imprisoning him in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Monastery_of_Dalmatou&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Monastery of Dalmatou (page does not exist)">Monastery of Dalmatou</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarland20172_21-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarland20172-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Leontius stayed in the monastery under guard until Justinian retook the throne with the assistance of the <a href="/wiki/Bulgars" title="Bulgars">Bulgar</a> king <a href="/wiki/Tervel" class="mw-redirect" title="Tervel">Tervel</a> in 705. Justinian then had both Leontius and Tiberius dragged to the Hippodrome and publicly humiliated, before being taken away and beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore1999_7-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore1999-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan19912084_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan19912084-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The execution took place on 15 February 706 according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Altinate" title="Chronicon Altinate">Chronicon Altinate</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> The body of Leontius was thrown into the sea alongside Tiberius, but was later recovered and buried in a church on the island of <a href="/wiki/Prote" class="mw-redirect" title="Prote">Prote</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPmbZ&#91;httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)&#93;_8-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPmbZ[httpswwwdegruytercomviewPMBZPMBZ15713_Leontius_(#4547/corr.)]-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
