<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Artemius of Antioch</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Artemios.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Artemios.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/04/Artemios.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="247" data-file-width="180" data-file-height="247" /></a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Megalomartyr ("Great Martyr"), Shalliṭā</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">unknown</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">362<br /><a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> (relics transferred to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>)</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">pre-congregation</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">20 October</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data">hernias</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Artemius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Flavius Artemius</i>; <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Ἀρτέμιος</span>; died 362<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) or <b>Shalliṭā</b> (<a href="/wiki/Classical_Syriac_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Syriac language">Classical Syriac</a>: <span lang="syc" dir="rtl">ܫܠܝܛܐ</span>) was a general of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a> and <i>dux Aegypti</i> or imperial prefect of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Egypt" title="Roman Egypt">Roman Egypt</a>. He is considered a saint by the Catholic and the Orthodox Churches, with the name of <b>Artemius of Antioch</b>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Artemius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Few details are known of the life and death of Artemius, and many of those details are contradictory, or at least inconsistent, between <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> early sources. His place or year of birth are not indicated in any historical sources, although at least one tradition quoted in a contemporary source indicates that Artemius was an Egyptian by birth.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  &#160;According to the 8th century compilation, <i><a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemii_Passio" class="extiw" title="de:Artemii Passio">Artemii Passio</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> he was a Senator and “a notable participant in the highest affairs of [<a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine</a>]”.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> However, the author of the <i>Passio</i> attributes this information to <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a>, who does not in fact mention Artemius in any of his writings, and this information cannot be confirmed by any other known historical records. Furthermore, stories that place Artemius with Constantine at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Milvian_Bridge" title="Battle of the Milvian Bridge">Battle of the Milvian Bridge</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> would make Artemius at least eighty years old when martyred by <a href="/wiki/Julian_(emperor)" title="Julian (emperor)">Julian</a>, which would seem doubtful given his activity at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The assertion that Artemius was a friend and companion of <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> seems reliable.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Given the fact that Artemius held the position of <i>dux Aegypti</i> in the final years of Constantinus’ reign, as is asserted by a number of early sources, both pagan and Christian, it is clear that it is Constantius who must have awarded Artemius this position.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/AD_360" title="AD 360">360</a> <a href="/wiki/Common_Era" title="Common Era">CE</a>, he was listed in a minute of the <a href="/wiki/Oxyrhynchus" title="Oxyrhynchus">Oxyrhyncian</a> <a href="/wiki/Senate" title="Senate">Senate</a>, under the name of Flavius Artemius, as holding the rank of <i>dux Aegypti</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Artemii Passio</i> attributes Artemius’ ascension to this high position to his successful completion of Constantius’ orders to recover the relics of the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">Apostles</a> <a href="/wiki/Andrew_the_Apostle" title="Andrew the Apostle">Andrew</a>, <a href="/wiki/Luke_the_Evangelist" title="Luke the Evangelist">Luke</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Timothy" title="Saint Timothy">Timothy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> According to this narrative, Constantius sent Artemius to <a href="/wiki/Achaea" title="Achaea">Achaea</a> to claim the body of Andrew from <a href="/wiki/Patras" title="Patras">Patras</a> and the body of Luke from <a href="/wiki/Boeotia" title="Boeotia">Boeotia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Artemius is also credited there with translating the relics of Timothy from <a href="/wiki/Ionia" title="Ionia">Ionian</a> <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Apparently in return for these tasks, Constantius awarded Artemius with the administration of Roman Egypt.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> However, this attribution is not certain, given that other Christian sources that refer to the translation of St. Andrew's remains, including the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Paschale" title="Chronicon Paschale">Chronicon Paschale</a></i>, written a century earlier, do not refer to Artemius in this regard.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While serving as the military governor of Egypt, Artemius supported the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> bishop of <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, <a href="/wiki/George_of_Cappadocia" title="George of Cappadocia">George</a> the <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocian</a>, and took part in the latter's campaign against both the pagan and <a href="/wiki/Orthodoxy" title="Orthodoxy">Orthodox Christian</a> populations. Documented examples of this support include the sacking of the <a href="/wiki/Serapeum" title="Serapeum">Temple of Serapis</a> in Alexandria,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> as well as the pursuit of the former, Orthodox, bishop of Alexandria, <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/361" title="361">361</a> CE, Constantius was succeeded by his cousin <a href="/wiki/Julian_(Roman_emperor)" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian (Roman emperor)">Julian</a>, who, on becoming <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Emperor</a>, broke with his Christian upbringing and took up traditional Roman pagan pantheism. Shortly thereafter, Artemius met his death, in circumstances that are not entirely clear. The <i>Artemii Passio</i> places his death at the hands of Julian, in <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, because upon being summoned by Julian from Alexandria, he supported <a href="/wiki/Eugenius_(Antioch)" title="Eugenius (Antioch)">Eugenius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Macarius</a>, two Christian priests from Antioch, against the tortures to which they were subjected by Julian. Artemius is further accused by Julian of murdering Julian's half-brother, <a href="/wiki/Constantius_Gallus" title="Constantius Gallus">Gallus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Despite Artemius' denials, Julian proceeds to have Artemius tortured in various fashions while exhorting Artemius to renounce his Christianity. Artemius repeatedly confirms his faith, and ultimately has his head cut off.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i>Chronicon Paschale</i>, Artemius dies in Alexandria.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Ammianus_Marcellinus" title="Ammianus Marcellinus">Ammianus</a> describes Artemius’ death as occurring after Artemius is no longer <i>dux Aegypti,</i> when he is executed for the crimes he was accused of committing against the people of Alexandria.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Julian himself provides a similar reason.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> While Ammianus does not provide the location of Artemius’ death, his language indicates that it was not at Alexandria,<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> thus perhaps strengthening the contention that it was indeed at Antioch.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Artemius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Artemius is considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox Churches</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> Churches, despite his apparent adherence to <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a> during his life. His acceptance as a martyr is based on the tradition found in Christian sources that he was tortured and executed by Julian for speaking on behalf of Christianity in the face of the <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostate</a> <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Julian" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Julian">Emperor Julian</a>, and his unwillingness to renounce his Christianity despite Julian's promises to allow him to live and restore him to his offices.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> is based on the anonymous late 7th century compilation of 45 <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> with which he is attributed.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> As described in the 45 miracles attributed to him, St. Artemios specializes in miracles dealing with medicine and healing, and in particular with <a href="/wiki/Hernia" title="Hernia">hernias</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Testicle" title="Testicle">testicular</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sex_organ" title="Sex organ">genital</a> <a href="/wiki/Disease" title="Disease">maladies</a> in men. One miracle dealing with the healing of a woman is attributed to St. Artemios through his sending <a href="/wiki/Febronia_of_Nisibis" title="Febronia of Nisibis">St. Febronia</a>, who occupies a parallel construct for women.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was set as 20 October.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> His cult site was the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_John_the_Baptist_(Constantinople)" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Saint John the Baptist (Constantinople)">Church of Saint John the Forerunning</a> (St John the Baptist) in Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> St. Artemius is invoked by those suffering from <a href="/wiki/Hernia" title="Hernia">hernias</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
