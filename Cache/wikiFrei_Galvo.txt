May 13, 1739 – December 23, 1822), was a <a href="/wiki/Brazilian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Brazilian people">Brazilian</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> Order.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> One of the best-known religious figures in <a href="/wiki/Colony_of_Brazil" class="mw-redirect" title="Colony of Brazil">colonial Brazil</a>, renowned for his healing powers,<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Galvão was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> on May 11, 2007, becoming the first Brazilian-born saint.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BBC1_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was the second Brazilian to be proclaimed a saint by the Catholic Church, after <a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austro-Hungarian</a>-born <a href="/wiki/Pauline_of_the_Agonizing_Heart_of_Jesus" title="Pauline of the Agonizing Heart of Jesus">Pauline of the Agonizing Heart of Jesus</a>' was canonized in 2002.<sup id="cite_ref-IPS_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IPS-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frei_Galv%C3%A3o&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frei_Galv%C3%A3o&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Galvão was born in the <a href="/wiki/Freguesia" title="Freguesia">freguesia</a> of <a href="/wiki/Guaratinguet%C3%A1" title="Guaratinguetá">Santo Antonio of Guaratinguetá</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_(state)" title="São Paulo (state)">State of São Paulo</a>. He was the fourth of ten children in a deeply religious family of high social and political status.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His father, Antônio Galvão de França, was the <a href="/wiki/Captaincy" title="Captaincy">Captain</a> of the village. Although he was active in the world of politics and commerce, Antônio the father also belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> and was known for his generosity.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His mother, Isabel Leite de Barros, was from a farming family, and was a great-granddaughter of the famous <a href="/wiki/Bandeirantes" title="Bandeirantes">bandeirante</a> explorer <a href="/wiki/Fern%C3%A3o_Dias_Pais" class="mw-redirect" title="Fernão Dias Pais">Fernão Dias Pais</a>, known as the "Emerald Hunter". She gave birth to eleven children before her premature death in 1755, at age 38.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Equally known for her generosity, Isabel was found to have given away all of her clothes to the poor at the time of her death.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At age 13, Galvão was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a>-run seminary Colégio de Belém<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Cachoeira" title="Cachoeira">Cachoeira</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bahia" title="Bahia">Bahia</a> by his father, which wished to provide <a href="/wiki/Humanities" title="Humanities">Humanities</a> and cultural training to his son. He followed his brother José, who was already studying there. At Colégio de Belém, which he attended from 1752 to 1756, Galvão made great progress in social studies and Christian practice. He aspired to become a Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, but the anti-Jesuit persecution led by <a href="/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_de_Carvalho_e_Melo,_Marquis_of_Pombal" class="mw-redirect" title="Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo, Marquis of Pombal">Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Marquis_of_Pombal" class="mw-redirect" title="Marquis of Pombal">Marquis of Pombal</a>, led him to enter the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> Order instead.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Taubat%C3%A9" title="Taubaté">Taubaté</a>, following the advice of his father.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Freigalvao11052007.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Freigalvao11052007.jpg/170px-Freigalvao11052007.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="273" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Freigalvao11052007.jpg/255px-Freigalvao11052007.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Freigalvao11052007.jpg/340px-Freigalvao11052007.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1710" data-file-height="2742" /></a><figcaption>Monument to Frei Galvão in his native town of Guaratinguetá.</figcaption></figure>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Franciscan_friar">Franciscan friar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frei_Galv%C3%A3o&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Franciscan friar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At age 16, Galvão gave up a promising future and his family's social influence, becoming a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> at the St. Bonaventure Friary in Vila de Macacu, <a href="/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro" title="Rio de Janeiro">Rio de Janeiro</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> There, he adopted the religious name of <b>Anthony of Saint Ann</b>, in honor of his family's devotion to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ann" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ann">Saint Ann</a>. During his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> year, he was known for his piety, zeal and exemplary virtues.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Galvão made his profession of <a href="/wiki/Solemn_vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Solemn vows">solemn vows</a> on 16 April 1761, taking the additional vow to defend the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>'s title of the "<a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>", which was still a controversial teaching at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 11 July 1762, Galvão was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest and transferred to St. Francis Friary in the city of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a>, where he continued his studies in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In early 1766, around the completion of his studies, Galvão made a spiritual submission of himself as a "servant and slave" of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Mother" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Mother">Blessed Mother</a>, signing a document to this effect, dated 9 March, which he signed in his own blood.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In 1768 he was appointed preacher, <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">porter</a> of the friary, an important post.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1769 to 1770 Galvão served as confessor to the Recollection of St. Teresa (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Recolhimento de Santa Teresa</i>) in the city of São Paulo, which was a <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> of women Recollects (<a href="/wiki/Recluse" title="Recluse">recluses</a> living in common but not under <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>), dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila" title="Teresa of Ávila">Teresa of Ávila</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> There, he met the nun Helena Maria of the Holy Spirit, a recluse who claimed to have had visions in which Jesus was asking her to found a new Recollect house.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Galvão, her confessor, studied these messages and consulted with others who recognized them as valid and <a href="/wiki/Supernatural" title="Supernatural">supernatural</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Galvão collaborated in the foundation of the new Recollect house, named Our Lady of the Conception of Divine Providence, which was established on 2 February 1774<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> in the same city. It was modeled after the <a href="/wiki/Conceptionists" title="Conceptionists">Conceptionist nuns</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and became the home for girls who wished to live a religious life but without taking vows.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> With Helena's sudden death on 23 February 1775, Galvão became the new superior of the community,<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> serving as the Recollects' new <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_leader" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual leader">spiritual leader</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg/170px-Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg/255px-Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg/340px-Guaratinguet%C3%A1_FreiGalv%C3%A3o.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="2048" /></a><figcaption>Frei Galvão in the Cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">St. Anthony</a> in <a href="/wiki/Guaratinguet%C3%A1" title="Guaratinguetá">Guaratinguetá</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Around that time, a change in São Paulo's provincial government brought an inflexible leader who ordered the closing of the hermitage.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Galvão accepted the decision, but the recluses refused to leave the premises, and due to popular pressure and the efforts of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of São Paulo">Bishop of São Paulo</a>, the hermitage was soon re-opened.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Subsequently, with the increasing number of new recluses, more living space was required.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It took Galvão 28 years to build the hermitage and church, with the latter being inaugurated on 15 August 1802.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In addition to the construction work and duties within and outside his Order, Galvão committed himself to the Recollect's formation.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Statutes he wrote for them was a guide for the interior life and religious discipline.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When things seemed more quiet, another government intervention brought Galvão a further trial.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Captain_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Captain General">Captain General</a> sentenced a soldier to death for having slightly offended his son, and the friar was sent into exile for coming to the soldier's defence.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Again, popular demand succeeded in having the order revoked.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1781, Galvão was appointed <a href="/wiki/Novice_master" title="Novice master">novice master</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cachoeiras_de_Macacu" title="Cachoeiras de Macacu">Cachoeiras de Macacu</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> However, the Recollects and the Bishop of São Paulo appealed to the <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Minister Provincial</a>, writing that "none of the inhabitants of this city will be able to bear the absence of this Religious for a single moment".<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As a result, he returned.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was later named <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Guardian</a> of St. Francis Friary in São Paulo in 1798, being re-appointed in 1801.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1811 Galvão founded St. Clare Friary in <a href="/wiki/Sorocaba" title="Sorocaba">Sorocaba</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Eleven months later, he returned to São Paulo.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In his old age, he obtained permission from the bishop and the Guardian to stay at the Recollect house.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He died there on 23 December 1822.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Galvão was laid to rest in the Recollection Church, and his tomb continues to be a destination for pilgrimages of the faithful, who obtain graces through his intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1929, the Convent of Our Lady of the Conception of Divine Providence became a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Immaculate_Conception" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of the Immaculate Conception">Order of the Immaculate Conception</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_2-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The building, now called the "Monastery of Light", has been declared a world cultural heritage site by <a href="/wiki/UNESCO" title="UNESCO">UNESCO</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DPA_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DPA-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The complex now serves as the <a href="https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_de_Arte_Sacra_de_S%C3%A3o_Paulo" class="extiw" title="pt:Museu de Arte Sacra de São Paulo">Museum of the Sacred Arts of São Paulo</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mysticism">Mysticism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frei_Galv%C3%A3o&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Mysticism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Galvão was a man of great and intense prayer, and mystic phenomena attributed to him include <a href="/wiki/Telepathy" title="Telepathy">telepathy</a>, <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/premonition" class="extiw" title="wikt:premonition">premonition</a> and <a href="/wiki/Levitation_(physics)" title="Levitation (physics)">levitation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DPA_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DPA-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was reportedly in <a href="/wiki/Bilocation" title="Bilocation">two different places at the same time</a> in order to take care of sick or dying people who had asked for his help.<sup id="cite_ref-DPA_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DPA-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:BentoXVI-51-11052007_(frag).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/BentoXVI-51-11052007_%28frag%29.jpg/170px-BentoXVI-51-11052007_%28frag%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/BentoXVI-51-11052007_%28frag%29.jpg/255px-BentoXVI-51-11052007_%28frag%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/BentoXVI-51-11052007_%28frag%29.jpg/340px-BentoXVI-51-11052007_%28frag%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="416" data-file-height="550" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> celebrates Holy Mass at the <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of Frei Galvão in <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> on 11 May 2007</figcaption></figure>
<p>Many sought Galvão for his reputed healing powers, particularly when medical resources were expensive or unavailable. Galvão became known for his "paper pills": he wrote a <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> phrase from the <a href="/wiki/Little_Office_of_Our_Lady" class="mw-redirect" title="Little Office of Our Lady">Little Office of Our Lady</a> ("After childbirth thou didst remain a Virgin: O Mother of God, intercede for us") on a piece of paper, rolled it like a pill, and gave it to  suffering patients.<sup id="cite_ref-CNS_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CNS-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CBS_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CBS-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Once he gave the paper pill to a young woman in excruciating pain from kidney stones: the pain ceased immediately after she consumed it, and she expelled a large amount of <a href="/wiki/Renal_calculus" class="mw-redirect" title="Renal calculus">renal calculus</a>. On other occasions, he gave it to women suffering during difficult childbirths. After Galvão gave a paper pill to a man with such a suffering wife, the child was quickly born without further complications.<sup id="cite_ref-DPA_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DPA-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CNS_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CNS-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The story of the miracle pill spread, and Galvão had to teach the Recollect Sisters to make them, which they still do nowadays. They are handed out for free to some 300 faithful who request them daily.<sup id="cite_ref-DPA_10-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DPA-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg/170px-BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="248" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg/255px-BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg/340px-BentoXVI-49-11052007.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1705" data-file-height="2484" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of Frei Galvão (in the picture).</figcaption></figure>
<p>On October 25, 1998, Galvão became the first Brazilian-born person to be <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Vatican</a>, having been declared <i><a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a></i> a year earlier, on March 8, 1997.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_7-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> On May 11, 2007, he became the first Brazilian-born person <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by the Roman Catholic Church, during <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>'s five-day visit to Brazil.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC1_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The open-air ceremony, which lasted over two hours in the <a href="/wiki/Campo_de_Marte_Airport" title="Campo de Marte Airport">Campo de Marte Military Airport</a>, near downtown <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a>, drew 800,000 people, according to official estimates.<sup id="cite_ref-IPS_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IPS-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Galvão was the first saint canonized by Pope Benedict XVI in a ceremony held outside the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CBS_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CBS-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His proclamation as a saint came after the Catholic Church accepted that two <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> had taken place.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the Catholic Church, the cases of Sandra Grossi de Almeida and Daniella Cristina da Silva are miracles effected through the prayers of Galvão.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC1_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After taking one of the paper pills, Almeida, who had a <a href="/wiki/Uterine_malformation" title="Uterine malformation">uterine malformation</a> that should have made it impossible for her to carry a child for more than four months, gave birth to baby boy Enzo.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC1_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Galvão's pills are also certified by the church with the healing, in 1990, of Daniella Cristina da Silva, a four-year-old girl suffering from what doctors considered incurable <a href="/wiki/Hepatitis" title="Hepatitis">hepatitis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-IPS_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IPS-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-afp_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-afp-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Doctors, and some Catholic clergy, have dismissed the pills as <a href="/wiki/Placebos" class="mw-redirect" title="Placebos">placebos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CBS_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CBS-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The church recommends that only terminally ill patients take the pills.<sup id="cite_ref-afp_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-afp-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
