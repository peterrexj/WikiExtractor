<p><b>Saint Fructus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Fruitos, Frutos, Fructos</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Old_Castile" title="Old Castile">Castilian</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> of the eighth century venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> tradition states that he had two siblings, named <b>Valentine</b> (<i>Valentín</i>) and <b>Engratia</b> (<i>Engracia</i>).  They all lived as hermits on a mountain in the region of <a href="/wiki/Sep%C3%BAlveda,_Segovia" title="Sepúlveda, Segovia">Sepúlveda</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Engratia should not be confused with the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Engratia" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Engratia">4th-century Portuguese martyr of the same name</a>.
</p><p>Born in the 7th century to a noble family of <a href="/wiki/Segovia" title="Segovia">Segovia</a>, Fructus and his two siblings sold their family possessions after their parents' death and gave the earnings to poor.  Wishing to escape from the city and the turbulent times, they established themselves on the rocky terrain near the village of Sepulveda now known as the <a href="/wiki/Hoces_del_Rio_Durat%C3%B3n_National_Park" class="mw-redirect" title="Hoces del Rio Duratón National Park">Hoces del Duratón</a>, where they lived apart from one another in caves that ensured them complete solitude.<sup id="cite_ref-paginasdesegovia.com_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paginasdesegovia.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tradition holds that Valentine and Engratia were later martyred around 715 by advancing <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moorish</a> forces, and that Fructus died of <a href="/wiki/Natural_causes" class="mw-redirect" title="Natural causes">natural causes</a> in the same year at the age of 73.<sup id="cite_ref-villaseca.org.es_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villaseca.org.es-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fructus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A legend states that some locals, wishing to join Fructus in his retreat to his death, traveled there, only to be pursued by Moorish forces to the very door of Fructus’ <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a>.  Fructus attempted to convert the Muslim soldiers, but without success.  The legend goes on to state that Fructus drew a line across the earth, asking that the Moorish forces not cross it.  When they ignored him and attempted to cross, the earth miraculously opened up to swallow them up, at a crack in the rock now called La Cuchillada.  From that point on, the Moors did not bother Fructus.<sup id="cite_ref-paginasdesegovia.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paginasdesegovia.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Fructus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>They are venerated as the patron saints of <a href="/wiki/Segovia" title="Segovia">Segovia</a>, where their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> are <a href="/wiki/Enshrined" class="mw-redirect" title="Enshrined">enshrined</a> and are recognized as saints of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Orthodox Christianity">Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate" title="Patriarchate">Patriarchate</a> of Rome.
</p><p>Fructus, Valentine and Engratia are commemorated on 25 October by <a href="/wiki/Western_Rite_Orthodoxy" title="Western Rite Orthodoxy">Western Rite Orthodox</a> communities, and in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>Their relics were conserved in the hermitage of San Frutos from the 8th century to the 11th, when they were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/Segovia_Cathedral" title="Segovia Cathedral">Segovia Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-paginasdesegovia.com_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paginasdesegovia.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  The area of Fructus' hermitage suffered various political and military vicissitudes; this area was conquered by <a href="/wiki/Fern%C3%A1n_Gonz%C3%A1lez" class="mw-redirect" title="Fernán González">Fernán González</a> before being annexed by <a href="/wiki/Al-Mansur_Ibn_Abi_Aamir" class="mw-redirect" title="Al-Mansur Ibn Abi Aamir">Al-Mansur Ibn Abi Aamir</a> in 984.  It fell to Christian control once again in 1011 through the efforts of <a href="/wiki/Sancho_Garc%C3%ADa_of_Castile" title="Sancho García of Castile">Sancho García of Castile</a>, and in 1076 was repopulated by <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VI_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso VI of Castile">Alfonso VI of Castile</a>.  By the 1070s, the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictines</a> had established a church in honor of Saint Fructus in the area, as well as an adjoining monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-villaseca.org.es_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villaseca.org.es-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the night of 24 October is celebrated the procession in honor of Fructus known as the <i>Paso de la Hoja</i> ("Turn of the Page").<sup id="cite_ref-paginasdesegovia.com_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paginasdesegovia.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  A sculpture of Fructus rests in a <a href="/wiki/Niche_(architecture)" title="Niche (architecture)">niche</a> in this cathedral.  This sculpture has Fructus holding a book; according to local legend, it is the “Book of Life”: when Fructus turns to the last page, the world will end.<sup id="cite_ref-paginasdesegovia.com_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paginasdesegovia.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fructus' <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated with music and contests, and devotees also celebrate his feast day at the park of Hoces del Río Duratón, where they accompany a statue of Fructus.<sup id="cite_ref-paginasdesegovia.com_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-paginasdesegovia.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
