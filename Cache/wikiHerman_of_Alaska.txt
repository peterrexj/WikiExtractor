<p><b>Herman of Alaska</b> (Russian&#58; <span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Преподобный Ге́рман Аляскинский</span></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">tr.</a></small> <span title="Russian-language text"><i lang="ru-Latn">Prepodobny German Alaskinsky</i></span>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1756 – November 15, 1837) was a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox">Russian Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and missionary to <a href="/wiki/Alaska" title="Alaska">Alaska</a>, which was then part of <a href="/wiki/Russian_America" title="Russian America">Russian America</a>. His gentle approach and ascetic life earned him the love and respect of both the native Alaskans and the Russian colonists. He is considered by many Orthodox Christians as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of North America.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Herman_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Biographers disagree about Herman's early life. His official biography, which Valaam Monastery published in 1867, said that his pre-monastic name was unknown, but that Herman was born into a merchant's family in <a href="/wiki/Serpukhov" title="Serpukhov">Serpukhov</a>, a city in <a href="/wiki/Moscow_Governorate" title="Moscow Governorate">Moscow Governorate</a>. He was said to later become a novice at the <a href="/wiki/Trinity-St._Sergius_Hermitage" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinity-St. Sergius Hermitage">Trinity-St. Sergius Hermitage</a> near <a href="/wiki/St._Petersburg" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Petersburg">St. Petersburg</a> before going to <a href="/wiki/Valaam" title="Valaam">Valaam</a> to complete his training and receive full tonsure as a monk.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> But, modern biographer Sergei Korsun found this account to be based on erroneous information provided by <a href="/wiki/Semyon_Yanovsky" title="Semyon Yanovsky">Semyon Yanovsky</a>, an administrator from 1818 through part of 1820 of the <a href="/wiki/Russian-American_Company" title="Russian-American Company">Russian-American Company</a> (RAC) in Alaska. He confused Herman's biographical information with that of another monk, Joseph (Telepnev).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another former RAC Chief Manager, <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_von_Wrangel" title="Ferdinand von Wrangel">Ferdinand von Wrangel</a>, stated Herman was originally from a prosperous peasant family in the <a href="/wiki/Voronezh_Governorate" title="Voronezh Governorate">Voronezh Governorate</a> and served in the military. He then entered monastic life as a novice at <a href="/wiki/Sarov" title="Sarov">Sarov</a> Monastery. This concurred with testimony of Archimandrite Theophan (Sokolov), and a letter written by Herman himself. These agree that Herman began his monastic life as a novice at Sarov, and later received the full tonsure at <a href="/wiki/Valaam" title="Valaam">Valaam</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A young military clerk named <b>Egor Ivanovich Popov</b>, from the <a href="/wiki/Voronezh_Governorate" title="Voronezh Governorate">Voronezh Governorate</a>, was tonsured with the name 'Herman' at Valaam in 1782.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>All biographers agree that at Valaam, Herman studied under Abbot Nazarius, previously of Sarov Monastery. The abbot had been influenced by the <a href="/wiki/Hesychastic" class="mw-redirect" title="Hesychastic">hesychastic</a> tradition of <a href="/wiki/Paisius_Velichkovsky" title="Paisius Velichkovsky">Paisius Velichkovsky</a>. Herman undertook various obediences and was well-liked by the brethren, but wanted a more solitary life. He became a hermit with Abbot Nazarius' blessing.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His hermitage, which later became known as "Herman's field" or Germanovo, was two kilometers from the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-Kovalskaya_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kovalskaya-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Metropolitan Gabriel of St. Petersburg offered to ordain Herman to the priesthood and twice offered to send him to lead the Russian Orthodox Mission in China, but he refused, preferring the solitary life and remaining a simple monk.<sup id="cite_ref-Kovalskaya_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kovalskaya-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Years after he left for North America, Herman continued to keep in touch with his spiritual home.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In a letter to Abbot Nazarius, he wrote, "in my mind I imagine my beloved Valaam, and constantly behold it across the great ocean."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mission_in_Alaska">Mission in Alaska</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Herman_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mission in Alaska">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Grigory_Shelikov.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Grigory_Shelikov.jpg/220px-Grigory_Shelikov.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Grigory_Shelikov.jpg/330px-Grigory_Shelikov.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Grigory_Shelikov.jpg/440px-Grigory_Shelikov.jpg 2x" data-file-width="480" data-file-height="479" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Grigory_Shelikov.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Grigory Shelikhov, the founder of the Kodiak Island settlement, invited the first Russian Orthodox missionaries to the New World.</div></div></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Russian_colonization_of_the_Americas" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian colonization of the Americas">Russian colonization of the Americas</a> began when <a href="/wiki/Vitus_Bering" title="Vitus Bering">Vitus Bering</a> and <a href="/wiki/Aleksei_Chirikov" title="Aleksei Chirikov">Aleksei Chirikov</a> discovered Alaska on behalf of the Russian Empire in 1741. The expedition harvested 1,500 <a href="/wiki/Sea_otter" title="Sea otter">sea otter</a> pelts, which Chinese merchants bought for 1,000 <a href="/wiki/Rubles" class="mw-redirect" title="Rubles">rubles</a> each at their trading post near <a href="/wiki/Lake_Baikal" title="Lake Baikal">Lake Baikal</a>. This spurred a "fur rush" from 1741 to 1798 in which frontiersmen known as <i><a href="/wiki/Promyshlenniki" title="Promyshlenniki">promyshlenniki</a></i> explored Alaska and the <a href="/wiki/Aleutian_Islands" title="Aleutian Islands">Aleutian Islands</a>. They alternately fought with and intermarried the native peoples.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Grigory_Shelikhov" title="Grigory Shelikhov">Grigory Shelikhov</a>, a fur-trader, subjugated the native population of <a href="/wiki/Kodiak_Island" title="Kodiak Island">Kodiak Island</a>. With Ivan Golikov, he founded a fur-trading company that eventually received a monopoly from the Imperial government; it became known as the Russian-American Company. Shelikhov founded a school for the natives, and many were converted to Russian Orthodox Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Shelikhov-Golikov_Company" title="Shelikhov-Golikov Company">Shelikhov-Golikov Company</a> appealed to the <a href="/wiki/Most_Holy_Synod" title="Most Holy Synod">Most Holy Synod</a> of the Russian Orthodox Church to provide a priest for the natives. <a href="/wiki/Catherine_the_Great" title="Catherine the Great">Catherine the Great</a> decided instead to send an entire mission to America. She entrusted the task of recruiting missionaries to Metropolitan Gabriel of St. Petersburg, who sent ten monks from Valaam, including Herman.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The missionaries arrived on Kodiak on September 24, 1794.<sup id="cite_ref-Oleksa,_p._109_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oleksa,_p._109-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Herman and the other missionaries encountered a harsh reality at Kodiak that did not correspond to Shelikhov's rosy descriptions. The native Kodiak population, called "Americans" by the Russian settlers, were subject to harsh treatment by the Russian-American Company, which was being overseen by Shelikhov's manager <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Andreyevich_Baranov" title="Alexander Andreyevich Baranov">Alexander Baranov</a>. He later became the first governor of the colony. 
</p><p>The men were forced to hunt for sea otter even during harsh weather, and women and children were abused.<sup id="cite_ref-Oleksa,_p._109_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oleksa,_p._109-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The monks were also shocked at the widespread alcoholism in the Russian population, and the fact that most of the settlers had taken native mistresses.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The monks themselves were not given the supplies that Shelikhov promised them,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and had to till the ground with wooden implements.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Despite these difficulties, the monks baptized more than 7,000 natives in the Kodiak region, and set about building a church and monastery. Herman was assigned in the bakery and acted as the mission's steward (<i>ekonom</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The monks became the defenders of the native Kodiak population. Herman was especially noted for his zeal in protecting them from the excessive demands of the RAC, and Baranov disparaged him in a letter as a "hack writer and chatterer."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> A contemporary historian compares him to <a href="/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_de_las_Casas" title="Bartolomé de las Casas">Bartolomé de las Casas</a>, the Roman Catholic friar who defended the rights of native South Americans against the Spanish colonists.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After over a decade spent in Alaska, Herman became the head of the mission in 1807, although he was not ordained to the priesthood. The local population loved and respected him, and he had established good relations with Baranov.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Herman ran the mission school, where he taught church subjects such as singing and catechism, alongside reading and writing. He also taught agriculture on <a href="/wiki/Spruce_Island_(Alaska)" title="Spruce Island (Alaska)">Spruce Island</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> But, because he still longed for the life of a hermit, he retired from active duty in the mission and moved to Spruce Island.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_on_Spruce_Island">Life on Spruce Island</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Herman_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Life on Spruce Island">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Herman moved to <a href="/wiki/Spruce_Island_(Alaska)" title="Spruce Island (Alaska)">Spruce Island</a> around 1811 to 1817.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> The island is separated from Kodiak by a mile-wide strait, making it ideal for eremitic life. Herman named his hermitage "New Valaam." He wore simple clothes and slept on a bench covered with a deerskin. When asked how he could bear to be alone in the forest, he replied, "I am not alone. God is here, as God is everywhere."<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite his solitary life, he soon gained a following. He received many visitors—especially native <a href="/wiki/Aleut" title="Aleut">Aleuts</a> —on Sundays and church feasts. Soon a chapel and guesthouse were built next to his hermitage, and then a school for orphans.<sup id="cite_ref-Kovalskaya_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kovalskaya-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Herman had a few disciples, including the Creole orphan Gerasim Ivanovich Zyrianov, a young Aleut woman named Sofia Vlasova, and others. 
</p><p>Entire families moved to the island in order to be closer to the Elder, who helped to sort out their disputes.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Herman had a deep love for the native Aleuts: he stood up for them against the excesses of the Russian-American Company, and once during an epidemic, he was the only Russian to visit them, working tirelessly to care for the sick and console the dying.<sup id="cite_ref-Kovalskaya_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kovalskaya-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Herman spent the rest of his life on Spruce Island, where he died on November 15, 1837.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Herman_of_Alaska&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg/250px-Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="364" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg/375px-Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg/500px-Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="651" data-file-height="948" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saints <a href="/wiki/Sergius_of_Valaam" title="Sergius of Valaam">Sergius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Valaam" title="Herman of Valaam">Herman of Valaam</a> Chapel, built in 1898 over the site where Herman was buried on <a href="/wiki/Spruce_Island" class="mw-redirect" title="Spruce Island">Spruce Island</a> in December 1836. Located near Monk's Lagoon, in the immediate vicinity of St. Herman's hermitage.</div></div></div>
<p>On March 11, 1969, the bishops of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a> (OCA) formally declared their intention to canonize Herman, "as a sublime example of the Holy Life, for our spiritual benefit, inspiration, comfort, and the confirmation of our Faith."<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> On August 9, 1970, Metropolitan <a href="/wiki/Ireney_(Bekish)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ireney (Bekish)">Ireney (Bekish)</a> of the OCA along with Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Paul_(Olmari)" title="Paul (Olmari)">Paul (Olmari)</a> of Finland and other hierarchs and clergy presided over the canonization service,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> which was held at Holy Resurrection Cathedral on Kodiak Island.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> His relics were transferred from his grave underneath the <a href="/wiki/Sts._Sergius_and_Herman_of_Valaam_Chapel" title="Sts. Sergius and Herman of Valaam Chapel">Sts. Sergius and Herman of Valaam Chapel</a> (i.e., the Saints <a href="/wiki/Sergius_of_Valaam" title="Sergius of Valaam">Sergius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Valaam" title="Herman of Valaam">Herman of Valaam</a> Chapel), on Spruce Island, to the Holy Resurrection Cathedral.
</p><p>On the same date, the bishops of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_of_Russia" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia</a> (ROCOR) also canonized Herman at the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Virgin_Cathedral" title="Holy Virgin Cathedral">Holy Virgin Cathedral</a> ("Joy of All Who Sorrow") in San Francisco. At the <a href="/wiki/All-night_vigil" title="All-night vigil">all-night vigil</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(hymnography)" title="Canon (hymnography)">canon</a> to Herman was read for the first time by Gleb Podmoshensky, one of the founding brothers of the St. Herman of Alaska Serbian Orthodox Brotherhood in 1963. He, <a href="/wiki/Seraphim_Rose" title="Seraphim Rose">Eugene (Seraphim) Rose</a>, and Lawrence Campbell gathered material for the Synod of Bishops in order to support the glorification of Herman, and also helped compose the liturgical service in his honor.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are several feast days throughout the year on which Saint Herman of Alaska is commemorated. Since there are two different calendars currently in use among various Orthodox churches, two dates are listed: the first date is the date on the traditional <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>, the second date, after the slash, is the same day on the modern <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>:
</p>
<ul><li><b>July 27/August 9—Glorification:</b> This is the anniversary of the joint-glorification (<a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>) of Herman of Alaska as a saint in 1970.</li>
<li><b>November 15/28—Repose:</b> This is the anniversary of the actual death of Herman.</li>
<li><b>December 13/26—Repose:</b> Due to an error in record keeping, this was originally thought to be the day of Herman's death, and because of the long-established tradition of celebrating his memory on this day, it has remained a feast day. It is more likely that this is the day he was buried. For those Orthodox Christians who follow the Julian Calendar, this day falls on December 25 of the Gregorian Calendar.</li>
<li><b>Second Sunday after Pentecost:</b>, as one of the saints commemorated on the Synaxis of the Saints of North America—this is a <a href="/wiki/Moveable_feast" title="Moveable feast">moveable feast</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_year" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecclesiastical year">ecclesiastical year</a>, and the date of its observance will change from year to year.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>The major portion of his relics are preserved at Holy Resurrection Cathedral in <a href="/wiki/Kodiak,_Alaska" title="Kodiak, Alaska">Kodiak, Alaska</a>, His burial site at the Sts. Sergius and Herman Chapel, Spruce Island, Alaska is an important <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrimage</a> site. The devout will often take soil from his grave and water from the spring named in his honour.  
</p><p>A portion of his relics are enshrined at the <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch" title="Ignatius of Antioch">St. Ignatius</a> Chapel at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Antiochan_Village&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Antiochan Village (page does not exist)">Antiochan Village</a> in <a href="/wiki/Pennsylvania" title="Pennsylvania">Pennsylvania</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> a conference and retreat center of the <a href="/wiki/Antiochian_Orthodox_Christian_Archdiocese_of_North_America" title="Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese of North America">Antiochian Orthodox Christian Archdiocese of North America</a>. He is regarded as one of their patron saints.
</p><p>In 1963, with the blessing of <a href="/wiki/John_of_Shanghai_and_San_Francisco" title="John of Shanghai and San Francisco">John Maximovitch</a>, Archbishop of Shanghai and San Francisco, a community of Orthodox booksellers and publishers called the St. Herman of Alaska Brotherhood was formed to publish Orthodox missionary information in English. 
</p><p>One of the founders was Father <a href="/wiki/Seraphim_Rose" title="Seraphim Rose">Seraphim Rose</a>. The Brotherhood did much to advance the cause of Herman's glorification as a saint. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Herman%27s_Orthodox_Theological_Seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Herman&#39;s Orthodox Theological Seminary">Saint Herman's Orthodox Theological Seminary</a> in Kodiak, Alaska is named in his honor, as are numerous <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish churches</a> throughout the world.
</p><p>On Tuesday, August 4, 1970, the <a href="/wiki/91st_United_States_Congress" title="91st United States Congress">91st</a> <a href="/wiki/Congress_of_the_United_States" class="mw-redirect" title="Congress of the United States">Congress of the United States</a> acknowledged the glorification of Herman of Alaska with a speech in the <a href="/wiki/United_States_Senate" title="United States Senate">Senate</a>, and his biography was formally entered into the <a href="/wiki/Congressional_Record" title="Congressional Record">Congressional Record</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1993, <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Moscow">Patriarch</a> <a href="/wiki/Alexis_II_of_Moscow" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexis II of Moscow">Alexis II</a> visited Kodiak to venerate the relics of Saint Herman. He left as a gift an ornate <i><a href="/wiki/Lampada" class="mw-redirect" title="Lampada">lampada</a></i> (oil lamp) which burns constantly over the <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a>. Pilgrims from all over the world are <a href="/wiki/Anointing" title="Anointing">anointed</a> with <a href="/wiki/Chrism" title="Chrism">holy oil</a> from this <i>lampada</i>.
</p><p>In 2022, Herman was officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day on 15 November.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Finnish_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnish Orthodox Church">Finnish Orthodox</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Espoo_Orthodox_Church&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Espoo Orthodox Church (page does not exist)">Espoo Church</a> in Tapiola, Espoo, is dedicated to St. Herman of Alaska.
</p>
