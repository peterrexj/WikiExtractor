<p><b>Saint Macarius of Alexandria</b> (died 395)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Nitrian_Desert" title="Nitrian Desert">Nitrian Desert</a>. He was a slightly younger contemporary of <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Macarius of Egypt</a>, and is thus also known as <b>Macarius the Younger</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-abanoub_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abanoub-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Macarius_of_Alexandria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Macarius was born about the year 300 in Alexandria. He was a merchant selling confections<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> until the age of 40, when he was baptized and went off into the desert. After several years of ascetic life, he was ordained a presbyter and appointed prior of a monastery known as the "Kellii", or "cells" in the Egyptian desert, between the Nitria mountain and a skete in which monastic hermits lived in silence, each in his own cell.<sup id="cite_ref-stjohns_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stjohns-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>About the year 335, Macarius of Alexandria retired to live alone as a recluse in el-Natroun desert.<sup id="cite_ref-abanoub_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abanoub-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Many miracles were ascribed to him. He presided over five thousand <a href="/wiki/Nitrian_Desert" title="Nitrian Desert">Nitric</a> monks.
</p><p>Having learned of the extremely strict rule for monastic life observed at the Tabbenesiot Monastery, whose prior was Venerable St. <a href="/wiki/Pachomius_the_Great" title="Pachomius the Great">Pachomios the Great</a> (+ 348), St. Macarius disguised himself in secular clothing, and over the course of the entire Quadragesima [the 40-day Great Lent] neither ate bread nor drank water.<sup id="cite_ref-stjohns_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stjohns-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> No one saw him eating or sitting down. He was making baskets of palm leaves while he was standing. The monks said to <a href="/wiki/Pachomius" class="mw-redirect" title="Pachomius">Saint Pachomius</a>: "Cast out this man from here, for he is not human." A divine inspiration subsequently revealed Macarius' identity to him, and the monks rushed to receive his blessings.<sup id="cite_ref-abanoub_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abanoub-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Having demonstrated humility and taught a lesson to all, St. Macarius returned to his own monastery.
</p><p>At the age of 73 Macarius of Alexandria was exiled by <a href="/wiki/Valens" title="Valens">Emperor Valens</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Macarius of Egypt</a> to an island,<sup id="cite_ref-stjohns_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stjohns-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which they subsequently <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianized</a>.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Eastern Orthodox</a> tradition, Macarius of Alexandria died on 2 January 395. According to the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox">Coptic Orthodox</a> tradition, he died on 1 May 395. (<a href="/wiki/Pashons_6_(Coptic_Orthodox_liturgics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Pashons 6 (Coptic Orthodox liturgics)">6 Pashons</a>, 111 A.M.)
</p><p>In addition to a monastic rule and three brief apothegms, a homily "On the End of the Souls of the Righteous and of Sinners" is ascribed to him, although excellent Vienna manuscripts assign the latter to a monk named Alexander. <a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia" title="Palladius of Galatia">Palladius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sozomen" title="Sozomen">Sozomen</a> also mention a Macarius the Younger of <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a>, who lived in a cell for more than twenty three years to atone for a murder which he had committed.
</p>
