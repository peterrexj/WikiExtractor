<p><b>Robert of Knaresborough</b> (<b>St. Robert</b>, born <b>Robert Flower</b>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1160</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;24 September 1218)<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a British hermit who lived in a cave by the <a href="/wiki/River_Nidd" title="River Nidd">River Nidd</a>, <a href="/wiki/Knaresborough" title="Knaresborough">Knaresborough</a>, <a href="/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="West Riding of Yorkshire">North Yorkshire</a>. His feast day is on the 24th of September.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Knaresborough&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born Robert Flower (Floure or Fleur), the son of Touk Flower, mayor of <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> in York in 1160.<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Very early in his life he became a sub-deacon and a novice at the Cistercian <a href="/wiki/Newminster_Abbey" title="Newminster Abbey">Newminster Abbey</a>, but he stayed there only a few months.<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Seeking a life of solitude, he visited a knight-hermit who lived in a cave by the River Nidd at <a href="/wiki/Knaresborough" title="Knaresborough">Knaresborough</a>, hiding from Richard I. On the death of the king, the knight returned home to his family leaving Robert on his own. The cave had a small chapel dedicated to St. Giles built around it. He continued to live there for some years, until a wealthy widow, Juliana, offered him a cell at St. Hilda's Chapel in nearby Rudfarlington. There, he developed a reputation as a wise and holy man who cared for the poor. He stayed just a year before his hermitage was destroyed by bandits. Robert, dispossessed of his home, lived for a time under the church wall at Spofforth, and then he tried living with the monks at Hedley, near Tadcaster, but he found them far too easygoing for his style of life. By this time, the area had calmed down and he returned to Rudfarlington.<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Robert was well known for his charity to the poor and destitute. His favorite form of charity was to redeem men from prison. For a time, Robert prospered, having four servants and keeping cattle, but he was soon in trouble again, this time with <a href="/wiki/William_de_Stuteville" title="William de Stuteville">William de Stuteville</a>, the constable of Knaresborough castle, who accused him of harbouring thieves and outlaws.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Having his hermitage destroyed for the second time, this time by the forces of law and order under William de Stuteville, Robert returned to the cave at Knaresborough, where he stayed for the rest of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site,_Knaresborough,_Yorkshire.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site%2C_Knaresborough%2C_Yorkshire.jpg/250px-St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site%2C_Knaresborough%2C_Yorkshire.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="141" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site%2C_Knaresborough%2C_Yorkshire.jpg/375px-St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site%2C_Knaresborough%2C_Yorkshire.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site%2C_Knaresborough%2C_Yorkshire.jpg/500px-St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site%2C_Knaresborough%2C_Yorkshire.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="1080" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Robert_of_Knaresborough_cave_and_chapel_site,_Knaresborough,_Yorkshire.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Robert's grave and chapel</div></div></div>
<p>As a hermit, Knaresborough lived on a diet of <a href="/wiki/Herb" title="Herb">herbs</a>, <a href="/wiki/Root" title="Root">roots</a> and water.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Although living as a recluse, his piëty soon attracted followers and gifts from local benefactors, gifts that included land alongside the river. A number of stories of St. Robert exist both in Latin and early English verse. One concerns his complaining about the King's deer eating his crops. Sir William, making fun of the saint, invites Robert to catch the offending beasts. Robert manages not only to herd the deer into his barn as if they were a tame flock of sheep but also harnesses them to his plough and sets them to work.<sup id="cite_ref-pannal_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pannal-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Robert died on 24 September 1218.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Before his death, St Robert established an order of Trinitarian Friars at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Knaresborough_Priory&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Knaresborough Priory (page does not exist)">Knaresborough Priory</a>, but he warned them that, when his time came, the monks of <a href="/wiki/Fountains_Abbey" title="Fountains Abbey">Fountains Abbey</a> would try to carry his body away to their own establishment. He urged his followers to resist them, which they did, and so St. Robert was buried in his chapel cut from the steep rocky crags by the river, where, it was said, a medicinal oil flowed from his tomb; pilgrims came from near and far to be healed by it.<sup id="cite_ref-wells_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wells-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Robert.27s_Cave"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Robert's_Cave">St. Robert's Cave</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Knaresborough&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: St. Robert&#039;s Cave">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/St._Robert%27s_Cave" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Robert&#39;s Cave">St. Robert's Cave</a></div>
<p>Robert lived in various places in the vicinity of Knaresborough before taking up residence in a cave by the River Nidd (then known as St. Giles' Priory). It is said that <a href="/wiki/John_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="John of England">King John</a> visited him and <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Trinitarian friars</a> also venerated him.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Towards the end of his life, pilgrims flocked to see Robert to seek spiritual guidance and to be healed of physical ailments.<sup id="cite_ref-harrogate_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-harrogate-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His brother Walter, then Mayor of York, came and paid for some new buildings, including a <a href="/wiki/St_Robert%27s_Cave_and_Chapel_of_the_Holy_Cross" title="St Robert&#39;s Cave and Chapel of the Holy Cross">chapel dedicated to the Holy Cross</a>. The floorplan of this can still be seen alongside Robert's cave in Knaresborough.<sup id="cite_ref-pannal_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pannal-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Robert.27s_Well"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Robert's_Well">St. Robert's Well</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Knaresborough&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: St. Robert&#039;s Well">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG/220px-Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="164" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG/330px-Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG/440px-Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="956" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sign_at_St_Robert%27s_Cave.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sign at St Robert's Cave</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/w/index.php?title=Michael_Calvert_(antiquarian)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Michael Calvert (antiquarian) (page does not exist)">Michael Calvert</a>'s <i>History of Knaresborough</i> (1844) describes St Robert's Well as being near the York Road, about one mile from the town. Calvert also writes that prior to 1791 it had been an open well about two feet deep, but in 1791 bathing facilities were built at the well because of its value as a cold bath.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> An 1850s OS map marks a "Cold Bath" near the York Road, as described by Calvert, and this site was connected by a track to St Robert's cave and chapel 400&#160;m (1,300&#160;ft) to the south-west.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The Monkswell business park was built on the site of St Robert's well or Cold Bath, where a well shaft preserves the site of the spring that fed it. Visitors drop coins (as well as litter) through the metal grid that covers the wellshaft.<sup id="cite_ref-wells_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wells-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Knaresborough&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Robert's feast day is on 24 September although he was never officially canonised. Seven stained-glass panels of his life, originally from Dale Abbey, survive at St. Matthew's Church in <a href="/wiki/Morley,_Derbyshire" title="Morley, Derbyshire">Morley, Derbyshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Knaresborough&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cave, carved into a limestone cliff, can still be visited by the public. A small chapel and evidence of a small living area are all that remain.<sup id="cite_ref-harrogate_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-harrogate-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In North Yorkshire, Churches are dedicated to St. Robert at Knaresborough,<sup id="cite_ref-bognor_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bognor-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and at <a href="/wiki/St_Robert%27s_Church,_Pannal" title="St Robert&#39;s Church, Pannal">Pannal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Harrogate" title="Harrogate">Harrogate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-pannal_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pannal-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
