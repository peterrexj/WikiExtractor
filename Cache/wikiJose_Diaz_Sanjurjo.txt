<p>The <b>17 Thomasian Martyrs</b> were the 12 <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> priests, 1 <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> priest and 3 Dominican bishops who became administrators, professors, or students in the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" title="University of Santo Tomas">University of Santo Tomas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> they are <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> regarded them as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> and declared as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">blesseds</a> by several popes throughout the 20th and 21st century, All of them gave up their lives for their Christian faith, some in <a href="/wiki/Japan" title="Japan">Japan</a>, others in <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a>, and in the 20th century, in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>. <a href="/wiki/Lorenzo_Ruiz" title="Lorenzo Ruiz">Lorenzo Ruiz de Manila</a> was among the lay companions of the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomasian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Japan" title="Martyrs of Japan">Martyrs of Japan</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> their feast day is celebrated every year on November 6.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Martyrs_of_Japan">The Martyrs of Japan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The Martyrs of Japan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Conversion of some Japanese to the Christian faith took place between the years 1549–1640 in Japan through the efforts of missionary evangelizers. During this era, the country was ruled by the <a href="/wiki/Bakufu" class="mw-redirect" title="Bakufu">bakufu</a>, a military government headed by the <i><a href="/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgun" class="mw-redirect" title="Shōgun">shōguns</a></i> who governed the country in the name of the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_of_Japan" title="Emperor of Japan">Emperor</a>. The Emperor at this time had become a mere figurehead, secluded in his palace in <a href="/wiki/Kyoto" title="Kyoto">Kyoto</a>.
</p><p>Factors in the outbreak of severe repression of Christianity in Japan certainly included the fear of the Shoguns of all foreign influence. Contributory were the quarrels between Christian denominations, and imprudent acts of foreign navigators.
</p>
<h3><span id="Antonio_Gonz.C3.A1lez"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Antonio_González">Antonio González</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Antonio González">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">León, Spain</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Gonz%C3%A1lez_(martyr)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antonio González (martyr)">Antonio Gonzalez</a> entered the Dominican Order at the age of 16. His favorite saint was <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Verona" title="Peter of Verona">Peter of Verona</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a> of the Dominican order, thus his religious enthusiasm gave rise to his living desire for martyrdom. After completing his studies, he was ordained and sent to <a href="/wiki/Piedrahita" class="mw-redirect" title="Piedrahita">Piedrahita</a> as a theology professor.<sup id="cite_ref-orden_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  When an invitation was sent to their convent asking for volunteer missionaries for the Far East, González was among those who eagerly volunteered. His target destination was Japan, but he had to prepare for this mission in the Philippines. He arrived in Manila in May 1632. He became professor and acting rector of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" title="University of Santo Tomas">University of Santo Tomas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-orden_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1636, González was finally able to fulfill his dream of going to Japan. After a year of missionary activity, he was arrested, proudly wearing his habit to signify that he went to Japan for the sake of the Gospel. After suffering tremendous torture, he was found dead in his cell on the morning of September 24, 1637. González was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on October 18, 1987, together with his companion martyrs.
</p>
<h3><span id="Domingo_Ib.C3.A1.C3.B1ez_de_Erquicia"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Domingo_Ibáñez_de_Erquicia">Domingo Ibáñez de Erquicia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Domingo Ibáñez de Erquicia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Domingo_Ib%C3%A1%C3%B1ez_de_Erquicia" title="Domingo Ibáñez de Erquicia">Domingo Ibáñez de Erquicia</a></div>
<p>Born in February, 1589 in <a href="/wiki/Errezil" title="Errezil">Regil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Guipuzcoa" class="mw-redirect" title="Guipuzcoa">Guipuzcoa</a>, Spain, <a href="/wiki/Domingo_Ib%C3%A1%C3%B1ez_de_Erquicia" title="Domingo Ibáñez de Erquicia">Domingo Ibáñez de Erquicia</a> entered the Dominican Priory of San Thelmo at the age of 16. Realizing the need for missionaries in the Far East, he joined the Dominicans who went to the Philippines and arrived in Manila in the year 1611 and became professor of Theology at the University of Santo Tomás. Ten years later, he was sent to Japan. Constantly faced with danger, he spent his decade of mission in Japan faithfully preaching the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> and administering the sacraments, until he was finally caught by the Japanese authorities and killed through the "gallows and hole" torture. After thirty hours of continuous torture, he finally expired on August 14, 1633.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lucas_del_Espiritu_Santos">Lucas del Espiritu Santos</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Lucas del Espiritu Santos">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Zamora,_Spain" title="Zamora, Spain">Zamora</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> on October 18, 1594, he entered the Dominican Order at the age of 16. In the year 1617, he answered the missionary calling to evangelize in the Far East. He became Lecturer of Arts at the University of Santo Tomás. Later on he was sent to Japan and arrived there in June 1623. For ten years, he engaged in underground apostolate. On September 8, 1633, he was arrested in <a href="/wiki/Osaka" title="Osaka">Osaka</a>, and a month later, on October 18, he was subjected to the "gallows and hole" torture. He died on the following day.
</p>
<h3><span id="Tom.C3.A1s_Hioji_de_San_Jacinto"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Tomás_Hioji_de_San_Jacinto">Tomás Hioji de San Jacinto</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Tomás Hioji de San Jacinto">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Born of <a href="/wiki/Kirishitan" title="Kirishitan">Christian Japanese</a> parents in 1590, in <a href="/wiki/Ky%C5%ABsh%C5%AB" class="mw-redirect" title="Kyūshū">Kyūshū</a>, Japan, he witnessed how his parents were martyred for their Christian faith. He went to the Philippines and sought admission to the Dominican Order. He studied philosophy at the University of Santo Tomás. He went back to Japan on November 10, 1629.  Being Japanese, Thomas was able to move about with some freedom. While doing his missionary endeavors, he chronicled the martyrdom of his fellow Dominicans. The Japanese authorities eventually arrested him. He was subjected to the "gallows and hole" torture and died on November 15, 1634.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Guillaume_Courtet">Guillaume Courtet</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Guillaume Courtet">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Guillaume_Courtet" title="Guillaume Courtet">Guillaume Courtet</a></div>
<p>Guillaume Courtet was a French <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> of noble origins born in 1590. He joined the Order at the age of 17. He became prior of the Community in <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. His childhood dream to be a missionary was fulfilled when he set sail for the Philippines in 1634. He became professor of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at the University of Santo Tomás. Because of his holiness and zeal for the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>, in 1636 he was sent to be a missionary in Japan. A year later, he was arrested. In his trial, he affirmed that only <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> truth will save mankind. For this, he was condemned to death. He died in September 1637 through the "gallows and hole" torture.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Martyrs_of_Vietnam">The Martyrs of Vietnam</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: The Martyrs of Vietnam">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Vietnamese_Martyrs" title="Vietnamese Martyrs">Vietnamese Martyrs</a></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a> was first introduced to Christianity in the year 1627. The Dominicans set foot on the country in 1676. Among the milestones of the Dominican missions were the training of <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechists</a> and the establishment of charitable institutions.  The predominant religions during the era of martyrs were <a href="/wiki/Taoism" title="Taoism">Taoism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Buddhism" title="Buddhism">Buddhism</a>.
</p><p>The government of <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a> was similar to <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>.  It was an empire ruled by dynasties that succeeded each other. The Emperor was regarded as an absolute monarch.  In 1711, Emperor An Vuong issued the first Edict of Persecution of Christians.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Domingo_Henares">Domingo Henares</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Domingo Henares">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The 30-year-old <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a>-born Domingo Henares arrived in Manila on July 9, 1796. He completed his studies in the University of Santo Tomás and there became a professor of <a href="/wiki/Humanities" title="Humanities">Humanities</a>. He went to Vietnam in 1790 where eventually he became bishop. His knowledge of medicine, astronomy, and the sciences was greatly appreciated by the Vietnamese, even respected by the <a href="/wiki/Mandarin_(bureaucrat)" title="Mandarin (bureaucrat)">Mandarins</a>. Still, the fact that he was <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> made him subject to persecution. On June 9, 1838, he was arrested and a month later, on July 25, 1838, he was beheaded.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Vicente_Liem_de_la_Paz">Vicente Liem de la Paz</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Vicente Liem de la Paz">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Vicente_Liem_de_la_Paz" title="Vicente Liem de la Paz">Vicente Liem de la Paz</a> was a native <a href="/wiki/Vietnamese_people" title="Vietnamese people">Vietnamese</a> born in 1731.  This brilliant student was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> to study at the <a href="/wiki/Colegio_de_San_Juan_de_Letran" title="Colegio de San Juan de Letran">Colegio de San Juan de Letran</a>. In 1753, he entered the Dominican order and studied in the University of Santo Tomás where he was later ordained a priest. After his petition to serve his people was approved, he went back to Vietnam as a missionary, working under Jacinto Castañeda, until he was arrested, tortured, and beheaded on November 7, 1773.
</p>
<h3><span id="Jose_Mar.C3.ADa_D.C3.ADaz_Sanjurjo"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Jose_María_Díaz_Sanjurjo">Jose María Díaz Sanjurjo</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Jose María Díaz Sanjurjo">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>José María Díaz Sanjurjo was born in <a href="/wiki/Lugo" title="Lugo">Lugo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> on August 25, 1818. He secretly entered the Dominican Priory in <a href="/wiki/Oca%C3%B1a,_Spain" title="Ocaña, Spain">Ocaña</a>, and in 1842, he received the Dominican habit. He was a famous <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> scholar, theologian, and legal expert. He arrived in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a> on September 14, 1844. He completed his studies while teaching at the University of Santo Tomás. After a year, he left for the Vietnamese missions. In March 1849, he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. Even as he was raised to the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_polity" title="Episcopal polity">episcopal</a> rank, he remained a lowly servant of the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>. According to him, "Here, the dignities mean more work. I don't have any means of transportation at all, and although I did not vow to go barefoot, I do and sometimes with mud up to my knees" In 1856, he was arrested and was beheaded a year later.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pedro_Almato">Pedro Almato</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Pedro Almato">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pedro Almato was born in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> on <a href="/wiki/All_Saints%27_Day" title="All Saints&#39; Day">All Saints' Day</a>, 1830.  He went to <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a>, studied in the University of Santo Tomás, and was ordained in 1853. Learning of the persecutions in <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a>, he obtained permission from his superiors to go on mission in the said country. In October 1861, after several years of missionary work, Almato was captured and was beheaded on his birthday.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jeronimo_Hermosilla">Jeronimo Hermosilla</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Jeronimo Hermosilla">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Born in the year 1800 to a poor family in Santo Domingo de la Calzada in Spain, Jeronimo Hermosilla entered the Diocesan Seminary of Valencia when he was fifteen years of age, but later sought admission to the Order of Preachers after being captivated by his Dominican professors. In 1824, he went to Manila and completed his theological studies at the University of Santo Tomas. He was ordained priest in 1828.  A year later he was sent as a missionary to Tonkin. There he learned the native language and taught catechism in collaboration with local catechists and other missionary Dominicans. In 1841 he was appointed bishop, and despite the persecution, continued his ministry hiding in safe places, until he was finally captured and beheaded in 1861.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Martyrs_of_the_Religious_Persecution_in_Spain">The Martyrs of the Religious Persecution in Spain</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: The Martyrs of the Religious Persecution in Spain">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Last October 28, 2007, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> beatified 498 <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_the_Spanish_Civil_War" title="Martyrs of the Spanish Civil War">martyrs of the Spanish Civil War</a>, in a Holy Mass presided over by <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saraiva_Martins" title="José Saraiva Martins">Cardinal José Saraiva Martins</a>, prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints. This was the largest mass beatification ever in the history of the Catholic Church.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_persecution_in_Spain_during_the_Spanish_Civil_War">Religious persecution in Spain during the Spanish Civil War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Religious persecution in Spain during the Spanish Civil War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the fall of the monarchy, the <a href="/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic" title="Second Spanish Republic">Second Spanish Republic</a> was established. The Republican government and its supporters, a combined force of communists, socialists and anarchists, was particularly antagonistic towards the nobility, the land owners, and the Catholic Church. In 1936, a <a href="/wiki/Coup_d%27etat" class="mw-redirect" title="Coup d&#39;etat">coup d'etat</a> staged by rebel forces (Nacionalistas) attempted to overthrow the communist republicans, leading to a three-year political unrest characterized by extreme brutality and violence resulting to countless deaths among the government and rebel forces, as well as ordinary citizens caught in the war.
</p><p>Since the Catholic Church has been among those considered as enemies of the republicans, thousands of priests and religious perished in organized persecutions. Though the civil war began in 1936, communist forces had started torturing and executing priests and religious as early as 1933.
</p><p>Seventy-four Dominicans, most of them friars, were among those beatified last October 28<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Avoid_weasel_words" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:Avoid weasel words"><span title="The material near this tag possibly uses too vague attribution or weasel words. (December 2014)">which?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>. Of these, eight stayed for some time in the Philippines, six of them, Thomasians.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Buenaventura_Garc.C3.ADa_Paredes"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Buenaventura_García_Paredes">Buenaventura García Paredes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Buenaventura García Paredes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Buenaventura_Garc%C3%ADa_de_Paredes" title="Buenaventura García de Paredes">Buenaventura García de Paredes</a></div>
<p>Buenaventura Paredes was the 78th successor of Dominic as Master of the Order of Preachers. Born to a pious family in Castañedo de Valdes, Luarca, Spain on April 19, 1866, Paredes eventually decided to enter the Order of Preachers and received the Dominican habit on August 30, 1833. True to the Dominican tradition of scholarship, he studied theology, civil law, and philosophy and letters prior to his ordination to the priesthood on July 25, 1891. After his ordination, he pursued further studies with which he earned his doctorate in philosophy and letters and in jurisprudence. He then traveled to the Philippines as a missionary assigned to the Dominican Province of Our Lady of the Most Holy Rosary. While in Manila, he obtained the degree of lector in theology, which was a requirement for teaching in the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" title="University of Santo Tomas">University of Santo Tomas</a>. Paredes was a professor of political and administrative law at the UST Faculty of Civil Law which was then in <a href="/wiki/Intramuros" title="Intramuros">Intramuros</a>. He was also director of the UST published Catholic newspaper "Libertas".
</p><p>In 1901, he returned to Spain where he assumed several positions of responsibility. He came back to Manila when in 1910, he was elected as the Prior Provincial of the Holy Rosary Province, a position he held for seven years. During his term as Provincial, he became among those responsible for the procurement of a land in Sulucan Hills on which the present UST Campus now stands. After his term ended, he went back to Spain to serve as the superior of a Dominican convent in Madrid. In 1926, despite his plea to be relieved of such great responsibility, Paredes was elected by the General Chapter as Master General of the Order. Due to some serious problems in the Order which weakened his health, Paredes resigned his office in 1929. He then retired to the convent in Ocaña.
</p><p>When the civil war broke out in July 1936, Paredes was in Madrid. A month earlier, realizing that war was imminent, he expressed his desire to travel back to the Philippines. However, even after he was permitted by his superiors in Rome, Paredes could not leave Spain because the government would not issue him a passport. Providentially, Paredes had left his Madrid convent the night before it was attacked by armed men on July 19, 1936. After this event, Paredes, along with other Dominicans, had to be sheltered by a benefactor, Don Pedro Errazquin, himself murdered after a chalice belonging to Paredes was found in his house. Being constantly under police surveillance, Paredes had to stay in a boarding house, where he continued to perform his priestly duties: hearing confessions, praying the office, and celebrating the Eucharist. On August 11, he was arrested by armed men, and bravely he declared himself a priest and a religious. He was taken to a place of torture, and in the morning of the following day, he was shot in Valdesenderín del Encinar. His rosary and his breviary were found near his cadaver.
</p><p>In his honor, P. Paredes street, a street close to UST was named after him.
</p>
<h3><span id="Jesus_Villaverde_Andr.C3.A9s"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Jesus_Villaverde_Andrés">Jesus Villaverde Andrés</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Jesus Villaverde Andrés">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Jesus Villaverde Andres was born in San Miguel de Dueñas, <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">León, Spain</a>, on December 4, 1877. In 1894, he entered the Dominican Order and after completing his theological studies, he was ordained to the priesthood on June 26, 1903.  He was then sent to the Philippines, and around the years 1905-1910, he taught at the Colegio de San Juan de Letran. He returned to Spain and was assigned to the Convent of Valencia. In 1916, he went back to the Philippines to teach in the University of Santo Tomas where he held a professorial chair in theology after obtaining his Doctorate in Sacred Theology from the UST Faculty of Sacred Theology in 1919. He became professor of dogmatic theology and canon law in the same faculty. He held several positions of responsibility in UST. He was secretary general from 1919 to 1921 and treasurer from 1929 to 1932. During the years 1921-1924, Villaverde was in the United States, serving as prior of the Dominican Community in Rosaryville, New Orleans. When he returned to Manila, he became the rector of the Colegio de San Juan de Letrán from 1924 to 1927. He then became dean of the UST Faculty of Sacred Theology from 1932 to 1934.  Among the new Thomasian blesseds, it was only Jesús Villaverde Andrés who was able to see UST in its present location, having resided at the present Priory of St. Thomas Aquinas. Villaverde occupied one of the rooms in the UST Fathers' Residence.
</p><p>Villaverde went back to Spain to serve as prior of the Santo Tomás Convent in Avila and later on was assigned to Madrid. While in Madrid, his convent was attacked by the communists on July 1936. Villaverde had to hide in his mother's house in Cuesta de los Descargos. Later on, his brother, Carlos, a military man, took custody of him for three months. In his brother's house he did nothing but pray and console his family. On October 15, he was arrested by the milicianos. Carlos' children tried to save Villaverde by telling the arresting officers that there was no priest in their house, but when the milicianos threatened to take Carlos instead, Villaverde voluntarily showed up and handled himself over to the arresting officers. He was brought to the place of torture and later on executed; the manner of which still remains a mystery.
</p><p>Throughout his life as a Dominican, Villaverde faithfully carried out all his duties as a priest and a religious. Often criticized for his being strict and somewhat temperamental, unknown to many, Villaverde was quietly suffering the pains brought on him by his liver disease. Witnesses of his priestly life affirm that he was an excellent preacher, and his brilliance as a theology earned him the respect of the Holy See.
</p>
<h3><span id="Pedro_Ib.C3.A1.C3.B1ez_Alonzo.2C_Manuel_Moreno_Martines.2C_Maximino_Fendandez_Mari.C3.B1as_and_Jos.C3.A9_Mar.C3.ADa_L.C3.B3pez_Carillo"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Pedro_Ibáñez_Alonzo,_Manuel_Moreno_Martines,_Maximino_Fendandez_Mariñas_and_José_María_López_Carillo">Pedro Ibáñez Alonzo, Manuel Moreno Martines, Maximino Fendandez Mariñas and José María López Carillo</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=17" title="Edit section: Pedro Ibáñez Alonzo, Manuel Moreno Martines, Maximino Fendandez Mariñas and José María López Carillo">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>These four martyrs of the Religious Persecution in Spain sailed to the Philippines as young Dominican missionaries assigned to the Holy Rosary Province. They stayed for sometime in the convent of Santo Domingo in Intramuros while taking up theological studies in the University of Santo Tomás. They were ordained as priests in Santo Domingo Church, and after several assignments both in and out of the Philippines, they went back to Spain and were assigned to the Holy Rosary Convent in Madrid. After their convent was attacked by the Republicans, they went into hiding until they were found and arrested by the milicianos who tortured and eventually executed them. Alonzo was a missionary in China before going back to Spain. He and Carillo tried to escape the persecution but the milicianos caught and executed them. Martines, who studied moral theology in UST, was executed after being caught by milicianos in a train station. Eleven gunshot wounds were found on the body of Mariñas.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jose_Maria_de_Manila">Jose Maria de Manila</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomasian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=18" title="Edit section: Jose Maria de Manila">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_of_Manila" title="José María of Manila">José María of Manila</a></div>
<p>Born on September 5, 1880, in the old city of Manila, Eugenio Saz-Orozco took the name Jose Maria when he entered the Order of the Friars Minor Capuchin in Spain. His father was the last mayor of Manila during the last years of the Spanish government that ended in 1898.
</p><p>He finished his secondary schooling in 1895 at the University of Santo Tomas while remaining “alumno interno” at San Juan de Letran. It was his school records in UST that identified him as “de Manila” thus indicating he was born in the City. He went to Spain in 1896 for the purpose of making the necessary higher studies in preparation for a career. It was in Spain that he decided to join the Capuchin Order. He and was ordained to the priesthood on November 30, 1910.
</p><p>He had longed to go back as a missionary to the Philippines, his country of birth, but on August 17, 1936, during the religious persecution in Spain. His last words before he died were: “Long live Christ the King!”
</p>
