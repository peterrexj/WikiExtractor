<p><b>Pope Marcellus II</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Marcello II</i>; 6 May 1501&#160;–&#160;1 May 1555), born <b>Marcello Cervini degli Spannocchi</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papalini</a> Catholic prelate who served as head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 9 April 1555<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> until his death 22 days later.
</p><p>He succeeded <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_III" title="Pope Julius III">Pope Julius III</a>. Before his accession as pope he had been <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal-Priest</a> of <i><a href="/wiki/Santa_Croce_in_Gerusalemme" title="Santa Croce in Gerusalemme">Santa Croce in Gerusalemme</a></i>. He is the most recent pope to choose to retain his birth name as his regnal name upon his accession, and the most recent pope to date with the regnal name "Marcellus".
</p><p>Cervini was the maternal uncle of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine" title="Robert Bellarmine">Robert Bellarmine</a>. His father, Ricardo Cervini, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a> were personal friends. Cervini served in the household of Cardinal Alessandro Farnese. When Farnese became pope, Cervini served as his secretary and was employed on a number of diplomatic missions. On 10 April, 1555, he was elected to succeed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_III" title="Pope Julius III">Pope Julius III</a>. He died of a stroke twenty-two days later.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Marcellus_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A native of <a href="/wiki/Montefano" title="Montefano">Montefano</a>, a small village near Macerata and Loreto<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he was the son of Ricardo Cervini who was the Apostolic Treasurer in <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The family originated in Tuscany, in the town of Montepulciano, which had once been subject to Siena, but later was under the control of Florence. Marcello had two half-brothers, Alexander and Romulus.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  One of his sisters, Cinzia Cervini, married Vincenzo Bellarmino, and was the mother of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine" title="Robert Bellarmine">Robert Bellarmine</a>.
</p><p>Marcello was educated locally, and at Siena and Florence,  where he became proficient in writing Latin, Greek, and Italian. He also received instruction in jurisprudence, philosophy, and mathematics.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His father had an interest in astrology and upon discovering that his son's horoscope presaged high ecclesiastical honours, Riccardo set the young Cervini on a path to the priesthood.<sup id="cite_ref-Priere_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Priere-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Marcellus_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his period of study at Siena, Cervini traveled to Rome in the company of the delegation sent by Florence to congratulate the new Pope on his election. His father and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a> were personal friends, and Marcello was made <i>Scrittore Apostolico</i>. He was set to work on astronomical and calendar studies, a project which was intended to bring the year back into synchronization with the seasons. In 1527, he fled home after the <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(1527)" title="Sack of Rome (1527)">Sack of Rome</a>, but eventually returned and was taken into the household of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Cardinal Alessandro Farnese <i>senior</i></a>. Cervini was ordained a priest in 1535.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinalate">Cardinalate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Marcellus_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cardinalate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1534, after Farnese had become pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a>, Cervini was appointed a papal secretary (1534–49) and served as a close advisor to the pope's nephew Alessandro Farnese. He was made a papal <a href="/wiki/Protonotary_apostolic" title="Protonotary apostolic"> protonotary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He travelled in the suite of the Pope during the papal visit to Nice, where Paul III was promoting a truce between <a href="/wiki/Francis_I_of_France" title="Francis I of France">Francis I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V</a>. He then accompanied the young Cardinal Farnese on a journey to Spain, France and the Spanish Netherlands to help implement the terms of the truce. Paul III later appointed him <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Nicastro" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Nicastro">Bishop of Nicastro</a> in 1539. Cervini was not, however, consecrated bishop until the day he himself was elected pope.  On 19 December 1539, while Cervini was still on the embassy to the Netherlands, Paul III created him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Priest">Cardinal-Priest</a> of <i><a href="/wiki/Santa_Croce_in_Gerusalemme" title="Santa Croce in Gerusalemme">Santa Croce in Gerusalemme</a></i>.
</p><p>When, almost immediately afterwards, Cardinal Farnese was recalled to Rome, Cervini stayed on in Spain as nuncio. Over the course of the next decade Cervini also became the apostolic administrator of the dioceses of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Reggio_Calabria-Bova" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Reggio Calabria-Bova">Reggio</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Gubbio" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Gubbio"> Gubbio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His house in Rome became a center of Renaissance culture, and he himself corresponded with most of the leading humanists.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a> he was elected one of the council's three presidents,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> along with fellow cardinals <a href="/wiki/Reginald_Pole" title="Reginald Pole">Reginald Pole</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_III" title="Pope Julius III">Giovanni Maria Ciocchi del Monte</a> (the future Pope Julius III). He continued to serve in that role throughout the remainder of Paul III's papacy, after which he was replaced to placate the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V</a> (1519–56). He was credited with defending not only orthodoxy and Church discipline, but also the universal claims of the Papacy in spiritual and temporal affairs, and with such vigor that the Emperor was affronted. In 1548 (or 1550) Cervini was placed in charge of the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Library" title="Vatican Library">Vatican Library</a>, with the title of <i>Protettore della Biblioteca Apostolica</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Apostolic Brief of his appointment, however, came from the new pope, Julius III, on 24 May 1550, and in it he was named not Vatican Librarian, but <i>Bibliothecarius Sanctae Romanae Ecclesiae</i> because he was the first cardinal to be placed in charge of the library.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> During his administration, he employed the services of Marcello and Sirleto, as well as <a href="/wiki/Onofrio_Panvinio" title="Onofrio Panvinio">Onofrio Panvinio</a> (who was especially consulted in matters of Christian archaeology). He added more than 500 codices to the holdings of the Library, including 143 Greek codices, as his own entry book (which still survives as <i>Vaticanus Latinus</i> 3963) testifies.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg/200px-Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="244" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg/300px-Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f7/Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg 2x" data-file-width="392" data-file-height="478" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_marcellus_II.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Coat of Arms of Pope Marcellus II</div></div></div>
<p>In the conclave of 1549–50 held to elect a successor to Paul III,  fifty-one cardinals, including Marcello Cervini, participated at the opening on 3 December 1549. The initial candidates included Cardinals <a href="/wiki/Reginald_Pole" title="Reginald Pole">Pole</a>, <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Sfondrati" title="Francesco Sfondrati">Sfondrati</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rodolfo_Pio_da_Carpi" title="Rodolfo Pio da Carpi">Carpi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Ridolfi" title="Niccolò Ridolfi">Ridolfi</a> (who died on the night of 31 January). Pole, the favorite of the Emperor Charles V, came within two votes of being elected in the first scrutinies, but he failed to attract any additional votes. <a href="/wiki/Juan_%C3%81lvarez_de_Toledo" title="Juan Álvarez de Toledo">Juan Álvarez de Toledo</a>, Bishop of Burgos, another Imperial favorite, was proposed, and he too failed, because of strong opposition from the faction of Cardinal Alessandro Farnese, nephew of the late Pope Paul III and from the French.
</p><p>On 12 December, five more French voters arrived, and though they could not advance the candidacy of their favorite, <a href="/wiki/Ippolito_II_d%27Este" title="Ippolito II d&#39;Este">Ippolito d'Este</a>, they did have Cardinal Cervini on their list of possible candidates. Farnese and his faction were also favorably disposed to him. Unfortunately, the Imperial faction was not.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Worst of all, on 22 December, Cardinal Cervini left the Conclave, suffering from a <a href="/wiki/Quartan_fever" title="Quartan fever">quartan fever</a>. Finally, on 7 February 1550, the cardinals chose Giovanni Maria Ciocchi del Monte, who took the name Julius III.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_election">Papal election</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Marcellus_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Papal election">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/April_1555_papal_conclave" title="April 1555 papal conclave">April 1555 papal conclave</a></div>
<p>The first conclave of 1555, following the death of <a href="/wiki/Julius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Julius III">Julius III</a> (1550–55), involved a struggle between French interests in Italy (which had been favored by Julius III) and Imperial interests, which were intent on Church reform through a Church council, but with the Emperor controlling the outcome.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> On 9 April 1555, on the evening of the fourth day of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave" title="Papal conclave">papal conclave</a>, Cervini was "adored" as pope, despite efforts by cardinals loyal to Emperor Charles V to block his election.<sup id="cite_ref-Priere_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Priere-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Next morning, a formal vote was taken in the Capella Paolina, in which all of the votes cast were for Cardinal Cervini except his own, which he cast for the dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals, Giampietro Carafa.
</p><p>The new pope chose to retain his birth name, the most recent pope to do so, reigning as Marcellus II. He was both consecrated as a bishop and crowned pope on the next day in a ceremony that was subdued on account of it falling during the Lenten season.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Marcellus_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_(Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani,_Vatican).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_%28Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani%2C_Vatican%29.jpg/220px-Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_%28Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani%2C_Vatican%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="284" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_%28Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani%2C_Vatican%29.jpg/330px-Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_%28Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani%2C_Vatican%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_%28Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani%2C_Vatican%29.jpg/440px-Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_%28Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani%2C_Vatican%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3561" data-file-height="4596" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Portrait_of_Pope_Marcellus_II_Cervini_(Vatican_Museums_-_Musei_Vaticani,_Vatican).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Marcellus II depicted in an anonymous 16th century painting, located in the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Museums" title="Vatican Museums">Vatican Museums</a></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg/200px-Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg/300px-Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg/400px-Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tomb_of_Marcellus_II.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/List_of_papal_tombs" class="mw-redirect" title="List of papal tombs">tomb of Pope Marcellus II</a> in the grottoes of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a></div></div></div>
<p>Though Marcellus II desired to reform many of the inner workings of the Church, his feeble constitution succumbed to the fatigues of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave" title="Papal conclave">conclave</a>, the exhausting ceremonies connected with his accession, the anxieties arising from his high office, and overexertion in his performance of the pontifical functions of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week" title="Holy Week">Holy Week</a> and <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> He quickly fell ill.
</p><p>He was bled, and appeared to begin to recover. In an audience he gave to the cardinals, who wanted him to sign the Electoral Capitulations from the conclave and to guarantee that he would make no more cardinals than those agreements allowed, he refused to sign, stating that he would show his intent by deeds not words. In his first audience with the ambassadors of France and Spain, he warned the ambassadors that their monarchs should keep the peace that had been agreed upon, and that if they did not, not only would they be sent nuncios and legates, but that the pope himself would come and admonish them. He wrote letters to Emperor Charles V, to Queen <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Mary I of England</a>, and to Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Reginald_Pole" title="Reginald Pole">Reginald Pole</a> (in which he confirmed Pole as legate in England).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> When the Spanish ambassador asked for pardon for having killed a man, Marcellus replied that he did not want to start his reign with such auspices as absolution from homicide, and ordered the appropriate tribunals to observe the law.
</p><p>He did not want his relatives descending on Rome, nor did he want them to be enriched beyond the station of a member of the nobility, and he did not allow his two nephews, Riccardo and Herennius (sons of his half-brother Alexander), who lived in Rome under his care, to have formal visits. He instituted immediate economies in the expenditure of the Holy See. On 28 April, he was able to receive the Duke of Urbino in audience, and on 29 April, the Duke of Ferrara. He also gave audience to four cardinals, Farnese, D'Este, Louis de Guise and Ascanio Sforza, the leaders of the French faction in the recent conclave. That night he had difficulty sleeping. On the morning of the 30th he suffered a stroke (<i>hora XII apoplexi correptus</i>) and slipped into a coma. That night he died, on the 22nd day after his election.<sup id="cite_ref-Priere_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Priere-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Marcellus_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Pierluigi_da_Palestrina" title="Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina">Palestrina</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Missa_Papae_Marcelli" title="Missa Papae Marcelli">Missa Papae Marcelli</a></i> (dating from 1565 or before<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>), one of the glories of polyphonic sacred choral music, is traditionally believed to have been composed in his memory, ca. 1562.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Having reigned for just 22 calendar days, Pope Marcellus II ranks sixth on the <a href="/wiki/List_of_10_shortest-reigning_popes" class="mw-redirect" title="List of 10 shortest-reigning popes">list of 10 shortest-reigning popes</a>. His successor was Giampietro Carafa, Dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals, who reigned as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_IV" title="Pope Paul IV">Pope Paul IV</a> (1555–59).
</p>
