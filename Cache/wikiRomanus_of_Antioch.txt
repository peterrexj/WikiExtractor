<p><b>Romanus of Caesarea</b> (also known as <b>Romanus of Antioch</b>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. A deacon of Caesarea, he was martyred at Antioch.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 303 or 304, at the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> called Romanus, served in Caesarea in <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>. He was living in <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> where in the midst of the persecutions, he encouraged the Christians to stand firm.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During a pagan festival, he upbraided the participants for worshiping idols. Taken prisoner, he was condemned to death by fire, and was bound to the stake. When rain extinguished the flames,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Romanus was brought before <a href="/wiki/Galerius" title="Galerius">Emperor Galerius</a> who was then in Antioch. At the emperor's command Romanus' <a href="/wiki/Tongue" title="Tongue">tongue</a> was cut out. Tortured in various ways in prison he was finally <a href="/wiki/Strangulation" class="mw-redirect" title="Strangulation">strangled</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> speaks of his martyrdom in <i>De martyribus Palaestinae</i>. <a href="/wiki/Prudentius" title="Prudentius">Prudentius</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> relates other details and gives Romanus a companion in martyrdom, a Christian by name <a href="/wiki/Barulas" class="mw-redirect" title="Barulas">Barulas</a>. On this account several historians, among them <a href="/wiki/Baronius" class="mw-redirect" title="Baronius">Baronius</a>, consider that there were two martyrs named Romanus at Antioch, though more likely there was but the one whom Eusebius mentions. Prudentius has introduced legendary features into his account, and his connection of the martyrdom of Barulas with that of Romanus is probably arbitrary.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Saint Romanus is observed on 18 November.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The church of San Román in <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a> is dedicated to Romanus. Prudentius wrote a 1140 line hymn to Romanus, the <i>Romane Christi fortis</i>, the tenth hymn in his <i>Peristephanon</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Barulas">Barulas</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romanus_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Barulas">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><b>Barulas</b> (died 303) was a seven year old boy who was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> along with Romanus of Caesarea by <a href="/wiki/Galerius" title="Galerius">Emperor Galerius</a> by being whipped and beheaded for their <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> beliefs.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Barulas, like <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quiricus_and_Saint_Julietta" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Quiricus and Saint Julietta">Quiricus</a>, is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Child_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Child saint">child-martyr</a>. His feast day is on November 18.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Barulas was a child in the crowd of people who witnessed the torture of Romanus. Romanus told the <a href="/wiki/Eparchy#Imperial_Roman_administration" title="Eparchy">Eparch</a> Asclypiades that the boy was wiser than he was, because he knew the true God while the Eparch did not.
</p><p>Asclypiades asked the boy what gods he worshiped, and he replied that he worshiped <a href="/wiki/Christ_(title)" title="Christ (title)">Christ</a>. “Your gods are demons,” the child stated, “and they have not created anything.”
</p><p>With these and other words, the young child put the idolaters to shame. Seeing that he could not convince Barulas to worship the false gods, he had the boy tortured. His mother stood by, encouraging him to remain faithful to his Saviour. After the child was executed, his mother took his body and buried it, rejoicing because he had shed his blood for Christ.
</p>
