<p><b>Pancras</b> or <b>Pancratius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Greek-language text"><span lang="el">Παγκράτιος</span></span>, <i>Pankratios</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Pancrazio</i>) is an Italian saint associated with <a href="/wiki/Taormina" title="Taormina">Taormina</a> and venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a>. His surviving <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> is purely legendary. He is, however, recorded in some early <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Evidence">Evidence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pancras_of_Taormina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Evidence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pancras is commemorated on 8 July and 3 April in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> (5th century). He is also listed for 8 July in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Neapolitan_marble_calendar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Neapolitan marble calendar (page does not exist)">Neapolitan marble calendar</a> (9th century, but dependent on lost early material). In 591, a church at <a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a> was dedicated to Saints Stephen, Pankratios and Euplus, which probably refers to Pancras of Taormina and <a href="/wiki/Euplius_of_Catania" title="Euplius of Catania">Euplius of Catania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pancras_of_Taormina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the legendary <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Life_of_Saint_Pankratios_of_Taormina&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Life of Saint Pankratios of Taormina (page does not exist)">Life of Saint Pankratios of Taormina</a></i>, he was born in <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a> (the modern <a href="/wiki/Adana" title="Adana">Adana</a>). He travelled to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> with his parents during the earthly ministry of Jesus; later the entire family was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> in Antioch. Pancras withdrew to a cave in <a href="/wiki/Pontus_(region)" title="Pontus (region)">Pontus</a> where he was discovered by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and was sent to <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> in the year 40 to be the first Bishop of Tauromenium (the modern <a href="/wiki/Taormina" title="Taormina">Taormina</a>). There he met his death by <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoning</a> at the hands of <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> opponents of the new religion.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pancras_of_Taormina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Hieromartyr" title="Hieromartyr">Hieromartyr</a>.
</p><p>In the Catholic Church his <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> is concentrated on the island of Sicily, where the veneration of saints from the eastern Mediterranean was particularly encouraged during the period of <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> rule. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Taormina and <a href="/wiki/Canicatt%C3%AC" title="Canicattì">Canicattì</a>. His feast day was entered into the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> as 3 April; later this was amended to 8 July. More often he is celebrated on 9 July, the traditional day of his martyrdom. The largest portion of his relics are preserved at Rome.
</p><p>The Eastern Orthodox Church venerates him on 9 July (22 July, N.S.). He is also, together with martyrs Marcellus and Philagrus, commemorated on 9 February. The Greek calendar also commemorates, on 7 June, the holy women Aesia and Susanna, disciples of Pancras and martyred with him. A portion of his relics are kept on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>.
</p><p>The Armenian Apostolic Church venerates him on the Thursday following the first Sunday of Advent. He is known in Armenian as Bagarat (Բագարատ). The Armenian synaxarion (Յայսմաւուրք) gives an extensive account of Pancras' acts, including his evangelization of all the cities and villages of Sicily.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Saint Pancras of Taormina should not be confused with Saint <a href="/wiki/Pancras_of_Rome" title="Pancras of Rome">Pancras of Rome</a>, a young man who was martyred by being beheaded around the year 304.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Depiction_in_art">Depiction in art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pancras_of_Taormina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Depiction in art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In iconography, St Pancras is depicted as an old man with yellowing grey hair, vested as a bishop, holding a cross in his right hand, and a Gospel book in his left. The cross commemorates a miracle attributed to St Pancras whereby he saved the city of Taormina from destruction by the pagan commander Aquilinus. The Gospel represents his preaching of the Christian faith.
</p>
