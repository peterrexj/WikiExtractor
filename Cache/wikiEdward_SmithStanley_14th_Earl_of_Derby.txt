<p><b>Edward George Geoffrey Smith-Stanley, 14th Earl of Derby</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Knight_Grand_Cross_of_the_Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George">GCMG</a>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Ireland" title="Privy Council of Ireland">PC (Ire)</a></span> (29 March 1799 – 23 October 1869), known as <b>Lord Stanley</b> from 1834 to 1851<b>,</b> was a British statesman and <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)" title="Conservative Party (UK)">Conservative</a> politician who served three times as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of the United Kingdom</a>. To date, he is the longest-serving <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Conservative_Party_(UK)" title="Leader of the Conservative Party (UK)">leader of the Conservative Party</a>. He is one of only four British prime ministers to have three or more separate periods in office.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, his ministries each lasted less than two years and totalled three years and 280 days.  Derby introduced the state education system in Ireland, and reformed Parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Historian Frances Walsh has written that it was Derby:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>who educated the party and acted as its strategist to pass the last great <a href="/wiki/Whigs_(British_political_party)" title="Whigs (British political party)">Whig</a> measure, the <a href="/wiki/Reform_Act_1867" title="Reform Act 1867">1867 Reform Act</a>. It was his greatest achievement to create the modern Conservative Party in the framework of the Whig constitution, though it was <a href="/wiki/Disraeli" class="mw-redirect" title="Disraeli">Disraeli</a> who laid claim to it.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Scholars long ignored his role but in the 21st century rank him highly among all British prime ministers.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span id="Early_political_career.2C_1822.E2.80.931852"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career,_1822–1852">Early political career, 1822–1852</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early political career, 1822–1852">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1822 Edward Stanley, as he was then, was elected to Parliament in the <a href="/wiki/Rotten_Borough" class="mw-redirect" title="Rotten Borough">rotten borough</a> of <a href="/wiki/Stockbridge_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Stockbridge (UK Parliament constituency)">Stockbridge</a> as a <a href="/wiki/British_Whig_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig Party">Whig</a>, the traditional party of his family.<sup id="cite_ref-Hawkins_Vol_I_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hawkins_Vol_I-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1824, however, he alienated some of his Whig colleagues by voting against <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Hume" title="Joseph Hume">Joseph Hume</a>'s motion for an investigation into the established Protestant <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He lost his seat in 1826. When the Whigs returned to power in 1830, Stanley became <a href="/wiki/Chief_Secretary_for_Ireland" title="Chief Secretary for Ireland">Chief Secretary for Ireland</a> in <a href="/wiki/Charles_Grey,_2nd_Earl_Grey" title="Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey">Lord Grey's</a> Government, and entered the Cabinet in 1831. As Chief Secretary Stanley pursued a series of coercive measures which frequently brought him into conflict with the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland" title="Lord Lieutenant of Ireland">Lord Lieutenant of Ireland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Paget,_1st_Marquess_of_Anglesey" title="Henry Paget, 1st Marquess of Anglesey">Lord Anglesey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  In October 1831, Stanley wrote a letter, the <a href="/wiki/Stanley_Letter" class="mw-redirect" title="Stanley Letter">Stanley Letter</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Augustus_FitzGerald,_3rd_Duke_of_Leinster" title="Augustus FitzGerald, 3rd Duke of Leinster">the Duke of Leinster</a> establishing the system of National Education in Ireland. This letter remains today the legal basis for the predominant form of primary education in Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  In 1833, Stanley moved up to the more important position of <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_and_the_Colonies" title="Secretary of State for War and the Colonies">Secretary of State for War and the Colonies</a>, overseeing the passage of the <a href="/wiki/Slavery_Abolition_Act_1833" title="Slavery Abolition Act 1833">Abolition of Slavery Bill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stanley, a religiously devout Anglican, broke with the ministry over the reform of the Anglican <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> in 1834 and resigned from the government. He then formed a group called the "<a href="/wiki/Derby_Dilly" title="Derby Dilly">Derby Dilly</a>" and attempted to chart a middle course between what they saw as the increasingly radical Whiggery of Lord John Russell and the conservatism of the Tories. Tory leader Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a>'s turn to the centre with the 1834 <a href="/wiki/Tamworth_Manifesto" title="Tamworth Manifesto">Tamworth Manifesto</a>, published three days before Stanley's "Knowsley Creed" speech, robbed the Stanleyites of much of the uniqueness of their programme.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The term "Derby Dilly" was coined by Irish Nationalist leader <a href="/wiki/Daniel_O%27Connell" title="Daniel O&#39;Connell">Daniel O'Connell</a>.  Besides Stanley, the other principal members of the Dilly were <a href="/wiki/Sir_James_Graham,_2nd_Baronet" title="Sir James Graham, 2nd Baronet">Sir James Graham</a>, who had resigned as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty" title="First Lord of the Admiralty">First Lord of the Admiralty</a>; <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Robinson,_1st_Earl_of_Ripon" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick Robinson, 1st Earl of Ripon">Lord Ripon</a>, who had resigned as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Privy_Seal" title="Lord Privy Seal">Lord Privy Seal</a>; and the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Gordon-Lennox,_5th_Duke_of_Richmond" title="Charles Gordon-Lennox, 5th Duke of Richmond">Duke of Richmond</a>, who had resigned as <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom_Postmaster_General" class="mw-redirect" title="United Kingdom Postmaster General">Postmaster General</a>. These four ministers had come from notably different political backgrounds—Stanley and Graham were old Whigs, Ripon was a former <a href="/wiki/Canningite" title="Canningite">Canningite</a> Tory prime minister, while Richmond was an arch-conservative Tory who had incongruously found himself in the Grey cabinet.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Frederick_Richard_Say_(1805-1868)_-_Edward_Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Frederick_Richard_Say_%281805-1868%29_-_Edward_Stanley%2C_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg/220px-Frederick_Richard_Say_%281805-1868%29_-_Edward_Stanley%2C_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="286" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Frederick_Richard_Say_%281805-1868%29_-_Edward_Stanley%2C_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg/330px-Frederick_Richard_Say_%281805-1868%29_-_Edward_Stanley%2C_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Frederick_Richard_Say_%281805-1868%29_-_Edward_Stanley%2C_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg/440px-Frederick_Richard_Say_%281805-1868%29_-_Edward_Stanley%2C_14th_Earl_of_Derby_-_NPG_1806_-_National_Portrait_Gallery.jpg 2x" data-file-width="616" data-file-height="800" /></a><figcaption>Lord Stanley in 1844 by <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Richard_Say" title="Frederick Richard Say">Frederick Richard Say</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Although they did not participate in Peel's short-lived 1835 ministry, over the next several years they gradually merged into Peel's Conservative Party, with several members of the "Derby Dilly" taking prominent positions in <a href="/wiki/Second_Peel_ministry" title="Second Peel ministry">Peel's second ministry</a>.  Joining the Conservatives, Stanley again served as Colonial Secretary in Peel's second government in 1841.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1844 he was summoned to the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> as Lord Stanley of Bickerstaffe in his father's <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Derby" title="Earl of Derby">Barony of Stanley</a> by Writ of Acceleration.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Saintsbury_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saintsbury-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  He broke with the Prime Minister again in 1845, this time over the repeal of the <a href="/wiki/Corn_Laws" title="Corn Laws">Corn Laws</a>, and managed to bring the majority of the Conservative Party with him (including, among others, the young <a href="/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli" title="Benjamin Disraeli">Benjamin Disraeli</a>). He thereafter led the <a href="/wiki/Protectionism" title="Protectionism">protectionist</a> faction of the Conservative Party.  In the House of Lords, on 23 November 1847, he accused the Irish Catholic clergy of using the confessional to encourage lawlessness and crime.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> This was disputed in a series of letters by the coadjutor <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Derry" title="Bishop of Derry">Bishop of Derry</a>, <a href="/wiki/Edward_Maginn" title="Edward Maginn">Edward Maginn</a>.  In 1851 he succeeded his father as Earl of Derby.
</p><p>The party system was in a state of flux when the Conservatives left office in 1846, the outstanding issues being the question of Ireland and the unresolved franchise.  The protectionists had a core of leaders, of whom Derby was a leading light.
</p>
<h2><span id="Premierships.2C_1852.E2.80.931869"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Premierships,_1852–1869">Premierships, 1852–1869</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Premierships, 1852–1869">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is the only modern-era prime minister who never enjoyed a parliamentary majority. In his private diary, the <a href="/wiki/James_Harris,_3rd_Earl_of_Malmesbury" title="James Harris, 3rd Earl of Malmesbury">Earl of Malmesbury</a> in 1857 commented on Derby's failure to exploit the press:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Lord Derby has never been able to realise the sudden growth and power of the Political Press, for which he has no partiality, which feeling is reciprocated by its members. In these days this is a fatal error in men who wish to obtain public power and distinction. Lord Derby is too proud a man to flatter anybody, even his greatest friends and equals, much less those of whom he knows nothing.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="First_government">First government<span class="anchor" id="First_premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: First government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Who%3F_Who%3F_ministry" title="Who? Who? ministry">Who? Who? ministry</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Lord-Derby-1852.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/2/28/Lord-Derby-1852.jpg/110px-Lord-Derby-1852.jpg" decoding="async" width="110" height="193" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/2/28/Lord-Derby-1852.jpg/165px-Lord-Derby-1852.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/2/28/Lord-Derby-1852.jpg/220px-Lord-Derby-1852.jpg 2x" data-file-width="257" data-file-height="450" /></a><figcaption>1852 illustration of Derby</figcaption></figure>
<p>Derby formed a <a href="/wiki/Minority_government" title="Minority government">minority government</a> in February 1852 following the collapse of <a href="/wiki/John_Russell,_1st_Earl_Russell" title="John Russell, 1st Earl Russell">Lord John Russell</a>'s Whig Government. In this new ministry, Benjamin Disraeli was appointed <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a>. With many senior Conservative ministers having followed Peel, Derby was forced to appoint many new men to the office of the Cabinet, only three were pre-existing <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Counsellors</a>. When the aged <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a>, by then very deaf, heard the list of inexperienced cabinet ministers being read aloud in the House of Lords, he gave the government its nickname by shouting "Who? Who?". From then this government would be known as the "Who? Who?" ministry.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Traditionally Derby's ministries were thought in hindsight to have been dominated by Disraeli. However, recent research suggests that this was not always the case, especially in the government's conduct of foreign policy.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> There, Derby and his <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_(UK)" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs (UK)">Foreign Secretaries</a>, <a href="/wiki/James_Howard_Harris,_3rd_Earl_of_Malmesbury" class="mw-redirect" title="James Howard Harris, 3rd Earl of Malmesbury">Lord Malmesbury</a> and later his son <a href="/wiki/Edward_Stanley,_15th_Earl_of_Derby" title="Edward Stanley, 15th Earl of Derby">Lord Stanley</a>, pursued a course of action that was aimed at building up power through financial strength, seeking to avoid wars at all costs, co-operating with other powers, and working through the <a href="/wiki/Concert_of_Europe" title="Concert of Europe">Concert of Europe</a> to resolve diplomatic problems. This contrasted sharply with the policy of military strength and prestige that Disraeli would later pursue, and Derby's very different take on foreign policy could be seen as the precursor of "<a href="/wiki/Splendid_isolation" title="Splendid isolation">splendid isolation</a>", as well as the diplomatic settlement of Europe pursued by later Conservatives in the late 19th century and the 1930s.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/1852_United_Kingdom_general_election" title="1852 United Kingdom general election">general election of June 1852</a>, the Conservative party under Derby and Disraeli won only 330 seats in the House of Commons—42.9% of the total.  Although the Whigs actually won fewer seats—292 seats—there were several small groups in Parliament that might be willing to side with the Whigs on particular issues, including the 38 Conservative members of Parliament who were <a href="/wiki/Peelites" class="mw-redirect" title="Peelites">Peelites</a>, who had already joined with the Whigs in June 1846 to repeal the <a href="/wiki/Corn_Laws" title="Corn Laws">Corn Laws</a>; the 113 members who were Free Traders and who were interested in eliminating all tariffs on consumer goods; and the 63 members of the <a href="/wiki/Independent_Irish_Party" title="Independent Irish Party">Irish Brigade</a> who were interested in the independence of Ireland and Tenant's Rights for Irish tenants.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  Immediately following the election in June 1852, none of these small groups were willing to work with the Whigs to form a government.  Accordingly, the Earl of Derby was invited to form a minority government.  Derby did so and appointed Disraeli as the new Chancellor of the Exchequer.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As with all minority governments, Derby's minority government had a difficult time governing.  Their main preoccupation was avoiding any issue which might cause any of the government's small components to go over to Whigs and cause a "no confidence" vote.  However, the real issues facing Parliament could not be postponed for long, and when Disraeli submitted his first budget to Parliament in December 1852, it proved so unpopular with the Peelites, the Free Traders, and the Irish Brigade that it was voted down in a "no confidence" vote.  As a result, Derby's minority government fell, making way for a <a href="/wiki/Aberdeen_ministry" title="Aberdeen ministry">Peelite–Whig coalition</a> under <a href="/wiki/George_Hamilton-Gordon,_4th_Earl_of_Aberdeen" title="George Hamilton-Gordon, 4th Earl of Aberdeen">Lord Aberdeen</a>.  When Aberdeen's administration fell in 1855, <a href="/wiki/Queen_Victoria" title="Queen Victoria">Queen Victoria</a> asked Derby to form a government.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Much to the consternation of some sections of his party, including Disraeli, Derby declined this offer, believing that he would be in a position to form a stronger government after a short-lived failed administration led by one of the Conservative Party's rivals such as <a href="/wiki/Lord_John_Russell,_1st_Earl_Russell" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord John Russell, 1st Earl Russell">Lord John Russell</a> or <a href="/wiki/Henry_John_Temple,_3rd_Viscount_Palmerston" title="Henry John Temple, 3rd Viscount Palmerston">Lord Palmerston</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_government">Second government<span class="anchor" id="Second_premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Second government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Second_Derby%E2%80%93Disraeli_ministry" title="Second Derby–Disraeli ministry">Second Derby–Disraeli ministry</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg/170px-Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="220" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg/255px-Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg/340px-Edward-Stanley-14th-Earl-of-Derby.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1826" data-file-height="2368" /></a><figcaption>Derby in 1861</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1858, Derby formed another minority government upon the resignation of Lord Palmerston following a parliamentary defeat to an opposition motion which, in the context of a failed plot to assassinate <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_III" title="Napoleon III">Napoleon III</a> of France, charged that "the ministry had admitted they sheltered assassins".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Disraeli was again at the Exchequer and Leader of the Commons. Among the notable achievements of this administration was the end of the <a href="/wiki/British_East_India_Company" class="mw-redirect" title="British East India Company">British East India Company</a> following the <a href="/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857" title="Indian Rebellion of 1857">Indian Rebellion of 1857</a>, which brought India under direct British control for the first time. Once again the government was short-lived, resigning after only one year, having narrowly lost a vote of no-confidence brought by <a href="/wiki/Spencer_Cavendish,_8th_Duke_of_Devonshire" title="Spencer Cavendish, 8th Duke of Devonshire">Lord Hartington</a> on behalf of various Whig and Radical factions which had coalesced at the Willis's Rooms meetings in St James's Street to mark the birth of the <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Party_(UK)" title="Liberal Party (UK)">Liberal Party</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> In July 1859, Derby was appointed a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter" title="Order of the Garter">Knight of the Garter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Back in opposition, Derby pursued a strategy of trying to lure the Prime Minister, Lord Palmerston, away from his more radical colleagues, Lord John Russell in particular.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  This tactic was thwarted by Russell's declining influence and by Chancellor of the Exchequer <a href="/wiki/William_Ewart_Gladstone" title="William Ewart Gladstone">Gladstone</a>'s 1861 budget which united the cabinet and increased divisions amongst the Conservatives.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Palmerston continued as Prime Minister until his death in 1865, when he was succeeded by the frail Russell.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Third_government">Third government<span class="anchor" id="Third_premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Third government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Third_Derby%E2%80%93Disraeli_ministry" title="Third Derby–Disraeli ministry">Third Derby–Disraeli ministry</a></div>
<p>Derby returned to power for the third and last time in 1866, following the collapse of Lord Russell's second government after its failed attempt at further electoral reform.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Once again, Disraeli was a leading figure. This administration was particularly notable for the passage of the <a href="/wiki/Reform_Act_1867" title="Reform Act 1867">Reform Act 1867</a>, which greatly expanded the suffrage but which provoked the resignation of three cabinet ministers including the Secretary for India and future three-time Prime Minister, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_3rd_Marquess_of_Salisbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Cecil, 3rd Marquess of Salisbury">Lord Cranborne</a> (later Lord Salisbury).<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> In early 1868, Derby retired from political life on medical advice, leaving Disraeli to succeed him.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> In 1869, he was appointed a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" title="Order of St Michael and St George">Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George</a> in recognition of his former role as Secretary of State for War and the Colonies.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> He was appointed <a href="/wiki/Colonel_(United_Kingdom)#Honorary_Colonel" title="Colonel (United Kingdom)">Honorary Colonel</a> of the <a href="/wiki/1st_Lancashire_Rifle_Volunteer_Corps" class="mw-redirect" title="1st Lancashire Rifle Volunteer Corps">1st Lancashire Rifle Volunteer Corps</a> on 10 September 1862, beginning a family connection with the regiment that endured for over 100 years.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although a great orator, Derby was frequently criticised for his languid leadership. Nevertheless, he had many significant achievements, both as minister and Prime Minister, and has been described as the father of the modern Conservative Party.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2007)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> His tenure of 22 years as party leader still stands as the longest in Conservative Party history and indeed the history of any other political party in British history. Only <a href="/wiki/Labour_Party_(UK)" title="Labour Party (UK)">Labour</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Clement_Attlee" title="Clement Attlee">Clement Attlee</a> came close, at 20 years.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg/170px-Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="259" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg/255px-Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg/340px-Emma_Smith-Stanley.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1230" data-file-height="1872" /></a><figcaption>Emma Smith-Stanley</figcaption></figure>

Emma Bootle-Wilbraham</a>, the second daughter of <a href="/wiki/Edward_Bootle-Wilbraham,_1st_Baron_Skelmersdale" title="Edward Bootle-Wilbraham, 1st Baron Skelmersdale">Edward Bootle-Wilbraham</a>, on 31 May 1825. He left three children:<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Stanley's ancestors were <a href="/wiki/King_of_Man" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Man">Kings of Man</a> from 1405 and later <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_Mann" title="Lord of Mann">Lords of Man</a>. <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Stanley,_1st_Earl_of_Derby" title="Thomas Stanley, 1st Earl of Derby">Thomas Stanley, 1st Earl of Derby</a> famously switched sides at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Bosworth" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Bosworth">Battle of Bosworth</a> and placed the crown of the fallen King <a href="/wiki/Richard_III_of_England" title="Richard III of England">Richard III</a> upon the head of <a href="/wiki/Henry_VII_of_England" title="Henry VII of England">Henry Tudor</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Smith-Stanley,_14th_Earl_of_Derby&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Statue_of_the_Earl_of_Derby,_Parliament_Square" title="Statue of the Earl of Derby, Parliament Square">Statue of the Earl of Derby, Parliament Square</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg/170px-Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg/255px-Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg/340px-Earl_of_Derby_statue.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="2048" /></a><figcaption>Statue in London's <a href="/wiki/Parliament_Square" title="Parliament Square">Parliament Square</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Historian David Cannadine argues:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Although almost entirely forgotten today, Derby was one of the great figures of 19th century British public, social and cultural life: he was a fine debater, a classical scholar of note and a significant patron of the turf; he was also an authentic grandee, with very rich, coal-bearing estates in Lancashire, and leader of the Conservative Party for an unrivaled span of 22 years.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Historian Frances Walsh has written:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Although he was the first politician to become prime minister three times and remains the longest-serving party leader in British history he has not received the recognition one would expect. As a landed aristocrat with Whig antecedents, literary tastes, and a passionate interest in shooting and the turf he seemed to represent an obsolete, amateur tradition in politics, while the  mythologizing of Disraeli as the architect of conservative survival and success tended to cast him into the shadows. This neglect was compounded by the absence of an official biography and problems of access to the Derby archives for a century after his death. Recent Studies have done something to redress the balance… It was Derby who educated the party and acted as its strategist to pass the last great Whig measure, the <a href="/wiki/Reform_Act_1867" title="Reform Act 1867">1867 Reform Act</a>. It was his greatest achievement to create the modern Conservative Party in the framework of the Whig constitution, though it was Disraeli who laid claim to it.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/National_school_(Ireland)" title="National school (Ireland)">National School system in Ireland</a>, the predominant form of primary school education, remains based on the multi-denominational system set up by Stanley in the <a href="/wiki/Stanley_Letter" class="mw-redirect" title="Stanley Letter">Stanley Letter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-irish-education-documents-vol1_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-irish-education-documents-vol1-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> The letter had tried to deal with the seemingly intractable issue of different Christian religions living together in Ireland.
</p><p>The former site of <a href="/wiki/Fort_Langley,_British_Columbia" class="mw-redirect" title="Fort Langley, British Columbia">Fort Langley, British Columbia</a> was renamed <a href="/wiki/Derby,_British_Columbia" title="Derby, British Columbia">Derby</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Engineers" title="Royal Engineers">Royal Engineers</a> in 1858, apparently in honour of the Earl, who was British Prime Minister at the time. <a href="/wiki/Stanley,_Falkland_Islands" title="Stanley, Falkland Islands">Stanley</a> (sometimes referred to as "Port Stanley") on <a href="/wiki/East_Falkland" title="East Falkland">East Falkland</a>, capital of the <a href="/wiki/Falkland_Islands" title="Falkland Islands">Falkland Islands</a>, is named after Edward Smith-Stanley as are <a href="/wiki/Port_Stanley,_Ontario" title="Port Stanley, Ontario">Port Stanley</a> in Ontario, Canada (he did not visit his namesake but he had visited nearby <a href="/wiki/Port_Talbot,_Ontario" title="Port Talbot, Ontario">Port Talbot, Ontario</a> during his Canadian/American trip in 1824<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>), as well as the area <a href="/wiki/Stanley,_Hong_Kong" title="Stanley, Hong Kong">Stanley</a> in Hong Kong. Stanley was Prime Minister when Queen Victoria opened <a href="/wiki/Wellington_College,_Berkshire" title="Wellington College, Berkshire">Wellington College</a>, in Berkshire, a tribute to the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Wellington" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a>, where the boarding house Stanley is named after him. The county of Stanley in <a href="/wiki/Queensland" title="Queensland">Queensland</a>, Australia, is named after the Earl. Notably, it contains the important Australian city of <a href="/wiki/Brisbane" title="Brisbane">Brisbane</a>.
</p><p>A library book about Smith-Stanley titled <i>The Earl of Derby</i>, written by <a href="/wiki/George_Saintsbury" title="George Saintsbury">George Saintsbury</a> and published in 1892, was borrowed from the Newtown Library in <a href="/wiki/Wellington" title="Wellington">Wellington</a>, New Zealand, in March 1902 and returned in August 2020 (118 years later) after being discovered in Sydney, Australia. The book was described as being "in OK condition".<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
