					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">9th-century Spanish archbishop and saint</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Agobard</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Archbishop</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;779</span><br /><a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a><sup id="cite_ref-patron_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">840 (aged&#160;60&#8211;61)</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">6 June</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Agobard of Lyon</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;779</span>–840) was a <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a>-born priest and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lyon">archbishop of Lyon</a>, during the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Renaissance" title="Carolingian Renaissance">Carolingian Renaissance</a>. The author of multiple treatises, ranging in subject matter from the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm" title="Byzantine Iconoclasm">iconoclast controversy</a> to <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Adoptionism" title="Spanish Adoptionism">Spanish Adoptionism</a> to critiques of the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> royal family, Agobard is best known for his critiques of <a href="/wiki/Judaism" title="Judaism">Jewish</a> religious practices and political power in the <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Frankish</a>-<a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian</a> realm. He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Amulo" title="Amulo">Amulo of Lyons</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agobard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A native of Spain, Agobard moved to Lyon in 792. He was ordained as a priest <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;804</span>, and was well-liked by the archbishop of Lyon, <a href="/wiki/Leidrad" title="Leidrad">Leidrad</a> (r. 799–816). At some point, Agobard was ordained as a <a href="/wiki/Chorbishop" title="Chorbishop">chorbishop</a>, or assistant bishop. Controversy arose in 814, when the aging Leidrad retired into a monastery, appointing Agobard as his successor. While Carolingian emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> did not object to the appointment, some of the other bishops did, calling a synod at Arles to protest the elevation of a new bishop while the old bishop still lived. Archbishop Leidrad died in 816, and the controversy fizzled out, leaving Agobard as the new archbishop. Soon after taking office, he confronted several issues, which included opposing <a href="/wiki/Trial_by_ordeal" title="Trial by ordeal">trials by ordeal</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and, in 818, writing against <a href="/wiki/Felix_of_Urgel" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix of Urgel">Felix of Urgel</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Adoptionism" title="Spanish Adoptionism">Spanish Adoptionist</a> Christology.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Anti-Jewish_Polemic">Anti-Jewish Polemic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agobard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Anti-Jewish Polemic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agobard is notorious for his vocal attacks on the local Jewish population and their religious practices. Jewish communities living in the <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Frankish</a> or <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian</a> realm had been granted considerable freedoms under <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> son of Charlemagne, including a prohibition on Christian proselytizing. Louis appointed a magister Iudaeorum to ensure Jewish legal protection, and did not force Jews to allow baptism for their slaves. Agobard found this last provision particularly galling, and wrote his first anti-Jewish tract on the matter: <i>De Baptismo Judaicorum Mancipiorum</i> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;823</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> For the rest of the decade, Agobard campaigned against what he saw as the dangerous growth in power and influence of Jews in the kingdom that was contrary to canon law.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was during this time that he wrote such works as <i>Contra Praeceptum Impium</i><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;826</span>), <i>De Insolentia Judeorum</i><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;827</span>), <i>De Judaicis Superstitionibus</i><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;827</span>), and <i>De Cavendo Convictu et Societate Judaica</i><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;827</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Agobard's rhetoric, which included describing Jews as "<i>filii diaboli</i>" ("children of the devil") was indicative of the developing anti-Jewish strain of medieval Christian thought. As Jeremy Cohen has claimed, Agobard's response was paradoxically both stereotypical and knowledgeable (he showed a great knowledge of contemporary Judaism, while maintaining and perpetuating stereotypes).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Icons">Icons</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agobard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Icons">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the 820s, a controversy emerged over the iconoclastic policies of bishop <a href="/wiki/Claudius_of_Turin" title="Claudius of Turin">Claudius of Turin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> This stance was opposed by <a href="/wiki/Dungal_of_Bobbio" title="Dungal of Bobbio">Dungal of Bobbio</a> at the request of Louis the Pious. Agobard, in his <i>Book on Paintings and Images</i>, came out in opposition to Dungal's method of using secular knowledge to justify veneration of images.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_Problems">Political Problems</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agobard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Political Problems">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the 820s, Agobard had already shown his willingness to challenge Louis the Pious on the subject of Jews and on secular holdings of church land.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Agobard continued to confront the emperor, particularly on the issues of royal succession and the matter of land ownership. Agobard accused the emperor of abandoning his 817 Ordinatio imperii decree, which promoted an all-encompassing unity of church and empire.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In both of the two rebellions against Louis, 830 and 833, Agobard supported the ill-fated revolt of Louis' son <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair I</a>. In 833, when Lothair launched his second revolt, Agobard published his support for Lothair once more in several works: <i>A Comparison of Ecclesiastical and Political Government and Wherein the Dignity of the Church Outshines the Majesty of Empires</i> and the <i>Liber Apologeticus</i> in defense of the rebelling sons of Louis.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Exile_and_Return">Exile and Return</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agobard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Exile and Return">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Louis was restored to his power, backed by his sons <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pepin_I_of_Aquitaine" title="Pepin I of Aquitaine">Pepin I of Aquitaine</a>, Agobard was suspended from his episcopate by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Thionville" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Thionville">Council of Thionville</a> and exiled, replaced by the <a href="/wiki/Chorbishop" title="Chorbishop">chorbishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Amalarius_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Amalarius of Metz">Amalarius of Metz</a> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;775</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;850</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> During his tenure in Lyon, Amalarius worked to impose liturgical reforms upon the archdiocese of Lyon.  Amalarius' reforms were characterized by a heavy reliance upon allegorical and symbolic representations within the Mass. Agobard, on the other hand, disdained Amalarius' reforms as "theatrical" and "showy" and favored a more plain liturgy.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Amalarius' reforms were also opposed by Agobard's disciple <a href="/wiki/Florus_of_Lyon" title="Florus of Lyon">Florus of Lyon</a>; Amalarius was deposed and accused of heresy in 838.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Agobard wrote three works against Amalarius: <i>On Divine Psalmody</i>, <i>On the Correction of the Antiphonary</i>, and <i>Liber officialis</i>. When he returned to Lyon, Agobard worked to roll back Amalarius' actions, with the support of Florus.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_works">Other works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agobard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Other works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During his life, Agobard wrote more works on other issues, including several against pagan practices,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> two on the role of clergy,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> and a treatise on icons.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agobard also wrote a treatise arguing against weather magic called <i>De Grandine et Tonitruis</i> ("On Hail and Thunder"). A passage in it mentions the popular belief in ships in the clouds whose sailors were thought to take crops damaged by hail or storms to their land of <a href="/wiki/Magonia_(mythology)" class="mw-redirect" title="Magonia (mythology)">Magonia</a>.
</p><p>Many of his works were lost <a href="/wiki/Editio_princeps" title="Editio princeps">until 1605</a>, when a manuscript was discovered in Lyons and published by <a href="/wiki/Jean_Papire_Masson" title="Jean Papire Masson">Papirius Masson</a>, and again by <a href="/wiki/Baluze" class="mw-redirect" title="Baluze">Baluze</a> in 1666.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Agobard's complete works can be found in Volume 104 of <a href="/wiki/Jacques_Paul_Migne" title="Jacques Paul Migne">J.P. Migne's</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologia Latina</a></i>, and, in a more recent edition, in Van Acker's <i>Agobardi Lugdunensis Opera Omnia</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
