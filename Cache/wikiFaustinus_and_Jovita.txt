<p><b>Saints Jovita and Faustinus</b> were said to be <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> under <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a>.  Their traditional date of death is 120.  They are <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron saints">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Traditional_vita">Traditional vita</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Faustinus_and_Jovita&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Traditional vita">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition states that they were members of a noble family of Brescia in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> (northern Italy).  Jovinus, the older brother, was a preacher; Faustinus, a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>. For their fearless preaching of the Gospel, they were arraigned before the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> Hadrian, who at Brescia, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, subjected them to frightful torments, after which they were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> at Brescia in the year 120, according to the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a>, although historian <a href="/wiki/Paul_Allard" title="Paul Allard">Paul Allard</a> (<i>Histoire des Persécutions pendant les Deux Premiers Siècles</i>, Paris, 1885) places the date as early as 118.<sup id="cite_ref-Murphy_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Murphy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Faustinus_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Faustinus of Brescia">Saint Faustinus of Brescia</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">bishop of Brescia</a> and an alleged descendant, compiled their <i>Acts</i>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Faustinus_and_Jovita&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The many "Acts" of these saints are chiefly of a legendary character. The <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> Fedele Savio questioned nearly every fact related of them except their existence of the martyrdom, which are too well attested by their inclusion in so many of the early <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a> and their extraordinary cult in their native city, of which from time immemorial they have been the chief patrons.<sup id="cite_ref-Murphy_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Murphy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Savio emphasizes that the saints are not to be identified with the fabulous figures in the Acts.
</p><p>It is believed that they were martyred at a site that either was, or later became, a Roman cemetery. A church was built there called <i>Santi Faustino e Giovita ad sanguinem</i>. Its dedication was later changed to Saint Afra. {Saint Afra's was destroyed during the bombing of World War II).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their common feast day on 15 February, the traditional date of their martyrdom, was inserted into the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>. It was removed in 1969, because their "Acts are completely fabulous, treating Jovita as a preacher, although she was a woman and a man was Faustinus."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>   The two saints remain listed in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, the official, through professedly incomplete, list of the saints recognized by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The cities of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a> and <a href="/wiki/Male%C4%8Dnik" title="Malečnik">Malečnik</a> share with Brescia possession of their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.
</p><p>A lake partially in the town of <a href="/wiki/St._Leo,_Florida" title="St. Leo, Florida">St. Leo, Florida</a> has been called Lake Jovita since its discovery by Judge <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Francis_Dunne" title="Edmund Francis Dunne">Edmund F. Dunne</a> on February 15, 1882. The nearby community of <a href="/wiki/San_Antonio,_Florida" title="San Antonio, Florida">San Antonio</a> changed its name to Lake Jovita in 1927 before reverting in 1933.<sup id="cite_ref-Horgan,_James_J._1990_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Horgan,_James_J._1990-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
