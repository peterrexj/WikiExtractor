<p><b>Margaret Ward</b> (c. 1550-30 August 1588), the "pearl of Tyburn", was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who was executed during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a> for assisting a <a href="/wiki/Clergy" title="Clergy">priest</a> to escape from prison. She was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> in 1970, as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Ward&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret Ward was born in <a href="/wiki/Congleton" title="Congleton">Congleton</a>, Cheshire around 1550.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was living in London in the service of a lady of the "first rank" when she learned of the severe maltreatment of Richard Watson, a priest confined at <a href="/wiki/Bridewell_Palace" title="Bridewell Palace">Bridewell Prison</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She obtained permission to visit him. She was thoroughly searched before and after every visit, but gradually the authorities became less cautious, and she managed to smuggle a rope into the prison. Fr. William Watson jumped off the wall, slightly hurting himself. He escaped, but left the rope hanging from the window. The boatman whom Ward had engaged to convey him down the river then refused to carry out the bargain. Ward, in her distress, confided in another boatman, <a href="/wiki/Blessed_John_Roche" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed John Roche">John Roche</a>, who undertook to assist her. He provided a boat and exchanged clothes with the priest. Watson escaped, but Roche was captured in his place, and Ward, having been Watson's only visitor, was also arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-Burton_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Margaret Ward was kept in irons for eight days, was hung up by the hands, and <a href="/wiki/Scourge" title="Scourge">scourged</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but absolutely refused to disclose the priest's whereabouts. At her trial, she admitted to having helped Watson to escape, and rejoiced in "having delivered an innocent lamb from the hands of those bloody wolves". She was offered a pardon if she would attend a <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> service but refused.<sup id="cite_ref-Burton_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was hanged at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> on 30 August 1588,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> along with Edward Shelley, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Martin_(martyr)" title="Richard Martin (martyr)">Richard Martin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Leigh_(martyr)" title="Richard Leigh (martyr)">Richard Leigh</a>, Richard Lloyd (alias Flower) and <a href="/wiki/John_Roche_(martyr)" title="John Roche (martyr)">John Roche</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Ward&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret Ward was beatified in 1929<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 25 October 1970, as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day, along with all the English Martyrs, is on 4 May. However, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> dioceses of England, she shares a feast day with fellow female martyr saints <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Clitherow" title="Margaret Clitherow">Margaret Clitherow</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anne_Line" title="Anne Line">Anne Line</a>, on 30 August.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The three were officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day on 30 August.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ward is depicted in panels in St Joseph's, Sale and St Alban's, Wallasey.<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There are several schools named after her, including <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret_Ward_Catholic_Academy" title="St Margaret Ward Catholic Academy">St Margaret Ward Catholic Academy</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tunstall,_Staffordshire" title="Tunstall, Staffordshire">Tunstall, Staffordshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
