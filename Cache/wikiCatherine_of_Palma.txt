<p><b>Catherine of Palma</b> (1531&#8211;1574, born <b>Catalina Thomás</b>) was a Spanish nun canonised in 1930.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dunbar_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was born 1 May 1531 at <a href="/wiki/Valldemossa" title="Valldemossa">Valldemossa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mallorca" title="Mallorca">Mallorca</a>, Spain, in a peasant family. She worked as a servant in a household in Palma where she learned to read and embroider, before joining the Canonesses of St Augustine at the convent of St Mary Magdalene in Palma. According to legend, she was visited by devils and angels, and went into ecstasy for the last years of her life. She died 5 April 1574 at <a href="/wiki/Palma,_Mallorca" class="mw-redirect" title="Palma, Mallorca">Palma</a>, Mallorca, of natural causes.  As of 1904<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Palma&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup> her hat, thimble, and other relics were kept, and her body preserved in a marble sarcophagus, in the convent of St Mary Magdalene, Palma.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dunbar_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her death she was celebrated locally as a saint for half a century until a decree of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VII" title="Pope Urban VII">Pope Urban VII</a> forbade the worship of unrecognised saints. Local people appealed to Rome and eventually she was beatified on 12 August 1792 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a><sup id="cite_ref-mestre_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mestre-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and canonised on 22 June 1930 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dunbar_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The house in Valldemossa where she was born, Carrer Rectoria 5, has become a shrine, and many houses in the village bear a plaque in her honour.<sup id="cite_ref-maps_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maps-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-see_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-see-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is considered the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Mallorca,<sup id="cite_ref-maps_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maps-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-way_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-way-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> although other sources apply this title to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian" title="Saint Sebastian">Sebastian</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-balears_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-balears-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Rodriguez" title="Alphonsus Rodriguez">Alphonsus Rodriguez</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> or the Virgen de Lluc of the <a href="/wiki/Santuari_de_Lluc" title="Santuari de Lluc">Santuari de Lluc</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG/220px-Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG/330px-Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG/440px-Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1413" data-file-height="1060" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Catalina_Thomas.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Plaque commemorating the saint</div></div></div>
<p>She is commemorated on 1 April, and on 27 and 28 July in her home town of Valldemossa.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Spellings of her names found in sources include Catalina,<sup id="cite_ref-see_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-see-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Caterina, Cathalina<sup id="cite_ref-mestre_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mestre-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and Catherine,<sup id="cite_ref-cath_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and Thomas,<sup id="cite_ref-see_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-see-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Thomás, Tomas,<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Tomàs Gallard,<sup id="cite_ref-acces_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-acces-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and Tomàs i Gallard.<sup id="cite_ref-acces_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-acces-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
