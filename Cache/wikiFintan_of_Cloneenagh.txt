<p class="mw-empty-elt">

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Fintan of Clonenagh</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 526<sup id="cite_ref-Berry_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Berry-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">pre-congregation</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">17 February</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Fintan of Clonenagh</b> (c. 526 – 603) was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. He was an Abbot and disciple of <a href="/wiki/Columba_of_Terryglass" title="Columba of Terryglass">Columba of Terryglass</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fintan_of_Clonenagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fintan was born in about 526, the son of Christians Gabhren and Findlath.<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan1829_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan1829-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The monastery at Clonenagh was founded in the 6th century by Fintan of Clonenagh who entrusted it to his disciple Columba when Colum moved on to <a href="/wiki/Terryglass" title="Terryglass">Terryglass</a> around 548. Fintan received his religious formation under the Abbot Colum, and was deeply influenced by his penitential practices and the severity of his Rule. Under his direction Fintan developed a reputation for austerity.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fintan gave his monks very strict rules not to consume any animal products.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The community did not have even one cow and so they had neither milk nor butter. The monks complained they couldn’t do hard work on so meagre a diet. A deputation of local clergy headed by <a href="/wiki/Cainnech_of_Aghaboe" title="Cainnech of Aghaboe">Canice</a> of Aghaboe came to urge him to improve it. He agreed for his monks, but he elected to keep to the strict diet himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Fintan was reported to have lived on only "bread of woody barley and clayey water of clay".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His disciples included St Colmán of <a href="/wiki/Oughaval,_County_Laois" title="Oughaval, County Laois">Oughaval</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">St. Comgall of Bangor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  He has been compared by the Irish annalists to St. Benedict, and is styled "Father of the Irish Monks".<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fintan of Clonenagh is regarded as one of three patron saints of county Laois which include Colman Mac ua Laoise and Mochua of Timahoe.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He died in 603. His feast day is 17 February.<sup id="cite_ref-Berry_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Berry-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Though he is sometimes confused with Saint <a href="/wiki/Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon" title="Fintán of Taghmon">Fintán or Munnu</a>, abbot of Taghmon, they are distinct.
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Fintan.27s_Tree.2C_Clonenagh"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Fintan's_Tree,_Clonenagh">St. Fintan's Tree, Clonenagh</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fintan_of_Clonenagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: St. Fintan&#039;s Tree, Clonenagh">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg/220px-Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg/330px-Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg/440px-Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3504" data-file-height="2336" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Clonenagh_St._Fintan%27s_Tree_2010_09_03.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Fintan's Tree, Clonenagh</div></div></div>
<p>This tree, an <i><a href="/wiki/Acer_pseudoplatanus" title="Acer pseudoplatanus">acer pseudoplatanus</a></i>, was planted in the late 18th or early 19th century at the site of the Early Christian monastic site of Clonenagh. The tree is dedicated to St. Fintan and it became custom to insert coins into the tree from which the tree suffered and was believed to be dead until the tree started to recover with some new shoots." (<i>Heritage Tree Database</i>, Tree Council of Ireland)
</p>
