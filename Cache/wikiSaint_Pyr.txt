<p><b>Pyr</b> (<b>Pŷr</b> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="cy-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Welsh" title="Help:IPA/Welsh">[pɨːr, piːr]</a></span>; sometimes known as <b>Piro</b> in English) was a <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the 6th century who may later have been revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by some (though he was never canonized).  Most of what is known of him comes from the First Life of <a href="/wiki/Samson_of_Dol" title="Samson of Dol">St. Samson</a>.
</p><p>He  has been described in one handbook as being "an unsuitable abbot and...one of those <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Celtic</a> 'saints' who would never have been <a href="/wiki/Canonize" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonize">canonized</a> by any formal process".<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>, but there seems no evidence that he was ever considered as a saint by anybody.  Little is known about him apart from the fact that he was the abbot of the monastery on <a href="/wiki/Caldey_Island" title="Caldey Island">Caldey Island</a> (<a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a> <i>Ynys Bŷr</i> "Pŷr's Island").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  According to Gerald of Wales, "Pyrrus" was also the owner of the island, and of a castle in Wales.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pyr is said to have become so drunk one night that on the way back to his cell he fell into a <a href="/wiki/Water_well" class="mw-redirect" title="Water well">well</a>. He died soon after being pulled out. He was replaced as abbot by <a href="/wiki/Samson_of_Dol" title="Samson of Dol">Samson</a>, who resigned in disgust when he found that the young monks had become ungovernable due to the laxity of Pyr's rule.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
