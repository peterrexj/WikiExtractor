<p><b>Magloire</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> better known as <b>Saint Magloire of Dol</b>, is a <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Breton</a> saint. Little reliable information is known of Magloire as the earliest written sources appeared three centuries after his death. These sources claim that he was a monk from Wales<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who became the Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Dol-de-Bretagne" title="Dol-de-Bretagne">Dol-de-Bretagne</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> during the 6th century, and ended his life on the island of <a href="/wiki/Sark" title="Sark">Sark</a>, where he was abbot of a monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Magloire&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although unlikely to contain any reliable biographic information concerning Magloire, the <i>Vita Sancti Maglorii</i> presents a narrative of his life. According to the text, Magloire was born in the early 6th century to <a href="/wiki/Afrelia" class="mw-redirect" title="Afrelia">Afrelia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Umbrafel" title="Umbrafel">Umbrafel</a>, the aunt and uncle of <a href="/wiki/Samson_of_Dol" title="Samson of Dol">Samson of Dol</a>. As a child, he studied at <a href="/wiki/Cor_Tewdws" class="mw-redirect" title="Cor Tewdws">Cor Tewdws</a> under the tutelage of St. <a href="/wiki/Illtud" title="Illtud">Illtud</a>. After his ordination, he was made abbot of a <a href="/wiki/Lanmeur" title="Lanmeur">Lanmeurian</a> monastery where he governed for 52 years. Afterwards, he journeyed to <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> with his cousin Samson, who became the archbishop of Dol.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After Samson's death, Magloire succeeded him as his chosen successor to the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric">archbishopric</a>, although, having received instructions from a visiting angel, he soon resigned his post to <a href="/wiki/Budoc" title="Budoc">Budoc</a> and withdrew to the island of Sark, where he established a community of 62 monks.<sup id="cite_ref-sark_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sark-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to Butler's dating, he died around 575, but since the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> gives no dates, such statements are highly approximate.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Magloire&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Vita Sancti Maglorii</i> attributes several <a href="/wiki/Miracles" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracles">miracles</a> to St. Magloire, and claims that he acquired large swaths of land as a result of these miracles. It argues that Count Loisescon, whose illness was miraculously cured by Magloire, gave him a sixth of all his wealth. It also argues that Nivo, the owner of <a href="/wiki/Guernsey" title="Guernsey">Guernsey</a>, asked for Magloire's help to cure his daughter who was deaf and intellectually disabled. Magloire was supposedly granted a third of Guernsey for doing so.
</p><p>One of the most well-known and detailed stories about Magloire concerns his rescue of a group of children. The children were playing in an abandoned wreck on the beach below the monastery when a sudden violent storm swept them out to sea. Magloire is said to have swum out to sea when he heard their cries and saved them and their boat, steering it to the safety of the shore before vanishing.<sup id="cite_ref-sark_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sark-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Other tales include records that he travelled to the island of <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a> and destroyed a dragon, that he resurrected a drowned fisherman of Sark, and that he led the islanders to fight off an anachronistic fleet of <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> (these may have been <a href="/wiki/Proto-Norse" class="mw-redirect" title="Proto-Norse">proto-Norse</a> raiders).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
A legend is also told that Magloire had trouble keeping a vow to drink neither <a href="/wiki/Wine" title="Wine">wine</a> nor <a href="/wiki/Ale" title="Ale">ale</a>, and to fast from all food twice a week. After his struggles, an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angelic</a> visitation released him from his vow.
</p><p>Posthumous miracles of St. Magloire are also included in the surviving texts. After his death, <a href="/wiki/Sark" title="Sark">Sark</a> was attacked again by <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Vikings</a>, who sacked the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> and killed the monks. When seven of the Vikings attempted to open St. Magloire's tomb, they were blinded, and many of the others turned and began to kill each other.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Magloire&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the reign of <a href="/wiki/Nominoe" title="Nominoe">Nominoe</a> (846–851), the body of Magloire was stolen by the <a href="/wiki/Monks" class="mw-redirect" title="Monks">monks</a> of Lehon Abbey. He was revered by the monks as their primary <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, and it seems likely that the majority of his hagiography was written there in the late 9th century.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> With an increase in <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Viking raids</a> in the early 10th century, his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were transported to Paris by the monks, where <a href="/wiki/Hugh_the_Great" title="Hugh the Great">Hugh the Great</a> granted them land to establish a new monastery. In 1572, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_de%27_Medici" title="Catherine de&#39; Medici">Catherine de' Medici</a> decided to use the site as a home for a group of <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monks who had been expelled from their <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> of Saint-Magloire. In 1620, the seminary of the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Jesus" title="Oratory of Jesus">Oratorians</a> under <a href="/wiki/Pierre_de_B%C3%A9rulle" title="Pierre de Bérulle">Pierre de Bérulle</a>—the first seminary in France—replaced the Benedictines. It was known as the seminary of Saint-Magloire. The relics of St. Magloire and his disciples were transferred to the hospital at the site of the <a href="/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Jacques-du-Haut-Pas" class="mw-redirect" title="Église Saint-Jacques-du-Haut-Pas">Église Saint-Jacques-du-Haut-Pas</a>, which became a monastery. The relics were buried secretly during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a> and were found in 1835, during the installation of a new high altar.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Magloire&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><i>Vita Sancti Maglorii</i>, a work of uncertain provenance written in Latin, details almost all of the knowledge of Maglorius. Folklorist <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_Duine&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="François Duine (page does not exist)">François Duine</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Duine" class="extiw" title="fr:François Duine">fr</a>&#93;</span> (1874–1924) called the work a masterpiece of ancient Breton literature. Scholars place its composition between the late 9th century and the mid-10th century. There is also the <i>Translatio Parisios</i>, which recounts the flight of <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9hon" title="Léhon">Lehonese</a> monks to Paris in the 10th century, and is the primary source for the foundation of their monastery in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Vita</i> is untranslated, but is accessible in the <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i> series.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Magloire&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Magloire is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> on 24 October.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
