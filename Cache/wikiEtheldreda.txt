<p><b>Æthelthryth</b> (or <b>Æðelþryð</b> or <b>Æþelðryþe</b>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;636</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;23 June 679 AD) was an <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_East_Angles" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the East Angles">East Anglian</a> princess, a <a href="/wiki/The_Fens" title="The Fens">Fenland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> queen and <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Ely" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Ely">Abbess of Ely</a>. She is an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> saint, and is also known as <b>Etheldreda</b> or <b>Audrey</b>, especially in religious contexts. She was a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Anna_of_East_Anglia" title="Anna of East Anglia">Anna</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia" title="Kingdom of East Anglia">East Anglia</a>, and her siblings were <a href="/wiki/Wendreda" title="Wendreda">Wendreda</a> and <a href="/wiki/Seaxburh_of_Ely" title="Seaxburh of Ely">Seaxburh of Ely</a>, both of whom eventually retired from secular life and founded <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbeys</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86thelthryth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Æthelthryth was probably born in <a href="/wiki/Exning" title="Exning">Exning</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Newmarket,_Suffolk" title="Newmarket, Suffolk">Newmarket</a> in Suffolk.  She was one of the four saintly daughters of <a href="/wiki/Anna_of_East_Anglia" title="Anna of East Anglia">Anna of East Anglia</a>, including <a href="/wiki/Wendreda" title="Wendreda">Wendreda</a> and <a href="/wiki/Seaxburh_of_Ely" title="Seaxburh of Ely">Seaxburh of Ely</a>, all of whom eventually retired from secular life and founded <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbeys</a>.
</p><p>Æthelthryth made an early first marriage in around 652 to Tondberct, chief or prince of the <a href="/wiki/Gyrwas" title="Gyrwas">South Gyrwe</a>. She managed to persuade her husband to respect her vow of perpetual virginity that she had made prior to their marriage. Upon his death in 655, she retired to the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Ely" title="Isle of Ely">Isle of Ely</a>, which she had received from Tondberct as a <a href="/wiki/Dower" title="Dower">morning gift</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Macpherson_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Macpherson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Æthelthryth was subsequently remarried for political reasons in 660, this time to <a href="/wiki/Ecgfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Ecgfrith of Northumbria">Ecgfrith of Northumbria</a>, who was fourteen or fifteen at the time. Shortly after his accession to the throne in 670, Æthelthryth wished to become a nun. This step possibly led to Ecgfrith's long quarrel with <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>, bishop of <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>, who was her spiritual counsellor. One account relates that while Ecgfrith initially agreed Æthelthryth should continue to remain a virgin, about 672 he appealed to Wilfrid for the enforcement of his marital rights as against Etheldreda's religious vocation. The bishop succeeded at first in persuading the king to consent that Etheldreda should live for some time in peace as a sister of the Coldingham nunnery, founded by his aunt, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86bbe_of_Coldingham" title="Æbbe of Coldingham">Æbbe of Coldingham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Macpherson_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Macpherson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Eventually, in light of the danger of being forcibly carried off by the king, Æthelthryth then fled back to the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Ely" title="Isle of Ely">Isle of Ely</a> with two nuns as companions.<sup id="cite_ref-Macpherson_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Macpherson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> They managed to evade capture, thanks in part to the rising of the <a href="/wiki/Tide" title="Tide">tide</a>.
</p><p>Another version of the legend related that she halted on the journey at 'Stow' and sheltered under a miraculously growing <a href="/wiki/Fraxinus" title="Fraxinus">ash tree</a> which came from her staff planted in the ground. Stow came to be known as 'St Etheldred's Stow', when a church was built to commemorate this event. It is more likely that this 'Stow' actually refers to <a href="/wiki/Stow_Fair,_Lincolnshire" title="Stow Fair, Lincolnshire">another fair</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> near <a href="/wiki/Threekingham" title="Threekingham">Threekingham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Ecgfrith later married Eormenburg and expelled Wilfrid from his kingdom in 678. According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i>, Æthelthryth founded a <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Ely,_Cambridgeshire" title="Ely, Cambridgeshire">Ely</a> in 673, which was later destroyed in the <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Danish</a> invasion of 870.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86thelthryth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_(Early_Saxon_period).svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_%28Early_Saxon_period%29.svg/220px-The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_%28Early_Saxon_period%29.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="221" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_%28Early_Saxon_period%29.svg/330px-The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_%28Early_Saxon_period%29.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_%28Early_Saxon_period%29.svg/440px-The_kingdom_of_East_Anglia_%28Early_Saxon_period%29.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="647" data-file-height="649" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia" title="Kingdom of East Anglia">Kingdom of East Anglia</a> (early Anglo-Saxon period)</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> told how after her death, Æthelthryth's bones were disinterred by her sister and successor, <a href="/wiki/Seaxburh_of_Ely" title="Seaxburh of Ely">Seaxburh</a> and that her <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">uncorrupted</a> body was later buried in a white, marble <a href="/wiki/Coffin" title="Coffin">coffin</a>. In 695, Seaxburh translated the remains of her sister Æthelthryth, who had been dead for sixteen years,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> from a common grave to the new church at Ely. The <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Eliensis" title="Liber Eliensis">Liber Eliensis</a></i> describes these events in detail.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> When her grave was opened, Æthelthryth's body was discovered to be uncorrupted and her coffin and clothes proved to possess miraculous powers. A <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> made of white marble was taken from the Roman ruins at <a href="/wiki/Grantchester" title="Grantchester">Grantchester</a>, which was found to be the right fit for Æthelthryth.  Seaxburh supervised the preparation of her sister's body, which was washed and wrapped in new robes before being reburied.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> She apparently oversaw the translation of her sister's remains without the supervision of her bishop, using her knowledge of procedures gained from her family's links with the <a href="/wiki/Faremoutiers_Abbey" title="Faremoutiers Abbey">Faremoutiers Abbey</a> as a basis for the ceremony.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Seaxburh, Æthelthryth's niece and her great-niece, both of whom were royal princesses, succeeded her as abbess of Ely.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_2">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86thelthryth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Etheldreda is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/June_23" title="June 23">23 June</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Etheldreda%27s_Church,_London" title="St Etheldreda&#39;s Church, London">St Etheldreda's Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ely_Place" title="Ely Place">Ely Place</a> in <a href="/wiki/Holborn" title="Holborn">Holborn</a> is dedicated to the saint.  It was originally part of the London palace of the <a href="/wiki/Bishops_of_Ely" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishops of Ely">bishops of Ely</a>. After the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>, part was briefly used by a Spanish ambassador for <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> worship. The chapel was purchased by the Catholic Church in 1874 and is one of the oldest churches in England to be in current use by the Catholic Church.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Etheldreda%27s_Church,_Hatfield" title="St Etheldreda&#39;s Church, Hatfield">St Etheldreda's Church, Hatfield</a> is 13th century and was originally Anglo-Saxon. It was named for St Etheldreda because it was adjacent to a palace of the Bishops of Ely who held her as their patron saint.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Etheldreda%27s_Church,_Ely" title="St Etheldreda&#39;s Church, Ely">St Etheldreda's</a> is a Roman Catholic parish church in Ely, Cambridgeshire. It is part of the Diocese of East Anglia within the Province of Westminster. The church contains the shrine and relics of Æthelthryth, including her hand.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg/150px-S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg/225px-S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg/300px-S95EtheldredaShrineEly.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1152" data-file-height="2048" /></a><figcaption>Site of shrine in <a href="/wiki/Ely_Cathedral" title="Ely Cathedral">Ely Cathedral</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>St Etheldreda's Church in <a href="/wiki/White_Notley" title="White Notley">White Notley</a>, Essex, is a Church of England parish church, of Anglo-Saxon construction, built on the site of a Roman temple, with a large quantity of Roman brick in its fabric. The church has a small Mediaeval English stained-glass window, depicting St Etheldreda, which is set in a stone frame made from a very early Insular Christian Roman Chi Rho grave marker.
</p><p>The church of St Etheldreda  <a href="/wiki/Histon" title="Histon">Histon</a> was demolished, it is commemorated in a stained glass window.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The common version of Æthelthryth's name was St Audrey, which is the origin of the word <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tawdry" class="extiw" title="wikt:tawdry">tawdry</a></i>, which derived from the fact that her admirers bought modestly concealing <a href="/wiki/Lace" title="Lace">lace</a> goods at an annual <a href="/wiki/Fair" title="Fair">fair</a> held in her name in Ely. By the 17th century, this lacework had become seen as old-fashioned, <a href="/wiki/Vanity" title="Vanity">vain</a>, or cheap and of poor quality, at a time when the <a href="/wiki/Puritan" class="mw-redirect" title="Puritan">Puritans</a> of eastern England disdained <a href="/wiki/Ornament_(art)" title="Ornament (art)">ornamental</a> dress.<sup id="cite_ref-waite_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-waite-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%86thelthryth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are a number of accounts of Æthelthryth's life in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Old_English" title="Old English">Old English</a>, <a href="/wiki/Old_French" title="Old French">Old French</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Middle_English" title="Middle English">Middle English</a>. According to Jocelyn Wogan-Browne, "more medieval vernacular lives [about Æthelthryth] were composed in England than any other native female saint".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Æthelthryth appears in <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_History_of_the_English_People" title="Ecclesiastical History of the English People">Ecclesiastical History of the English People</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfric_of_Eynsham" title="Ælfric of Eynsham">Ælfric</a>'s <i>Lives of Saints</i>, <a href="/wiki/Goscelin" title="Goscelin">Goscelin of Saint-Bertin</a>'s <i>Lives of Female Saints</i>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Eliensis" title="Liber Eliensis">Liber Eliensis</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Marie_de_France" title="Marie de France">Marie de France</a>'s <i>La vie seinte Audree</i>, the <i><a href="/wiki/South_English_Legendary" class="mw-redirect" title="South English Legendary">South English Legendary</a></i>, and a <a href="/wiki/Middle_English" title="Middle English">Middle English</a> life in BL Cotton Faustina B.iii, among others. A modern fictional account has been written by <a href="/wiki/Moyra_Caldecott" title="Moyra Caldecott">Moyra Caldecott</a>.
</p>
