<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Anastasia the Patrician</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">6th Century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">6th Century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodoxy">Oriental Orthodoxy</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">10 March<br />26 <a href="/wiki/Tobi_(month)" title="Tobi (month)">Tobi</a> (<a href="/wiki/Coptic_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Christianity">Coptic Christianity</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-coptic_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coptic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr></tbody></table>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg/220px-Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="155" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg/330px-Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg/440px-Theodora_mosaik_ravenna.jpg 2x" data-file-width="707" data-file-height="497" /></a><figcaption>Empress Theodora and attendants.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint <b>Anastasia the Patrician</b> (<i>Anastasia Patricia</i>; fl. 576) was a Byzantine courtier and later saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was a <a href="/wiki/Lady-in-waiting" title="Lady-in-waiting">lady-in-waiting</a> to the Byzantine empress <a href="/wiki/Theodora_(6th_century)" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodora (6th century)">Theodora</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a>, Theodora's husband, may have pursued her, as Theodora grew jealous of her.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Anastasia, to avoid any trouble, left for <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> in <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She arrived at a place called <a href="/wiki/Pempton" title="Pempton">Pempton</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, where she founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> which would later be named after her. She lived with monastic discipline and wove cloth to support herself.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following the death of Theodora in 548, Justinian attempted to get Anastasia to return to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, to no avail.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Instead, Anastasia left for <a href="/wiki/Scetis" class="mw-redirect" title="Scetis">Scetis</a>, looking for help from Abba Daniel, <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> To safeguard Anastasia, he let her move into a <i>laura</i> or monastery cell 18 miles from Scetis in the desert, and dress as a (male) <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a><sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and take up the life of a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> at a time when this was only permitted of men. He visited her every week and ensured that one of his disciples supplied her with jugs of water.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Anastasia dwelt in seclusion for twenty-eight years.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 576,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> aware of her approaching death, she wrote several words for Abba Daniel on a piece of broken pottery and placed it at the entrance to the cave. The disciple found an <a href="/wiki/Ostracon" title="Ostracon">ostracon</a> with the words "Bring the spades and come here." When Daniel heard this, he knew Anastasia was near death.<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He went to visit her with his disciple and to give her <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">communion</a> and hear her last words.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Daniel revealed the full details of her story to his disciple after her death.
</p><p>Her story survives in one recension of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Copto-Arabic_Synaxarion&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Copto-Arabic Synaxarion (page does not exist)">Copto-Arabic Synaxarion</a> and by a tale of Daniel of Scetis. Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 10 March<sup id="cite_ref-Swan_2001_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swan_2001-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, and on 26 <a href="/wiki/Month_of_Tobi" class="mw-redirect" title="Month of Tobi">Tobi</a> in the calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-coptic_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coptic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the date of her death given in the Ethiopic <i>Life of Daniel of Scetis</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
