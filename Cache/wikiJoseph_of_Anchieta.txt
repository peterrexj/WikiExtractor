<p><b>José de Anchieta y Díaz de Clavijo</b> (Joseph of Anchieta) (19 March 1534&#160;– 9 June 1597) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> to the Portuguese <a href="/wiki/Colonial_Brazil" title="Colonial Brazil">colony of Brazil</a> in the second half of the 16th century. A highly influential figure in Brazil's history in the first century after its European discovery, Anchieta was one of the founders of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a> in 1554 and of <a href="/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro" title="Rio de Janeiro">Rio de Janeiro</a> in 1565. He is the first playwright, the first <a href="/wiki/Linguist" class="mw-redirect" title="Linguist">grammarian</a> and the first poet born in the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>, and the father of <a href="/wiki/Brazilian_literature" title="Brazilian literature">Brazilian literature</a>. Anchieta was also involved in the religious instruction and conversion to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> faith of the <a href="/wiki/Native_American_(Americas)" class="mw-redirect" title="Native American (Americas)">Indian</a> population. His efforts along with those of another Jesuit missionary, <a href="/wiki/Manuel_da_N%C3%B3brega" title="Manuel da Nóbrega">Manuel da Nóbrega</a>, at Indian pacification were crucial to the establishment of stable colonial settlements in the colony.
</p><p>With his book <i>Arte de gramática da lingoa mais usada na costa do Brasil</i> (1595, as Ioseph de Anchieta), Anchieta became the first person to provide an <a href="/wiki/Orthography" title="Orthography">orthography</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Old_Tupi" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Tupi">Old Tupi</a> language most commonly spoken by the indigenous people of Brazil.
</p><p>Anchieta is commonly known as "the Apostle of Brazil". He was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> on 3 April 2014. He was the second native of the Canary Islands, after <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Saint_Joseph_Betancur" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter of Saint Joseph Betancur">Peter of Saint Joseph Betancur</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> also a missionary to Latin America, declared a saint by the Catholic Church. Anchieta is also considered the third saint of Brazil.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anchieta was born on 19 March 1534, in <a href="/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" title="San Cristóbal de La Laguna">San Cristóbal de La Laguna</a> on <a href="/wiki/Tenerife" title="Tenerife">Tenerife</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>, Spain, to a wealthy family.<sup id="cite_ref-sladky_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sladky-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was baptized on 7 April 1534 in the Parish of Our Lady of Remedies (now <a href="/wiki/La_Laguna_Cathedral" title="La Laguna Cathedral">La Laguna Cathedral</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He lived in the building now known as <a href="/wiki/Casa_Anchieta" title="Casa Anchieta">Casa Anchieta</a> in La Laguna until he was 14 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His father, Juan López de Anchieta y Zelayaran, was a landowner from Urrestilla, in the <a href="/wiki/Basque_Country_(historical_territory)" class="mw-redirect" title="Basque Country (historical territory)">Basque Country</a>, who had escaped to Tenerife in 1525 after participating in an unsuccessful <a href="/wiki/Rebellion" title="Rebellion">rebellion</a> against the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V</a>. Through him, Anchieta was related to <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a>, founder of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sladky_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sladky-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His mother was Mencia Díaz de Clavijo y Llarena, a descendant of the conquerors of Tenerife. Mencia was the daughter of Sebastián de Llarena, a <a href="/wiki/Converso" title="Converso">Jew who had converted to Christianity</a>, from the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile" title="Kingdom of Castile">kingdom of Castile</a>.
</p><p>When he was 14 years old, Anchieta went to study in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> at the Royal College of Arts in <a href="/wiki/Coimbra" title="Coimbra">Coimbra</a>. He was intensely religious and felt he had a vocation to the priesthood. He sought admission to the Jesuit College of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Coimbra" title="University of Coimbra">University of Coimbra</a> and was accepted into the Jesuits on 1 May 1551, at the age of 17.<sup id="cite_ref-sladky_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sladky-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> While he was a novice, he nearly ruined his health by his excessive austerity, causing an injury to the spine that made him almost a hunchback.<sup id="cite_ref-campbell_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-campbell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He learned to write <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> as well as his mother tongue.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_in_Brazil">Missionary in Brazil</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Missionary in Brazil">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta,_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta%2C_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg/200px-Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta%2C_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="277" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta%2C_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg/300px-Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta%2C_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta%2C_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg/400px-Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta%2C_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2425" data-file-height="3364" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Benedito_Calixto_de_Jesus_-_Retrato_do_Padre_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta,_Acervo_do_Museu_Paulista_da_USP.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>José de Anchieta, by painter <a href="/wiki/Benedito_Calixto" title="Benedito Calixto">Benedito Calixto</a></div></div></div>

<p>In 1553, the Jesuits included Anchieta among the third group of their members sent to the Portuguese colony of Brazil, believing that the climate would improve his health.<sup id="cite_ref-sladky_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sladky-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-campbell_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-campbell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> After a perilous journey and a <a href="/wiki/Shipwreck" title="Shipwreck">shipwreck</a>, Anchieta and his small group arrived in <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente,_S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Vicente, São Paulo">São Vicente</a>, the first village that had been founded in Brazil in 1532. There he made his first contact with the Tapuia Indians living in the region.
</p><p>In late 1553, <a href="/wiki/Manuel_da_N%C3%B3brega" title="Manuel da Nóbrega">Manuel da Nóbrega</a>, the first <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Provincial</a> of the Jesuits in Brazil, sent 13 Jesuits including Anchieta to climb the <a href="/wiki/Serra_do_Mar" title="Serra do Mar">Serra do Mar</a> to a <a href="/wiki/Plateau" title="Plateau">plateau</a> along the <a href="/wiki/Tiet%C3%AA_River" title="Tietê River">Tietê River</a> that the Indians called <i>piratininga</i> (from <a href="/wiki/Tupi_language" title="Tupi language">Tupi</a> <i>pira</i> "fish" + <i>tininga</i> "drying" – according to Anchieta, more than 12,000 fish could be found drying along the Tietê river <a href="/wiki/Floodplain" title="Floodplain">floodplains</a> after one of its customary floods). There the Jesuits established a small missionary settlement and celebrated Mass for the first time on 25 January 1554, date of the <a href="/wiki/Conversion_of_Saint_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Conversion of Saint Paul">conversion of Saint Paul</a>, according to tradition. That date is now celebrated as the founding of São Paulo.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Anchieta and his Jesuit colleagues began their efforts to instruct the native people in the rudiments of Christianity and convert them, while also introducing basic education in other subjects. He taught Latin to the Indians, began to learn their language, <a href="/wiki/Old_Tupi" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Tupi">Old Tupi</a>, and started compiling a <a href="/wiki/Dictionary" title="Dictionary">dictionary</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Grammar" title="Grammar">grammar</a>. Their mission settlement, the <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A1tio_do_Col%C3%A9gio" title="Pátio do Colégio">Jesuit College of São Paulo of Piratininga</a>, soon developed into a small population center.
</p><p>Anchieta and Nóbrega had long opposed the way the Portuguese colonists were treating the Indians and had a serious conflict about it with <a href="/w/index.php?title=Duarte_da_Costa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Duarte da Costa (page does not exist)">Duarte da Costa</a>, who served as second Governor-General of Brazil from 1553 to 1558. They nevertheless supported the Portuguese against their French rivals in establishing claims to Brazil and welcomed the support of Portuguese authorities against the Huguenot Protestants whom the French at times welcomed to their settlements. In fact, the two Jesuits saw the French colony as a generally Protestant enterprise, ignoring its Catholic components and making no distinction between Lutherans and Calvinists.<sup id="cite_ref-nowell_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nowell-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Anchieta recognized that violence could be necessary to create the conditions for evangelizing the indigenous inhabitants and later praised the colony's third Governor General, <a href="/wiki/Mem_de_S%C3%A1" title="Mem de Sá">Mem de Sá</a> (1500–1572), for what he accomplished in killing large numbers of Amerindians.<sup id="cite_ref-alida_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alida-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Due to the systematic killings and ransacking of their villages by the Portuguese colonists and attempts at <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">enslaving</a> them, the Indian tribes along the coast of the present-day states of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a>, Rio de Janeiro and <a href="/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo" title="Espírito Santo">Espírito Santo</a> rebelled and formed an alliance, the <a href="/wiki/Tamoyo_Confederation" class="mw-redirect" title="Tamoyo Confederation">Tamoyo Confederation</a>, which soon allied themselves to the French colonists who had settled in <a href="/wiki/Guanabara_Bay" title="Guanabara Bay">Guanabara Bay</a> in 1555 under the command of Vice-Admiral <a href="/wiki/Nicolas_Durand_de_Villegaignon" title="Nicolas Durand de Villegaignon">Nicolas Durand de Villegaignon</a>. The conflict was brutal and at once international and inter-religious. In one instance the Portuguese hung ten Frenchmen in an attempt to intimidate their enemies into submission. In another in 1557, a Protestant named Jacques le Balleur was put to death and Anchieta, in some interpretations, helped the executioner carry out the sentence,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> though the facts are much disputed.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Tamoyo Confederation attacked São Paulo several times between 1562 and 1564 without success. Anchieta and Nóbrega initiated peace negotiations with the Tamoyos in the village of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Iperoig&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Iperoig (page does not exist)">Iperoig</a> in modern <a href="/wiki/Ubatuba" title="Ubatuba">Ubatuba</a> on the northern coast of São Paulo state. Anchieta's skill with the <a href="/wiki/Old_Tupi" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Tupi">Tupi language</a> was crucial in these efforts. After many incidents and the near massacre of Anchieta and Nóbrega by the Indians, they finally succeeded in gaining the Indians' confidence, and peace was established between the Tamoyo and <a href="/wiki/Tupiniquim" title="Tupiniquim">Tupiniquim</a> nations and the Portuguese.
</p><p>Portuguese-French hostilities were renewed when <a href="/wiki/Est%C3%A1cio_de_S%C3%A1" title="Estácio de Sá">Estácio de Sá</a>, a nephew of the new Governor-General of Brazil, Mem de Sá, was ordered to expel the French colonists. With the support and blessings of Anchieta and Nóbrega, he departed with an army from São Vicente and founded the ramparts of <a href="/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro" title="Rio de Janeiro">Rio de Janeiro</a> at the foot of <a href="/wiki/Sugarloaf_Mountain,_Rio_de_Janeiro" class="mw-redirect" title="Sugarloaf Mountain, Rio de Janeiro">Pão de Açúcar</a>, in 1565. Anchieta was with him and participated in a number of battles between the Portuguese and the French, each side supported by their Indian allies. He acted as a surgeon and <a href="/wiki/Interpreting" class="mw-redirect" title="Interpreting">interpreter</a>. He was also responsible for reporting back to the governor-general's headquarters in <a href="/wiki/Salvador,_Brazil" class="mw-redirect" title="Salvador, Brazil">Salvador</a>, Bahia, and participated in the final victorious battle against the French in 1567.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg/250px-Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="333" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg/375px-Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg/500px-Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Estatua_de_Padre_Anchieta_em_Santos.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Statue of Father Anchieta in <a href="/wiki/Santos_(S%C3%A3o_Paulo)" class="mw-redirect" title="Santos (São Paulo)">Santos</a>, Brazil</div></div></div></div>
<p>After the peace settlement, a Jesuit college was founded in Rio under the direction of Nóbrega. Anchieta was invited to remain and succeeded him upon his death in 1570. Despite his frailty and ill health, and the rigors of slow travel by foot and ship of the time, over the next ten years Anchieta traveled extensively between Rio de Janeiro, Bahia, Espírito Santo and São Paulo, consolidating the Jesuit mission in Brazil. In 1577 the fourth superior general of the Jesuits, <a href="/wiki/Everard_Mercurian" title="Everard Mercurian">Everard Mercurian</a>, appointed Anchieta <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">provincial superior</a> of the order's members in Brazil.
</p><p>As his health worsened, Anchieta requested relief from his duties in 1591. He died in Brazil on 9 June 1597, at <a href="/wiki/Anchieta,_Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo" title="Anchieta, Espírito Santo">Reritiba</a>, Espírito Santo, mourned by more than 3,000 Indians.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the tradition of Jesuits, Anchieta was a prolific rapporteur, communicating by letters to his superiors. His reports establish him as an ethnographer, though he focused on Amerindian behavior that did not follow European norms, such as their choice of marriage partners, cannibalism, and the role of sorcerers.<sup id="cite_ref-wasserman_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wasserman-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His detailed testimony with respect to cannibalism is often cited by anthropologists. He explained, for example, that the Amerindians "believe that true kinship comes from the side of the fathers, who are the agents, and ... that the mothers are nothing more than bags in which the children grow" and therefore treat the children of a captured female and a member of their tribe with respect but sometimes eat the children of a captured male and a female member of their own tribe.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> He detailed the practice of polygamy and, because it had produced dense networks of interrelations, advocated easing the church's <a href="/wiki/Consanguinity#Religious_and_traditional_law" title="Consanguinity">consanguinity</a> rules to allow all but brothers and sisters to marry.<sup id="cite_ref-alida_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alida-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anchieta was a pioneer in transcribing the <a href="/wiki/Old_Tupi" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Tupi">Old Tupi</a> language and authored the first published work on that language, a "pathbreaking" grammar, <i>Arte de gramática da língua mais usada na costa do Brasil</i>, written in 1555 and published in 1589.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> According to one assessment, "His grammar and dictionary still rank among the best ever produced of a Brazilian language, nearly 500 years later.... Anchieta was a dedicated linguist whose work can be considered the beginning of Amazonian linguistics (indeed it would not be stretching matters too far to call his work the beginning of linguistics in the Americas."<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His written works in the indigenous language span theology, religious instruction, theater and poetry.
</p><p>He was also a historian, author of a biography of Mem de Sá. Composed of hexameters, <i><a href="/wiki/De_Gestis_Meni_de_Saa" title="De Gestis Meni de Saa">De Gestis Meni de Saa</a></i> is the first epic about the Americas. It presents de Sá as "a Christian Ulysses determined to oust Satan" who "presides ... over hordes of demonic Amerindians, creatures devoted to dismembering bodies". With the arrival of the Jesuits, "the Cross expels demons" and "shamans lose preternatural power as they move from the wilderness into the civilized missions".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> That work of history was one of his two major poems. The other was <i>De Beata Virgine Dei Matre</i>, a poem to the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_the_mother_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary, the mother of Jesus">Virgin Mary</a>. Tradition holds that Anchieta composed it while in captivity at Iperoig in 1563 by writing verses in the wet sand of the beach and memorizing each day's lines so that upon his release he could write its 4,900 verses on paper in their entirety.<sup id="cite_ref-companion_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-companion-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:228px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta_(zincogravura)_1807.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/90/Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta_%28zincogravura%29_1807.jpg" decoding="async" width="226" height="279" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="226" data-file-height="279" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption">Anchieta in an 1807 engraving.</div></div></div>
<p>His dramas, written in a combination of Tupi, Portuguese, Spanish, and Latin, were not meant for the stage but for performance by local amateurs in village squares and churchyards. They were traditional in form, written in verse with five-line stanzas, a literary form known as the <i>auto</i>, a Portuguese devotional drama, following the tradition the Jesuits had developed of using the theater first in classrooms and then for popular instruction. Casts were all male, both native and European, and both groups were meant to learn from the dramas' instruction in Christian morals. They were written for special occasions like a saint's feast day or to mark the arrival of relics in the colony. Scholars have noted that they contain considerable "contextual information", that is references to local events such as village rivalries. For example, Amerindian cannibalism is juxtaposed with the roasting of <a href="/wiki/Lawrence_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Lawrence of Rome">Lawrence of Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-wasserman_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wasserman-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Few of his plays survive, but those that do have been praised, despite being crafted for a local audience with a didactic purpose, for their "remarkable feeling for spectacle, calling for the use of body paint, native costumes, song and dance, fights, torches, and processions".<sup id="cite_ref-companion_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-companion-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> A performance might even call for cannon fire from a nearby ship, though the plays were typically "short on action and long on explanations of doctrine" and characters fall clearly into positive and negative types.<sup id="cite_ref-wasserman_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wasserman-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Anchieta's "auto da pregação universel" of 1567 and published in 1672 is the first dramatic text in Brazilian letters.<sup id="cite_ref-companion_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-companion-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a keen <a href="/wiki/Natural_history" title="Natural history">naturalist</a>, he described several new plants and animals among the novelties of Brazil's wildlife and <a href="/wiki/Geography" title="Geography">geography</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Quattrocchi_2000_131_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quattrocchi_2000_131-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His lucid and detailed reports are still important for understanding the beliefs, manners, and customs of the native peoples and European settlers of the sixteenth century.
</p><p>He was also an excellent surgeon and physician.
</p><p>His manuscripts were gathered from archives in Portugal and Brazil in the 1730s as part of the process for his beatification and deposited in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-wasserman_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wasserman-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His works have been published as <i>Cartas, Informações, Fragmentos Históricos e Sermões</i> (Letters, Reports, Historical Fragments and Sermons).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>José de Anchieta is celebrated as the founder of Brazilian letters and, with Nóbrega, Apostle of Brazil.<sup id="cite_ref-campbell_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-campbell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He has given his name to two cities, <a href="/wiki/Anchieta,_ES,_Brazil" class="mw-redirect" title="Anchieta, ES, Brazil">Anchieta</a>, in the State of <a href="/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo" title="Espírito Santo">Espírito Santo</a> (formerly called Reritiba, the place where he died), and <a href="/wiki/Anchieta,_Santa_Catarina" title="Anchieta, Santa Catarina">Anchieta</a>, in the state of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catarina_(state)" title="Santa Catarina (state)">Santa Catarina</a>, as well as many other places, roads, institutions, hospitals, and schools. The French botanist <a href="/wiki/A.St.-Hil." class="mw-redirect" title="A.St.-Hil.">A.St.-Hil.</a>, who explored Brasil in the 19th century, named a climbing vine of the <a href="/wiki/Violaceae" title="Violaceae">Violaceae</a> family for the late Jesuit: <i><a href="/wiki/Anchietea" title="Anchietea">Anchietea</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Quattrocchi_2000_131_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quattrocchi_2000_131-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1965, the Spanish postal service issued a stamp with the image of Anchieta, in a series called "Los Forjadores de América".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Ney_Latorraca" title="Ney Latorraca">Ney Latorraca</a> starred in the Brazilian biographical film, <i>Anchieta, José do Brasil</i>, which was released in 1977.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg/200px-Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="280" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg/300px-Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg/400px-Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3267" data-file-height="4575" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Escultura_Padre_Anchieta_02.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Monument_to_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta" title="Monument to José de Anchieta">Monument to José de Anchieta</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" title="San Cristóbal de La Laguna">San Cristóbal de La Laguna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tenerife" title="Tenerife">Tenerife</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>When <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> in 1980, Anchieta acquired the title "Blessed José de Anchieta." <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> announced his canonization as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> on 3 April 2014. The announcement was first communicated to three priests from the Canary Islands<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> (hometown of Anchieta) who attended the Mass of the Pope in his residence in <a href="/wiki/Domus_Sanctae_Marthae" title="Domus Sanctae Marthae">Santa Marta</a>, who communicated it to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of San Cristóbal de La Laguna">Bishop of Tenerife</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bernardo_%C3%81lvarez_Afonso" title="Bernardo Álvarez Afonso">Bernardo Álvarez Afonso</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He used a process known as <a href="/wiki/Equivalent_canonization" title="Equivalent canonization">equivalent canonization</a> that dispenses with the standard judicial procedures and ceremonies in the case of someone long venerated.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Anchieta was the first Spaniard canonized by Pope Francis.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During and after his life, José de Anchieta was considered almost a <a href="/wiki/Supernatural" title="Supernatural">supernatural</a> being. Many legends formed around him, such as that he once preached to and calmed an attacking <a href="/wiki/Jaguar" title="Jaguar">jaguar</a>. To this day, a popular devotion holds that praying to Anchieta protects against animal attacks.
</p><p>José de Anchieta is highly revered in the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>. A bronze statue by Brazilian artist <a href="/wiki/Bruno_Giorgi" title="Bruno Giorgi">Bruno Giorgi</a> in the city of San Cristóbal de La Laguna depicts José de Anchieta departing for Portugal. It was a gift from the <a href="/wiki/Government_of_Brazil" class="mw-redirect" title="Government of Brazil">Government of Brazil</a> to Anchieta's hometown, where a wooden image of him is also venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_La_Laguna" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of La Laguna">Cathedral of La Laguna</a> and carried in procession through the streets every 9 June. In the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Candelaria" title="Basilica of Candelaria">Basilica of Our Lady of Candelaria</a> (patron saint of the Canary Islands), there is a painting of José de Anchieta founding the city of São Paulo. In 1997, a biographical comic book was published.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>José de Anchieta is the patron saint and model of catechists. He was also declared by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> as one of the thirteen Intercessors of the <a href="/wiki/World_Youth_Day_2013" title="World Youth Day 2013">World Youth Day 2013</a>, held in <a href="/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro" title="Rio de Janeiro">Rio de Janeiro</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In April 2015 he was declared by the <a href="/wiki/National_Conference_of_Bishops_of_Brazil" class="mw-redirect" title="National Conference of Bishops of Brazil">National Conference of Bishops of Brazil</a> a copatron of Brazil,<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> whose patron saint is <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Aparecida" title="Our Lady of Aparecida">Our Lady of Aparecida</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrines_in_his_honor">Shrines in his honor</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Shrines in his honor">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The main shrines dedicated to Saint José de Anchieta in Brazil and the Canary Islands are those that are directly related to his life:
</p>
<ul><li><span class="flagicon"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/9a/Flag_of_Spain.svg/23px-Flag_of_Spain.svg.png" decoding="async" width="23" height="15" class="thumbborder" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/9a/Flag_of_Spain.svg/35px-Flag_of_Spain.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/9a/Flag_of_Spain.svg/45px-Flag_of_Spain.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="750" data-file-height="500" />&#160;</span><a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> (<a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>): In the city of <a href="/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" title="San Cristóbal de La Laguna">San Cristóbal de La Laguna</a>, his place of birth, the main diocesan shrine is the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of San Cristóbal de La Laguna">Cathedral of San Cristóbal de La Laguna</a>, where he was baptized in 1534 and where his image is venerated.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Each 9 June in this city flowers are strewn at the large bronze statue of the saint<sup id="cite_ref-Fiesta_de_San_José_de_Anchieta_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fiesta_de_San_José_de_Anchieta-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> and a solemn Mass is held in the cathedral with the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tenerife" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tenerife">Bishop of Tenerife</a> presiding, followed by a procession with his image through the streets to his birthplace, where flowers are again strewn.<sup id="cite_ref-Fiesta_de_San_José_de_Anchieta_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fiesta_de_San_José_de_Anchieta-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><span class="flagicon"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/0/05/Flag_of_Brazil.svg/22px-Flag_of_Brazil.svg.png" decoding="async" width="22" height="15" class="thumbborder" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/0/05/Flag_of_Brazil.svg/33px-Flag_of_Brazil.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/0/05/Flag_of_Brazil.svg/43px-Flag_of_Brazil.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="720" data-file-height="504" />&#160;</span><a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>: The <a href="/w/index.php?title=National_Shrine_of_San_Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="National Shrine of San José de Anchieta (page does not exist)">National Shrine of San José de Anchieta</a> is located in the town of <a href="/wiki/Anchieta,_Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo" title="Anchieta, Espírito Santo">Reritiba</a> (now called <i>Anchieta</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Esp%C3%ADrito_Santo" title="Espírito Santo">state of Espirito Santo</a>. The sanctuary has an important museum of sacred art and is built in the place where he lived the last years of his life and died.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> Here the national holiday dedicated to the saint is celebrated with pilgrimages of faithful from throughout Brazil. Following the declaration of Anchieta as copatron of Brazil in 2015, the church was declared a <i>National Shrine</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
