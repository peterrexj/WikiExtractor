					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">7th-century bishop and Catholic saint</div>

<a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Shrines</th><td class="infobox-data">Jouarre Abbey</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Agilbert</b> (<abbr title="floruit (&#39;flourished&#39;&#160;– known to have been active at a particular time or during a particular period)">fl.</abbr> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 650–680) was the second bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_West_Saxons" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the West Saxons">West Saxon</a> kingdom and later <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Paris">Bishop of Paris</a>. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> within the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, with his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> falling on 11 October.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The date and place of Agilbert's birth are unknown, but evidence suggests it took place between 610 and 620.<sup id="cite_ref-Hunter_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hunter-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Son of a <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustrian</a> noble named Betto, he was a first cousin of <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Audoin</a> and related to the <a href="/wiki/Faronids" class="mw-redirect" title="Faronids">Faronids</a> and <a href="/wiki/Agilolfings" title="Agilolfings">Agilolfings</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and less certainly to the <a href="/wiki/Merovingians" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingians">Merovingians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His name, the <a href="/wiki/Frankish_language" title="Frankish language">Frankish language</a> equivalent of Æthelberht, has been taken to suggest a link with the royal family of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kent" title="Kingdom of Kent">Kingdom of Kent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agilbert was consecrated as a bishop in <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a> before he travelled to <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain" title="Great Britain">Britain</a>.  He arrived in the West Saxon kingdom after the return to power of King <a href="/wiki/Cenwalh_of_Wessex" title="Cenwalh of Wessex">Cenwalh of Wessex</a>, who had been driven out by <a href="/wiki/Penda_of_Mercia" title="Penda of Mercia">Penda of Mercia</a>, either in the late 640s or 650s. He was appointed to succeed <a href="/wiki/Birinus" title="Birinus">Birinus</a> (also later canonised, and attributed with conversion of Wessex to Christianity) as bishop of the West Saxons, or the Wessex folk, who following their seizure of part of Christian <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a> set up the first Wessex see as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Dorchester</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a>.  Nothing remains above the surface of the Saxon cathedral, succeeded in the faith by Norman <a href="/wiki/Dorchester_Abbey" title="Dorchester Abbey">Dorchester Abbey</a> church which has decorative memorials to the two early bishops.  Agilbert, according to <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum</a></i>, had "spent a long time in Ireland for the purpose of studying the Scriptures".<sup id="cite_ref-HE_III_7_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HE_III_7-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His appointment was due to Cenwalh.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby48_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby48-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, it appears that Agilbert did not speak Old English, and it is said that his see was divided in two, with <a href="/wiki/Wine_(bishop)" title="Wine (bishop)">Wine</a> being given half, because King Cenwalh "tired of his barbarous speech",<sup id="cite_ref-HE_III_7_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HE_III_7-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> although this may be mistaken.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> This insult supposedly led to Agilbert's resignation. He then travelled north to <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>, where he ordained <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was present at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a> in 664, where he led the pro-Roman party, but he had the young Wilfrid speak on his behalf.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg/220px-Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="252" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg/330px-Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg/440px-Charter_of_Clotilde.jpg 2x" data-file-width="671" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>The charter of Clotilde, 10 March 673, endowing the monastery of Bruyères-le-Châtel; witnessed by Agilbert, this is his last appearance in year-dated records</figcaption></figure>
<p>Returning to Francia, Agilbert later took part in Wilfrid's consecration as a bishop at <a href="/wiki/Compi%C3%A8gne" title="Compiègne">Compiègne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Agilbert became bishop of Paris between 666 and 668, and hosted <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore of Tarsus</a>. He was later invited to return by Cenwalh, to become <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>, but sent his nephew <a href="/wiki/Leuthhere" class="mw-redirect" title="Leuthhere">Leuthhere</a> in his place.<sup id="cite_ref-Fouracre;_Riché._13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fouracre;_Riché.-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One modern historian, D. P. Kirby, is unsure if Agilbert actually went to Northumbria after being expelled from Dorchester, suggesting it is just as likely that he went directly to the continent.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby48_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby48-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agilbert died at some time after 10 March 673, on which date he witnessed <a href="/wiki/Clotilde_(floruit_673)" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotilde (floruit 673)">Clotilde</a>'s foundation charter for the Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Bruy%C3%A8res-le-Ch%C3%A2tel" title="Bruyères-le-Châtel">Bruyères-le-Châtel</a>, and probably between 679 and 690. He was buried at <a href="/wiki/Jouarre_Abbey" title="Jouarre Abbey">Jouarre Abbey</a> where his sister Theodechildis was abbess. His fine sculpted <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> can be seen there in the crypts, as can that of his sister.<sup id="cite_ref-Fouracre;_Riché._13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fouracre;_Riché.-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
