<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Renate" redirects here. For the Italian municipality, see <a href="/wiki/Renate,_Lombardy" title="Renate, Lombardy">Renate, Lombardy</a>.</div>
<p><b>Renatus</b> is a first name of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> origin which means "<a href="/wiki/Born_again" title="Born again">born again</a>" (natus = born). In <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a> it exists in masculine and feminine forms: <b>Renato</b> and <b>Renata</b>. In French they have been translated to <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9" title="René">René</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e" title="Renée">Renée</a>. <b>Renata</b> is a common female name in the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a>. The feminine <b>Renate</b> is common in German, <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_language" title="Norwegian language">Norwegian</a>. In Russia the names <b>Renat</b> (Russian: <span lang="ru">Ренат</span>) (usually as Rinat) and <b>Renata</b> (Russian: <span lang="ru">Рената</span>) are widespread among the <a href="/wiki/Tatar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tatar">Tatar</a> population.
</p><p>The name has a spiritual meaning, i.e., to be <a href="/wiki/Born_again" title="Born again">born again</a> with <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>, i.e., from water and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit" title="Holy Spirit">Holy Spirit</a>. It was extensively adopted by early Christians in <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">ancient Rome</a>, due to the importance of baptism. The <a href="/wiki/Onomastic" class="mw-redirect" title="Onomastic">onomastic</a> is <a href="/wiki/Saint_Renatus" title="Saint Renatus">Saint Renatus</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sorrento" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sorrento">Bishop of Sorrento</a> in the 5th century, which is celebrated on 6 October.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persian</a> <a href="/wiki/Mithraism" title="Mithraism">Mithraism</a>, which spread widely in the West as a religion of the soldiers and officials under the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>, persons initiated into its mysteries were designated <i>renatus</i> (with the meaning of <i><a href="/wiki/Regeneration_(theology)" title="Regeneration (theology)">regenerated</a></i>).
</p><p>With a completely different origin, the name "Renata" is a New Zealand <a href="/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language" title="Māori language">Maori</a> transliteration of the name "Leonard", and is a male's name.
</p>
