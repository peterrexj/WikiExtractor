<p><b>Renatus</b> is a first name of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> origin which means "<a href="/wiki/Born_again" title="Born again">born again</a>" (natus = born).
</p><p>The name has a spiritual meaning, i.e., to be <a href="/wiki/Born_again" title="Born again">born again</a> with <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>, i.e., from water and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit" title="Holy Spirit">Holy Spirit</a>. It was extensively adopted by early Christians in <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">ancient Rome</a>, due to the importance of baptism. The <a href="/wiki/Onomastic" class="mw-redirect" title="Onomastic">onomastic</a> is <a href="/wiki/Saint_Renatus" title="Saint Renatus">Saint Renatus</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sorrento" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sorrento">Bishop of Sorrento</a> in the 5th century, who is celebrated on 6 October.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persian</a> <a href="/wiki/Mithraism" title="Mithraism">Mithraism</a>, which spread widely in the West as a religion of the soldiers and officials under the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>, persons initiated into its mysteries were designated <i>renatus</i> (with the meaning of <i><a href="/wiki/Regeneration_(theology)" title="Regeneration (theology)">regenerated</a></i>).
</p><p>Notable people with this forename include:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Renatus_Cartesius" class="mw-redirect" title="Renatus Cartesius">Renatus Cartesius</a> (1596–1650), also known as René Descartes, French philosopher, mathematician, scientist and writer</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Publius_Flavius_Vegetius_Renatus" class="mw-redirect" title="Publius Flavius Vegetius Renatus">Publius Flavius Vegetius Renatus</a>, Later Roman Empire writer (4th century)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Renatus_Profuturus_Frigeridus" title="Renatus Profuturus Frigeridus">Renatus Profuturus Frigeridus</a>, historian (5th century)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Renatus_of_Ch%C3%A2lon" class="mw-redirect" title="Renatus of Châlon">Renatus of Châlon</a> (1519–1544), Prince of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Orange" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Orange">House of Orange</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Renatus_Harris" title="Renatus Harris">Renatus Harris</a> (c. 1652–1724), English master organ maker</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Derived_names">Derived names</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Renatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Derived names">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a> it exists in masculine and feminine forms: <a href="/wiki/Renato" title="Renato">Renato</a> and <a href="/wiki/Renata" title="Renata">Renata</a>.
</p><p>In French they have been translated to <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9" title="René">René</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9e" title="Renée">Renée</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Renata" title="Renata">Renata</a> is a common female name in the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a>.
</p><p>The feminine <a href="/wiki/Renate" title="Renate">Renate</a> is common in German, <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norwegian_language" title="Norwegian language">Norwegian</a>.
</p><p>In Russia the names <a href="/wiki/Renat" title="Renat">Renat</a> (Russian: <span lang="ru">Ренат</span>) (usually as <a href="/wiki/Rinat" title="Rinat">Rinat</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Renata" title="Renata">Renata</a> (Russian: <span lang="ru">Рената</span>) are widespread among the <a href="/wiki/Tatar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tatar">Tatar</a> population.
</p>
