<p><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <b>Hermann of Reichenau</b> or <b>Herman the Cripple</b> (18 July 1013<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>&#8211; 24 September 1054), also known by <a href="#Names">other names</a>,  was an 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monk" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monk">Benedictine monk</a> and scholar. He composed works on <a href="/wiki/Chronicle" title="Chronicle">history</a>, <a href="/wiki/Music_theory" title="Music theory">music theory</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mathematics" title="Mathematics">mathematics</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Astronomy" title="Astronomy">astronomy</a>, as well as many <a href="/wiki/Hymns" class="mw-redirect" title="Hymns">hymns</a>. He has traditionally been credited with the composition of "<a href="/wiki/Salve_Regina" title="Salve Regina">Salve Regina</a>", "<a href="/wiki/Veni_Sancte_Spiritus" title="Veni Sancte Spiritus">Veni Sancte Spiritus</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-eb9_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eb9-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and "<a href="/wiki/Alma_Redemptoris_Mater" title="Alma Redemptoris Mater">Alma Redemptoris Mater</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> although these attributions are sometimes questioned. His <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> were confirmed by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> in 1863.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Names">Names</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_of_Reichenau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Names">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hermann's name is sometimes <a href="/wiki/Anglicization" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglicization">anglicized</a> as <a href="/wiki/Herman_(name)" title="Herman (name)">Herman</a> or <a href="/wiki/Romanization" title="Romanization">Latinized</a> as Hermannus; it sometimes also appears in the older form Heriman. He is sometimes distinguished as Hermann of Vöhringen (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Hermannus de Voringen</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Hermann von Vöhringen</i>) from his birthplace. He is better known as Hermann of Reichenau (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Hermannus Augiensis</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Hermann von Reichenau</i>) from the location of his monastery on <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Island" title="Reichenau Island">Reichenau Island</a> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Augia</i>) in <a href="/wiki/Lake_Constance" title="Lake Constance">Lake Constance</a>. He was traditionally distinguished in Latin as <i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Hermannus Contractus</i></span></i><sup id="cite_ref-eb9_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-eb9-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Hermann Contract</i>), which appears in English as "Hermann the Lame" (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Hermann der Lahme</i>) or "Hermann the Cripple" (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Hermann le Contrefait</i>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_of_Reichenau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hermann was a son of the <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">Count</a> of <a href="/wiki/Altshausen" title="Altshausen">Altshausen</a>. He was disabled due to a <a href="/wiki/Paralysis" title="Paralysis">paralytic</a> disease from early childhood. He was born on 18 July 1013 with a <a href="/wiki/Cleft_palate" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleft palate">cleft palate</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cerebral_palsy" title="Cerebral palsy">cerebral palsy</a> and is said to have had <a href="/wiki/Spina_bifida" title="Spina bifida">spina bifida</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Based on the evidence, however, more recent scholarship indicates Hermann possibly had either <a href="/wiki/Amyotrophic_lateral_sclerosis" class="mw-redirect" title="Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis">amyotrophic lateral sclerosis</a> or <a href="/wiki/Spinal_muscular_atrophy" title="Spinal muscular atrophy">spinal muscular atrophy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  As a result, he had great difficulty moving and <a href="/wiki/Speech_impediment" class="mw-redirect" title="Speech impediment">could hardly speak</a>.  At seven, he was placed in a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery by his parents who could no longer look after him.
</p><p>He grew up in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Reichenau" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Reichenau">Abbey of Reichenau</a>, an island on <a href="/wiki/Lake_Constance" title="Lake Constance">Lake Constance</a> in Germany. He learned from the monks and developed a keen interest in both <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and the world around him. At twenty, Hermann entered their order as a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monk" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monk">Benedictine monk</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> becoming literate in several languages (including <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>) and contributing to all four arts of the <a href="/wiki/Quadrivium" title="Quadrivium">quadrivium</a>.
</p><p>He wrote about history, <a href="/wiki/Mathematics" title="Mathematics">mathematics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Astronomy" title="Astronomy">astronomy</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He wrote a <a href="/wiki/Treatise" title="Treatise">treatise</a> on the science of <a href="/wiki/Music" title="Music">music</a>, several works on <a href="/wiki/Geometry" title="Geometry">geometry</a> and <a href="/wiki/Arithmetic" title="Arithmetic">arithmetics</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Astronomy" title="Astronomy">astronomical</a> treatises including instructions for the construction of an <a href="/wiki/Astrolabe" title="Astrolabe">astrolabe</a> which caused him to sometimes be credited as its inventor. As an historian, he wrote a detailed <a href="/wiki/Chronicle" title="Chronicle">chronicle</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Birth_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Birth of Christ">birth of Christ</a> to his own present day, ordering them after the reckoning of the <a href="/wiki/Christian_era" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian era">Christian era</a>. It was later extended by his pupil <a href="/wiki/Berthold_of_Reichenau" title="Berthold of Reichenau">Berthold of Reichenau</a>.
</p><p>He was a renowned religious poet and <a href="/wiki/Music" title="Music">musical</a> composer. Among his surviving works are <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">officia</a> for <a href="/wiki/St._Afra" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Afra">St. Afra</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Wolfgang" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Wolfgang">St. Wolfgang</a>. When he went <a href="/wiki/Blindness" class="mw-redirect" title="Blindness">blind</a> in later life, he began writing hymns. He was famous enough that he appears to have been credited with compositions by later writers; among the works traditionally attributed to him are the <i><a href="/wiki/Salve_Regina" title="Salve Regina">Salve Regina</a></i> ("Hail Queen"), <i><a href="/wiki/Veni_Sancte_Spiritus" title="Veni Sancte Spiritus">Veni Sancte Spiritus</a></i> ("Come Holy Spirit"), and <i><a href="/wiki/Alma_Redemptoris_Mater" title="Alma Redemptoris Mater">Alma Redemptoris Mater</a></i> ("Nourishing Mother of the Redeemer").
</p><p>Herman died on Reichenau on 24 September 1054, aged 41.  The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> him in 1863.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_and_influence">Legacy and influence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hermann_of_Reichenau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy and influence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Three of five symphonies that were written by <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russian</a> composer <a href="/wiki/Galina_Ustvolskaya" title="Galina Ustvolskaya">Galina Ustvolskaya</a> are based on his texts.
</p>
