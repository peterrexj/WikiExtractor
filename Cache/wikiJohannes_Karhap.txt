<p><b>Johannes Vasilinpoika Karhapää</b> (or <b>Ivan Vasilievich Karhapää</b>, (13 July 1884, Sonkajanranta, <a href="/wiki/Ilomantsi" title="Ilomantsi">Ilomantsi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland" title="Grand Duchy of Finland">Grand Duchy of Finland</a> – 7 or 8 March 1918,<sup id="cite_ref-narc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-narc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Joensuu" title="Joensuu">Joensuu</a>, <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>) was a <a href="/wiki/Finnish_Karelians" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnish Karelians">Finnish Karelian</a> <a href="/wiki/Teacher" title="Teacher">teacher</a> and an <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> who was killed by the <a href="/wiki/White_Guard_(Finland)" title="White Guard (Finland)">White Guards</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War" title="Finnish Civil War">Finnish Civil War</a>. He was canonized in 2018 as the <b>Holy Martyr and Confessor John of Sonkajanranta</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-patriarchate_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patriarchate-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Karhapää was born in the Sonkajanranta village at the Nissinen homestead in <a href="/wiki/Ilomantsi" title="Ilomantsi">Ilomantsi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His parents were Vasili and Anastasia Karhapää. He had a younger brother Jaakko and two half brothers, Feodor and Ilja. He married Katri Timola and had two sons with her, Sergei, who died in infancy, and Aleksi. However, the wife died in 1910, and Karhapää married again, this time to Anna Palviainen.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Activities_as_an_enlightener">Activities as an enlightener</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Activities as an enlightener">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During his childhood, developed a strong Orthodox faith, and he participated in the activities of the local youth association of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saints_Sergei_and_Herman_Brotherhood&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saints Sergei and Herman Brotherhood (page does not exist)">Saints Sergei and Herman Brotherhood</a>. At the turn of the century, the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Karelian_Evangelical_Society&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Karelian Evangelical Society (page does not exist)">Sortavala Evangelical Society</a> had initiated a fanatical campaign to convert Orthodox Christians to the Lutheran faith, and as a countermeasure, the <a href="/wiki/Karelian_Brotherhood" title="Karelian Brotherhood">Karelian Brotherhood</a> was founded in <a href="/wiki/Olonets" title="Olonets">Olonets</a> in 1907, at the initiative of Hieromonk, future Vicar Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Kiprian_(Shnitnikov)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Kiprian (Shnitnikov) (page does not exist)">Kiprian (Shnitnikov)</a>. The brotherhood quickly established dozens of <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian language</a> schools in Karelia and published study materials for these schools. The schools became quite popular, partly due to social benefits offered to the student.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The original purpose of the brotherhood was to engage itself in vigorous <a href="/w/index.php?title=Domestic_mission&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Domestic mission (page does not exist)">domestic mission</a> work, but during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Governor-general" title="Governor-general">Governor-general</a> <a href="/wiki/Franz_Albert_Seyn" title="Franz Albert Seyn">F. A. Seyn</a> in 1909–17, the Karelian Brotherhood was harnessed for the purposes of <a href="/wiki/Russification" title="Russification">Russification</a> of Karelia.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Karhapää also got worried about the growing Lutheran mission work in his home area, and in 1906, he approached <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Sergius_of_Moscow" title="Patriarch Sergius of Moscow">Archbishop (<i>later</i> patriarch of Moscow) Sergius</a> of Finland in a letter. He asked for help in defending Orthodoxy and expressed a wish to have a school in Sonkajanranta.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As a result of Karhapää's activity, an association called Orthodox Association was founded in Sonkajanranta, as a subdivision of the Saints Sergei and Herman Brotherhood. The archbishop gave his blessing to the association to found an Orthodox church school together with the Karelian Brotherhood. A school was built in Sonkajanranta the following year, with the <a href="/wiki/Finnish_language" title="Finnish language">Finnish language</a> as the medium of instruction. By 1911, the school operated four classes. Poor students as well as those coming from farther away had a possibility to live and dine at the school, and the school was also used to house teaching, enlightenment and festive occasions for the adult population.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Working for the Saints Sergei and Herman Brotherhood, Karhapää also engaged himself as a <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechist</a> in Ilomantsi, going around with Hieromonk Isaaki of the <a href="/wiki/Valamo_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Valamo Monastery">Valamo Monastery</a>, selling prints and literature and conducting popular Christian events, and in 1914 the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Viipuri_Spiritual_Consistory&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Viipuri Spiritual Consistory (page does not exist)">Viipuri Spiritual Consistory</a> appointed the self-taught Karhapää to be a religion teacher for the entire <a href="/wiki/Kuopio_Province" title="Kuopio Province">Kuopio Province</a>, with his salary being paid for by the Grand Duchy of Finland.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Karhapää's activity was the greatest from the beginning of <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>. However, at the same time he accumulated more and more enemies, and he was labelled as henchman of the tsarist regime and even a spy employed by the <a href="/wiki/Okhrana" title="Okhrana">Okhrana</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The situation of the Sonkajanranta school became more difficult when Vicar Bishop Kiprian died in 1914 and the Karelian Brotherhood was paralyzed. However, the following year the <a href="/wiki/St._Petersburg" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Petersburg">St. Petersburg</a> merchant Nesteror donated 10&#160;000 <a href="/wiki/Roubles" class="mw-redirect" title="Roubles">roubles</a> to the school and to the building of a church next to it. The church was completed in the autumn of 1915 and consecrated to the memory of <a href="/wiki/Anna_the_Prophetess" title="Anna the Prophetess">Anna the Prophetess</a>. The blueprint for the church was drawn by monk Gennadi from the Valamo Monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The church was not under the jurisdiction of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ilomantsi_Orthodox_Parish&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ilomantsi Orthodox Parish (page does not exist)">Ilomantsi Orthodox Parish</a> but under that of the Karelian Brotherhood.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Karhapää's input in the school and church projects was significant, and because of his exemplary activities in church and enlightenment work, he received a number of distinctions. However, new malicious rumours were circulated about him, and his activities aroused much hatred among the local <a href="/wiki/Fennoman_movement" title="Fennoman movement">Fennomen</a>, as he was considered to be a propagator of the ″Russian faith″ (Finnish:″Ryssän usko″, the word <i><a href="/wiki/Ryss%C3%A4" title="Ryssä">ryssä</a></i> being <a href="/wiki/Racist_slur" class="mw-redirect" title="Racist slur">racist slur</a> for Russian).<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was especially hated for having in 1912 received Governor-general Seyn at his home. During the visit Seyn had advised Karhapää to apply for funds from the government so that a proper road could be constructed to the school. After the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution" title="Russian Revolution">Russian Revolution</a> in 1917, accusations against Karhapää intensified, and he was called a henchman of the tsarist regime and a snoop and a <a href="/wiki/Bolshevist" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolshevist">Bolshevist</a>. It was even said that he had an influence on who would be appointed priests in his home region.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was also known that Karhapää was a supporter of the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_of_All_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor of All Russia">Tsar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_trouble_in_the_1910s">In trouble in the 1910s</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In trouble in the 1910s">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Karhapää had been slandered and calumniated in may ways since 1910. At that time the <a href="/wiki/Kemi" title="Kemi">Kemi</a> newspaper <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Per%C3%A4-Pohjolainen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Perä-Pohjolainen (page does not exist)">Perä-Pohjolainen</a></i> claimed that Karhapää and his father were in favour of the Russian anti-Finnish policies and called them “miserable Finns”.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the Russian Revolution, the slandering became more intense. In the Fennoman papers of <a href="/wiki/North_Karelia" title="North Karelia">North Karelia</a>, e.g. in <i><a href="/wiki/Karjalan_Sanomat" title="Karjalan Sanomat">Karjalan Sanomat</a></i>, he was called “one of the dark powers of North Karelia”,<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and his mission travels and his decorations were made fun of. It was claimed that he sowed hatred and discord among the Karelian population, which according to the claims was not properly aware of its true faith and ethnicity. In addition to this, he was called a strong and ruthless advocate of Russification, who supposedly wanted to lead all Finns into subjugation to Russia. It was also claimed that he considered himself to be a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and that he caused fear in the local population and that he ridiculed the Christian faith and other sacred things and that he deceived simple persons. Similar accusations were levelled at his father and brother. However, Karhapää himself did not answer these accusations and slandering.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to professor of Church history <a href="/wiki/Kauko_Pirinen" title="Kauko Pirinen">Kauko Pirinen</a>, this criticism was untoward, since Karhapää's only motivation was his deep religious conviction.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Finally he began to be opposed among the Orthodox faithful as well. Just before <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> 1917, a meeting of the people of the Ilomantsi Orthodox Parish condemned Karhapää and the Sonkajanranta school as non-national oppression, which was mixed with religion. It was demanded that Karhapää be dismissed from his positions of positions of trust and from the parish council. In the autumn of 1917, he also lost the support of Archbishop Serafim, and he was dismissed from his position as a teacher.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The schools of the Karelian Brotherhood in <a href="/wiki/Karelia_(historical_province_of_Finland)" title="Karelia (historical province of Finland)">Ladoga Karelia</a> were turned over to the local municipalities, in Sonkajanranta in the autumn of 1917, a fierce nationalist <a href="/wiki/J%C3%A4ger_Movement" title="Jäger Movement">Jäger</a> from near by Kovero became its teacher. When Karhapää soon after this lost the support of Archbishop Serafim, he became practically an outlaw in the turmoil of the <a href="/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War" title="Finnish Civil War">Finnish Civil War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-keinonen_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keinonen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_death">Imprisonment and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In March 1918, Karhapää and his brother Jaakko were ordered to the <a href="/wiki/Posse_comitatus" title="Posse comitatus">call-up</a> of the <a href="/wiki/White_Guard_(Finland)" title="White Guard (Finland)">White Guards</a> to the Tuupovaara Primary School. There they were arrested on the basis of a tip-off. An incriminating piece of evidence was a small icon, found in the pocket of Karhapää's coat, intended for use school instruction. Karhapää was allowed to visit his home and bid farewell to his family, but then he was taken to Joensuu, at gunpoint. During a stop at a nearby house, Karhapää told the lady of the house that his days were numbered. In Joensuu Karhapää and his brother were locked up in the basement of the <a href="/wiki/Joensuu_Town_Hall" class="mw-redirect" title="Joensuu Town Hall">Joensuu Town Hall</a> together with other prisoners, mainly <a href="/wiki/Red_Guards_(Finland)" title="Red Guards (Finland)">Red Guard</a> and Russian <a href="/wiki/Prisoner_of_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Prisoner of War">POW's</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In early March 1918, Karhapää was taken to the Siilainen neighbourhood of Joensuu, where he was executed. At the time, executions were carried out randomly and without a trial, the executioners often being drunk. The local military districts were unable to curb the White Guards’ urge to carry out executions. Karhapää and the others to be executed were ordered in a row, and five executioners fired a <a href="/wiki/Volley_fire" title="Volley fire">volley</a>. It is said that Karhapää did not die instantly, and another volley was needed to finish him off. In his tombstone, the date of death is given as August 8.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the database <a href="/wiki/War_Victims_of_Finland_1914%E2%80%931922" title="War Victims of Finland 1914–1922">War Victims of Finland 1914–1922</a>, the date of Karhapää's death is recorded as March 7.<sup id="cite_ref-narc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-narc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, according to other data, he would have been executed some three weeks later, around Easter, and according to yet other data, only on 14 April together with 99 Russians taken prisoners during the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Rautu_(1918)" title="Battle of Rautu (1918)">Battle of Rautu</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-loima_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loima-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Karhapää's wife tried to obtain her husband's body, but she only succeeded after months had passed since his death. The body was identified from the stripes of the woollen socks. Karhapää was buried at the Kokonniemi cemetery in Ilomantsi, with a large group of people attending, including both Orthodox and Lutheran Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The slandering talk and writings continued even after his death, and the tombstone on his grave was twice thrown into nearby lake. Finally it was cast in concrete.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1958, an old man came to Karhapää's brother Jaakko, clothed in a frieze coat. He told him that he was one of the executioners. The old and sickly man suffered from a guilty conscience. Jaakko Karhapää had a conversation with the man and allowed him to stay the night at his house.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Johannes Karhapää's name came up when the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Synod" title="Holy Synod">Synod of Bishops</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Finnish_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnish Orthodox Church">Finnish Orthodox Church</a> considered Finns for a possible <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>. In March 2016, the synod of bishops of the <a href="/wiki/Finnish_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnish Orthodox Church">Finnish Orthodox Church</a> made a proposal on the canonization of Johannes Karhapää and <a href="/wiki/Schema-Igumen_John_of_Valamo" title="Schema-Igumen John of Valamo">Schema-Igumen John of Valaam (Alekseev)</a>. On 29 November 2018, the Holy Synod of the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> “recorded in the Hagiologion of the Orthodox Church” the two proposed names.<sup id="cite_ref-patriarchate_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patriarchate-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His liturgical canonization was hold 12–13 July 2019 in Church of Anna the Prophetess in Sonkajanranta. His yearly feast is 8 March.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Additional_information">Additional information</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Johannes_Karhap%C3%A4%C3%A4&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Additional information">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A documentary film on Karhapää was aired by <a href="/wiki/Yle" title="Yle">Yle</a> in 2008, entitled “The Rise and Fall of an Orthodox Man”, directed by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mikko_Keinonen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Mikko Keinonen (page does not exist)">Mikko Keinonen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> A book written by Liisa Pajukaarre was also due to appear the same year, but the publication was cancelled.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/Independence_of_Finland" title="Independence of Finland">Independence of Finland</a>, the Sonkajanranta school was given over to the local municipality. It burned down in the 1950s, and many items that had belonged to Karhapää were destroyed. The church of Anna the Prophetess is now one of the churches of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Joensuu_Orthodox_Parish&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Joensuu Orthodox Parish (page does not exist)">Joensuu Orthodox Parish</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ort.fi_5-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ort.fi-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
