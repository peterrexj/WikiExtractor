					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Head of the Catholic Church from 891 to 896</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color: #F7D79C;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;display:inline;">Pope</div><br /><div style="display:inline" class="fn">Formosus</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-subheader role"><a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Church</th><td class="infobox-data org"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Papacy began</th><td class="infobox-data">6 October 891</td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Papacy ended</th><td class="infobox-data">4 April 896</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Predecessor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_V" title="Pope Stephen V">Stephen V</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Successor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Boniface_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface VI">Boniface VI</a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Personal details</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;816</span><br /><div style="display:inline" class="birthplace"><a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a></div></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><span style="display:none">(<span class="dday deathdate">896-04-04</span>)</span>4 April 896 (aged c. 80)<br />Rome, Papal States</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Pope Formosus</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;816</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;896) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 6 October 891 until his death on 4 April 896. His reign as <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> was troubled, marked by interventions in power struggles over the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Constantinople">Patriarchate of Constantinople</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_West_Francia" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of West Francia">Kingdom of West Francia</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a>. Because he sided with <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Carinthia" title="Arnulf of Carinthia">Arnulf of Carinthia</a> against <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Spoleto" class="mw-redirect" title="Lambert of Spoleto">Lambert of Spoleto</a>, Formosus's remains were <a href="/wiki/Exhumation" class="mw-redirect" title="Exhumation">exhumed</a> and put on trial in the <a href="/wiki/Cadaver_Synod" title="Cadaver Synod">Cadaver Synod</a>. Several of his immediate successors were primarily preoccupied by the controversial legacy of his pontificate.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_career">Early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Formosus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Probably a native of Rome, Formosus was born around 816.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He became <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal bishop">cardinal bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Porto-Santa_Rufina" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Suburbicarian Diocese of Porto-Santa Rufina">Porto</a> in 864.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139Mann191046_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139Mann191046-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Two years later, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> appointed him a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legate</a> to <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgaria</a> (866).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He also undertook diplomatic missions to <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">France</a> (869 and 872).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139–140_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139–140-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon the death of <a href="/wiki/Louis_II_of_Italy" title="Louis II of Italy">Louis II of Italy</a> in 875, the nobles elected his uncle <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a> to be the new <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">emperor</a>. Formosus conveyed <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_VIII" title="Pope John VIII">Pope John VIII</a>'s invitation for Charles to come to Rome to be crowned. Charles took the crown at Pavia and received the imperial insignia in Rome on 29 December. The supporters of Louis' other uncle, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>, or Louis's widow, <a href="/wiki/Engelberga" title="Engelberga">Engelberga</a>, opposed the coronation. Fearing political retribution, many of them left Rome surreptitiously. Formosus fled to Tours after despoiling the cloisters in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On April 19, John VIII called a synod which ordered Formosus and other papal officials to return to Rome. When Formosus did not comply, he was removed from the ranks of the clergy and <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> on the grounds that he had deserted his diocese without papal permission, and had aspired to the position of archbishop of Bulgaria. Additional charges included the accusations that he had opposed the emperor; "conspired with certain iniquitous men and women for the destruction of the Papal See"; and had despoiled the <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloisters</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The condemnation of Formosus and others was announced in July 876.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909140_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909140-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 878 the sentence of excommunication was withdrawn after he promised never to return to Rome or exercise his priestly functions.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_of_popes_3_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives_of_popes_3-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 867, while Formosus was serving as legate to the Bulgarian court, <a href="/wiki/Boris_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Boris I">Boris I</a> requested that he be named archbishop of Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Since the canons forbade a bishop to <a href="/wiki/Translation_(ecclesiastical)" title="Translation (ecclesiastical)">change sees</a>, the request was denied.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909139-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> As early as 872 he was a candidate for the papacy; <a href="/wiki/Johann_Peter_Kirsch" title="Johann Peter Kirsch">Johann Peter Kirsch</a> suggests that the pope may have viewed the cardinal as a potential rival.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909140_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909140-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 883, John VIII's successor, <a href="/wiki/Marinus_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Marinus I">Marinus I</a>, restored Formosus to his <a href="/wiki/Suburbicarian" class="mw-redirect" title="Suburbicarian">suburbicarian</a> <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Portus" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Portus">diocese of Portus</a>. Following the reigns of Marinus, <a href="/wiki/Adrian_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Adrian III">Adrian III</a> (884–885) and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_V" title="Pope Stephen V">Stephen V</a> (885–891), Formosus was unanimously elected <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> on 6 October 891.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Formosus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13,_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_(cropped).gif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13%2C_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_%28cropped%29.gif/220px-Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13%2C_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_%28cropped%29.gif" decoding="async" width="220" height="260" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13%2C_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_%28cropped%29.gif/330px-Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13%2C_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_%28cropped%29.gif 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13%2C_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_%28cropped%29.gif/440px-Facial_Chronicle_-_b.13%2C_p.507_-_Pope_Formosus_%28cropped%29.gif 2x" data-file-width="726" data-file-height="858" /></a><figcaption>A 16th century depiction of Formosus' Papacy, from the <a href="/wiki/Facial_Chronicle" class="mw-redirect" title="Facial Chronicle">Facial Chronicle</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Shortly after his election, Formosus was asked to intervene in the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Constantinople">Patriarchate of Constantinople</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Photius_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Photius I">Photius I</a> had been ejected and <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Constantinople" title="Stephen I of Constantinople">Stephen I</a>, the son of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Basil_I" title="Basil I">Basil I</a>, had taken the office. Formosus refused to reinstate those who had been ordained by Photius, as his predecessor, Stephen V, had nullified all of Photius' ordinations. However, the Eastern bishops determined to recognize Photius' ordinations nonetheless. Formosus also immediately immersed himself in the dispute between <a href="/wiki/Odo_of_France" title="Odo of France">Odo of Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Simple" title="Charles the Simple">Charles the Simple</a> for the French throne. Siding with Charles, Formosus zealously exhorted Odo to cede the throne to Charles, to no avail.
</p><p>Formosus was deeply distrustful of <a href="/wiki/Guy_III_of_Spoleto" title="Guy III of Spoleto">Guy III of Spoleto</a>, the reigning emperor, and began looking for support against him.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_of_popes_3_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives_of_popes_3-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> To bolster his position, Guy forced Formosus to crown his son <a href="/wiki/Lambert_II_of_Spoleto" class="mw-redirect" title="Lambert II of Spoleto">Lambert</a> as co-emperor in April 892. The following year, however, Formosus persuaded <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Carinthia" title="Arnulf of Carinthia">Arnulf of Carinthia</a> to advance to Rome and liberate Italy from Guy's control. In 894, Arnulf's army occupied all the country north of the <a href="/wiki/Po_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Po River">Po River</a>. Guy died in December, leaving his son Lambert in the care of his mother, <a href="/wiki/Agiltrude" class="mw-redirect" title="Agiltrude">Agiltrude</a>, an opponent of the <a href="/wiki/Carolingians" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingians">Carolingians</a>. In autumn 895 Arnulf undertook his second Italian campaign, progressing to Rome by February and seizing the city from Agiltrude by force on February 21. The following day, Formosus crowned Arnulf as emperor in <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>. The new emperor moved against Spoleto but was struck with <a href="/wiki/Paralysis" title="Paralysis">paralysis</a> on the way and was unable to continue the campaign.
</p><p>During his papacy, Formosus also had to contend with the <a href="/wiki/Saracens" class="mw-redirect" title="Saracens">Saracens</a>, who were attacking <a href="/wiki/Lazio" title="Lazio">Lazio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWickham201422_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWickham201422-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> On 4 April 896, Formosus died.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909141_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909141-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Boniface_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface VI">Boniface VI</a>, whose papacy lasted 15 days.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909141_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1909141-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Formosus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg/220px-Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg/330px-Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg/440px-Jean_Paul_Laurens_Le_Pape_Formose_et_Etienne_VI_1870.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2000" data-file-height="1329" /></a><figcaption>The Cadaver Synod as portrayed by <a href="/wiki/Jean-Paul_Laurens" title="Jean-Paul Laurens">Jean-Paul Laurens</a> in 1870</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_VI" title="Pope Stephen VI">Stephen VI</a>, the successor of Boniface VI, influenced by Lambert and Agiltrude, sat in judgment of Formosus in 897, in what is known as the <a href="/wiki/Cadaver_Synod" title="Cadaver Synod">Cadaver Synod</a>. The corpse was disinterred, clad in papal vestments, and seated on a throne to face all the charges from John VIII. The verdict was that the deceased had been unworthy of the pontificate. The <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Damnatio_memoriae" title="Damnatio memoriae">damnatio memoriae</a></i></span> was applied to Formosus, all his measures and acts were annulled, and the orders conferred by him were declared invalid. The papal vestments were torn from his body, the three fingers from his right hand he had used in blessings were cut off, and the corpse was thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a>, later to be retrieved by a monk.
</p><p>Following the death of Stephen VI, Formosus' body was reinterred in <a href="/wiki/St_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="St Peter&#39;s Basilica">St Peter's Basilica</a>. Further trials of this nature against deceased persons were banned, but <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_III" title="Pope Sergius III">Sergius III</a> (904–911) reapproved the decisions against Formosus. Sergius demanded the re-ordination of the bishops consecrated by Formosus, who in turn had conferred orders on many other clerics, causing great confusion. Later, the validity of Formosus' pontificate was re-reinstated. The decision of Sergius with respect to Formosus has subsequently been universally disregarded by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, since Formosus' condemnation had little to do with piety and more to do with politics.
</p>
