<p>Saint <b>Geremarus</b> (or <b>Germer, Geremar, Geremaro</b>; died 658) was a Frankish monk and abbot.
His feast day is 24 September.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Geremarus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The oldest surviving biography of Saint Geremarus was composed in the 9th century, and was revised three centuries later, so its historical accuracy is very dubious.
It is said that he was born in Vardes, <a href="/wiki/Neuf-March%C3%A9" title="Neuf-Marché">Neuf-Marché</a>, 30 kilometres (19&#160;mi) west of <a href="/wiki/Beauvais" title="Beauvais">Beauvais</a>, France, and was educated at the Episcopal School in Beauvais.
He held high positions in the courts of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> kings <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> (r. 629–639) and <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> (r. 639–657).
He had two daughters and a son who died in infancy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChlumský_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChlumský-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Geremarus met <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Audoin</a> at Dagobert's court, and on his advice founded the Isle-sur-Epte Abbey (now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Pierre-Bois" title="Saint-Pierre-Bois">Saint-Pierre-Bois</a>).
Audoin later ordained him as a priest.
About 649 he moved to the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pental_Abbey&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pental Abbey (page does not exist)">Pental Abbey</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbaye_de_Pental" class="extiw" title="fr:Abbaye de Pental">fr</a>&#93;</span>, where he became the superior, but left after a revolt against his leadership.
Again on Audoin's advice he founded a new monastery in Fly, now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Germer-de-Fly_Abbey" title="Saint-Germer-de-Fly Abbey">Saint-Germer-de-Fly Abbey</a>.
He died about three years afterwards.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChlumský_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChlumský-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Geremarus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a> wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921), 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Geremarus (St.) Abbot (Sept. 24)<br />(7th century) Born A.D. 608, of rich and noble parents, the Merovingian King <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> made him (with his friends Eloi and <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Ouen)</a> Royal Councillors. By his saintly wife, Domana, he had three children, of whom the youngest, <a href="/wiki/Amalberga_of_Maubeuge" title="Amalberga of Maubeuge">Amalberga</a>, is honoured as a Saint. When free to do so, he entered a monastery, and later became its Abbot; but, after an attempt on his life, he retired for five years to a hermit’s cell. Finally, he founded another monastery near <a href="/wiki/Beauvais" title="Beauvais">Beauvais</a>, and a few years afterwards died a holy death as Abbot of the same (A.D. 658).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921122_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921122-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Geremarus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> (1710–1773) wrote in his <i>Lives of the Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i> under September 24, 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Saint Germer, or Geremar, Abbot
</p><p>
His parents, Rigobert and Aga, were of the prime nobility in the territory of Beauvais. He was born at their castle in the village Warandra, in the reign of King <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Clotaire</a>; married a pious lady named Domana, and whilst yet a layman, built a monastery in honour of Saint Peter, called the Island, which was afterwards destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a>, and is now an estate belonging to <a href="/wiki/Saint-Germer-de-Fly_Abbey" title="Saint-Germer-de-Fly Abbey">Saint Germer’s abbey</a>. Germer, by the advice of Saint <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Owen</a>, made his monastic profession in the monastery of Pental, in the territory of <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a>. He was soon after chosen abbot, but finding the monks averse to regularity he left the abbacy, and led an anchoretical life in a cave near the river <a href="/wiki/Seine" title="Seine">Seine</a> five years and six months. His only son Amalbert, dying, was buried in Saint Peter’s monastery. Germer, with the estate which reverted to him from his son’s death, founded the monastery of Fley or Flaviacum, now Saint Germer’s, five leagues from Beauvais towards Rouen, in which he assembled a community of fervent monks, in 655. Having governed this house three years and a half, he happily died on the 24th of September, 658. His body was interred in the church of his abbey, which soon after took his name. His relics, for fear of the Norman plunderers, were conveyed secretly to Beauvais, where they are still kept in the cathedral, except the bones of one arm, which have been given back to Saint Germer’s.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1866280_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1866280-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1643 <a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustin_Potier&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Augustin Potier (page does not exist)">August Potier</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Beauvais" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Beauvais">bishop of Beauvais</a>, placed monks of the congregation of Saint Maur in this abbey, and erected in it a great school for the humanity studies to the end of rhetoric.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1866280–281_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1866280–281-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
