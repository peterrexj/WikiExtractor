<p>Saints <b>Secundian(us), Marcellian and Verian</b> (also known as <i>Secondianus, Marcellianus, and Verianus</i>) (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Secondino, Marcelliano, e Veriano</i>) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a>.  They were <a href="/wiki/Martyred" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyred">martyred</a> in 250 AD near <a href="/wiki/Civitavecchia" title="Civitavecchia">Civitavecchia</a> or <a href="/wiki/Santa_Marinella" title="Santa Marinella">Santa Marinella</a> during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a>.  Secundian was a <a href="/wiki/Senator" class="mw-redirect" title="Senator">senator</a> or some sort of prominent official; Marcellian and Verian were scholars or students.  Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is August 9.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Secundian,_Marcellian_and_Verian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A fifteenth century account relates that under Emperor Decius, the three were zealous persecutors of Christians. However, upon reading <a href="/wiki/Virgil" title="Virgil">Virgil</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Eclogue_4" title="Eclogue 4">Eclogue 4</a></i>, they, like <a href="/wiki/Statius" title="Statius">Statius</a> in the <i><a href="/wiki/Divine_Comedy" title="Divine Comedy">Divine Comedy</a></i>, became Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to one version, they were baptized by a priest named Timotheus (Timothy) and confirmed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_II" title="Pope Sixtus II">Pope Sixtus II</a>.  By order of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a>, they were arrested by the prefect Valerian and decapitated at Civitavecchia and then their bodies were thrown into the sea.
</p><p>In a second account, the place of their martyrdom was <i>appellatur Coloniacum, qui dicitur Colonia</i> ("called Coloniacum, that is to say, Colonia"), which may be <i>Colonia Iulia Castrumnovurn</i>. Their bodies were collected by a man named Deodatus and buried in that place.
</p><p>According to a third account, their cult was localized in the basilica of <a href="/wiki/San_Pietro,_Tuscania" title="San Pietro, Tuscania">San Pietro, Tuscania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Acts of <a href="/wiki/Felinus_and_Gratian" title="Felinus and Gratian">Felinus and Gratian</a> were based on those of Secundian and his companions.  <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a> writes that "the so-called <i>Acts of SS. Gratian and Felinus</i>, used as lections in the <i>Arona Passionale</i>, are extracted from the Acts of SS. Florentinus and Companions, martyrs at Perugia commemorated the same day.  But these Acts are in their turn not genuine; they are, in fact, the Acts of SS. Secundianus and Comp. (August 9)."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Secundian,_Marcellian_and_Verian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The names of these saints appear in <a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Jerome's martyrology</a> under August 9.  The <i>Codex Epternacense</i> indicates that the place of their death was <a href="/wiki/Tuscia" title="Tuscia">Tuscia</a>; the <i>Codex Wissemburgense</i> lists the place of death as "Colonia" (not necessarily <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>); and the <i>Codex Bernense</i> finally specifies the place of death as <i>in Colon(n)i Tusciae via miliario Aureliax XV.</i>  One scholar has identified this as <i>Colonia Iulia Castrumnovurn</i>, a town in Tuscia, situated on the <a href="/wiki/Aurelian_Way" class="mw-redirect" title="Aurelian Way">Aurelian Way</a>, situated near the present-day Santa Marinella (which is near Civitavecchia).<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
