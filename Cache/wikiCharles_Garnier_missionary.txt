<p><b>Charles Garnier</b>, <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">S.J.</a>, (baptised at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, May 25, 1606 &#8211; December 7, 1649) was a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> working in <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a>. He was killed by <a href="/wiki/Iroquois" title="Iroquois">Iroquois</a> in a <a href="/wiki/Petun" title="Petun">Petun</a> (Tobacco Nation) village on December 7, 1649.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Garnier_(missionary)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The son of a secretary to King <a href="/wiki/Henri_III_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Henri III of France">Henri III of France</a>, Garnier was born in Paris in 1606. He attended the <a href="/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Louis-le-Grand" title="Lycée Louis-le-Grand">Collège de Clermont</a> in Paris and joined the Jesuit seminary in <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a> in September 1624.<sup id="cite_ref-Lindsay_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindsay-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his novitiate, he returned to the College of Clermont as Prefect. After finishing his studies in rhetoric and philosophy, he spent two years teaching at the <a href="/wiki/Eu,_Seine-Maritime" title="Eu, Seine-Maritime">College of Eu</a> as a teacher. Completing years of studies in language, culture and theology, he was ordained as a priest in 1635. His father initially forbade him from travelling to Canada where he would face almost certain death as a missionary, but he was eventually allowed to go.<sup id="cite_ref-tjc_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tjc-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Embarking on March 25, 1636, he described the crossing in a letter to his father,
</p>
<blockquote><p>We gave Viaticum to a sailor who had fallen from the top of the mizzenmast to the deck. He was well-disposed to die. However, as I saw him in great discomfort, unable to sleep, I gave him my cabin and went in with Father Chastelain in his, but the sick man found this cabin too stuffy so the next day I occupied it again but left him my mattress so he could sleep even in the midst of the cannons. Hearing this, the Captain made me take one of his.<sup id="cite_ref-McGivern_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGivern-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>He reached the colony of <a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a> in June. He travelled immediately to the Huron mission with fellow Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Pierre_Chastellain" title="Pierre Chastellain">Pierre Chastellain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> By early August he had arrived among the <a href="/wiki/Nipissing_First_Nation" title="Nipissing First Nation">Nipissings</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McGivern_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGivern-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He served for the rest of his life as a missionary among the <a href="/wiki/Wyandot_people" title="Wyandot people">Huron</a>, never returning to <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. The Huron nicknamed him <i>Ouracha</i>, or "rain-giver", after his arrival was followed by a drought-ending rainfall. He was greatly influenced by fellow missionary <a href="/wiki/Jean_de_Br%C3%A9beuf" title="Jean de Brébeuf">Jean de Brébeuf</a>, and was known as the "lamb" to Brebeuf's "lion".<sup id="cite_ref-Lindsay_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lindsay-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1639 and 1640 he wintered in the land of the <a href="/wiki/Petun" title="Petun">Petun</a>. From 1641 to 1646 Garnier was at the Saint-Joseph mission.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There were raids between Iroquois and Huron forces. When he learned that Brébeuf and Lalemant were killed in March 1649 by Iroquois after a raid on a Huron village, Garnier knew he too might soon die. On December 7, 1649, he was killed by musket fire from the <a href="/wiki/Iroquois" title="Iroquois">Iroquois</a> during an attack on the <a href="/wiki/Petun" title="Petun">Petun</a> village where he was living.<sup id="cite_ref-McGivern_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGivern-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Charles Garnier was canonized in 1930 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> with the other seven <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Martyrs" title="Canadian Martyrs">Canadian Martyrs</a> (also known as the <b>North American Martyrs.</b>)<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is October 19.
</p>
