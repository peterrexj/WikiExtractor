<p><b>Bertulf</b> (died 640) was the third abbot of the monastery of Bobbio.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bertulf_of_Bobbio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bertulf was the son of an <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasian</a> nobleman and a near relative of <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Metz" title="Arnulf of Metz">Arnulf of Metz</a>, whose example had such an influence on Bertulf that he became a Christian and in 620 entered the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil" class="mw-redirect" title="Luxeuil">Luxeuil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A few years later he became acquainted with <a href="/wiki/Abbot_Attala" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot Attala">Abbot Attala</a>, who had come to Luxeuil on a visit, and, with permission of Abbot <a href="/wiki/Eustace_of_Luxeuil" title="Eustace of Luxeuil">Eustace of Luxeuil</a>, accompanied Attala back to Bobbio in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Upon the death of Attala in 627, Bertulf was elected by the monks of Bobbio as their abbot. Like his  predecessor, he insisted on the observance of the austere rule introduced by <a href="/wiki/St._Columban" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Columban">St. Columban</a>, the founder of <a href="/wiki/Bobbio_Abbey" title="Bobbio Abbey">Bobbio Abbey</a>, and preached fearlessly against <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>, which had gained a firm foothold in Italy under the Lombard kings.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 628, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tortona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tortona">Bishop of Tortona</a> endeavoured to bring Bobbio under his own jurisdiction, Bertulf hastened to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_I" title="Pope Honorius I">Pope Honorius I</a> received him kindly and granted the monastery entire exemption from episcopal jurisdiction, and subject directly to the Papal See.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Jonas_of_Bobbio" title="Jonas of Bobbio">Jonas</a>, a monk of Bobbio, who accompanied Bertulf on his journey to Rome, relates that, while returning to his monastery, Bertulf was attacked by a deadly fever, and cured miraculously by <a href="/wiki/St._Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter">St. Peter</a>. The same author ascribes a few other miracles to the prayers of St. Bertulf. Bertulf served seventeen years as abbot and died on August 19, 640.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He is buried in the crypt of the Basilica of San Colombano.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Most martyrologies give him the title of saint. His feast is celebrated on August 19.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
