					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfano_I_(Archbishop_of_Salerno)&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfano I (Archbishop of Salerno)">Alfano I (Archbishop of Salerno)</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>Saint <b>Alfanus I</b> or <b>Alfano I</b> (died 1085) was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Salerno" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Salerno">archbishop of Salerno</a> from 1058 until his death. He was famed as a translator, writer, theologian, and medical doctor. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfanus_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alfanus was born to a noble <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Langobard</a> family of Salerno between 1015 and 1020. He had an excellent education in the liberal arts and developed a wide knowledge of literature. Alfanus was a physician, one of the earliest great doctors of the <a href="/wiki/Schola_Medica_Salernitana" title="Schola Medica Salernitana">Schola Medica Salernitana</a>. The young monk Desiderius (later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_III" title="Pope Victor III">Pope Victor III</a>) fell ill and traveled from Montecassino seeking treatment. He and Alfanus became life-long friends.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Alfanus joined the <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Abbey of Montecassino</a> in 1056, but did not remain there long before being sent to take charge of the Benedictine monastery in Salerno. In 1058, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_IX" title="Pope Stephen IX">Pope Stephen IX</a>, the abbot of Montecassino, named Alfanus archbishop of Salerno. He made a pilgrimage to Jerusalem with <a href="/wiki/Gisulf_II_of_Salerno" title="Gisulf II of Salerno">Gisulf II of Salerno</a>, stopping by Constantinople in 1062,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where Gisulf sought Byzantine assistance in holding off <a href="/wiki/Robert_Guiscard" title="Robert Guiscard">Robert Guiscard</a>'s expansion into his territory. That same year Alfanus gave the prince three strongholds in exchange for the monastery of San Vito. Efforts to withstand Guiscard ultimately proved unsuccessful.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a translator, Alfanus was well-versed in both <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a> and he translated many manuscripts from the latter into the former. His interest in medicine and the translation of Arabic treatises on the subject led him to invite <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_African" title="Constantine the African">Constantine the African</a> from Carthage (in what is now Tunisia) to Salerno to assist him. Constantine brought with him a library of Arabic medical texts which he commenced to translate into Latin. Alfanus also translated Greek medical treatises into Latin.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg/220px-Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="222" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg/330px-Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg/440px-Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3606" data-file-height="3638" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Salerno_2013-05-17_09-37-10.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>San Matteo, Facade and Cloister</div></div></div><p> He also wrote a number of poems and hymns.
</p><p>As archbishop, Alfanus re-organized the archdiocese. He was a friend of Hildebrand of Sovana (later Pope Gregory VII) and Abbot Desiderius of Monte Cassino. He was also a patron of the arts and sciences,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and politically influential.
</p><p>In wielding control of southern Italy, Robert Guiscard sought to cultivate popular support. He married <a href="/wiki/Sikelgaita" title="Sikelgaita">a Lombard princess</a> from Salerno, retained Lombard coinage and local officials, and promoted the cult of the local patron, St. Matthew. In this he relied on the influential support of the Archbishop who praised and promoted the strong ties between the people of Salerno and St. Matthew's cult.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1076, Guiscard laid the foundations for the new <a href="/wiki/Salerno_Cathedral" title="Salerno Cathedral">Salerno Cathedral</a>. It was dedicated to Saint Matthew, whose relics were translated to the new crypt in 1080.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Alfanus' later days as archbishop, he sheltered the exiled reformer, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>, who died in Salerno.
</p>
