<p><b>Saint Eleutherius of Tournai</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Eleuthère</i>) (died c. 532) is venerated as a saint and considered the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tournai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tournai">bishop of Tournai</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> writes that "historically there is very little known about St. Eleutherius, but he was without doubt the first Bishop of Tournai."<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tradition makes him a lifelong friend of <a href="/wiki/St._Medardus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Medardus">St. Medardus</a>, and the two saints had been courtiers before becoming bishops.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eleutherius was probably named bishop of Tournai after <a href="/wiki/St._Remigius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Remigius">St. Remigius</a> organized the church hierarchy of northern <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> at the end of the fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some sermons on the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus" title="Nativity of Jesus">Nativity</a>, and the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Annunciation" title="Annunciation">Annunciation</a> (<i>Bibliotheca Patrum, vol. XV</i>) are falsely attributed to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eleutherius_of_Tournai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eleutherius is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> although this legend is considered an invention of the <a href="/wiki/Canon_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon priest">canon priest</a> Henri of Tournai, who wrote a <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> of Eleutherius in 1141.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the end of the 11th century the Church of Tournai had been trying to become independent from the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Noyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Noyon">diocese of Noyon</a>, and Henri had been motivated by the need to prove the antiquity of the Church of Tournai.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  <i>Catholic Online</i> repeats the legend that a "group of <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arians</a> enraged by his preaching beat him severely He died some weeks later [sic]."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg/220px-Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg/330px-Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg/440px-Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2304" data-file-height="1728" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Blandain_-_Eglise_Saint-Eleuth%C3%A8re.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Church of Saint-Eleuthère at Blandain.</div></div></div>
<p>Henri's account states that Eleutherius was a native of Tournai who was born during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Childeric_I" title="Childeric I">Childeric I</a>.  Eleutherius' parents were Christians and were named Terenus and Blanda; Terenus was a descendant in the family of <a href="/wiki/Irenaeus_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Irenaeus of Lyons">Irenaeus of Lyons</a>.  Persecutions of Christians forced the family to flee to a village named <i>Blandinium</i> (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Blandain&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Blandain (page does not exist)">Blandain</a>), but after the conversion of <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis</a> to Christianity, the family built a church at Blandinium (Blandain).  Eleutherius eventually became bishop of Tournai, and was consulted by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hormisdas" title="Pope Hormisdas">Pope Hormisdas</a> on the matter of eradicating <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>.  Eleutherius convened a church council, and argued effectively against the Arians, who were angered by this.  One day, as he was going to church, he was beaten up by a group of Arians and left for dead.  He subsequently died from the wounds he received, on his death-bed confiding his flock to Medardus.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG/220px-Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="175" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG/330px-Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG/440px-Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2731" data-file-height="2171" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tournai_Kathedrale7.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Reliquary_Shrine_of_Saint_Eleutherius" title="Reliquary Shrine of Saint Eleutherius">Reliquary Shrine of Saint Eleutherius</a>, 1247, in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Tournai" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Tournai">Cathedral of Tournai</a></div></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eleutherius_of_Tournai&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There exists a testimony recording the recovery of his relics during the episcopate of Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hedilo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hedilo (page does not exist)">Hedilo</a> of Tournai, in 897 or 898.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Baudoin <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> Eleutherius' relics in 1064 or 1065.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Eleutherius' relics were translated again in 1247,<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> when the great <a href="/wiki/Reliquary_Shrine_of_Saint_Eleutherius" title="Reliquary Shrine of Saint Eleutherius">reliquary shrine</a> was commissioned by Bishop Walter de Marvis. In its gable end St Eleutherius appears, holding his <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> in one hand and in the other a model of the cathedral with its five spires.
</p><p>The monastery of St. Martin at Tournai and the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Bruges" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Bruges">cathedral of Bruges</a> also claimed some of the saint's relics.<sup id="cite_ref-Advent_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Advent-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
