.mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" <p><b>Prokopios Lazaridis</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Προκόπιος Λαζαρίδης</span>, 1859–1923) was a <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan bishop</a>, who served as a head in a number of bishoprics during the late <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman</a> period. As a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Iconium" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconium">Iconium</a>, modern <a href="/wiki/Konya" title="Konya">Konya</a>, in central <a href="/wiki/Anatolia" title="Anatolia">Anatolia</a>, he was involved with the issue of the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Turkish_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkish Orthodox Church">Turkish Orthodox Church</a>. He died in Turkish prison in 1923.<sup id="cite_ref-Kiminas_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kiminas-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is commemorated by the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox Church</a> as Hieromartyr (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">ιερομάρτυρας</span>) and his feast day is celebrated on the Sunday before the <a href="/wiki/Elevation_of_the_Holy_Cross" title="Elevation of the Holy Cross">Exaltation of the Holy Cross</a> each year (September 7–13).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Prokopios_Lazaridis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Prokopios Lazaridis was born in <a href="/wiki/Tyana" title="Tyana">Tyana</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Konya_Province" title="Konya Province">Konya Province</a> in 1859. He attended the <a href="/wiki/Halki_Seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Halki Seminary">Halki Seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> (<a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>) at 1875. In 1899 he became <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Eparchy_of_Dyrrachium" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Eparchy of Dyrrachium">Metropolitan of Dyrrachium</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Durr%C3%ABs" title="Durrës">Durrës</a> in central <a href="/wiki/Albania" title="Albania">Albania</a>), where he developed significant activity among the local Orthodox communities. In 1906 he was installed in the Anatolian city of Philadelphia (<a href="/wiki/Ala%C5%9Fehir" title="Alaşehir">Alaşehir</a>, <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>), as the head of the <a href="/wiki/Metropolis_of_Philadelphia" title="Metropolis of Philadelphia">local bishopric</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kiminas_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kiminas-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Tsiris_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tsiris-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1911 he became metropolitan bishop of <a href="/wiki/Iconium" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconium">Iconium</a>, in central Anatolia. During the <a href="/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_(1919-1922)" class="mw-redirect" title="Greco-Turkish War (1919-1922)">Greco-Turkish War (1919-1922)</a>, the nationalist Turkish authorities in an attempt to <a href="/wiki/Turkification" title="Turkification">Turkify</a> and take the Orthodox church under their full control, founded the <a href="/wiki/Turkish_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkish Orthodox Church">Turkish Orthodox Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Kayseri</a> and installed <a href="/wiki/Papa_Eftim_I" title="Papa Eftim I">Papa Eftim</a> as its head. Prokopios was against these developments and tried to reach a solution concerning the administration of the Orthodox Church in Anatolia.<sup id="cite_ref-Tsiris_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tsiris-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In September 20, 1920, as a result of his intervention, Prokopios, as well as the local Armenian bishop of Konya, were imprisoned by the nationalist authorities of <a href="/wiki/Mustafa_Kemal_Atat%C3%BCrk" title="Mustafa Kemal Atatürk">Mustafa Kemal</a>. Prokopios then was transferred to the prison of <a href="/wiki/Erzurum" title="Erzurum">Erzurum</a>, and stayed there from October 1920 to May 1922. Soon after his release, he visited <a href="/wiki/Ankara" title="Ankara">Ankara</a>, where he met Mustafa Kemal in an attempt to ask him to abstain from the ecclesiastical dispute concerning the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Turkish_Orthodox_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkish Orthodox Patriarchate">Turkish Orthodox Patriarchate</a>, due to severe health problems he suffered. Nevertheless, his proposal was rejected.<sup id="cite_ref-Tsiris_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tsiris-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Prokopios presided in the following ecclesiastical council at January 1923, where he was obliged to proclaim the Turkish Orthodox Church. However, Turkish interests subsided, due to the <a href="/wiki/Greco-Turkish_War_(1919-1922)" class="mw-redirect" title="Greco-Turkish War (1919-1922)">Greek defeat in Anatolia</a> and the subsequent <a href="/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey" title="Population exchange between Greece and Turkey">deportation of the Greek Orthodox communities</a> from Anatolia. Prokopios was again imprisoned and died in detention in April 1923 in <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Kayseri</a> prison.<sup id="cite_ref-Kiminas_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kiminas-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Tsiris_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tsiris-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
