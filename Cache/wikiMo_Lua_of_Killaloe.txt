					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>

<b>Saint Molua</b> (d. c 609),<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (also known as <i>Lua</i>, <i>Da Lua</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, who was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages" title="Early Middle Ages">Early Middle Ages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  <b>Saint Molua's</b> feast day is on 4 August.<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mo_Lua_of_Killaloe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St Molua was an Irish priest of the 6th century who like Columba and Gall trained in the monastery at Bangor,<sup id="cite_ref-stormont_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stormont-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> County Down (about twelve miles from Belfast). The saint's real name was originally Lughaidh (pronounced Lua). His father is believed to have been Coche or Carthach of the <a href="/wiki/Corca_O%C3%ADche" title="Corca Oíche">Corca Oiche</a>, a sept associated with the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Fidgenti" title="Uí Fidgenti">Ui Fidgenti</a> from the Limerick area. His mother, Sochla was from <a href="/wiki/Osraige" title="Osraige">Ossory</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stormont_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stormont-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Local Historians note of stories that tarnished the Saint's reputation in his time, those being that he had fathered many children to the daughter of a local <a href="/wiki/E%C3%B3ganachta" title="Eóganachta">Eóganachta</a> Chieftain in <a href="/wiki/County_Clare" title="County Clare">County Clare</a>. These children were named locally as Ó Maoldomhnaigh which in turn birthed the family name of <a href="/wiki/Moloney_(surname)" title="Moloney (surname)">Moloney</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Little is known on Molua other than he was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, a builder and possibly a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Molua was the founder of <a href="/wiki/Killaloe,_County_Clare" title="Killaloe, County Clare">Killaloe</a> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Cill-da-Lua</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-08641a_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-08641a-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which bears his name <i>Lua</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-08641a_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-08641a-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Molua had his <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> on Friar's Island, later replaced by a <a href="/wiki/St._Molua%27s_Church,_Killaloe" title="St. Molua&#39;s Church, Killaloe">stone church</a><sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> near the present village of Killaloe.<sup id="cite_ref-08641a_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-08641a-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Like most Irish saints he appears to have been very hospitable, believing that in entertaining others he was entertaining Christ. He was kind to animals as well as humans and it was said that when he died all living creatures bewailed him.<sup id="cite_ref-stormont_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stormont-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mo_Lua_of_Killaloe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Molua's principal disciple was <a href="/wiki/Saint_Flannan" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Flannan">Saint Flannan</a>, who succeeded Molua.<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  His monastery in Clonfert-Mulloe in Osraige produced the scholar <a href="/wiki/Laidcenn_mac_Buith_Bannaig" title="Laidcenn mac Buith Bannaig">Laidcenn mac Buith Bannaig</a>.
</p>
