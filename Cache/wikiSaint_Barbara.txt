<p><b>Saint Barbara</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Ἁγία Βαρβάρα</span>; <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop">Ϯⲁⲅⲓⲁ Ⲃⲁⲣⲃⲁⲣⲁ</span>; <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Church Slavonic language">Church Slavonic</a>: <span lang="cu">Великомученица Варва́ра Илиопольская</span>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">القديسة الشهيدة بربارة</span>), known in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> as the <b>Great Martyr Barbara</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christianity">early Christian</a> Lebanese and Greek <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. Accounts place her in the 3rd century in Heliopolis Phoenicia, present-day <a href="/wiki/Baalbek" title="Baalbek">Baalbek</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>, and recent discovered texts in the Saida early church archives suggest her maternal grandmother is a descendant from <a href="/wiki/Miye_ou_Miye" title="Miye ou Miye">Miye ou Miye</a> village.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There is no reference to her in the authentic early Christian writings nor in the original <a href="/wiki/Recension" title="Recension">recension</a> of <a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Saint Jerome's martyrology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite the legends detailing her story, the earliest references to her supposed 3rd-century life do not appear until the 7th century, and veneration of her was common, especially in the East, from the 9th century.<sup id="cite_ref-williams_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Because of doubts about the historicity of her legend,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> she was removed from the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a>, though not from the Catholic Church's list of saints.<sup id="cite_ref-MR_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Barbara is often portrayed with miniature chains and a <a href="/wiki/Tower" title="Tower">tower</a>. As one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>, Barbara continues to be a popular saint in modern times, perhaps best known as the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Armourer" title="Armourer">armourers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Artillerymen" class="mw-redirect" title="Artillerymen">artillerymen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Military_engineer" class="mw-redirect" title="Military engineer">military engineers</a>, miners and others who work with <a href="/wiki/Explosive" title="Explosive">explosives</a> because of her legend's association with <a href="/wiki/Lightning" title="Lightning">lightning</a>, and also of <a href="/wiki/Mathematician" title="Mathematician">mathematicians</a>. A 15th-century French version of her story credits her with thirteen miracles, many of which reflect the security she offered that her devotees would not die before getting to make confession and receiving <a href="/wiki/Extreme_unction" class="mw-redirect" title="Extreme unction">extreme unction</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-williams_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_(detail).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_%28detail%29.jpg/170px-Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_%28detail%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_%28detail%29.jpg/255px-Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_%28detail%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b9/Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_%28detail%29.jpg/340px-Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_%28detail%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="2048" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Kalteysen_St._Barbara_Altarpiece_(detail).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Torture of St. Barbara</div></div></div>
<p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-williams_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Calendarium_Romanum_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendarium_Romanum-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Barbara, the daughter of a rich <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> named Dioscorus, was carefully guarded by her father who kept her locked up in a tower in order to preserve her from the outside world. Having secretly become a Christian, she rejected an offer of marriage that she received through her father.
</p><p>Before going on a journey, her father commanded that a private <a href="/wiki/Bathroom" title="Bathroom">bath-house</a> be erected for her use near her dwelling, and during his absence, Barbara had three windows put in it, as a symbol of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Trinity">Holy Trinity</a>, instead of the two originally intended. When her father returned, she acknowledged herself to be a Christian. Dragged before the prefect of the province, Martinianus, who had her cruelly tortured, Barbara held true to her Christian faith. During the night, the dark prison was bathed in light and new miracles occurred. Every morning, her wounds were healed. Torches that were to be used to burn her went out as soon as they came near her. Finally, she was condemned to death by <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheading</a>. Her father himself carried out the death sentence. However, as <a href="/wiki/Divine_retribution" title="Divine retribution">punishment</a>, he was struck by lightning on the way home and his body was consumed by flame. Barbara was buried by a Christian, Valentinus, and her tomb became the site of miracles. This summary omits picturesque details, supplemented from Old French accounts.<sup id="cite_ref-williams_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i>, her martyrdom took place on December 4 "in the reign of emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximianus</a> and Prefect Marcien" (<abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr> 286–305); the year was given as 267 in the French version edited by Father Harry F. Williams of the <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> <a href="/wiki/Community_of_the_Resurrection" title="Community of the Resurrection">Community of the Resurrection</a> (1975).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg/170px-Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="289" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg/255px-Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg/340px-Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg 2x" data-file-width="530" data-file-height="900" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger_-_Obglavljenje_sv._Barbare.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The beheading of St. Barbara</i> by <a href="/wiki/Giulio_Quaglio_the_Younger" title="Giulio Quaglio the Younger">Giulio Quaglio the Younger</a> (1721–1723)</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG/170px-Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG/255px-Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG/340px-Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1944" data-file-height="2592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sainte_Barbe_bis.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Barbara and her tower, French (<a href="/wiki/Villeloup" title="Villeloup">Villeloup, Aube</a>), <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1520–30</span>, polychromed limestone</div></div></div>
<p>The name of Saint Barbara was known in Rome in the 7th century;<sup id="cite_ref-williams_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-williams-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> her <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> can be traced to the 9th century, at first in the East. Since there is no mention of her in the earlier <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>, her historicity is considered doubtful.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her legend is included in <a href="/wiki/Vincent_of_Beauvais" title="Vincent of Beauvais">Vincent of Beauvais</a>' <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Speculum_historiale" class="mw-redirect" title="Speculum historiale">Speculum historiale</a></i></span> (xii.64) and in later versions of the <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> (and in <a href="/wiki/William_Caxton" title="William Caxton">William Caxton</a>'s version of it).
</p><p>Various versions, which include two surviving <a href="/wiki/Mystery_play" title="Mystery play">mystery plays</a>, differ on the location of her martyrdom, which is variously given as <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Baalbek" title="Baalbek">Baalbek</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Nicomedia" title="Nicomedia">Nicomedia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Barbara is one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>. Her association with the lightning that killed her father has caused her to be invoked against lightning and fire; by association with explosions, she is also the patron of <a href="/wiki/Artillery" title="Artillery">artillery</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mining" title="Mining">mining</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Shaver_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shaver-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her feast, December 4, was introduced in Rome in the 12th century and included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>. In 1729, that date was assigned to the celebration of Saint <a href="/wiki/Peter_Chrysologus" title="Peter Chrysologus">Peter Chrysologus</a>, reducing that of Saint Barbara to a commemoration in his <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendarium_Romanum_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendarium_Romanum-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 98">&#58;&#8202;98&#8202;</span></sup> In 1969, it was removed from that calendar, because the accounts of her life and martyrdom were judged to be entirely fabulous, lacking clarity even about the place of her martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-Calendarium_Romanum_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calendarium_Romanum-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 147">&#58;&#8202;147&#8202;</span></sup> However, she is still mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> which, in addition, lists another ten martyr saints named Barbara.
</p><p>In the 12th century, the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of Saint Barbara were brought from <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> to the <a href="/wiki/St._Michael%27s_Golden-Domed_Monastery" title="St. Michael&#39;s Golden-Domed Monastery">St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kyiv</a>, where they were kept until the 1930s, when they were transferred to <a href="/wiki/St._Vladimir%27s_Cathedral,_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Vladimir&#39;s Cathedral, Kiev">St. Volodymyr's Cathedral</a> in the same city. In November 2012, Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Filaret_(Denysenko)" title="Filaret (Denysenko)">Filaret</a> of The <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_%E2%80%93_Kiev_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Orthodox Church – Kiev Patriarchate">Ukrainian Orthodox Church – Kiev Patriarchate</a> transferred a small part of St. Barbara's relics to St. Andrew Ukrainian Orthodox Cathedral in <a href="/wiki/Bloomingdale,_Illinois" title="Bloomingdale, Illinois">Bloomingdale, Illinois</a>.
</p><p>Her feast day for <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholics</a><sup id="cite_ref-MR_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and most <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglicans</a> is December 4.
</p><p>In 2022, Barbara was officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day she shares with <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria" title="Catherine of Alexandria">Catherine of Alexandria</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Margaret_the_Virgin" title="Margaret the Virgin">Margaret of Antioch</a> on 24 November.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:191px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Maria_Formosa,_cappella_laterale,_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._(cropped)_(cropped).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Santa_Maria_Formosa%2C_cappella_laterale%2C_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/189px-Santa_Maria_Formosa%2C_cappella_laterale%2C_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="189" height="455" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Santa_Maria_Formosa%2C_cappella_laterale%2C_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/284px-Santa_Maria_Formosa%2C_cappella_laterale%2C_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Santa_Maria_Formosa%2C_cappella_laterale%2C_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/378px-Santa_Maria_Formosa%2C_cappella_laterale%2C_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._%28cropped%29_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="765" data-file-height="1840" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Maria_Formosa,_cappella_laterale,_opere_di_Palma_il_Vecchio._(cropped)_(cropped).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Polyptych_of_Saint_Barbara_(Palma_Vecchio)" title="Polyptych of Saint Barbara (Palma Vecchio)"><i>Saint Barbara</i></a> in <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Formosa" title="Santa Maria Formosa">Santa Maria Formosa</a>, Venice, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1520s</span></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png/220px-Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="308" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png/330px-Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png/440px-Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png 2x" data-file-width="890" data-file-height="1247" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Barbara_with_cannon.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Barbara with chalice and cannon</div></div></div>
<p>Saint Barbara is venerated by Catholics who face the danger of sudden and violent death at work. She is invoked against thunder and lightning and all accidents arising from explosions of gunpowder.<sup id="cite_ref-Online_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Online-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> She became the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Artillerymen" class="mw-redirect" title="Artillerymen">artillerymen</a>, armourers, military engineers, gunsmiths, and anyone else who worked with cannon and explosives.<sup id="cite_ref-usfaa_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-usfaa-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Kinard_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kinard-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Following the widespread adoption of gunpowder in mining in the 1600s, she was adopted as the patron of miners, tunnellers,<sup id="cite_ref-Shaver_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shaver-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and other underground workers. As the geology and mine engineering developed in association with mining, she became patron of these professions.
</p><p>The Spanish word <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es"><a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/santab%C3%A1rbara" class="extiw" title="es:santabárbara">santabárbara</a></i></span>, the corresponding Italian word <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">santabarbara</i></span>, and the obsolete French <span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Sainte-Barbe</i></span>, signify the <a href="/wiki/Naval_magazine" class="mw-redirect" title="Naval magazine">powder magazine</a> of a ship or fortress.<sup id="cite_ref-Caprio_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Caprio-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> It was customary to have a statue of Saint Barbara at the magazine to protect the ship or fortress from suddenly exploding.<sup id="cite_ref-Caprio_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Caprio-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Saint Barbara is the patron of the <a href="/wiki/Italian_Navy" title="Italian Navy">Italian Navy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Online_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Online-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Kinard_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kinard-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Within the tunnelling industry, as a long-standing tradition, one of the first tasks for each new tunnelling projects is to establish a small shrine to Santa Barbara at the tunnel portal or at the underground junction into long tunnel headings. This is often followed with a dedication and an invocation to Santa Barbara for protection of all who work on the project during the construction period.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_English-speaking_countries">In English-speaking countries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In English-speaking countries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The church at <a href="/wiki/HMS_Excellent_(shore_establishment)" title="HMS Excellent (shore establishment)">HMS Excellent</a> (also known as Whale Island) Portsmouth, Hampshire, England, the former Gunnery School of the Royal Navy, is called St. Barbara's. From 'Britain's Glorious Navy': "On the dining-table in the ward-room at Whale Island, a delightful silver statuette of Saint Barbara, the patron saint of Artillerists, smiles down on successive generations of gunnery officers who will not let her, or the Navy, down. Their job is to get straddles with full-gun salvoes of a reasonable spread; Saint Barbara herself arranges the hits."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Saint Barbara was also the Patron saint of the Royal Army Ordnance Corps of the British Army, a church being dedicated to her initial at Hilsea Barracks Portsmouth later being moved to Backdown in Surrey, when the Corps moved its training establishment there.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Irish_Defence_Forces" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Defence Forces">Irish Army</a> venerates her as the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Artillery_Corps_(Ireland)" title="Artillery Corps (Ireland)">Artillery Corps</a> where she appears on the corps insignia, half dressed, holding a harp, sitting on a field cannon.
</p><p>Saint Barbara's Day, December 4, is celebrated by the British (<a href="/wiki/Royal_Artillery" title="Royal Artillery">Royal Artillery</a>, RAF Armourers, Royal Engineers), Royal Navy Fleet Air Arm Armourers, Australian (<a href="/wiki/Royal_Regiment_of_Australian_Artillery" class="mw-redirect" title="Royal Regiment of Australian Artillery">Royal Regiment of Australian Artillery</a>, RAAF Armourers), Canadian (Explosive Ordnance Disposal Technicians (EOD), Canadian Ammunition Technicians, Canadian Air Force Armourers, <a href="/wiki/Royal_Regiment_of_Canadian_Artillery" title="Royal Regiment of Canadian Artillery">Royal Canadian Artillery</a>, Canadian Military Field Engineers, Royal Canadian Navy Weapons Engineering Technicians), and New Zealand (RNZN Gunners Branch, <a href="/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Artillery" class="mw-redirect" title="Royal New Zealand Artillery">RNZA</a>, <a href="/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Army_Ordnance_Corps" title="Royal New Zealand Army Ordnance Corps">Royal New Zealand Army Ordnance Corps</a>, RNZAF Armourers) armed forces.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> It is celebrated by the <a href="/wiki/Norwich_University" title="Norwich University">Norwich University</a> Artillery Battery with a nighttime fire mission featuring multiple <a href="/wiki/M116_howitzer" title="M116 howitzer">M116 howitzers</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Saint Barbara's Day is celebrated by <a href="/wiki/United_States_Army" title="United States Army">United States Army</a> and <a href="/wiki/United_States_Marine_Corps" title="United States Marine Corps">Marine Corps</a> Field and Air Defense Artillery, many Marine Corps Explosive Ordnance Disposal Technicians.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The units and sub-units celebrate the day with church parades, sports days, guest nights, cocktail parties, dinners and other activities.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Several mining institutions also celebrate it, such as some branches of the Australian Institute of Mining and Metallurgy.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The West Australian Mining Club celebrate Saint Barbara's Day and use it to remember those people who have died working in the mining industry during the year.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Although they do not celebrate her saint's day, she is also the patron saint of US Navy and Marine Corps Aviation Ordnancemen.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Central_Europe">Central Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Central Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>In Germany, <span title="German-language text"><i lang="de">Barbarazweig</i></span> is the custom of bringing branches into the house on December 4 to bloom on Christmas.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Saint Barbara is revered as a patron saint of miners and in extension, the geosciences in general, including the tunneling industry.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> This connection is particularly strong in the Catholic areas of Germany, as for example <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>. Some university geology departments hold annual 'Barabarafests' if not on the 4th then the closest Friday,<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> or within <a href="/wiki/Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg" title="Baden-Württemberg">Baden-Württemberg</a>, see <a href="/wiki/University_of_T%C3%BCbingen" title="University of Tübingen">University of Tübingen</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/University_of_Freiburg" title="University of Freiburg">University of Freiburg</a><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> or <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bonn" title="University of Bonn">University of Bonn</a><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> or applied geosciences of the <a href="/wiki/Technische_Universit%C3%A4t_Darmstadt" title="Technische Universität Darmstadt">Technische Universität Darmstadt</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hesse" title="Hesse">Hesse</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>, a statue of Saint Barbara is placed near the future tunnel portal during the groundbreaking ceremony of most major tunneling projects, owing to her being the patron saint of miners.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>In Poland, the salt mine at <a href="/wiki/Wieliczka_Salt_Mine" title="Wieliczka Salt Mine">Wieliczka</a> honours Saint Barbara in Saint Kinga's chapel.
</p><p>In France, due to the historic link between the firefighters and the military sappers, Saint Barbara is also the patron of firefighters and has thus been celebrated by fire services throughout the country on December 4 since the <a href="/wiki/French_Third_Republic" title="French Third Republic">Third Republic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Spain.2C_Portugal_and_former_colonies"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Spain,_Portugal_and_former_colonies">Spain, Portugal and former colonies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Spain, Portugal and former colonies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Spanish military artillerymen, mining engineers and miners also venerate her as patron saint. Parades, masses, dinners and other activities are held in her honour.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara,_Barichara_02.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara%2C_Barichara_02.jpg/220px-Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara%2C_Barichara_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara%2C_Barichara_02.jpg/330px-Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara%2C_Barichara_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara%2C_Barichara_02.jpg/440px-Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara%2C_Barichara_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4739" data-file-height="3159" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Capilla_de_Santa_B%C3%A1rbara,_Barichara_02.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Santa Bárbara Church in <a href="/wiki/Barichara" title="Barichara">Barichara</a>, Colombia</div></div></div>
<p>A portion of the coast of <a href="/wiki/California" title="California">California</a>, now occupied by the city of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Barbara,_California" title="Santa Barbara, California">Santa Barbara, California</a> and located approximately 100 miles northwest of <a href="/wiki/Los_Angeles" title="Los Angeles">Los Angeles</a>, is named after her.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The City of Santa Barbara got its name from the early Spanish navigator Juan Cabrillo. On December 4, the explorer stopped at a particular place on the California coast. He chose to name the spot after the patron of that day, Saint Barbara.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> missionary church, the <a href="/wiki/Mission_Santa_Barbara" title="Mission Santa Barbara">Mission Santa Barbara</a>, was founded there on her feast day in 1786, and is one of the twenty-one such churches that were operated by the Franciscan Order and collectively known as the <i>California missions</i>. The <a href="/wiki/Presidio_of_Santa_Barbara" title="Presidio of Santa Barbara">Presidio of Santa Barbara</a> was built in 1782, with the mission of defending the Second Military District in Spanish California. <a href="/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Barbara County">Santa Barbara County</a> was one of the twenty-seven original counties of California, formed in 1850 at the time of statehood.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> The county's territory was later divided to create <a href="/wiki/Ventura_County,_California" title="Ventura County, California">Ventura County</a> in 1873.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Other Spanish and Portuguese settlements named Santa Barbara were established in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chile</a>, <a href="/wiki/Colombia" title="Colombia">Colombia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Honduras" title="Honduras">Honduras</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>, <a href="/wiki/Venezuela" title="Venezuela">Venezuela</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Afro-Cuban" class="mw-redirect" title="Afro-Cuban">Afro-Cuban</a> religion of <a href="/wiki/Santer%C3%ADa" title="Santería">Santería</a>, Barbara is <a href="/wiki/Syncretism" title="Syncretism">syncretized</a> with <a href="/wiki/Shango" title="Shango">Chango</a>, the deity of fire, lightning, and thunder. In <a href="/wiki/Afro-Brazilian" class="mw-redirect" title="Afro-Brazilian">Afro-Brazilian</a> religions of <a href="/wiki/Candombl%C3%A9" title="Candomblé">Candomblé</a> and <a href="/wiki/Umbanda" title="Umbanda">Umbanda</a>, she is often identified as <a href="/wiki/Yansan" class="mw-redirect" title="Yansan">Yansan</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Orisha" title="Orisha">orisha</a> of wind and storms.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Eastern_Europe.2C_Eastern_Orthodox_Church"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Eastern_Europe,_Eastern_Orthodox_Church">Eastern Europe, Eastern Orthodox Church</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Eastern Europe, Eastern Orthodox Church">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>, alleged<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> relics of Saint Barbara are kept in <a href="/wiki/St._Michael%27s_Golden-Domed_Monastery" title="St. Michael&#39;s Golden-Domed Monastery">St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> A church of Great-Martyr Barbara (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Храм великомучениці Варвари</span></span>) of <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_(Moscow_Patriarchate)" title="Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate)">Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate)</a> is located near <a href="https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BB%D1%96%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%BB%D1%96%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BA" class="extiw" title="uk:Кирилівський лісопарк">Kyrylivskyi Hai</a> (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Кирилівський Гай</span></span>) park in <a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>. In 2019 the <a href="/wiki/19th_Missile_Brigade_(Ukraine)" title="19th Missile Brigade (Ukraine)">19th Missile Brigade</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Ground_Forces" title="Ukrainian Ground Forces">Ukrainian Ground Forces</a> received the honorary title "Saint Barbara".<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a>, Saint Barbara's Day is celebrated as <span title="Georgian-language romanization"><i lang="ka-Latn">Barbaroba</i></span> on December 17 (which is December 4 in the <a href="/wiki/Old_style_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Old style calendar">old style calendar</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> The traditional festive food is <span title="Georgian-language romanization"><i lang="ka-Latn"><a href="/wiki/Lobiani" title="Lobiani">lobiani</a></i></span>, bread baked with a bean stuffing.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>, the day is celebrated by the Artillery Corps of the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Army">Greek Army</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Cypriot_National_Guard" title="Cypriot National Guard">Cypriot National Guard</a>. Artillery camps throughout the two countries host celebrations in honor of the saint, where the traditional sweet of <a href="/wiki/Loukoumades" class="mw-redirect" title="Loukoumades">loukoumades</a> is offered to soldiers and visitors, allegedly because it resembles cannonballs.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Saint Barbara is also the patron saint of the northern Greek city of <a href="/wiki/Drama,_Greece" title="Drama, Greece">Drama</a>, where a sweet called <span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">varvara</i></span>, which resembles a more liquid form of <a href="/wiki/Koliva" title="Koliva">koliva</a>, is prepared and consumed on her feast day.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a> Saint Barbara's day is celebrated by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a>, as <span title="Macedonian-language text"><span lang="mk">Варвара</span></span> <span title="Macedonian-language romanization"><i lang="mk-Latn">(Varvara)</i></span> on 17 December. Some Macedonians celebrate with their closest family and friends at home, while others refrain, believing that people who step in their house on Saint Barbara's day will give them either good or bad luck for the rest of the year.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a>, a cave shrine to Saint Barbara (<a href="/wiki/Kuys_Varvara" title="Kuys Varvara">Kuys Varvara</a>) is located on the slopes of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Ara" class="mw-redirect" title="Mount Ara">Mount Ara</a> and lit candles and flower garlands are used as tribute.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Eid_il-Burbara" title="Eid il-Burbara">Eid il-Burbara</a> or <i>Saint Barbara's day</i> is celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jordan" title="Jordan">Jordan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Israel" title="Israel">Israel</a> among <a href="/wiki/Arab_Christians" title="Arab Christians">Arab Christians</a> annually on December 4, in a feast day similar to that of North American Halloween.<sup id="cite_ref-Gerversp162_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gerversp162-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> The traditional food for the occasion is <i><a href="/wiki/Burbara" class="mw-redirect" title="Burbara">Burbara</a></i>, a bowl of boiled <a href="/wiki/Wheat" title="Wheat">wheat</a>, <a href="/wiki/Barley" title="Barley">barley</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pomegranate" title="Pomegranate">pomegranate</a> seeds, raisins, <a href="/wiki/Anise" title="Anise">anise</a> and sugar. Shredded <a href="/wiki/Coconut" title="Coconut">Coconut</a>, <a href="/wiki/Walnut" title="Walnut">Walnuts</a> or <a href="/wiki/Almond" title="Almond">almonds</a> can be added.<sup id="cite_ref-Carter_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Carter-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Baramki_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baramki-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> The general belief among <a href="/wiki/Christianity_in_Lebanon" title="Christianity in Lebanon">Lebanese Christians</a> is that Saint Barbara disguised herself in numerous characters to elude the Romans who were persecuting her, and the tradition states that when the saint was escaping from the army of the pagan king in <a href="/wiki/Baalbek" title="Baalbek">Baalbek</a>, she passed in a field of wheat, and the wheat grew miraculously in order to hide her footprints from the soldiers, and this is the reason of serving the traditional wheat dessert on the feast day. In the Christian village of <a href="/wiki/Aboud" title="Aboud">Aboud</a> in the <a href="/wiki/West_Bank" title="West Bank">West Bank</a>, there is a shrine in a cave that the saint reportedly took refuge in.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> It is celebrated on December 17.
</p><p>Many churches in <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a> are dedicated in her name, including one in Moscow, next to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Basil%27s_Cathedral" title="Saint Basil&#39;s Cathedral">Saint Basil's Cathedral</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Yaroslavl" title="Yaroslavl">Yaroslavl</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_feast_of_Santa_Barbara_in_Italy">The feast of Santa Barbara in Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: The feast of Santa Barbara in Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The feast of Santa Barbara is the main religious feast of <a href="/wiki/Patern%C3%B2" title="Paternò">Paternò</a>, in the province of Catania, dedicated to Santa Barbara, the patron saint of the city, originally from Nicomedia, in Bithynia (current İzmit in Turkey) and martyred according to tradition in 306 by father Dioscuro.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The event takes place annually on December 3, 4, 5 and 11, May 27 and July 27. December 4 represents the date of the martyrdom of the saint, May 27 is the feast of the patronage of Santa Barbara during which the miracle of the stopping of the eruption of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Etna" title="Mount Etna">Etna</a> in 1780 is remembered, while July 27 is the feast of the arrival of the relics that were brought to Paternò in 1576.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The feast of Santa Barbara is one of the most beautiful Catholic feasts in Italy.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span title="The material near this tag may use weasel words or too-vague attribution. (March 2022)">according to whom?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<p>The United States Army Field Artillery Association and the United States Army Air Defense Artillery Association maintain the <i>Order of Saint Barbara</i> as an honorary military society of the United States Army Field Artillery and the United States Army Air Defense Artillery. Members of both the United States Army and United States Marine Corps, along with their military and civilian supporters, are eligible for membership.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_legacy">Cultural legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Barbara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Cultural legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Major_Barbara_01.PNG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Major_Barbara_01.PNG/220px-Major_Barbara_01.PNG" decoding="async" width="220" height="226" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Major_Barbara_01.PNG/330px-Major_Barbara_01.PNG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Major_Barbara_01.PNG/440px-Major_Barbara_01.PNG 2x" data-file-width="583" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Major_Barbara_01.PNG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Major Barbara, <b>The Court Theatre 1904–1907</b> (1907)</div></div></div>
<p>The name of the <a href="/wiki/Barbiturate" title="Barbiturate">barbiturate</a> family of pharmaceutical drugs is believed to derive from the suggestion by an artilleryman commemorating the feast of Saint Barbara in 1864, whom the chemist <a href="/wiki/Adolf_von_Baeyer" title="Adolf von Baeyer">Adolf von Baeyer</a> encountered at a local tavern whilst celebrating his recent discovery of the parent compound.<sup id="cite_ref-rzepa_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rzepa-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Barbara is mentioned in Thomas Pynchon's novel <i><a href="/wiki/Against_the_Day" title="Against the Day">Against the Day</a></i>. The December fourth holiday is compared to the Fourth of July, as being more celebrated by the Dynamiters.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>
Saint Barbara is mentioned in <a href="/wiki/Federico_Garc%C3%ADa_Lorca" title="Federico García Lorca">Federico García Lorca</a>'s play <i><a href="/wiki/La_Casa_de_Bernarda_Alba" class="mw-redirect" title="La Casa de Bernarda Alba">La Casa de Bernarda Alba</a></i> (1936). According to this drama, a popular Spanish phrase regarding this saint in the early 20th century was:</p><blockquote>
<p>Blessed Santa Barbara, /
Your story is written in the sky, /
With paper and holy water.
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The first Spanish-language <i><a href="/wiki/Telenovela" title="Telenovela">telenovela</a></i> filmed in color for TV in the US was the 1973 production of <i>Santa Bárbara, Virgen y Mártir</i>, filmed entirely on location in <a href="/wiki/Hialeah,_Florida" title="Hialeah, Florida">Hialeah, Florida</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/G._K._Chesterton" title="G. K. Chesterton">G. K. Chesterton</a> wrote the Ballad of Saint Barbara,<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> interweaving the Legend of the Saint with the contemporary account of the huge artillery barrages that turned the <a href="/wiki/First_Battle_of_the_Marne" title="First Battle of the Marne">First Battle of the Marne</a>.
</p><p><i><a href="/wiki/Major_Barbara" title="Major Barbara">Major Barbara</a></i> is a play by <a href="/wiki/George_Bernard_Shaw" title="George Bernard Shaw">George Bernard Shaw</a> in which the title character is an officer in the <a href="/wiki/Salvation_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Salvation Army">Salvation Army</a> and grapples with the moral dilemma of whether this Christian denomination should accept donations from her father, who is an armaments manufacturer.<sup id="cite_ref-Albert_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Albert-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In "Time Bomb," an episode of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Closer" title="The Closer">The Closer</a></i>, the LAPD deploy a bomb-squad robot named Babs, after St. Barbara in her role as patron saint of artillery and explosives personnel.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Saint Barbara's story is mentioned in a live version of The Hold Steady's song "Don't Let Me Explode" from Lollapalooza.
</p><p>The original play "Hala and the King" <a class="external text" href="https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%87%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A9_%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%84%D9%83_(%D9%85%D8%B3%D8%B1%D8%AD%D9%8A%D8%A9)">هالة_والملك_(مسرحية)</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Rahbani_brothers" title="Rahbani brothers">Rahbani_brothers</a> starring <a href="/wiki/Fairuz" title="Fairuz">Fairuz</a> is based on the traditional celebrations of the Saint Barbara Feast in Lebanon, the songs of the first act use the same musical rhyme used by the children until today during the feast, and the concept of costumes in the play is based on the local practices during the feast.
</p>
