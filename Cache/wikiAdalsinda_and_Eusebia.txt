					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p><p><b>Adalsinda</b> or <b>Adalsindis of Hamay</b> and <b>Eusebia of Douai</b>, were 7th-century <a href="/wiki/Columbanus#Rule_of_Saint_Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columban</a> nuns, who were sisters from a prominent <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> family; Eusebia became an Abbess. They are venerated as saints in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Their parents were <a href="/wiki/Richtrudis" class="mw-redirect" title="Richtrudis">Richtrudis</a>, a Gascoigne-Basque heiress, and <a href="/wiki/Adalbard" title="Adalbard">Adalbard</a> I of Ostrevent, a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> duke of <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>. Both mother and father are also recognised as saints,<sup id="cite_ref-smet_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> as are another sister, <a href="/wiki/Clotsinda" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotsinda">Clotsinda</a>, and a brother, <a href="/wiki/Maurontius_of_Douai" title="Maurontius of Douai">Maurontius</a>. They are especially venerated in Northern France and Flanders.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eusebia's commemoration is on 16 March; Adalsinda's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 25 December, around the date of her death, by tradition "during the solemnities of Christmas".<sup id="cite_ref-bnb_Ram_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bnb_Ram-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_two_abbeys">The two abbeys</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalsinda_and_Eusebia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The two abbeys">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following their father's death in an attack, near <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigueux" title="Périgueux">Périgueux</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;652</span>, the sisters' mother, Richtrudis, retired to the <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes_Abbey" title="Marchiennes Abbey">Marchiennes Abbey</a> that she and her husband had founded in 630. Her three daughters accompanied her there. All four became nuns, Richtrudis taking the role of abbess at Marchiennes. As Marchiennes had been made a <a href="/wiki/Dual_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Dual monastery">dual monastery</a> by Richtrudis around 647, her son Maurontius, once he became a monk, was also at the same abbey for a time. The abbey followed the <a href="/wiki/Columbanus#Rule_of_Saint_Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Rule of Saint Columbanus</a> from its founding until 1024, when it became a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-smet_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The earlier established (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;625</span>) Hamay Abbey nearby at <a href="/wiki/Wandignies-Hamage" title="Wandignies-Hamage">Wandignies-Hamage</a> was located on the opposite side of the river <a href="/wiki/Scarpe_(river)" title="Scarpe (river)">Scarpe</a>; it, too, had a connection to the family, being overseen by the sisters' paternal great-grandmother who was its founding abbess, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Gertrude_of_Hamay&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Gertrude of Hamay (page does not exist)">Saint Gertrude of Hamay</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_d%27Hamage" class="extiw" title="fr:Gertrude d&#39;Hamage">fr</a>&#93;</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-smet_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-revue_1945_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-revue_1945-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Gertrude_4_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gertrude_4-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The smaller Hamay Abbey was later absorbed by its larger neighbour, Marchienne, possibly when Marchienne became a solely male Benedictine establishment in 1024.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Adalsinda">Adalsinda</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalsinda_and_Eusebia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Adalsinda">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Adalsinda<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Adalsinde</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> the youngest child of the family, entered Marchiennes Abbey in <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;653</span>, with her mother and sisters. Later she went to the abbey at Hamay, where her sister Eusebia had become abbess in succession to their great-grandmother, Saint Gertrude. Gertrudes's widowed daughter, Gerberta –who was Adalbard's mother, and so the sisters' grandmother– was also a nun of Hamay Abbey. Clotsinda remained at Marchiennes, with her mother.<sup id="cite_ref-Gertrude_4_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gertrude_4-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalsinda's year of death is uncertain; some histories recount that she predeceased her mother, who died in 688, either giving the year as <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;673</span> or stating that she died very young. For example, authors P.F.X. de Ram (1866) and Dunbar (1904) give this earlier timing for her death.<sup id="cite_ref-Dunbar_Adal_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dunbar_Adal-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-bnb_Ram_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bnb_Ram-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Writing in 2007, Dries van den Akker, a Jesuit author and editor stated, "more recent sources, which are based on historical research, give the year 715 as her date of death".<sup id="cite_ref-Akker_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akker-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This is the year given in the 1921 Benedictines of Ramsgate's <i>Book of Saints</i> and a 1945 essay by Cristiani.<sup id="cite_ref-revue_1945_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-revue_1945-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> At least one modern work (1985) shows both years for Adalsinda's death in different sections, as Akker notes.<sup id="cite_ref-Akker_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akker-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Adalisinda</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholicism">Catholicism</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Eastern Orthodoxy</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Pre-congregation" class="mw-redirect" title="Pre-congregation">Pre-congregation</a>; <a href="/wiki/December_25_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)#Pre-Schism_Western_saints" title="December 25 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">pre-Schism Western saints</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">25 December</td></tr></tbody></table>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Eusebia">Eusebia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalsinda_and_Eusebia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Eusebia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Eusebia<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> of Douai was born about 637, the eldest daughter of Richtrudis (or Rictrude) and Adalbard. <a href="/wiki/Maurontius_of_Douai" title="Maurontius of Douai">Maurontius of Douai</a> was her elder brother. According to Dunbar's 1904 <i>Dictionary of Saintly Women</i>, Queen <a href="/wiki/Nanthild" title="Nanthild">Nanthild</a> was Eusebia's godmother and had gifted her with the fine estate of Verny near <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Dunbar_Adal_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dunbar_Adal-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eusebia (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Eusébie</i>), was sent to the nearby convent of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbaye_d%27Hamage&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbaye d&#39;Hamage (page does not exist)">Abbaye d'Hamage</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbaye_d%27Hamage" class="extiw" title="fr:Abbaye d&#39;Hamage">fr</a>&#93;</span> (alternatively known as 'Hamay' or 'Hamay-sur-la-<a href="/wiki/Scarpe_(river)" title="Scarpe (river)">Scarpe</a>'). This was at the request of her great-grandmother and founding abbess of Hamay, Saint Gertrude. In thus adopting a family member as her protégé, Gertrude's actions are consistent with the monastic system of the time. Controlled by the ruling, landholding class that was closely linked to the Merovingian monarchy, ensuring succession by close relatives was a way to retain power and prestige within families.<sup id="cite_ref-Gertrude_4_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gertrude_4-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before her death, Gertrude named Eusebia her successor and she was duly elected abbess upon her great-grandmother's demise. Eusebia was but twelve years old, and her mother considering her too young for such responsibility, placed Hamay under the direction of Marchiennes. Eusebia eventually returned to Hamay, where she assumed her role of abbess. Her younger sister Adalsinda later joined her there. Abbess Eusebia died around 680. In Belgium and northern France she is called Ysoie, Isoie or Eusoye.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
