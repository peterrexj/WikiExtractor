<p><b>Simon of Trent</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Simon von Trient</i>, also known as Simon Unverdorben (meaning Simon Immaculate in German); <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Simonino di Trento</i>), also known as <b>Simeon</b> (1472–1475), was a boy from the city of <a href="/wiki/Trento" title="Trento">Trent</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Trento" title="Trento">Trento</a> in northern Italy), in the <a href="/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Trent" title="Prince-Bishopric of Trent">Prince-Bishopric of Trent</a>, whose disappearance and death was blamed on the leaders of the city's <a href="/wiki/Jewish" class="mw-redirect" title="Jewish">Jewish</a> community, based on the confessions of Jews obtained under <a href="/wiki/Judicial_torture" class="mw-redirect" title="Judicial torture">judicial torture</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Events">Events</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Events">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the time of the events, Prince-Bishop <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Hinderbach" title="Johannes Hinderbach">Johannes Hinderbach</a> reigned in Trent under the ultimate jurisdiction of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor_Frederick_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Emperor Frederick III">Holy Roman Emperor Frederick III</a>. In March 1475, an itinerant <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> preacher, <a href="/wiki/Bernardine_of_Feltre" title="Bernardine of Feltre">Bernardine of Feltre</a>, had delivered a series of sermons in Trent in which he vilified the local Jewish community. That Jewish community consisted of three households headed by Samuel (who arrived in 1461), Tobias, and Engel.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They formed a distinct community marked by their professions and by their apparent wealth in comparison with the artisans and <a href="/wiki/Sharecropping" title="Sharecropping">sharecroppers</a> of the city: Samuel was a moneylender and Tobias a physician.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Prince-Bishop had renewed the Jewish community's permission to reside and practice their professions in Trent a few years previously, in 1469.<sup id="cite_ref-Hsia_1992_16_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hsia_1992_16-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This dependence on the protection of the authorities later inclined the Jews, upon finding Simon's body, to report the discovery.<sup id="cite_ref-finding_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-finding-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The events themselves have been reconstructed from a careful study of <a href="/wiki/Prozess_gegen_die_Juden_von_Trient" title="Prozess gegen die Juden von Trient">the trial records</a> by American historian <a href="/wiki/Ronnie_Hsia" title="Ronnie Hsia">Ronnie Hsia</a>. Simon, almost two and a half years old, went missing about 5 p.m. on the evening of Thursday, 23 March 1475. The following day, <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a>, Simon's father approached the prince-bishop to ask for help in finding his missing child.<sup id="cite_ref-searches_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-searches-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Podest%C3%A0" title="Podestà">podestà</a>, Giovanni de Salis, had his men spread a description of Simon through the city. Over the following couple of days, searches were carried out by Simon's family and neighbours, by the servants of the podestà, and also by the Jewish community, who had been alerted to a rumour that they had taken the child and were concerned about the possibility of being framed.<sup id="cite_ref-searches_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-searches-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-finding_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-finding-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> On Saturday, 25 March, Simon's father appealed to the podestà specifically to search the Jews' houses, saying he had been advised they might have taken his child.<sup id="cite_ref-searches_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-searches-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Despite these searches, no sign of the child was found. Samuel's property was extensive, including a hall that functioned as a synagogue, and a water cellar that was also used for <a href="/wiki/Mikvah" class="mw-redirect" title="Mikvah">ritual bathing</a> and was supplied with water from a channel that ran beneath the property.<sup id="cite_ref-Hsia_1992_16_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hsia_1992_16-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to the trial record, on Easter Sunday, 26 March, a cook named Seligman went to Samuel's cellar to fetch water to prepare the evening meal and found Simon's body in the water. Samuel himself, accompanied by two other Jews, went to the podestà to report the discovery.<sup id="cite_ref-finding_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-finding-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Later that evening, the podestà and some of his men retrieved the body, with his servant Ulrich being ordered to carry it to the hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The narrative summary based on the trial documents, drafted in 1478–1479, omitted the fact that the Jews had themselves reported finding the body, stating only that Ulrich had found Simon's body in a ditch next to Samuel's house.<sup id="cite_ref-searches_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-searches-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following the report of the body's discovery, the entire Jewish community (both men and women) were arrested and forced under torture to confess to having murdered Simon to use his blood for ritual purposes (a classic example of the <a href="/wiki/Blood_libel" title="Blood libel">blood libel</a> that Jews used Christian blood in their rituals). An examination of the corpse by city doctors determined that Simon had not died of natural causes but had been <a href="/wiki/Exsanguination" title="Exsanguination">exsanguinated</a>. In Hsia's view, "the narrative imperative, the official story of ritual murder, the trial record of 1475–76, represents nothing less than a Christian <a href="/wiki/Ethnography" title="Ethnography">ethnography</a> of Jewish rites".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fifteen of the Jews, including Samuel, the head of the community, were sentenced to death and <a href="/wiki/Burnt_at_the_stake" class="mw-redirect" title="Burnt at the stake">burnt at the stake</a>. The Jewish women were accused as accomplices but argued their gender did not allow them to participate in the rituals restricted to men. They were freed from prison in 1478 due to papal intervention. One Jew, Israel, was allowed to convert to Christianity for a short while, but he was arrested again after other Jews confessed he was part of the <a href="/wiki/Passover_Seder" title="Passover Seder">Passover Seder</a>. After a long period of torture he was also sentenced to death on 19 January.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The Trent trial's notoriety inspired a rise in Christian violence towards Jews in the surrounding areas of <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Tyrol" title="Tyrol">Tyrol</a>, along with accusations of ritual murder, culminating in <a href="/wiki/Vicenza" title="Vicenza">Vicenza</a> with the prohibition of Jewish moneylending in 1479 and the expulsion of all Jews in 1486.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_investigation">Papal investigation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papal investigation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 3 August 1475, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a> commanded Bishop Hinderbach to suspend judicial proceedings until the arrival of the papal representative, <a href="/wiki/Battista_dei_Giudici" title="Battista dei Giudici">Battista dei Giudici</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ventimiglia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ventimiglia">Bishop of Ventimiglia</a>, who would conduct a joint investigation with the Bishop of Trent. Giudici arrived in Trent in September. The local authorities worked against his investigation, preventing him from visiting Jews in prison and impeding his access to trial records.<sup id="cite_ref-Quaglioni_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quaglioni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the face of persistent hostility, he relocated to <a href="/wiki/Rovereto" title="Rovereto">Rovereto</a>, which was then under Venetian control,<sup id="cite_ref-Quaglioni_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quaglioni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and summoned Hinderbach and the podestà to answer for their conduct. Instead of appearing, Hinderbach had an account of the proceedings drawn up to vindicate his own actions, circulating it widely and so giving general credence to the notion that Simon of Trent had in fact been murdered by Jews.
</p><p>The case was reviewed in Rome, where Hinderbach had powerful friends, including the papal librarian <a href="/wiki/Bartolomeo_Sacchi" class="mw-redirect" title="Bartolomeo Sacchi">Bartolomeo Sacchi</a>, who accused Giudici of being in the pay of the Jews. Giudici wrote two treatises on the affair, an <i>Apologia Iudaeorum</i> defending the Jews, and an <i>Invectiva contra Platinam</i> (aimed at Sacchi) defending himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Quaglioni_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quaglioni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> A committee of cardinals, chaired by Giovan Francesco Pavini, former professor of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Padua" title="University of Padua">University of Padua</a> and an old friend of the bishop of Trent, exonerated Hinderbach and censured Giudici. A <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> was issued on 20 June 1478, accepting that the inquiries in Trent had been carried out in legal fashion but avoiding a finding of fact with regard to Simon's death, while also reasserting papal protection for the Jews and the unlawfulness of ritual murder trials, in line with a decree of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> from 1243.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:292px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Simonino_Trento.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Simonino_Trento.jpg/290px-Simonino_Trento.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="218" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Simonino_Trento.jpg/435px-Simonino_Trento.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Simonino_Trento.jpg/580px-Simonino_Trento.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Simonino_Trento.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>School of <a href="/wiki/Niklaus_Weckmann" title="Niklaus Weckmann">Niklaus Weckmann</a>,<br /> <i>The Martyrdom of Saint Simonino</i></div></div></div>
<p>Simon became the focus of attention for the local Catholic Church. The local bishop, Hinderbach of Trent, tried to have Simon canonized, producing a large body of documentation of the event and its aftermath.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Over one hundred miracles were directly attributed to Simon within a year of his disappearance, and his cult spread across Italy, Austria and Germany. However, there was also skepticism from the beginning, as Giudici's investigation showed.
</p><p>Maximilian I, a future Holy Roman Emperor, was a strong proponent of Simon's veneration and commissioned a silver monument of the child.<sup id="cite_ref-getty_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-getty-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He also had Simon's relics carried in procession when he was made emperor in 1508.
</p><p>The veneration received wider liturgical impetus in the 16th century. <a href="/wiki/Joannes_Molanus" title="Joannes Molanus">Joannes Molanus</a> included a footnote on Simon of Trent in his 1568 edition of <a href="/wiki/Usuard" title="Usuard">Usuard</a>'s martyrology,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and this was then incorporated into the new official edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology"><i>Martyrologium Romanum</i></a> in 1583,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> with 24 March having the additional text: <i>Tridenti passio sancti Simeonis pueri, a Judaeis saevissime trucidati, qui multis postea miraculis coruscavit.</i> ("At Trent, the suffering of the holy boy Simeon, barbarously murdered by the Jews, who was afterwards glorified by many miracles.")<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In 1584 the use of this martyrology became obligatory in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a>. Furthermore, in 1588 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V" title="Pope Sixtus V">Pope Sixtus V</a> gave recognition to the local veneration of Simon as an established devotion, functionally equivalent to a decree of <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-getty_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-getty-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Simon could thus be considered a martyr and a patron of kidnap and torture victims.
</p><p>In 1758, Cardinal Ganganelli (later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIV" title="Pope Clement XIV">Pope Clement XIV</a>, 1769–1774) prepared a legal memorandum which, to the exclusion of all other allegations of ritual murders of infants which records were thoroughly made available to him, expressly admitted as proven only two: that of Simon of Trent and that of <a href="/wiki/Andreas_Oxner" title="Andreas Oxner">Andreas Oxner</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> At the same time, he extols the glories and accomplishments of the Jewish people across history, writing that the murder of Simon of Trent does not suffice to injure the reputation of the entire Jewish people.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Simon's cultus was permitted by the Popes for local public liturgical observance (effectively <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>) within the Diocese of Trent.<sup id="cite_ref-getty_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-getty-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In a letter to Fr. Benedetto Vetrani, Promoter of Faith, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a> recognised that this was the case, but was careful to distinguish such authorization from canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As was stated in one publication, "It is simply untrue to say that the Church has <i>canonized</i> little Simon of Trent. A decree of beatification was issued by Sixtus V, which took the form simply of a confirmation of cultus and which allowed a Mass to be said locally in honour of the boy martyr. Everyone knows that beatification differs from canonization in this, that in the former case the infallibility of the Holy See is not involved, in the latter it is."<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following studies on the case, on 28 October 1965 (the same day as the publication of <i><a href="/wiki/Nostra_aetate" title="Nostra aetate">Nostra aetate</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>) archbishop of Trent <a href="/w/index.php?title=Alessandro_Maria_Gottardi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Alessandro Maria Gottardi (page does not exist)">Alessandro Maria Gottardi</a> abolished the cult of Simon, and the yearly procession with his relics was supressed.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> "Simon of Trent is not in the new Roman Martyrology of 2000, nor on any modern Catholic calendar."<sup id="cite_ref-getty_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-getty-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His relics, removed from their resting place in Saints Peter and Paul church in Trent upon the cult's suppression, were returned there in 2021, together with an exhibit about him curated by the <i>Museo diocesano tridentino</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Controversies">Controversies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Controversies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the 21st century, historian <a href="/wiki/Ariel_Toaff" title="Ariel Toaff">Ariel Toaff</a>, writing about the case of Simon of Trent, hypothesized that the notion that some Jews killed children to use their blood for ritual purposes may have been tenuously based on an actual "ritual of blood" that did not involve infanticide. In the same work, <i><a href="/wiki/Passovers_of_Blood" title="Passovers of Blood">Passovers of Blood</a></i>, Toaff theorises that some blood libel cases may be based on actual instances of infanticide where a Jewish perpetrator acted in retaliation for persecutions against the community.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> After criticism, the book was withdrawn from circulation and redacted by its author.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2020, the Italian artist <a href="/w/index.php?title=Giovanni_Gasparro&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Giovanni Gasparro (page does not exist)">Giovanni Gasparro</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Gasparro" class="extiw" title="it:Giovanni Gasparro">it</a>&#93;</span> painted a depiction of Simon's death. He was later accused of Antisemitism for this painting which sold to a private collector.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In February 2022, a sculpture of Simon of Trent depicting the blood libel was used by <a href="/wiki/QAnon" title="QAnon">QAnon</a> conspiracy theorists to promote the conspiracy theory that Hollywood elites <a href="/wiki/Adrenochrome#In_popular_culture" title="Adrenochrome">are harvesting adrenochrome from children</a> through <a href="/wiki/Satanic_ritual_abuse" class="mw-redirect" title="Satanic ritual abuse">Satanic ritual abuse</a> in order to become <a href="/wiki/Immortality" title="Immortality">immortal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
