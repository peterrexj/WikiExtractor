<p><b>Saints Justa and Rufina (Ruffina)</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Santa Justa y Santa Rufina</i>) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>. They are said to have been martyred at <a href="/wiki/Hispalis" class="mw-redirect" title="Hispalis">Hispalis</a> (<a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>) during the 3rd century.
</p><p>Only St. Justa (sometimes "Justus" in early manuscripts) is mentioned in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> (93), but in the historical martyrologies. Rufina is also mentioned, following the legendary <i>Acts</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The two saints are highly honored in the <a href="/wiki/Mozarabic_Rite" title="Mozarabic Rite">Mozarabic Liturgy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justa_and_Rufina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their legend states that they were sisters and natives of Seville who are said to have lived in the neighborhood of <a href="/wiki/Triana_(Seville)" class="mw-redirect" title="Triana (Seville)">Triana</a>. Justa was born in 268 AD, Rufina in 270 AD, of a poor but pious Christian family. They made fine <a href="/wiki/Earthenware" title="Earthenware">earthenware</a> <a href="/wiki/Pottery" title="Pottery">pottery</a> for a living, with which they supported themselves and helped many of the city's poor. Like many other merchants, they sold their pottery from booths set up out of doors where people could see their wares. During a <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> festival, they refused to sell their wares for use in these celebrations.  In anger, locals broke all of their dishes and pots.  Justa and Rufina retaliated by smashing an image of <a href="/wiki/Venus_(mythology)" title="Venus (mythology)">Venus</a>. They were immediately arrested.<sup id="cite_ref-rcl_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rcl-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The city's prefect, Diogenianus, ordered them to be imprisoned.  Failing to convince them to renounce their faith, he had them <a href="/wiki/Tortured" class="mw-redirect" title="Tortured">tortured</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">rack</a> and with iron hooks.  
They were then forced to walk barefoot to the <a href="/wiki/Sierra_Morena" title="Sierra Morena">Sierra Morena</a>; when this did not break their resolve, they were imprisoned without water or food. Justa died first. Her body, thrown into a well, was later recovered by the Bishop Sabinus. Diogenianus believed that the death of Justa would break the resolve of Rufina. Rufina refused to renounce her faith and was thus thrown to the lions. However the lions in the <a href="/wiki/Amphitheatre" title="Amphitheatre">amphitheatre</a> refused to attack Rufina, remaining as docile as house cats.  Infuriated, Diogenianus had Rufina's neck broken<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and her body burned. Her remains were recovered by Sabinus and buried alongside her sister.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justa_and_Rufina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santas_Justa_y_Rufina,_de_Juan_de_Espinal_(Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Santas_Justa_y_Rufina%2C_de_Juan_de_Espinal_%28Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla%29.jpg/220px-Santas_Justa_y_Rufina%2C_de_Juan_de_Espinal_%28Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="175" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Santas_Justa_y_Rufina%2C_de_Juan_de_Espinal_%28Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla%29.jpg/330px-Santas_Justa_y_Rufina%2C_de_Juan_de_Espinal_%28Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Santas_Justa_y_Rufina%2C_de_Juan_de_Espinal_%28Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla%29.jpg/440px-Santas_Justa_y_Rufina%2C_de_Juan_de_Espinal_%28Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4500" data-file-height="3589" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santas_Justa_y_Rufina,_de_Juan_de_Espinal_(Ayuntamiento_de_Sevilla).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Santas Justa y Rufina, de Juan de Espinal (Ayuntamiento de Sevilla)</div></div></div>
<p>Their cult in Seville was ancient and strong, and soon spread elsewhere in Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/La_Seo_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="La Seo Cathedral">La Seo Cathedral</a> (<a href="/wiki/Zaragoza" title="Zaragoza">Zaragoza</a>) contains a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> dedicated to Justa and Rufina.  <a href="/wiki/Agost" title="Agost">Agost</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Valencia_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Valencia (province)">Valencia province</a>, is the location of a <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> dedicated to these saints (<i>Ermita de Santa Justa y Rufina</i>), built in 1821.  <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a> also has a church dedicated to them.
</p><p>There is a shrine to the saints in <a href="/wiki/Alicante" title="Alicante">Alicante</a> where a three-day fiesta is held in their honor in July.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justa_and_Rufina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Justa and Rufina are the patron saints of the cities Seville<sup id="cite_ref-goya_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goya-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Orihuela" title="Orihuela">Orihuela</a>, where there is a parish Church of Saints Justa and Rufina built on the site of a Visigoth church that was later used as a mosque.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  They are also the patrons of potters.<sup id="cite_ref-rcl_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rcl-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to tradition, they are protectors of the <a href="/wiki/Giralda" title="Giralda">Giralda</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Seville" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Seville">Cathedral of Seville</a>, and are said to have protected both during the <a href="/wiki/Lisbon_earthquake_of_1755" class="mw-redirect" title="Lisbon earthquake of 1755">Lisbon earthquake of 1755</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justa_and_Rufina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Their feast day is 19 July.<sup id="cite_ref-aoca_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aoca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During the Middle Ages their feast was celebrated in the Iberian Peninsula on 17 July, as attested by calendars of the time, such as for example by that in the Antiphonary of <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">León</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justa_and_Rufina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>They are often depicted as young women with their heads uncovered (indicating their status as unmarried), with clay pots, palms (representing martyrdom), and a lion licking Rufina's bare foot.<sup id="cite_ref-goya_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goya-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> As patrons of Seville, they are often pictured flanking the "Giralda".<sup id="cite_ref-Stracke_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stracke-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Justa_and_Rufina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Justa and Rufina were a popular subject for Spanish artists.
</p><p>A 1540 <a href="/wiki/Retable" title="Retable">retable</a> is the earliest known piece of artwork depicting these two saints.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  A painting of the saints was done by <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Camilo" title="Francisco Camilo">Francisco Camilo</a> in 1644. <a href="/wiki/Goya" class="mw-redirect" title="Goya">Goya</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-goya_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goya-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><a href="/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Murillo" title="Bartolomé Esteban Murillo">Murillo</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Francisco_de_Zurbar%C3%A1n" title="Francisco de Zurbarán">Zurbarán</a> also painted these saints.
</p><p>A 1989 painting is a modern interpretation of these saints.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
