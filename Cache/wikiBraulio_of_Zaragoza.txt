<p><b>Braulio</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Braulius Caesaraugustanus</i>; 585 – 651 AD) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Zaragoza" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Zaragoza">bishop of Zaragoza</a> and a learned cleric living in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Visigoths" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the Visigoths">Kingdom of the Visigoths</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Braulio_of_Zaragoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Braulio was born of a noble Hispano-Roman family. His father was <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Osma-Soria" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Osma-Soria">Bishop of Osma</a>. In 610 Braulio became a monk, and later studied under <a href="/wiki/Isidore_of_Seville" title="Isidore of Seville">Isidore</a> at <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>. Archbishop Isidore used education to counteract increasingly influential Gothic barbarism in his jurisdiction. Braulio was ordained by Isadore in 624. In 625 Braulio returned to Zaragoza where his brother John was then bishop, and served as his <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">archdeacon</a>. Upon his brother's death in 631, Braulio succeeded him as bishop. Known for almsgiving and preaching, he was an advisor and confidant of several <a href="/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom" title="Visigothic Kingdom">Visigoth kings</a>, including <a href="/wiki/Chindasuinth" title="Chindasuinth">Chindasuinth</a>, whose son <a href="/wiki/Recceswinth" title="Recceswinth">Recceswinth</a> he recommended be installed as associate king.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Braulio worked with Isidore to convert the Visigoths from Arianism.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He is reported to have encouraged Isidore of Seville in his <a href="/wiki/Etymologiae" title="Etymologiae">encyclopaedic</a> ambitions, and to have had a hand in the revision of his works. Bishop Braulio, to whom Isidore dedicated it and sent it for correction, divided it into its twenty books.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Braulio called it <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">quaecunque fere sciri debentur</i></span>, "practically everything that it is necessary to know".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was present at the <a href="/wiki/Councils_of_Toledo" title="Councils of Toledo">councils of Toledo</a> in 633, 636, and 638 and he responded on behalf of the <a href="/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula" title="Iberian Peninsula">Iberian</a> clergy to <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/Honorius_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Honorius I">Honorius I</a>'s charge that they were neglectful of their duties. He wrote a life of <a href="/wiki/Emilian_of_Cogolla" class="mw-redirect" title="Emilian of Cogolla">San Millan</a>.  Towards the end of his life, he lost his eyesight.<sup id="cite_ref-Weber_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Weber-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in what is now the church of <a href="/wiki/Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_del_Pilar_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuestra Señora del Pilar Basilica">Nuestra Señora del Pilar</a> in Zaragoza.  He was succeeded as bishop of Zaragoza by <a href="/wiki/Taius" title="Taius">Taius</a> (Taio), who had been his pupil.
</p><p>He is buried in <a href="/wiki/La_Seo_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="La Seo Cathedral">La Seo Cathedral</a>, Zaragoza, and is the patron saint of Aragon and of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Zaragoza" title="University of Zaragoza">University of Zaragoza</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oqr_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oqr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
