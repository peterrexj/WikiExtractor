<p><b>Obitius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sant'Obizio</i>) (February 4, c. 1150 - December 6, c. 1204) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  He was born in <a href="/wiki/Niardo" title="Niardo">Niardo</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Brescia" title="Province of Brescia">province of Brescia</a>, around 1150 (tradition holds that the day was February 4).  His father, Gratiadeus (Graziodeo), was a <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a> and governor of <a href="/wiki/Valcamonica" class="mw-redirect" title="Valcamonica">Valcamonica</a>.  Obitius was devoted to <a href="/wiki/Margaret_the_Virgin" title="Margaret the Virgin">Margaret the Virgin</a> as a youth, and displayed an image of this virgin martyr and his armorial device.<sup id="cite_ref-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His family had connections with the local religious community.  An uncle had founded two <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> and another relative had befriended <a href="/wiki/Constantius_of_Perugia" title="Constantius of Perugia">Constantius of Perugia</a>, the other <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Niardo.  Obitius nevertheless became a knight and married the countess Inglissenda Porro, with whom he had four children: Jacopo, Berta, Margherita, and Maffeo.  Obitius had a successful military career as a knight, and participated in the wars between Cremona and Brescia.<sup id="cite_ref-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion">Conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Obitius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>An event led Obitius to abandon his military career.  On July 7, 1191, at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_the_Malamorte&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Battle of the Malamorte (page does not exist)">Battle of the Malamorte</a>, on the <a href="/wiki/Oglio_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Oglio River">Oglio River</a>, in Brescian territory, Obitius was leading an army against the <a href="/wiki/Bergamo" title="Bergamo">Bergamaschi</a>.  The Bergamese retreated across a wooden bridge, and they were pursued by Obitius and his fellow knights.  The collective weight of the soldiers, the knights’ armor, and the knights' horses caused the bridge to collapse, and Obitius and all the combatants plunged into the water.   According to his legend, while he was in the water, he had a terrifying vision of <a href="/wiki/Hell" title="Hell">Hell</a>.  Obitius managed to escape from <a href="/wiki/Drowning" title="Drowning">drowning</a> and thereafter decided to dedicate himself to a spiritual life.  Despite resistance to this idea from his family, Obitius’ determination eventually swayed them, and two of his children, Margherita and Maffeo, also became a nun and monk, respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>    
</p><p>Obitius lived in completely poverty, <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a>, and prayer and dedicated himself to working for a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>.  In 1197, he was eventually allowed to become an <a href="/wiki/Oblate_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblate (religion)">oblate</a> in the monastery of Santa Giulia in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>.  Obitius spent the rest of his life at the monastery, performing various acts of <a href="/wiki/Charity_(practice)" title="Charity (practice)">charity</a>.  <a href="/wiki/Miracles" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracles">Miracles</a> were attributed to him.  He was buried with great honor at the monastery church of Santa Giulia in  Brescia.<sup id="cite_ref-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sant&#39;_Obizio_da_Niardo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Obitius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1498, it was reported that the <a href="/wiki/Urn" title="Urn">urn</a> containing Obitius’ <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> began to miraculously issue water.  His relics were translated to the altar of Santa Giulia and in 1553, the same phenomenon was again reported.  In the <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christianity)">jubilee year</a> of 1600, his cult was approved by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a>.
</p><p>In 1526-7, <a href="/wiki/Romanino" title="Romanino">Romanino</a> painted the story of Obitius’ life in a series of frescoes (<i>Storie di sant'Obizio</i>), in the basilica of San Salvatore at Brescia.
</p><p>When the monastery of Santa Giulia was suppressed in 1797 during the era of the <a href="/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic" title="Cisalpine Republic">Cisalpine Republic</a>, Niardo demanded the relics of Obitius from Brescia and are today contained in the main altar of the parish church of Niardo.
</p>
