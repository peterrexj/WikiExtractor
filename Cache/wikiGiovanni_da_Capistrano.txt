<p><b>John of Capistrano</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Giovanni da Capestrano</i>, <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Kapisztrán János</i>, <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Jan Kapistran</i>, <a href="/wiki/Croatian_language" title="Croatian language">Croatian</a>: <i lang="hr">Ivan Kapistran</i>) (24 June 1386 – 23 October 1456) was a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> from the Italian town of <a href="/wiki/Capestrano" title="Capestrano">Capestrano</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abruzzo" title="Abruzzo">Abruzzo</a>. Famous as a preacher, theologian, and <a href="/wiki/Inquisitor" title="Inquisitor">inquisitor</a>, he earned himself the nickname "the Soldier Saint" when in 1456 at age 70 he led a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a> against the invading <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_(1456)" title="Siege of Belgrade (1456)">siege of Belgrade</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungarian</a> military commander <a href="/wiki/John_Hunyadi" title="John Hunyadi">John Hunyadi</a>.
</p><p>Elevated to sainthood, he is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Jurist" title="Jurist">jurists</a> and <a href="/wiki/Military_chaplain" title="Military chaplain">military chaplains</a>, as well as the namesake of the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/List_of_Spanish_missions" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Spanish missions">missions</a>  <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Juan_Capistrano" title="Mission San Juan Capistrano">San Juan Capistrano</a> in <a href="/wiki/Southern_California" title="Southern California">Southern California</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Juan_Capistrano_(Texas)" title="Mission San Juan Capistrano (Texas)">San Juan Capistrano</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Antonio,_Texas" class="mw-redirect" title="San Antonio, Texas">San Antonio, Texas</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As was the custom of this time, John is denoted by the village of <a href="/wiki/Capestrano" title="Capestrano">Capestrano</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Sulmona" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Sulmona">Diocese of Sulmona</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Abruzzi" class="mw-redirect" title="Abruzzi">Abruzzi</a> region, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>. His father had come to Italy with the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Valois-Anjou" title="House of Valois-Anjou">Angevin</a> court of <a href="/wiki/Louis_I_of_Anjou" title="Louis I of Anjou">Louis I of Anjou</a>, titular King of <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>. He studied law at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Perugia" title="University of Perugia">University of Perugia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hess_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hess-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1412, King <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_of_Naples" title="Ladislaus of Naples">Ladislaus of Naples</a> appointed him Governor of Perugia, a tumultuous and resentful papal fief held by Ladislas as the pope's <a href="/wiki/Champion" title="Champion">champion</a>, in order to effectively establish public order. When war broke out between Perugia and the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Malatesta" title="House of Malatesta">Malatestas</a> in 1416, John was sent as ambassador to broker a peace, but Malatesta threw him in prison. It was during this imprisonment that he began to study theology. When he was released in he decided to give up the world and become a Franciscan friar,<sup id="cite_ref-FaithND_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FaithND-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> citing a dream where Saint <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> ordered him to enter the Order. He had married before the war, but asserted the marriage was never consummated and received permission to take <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders" title="Holy orders">holy orders</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Friar_and_preacher">Friar and preacher</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Friar and preacher">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Together with <a href="/wiki/James_of_the_Marches" title="James of the Marches">James of the Marches</a>, John entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> at <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> on 4 October 1416. Along with James, he studied <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at <a href="/wiki/Fiesole" title="Fiesole">Fiesole</a>, near Florence,<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> under <a href="/wiki/Bernardine_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernardine of Siena">Bernardine of Siena</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hess_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hess-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He soon gave himself up to the most rigorous <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>, violently defending the ideal of strict observance and <a href="/wiki/Orthodoxy" title="Orthodoxy">orthodoxy</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> following the example set by Bernardine. From 1420 onwards, he preached with great effect in numerous cities and eventually became well known. He was ordained in 1425.
</p><p>Unlike most Italian preachers of <a href="/wiki/Repentance" title="Repentance">repentance</a> in the 15th century, John was effective in <a href="/wiki/Northern_Europe" title="Northern Europe">northern</a> and <a href="/wiki/Central_Europe" title="Central Europe">central Europe</a>—in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">German</a> states of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Moravia" title="Moravia">Moravia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_(1385%E2%80%931569)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Poland (1385–1569)">Kingdom of Poland</a>. The largest churches could not hold the crowds, so he preached in the public squares—at <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> in Italy, he preached to a crowd of 126,000.<sup id="cite_ref-hess_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hess-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reformer">Reformer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Reformer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg/220px-Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg/330px-Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg/440px-Santi_Buglioni_-_San_Giovanni_di_Capistrano_c1550.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2336" data-file-height="3504" /></a><figcaption>San Giovanni di Capistrano, by Santi Buglioni <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1550</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>When he was not preaching, John was  writing tracts against <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a> of every kind. This facet of his life is covered in great detail by his early biographers, Nicholas of Fara, Christopher of Varese and Girlamo of Udine. While he was thus evangelizing, he was actively engaged in assisting Bernardine of Siena in the reform of the Franciscan Order, largely in the interests of a more rigorous discipline in the Franciscan communities.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Like Bernardine, he strongly emphasized devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Name_of_Jesus" title="Holy Name of Jesus">Holy Name of Jesus</a>, and, together with Bernardine, was accused of <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a> on this account. In 1429, these Observant friars were called to Rome to answer charges of heresy, and John was chosen by his companions to speak for them. They were both acquitted by the Commission of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinals</a> appointed to judge the accusations.
</p><p>He was frequently deployed to embassies by Popes <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Eugene IV</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V" title="Pope Nicholas V">Nicholas V</a>: in 1439, he was sent as <a href="/wiki/Legatus" title="Legatus">legate</a> to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy" title="Duchy of Burgundy">Burgundy</a>, to oppose the claims of the <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_VIII_of_Savoy" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus VIII of Savoy">Antipope Felix V</a>; in 1446, he was on a mission to the King of France; in 1451 he went at the request of the emperor as <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Nuncio" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Nuncio">Apostolic Nuncio</a> to Austria. During the period of his nunciature, John visited all parts of the Empire. As legate, or inquisitor, he persecuted the last <a href="/wiki/Fraticelli" title="Fraticelli">Fraticelli</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ferrara" title="Ferrara">Ferrara</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Jesuati" title="Jesuati">Jesuati</a> of <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Crypto-Jew" class="mw-redirect" title="Crypto-Jew">Crypto-Jews</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Moldavia" class="mw-redirect" title="Principality of Moldavia">Moldavia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, and, above all, the <a href="/wiki/Hussite" class="mw-redirect" title="Hussite">Hussites</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Germany" title="Kingdom of Germany">Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Hungary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Crown of Bohemia">Bohemia</a>; his aim in the last case was to make talks impossible between the representatives of Rome and Bohemian "Hussite king" <a href="/wiki/George_of_Podiebrad" class="mw-redirect" title="George of Podiebrad">George of Podiebrad</a>, for every attempt at conciliation seemed to him to be conniving at heresy.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He also worked for the reform of the Order of Friars Minor. He upheld, in his writings, speeches and sermons, theories of <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">papal supremacy</a> rather than the theological wranglings of councils (see <a href="/wiki/Conciliar_Movement" class="mw-redirect" title="Conciliar Movement">Conciliar Movement</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> John, together with his teacher, Bernardine, his colleague, James of the Marche, and <a href="/wiki/Albert_Berdini_of_Sarteano" title="Albert Berdini of Sarteano">Albert Berdini of Sarteano</a>, are considered the four great pillars of the Observant reform among the Friars Minor.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Anti-Jewish_incitement">Anti-Jewish incitement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Anti-Jewish incitement">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was known as the "Scourge of the Jews".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He saw it as an act of love to preach against Judaism because they did not accept Jesus as their Messiah.  In 1450 the Franciscan "Jew-baiter" arranged a forced disputation at Rome with a certain Gamaliel called "Synagogæ Romanæ magister".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-JE-Disputations_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE-Disputations-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BenSasson-JVL-Debates_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BenSasson-JVL-Debates-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Between 1451 and 1453, his fiery sermons against Jews persuaded  many southern German regions to expel their entire Jewish population. <sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> However, “That in dealing with heretics and Jews he transgressed established bounds and thereby failed against Christian charity is a thought practically unknown to contemporaries. He was at times censured as impractical, but never as uncharitable or inhuman. Even Doering, one of his severest critics, finds nothing to blame in Capistrano’s behavior toward the Jews.”<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Soldier_Priest">The Soldier Priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: The Soldier Priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:JohnofCapistranoCoat.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/9/94/JohnofCapistranoCoat.jpg" decoding="async" width="132" height="220" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="132" data-file-height="220" /></a><figcaption>The saint's coat of arms, with a sword piercing a crescent moon, on the Papal <a href="/wiki/Ombrellino" class="mw-redirect" title="Ombrellino">Ombrellino</a> at <a href="/wiki/Mission_Basilica_San_Juan_Capistrano" title="Mission Basilica San Juan Capistrano">Mission Basilica San Juan Capistrano</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png/220px-Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png/330px-Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png/440px-Coat_of_Arms_Saint_John_of_Capistrano_reconstruction.png 2x" data-file-width="3465" data-file-height="3465" /></a><figcaption>Reconstructed Coat of arms of John of Capistrano</figcaption></figure> 
<p>After the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople" title="Fall of Constantinople">Fall of Constantinople</a> in 1453, the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>, under Sultan <a href="/wiki/Mehmed_II" title="Mehmed II">Mehmed II</a>, threatened Christian Europe. That following year <a href="/wiki/Pope_Callixtus_III" title="Pope Callixtus III">Pope Callixtus III</a> sent John, who was already aged seventy, to preach a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">Crusade</a> against the invading <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Turks</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Diet_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" title="Imperial Diet (Holy Roman Empire)">Imperial Diet</a> of <a href="/wiki/Frankfurt" title="Frankfurt">Frankfurt</a>. Gaining little response in Bavaria and Austria, he decided to concentrate his efforts in Hungary. By July 1456 Capistrano managed to raise a large, albeit poorly trained force made up of peasants and local countryside landlords, with which he advanced towards Belgrade which was under siege by Turkish forces. Though poorly equipped they were highly motivated. Capistrano and <a href="/wiki/John_Hunyadi" title="John Hunyadi">John Hunyadi</a> traveled together, though commanding the army separately. Between them, they had gathered around 40,000–50,000 troops altogether. 
</p><p>Hunyadi managed to break the naval blockade on the Danube, and breach the siege, bringing reinforcements and supplies to the city. By some accounts, the peasant soldiers started a spontaneous action, and forced Capistrano and Hunyadi to take advantage of it. Despite Hunyadi's orders to the defenders not to try to loot the Ottoman positions, some of the units crept out from demolished ramparts, took up positions across from the Ottoman line, and began harassing enemy soldiers. Ottoman Sipahis tried without success to disperse the harassing force. As more defenders joined those outside the wall. What began as an isolated incident quickly escalated into a full-scale battle.
</p><p>John of Capistrano at first tried to order his men back inside the walls, but soon found himself surrounded by about 2,000 peasant levymen. He then began leading them toward the Ottoman rear across the Sava river. At the same time, Hunyadi started a desperate charge out of the fort to take the cannon positions in the Ottoman encampment. In the aftermath of a fierce battle, the Ottomans withdrew and retreated under cover of darkness; the siege was lifted. His involvement led to Capistrano being called "the Soldier Priest". Although he survived the battle, John fell victim to the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a>, which flourished in the unsanitary conditions prevailing among armies of the day. He died on 23 October 1456<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> at the nearby town of <a href="/wiki/Ilok" title="Ilok">Ilok</a> (now a <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatian</a> border town on the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood_and_feast_day">Sainthood and feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Sainthood and feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The year of John of Capistrano's <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> is variously given as 1690 <sup id="cite_ref-lodi_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lodi-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a> or as 1724 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a>. In 1890, his feast day was included for the first time in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> and assigned to 28 March.<sup id="cite_ref-hun-saints_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hun-saints-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In 1969, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> moved his feast day to 23 October, the day of his death. Where Mass and the Office are said according to the <a href="/wiki/1962_Roman_Missal" class="mw-redirect" title="1962 Roman Missal">1962 Roman Missal</a> and its concomitant calendar, his feast day remains on March 28.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Eponym">Eponym</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Eponym">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a Franciscan reformer preaching simplicity, John became the <a href="/wiki/Eponym" title="Eponym">eponym</a> of two Spanish <a href="/wiki/List_of_Spanish_missions" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Spanish missions">missions</a> founded by the Franciscan friars in the north of the then-Spanish Americas: <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Juan_Capistrano" title="Mission San Juan Capistrano">Mission San Juan Capistrano</a> in present-day <a href="/wiki/Southern_California" title="Southern California">Southern California</a><sup id="cite_ref-FaithND_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FaithND-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Juan_Capistrano_(Texas)" title="Mission San Juan Capistrano (Texas)">Mission San Juan Capistrano</a> just outside the city center of present-day <a href="/wiki/San_Antonio,_Texas" class="mw-redirect" title="San Antonio, Texas">San Antonio</a> in Texas.<sup id="cite_ref-engelhardt_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-engelhardt-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patron_saint">Patron saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Capistrano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Patron saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is the patron saint of military chaplains and jurists.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
