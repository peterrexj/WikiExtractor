<p><b>Mary Helen MacKillop</b> <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sister_of_St_Joseph" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sister of St Joseph">RSJ</a> (15 January 1842 – 8 August 1909) was an Australian religious sister who has been declared a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, as <b>St Mary of the Cross</b>. Of <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Australian" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Australian">Scottish descent</a>, she was born in <a href="/wiki/Melbourne" title="Melbourne">Melbourne</a> but is best known for her activities in <a href="/wiki/South_Australia" title="South Australia">South Australia</a>. Together with <a href="/wiki/Julian_Tenison-Woods" title="Julian Tenison-Woods">Julian Tenison-Woods</a>, she founded the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_St_Joseph_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Sisters of St Joseph of the Sacred Heart">Sisters of St Joseph of the Sacred Heart</a> (the Josephites), a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">religious sisters</a> that established a number of schools and welfare institutions throughout Australia and New Zealand, with an emphasis on education for the rural poor.
</p><p>The process to have MacKillop declared a saint began in the 1920s, and she was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in January 1995 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict&#160;XVI</a> prayed at her tomb during his visit to <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a> for <a href="/wiki/World_Youth_Day_2008" title="World Youth Day 2008">World Youth Day 2008</a> and in December 2009 approved the Catholic Church's recognition of a second miracle attributed to her <a href="/wiki/Intercession_of_saints" title="Intercession of saints">intercession</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> on 17 October 2010, during a public ceremony in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">St Peter's Square</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SMH-saint_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SMH-saint-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She is the first Australian to be recognised by the Catholic Church as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-AlJazeera_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AlJazeera-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Mary MacKillop is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Brisbane" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Brisbane">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Brisbane</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Brisbaned_Patron_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brisbaned_Patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_ministry">Early life and ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mary Helen MacKillop was born on 15 January 1842 in what is now the Melbourne <a href="/wiki/Suburb" title="Suburb">suburb</a> of <a href="/wiki/Fitzroy,_Victoria" title="Fitzroy, Victoria">Fitzroy, Victoria</a> (at the time part of an area called Newtown in the then British colony of <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales" title="New South Wales">New South Wales</a>), to Alexander MacKillop and Flora MacDonald.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Although she continued to be known as "Mary", when she was baptised six weeks later she received the names Maria Ellen.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop's parents lived in <a href="/wiki/Roybridge" title="Roybridge">Roybridge</a> (<a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Gaelic language">Scottish Gaelic</a>: <i lang="gd">Drochaid Ruaidh</i>), <a href="/wiki/Lochaber" title="Lochaber">Lochaber</a>, <a href="/wiki/Inverness-shire" title="Inverness-shire">Inverness-shire</a>, Scotland, prior to emigrating to Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Others on both sides of the family had emigrated previously. MacKillop visited the village in the 1870s where St Margaret's, the local parish church of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Argyll_and_the_Isles" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Argyll and the Isles">Roman Catholic Diocese of Argyll and the Isles</a>, now has a shrine to her.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop's father, Alexander MacKillop, was born in <a href="/wiki/Perthshire" title="Perthshire">Perthshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He began his studies for the priesthood at age twelve, and two years later went to the <a href="/wiki/The_Scots_College_(Rome)" class="mw-redirect" title="The Scots College (Rome)">Scots College</a> in Rome; he also studied at <a href="/wiki/Blairs_College" title="Blairs College">Blairs College</a> in Kincardineshire, but at the age of 29 left, just before he was due to be <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a>. He migrated to Australia and arrived in Sydney in 1838.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> MacKillop's mother, Flora MacDonald, born in <a href="/wiki/Fort_William,_Scotland" title="Fort William, Scotland">Fort William</a>, had left Scotland and arrived in Melbourne in 1840.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her father and mother married in Melbourne on 14 July 1840. MacKillop was the eldest of their eight children.  Her younger siblings were Margaret ("Maggie", 1843–1872), John (1845–1867), Annie (1848–1929), Alexandrina ("Lexie", 1850–1882), Donald (1853–1925), Alick (who died at 11 months old) and Peter (1857–1878).<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Donald became a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest and worked among the <a href="/wiki/Aboriginal_Australians" title="Aboriginal Australians">Aboriginal</a> peoples in the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Territory" title="Northern Territory">Northern Territory</a>. Lexie became a member of the Good Shepherd Sisters in Melbourne.<sup id="cite_ref-Coleman_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coleman-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop was educated at private schools and by her father. She received her <a href="/wiki/First_Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="First Holy Communion">First Holy Communion</a> on 15 August 1850 at the age of nine. In February 1851, Alexander MacKillop left his family behind after having mortgaged the farm and their livelihood and made a trip to Scotland lasting some 17 months. Throughout his life he was a loving father and husband but not successful as either a farmer or gold prospector. Consequently, the family faced many hardships.<sup id="cite_ref-Coleman_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coleman-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop started work at the age of 16 as a clerk in a stationery store in Melbourne.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> To provide for her needy family, in 1860 she took a job as governess<sup id="cite_ref-sosjms_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sosjms-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> at the estate of her aunt and uncle, Alexander and Margaret MacKillop Cameron in <a href="/wiki/Penola,_South_Australia" title="Penola, South Australia">Penola, South Australia</a> where she was to look after their children and teach them.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Already set on helping the poor whenever possible, she included the other farm children on the Cameron estate as well. This brought her into contact with Fr Julian Tenison-Woods, who had been the parish priest in the south east since his ordination to the priesthood in 1857 after completing his studies at <a href="/wiki/Sevenhill" class="mw-redirect" title="Sevenhill">Sevenhill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop stayed for two years with the Camerons before accepting a job teaching the children of <a href="/wiki/Portland,_Victoria" title="Portland, Victoria">Portland, Victoria</a> in 1862. Later she taught at the Portland school and after opening her own boarding school, Bay View House Seminary for Young Ladies, now Bayview College, in 1864,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> was joined by the rest of her family.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founding_of_school_and_religious_congregation">Founding of school and religious congregation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Founding of school and religious congregation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg/170px-MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="254" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg/255px-MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg/340px-MaryMackillopMuseum0002.jpg 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="764" /></a><figcaption>MacKillop museum on Mount Street, <a href="/wiki/North_Sydney,_New_South_Wales" title="North Sydney, New South Wales">North Sydney</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1866, Julian Tenison-Woods invited MacKillop and her sisters Annie and Lexie to come to <a href="/wiki/Penola,_South_Australia" title="Penola, South Australia">Penola</a> and to open a Catholic school.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Woods was appointed director of education and became the founder, along with MacKillop, of a school they opened in a stable there. After renovations by their brother, the MacKillops started teaching more than 50 children.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BioGrowth_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioGrowth-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> At this time MacKillop made a declaration of her dedication to God and bega wearing black.<sup id="cite_ref-Vermeer_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermeer-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 21 November 1866, the feast day of the <a href="/wiki/Presentation_of_Mary" title="Presentation of Mary">Presentation of Mary</a>, several other women joined MacKillop and her sisters. MacKillop adopted the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of "Sister Mary of the Cross" and she and Lexie began wearing simple religious habits. The small group began to call themselves the Sisters of St Joseph of the Sacred Heart<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and moved to a new house in <a href="/wiki/Grote_Street,_Adelaide" class="mw-redirect" title="Grote Street, Adelaide">Grote Street, Adelaide</a>. There they founded a new school at the request of the bishop, <a href="/wiki/Laurence_Sheil" title="Laurence Sheil">Laurence Sheil</a> <a href="/wiki/Ordo_Fratrum_Minorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordo Fratrum Minorum">OFM</a>.<i><b><sup id="cite_ref-sosjms_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sosjms-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></b></i>
</p><p>The "rule of life" developed by Woods and MacKillop for the community emphasized poverty, a dependence on divine providence, no ownership of personal belongings, faith that God would provide and willingness to go where needed.<sup id="cite_ref-BioBeginnings_6-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioBeginnings-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The rule of life was approved by Bishop Sheil. By the end of 1867, ten other women had joined the Josephites, who adopted a plain brown <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a>. Due to the colour of their attire and their name, the Josephite sisters became colloquially known as the "<a href="/wiki/Joey_(marsupial)" class="mw-redirect" title="Joey (marsupial)">Brown Joeys</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-Vermeer_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermeer-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Expansion_of_the_Sisters_of_St_Joseph">Expansion of the Sisters of St Joseph</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Expansion of the Sisters of St Joseph">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg/170px-Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="266" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg/255px-Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg/340px-Mary_Mackillop_Memorial_Chapel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="488" data-file-height="764" /></a><figcaption>Mary MacKillop Chapel in <a href="/wiki/North_Sydney,_New_South_Wales" title="North Sydney, New South Wales">North Sydney</a>, which holds MacKillop's tomb</figcaption></figure>
<p>In an attempt to provide education to all the poor, particularly in rural areas, a school was opened in <a href="/wiki/Yankalilla,_South_Australia" title="Yankalilla, South Australia">Yankalilla, South Australia</a>, in October 1867. By the end of 1869, more than 70 members of the Sisters of St Joseph were educating children at 21 schools in Adelaide and the country. MacKillop and her Josephites were also involved with an <a href="/wiki/Orphanage" title="Orphanage">orphanage</a>; neglected children; girls in danger; the aged poor; a reformatory (in Johnstown near Kapunda); and a home for the aged and incurably ill.<sup id="cite_ref-MMKSum_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMKSum-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Generally, the Josephite sisters were prepared to follow farmers, railway workers and miners into the isolated <a href="/wiki/Outback" title="Outback">outback</a> and live as they lived.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2010)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In December 1869, MacKillop and several other sisters travelled to Brisbane to establish the order in <a href="/wiki/Queensland" title="Queensland">Queensland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BioGrowth_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioGrowth-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> They were based at <a href="/wiki/Kangaroo_Point,_Queensland" title="Kangaroo Point, Queensland">Kangaroo Point</a> and took the ferry or rowed across the <a href="/wiki/Brisbane_River" title="Brisbane River">Brisbane River</a> to attend Mass at <a href="/wiki/The_Cathedral_of_St_Stephen,_Brisbane" class="mw-redirect" title="The Cathedral of St Stephen, Brisbane">St Stephen's Cathedral</a>. Two years later, she was in <a href="/wiki/Port_Augusta" title="Port Augusta">Port Augusta</a>, South Australia for the same purpose. The Josephite congregation expanded rapidly and, by 1871, 130 sisters were working in more than 40 schools and charitable institutions across South Australia and Queensland.<sup id="cite_ref-MMKSum_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMKSum-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop clashed with the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Bishop_of_Brisbane" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Bishop of Brisbane">Roman Catholic Bishop of Brisbane</a>, <a href="/wiki/James_Quinn_(bishop)" title="James Quinn (bishop)">James Quinn</a>, over the control of the many schools she established; MacKillop believed the sisters should control the schools while Quinn believed the diocese should control them. In 1879, relationships between them had deteriorated to the point that Quinn directed the sisters to leave his diocese. Despite protests by the laity, Quinn was determined and MacKillop and her Josephite sisters had left the diocese by mid-1880 with other Catholic orders taking over the operation of their schools. When the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Rockhampton" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Rockhampton">Diocese of Rockhampton</a> was excised from the Brisbane diocese on 29 December 1882, it enabled MacKillop and her sisters to return to Queensland, where they established a school in <a href="/wiki/Clermont,_Queensland" title="Clermont, Queensland">Clermont</a> and then in other places within the new diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1881, Elzear Torreggiani, then Bishop of Armidale and a Capuchin who had worked in both North Wales at Pantasaph and London at Peckham, prior to being consecrated in London in 1879, for the Armidale Diocese; established Mother Mary MacKillop's Sisters of St Joseph at Tenterfield and defended their power of central government at the 1885<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Plenary Council.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the time Torreggiani was Bishop of Armidale the Sisters of St Joseph established foundations at Tenterfield (1880), Inverell (1880), Narrabri (1882), Glen Innes (1883), Uralla (1886), Quirindi (1888), Hillgrove (1889), Tingha (1890), Bingara (1902), Walgett (1902), Warialda (1904) and Manilla (1904).<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Subsequently, the sisters established Bundarra (1908), Barraba (1910), Boggabri (1911), Tamworth West (1919), Dungowan (1930), Tamworth South (1954), Lightning Ridge (1980), Mungindi (1995) and Attunga (1995). Wee Waa and Werris Creek were also "Motor Mission" centres.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Excommunication">Excommunication</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Excommunication">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bishop Sheil spent less than two years of his episcopate in Adelaide and his absences and poor health left the diocese effectively without clear leadership for much of his tenure. This resulted in bitter factionalisms within the clergy and disunity among the lay community. After the founding of the Josephites, Sheil appointed Fr Julian Woods as director general of Catholic education.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In Sept 2010, Fr Paul Gardiner,<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> historian and chaplain with the Mary MacKillop Penola Centre <sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> told Australian media that in 1871 Mary MacKillop had been banished following a series of events, including her uncovering and reporting clergy child sex abuse.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Gardiner explained that after hearing disturbing stories of alleged child abuse involving Fr Keating of the Kapunda parish in South Australia, MacKillop and other nuns spoke to Father Woods, who in turn approached the Vicar General in Adelaide. As a result, Keating was removed from his parish and sent back to Ireland where he continued as a priest. One of Keating's fellow priests, Father <a href="/wiki/Charles_Horan" title="Charles Horan">Charles Horan</a> OFM, was so angered by what Woods and the Josephites had exposed, he swore vengeance on them. 
</p><p>In addition to reporting the child abuse, Fr Woods and the Josephites also came into conflict with some clergy over educational matters<sup id="cite_ref-ADB_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ADB-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> and local clergy began a campaign to discredit the Josephites. As well as allegations of financial incompetence, rumours were also spread that MacKillop had a drinking problem. A 2010 investigation by Fr Paul Gardiner found no evidence to support these allegations.<sup id="cite_ref-Vermeer_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermeer-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In fact, it was widely known that she drank alcohol on doctor's orders to relieve the symptoms of <a href="/wiki/Dysmenorrhea" title="Dysmenorrhea">dysmenorrhea</a>, which often led to her being bedridden for days at a time.
Father Horan met with Sheil on 21 September 1871 and convinced him that the Josephites' constitution should be changed in a way that could have left the Josephite nuns homeless; the following day, when MacKillop apparently did not accede to the request, Sheil <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunicated</a> her, citing insubordination as the reason.<sup id="cite_ref-ADB_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ADB-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-abc_uncovering_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abc_uncovering-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Candid coverage in the Catholic newspaper <i><a href="/wiki/The_Irish_Harp_and_Farmers%27_Herald" title="The Irish Harp and Farmers&#39; Herald">The Irish Harp and Farmers' Herald</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> earned for its editor <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox_(editor)" title="Charles James Fox (editor)">C. J. Fox</a> ostracism and expulsion from the Catholic Association of which he was president.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Though the Josephites were not disbanded, most of their schools were closed in the wake of this action.<sup id="cite_ref-ADB_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ADB-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Forbidden to have contact with anyone in the church, MacKillop was given the rent-free use of two houses in <a href="/wiki/Flinders_Street,_Adelaide" title="Flinders Street, Adelaide">Flinders Street, Adelaide</a> by prominent Jewish merchant <a href="/wiki/Emanuel_Solomon" title="Emanuel Solomon">Emanuel Solomon</a><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> and was also sheltered by <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> priests. Some of the sisters chose to remain under <a href="/wiki/Diocesan" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocesan">diocesan</a> control, becoming popularly known as "Black Joeys".<sup id="cite_ref-Vermeer_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermeer-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On his deathbed, Sheil instructed Horan to lift the excommunication on MacKillop.<sup id="cite_ref-Vermeer_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermeer-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> On 21 February 1872, he met her on his way to <a href="/wiki/Willunga,_South_Australia" title="Willunga, South Australia">Willunga</a> and absolved her in the <a href="/wiki/Morphett_Vale" class="mw-redirect" title="Morphett Vale">Morphett Vale</a> church.<sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> An <a href="/wiki/Episcopate" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopate">episcopal commission</a> later completely exonerated her.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Rome">Rome</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Rome">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the acquisition of the Mother House in Kensington in 1872, MacKillop made preparations to leave for Rome to have the "Rule of Life" of the Sisters of St Joseph officially approved.
</p><p>MacKillop travelled to Rome in 1873 to seek papal approval for the <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> and was encouraged in her work by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The authorities in Rome made changes to the way Josephite sisters lived in regards to their commitment to poverty<sup id="cite_ref-MMKSum_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMKSum-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and declared that the superior general and her council were the authorities in charge of the congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> They assured MacKillop that the congregation and their "Rule of Life" would receive final approval after a trial period.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The resulting alterations to the "Rule of Life" regarding ownership of property caused a breach between MacKillop and Woods, who felt that the revised document compromised the ideal of vowed poverty and blamed MacKillop for not getting the document accepted in its original form.<sup id="cite_ref-ADB_28-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ADB-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Before Woods' death on 7 October 1889, he and MacKillop were personally reconciled, but he did not renew his involvement with the congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in Europe, MacKillop travelled widely to observe educational methods.<sup id="cite_ref-ADB_28-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ADB-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During this period, the Josephites expanded their operations into <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales" title="New South Wales">New South Wales</a> and New Zealand. MacKillop relocated to Sydney in 1883 on the instruction of <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Augustine_Reynolds" title="Christopher Augustine Reynolds">Bishop Reynolds</a> of Adelaide.<sup id="cite_ref-MMKSum_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMKSum-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_from_Rome">Return from Rome</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Return from Rome">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When MacKillop returned to Australia in January 1875, after an absence of nearly two years, she brought approval from Rome for her sisters and the work they did, materials for her school, books for the convent library, several priests and most of all, 15 new Josephites from Ireland. Regardless of her success, she still had to contend with the opposition of priests and several bishops. This did not change after her unanimous election as superior general in March 1875.<sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Josephites were unusual among Catholic church ministries in two ways. Firstly, the sisters lived in the community rather than in convents. Secondly the congregation's constitutions required administration by a superior general chosen from within the congregation rather than by the bishop, which was uncommon in its day. However, the issues which caused friction were that the Josephites refused to accept government funding, would not teach instrumental music (then considered an essential part of education by the church) and were unwilling to educate girls from more affluent families. This structure resulted in the sisters being forced to leave <a href="/wiki/Bathurst,_New_South_Wales" title="Bathurst, New South Wales">Bathurst</a> in 1876 and <a href="/wiki/Queensland" title="Queensland">Queensland</a> by 1880 due to the local bishops' refusal to accept this working structure.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Notwithstanding all the trouble, the congregation did expand. By 1877, it operated more than 40 schools in and around Adelaide, with many others in Queensland and New South Wales. With the help from Benson, Barr Smith, the Baker family, <a href="/wiki/Emanuel_Solomon" title="Emanuel Solomon">Emanuel Solomon</a> and other non-Catholics, the Josephites, with MacKillop as their leader and superior general, were able to continue the religious and other good works, including visiting prisoners in jail.
</p><p>After the appointment of <a href="/wiki/Roger_Vaughan" title="Roger Vaughan">Roger Vaughan</a> as Archbishop of Sydney in 1877, life became a little easier for MacKillop and her sisters. Until his death in 1882, the Revd Joseph Tappeiner had given MacKillop his solid support and, until 1883, she also had the support of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Reynolds" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Reynolds">Bishop Reynolds</a> of Adelaide.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>After the death of Vaughan in 1883, <a href="/wiki/Patrick_Francis_Moran" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrick Francis Moran">Patrick Francis Moran</a> became archbishop. Although he had a somewhat positive outlook toward the Josephites, he removed MacKillop as superior general and replaced her with Bernard Walsh.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MMKSum_18-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMKSum-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> gave official approval to the Josephites as a congregation in 1885, with its headquarters in Sydney.<sup id="cite_ref-BioChallenge_34-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BioChallenge-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 31 May 1886, Mary MacKillop's mother, Flora MacKillop was travelling from Melbourne to Sydney in the SS Ly-ee-Moon, to visit Mary and another daughter who was also a nun.  The ship struck a reef near the <a href="/wiki/Green_Cape_Lighthouse" title="Green Cape Lighthouse">Green Cape Lighthouse</a>.  Flora, along with 70 others, died.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Leo XIII gave the final approval to the Sisters of Saint Joseph of the Sacred Heart in 1888.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although still living through <a href="/wiki/Alms" title="Alms">alms</a>, the Josephite sisters had been very successful. In South Australia, they had schools in many country towns including, <a href="/wiki/Willunga,_South_Australia" title="Willunga, South Australia">Willunga</a>, Willochra, <a href="/wiki/Whyte_Yarcowie" class="mw-redirect" title="Whyte Yarcowie">Yarcowie</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mintaro" class="mw-redirect" title="Mintaro">Mintaro</a>, <a href="/wiki/Auburn,_South_Australia" title="Auburn, South Australia">Auburn</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jamestown,_South_Australia" title="Jamestown, South Australia">Jamestown</a>, <a href="/wiki/Laura,_South_Australia" title="Laura, South Australia">Laura</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sevenhill" class="mw-redirect" title="Sevenhill">Sevenhill</a>, <a href="/wiki/Quorn,_South_Australia" title="Quorn, South Australia">Quorn</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spalding,_South_Australia" title="Spalding, South Australia">Spalding</a>, <a href="/wiki/Georgetown,_South_Australia" title="Georgetown, South Australia">Georgetown</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robe,_South_Australia" title="Robe, South Australia">Robe</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pekina" title="Pekina">Pekina</a>, Appila and several others. MacKillop continued her work for the Josephites in Sydney and tried to provide as much support as possible for those in South Australia. In 1883 the order was successfully established at <a href="/wiki/Temuka" title="Temuka">Temuka</a> in <a href="/wiki/New_Zealand" title="New Zealand">New Zealand</a>, where MacKillop stayed for over a year.<sup id="cite_ref-MMNZ_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMNZ-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> In 1889 it was also established in the Australian state of <a href="/wiki/Victoria_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Victoria (Australia)">Victoria</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>During all these years MacKillop assisted Mother Bernard with the management of the Sisters of St Joseph. She wrote letters of support, advice and encouragement or just to keep in touch. By 1896, MacKillop was back in South Australia, visiting fellow sisters in <a href="/wiki/Port_Augusta" title="Port Augusta">Port Augusta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Burra,_South_Australia" title="Burra, South Australia">Burra</a>, Pekina, <a href="/wiki/Kapunda" title="Kapunda">Kapunda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jamestown,_South_Australia" title="Jamestown, South Australia">Jamestown</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gladstone,_South_Australia" title="Gladstone, South Australia">Gladstone</a>. That same year, she travelled again to New Zealand, spending several months in <a href="/wiki/Port_Chalmers" title="Port Chalmers">Port Chalmers</a> and <a href="/wiki/Arrowtown" title="Arrowtown">Arrowtown</a> in <a href="/wiki/Otago" title="Otago">Otago</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MMNZ_39-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MMNZ-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-NZ_Herald_10680874_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NZ_Herald_10680874-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> During her time in New Zealand with the Sisters of St Joseph, a school was established in Arrowtown, near Queenstown, South Island. Located in the grounds of St Patrick's Church, the small yellow cottage now known as Mary MacKillop cottage was originally built as a miner's house around 1870. It was bought by the church and incorporated into the church school in 1882 and then in 1897, MacKillop had the cottage and some of the school converted to a convent for the Sisters of St Joseph of the Sacred Heart who worked in New Zealand and Australia.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 1897, Bishop Maher of Port Augusta arranged for the Sisters of St Joseph to take charge of the St Anacletus Catholic Day School at Petersburg (now <a href="/wiki/Peterborough,_South_Australia" title="Peterborough, South Australia">Peterborough</a>).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Mary_MacKillop,_1890.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Saint_Mary_MacKillop%2C_1890.jpg/220px-Saint_Mary_MacKillop%2C_1890.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="320" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Saint_Mary_MacKillop%2C_1890.jpg/330px-Saint_Mary_MacKillop%2C_1890.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Saint_Mary_MacKillop%2C_1890.jpg/440px-Saint_Mary_MacKillop%2C_1890.jpg 2x" data-file-width="825" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>Saint Mary MacKillop, 1890</figcaption></figure>
<p>MacKillop founded a convent and base for the Sisters of St Joseph in Petersburg on 16 January 1897. "On January&#160;16th, 1897, the founder of the Sisters of St Joseph of the Sacred Heart, Mother Mary of the Cross,<sup id="cite_ref-ADB_28-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ADB-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> arrived in Petersburg to take over the school. She was accompanied by fellow Sisters Benizi (who was placed in charge of the school), M. Joseph, Clotilde and Aloysius Joseph. They were met at the station by the priest Norton who took them to the newly blessed convent, purchased for them on Railway Terrace."<sup id="cite_ref-40RailwayTerrace_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40RailwayTerrace-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> The property at 40 Railway Terrace is identified as the convent by a plaque placed by the Catholic diocese of Peterborough.<sup id="cite_ref-40RailwayTerrace_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40RailwayTerrace-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Mother Bernard, MacKillop was once more elected unopposed as superior general in 1899,<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> a position she held until her own death. During the later years of her life she had many problems with her health which continued to deteriorate. She suffered from <a href="/wiki/Rheumatism" title="Rheumatism">rheumatism</a> and after a <a href="/wiki/Stroke" title="Stroke">stroke</a> in <a href="/wiki/Auckland" title="Auckland">Auckland</a>, New Zealand in 1902, became paralysed on her right side. For seven years, she had to rely on a wheelchair to move around, but her speech and mind were as good as ever and her letter writing had continued unabated after she learned to write with her left hand. Even after suffering the stroke, the Josephite nuns had enough confidence in her to re-elect her in 1905.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg/220px-Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg/330px-Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg/440px-Klarfeld_First_Australian_Saint_St_Mary_Mackillop.jpg 2x" data-file-width="666" data-file-height="1000" /></a><figcaption>Life-size bronze statue of St Mary Mackillop by sculptor <a href="/wiki/Linda_Klarfeld" title="Linda Klarfeld">Linda Klarfeld</a> at the Australian Catholic University in North Sydney</figcaption></figure>
<p>MacKillop died on 8 August 1909 at the Josephite convent in <a href="/wiki/North_Sydney,_New_South_Wales" title="North Sydney, New South Wales">North Sydney</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sosjms_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sosjms-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The Archbishop of Sydney, Cardinal Moran, said: "I consider this day to have assisted at the deathbed of a saint."<sup id="cite_ref-Vermeer_17-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vermeer-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> She was laid to rest at the <a href="/wiki/Gore_Hill_Cemetery" title="Gore Hill Cemetery">Gore Hill Cemetery</a>, a few kilometres up the <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Highway,_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Pacific Highway, Australia">Pacific Highway</a> from North Sydney.
</p><p>After MacKillop's burial, people continually took earth from around her grave. As a result, her remains were <a href="/wiki/Exhumation" class="mw-redirect" title="Exhumation">exhumed</a> and transferred on 27 January 1914 to a <a href="/wiki/Burial_vault_(tomb)" title="Burial vault (tomb)">vault</a> before the <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a> in the newly built memorial chapel in Mount Street, North Sydney.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> The vault was a gift of Joanna Barr Smith, a lifelong friend and admiring <a href="/wiki/Presbyterian" class="mw-redirect" title="Presbyterian">Presbyterian</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonisation_and_commemoration">Canonisation and commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Canonisation and commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1925, the Mother Superior of the Sisters of St Joseph, Mother Laurence, began the process to have MacKillop declared a saint and <a href="/wiki/Michael_Kelly_(bishop)" title="Michael Kelly (bishop)">Michael Kelly</a>, Archbishop of Sydney, established a tribunal to carry the process forward. The process for MacKillop's beatification began in 1926, was interrupted in 1931 but began again in April 1951 and was closed in September of that year. After several years of hearings, close examination of MacKillop's writings and a 23-year delay, the initial phase of investigations was completed in 1973.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> 
A longtime and prominent non-Catholic promoter of her cause was poet-bookseller <a href="/wiki/Max_Harris_(poet)" title="Max Harris (poet)">Max Harris</a>.
After further investigations, MacKillop's "<a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>" was declared in 1992. That same year, the church endorsed the belief that Veronica Hopson, apparently dying of leukaemia in 1961, was cured by praying for MacKillop's <a href="/wiki/Intercession_of_saints" title="Intercession of saints">intercession</a>; MacKillop was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 19 January 1995 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_15-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> For the occasion of the beatification, the Croatian-Australian artist <a href="/wiki/Charles_Billich" title="Charles Billich">Charles Billich</a> was commissioned to paint MacKillop's official commemorative.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 19 December 2009, the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> issued a <a href="/wiki/Papal_decree" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal decree">papal decree</a> formally recognising a second miracle, the complete and permanent cure of Kathleen Evans<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> of inoperable lung and secondary brain cancer in the 1990s.<sup id="cite_ref-Maley2009-12-20_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maley2009-12-20-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> Kathleen Evans went on to publish in 2012 with Penguin Books, "The Story Behind Saint Mary MacKillop's second Miracle."<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonisation</a> was announced on 19 February 2010 and subsequently took place on 17 October 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-ABC2010-02-19_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABC2010-02-19-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> This made her the first Australian to be recognised as a saint by the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-AlJazeera_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AlJazeera-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Recognition">Recognition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Recognition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the week leading up to her canonisation, the Australian federal government announced that it was protecting the use of MacKillop's name for commercial purposes.<sup id="cite_ref-NameProtect2_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NameProtect2-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> According to a statement from the office of the Prime Minister of Australia, <a href="/wiki/Julia_Gillard" title="Julia Gillard">Julia Gillard</a>, the only other individual Australian whose name has similar protection is Australian cricket legend Sir <a href="/wiki/Donald_Bradman" class="mw-redirect" title="Donald Bradman">Donald Bradman</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NameProtect3_50-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NameProtect3-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> Australia Post issued an official postage stamp to recognise MacKillop's canonisation.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg/220px-Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg/330px-Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg/440px-Mary_mackillop_rose.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>The Mary MacKillop rose</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg/220px-Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg/330px-Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg/440px-Diver_Derrik_and_Mary_McKillop_bridges_open_in_2010.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3006" data-file-height="2004" /></a><figcaption>The rail portion of the <a href="/wiki/Tom_%27Diver%27_Derrick_Bridge" title="Tom &#39;Diver&#39; Derrick Bridge">Tom 'Diver' Derrick Bridge</a>, which opened in <a href="/wiki/Port_River" title="Port River">Port River</a>, <a href="/wiki/Port_Adelaide" title="Port Adelaide">Port Adelaide</a> in 2008, is named after MacKillop</figcaption></figure>
<p>An estimated 8,000 Australians were present in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a> to witness the ceremony.<sup id="cite_ref-ABC-Pilgrims_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ABC-Pilgrims-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Museum" class="mw-redirect" title="Vatican Museum">Vatican Museum</a> held an exhibition of <a href="/wiki/Aboriginal_art" class="mw-redirect" title="Aboriginal art">Aboriginal art</a> to honour the occasion titled "Rituals of Life".<sup id="cite_ref-Vat-Art_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-Art-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> The exhibition contained 300 artifacts which were on display for the first time since 1925.<sup id="cite_ref-BPN-Rudd_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BPN-Rudd-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>MacKillop is remembered in numerous ways, particularly in Australia. Things named for her include the <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_MacKillop" title="Electoral district of MacKillop">electoral district of MacKillop</a> in South Australia and several <a href="/wiki/MacKillop_College_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect" title="MacKillop College (disambiguation)">MacKillop colleges</a>. In 1985, the Sisters of St Joseph approached one of Australia's foremost rose growers to develop the Mary MacKillop Rose.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> MacKillop was the subject of the first of the "Inspirational Australians" one dollar coin series, released by the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Australian_Mint" title="Royal Australian Mint">Royal Australian Mint</a> in 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-RAM_56-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RAM-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Several Australian composers have written sacred music to celebrate MacKillop. For the occasion of her beatification the MacKillop Secretariat commissioned eight composers in 1994 to write some of the first liturgical hymns to MacKillop. These were published in 1995 by the Secretariat as an anthology entitled <i>If I Could Tell The Love of God</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup> Hymns specifically used in St Mary of the Cross celebrations include <i>A Saint for Today</i> and <i>Mary MacKillop, Woman of Australia</i> by Josephite Sister Margaret Cusack<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup> and <i>If I Could Tell The Love of God</i>, <i>In Love God Leads Us</i> and <i>Psalm 103</i> by Jesuit Priest <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Willcock" title="Christopher Willcock">Christopher Willcock</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2009 <a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Buc&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Nicholas Buc (page does not exist)">Nicholas Buc</a> was commissioned by the <a href="/wiki/Shire_of_Glenelg" title="Shire of Glenelg">Shire of Glenelg</a> to write an hour-long cantata mass for the centenary of the death of MacKillop.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup> It was premiered by the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Melbourne_Philharmonic" title="Royal Melbourne Philharmonic">Royal Melbourne Philharmonic</a> in <a href="/wiki/Portland,_Victoria" title="Portland, Victoria">Portland, Victoria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> of Mary McKillop</i> is a setting for congregational singing, composed by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Joshua_Cowie&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Joshua Cowie (page does not exist)">Joshua Cowie</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral,_Adelaide.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral%2C_Adelaide.jpg/220px-Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral%2C_Adelaide.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral%2C_Adelaide.jpg/330px-Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral%2C_Adelaide.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral%2C_Adelaide.jpg/440px-Hand_in_Hand_St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral%2C_Adelaide.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="669" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Mary MacKillop outside <a href="/wiki/St_Francis_Xavier%27s_Cathedral,_Adelaide" title="St Francis Xavier&#39;s Cathedral, Adelaide">St Francis Xavier's Cathedral, Adelaide</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 2018 <a href="/wiki/The_University_of_Scranton" class="mw-redirect" title="The University of Scranton">The University of Scranton</a> in <a href="/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania" title="Scranton, Pennsylvania">Scranton, Pennsylvania</a> renamed a residence hall after Mary MacKillop.  The building which used to be McCormick Hall is now MacKillop Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_MacKillop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>MacKillop is also the subject of several artistic productions, including
</p>
<ul><li>1994 film <i><a href="/wiki/Mary_(1994_film)" title="Mary (1994 film)">Mary</a></i>, directed by Kay Pavlou with <a href="/wiki/Lucy_Bell" title="Lucy Bell">Lucy Bell</a> as MacKillop;<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup> released on DVD as <i>Mary: The Mary MacKillop Story</i></li>
<li><i>Her Holiness</i>, a play by <a href="/wiki/Justin_Fleming_(author)" title="Justin Fleming (author)">Justin Fleming</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>MacKillop</i>, a dramatic musical created by <a href="/wiki/Victoria_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Victoria (Australia)">Victorian</a> composer <a href="/w/index.php?title=Xavier_Brouwer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Xavier Brouwer (page does not exist)">Xavier Brouwer</a><sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup> and first performed for <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> at <a href="/wiki/World_Youth_Day_2008" title="World Youth Day 2008">World Youth Day 2008</a> in Melbourne.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Novelist <a href="/wiki/Pamela_Freeman" title="Pamela Freeman">Pamela Freeman</a>'s <i>The Black Dress</i> is a fictionalised biography of MacKillop's childhood and young adulthood.<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">&#91;69&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>At the Centre of Light</i> by Rosemary Johns, seeded at Explorations (<a href="/wiki/La_Mama_Theatre_(Melbourne)" title="La Mama Theatre (Melbourne)">La Mama</a>), had a regional Victorian tour. It was <a href="/wiki/Dramatic_reading" class="mw-redirect" title="Dramatic reading">read</a> in part with international and Indian actors at WPI Mumbai, and was later commercially produced at 12th Night Theatre Brisbane, 2010. An extract of the play was published in <i>Scenes from a Diverse World</i> anthology (published by the International Centre for Women Playwrights, U.S.A.) The play about MacKillop was written with permission and support of the Josephite Sisters in East Melbourne and is available at Australianplays.org</li></ul>
<p>In 2000, the <a href="/wiki/State_Transit_Authority" title="State Transit Authority">State Transit Authority</a> named a Sydney Harbour <a href="/wiki/Sydney_SuperCats" title="Sydney SuperCats">SuperCat ferry</a> after MacKillop. In 2008, a railway bridge in <a href="/wiki/Adelaide" title="Adelaide">Adelaide</a> was named <a href="/wiki/Mary_MacKillop_Bridge" title="Mary MacKillop Bridge">Mary MacKillop Bridge</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">&#91;70&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
