<p><b>Osanna of Mantua</b> (also <b>Hosanna</b>) (17 January 1449 &#8211; 18 June 1505) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Dominican tertiary</a> who gained notice as a <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmatic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osanna_of_Mantua&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Osanna was the daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">nobles</a> Niccolò Andreasi, whose family had originated in Hungary,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and of Agnese Gonzaga.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was reported to have had a vision of angels at age six.<sup id="cite_ref-short_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-short-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Feeling called to <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">consecrated life</a>, she rejected a <a href="/wiki/Arranged_marriage" title="Arranged marriage">marriage arranged</a> by her father. Unable to explain her attraction to religious life to her father, in 1463, at the age of 14, she secretly received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Third Order of St. Dominic</a>. She had been drawn to this Order from her admiration of two members of the Order, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>, and her contemporary, <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friar</a> <a href="/wiki/Girolamo_Savonarola" title="Girolamo Savonarola">Girolamo Savonarola</a>, who both represented to her lives of strict self-denial.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning home, Osanna explained that she had made a religious <a href="/wiki/Vow" title="Vow">vow</a> and had to wear it until she had fulfilled her promise.,<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> which is an ancient custom. She waited 37 years to complete her vows so she could care for her brothers and sisters after the death of her parents.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A legend states that Osanna, like <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>, miraculously learned to read and write. One day she saw a piece of paper with two words and said, "Those words are '<a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>' and '<a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Mary</a>.'" Allegedly, from that time on, anything relating to the spiritual was within her grasp.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Osanna was thirty years old, she received the stigmata on her head, her side and her feet. She also had a vision in which her heart was transformed and divided into four parts. For the rest of her life, she actively experienced the <a href="/wiki/Passion_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion (Christianity)">Passion of Jesus</a>, but especially intensely on Wednesdays and Fridays. Osanna confided these things in her biographer and "spiritual son," the <a href="/wiki/Olivetans" title="Olivetans">Olivetan</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dom_(title)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dom (title)">Dom</a> Jerome of Mount Olivet, as well as the fact that for years, she subsisted on practically no food at all.<sup id="cite_ref-ashley_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ashley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg/220px-Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="270" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg/330px-Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg/440px-Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg 2x" data-file-width="18066" data-file-height="22169" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Osanna_Andreasi_-_Unknown_artist_16th_century.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Osanna Andreasi - 16th century painting</div></div></div>
<p>Osanna was a mystic who would fall into ecstasies whenever she spoke of God, and a visionary who saw images of Christ bearing his cross. She bore <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a> along with red marks, but there was no bleeding. She helped the poor and sick and served as spiritual director for many, spending much of her family's considerable fortune to help the unfortunate. She spoke out against <a href="/wiki/Decadence" title="Decadence">decadence</a>, and criticized the <a href="/wiki/Aristocracy" title="Aristocracy">aristocracy</a> for a lack of morality. She was a friend of another member of her Order, <a href="/wiki/Columba_of_Rieti" title="Columba of Rieti">Columba of Rieti</a>, and is recorded to have sought counsel from another, the <a href="/wiki/Stephana_de_Quinzanis" title="Stephana de Quinzanis">Stephana de Quinzanis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>These phenomena brought Ossana to the attention of Mantua's ruling family. Most notably, she was sought by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_II_Gonzaga" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco II Gonzaga">Francesco II Gonzaga</a> and his wife, <a href="/wiki/Isabella_d%27Este" title="Isabella d&#39;Este">Isabella d'Este</a>, as both a spiritual guide and a counselor on matters of state. She frequently foretold correctly events which later came to pass, and gained the reputation of a <a href="/wiki/Clairvoyance" title="Clairvoyance">seer</a>. When she died in Mantua on 18 June 1505, all the members of the nobility and clergy attended her funeral, as her body was taken in procession to the Church of St. Dominic, where it was enshrined. Later her remains were transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Mantua_Cathedral" title="Mantua Cathedral">Cathedral of St. Peter</a>, where they are still venerated.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relationship_with_Girolamo_de_Monte_Oliveto">Relationship with Girolamo de Monte Oliveto</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osanna_of_Mantua&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relationship with Girolamo de Monte Oliveto">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her confidant, Dom Jerome (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Girolamo de Monte Oliveto</i>), wrote a <i>vita</i> (biography) of her life in 1507, very shortly after her death. Although Jerome noted that Osanna was not quick to discuss her spiritual experiences, in the last years of her life she adopted Jerome as a "spiritual son," "conceived in the Blood of Christ."<sup id="cite_ref-ashley_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ashley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Jerome's account is especially unique due to his intimate relationship with his subject. The biography takes the form of a detailed report of his conversations with Osanna. Jerome appended to his account <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> translations of twenty-four letters from Osanna, accompanied by documents certifying their authenticity.<sup id="cite_ref-ashley_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ashley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Father Benedict Ashley, these letters express an "intense and constant physical and inner suffering" made bearable only by "sublime experiences of union with God which [Osanna] cannot describe except in broken and inadequate language." A special source of misery for Osanna was the degradation of the Church under the abusive pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Alexander VI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ashley_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ashley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osanna_of_Mantua&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In a response to a request by the <a href="/wiki/Marquess" title="Marquess">Marchesa</a> <a href="/wiki/Isabella_d%27Este" title="Isabella d&#39;Este">Isabella d'Este</a> while on a visit to Rome, through a <a href="/wiki/Papal_brief" title="Papal brief">papal brief</a> of 8 January 1515 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_X" title="Pope Leo X">Leo X</a> authorised the celebration of her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the City of Mantua. Her local <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)#Roman_Catholic_&#39;&#39;cultus&#39;&#39;" title="Cult (religious practice)"><i>cultus</i></a> was confirmed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XII" title="Pope Innocent XII">Pope Innocent XII</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">Papal bull</a> of 24 November 1694, and extended to the whole of the Dominican Order two months later.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osanna_of_Mantua&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The content of the literary corpus of the Blessed Osanna ranges from mystical reflection to considerations on contemporary social and political questions. Her works consist of:
</p>
<ul><li><i>Lettere</i>, an index of correspondence, addressed to a varied readership, including her fellow Dominicans and members of Mantua's ruling family;</li>
<li><i>Colloqui Spirituali</i>, or dialogues on spiritual subjects between the Blessed Osanna and Girolamo Girolamo de Monte Oliveto Scolari.</li></ul>
