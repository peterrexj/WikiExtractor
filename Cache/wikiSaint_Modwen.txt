					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Nun and saint in England</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For Saint Moninne of Ireland, see <a href="/wiki/Moninne" title="Moninne">Moninne</a>. For the property company, see <a href="/wiki/St._Modwen_Properties" title="St. Modwen Properties">St. Modwen Properties</a>.</div>
<p>
<b>Modwenna</b>, or <b>Modwen</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>, who founded <a href="/wiki/Burton_Abbey" title="Burton Abbey">Burton Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Staffordshire" title="Staffordshire">Staffordshire</a> in the 7th century.
</p><p>According to the medieval <i>Life of St Modwenna</i><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> she was an <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> noblewoman by birth and founded the abbey on an island in the <a href="/wiki/River_Trent" title="River Trent">River Trent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Modwenna spent seven years at the abbey with two other Irish nuns called Lazar and Althea, before the three embarked on a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. Upon their return to England they built a church at <a href="/wiki/Stapenhill" title="Stapenhill">Stapenhill</a> in honour of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Saint Paul</a>.
</p><p>Modwenna is reported to have performed many <a href="/wiki/Holy_miracle" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy miracle">holy miracles</a> at Burton Abbey, and to this day the well on the site is said to have healing properties. After a time Modwenna left <a href="/wiki/Burton-upon-Trent" class="mw-redirect" title="Burton-upon-Trent">Burton-upon-Trent</a> and travelled to Scotland where she died in <a href="/w/index.php?title=Langfortin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Langfortin (page does not exist)">Langfortin</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Dundee" title="Dundee">Dundee</a>, reportedly at the age of 130. Her body was returned to Burton-upon-Trent for burial.<sup id="cite_ref-burton2000_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-burton2000-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Another abbess and saint, <a href="/wiki/Osyth" class="mw-redirect" title="Osyth">Osyth</a> (died 700), was raised under Modwenna's direction.
</p><p>Others, however, say that she has been confused with St Monenna (<a href="/wiki/Moninne" title="Moninne">Moninne</a>) of Ireland and with a Scottish saint also called Modwenna, said to have established seven churches in Scotland, including one in <a href="/wiki/Longforgan" title="Longforgan">Longforgan</a>, near Dundee, and that the Anglo-Norman text of her life has little historical value.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This may be how she comes to be associated with <a href="/wiki/Alfred_the_Great" title="Alfred the Great">Alfred the Great</a>, whom she is said to have known, even though he lived in the 9th century, some two hundred years later.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Modwen%27s,_Burton_upon_Trent" title="St Modwen&#39;s, Burton upon Trent">St Modwen's, Burton upon Trent</a> is dedicated to her as is the chapel at <a href="/wiki/Pillaton_Hall" title="Pillaton Hall">Pillaton Hall</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/St_Mary_and_St_Modwen_Church,_Burton-on-Trent" title="St Mary and St Modwen Church, Burton-on-Trent">St Mary and St Modwen Church, Burton-on-Trent</a>.
</p>
