<p><b>Yaropolk Iziaslavich</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Yaropolk Iziaslavych</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (died 1087) was a <i><a href="/wiki/Kniaz" class="mw-redirect" title="Kniaz">Kniaz</a></i> (prince) during the eleventh-century in the <a href="/wiki/Kievan_Rus%27" title="Kievan Rus&#39;">Kievan Rus'</a> kingdom and was the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Rus" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Rus">King of Rus</a> (1076–1087). The son of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Prince" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Prince">Grand Prince</a> <a href="/wiki/Iziaslav_I_of_Kiev" title="Iziaslav I of Kiev">Iziaslav I of Kiev</a> (<a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>) by a Polish princess named <a href="/wiki/Gertruda" class="mw-redirect" title="Gertruda">Gertruda</a>, he is visible in papal sources by the early 1070s but largely absent in contemporary Rus sources until his father's death in 1078. During his father's exile in the 1070s, Yaropolk can be found acting on his father's behalf in an attempt to gain the favor of the <a href="/wiki/List_of_German_monarchs" title="List of German monarchs">German emperors</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papal court</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>. His father returned to Kiev in 1077 and Yaropolk followed.
</p><p>After his father's death Yaropolk was appointed <a href="/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Galicia_and_Volhynia" class="mw-redirect" title="List of rulers of Galicia and Volhynia">Prince of Volhynia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Turov" title="Prince of Turov">Prince of Turov</a> in 1078 by the new Grand Prince, his uncle <a href="/wiki/Vsevolod_I_of_Kiev" title="Vsevolod I of Kiev">Vsevolod</a>. By 1085 Yaropolk had fallen into a state of enmity with the Grand Prince, and by extension the Grand Prince's son <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Monomakh" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir Monomakh">Vladimir Monomakh</a>, forcing him to flee to  Poland, his mother's homeland. He returned in 1086 and made peace with Monomakh but was murdered the same year on a journey to Zvenyhorod.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECurtin190831_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECurtin190831-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was remembered in Rus sources as extremely pious and generous to the church and is recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_background">Family background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yaropolk_Iziaslavich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Yaropolk was the son of <a href="/wiki/Iziaslav_I_of_Kiev" title="Iziaslav I of Kiev">Iziaslav I of Kiev</a> (<a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>) (died 1078), <a href="/wiki/Grand_Prince_of_Kiev" title="Grand Prince of Kiev">over-king of the Rus'</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Poland" title="Gertrude of Poland">Gertruda</a>, daughter of the Polish Duke-King <a href="/wiki/Mieszko_II_Lambert" title="Mieszko II Lambert">Mieszko II Lambert</a> (died 1034).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Since the Kievan succession war of 1015–1019, the Polish ruler <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolesław I of Poland">Bolesław</a> had taken an interest in Ruthenia (Kievan Rus), hoping to gain control of the land of <a href="/wiki/Red_Ruthenia" title="Red Ruthenia">Red Ruthenia</a> (Czerwien Grody), seized by <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_the_Great" title="Vladimir the Great">Vladimir the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-45_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-45-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite some initial success, the <a href="/wiki/Expedition_of_Boles%C5%82aw_to_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Expedition of Bolesław to Kiev">expedition of Bolesław to Kiev</a> in 1018 failed to establish <a href="/wiki/Sviatopolk_I_of_Kiev" title="Sviatopolk I of Kiev">Svyatopolk Vladimirovich</a> (Bolesław's son-in-law), Prince of Turov, on the Kievan throne.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-45_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-45-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Polish ruler did at least gain the Red Ruthenia (Czerwien Grody) very briefly, though that was recovered for the Ruthenians by Grand Prince Yaroslav and <a href="/wiki/Mstislav_of_Chernigov" title="Mstislav of Chernigov">Mstislav Vladimirovich</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Chernigov" title="Prince of Chernigov">Prince of Chernigov</a>, in 1031.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-45_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-45-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg/220px-Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="287" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg/330px-Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg/440px-Yaropolk_and_St_Peter.jpg 2x" data-file-width="453" data-file-height="590" /></a><figcaption>Illumination in the <i><a href="/wiki/Gertrude_Psalter" class="mw-redirect" title="Gertrude Psalter">Gertrude Psalter</a></i> depicting Yaropolk and his wife before the Apostle Peter, with Yaropolk's mother Gertruda at the Apostle's feet.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1042 Grand Prince Yaroslav forced Duke <a href="/wiki/Casimir_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Casimir I of Poland">Casimir of Poland</a> to settle for a peaceful deal. Casimir recognised Ruthenian control of Red Ruthenia and returned 800 Ruthenian prisoners who had been in Polish custody since being captured two decades before by Bolesław.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-45_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-45-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Peace was secured by two marriages. Casimir was married to Yaroslav's sister, while Casimir gave his own sister Gertruda to Yaroslav's son, Iziaslav.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-45_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-45-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_turmoil">Political turmoil</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yaropolk_Iziaslavich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Political turmoil">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1054, Yaropolk became the son of the Grand Prince, as in that year Iziaslav ascended the throne of Kiev.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-29_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-29-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Iziaslav's rule and thus Yaropolk's security were however quickly challenged by Iziaslav's brother <a href="/wiki/Sviatoslav_II_of_Kiev" title="Sviatoslav II of Kiev">Svyatoslav</a> and by his cousin <a href="/wiki/Vseslav_of_Polotsk" title="Vseslav of Polotsk">Vseslav Briacheslavich</a>. At this time among the Volodimerovichi, there were two senior branches that could claim the Grand Princeship through descent from <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_the_Great" title="Vladimir the Great">Vladimir the Great</a>; namely, the descendants of Yaroslav Vladimirovich and the descendants of <a href="/wiki/Iziaslav_of_Polotsk" title="Iziaslav of Polotsk">Iziaslav Vladimirovich</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-29_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-29-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1067, Vseslav rebelled in an attempt to overthrow Iziaslav and become Grand Prince himself, claiming the right as a great-grandson of Vladimir the Great through Iziaslav Vladimirovich. Vseslav's attempt was unsuccessful, and ended in his imprisonment. Troubles for Iziaslav were to continue though. In 1068, Iziaslav's alleged negligence to the advances and incursions of the <a href="/wiki/Cuman_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Cuman people">Cuman people</a> (Polovtsy) after the defeat at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Alta_River" title="Battle of the Alta River">Battle of the Alta River</a> led the <a href="/wiki/Kiev_uprising_of_1068" title="Kiev uprising of 1068">citizens of Kiev to revolt</a>; Vseslav was released, and took the Kievan throne while Iziaslav fled to <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_II_the_Generous" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolesław II the Generous">Bolesław</a> in Poland.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> With Polish assistance Iziaslav returned in May 1069, expelled Vseslav and retook the throne. This victory for Iziaslav secured the Kievan throne for the descendants of Yaroslav Vladimirovich, and thereafter the descendants of Iziaslav Vladimirovich confined themselves to Polotsk where they reigned with semi-independent status.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-29_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-29-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Svyatoslav_and_exile_to_the_west">Svyatoslav and exile to the west</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yaropolk_Iziaslavich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Svyatoslav and exile to the west">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Christ_irina,_Egbert_Psalter.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Christ_irina%2C_Egbert_Psalter.jpg/220px-Christ_irina%2C_Egbert_Psalter.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="292" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Christ_irina%2C_Egbert_Psalter.jpg/330px-Christ_irina%2C_Egbert_Psalter.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Christ_irina%2C_Egbert_Psalter.jpg/440px-Christ_irina%2C_Egbert_Psalter.jpg 2x" data-file-width="761" data-file-height="1011" /></a><figcaption>Christ crowning Yaropolk and his mother Gertruda, eleventh-century miniature from the <i>Gertrude Psalter</i>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Despite the successful exclusion of the Polotskian Volodimerovichi in 1073 Iziaslav was expelled from the Kievan throne by his brothers, <a href="/wiki/Sviatoslav_II_of_Kiev" title="Sviatoslav II of Kiev">Svyatoslav</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Chernigov" title="Prince of Chernigov">Prince of Chernigov</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Vsevolod" title="Vsevolod">Vsevolod</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Pereiaslavl%27" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince of Pereiaslavl&#39;">Prince of Pereyaslavl</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-45_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-45-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  King Bolesław was not as willing or able to assist Iziaslav this time around, and probably for this reason Iziaslav traveled further West; at <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a> in early 1075, Iziaslav sought the aid of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV of Germany</a>, but to no avail.<sup id="cite_ref-Cowdrey-452_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cowdrey-452-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Iziaslav sent Yaropolk to Rome to request aid from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cowdrey-452_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cowdrey-452-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It is likely that while there Yaropolk made complaints about Bolesław and about the money the latter had formerly seized from Iziaslav, as the pope subsequently issued a request that Bolesław return it.<sup id="cite_ref-Cowdrey-452_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cowdrey-452-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Pope Gregory addressed a letter to Iziaslav, called "Demetrius", "King of the Russians", and to his "queen" Gertruda.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The letter noted that Yaropolk, called "Peter", had given his own as well as his father's fidelity to the pope, and that it had been requested that the "kingdom of the Ruthenians" be held of St Peter.<sup id="cite_ref-Cowdrey-453_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cowdrey-453-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Two <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legates</a> were sent to Iziaslav, and Iziaslav was urged to give them his full co-operation.<sup id="cite_ref-Cowdrey-453_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cowdrey-453-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was only after Svyatoslav's death in late 1076 that Iziaslav was able to recover the status of Grand Prince. Vsevolod, who had previously fought against him, was appointed Prince of Chernigov and thus heir to the Kievan throne, explaining perhaps Vsevolod's lack of opposition.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-29_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-29-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The Poles were persuaded by the papacy to give Iziaslav assistance recovering his kingdom, and on July 15, 1077, Iziaslav re-entered Kiev.<sup id="cite_ref-Cowdrey-453_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cowdrey-453-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prince_Yaropolk">Prince Yaropolk</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yaropolk_Iziaslavich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Prince Yaropolk">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Yaropolk is found aiding his father and his uncle Vsevolod in 1078, when <a href="/wiki/Oleg_I_of_Chernigov" title="Oleg I of Chernigov">Oleg Svyatoslavich</a> (and his brother <a href="/wiki/Boris_Vyacheslavich" title="Boris Vyacheslavich">Boris</a>) attempted to gain the throne of Chernigov from Vsevolod. Oleg had been allied to the Polovtsy, and with their help defeated Vsevolod in battle. Grand Prince Iziaslav and Yaropolk, as well as Vsevolod's son <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_II_Monomakh" title="Vladimir II Monomakh">Vladimir Monomakh</a>, were able to reverse this result, and Oleg was forced to retreat to Tmutorokan. Grand Prince Iziaslav, Yaropolk's father, died as a result of the battle.<sup id="cite_ref-Martin-29_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martin-29-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Primary Chronicle</i> records that in 1078 before the death of Iziaslav, Yaropolk was "ruling in <a href="/wiki/Vyshhorod" title="Vyshhorod">Vyshhorod</a>", a city north of Kiev, while his brother <a href="/wiki/Sviatopolk_II_of_Kiev" title="Sviatopolk II of Kiev">Svyatopolk</a> ruled as <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Novgorod" title="Prince of Novgorod">Prince of Novgorod</a>, and Vladimir Monomakh ruled as <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Smolensk" title="Prince of Smolensk">Prince of Smolensk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> After his uncle Vsevolod had become Grand Prince, Yaropolk was given <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Vladimir-in-Volhynia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince of Vladimir-in-Volhynia">Vladimir-in-Volhynia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Turov" title="Prince of Turov">Turov</a>, while Monomakh received Chernigov.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Little is known for the following eight years, but by 1085 Vsevolod and Yaropolk are reported to have become entrenched against each other. The laconic account of these developments in the <i>Primary Chronicle</i> makes the course of events far from transparent. Vasilko and Vladimir Rostislavich, two <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Central_Europe)" class="mw-redirect" title="Galicia (Central Europe)">Galicia</a>-based princes unhappy with territorial settlement under Vsevolod, were said to have attempted to expel Yaropolk in 1084, but Grand Prince Vsevolod's son <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Monomakh" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir Monomakh">Vladimir Monomakh</a> drove these <i>Rostislavchi</i> away.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following this, a gift made by the Grand Prince to Davyd Igorevich, which included land in Volhynia and control of trade with the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>, is said to have made Yaropolk hostile to the Grand Prince. By 1085 Vsevolod and Yaropolk are reported to have become entrenched against each other, and Vsevolod ordered his son, <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Monomakh" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir Monomakh">Vladimir Monomakh</a> to march against Yaropolk.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Lacking confidence in his own resources, Yaropolk fled Volhynia to Poland, leaving his followers (and mother) at <a href="/wiki/Lutsk" title="Lutsk">Lutsk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FrankShep-263_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FrankShep-263-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Vladimir captured Lutsk and Yaropolk's family, attendants and treasure, and assigned his whole principality to Davyd Igorevich.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yaropolk_Iziaslavich&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Death_of_St_Yaropolk.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Death_of_St_Yaropolk.jpeg/220px-Death_of_St_Yaropolk.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="117" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/Death_of_St_Yaropolk.jpeg 1.5x" data-file-width="300" data-file-height="160" /></a><figcaption>Miniature from the <i>Radzivil Chronicle</i> allegedly depicting the death of Yaropolk.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>In the following year Yaropolk returned and reportedly came to an agreement with Vladimir Monomakh, but nothing specific is known of the terms other than Yaropolk being reinstated. In 1087, Yaropolk was murdered. The circumstances of his murder are not clear, but the <i>Primary Chronicle</i> suggested the complicity of Rurik Rostislavich, <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Peremyshl" title="Prince of Peremyshl">Prince of Peremyshl</a>, and his brother Vasilko Rostislavich, Prince of Terebovl.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> His murderer was a man called Neradets, who put a sword through Yaropolk before fleeing to hide with Rurik in Peremyshl; the date given is November 22, 1087.<sup id="cite_ref-deathnote_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deathnote-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> His body was taken to Kiev and buried in the church of St Peter, the church which Yaropolk himself had endowed.<sup id="cite_ref-deathnote_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-deathnote-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Yaropolk is said to have married to the German noble-woman, Kunigunde, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Margrave_of_Meissen" title="Otto I, Margrave of Meissen">Otto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Margrave_of_Meissen" class="mw-redirect" title="Margrave of Meissen">Margrave of Meissen</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Whether by Kunigunde or not, Yaropolk had several children whose names have come into the record. A daughter Anastasia married her far relative <a href="/wiki/Gleb_Vseslavich" title="Gleb Vseslavich">Gleb Vseslavich</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Prince_of_Minsk&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Prince of Minsk (page does not exist)">Prince of Minsk</a> who was a member of the Polotsk branch of the Rurik's family. The <i><a href="/wiki/Annalista_Saxo" title="Annalista Saxo">Annalista Saxo</a></i> records another daughter, and that she married Günther, Count of Schwarzburg, though it does not mention her name.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>  Three sons are known, namely, Yaroslav (died 1102), Viacheslav (died 1104) and Vasilko.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The fate of his descendants is as mysterious as his and only has some scarce records left.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Embedded in the <i>Primary Chronicle</i> is a eulogy to Prince Yaropolk, and among the honors assigned to him, is that he was in the habit of assigning a "tenth part of his wealth to the Mother of God".<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Probably due to his personal devotion to the papacy and to St Peter, Yaropolk established a new church of St Peter at the <a href="/wiki/St._Michael%27s_Golden-Domed_Monastery" title="St. Michael&#39;s Golden-Domed Monastery">monastery of St Demetrios</a> in the city of Kiev.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> He was said to have left all his wealth to the <a href="/wiki/Kiev_Pechersk_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev Pechersk Lavra">Monastery of the Caves</a> in Kiev.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Primary Chronicle's</i> eulogy is the first indication of saintly regard, and indeed today he is venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, with his feast day falling on the reported day of his death, November 22.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
