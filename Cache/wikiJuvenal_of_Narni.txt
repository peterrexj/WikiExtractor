					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Juvenal of Narni</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg" class="image"><img alt="San Giovenale Masaccio.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg/280px-San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg" decoding="async" width="280" height="196" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg/420px-San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg/560px-San_Giovenale_Masaccio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="841" /></a><div class="infobox-caption">The <i><a href="/wiki/San_Giovenale_Triptych" title="San Giovenale Triptych">San Giovenale Triptych</a></i>. <p><b>Saint Juvenal</b> (d. May 3, 369 or 377) (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Giovenale di Narni</i>) is venerated as the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Narni" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Narni">Bishop of Narni</a> in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>. Historical details regarding Juvenal's life are limited. A biography of Juvenal of little historical value, written after the seventh century, states that Juvenal was born in <a href="/wiki/Africa" title="Africa">Africa</a>, was ordained by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a>, was the first bishop of Narni, and was buried in the Porta Superiore on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Flaminia" title="Via Flaminia">Via Flaminia</a> on August 7, though his feast day was celebrated on May 3.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
This <i>Vita</i> does not call him a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> but calls him a <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
The martyrologies of <a href="/wiki/Florus_of_Lyon" title="Florus of Lyon">Florus of Lyon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado</a> describe Juvenal as a bishop and confessor rather than as a martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Saint_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gregory the Great">Saint Gregory the Great</a>, in his <i>Dialogues</i> (IV, 12) and <i>Homiliae in Evangelium</i>, speaks of a bishop of Narni named Juvenal, and describes him as a martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The title of martyr, though, was sometimes given to bishops who did not necessarily die for their faith.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Gregory also mentions a <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> associated with Juvenal at <a href="/wiki/Narni" title="Narni">Narni</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juvenal_of_Narni&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Gelasian Sacramentary</i> has a prayer in honor of the saint under May 3.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Codex Bernense</i> of the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> records his name under May 3 with those of three martyrs of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Nomentana" title="Via Nomentana">Via Nomentana</a>: <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eventius&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Eventius (page does not exist)">Eventius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_I" title="Pope Alexander I">Alexander I</a>, and Theodulus.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Juvenal appears, not as a martyr, but as a bishop and confessor, in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>, which allots him a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">commemoration</a> shared with these three martyrs within the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Cross" title="Feast of the Cross">Finding of the Cross</a> on May 3. When this feast was abolished in 1960, the four saints continued to be merely commemorated jointly within the celebration of the <a href="/wiki/Feria" title="Feria">weekday</a>. The same day continues to be Saint Juvenal's feast day, as indicated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> but since 1969, he is no longer included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>.
</p><p>His legend suggests that he saved Narni from both <a href="/wiki/Ligures" title="Ligures">Ligurian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sarmatians" title="Sarmatians">Sarmatian</a> invaders by calling down a divine thunderstorm.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The construction of Juvenal's sepulcher in Narni is attributed to his alleged successor, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_of_Narni&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Maximus of Narni (page does not exist)">St. Maximus</a> (d. 416 AD).<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Key_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Key-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
The author of the <i>Life</i> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">Pope Vigilius</a> (sixth century) in the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i> states that a monastery founded by <a href="/wiki/Belisarius" title="Belisarius">Belisarius</a> near <a href="/wiki/Orte" title="Orte">Orte</a> was dedicated to Juvenal.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
In 878, Juvenal's relics were taken to the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Frediano" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di San Frediano">Basilica di San Frediano</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lucca" title="Lucca">Lucca</a> with those of Saints <a href="/wiki/Cassius_of_Narni" title="Cassius of Narni">Cassius</a> and Cassius' wife Fausta by <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_I_of_Tuscany" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalbert I of Tuscany">Adalbert</a>, <a href="/wiki/Margrave_of_Tuscany" class="mw-redirect" title="Margrave of Tuscany">Margrave of Tuscany</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Key_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Key-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> but all of the relics were returned to Narni two years later.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Key_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Key-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The relics of Saint Cassius were built in a restored shrine later known as the Sacello di San Cassio.<sup id="cite_ref-Key_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Key-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Juvenal's relics are said to have been hidden.<sup id="cite_ref-Key_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Key-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Fossano" title="Fossano">Fossano</a> claims Juvenal as a patron and also claims to hold some of his relics, though these may belong to another saint of the same name.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
