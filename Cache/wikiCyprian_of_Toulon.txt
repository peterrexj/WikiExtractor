<p><b>Saint Cyprian of Toulon</b> (<i>Cyprianus Tolonensis</i>) (476 – October 3, 546) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Toulon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Toulon">bishop of Toulon</a> during the 6th century.  
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyprian_of_Toulon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born at <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a>, he was the favorite pupil of <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_of_Arles" title="Caesarius of Arles">St. Caesarius of Arles</a> by whom he was trained.  Caesarius ordained him in 506 to the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a>, and, in 516, consecrated him as bishop of Toulon.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>St. Cyprian appears to have been present in 524 at the <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> of <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a> and in the following years to have attended a number of councils. At all these assemblies he showed himself a vigorous opponent of <a href="/wiki/Semipelagianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Semipelagianism">Semipelagianism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He said to have converted to <a href="/wiki/Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholicism">Catholicism</a> two <a href="/wiki/Visigoth" class="mw-redirect" title="Visigoth">Visigoth</a> chiefs, Mandrier and Flavian, who became <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorites</a> and martyrs on the peninsula of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Mandrier-sur-Mer" title="Saint-Mandrier-sur-Mer">Mandrier</a>.  
</p><p>Soon after the death of Caesarius (d. 543) Cyprian wrote a life of his great teacher in two books, being moved to the undertaking by the entreaty of the Abbess Caesaria the Younger, who had been the head of the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> at Arles since 529. The life is one of the most valuable <a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">biographical</a> remains of the sixth century. Cyprian was aided in his task by the two bishops, Firminus and Viventius, friends of Caesarius, as well as by the priest Messianus and the deacon Stephen. The main part of the work up to the fortieth chapter of the first book was most probably written by Cyprian himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1892 the <i>Monumenta Germaniae Historica</i> series published another writing of his, a letter to the Bishop St <a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_of_Geneva&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Maximus of Geneva (page does not exist)">Maximus of Geneva</a>, which discusses some of the disputed theological questions of that age.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The biography was edited by d'Achery and <a href="/wiki/Jean_Mabillon" title="Jean Mabillon">Mabillon</a> in the <i>Acta Sanctorum Ord. S. Benedicti</i>, Venice 1733, vol. i. p.&#160;636ff, also in the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a>' <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i> under date of Aug. 27). A modern English translation is W.E. Klingshirn, <i>Caesarius of Arles: Life, Testament, Letters</i>. Translated Texts for Historians, 19 (Liverpool, 1994).
</p><p>The feast of St. Cyprian falls on 3 October.
</p>
