<p><b>Cuthbert Mayne</b> (c. 1543–29 November 1577) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest executed under the laws of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a>. He was the first of the <a href="/wiki/Seminary_priest" title="Seminary priest">seminary priests</a>, trained on the Continent, to be martyred. Mayne was beatified in 1886 and canonised as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a> in 1970.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mayne was born at <a href="/wiki/Youlston_Park" title="Youlston Park">Youlston</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Barnstaple" title="Barnstaple">Barnstaple</a>, <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>, the son of William Mayne. He was baptised at the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Peter,_Shirwell" title="Church of St Peter, Shirwell">Church of St Peter, Shirwell</a> on 20 March 1543/4, the feast day of St <a href="/wiki/Cuthbert" title="Cuthbert">Cuthbert</a>. An uncle who was a <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> priest paid for him to attend Barnstaple Grammar School.
</p><p>Mayne was instituted <a href="/wiki/Rector_(ecclesiastical)#Anglican_churches" title="Rector (ecclesiastical)">rector</a> of the parish of <a href="/wiki/Huntshaw" title="Huntshaw">Huntshaw</a> in December 1561.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He attended <a href="/wiki/Oxford_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford University">Oxford University</a>, first at <a href="/wiki/St_Alban_Hall" class="mw-redirect" title="St Alban Hall">St Alban Hall</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> then at <a href="/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Oxford" title="St John&#39;s College, Oxford">St John's College</a>, and was awarded a B.A. on 6 April 1566 and M.A. on 8 April 1570.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 27 April 1570, the papal bull <i><a href="/wiki/Regnans_in_Excelsis" title="Regnans in Excelsis">Regnans in Excelsis</a></i> excommunicated <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a> and those who obeyed her laws and commands, releasing her subjects from their allegiance to her.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Catholic_conversion">Catholic conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Catholic conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg/220px-Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg/330px-Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg/440px-Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1780" data-file-height="1183" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Barn_at_Golden_Manor_Geograph-1696874-by-D-Gore.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The barn at Golden Manor, a former secret chapel</div></div></div>
<p>At Oxford, Mayne met <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> and other Catholics, such as <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Martin_(scholar)" title="Gregory Martin (scholar)">Gregory Martin</a>, Humphrey Ely, Henry Shaw, Thomas Bramston, Henry Holland, Jonas Meredith, and Roland Russell. At some point, Mayne, too, became a Catholic. Late in 1570, a letter addressed to him from Gregory Martin, urging him to come to <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>, fell into the hands of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_London" title="Bishop of London">Bishop of London</a>, and he sent a <a href="/wiki/Pursuivant" title="Pursuivant">pursuivant</a> to arrest Mayne and others mentioned in the letter. Warned by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Ford_(martyr)" title="Thomas Ford (martyr)">Thomas Ford</a>, Mayne evaded arrest by going to Cornwall and then, in 1573, to the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College, Douai</a>, now in northern France.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mayne was ordained a priest in the Roman Catholic Church at Douai in 1575 and on 7 February in the following year he obtained the degree of <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Theology" title="Bachelor of Theology">Bachelor of Theology</a> of <a href="/wiki/Douai_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Douai University">Douai University</a>.
</p><p>On 24 April 1576, he left for the English mission in the company of another priest, <a href="/wiki/John_Payne_(martyr)" title="John Payne (martyr)">John Payne</a>. He soon joined the household of <a href="/wiki/Francis_Tregian_the_Elder" title="Francis Tregian the Elder">Francis Tregian</a> at Golden in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Probus,_Cornwall" title="Probus, Cornwall">Probus, Cornwall</a><sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where he posed as his steward. Francis Tregian (1548–1608) was one of the richest landowners in Cornwall.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Manor_House,_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/The_Manor_House%2C_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg/220px-The_Manor_House%2C_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/The_Manor_House%2C_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg/330px-The_Manor_House%2C_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/The_Manor_House%2C_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg/440px-The_Manor_House%2C_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="424" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Manor_House,_Golden_-_scene_of_St_Cuthbert%27s_arrest_-_geograph.org.uk_-_887858.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Golden Manor, Probus, scene of Mayne's arrest</div></div></div><p> Missionaries from Douai were looked upon as papal agents intent on overthrowing the queen. The authorities began a systematic search in June 1576, when the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Exeter" title="Bishop of Exeter">Bishop of Exeter</a> <a href="/wiki/William_Bradbridge" title="William Bradbridge">William Bradbridge</a> came to Cornwall. On 8 June 1577, the <a href="/wiki/High_Sheriff_of_Cornwall" title="High Sheriff of Cornwall">High Sheriff of Cornwall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Richard_Grenville" title="Richard Grenville">Richard Grenville</a>, conducted a raid on Tregian's house during which the crown officers "bounced and beat at the door" to Mayne's chamber. On gaining entry, Grenville discovered a Catholic devotional item, an <i><a href="/wiki/Agnus_Dei#Catholic_sacramental" title="Agnus Dei">Agnus Dei</a></i>, around Mayne's neck, and took him into custody along with his books and papers.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment_and_trial">Imprisonment and trial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Imprisonment and trial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While awaiting trial at the circuit <a href="/wiki/Assizes" title="Assizes">assizes</a> in September, Mayne was imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Launceston_Castle" title="Launceston Castle">Launceston Castle</a>. At the opening of the trial on 23 September 1577 there were five counts against him:<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> first, that he had obtained from the Roman See a "faculty" (or <i><a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bulla</a></i>), containing absolution of the Queen's subjects; second, that he had published the same at Golden; third, that he had taught the ecclesiastical authority of the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> and denied the <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Governor_of_the_Church_of_England" title="Supreme Governor of the Church of England">queen's ecclesiastical supremacy</a> while in prison; fourth, that he had brought into the kingdom an <i>Agnus Dei</i> (a Lamb of God sealed upon a piece of wax from the Paschal candle blessed by the pope)<sup id="cite_ref-friends_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-friends-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and delivered it to Francis Tregian; fifth, that he had celebrated Mass.
</p><p>Mayne answered all counts. On the first and second counts, he said that the supposed "faculty" was merely a copy printed at Douai of an announcement of the <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christianity)">Jubilee</a> of 1575, and that its application having expired with the end of the jubilee, he certainly had not published it either at Golden (the manor house of Francis Tregian) or elsewhere. On the third count, he said that he had asserted nothing definite on the subject to the three illiterate witnesses who swore to the contrary. On the fourth count, he said that the fact he was wearing an <i>Agnus Dei</i> at the time of his arrest did not establish that he had brought it into the kingdom or delivered it to Tregian. On the fifth count, he said that the presence of a Missal, a chalice, and vestments in his room did not establish that he had said Mass.
</p><p>The trial judge, Justice <a href="/wiki/Sir_Roger_Manwood" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Roger Manwood">Sir Roger Manwood</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> directed the jury to return a verdict of guilty, stating that, "where plain proofs were wanting, strong presumptions ought to take place".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Manwood also argued that it was illegal to introduce any papal letter into the country, no matter what it was. The jury found Mayne guilty of <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a> on all counts, and accordingly, he was sentenced to be <a href="/wiki/Hanging,_drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanging, drawing and quartering">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>. Mayne responded, "<i><a href="/wiki/Deo_gratias" title="Deo gratias">Deo gratias</a></i>".<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With him had been <a href="/wiki/Arraignment" title="Arraignment">arraigned</a> <a href="/wiki/Francis_Tregian_the_Elder" title="Francis Tregian the Elder">Francis Tregian</a> and eight other laymen. The eight were sentenced to seizure of their goods and life imprisonment.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Tregian was sentenced to die but was in fact incarcerated for 28 years<sup id="cite_ref-Grove_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grove-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> until, on the petition of his friends, he was released by King <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="James I of England">James I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Burton_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His execution was delayed because one of the judges, Jeffries, took exception to the proceedings and sent a report to the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Council</a>. The Council submitted the case to the whole bench of judges, which was inclined to Jeffries's view. Nevertheless, the council ordered the execution to proceed.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the examination of Mayne after the trial, Mayne admitted to having said mass. The Record Office also recorded that among his papers were notes which brought him under suspicion of the charge that Catholics were bound, in the right opportunity, to rise against the Queen. The same office also recorded him admitting to this during his examination after the trial:
</p>
<blockquote><p>The words found in a book of his signifying that though the catholic religion did now serve, swear and obey, yet if occasion were offered they would be ready to help the execution, &amp;c., were annexed to a text taken out of a general council of Lateran for the authority of the pope in his excommunication, and at the last council of Trent there was a consent of the catholic princes for a reformation of such realms and persons as had gone from the authority of the bishop of Rome when it was concluded that if any catholic prince took in hand to invade any realm to reform the same to the authority of the see of Rome, that then the catholics in that realm should be ready to assist and help them. And this was the meaning of the execution as he saith, which he never revealed to any man before.<sup id="cite_ref-Lake_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lake-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Mayne had also supposedly stated that "the people of England may be won unto the catholic religion of the see of Rome by such secret instructions as either are or may be within the realm; but what these secret instructions are he will not utter, but hopeth when time serveth they shall do therein as pleaseth God".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Execution">Execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A <a href="/wiki/Gallows" title="Gallows">gallows</a> was erected in the marketplace at <a href="/wiki/Launceston,_Cornwall" title="Launceston, Cornwall">Launceston</a>, and Mayne was executed there on 29 November 1577. Before being brought to the place of execution, Mayne was offered his life in return for a renunciation of his religion and an acknowledgement of the <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Governor_of_the_Church_of_England" title="Supreme Governor of the Church of England">supremacy</a> of the queen as head of the church. Declining both offers, he kissed a copy of the Bible, declaring that, "the queen neither ever was, nor is, nor ever shall be, the head of the church of England". He was not allowed to speak to the crowd but only to say his prayers quietly. It is unclear if he died on the gallows but all agree that he was unconscious, or almost so, when he was drawn and quartered. One source states that he was cut down alive, but in falling struck his head against the <a href="/wiki/Scaffold_(execution_site)" title="Scaffold (execution site)">scaffold</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_considerations">Political considerations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Political considerations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/A._L._Rowse" title="A. L. Rowse">A. L. Rowse</a> sees the condemnation of Mayne as arising from local rivalries between Protestant coastal and Catholic inland interests.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Grenville had been unsuccessful in his attempts to arrange a marriage between his daughter and the Tregian heir.<sup id="cite_ref-Lecercle_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lecercle-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The coming of Mayne and others made the English government fear the possibility of papal agents coming to the island to ready the populace to rise up in revolt in support of King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a> in an invasion of England. This helped support the case to pass harsher legislation against Catholicism in England. Establishing a threat from subversive Catholic elements also served Elizabeth's counsellors such as <a href="/wiki/William_Cecil,_1st_Baron_Burghley" title="William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley">Lord Burghley</a> in their attempts to persuade the Queen to support the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_Revolt" class="mw-redirect" title="Dutch Revolt">Dutch Revolt</a> against Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-Lake_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lake-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mayne was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> "<a href="/wiki/Canonization#Equipollent_canonization" title="Canonization">equipollently</a>" by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a>, by means of a decree of 29 December 1886 and was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> along with other martyrs of England and Wales by Pope <a href="/wiki/Paul_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul VI">Paul VI</a> on 25 October 1970.
</p><p>Mayne was the first <a href="/wiki/Seminary_priest" title="Seminary priest">seminary priest</a>, the group of priests who were trained not in England but in houses of studies on the Continent. He was also one of the group of prominent Catholic martyrs of the persecution who were later designated as the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">Relics</a> of Mayne's body survive. A portion of his skull is kept at <a href="/wiki/St_Mawgan_Monastery" title="St Mawgan Monastery">Lanherne Convent</a> in Cornwall.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Christopher M. B. Allison suggests that the silver reliquary discovered in 2015 at <a href="/wiki/Jamestown,_Virginia" title="Jamestown, Virginia">Jamestown, Virginia</a> in the grave of Captain Gabriel Archer (died 1609/10) may contain a relic of Mayne.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are many memorials to him in Launceston, and in 1977 the name of the Roman Catholic church on St Stephen's Hill there was changed from the Church of the English Martyrs to the Church of St Cuthbert Mayne; it is the site of the <a href="/wiki/National_Shrine" class="mw-redirect" title="National Shrine">National Shrine</a> to St Cuthbert Mayne.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In 1921 an annual June pilgrimage was initiated in Launceston to commemorate Mayne.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Cuthbert_Mayne_School" title="St Cuthbert Mayne School">St Cuthbert Mayne School</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Voluntary_aided_school" title="Voluntary aided school">voluntary aided</a> Roman Catholic and Church of England school<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Torquay" title="Torquay">Torquay</a>, and <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.cuthbertmayne.herts.sch.uk/">St Cuthbert Mayne Catholic Junior School</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hemel_Hempstead" title="Hemel Hempstead">Hemel Hempstead</a>, are named after him. The St Cuthbert Mayne RC High School in <a href="/wiki/Fulwood,_Lancashire" title="Fulwood, Lancashire">Fulwood, Lancashire</a> merged in 1988 to become <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady%27s_Catholic_High_School,_Fulwood" title="Our Lady&#39;s Catholic High School, Fulwood">Our Lady's Catholic High School</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Daniel_Fournier" title="Daniel Fournier">Daniel Fournier</a> engraved a mezzotint of Mayne. <a href="/wiki/Ushaw_College" title="Ushaw College">Ushaw College</a> has paintings of him.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stained-glass windows represent him in the following churches/schools:
</p>
<ul><li>The Chapel of St Cuthbert in <a href="/wiki/Ushaw_College" title="Ushaw College">Ushaw College</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Roman Catholic Church of Our Lady Star of the Sea in <a href="/wiki/Falmouth,_Cornwall" title="Falmouth, Cornwall">Falmouth, Cornwall</a><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>St Saviour's Church in <a href="/wiki/Lewisham" title="Lewisham">Lewisham</a><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St_Mary_of_the_Angels,_Bayswater" title="St Mary of the Angels, Bayswater">Church of St Mary of the Angels</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bayswater" title="Bayswater">Bayswater</a>, London<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St_Edmund%27s_College,_Ware" title="St Edmund&#39;s College, Ware">St Edmund's College, Ware</a><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Our Lady of Lourdes, <a href="/wiki/Harpenden" title="Harpenden">Harpenden</a>, Hertfordshire<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_fiction">In fiction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthbert_Mayne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: In fiction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the historical novel <i>The Grove of Eagles</i> by <a href="/wiki/Winston_Graham" title="Winston Graham">Winston Graham</a>, which is set in Cornwall some years after Mayne's death, there are several references to him. One character, a Catholic member of the prominent <a href="/wiki/Arundell_family" title="Arundell family">Arundell family</a> of Tolverne, says that his Protestant brother, who was one of the <a href="/wiki/Jurors" class="mw-redirect" title="Jurors">jurors</a> at Mayne's trial, will burn in Hell for his share in Mayne's death. The brother, filled with guilt for his share in the execution, has not only converted to the Roman Catholic faith but is risking his life by sheltering other priests.
</p>
