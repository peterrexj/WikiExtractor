<p><b>Adalbert</b> (or <b>Albert</b>) was elected <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in February 1101 and served for 105 days. He was a candidate of the <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Roman</a> party opposed to Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Paschal II</a> and is regarded today as an <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a>. Prior to his election he was created a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> by the antipope <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Clement III</a>. He was captured by partisans of Paschal II and forced to live out his days as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinal">Cardinal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Adalbert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Cardinal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The date of Adalbert's birth is unknown, but he was from the town of <a href="/wiki/Atella,_Basilicata" title="Atella, Basilicata">Atella</a> in southern Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-miranda_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miranda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was an early supporter of Clement III, who rewarded him with the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Porto-Santa_Rufina" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Suburbicarian Diocese of Porto-Santa Rufina">suburbicarian diocese of Silva Candida</a>. He was among the twelve cardinals of Clement III who gathered in the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Palace" title="Lateran Palace">Lateran Palace</a> to underwrite a <a href="/wiki/Papal_letter" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal letter">papal letter</a> on 4 November 1084.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbert can be traced at Rome throughout Clement's <a href="/wiki/Pontificate" title="Pontificate">pontificate</a>. He was by the antipope's side when Clement issued a privilege on 8 January 1089. On 7 August 1098 he was one of a group of prelates who presided over a <a href="/wiki/Conciliabulum" title="Conciliabulum">conciliabulum</a> convoked by Clement. The assembly condemned all the "old and new" <a href="/wiki/Heresies" class="mw-redirect" title="Heresies">heresies</a> of the monk Hildebrand, that is, Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Gregory VII</a>. It also issued an summons to the "schismatics", the followers of Gregory's successor, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Urban II</a>, enjoining them to be present in Rome on 1 November. Adalbert's name is atop the list of signatories of this letter, an indication of his importance in the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_curia" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman curia">curia</a></i> of Clement III.<sup id="cite_ref-miranda_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miranda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Clement's successor, <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Theodoric" title="Antipope Theodoric">Theodoric</a>, was captured by his opponents in February 1101, Adalbert was elected to succeed him.<sup id="cite_ref-miranda_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miranda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Antipope">Antipope</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Adalbert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Antipope">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The circumstances of Adalbert's <a href="/wiki/Papal_election" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election">election</a> are difficult to ascertain. It took place in the basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Apostoli,_Rome" title="Santi Apostoli, Rome">Santi Apostoli</a>. According to the German chroniclers <a href="/wiki/Frutolf_of_Michelsberg" title="Frutolf of Michelsberg">Frutolf of Michelsberg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ekkehard_of_Aura" title="Ekkehard of Aura">Ekkehard of Aura</a>, the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry IV">Henry IV</a> intervened to secure Adalbert's nomination, although sources nearer in space and time to events do not mention the emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Although the papal electors believed they were acting in the interests of the empire, Henry IV is not known to have had any contact with Adalbert.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Annales_Romani" title="Annales Romani">Annales Romani</a></i>, the richest source of information on Adalbert's pontificate, states simply that he was elected by that part of the clergy and people of Rome who had sided with Clement III.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is clear that Adalbert was elected, consecrated and <a href="/wiki/Papal_inauguration" title="Papal inauguration">enthroned</a> within days of Theodoric's capture in February 1101. Modern historians have often erroneously dated his election to 1102. The speed with which he was elected indicates that the pro-Imperial Clementine party in Rome was still well organized at the time. Among his known supporters were Romano, a cardinal who had taken part in the conciliabulum of 1098, and Romano's nephew, Giovanni Oddoline.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Adalbert retained his baptismal name as pope.<sup id="cite_ref-miranda_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miranda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> was made for Adalbert, although it could not be laid on <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_tomb" title="Saint Peter&#39;s tomb">Saint Peter's tomb</a>, because the Clementine faction did not control it.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbert's first public appearance drew a large crowd and rapidly degenerated into unrest. The situation eventually got so bad that he was forced to take refuge in the basilica of <a href="/wiki/San_Marcello_al_Corso" title="San Marcello al Corso">San Marcello al Corso</a> under the protection of Romano and Giovanni Oddoline. Many clergy who tried to reach the church were beaten and stripped naked by the mob. Paschal II then bribed Giovanni to hand the antipope over. Adalbert was stripped of his pallium and handed over to Paschal's forces.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His pontificate had lasted 105 days.<sup id="cite_ref-miranda_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miranda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was led behind a horse (as a sign of contempt) to the Lateran Palace, where Paschal was residing. He was then imprisoned in a tower. The <i>Annales Romani</i> and the biography of Paschal in the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_pontificalis" class="mw-redirect" title="Liber pontificalis">Liber pontificalis</a></i> agree that the acceptance of the bribe and the antipope's imprisonment took place in the space of one day.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalbert was eventually sent to the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> monastery of San Lorenzo in <a href="/wiki/Aversa" title="Aversa">Aversa</a>, where he spent the rest of his life. The date of his death and the place of burial are not known.<sup id="cite_ref-piazza_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-piazza-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1105, the pro-Imperial party elected Maginulf as <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Sylvester_IV" title="Antipope Sylvester IV">Sylvester IV</a> in opposition to Paschal, but he was no more successful than his predecessors for he too lacked imperial support.<sup id="cite_ref-Robinson_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robinson-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
