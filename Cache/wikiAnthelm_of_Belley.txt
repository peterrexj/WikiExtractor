<p><b>Anthelm of Belley</b> (1107&#8211;1178) was a <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Grand_Chartreuse" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Chartreuse">Grand Chartreuse</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Belley" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Belley">bishop of Belley</a>.
</p><p>He was born near <a href="/wiki/Chamb%C3%A9ry" title="Chambéry">Chambéry</a> in 1107. He would later receive an ecclesiastical benefice in the area of Belley. When he was thirty years old, he resigned from this position to become a Carthusian monk at Portes. Only two years after joining the <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">order</a>, he was made the prior of the Grande Chartreuse, the motherhouse of his order, which had recently incurred substantial damage.
</p><p>He was an effective administrator there. While under his direction, the community increased in numbers and fervency. He restored and improved the buildings, including constructing a defensive wall and an <a href="/wiki/Aqueduct_(water_supply)" title="Aqueduct (water supply)">aqueduct</a>. The rules of the order were standardized, and changed to allow women the opportunity to enter the order in their own houses. He also brought the other houses of the order into closer alignment with the motherhouse. The monks under his direction included <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Lincoln" title="Hugh of Lincoln">Hugh of Lincoln</a>, who expressed great fondness for Anthelm.
</p><p>Anthelm continued in his office almost constantly for twenty-four years, barring a period of a few years when he was a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>. After that period, in 1152, Anthelm returned to the Grand Chartreuse, and helped defend the sitting <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159%E2%80%931164)" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159–1164)">antipope Victor IV</a>. Alexander III appointed Anthelm bishop of Belley in 1163. In that position, he is said to have been fearless and uncompromising, working to reform the clergy and regulate the affairs of the diocese. One example of his fearlessness occurred in 1175, when Anthelm <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunicated</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Count_Humbert_of_Maurienne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Count Humbert of Maurienne (page does not exist)">Count Humbert of Maurienne</a> for having taken one priest captive and murdering another priest who had tried to free him. Humbert appealed his excommunication to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>, who reversed Humbert's excommunication. Anthelm, who believed that Humbert was not penitent for his misconduct, withdrew from his diocese in protest.
</p><p>Pope Alexander then commissioned Anthelm to travel to England to try to reconcile <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II of England</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a>. Anthelm's health was such that he was unable to take the journey. Anthelm returned to Belley to help care for the poor and the lepers of the area.
</p><p>Anthelm died at Belley in 1178. On his deathbed, he received Humbert, and recognized that at that time Humbert truly had repented of his earlier acts.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthelm_of_Belley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is considered a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of June 26. His feast has been celebrated by the Carthusians since 1607. His relics were enshrined in Belley.
</p><p>In art, Anthelm is depicted holding a lamp lit by a divine hand.
</p>
