					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Irish saint (c. 642–722)</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" <p><b>Máel Ruba</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 642–722) is an Irish saint of the Christian Church who was active in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. Originally from <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Down" title="County Down">County Down</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, he was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and founded the monastic community of <a href="/wiki/Applecross" title="Applecross">Applecross</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ross,_Scotland" title="Ross, Scotland">Ross</a>, one of the best attested early Christian monasteries in what is now <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. Forms of his name include <b>Máelrubai</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_Irish" title="Old Irish">Old Irish</a> spelling), <b>Maol Rubha (MoRubha/MaRuibhe)</b> (<a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic" title="Scottish Gaelic">Scottish Gaelic</a> spelling), or <b>Malruibhe</b>, and it is sometimes <a href="/wiki/Latinisation_of_names" title="Latinisation of names">latinised</a> as <b>Rufus</b>,
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1el_Ruba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Máel Ruba was descended from <a href="/wiki/Niall_of_the_Nine_Hostages" title="Niall of the Nine Hostages">Niall</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Ireland">King of Ireland</a>, via his father Elganach.  His mother, Subtan, was a niece of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Comgall" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Comgall">Saint Comgall</a> (d. 597 or 602) of <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a>.  Máel Ruba was born in the area of <a href="/wiki/Derry" title="Derry">Derry</a> and was educated at Bangor.  In 671, when he was thirty, he sailed from Ireland to Scotland with a group of monks.
</p><p>For two years he travelled around the area, chiefly in <a href="/wiki/Argyll" title="Argyll">Argyll</a>, perhaps founding some of the many churches still dedicated to him, before settling at <i>Aporcrosan</i> (<a href="/wiki/Applecross" title="Applecross">Applecross</a>) in 673, in Pictish territory in the west of Ross opposite the islands of <a href="/wiki/Skye" class="mw-redirect" title="Skye">Skye</a> and <a href="/wiki/Raasay" title="Raasay">Raasay</a>.  Thence he set out on missionary journeys: westward to the islands <a href="/wiki/Skye" class="mw-redirect" title="Skye">Skye</a> and <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Lewis" title="Isle of Lewis">Lewis</a>, eastward to <a href="/wiki/Forres" title="Forres">Forres</a> and <a href="/wiki/Keith,_Moray" title="Keith, Moray">Keith</a>, and northward to <a href="/wiki/Loch_Shin" title="Loch Shin">Loch Shin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Durness" title="Durness">Durness</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Farr,_Sutherland" title="Farr, Sutherland">Farr</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_monastery_at_Applecross">The monastery at Applecross</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1el_Ruba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The monastery at Applecross">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Gaelic name of Applecross, "<i>A' Chomraich</i>", 'The Sanctuary', derives from an area of inviolate ground which surrounded the monastery.  Its limits were originally marked by crosses.  Unfortunately, only a fragment of one of these has survived, within the farmyard at Camusterrach, south of Applecross village.
</p><p>Both Máel Ruba's voyage to 'Scotland' and his foundation of Applecross are recorded in contemporary Irish annals, implying that they were considered of great significance at the time. Máel Ruba's monastery was a major <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> centre and instrumental in the spread of both <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Gaelic</a> culture amongst the <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a> of northern <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>.
</p><p>The succession of the abbots ceases to be recorded in the Irish annals during the course of the ninth century. It is likely that this is the result of (unrecorded) raids by Vikings.
</p><p>A setting of two small stones in the graveyard at Applecross is still pointed out as the (supposed) site of his grave.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1el_Ruba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to local tradition, on his last journey he was killed by <a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Danish</a> <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">vikings</a>, probably at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Teampull&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Teampull (page does not exist)">Teampull</a>, around nine miles up <a href="/wiki/Strathnaver" title="Strathnaver">Strathnaver</a> from Farr, where he had built a <a href="/wiki/Monastic_cell" title="Monastic cell">monastic cell</a>, and was buried near the River <a href="/wiki/Naver" title="Naver">Naver</a>, not far from his cell, where his grave is still marked by "a rough cross-marked stone".  However, 722 may be too early for Scandinavian raiders to have been involved, as the first historically recorded Viking attacks on Scotland and Ireland date to the 790s.
</p><p>Another tradition, found in the <i>Aberdeen Breviary</i>, is that he was killed at <a href="/wiki/Urquhart,_Moray" title="Urquhart, Moray">Urquhart</a> and buried at Abercrossan.  This is probably a mistake arising from a confusion of <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Gaelic language">Gaelic</a> place-names.
</p><p>The most reliable sources, contemporary Irish annals, record that he 'died' at Applecross in his 80th year.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cult">Cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1el_Ruba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Máel Ruba was, after <a href="/wiki/St_Columba" class="mw-redirect" title="St Columba">St Columba</a>, perhaps the most popular saint of north-west Scotland. At least twenty-one churches are dedicated to him, and Dean Reeves enumerates about forty forms of his name.  His death occurred on 21 April, and in Ireland his feast has always been kept on this day; however, in Scotland (probably owing to the confusion with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rufus" title="Saint Rufus">Saint Rufus</a>) it has also been kept on 27 August. On 5 July 1898, Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a> restored his feast for the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Scotland" title="Catholic Church in Scotland">Catholic Church in Scotland</a>, to be kept on 27 August. In the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Scottish_Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Scottish Episcopal Church)">calendar of saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Episcopal_Church" title="Scottish Episcopal Church">Scottish Episcopal Church</a> he is honoured on 20 April. Because his feast day was on 25 August,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Folk_etymology" title="Folk etymology">folk etymology</a> led some people to confuse "Summereve's Fair" with a secular fair celebrating the season.
</p><p>Máel Ruba's name has given rise to numerous corruptions; for example in <a href="/wiki/Keith,_Moray" title="Keith, Moray">Keith, Moray</a>, he is referred to as "St Rufus", and <a href="/wiki/St_Rufus_Church" title="St Rufus Church">St Rufus Church</a> is dedicated to him. In other parts of Scotland, his name was variously rendered as "Maree" (as in the Loch), "Summereve" (i.e., St Maol Rubha) etc.  There are several locations named after Máel Ruba such as <a href="/wiki/Loch_Maree" title="Loch Maree">Loch Maree</a>. In the 17th century the <a href="/wiki/Presbyterian_polity#Presbytery" title="Presbyterian polity">Presbytery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dingwall" title="Dingwall">Dingwall</a> was disturbed by reports of several rituals, evidently of pagan origin, such as the sacrificing of bulls, on an island in Loch Maree.  These revolved round a debased memory of Máel Ruba, whose legacy had perhaps become mixed with an ancient pre-Christian cult of 'God Mourie'.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Areas_where_he_was_celebrated">Areas where he was celebrated</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1el_Ruba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Areas where he was celebrated">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Applecross" title="Applecross">Applecross</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Loch_Broom" title="Loch Broom">Loch Broom</a> area.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Keith,_Moray" title="Keith, Moray">Keith, Moray</a>, sometimes referred to anciently as 'Kethmalruff' (<i>Cèith Mhaol Rubha</i>)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Loch_Maree" title="Loch Maree">Loch Maree</a>, which has <a href="/wiki/Isle_Maree" title="Isle Maree">Isle Maree</a>/Eilean Maree in it. The island has a well dedicated to the saint.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Dingwall" title="Dingwall">Dingwall</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tain" title="Tain">Tain</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Easter_Ross" title="Easter Ross">Easter Ross</a> both of which held fairs celebrating the saint.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Amulree" title="Amulree">Amulree</a> <i>Ath Maol Rubha</i> (= Maol Rubha's ford), <a href="/wiki/Perthshire" title="Perthshire">Perthshire</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Ashaig" title="Ashaig">Ashaig</a>, on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Skye" title="Isle of Skye">Isle of Skye</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Contin_Island" title="Contin Island">Contin Island</a>, a river islet in Ross and Cromarty; the parish church of Contin is dedicated to Máel Ruba. There has probably been a church on this site since the 7th or 8th century.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The chapel of Kilmarie at Kirkton on the <a href="/wiki/Craignish" title="Craignish">Craignish</a> peninsula in Argyll; the chapel is said to have been founded by Máel Ruba, from whom the name Kilmarie is derived.</li>
<li>The medieval church of Kirkden (formerly Idvies), near <a href="/wiki/Letham,_Angus" title="Letham, Angus">Letham, Angus</a>; dedicated to Ruffus or Maelrubha in 1243</li>
<li>The medieval church of Kinnell, near <a href="/wiki/Friockheim" title="Friockheim">Friockheim</a>; dedicated to "St Malruib, Confessor"</li></ul>
