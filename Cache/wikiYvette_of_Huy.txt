<p><b>Yvette of Huy</b> (1158 – 13 January 1228) was a venerated Christian <a href="/wiki/Prophet" title="Prophet">prophet</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchoress</a>. Born in <a href="/wiki/Huy" title="Huy">Huy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, she was also known as <i>Ivette</i>, <i>Ivetta, </i>Jufta<i> or </i>Jutta<i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolton_1978_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolton_1978-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Wilson_and_Margolis_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilson_and_Margolis-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></i>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Yvette_of_Huy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Yvette was born into a wealthy, but not particularly religious family, close to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Li%C3%A8ge" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Liège">bishop of Liège</a>. From an early age Yvette was hesitant of marriage and wished to live a religious life. <sup id="cite_ref-Bolton_1978_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolton_1978-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Her father was a tax collector.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, Yvette was forced into an arranged marriage at aged thirteen. Her marriage produced three children (one died while still an infant) before she was widowed at eighteen. Like many medieval women, it was in widowhood that she gained more self-determination. She began to live a more religious life by attending mass regularly, giving to the poor, and deciding not to remarry. Her father objected to the latter two activities. He was so concerned about her excessive giving to the poor that he took her sons from her, fearing that she would give away all of their wealth. Her father and others in her family also tried to get her to remarry. He even took her to the Bishop of Liège, for whom he worked, but Yvette's Hagiography attests that when the bishop saw her devotion and humility he agreed to let her stay in holy widowhood. It was after this assurance from the Bishop that Yvette retired to a virtually derelict <a href="/wiki/Leper" class="mw-redirect" title="Leper">leper</a>  hospital in Statte, close to Huy, on the heights of the river <a href="/wiki/Meuse" title="Meuse">Meuse</a> to tend to the inmates, and more fully follow her religious calling.<sup id="cite_ref-Bolton_1978_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bolton_1978-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She left her two sons in the care of their grandfather. 
</p><p>Ten years later, she became an <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchoress</a> and was enclosed in a chapel cell near the colony in a ceremony conducted by the abbot of <a href="/wiki/Abbaye_Notre-Dame_d%27Orval" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbaye Notre-Dame d&#39;Orval">Abbaye Notre-Dame d'Orval</a>. From there she offered guidance to pilgrims who considered her a prophetess in the apostolic sense of having insight into the divine. She summoned priests and even the dean of the local church to her presence and confronted them about their behaviour. She was responsible for the conversion of her father and one of her two surviving sons. After a time, her power threatened the male clergy and canons. She was denounced.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Yvette died on 13 January 1228 in <a href="/wiki/Huy" title="Huy">Huy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>.
</p><p>Her life was recorded by the <a href="/wiki/Premonstratensian" class="mw-redirect" title="Premonstratensian">Premonstratensian</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_of_Floreffe&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hugh of Floreffe (page does not exist)">Hugh of Floreffe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wilson_and_Margolis_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilson_and_Margolis-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
Although never formally canonised as a saint, she is classed as ‘blessed’ by the Catholic Church; feast day January 13th, the date of her death.
One of her sons became a monk at the monastery of Orval and became its abbot. In time he too became classed as a ‘blessed': Eustachius of Huy, commemorated on March 13 in the Cistercian calendar.
</p>
