<p>St. <b>Methodios I</b> or <b>Methodius I</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Μεθόδιος Α΄</span>), (788/800 &#8211; June 14, 847) was <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople</a> from March 4, 843 to June 14, 847. He was born in <a href="/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily" title="Syracuse, Sicily">Syracuse</a> and died in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on June 14 in both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">East</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">West</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Methodios_I_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born to wealthy parents, Methodios was sent as a young man to Constantinople to continue his education and hopefully attain an appointment at court. But instead he entered a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a>, eventually becoming <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>.
</p><p>Under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Leo_the_Armenian" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo the Armenian">Leo V the Armenian</a> (813–820) the <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">Iconoclast persecution</a> broke out for the second time. In 815 Methodios went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, perhaps as an envoy of the deposed <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Nikephoros_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Nikephoros I of Constantinople">Patriarch Nikephorοs</a>.  Upon his return in 821 he was arrested and exiled as an <a href="/wiki/Iconodule" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconodule">iconodule</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">Iconoclast</a> regime of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_II" title="Michael II">Michael II</a>.  Methodios was released in 829 and assumed a position of importance at the court of the even more fervently iconoclast Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theophilos_(emperor)" title="Theophilos (emperor)">Theophilos</a>.
</p><p>Soon after the death of the emperor, in 843, the influential minister <a href="/wiki/Theoktistos" title="Theoktistos">Theoktistos</a> convinced the Empress Mother <a href="/wiki/Theodora_(9th_century)" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodora (9th century)">Theodora</a>, as <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">regent</a> for her two-year-old son <a href="/wiki/Michael_III" title="Michael III">Michael III</a>, to permit the restoration of <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icons</a> by arranging that her dead husband would not be <a href="/wiki/Anathema" title="Anathema">condemned</a>.  He then deposed the iconoclast Patriarch <a href="/wiki/John_VII_of_Constantinople" title="John VII of Constantinople">John VII Grammatikos</a> and secured the appointment of Methodios as his successor, bringing about the end of the iconoclast controversy.  A week after his appointment, accompanied by Theodora, Michael, and Theoktistos, Methodios made a triumphal procession from the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Mary_of_Blachernae_(Istanbul)" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Mary of Blachernae (Istanbul)">church of Blachernae</a> to <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a> on March 11, 843, restoring the icons to the church.  This heralded the restoration of Catholic orthodoxy, and became a holiday in the Byzantine Church, celebrated every year on the First Sunday of <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a>, and known as the "<a href="/wiki/Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Triumph of Orthodoxy">Triumph of Orthodoxy</a>".
</p><p>Throughout his short patriarchate, Methodios tried to pursue a moderate line of accommodation with members of the clergy who were formerly Iconoclasts.  This policy was opposed by extremists, primarily the monks of the <a href="/wiki/Stoudios" class="mw-redirect" title="Stoudios">Stoudios</a> monastery, who demanded that the former Iconoclasts be punished severely as heretics.  To rein in the extremists, Methodios was forced to excommunicate and arrest some of the more persevering monks.
</p><p>Methodios was indeed well-educated; engaged in both copying and writing of manuscripts.  His individual works included polemica, hagiographical and liturgical works, sermons and poetry.
</p>
