					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Barsimaeus</div><br /><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Bishop of Edessa</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">114<br /><a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">30 January (Roman calendar)<br />29 January (Eastern calendar)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Barsimaeus</b> (or <b>Barsimeus, Barses, Barsamya</b>; said to have died in 114&#160;AD) (<a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>: ܒܪܣܡܝܐ possibly “son of the blind man" or “son of the divine standard”)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a> who is revered as a martyr.
His feast day is 30 January.
In fact, his story probably derives from that of a much later bishop of Edessa who was persecuted around 250&#160;AD but not martyred.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Barsimaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The standard account says Barsimaeus was a missionary bishop of <a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a>, Syria. 
He was martyred in 114&#160;AD during the persecutions of Christians by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a> (r.  98–117).
His feast day is 30 January in the Roman calendar, and 29 January in the Eastern calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Barsimaeus_of_Edessa_CatholicSaints_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Barsimaeus_of_Edessa_CatholicSaints-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
Under 30 January the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> commemorates Barsimeo (Barsamya), bishop of Edessa, martyred under the emperor Trajan in 106 or 112 for having been too zealous in converting his fellow Christians, particularly <a href="/wiki/Charbel_(martyr)" title="Charbel (martyr)">Sarbil</a>, a pagan priest, and his sister Bebaia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESauget2013_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESauget2013-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It seems that this story was backdated about a century and a half in order to link the early Church of Edessa to the Apostles, and in fact refers to the persecution of Barsimaeus during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> (r. 249–251).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESauget2013_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESauget2013-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
He probably lived and worked in the middle of the 200s and succeeded <a href="/wiki/Palut" class="mw-redirect" title="Palut">Palut</a> as bishop of Edessa.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDen_hellige_Barsimaeus_av_Edessa_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDen_hellige_Barsimaeus_av_Edessa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the alternative version, Barsimeo, bishop of Edessa, was beaten with rods for his faith in Christ under the emperor Decius.
After he was released from prison, he devoted the rest of his life to the governance of the church that had been entrusted to him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESauget2013_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESauget2013-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
A variant says that Barsimaeus had baptized Sharbel, and was arrested and tried after Sharbel was martyred. 
The arrest triggered popular protests, and the judge delayed torturing or executing him despite his refusal to obey the emperor's decree.
Then a new decree reached Edessa that repealed the previous one and ordered his release.
The crowd received him with enthusiasm as a "persecuted confessor" and friend of Sharbel.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDen_hellige_Barsimaeus_av_Edessa_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDen_hellige_Barsimaeus_av_Edessa-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Barsimaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a>, wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921),
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>BARSIMAEUS (St.) Bp. M. (January 30)<br />(2nd century) The third Bishop of Edessa in Syria, put to death as a Christian by the President Lysias, under the Emperor Trajan, A.D. 114.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate192139_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate192139-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Barsimaeus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> wrote in his <i>Lives of the Primitive Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i>, under January 30,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St. Barsimæus, Bishop and Martyr [Called by the Syrians Barsaumas.] He was the third bishop of Edessa from Saint Thaddaeus, one of the seventy-two disciples. Saint Barsaumas was crowned with martyrdom, being condemned to die for his zeal in converting great multitudes to the faith, by the president Lysias, in the reign of Trajan, when that prince having passed the Euphrates, made the conquest of Mesopotamia in 114. Saint Barsimæus is mentioned on the 30th of January in the Roman Martyrology, and in the Greek Mænology.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1866_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1866-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
