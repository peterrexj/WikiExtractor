<p><b>Pierre-Rose-Ursule Dumoulin-Borie</b> (20 February 1808 – 24 November 1838) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> missionary priest and a member of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a>. He is a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1988 along with other <a href="/wiki/Vietnamese_Martyrs" title="Vietnamese Martyrs">Vietnamese Martyrs</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre_Dumoulin-Borie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg/220px-Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="292" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg/330px-Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg/440px-Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg 2x" data-file-width="467" data-file-height="619" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Pierre_Borie_MEP.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Pierre Borie (1808-1838).</div></div></div>
<p>Pierre Borie was the sixth of the twelve children of Guillaume Borie and his wife Rose Labrunie. The Borie family was a middle-class family of <a href="/wiki/Beynat" title="Beynat">Beynat</a> in <a href="/wiki/Limousin_(province)" title="Limousin (province)">Bas-Limousin</a>. Family members had different backgrounds. Pierre Dumoulin-Borie had as a godfather his uncle Pierre Borie, a priest refractory during the Revolution, and then <a href="/wiki/Cur%C3%A9" class="mw-redirect" title="Curé">curé</a> of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sionac&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sionac (page does not exist)">Sionac</a>; but an uncle, Jean Borie, was an administrator of <a href="/wiki/Corr%C3%A8ze" title="Corrèze">Corrèze</a>, deputy in the Legislative Parliament and Convention, and one of those who voted for the death of <a href="/wiki/Louis_XVI" title="Louis XVI">Louis XVI</a>.
</p><p>Pierre's godfather undertook the first rudiments of his education before he left for the Seminary of <a href="/wiki/Servi%C3%A8res" title="Servières">Servières</a> in 1824. He was ordained a priest in <a href="/wiki/Bayeux" title="Bayeux">Bayeux</a> on 21 November 1830, Pierre was sent overseas, reaching <a href="/wiki/Macao" class="mw-redirect" title="Macao">Macao</a> on 18 July 1831.
</p><p>He was brought to <a href="/wiki/Saigon" class="mw-redirect" title="Saigon">Saigon</a> by Chinese smugglers, evading the ban on proselytizing. He joined the South Tonkin mission, but was met with persecution. He persevered in fulfilling his pastoral task in the area which was entrusted to him. Pierre was arrested in 1838, and he learned in prison that he had been named <a href="/wiki/Titular_bishop" title="Titular bishop">titular bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Acanthus_(Greece)" class="mw-redirect" title="Acanthus (Greece)">Acanthus</a> on 30 January 1836. Shortly after this, on 24 November 1838, he was given a death sentence, which was carried out that day.
</p><p>Exhumed secretly eleven months later, the body of Saint Pierre rests today in The Martyr's Room, at the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a> in Paris.
</p>
