					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eochod&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Eochod">Eochod</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
<b>Eochaid</b> or <b>Eochaidh</b> (earlier <b>Eochu</b> or <b>Eocho</b>, sometimes Anglicised as <b>Eochy</b>, <b><a href="/wiki/Achaius_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Achaius (disambiguation)">Achaius</a></b> or <b><a href="/wiki/Haughey" title="Haughey">Haughey</a></b>) is a popular medieval <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a> and <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Gaelic" title="Scottish Gaelic">Scottish Gaelic</a> name deriving from <a href="/wiki/Old_Irish" title="Old Irish">Old Irish</a> <i>ech</i>, horse, borne by a variety of historical and legendary figures.
</p>

<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Eochaid_mac_Eirc" title="Eochaid mac Eirc">Eochaid mac Eirc</a>, <a href="/wiki/Irish_mythology" title="Irish mythology">mythological</a> king of the <a href="/wiki/Fir_Bolg" title="Fir Bolg">Fir Bolg</a> in the 16th or 20th century BC</li>

<li><a href="/wiki/Eochaid,_son_of_Rhun" class="mw-redirect" title="Eochaid, son of Rhun">Eochaid, son of Rhun</a>, historical king of Strathclyde (or perhaps of the Picts) in the 9th century AD</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Dallan_Forgaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Dallan Forgaill">Dallan Forgaill</a>, Eochaid Dallan Forgall, Saint and Poet 530 - 598</li></ul>
<p>Two legendary Irish High Kings were called <a href="/wiki/Rothechtaid_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Rothechtaid (disambiguation)">Rothechtaid</a>, which appears to mean "Eochaid's wheels"
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Fiction">Fiction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eochaid&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Fiction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>The name Eochaid was used by <a href="/wiki/Rutland_Boughton" title="Rutland Boughton">Rutland Boughton</a> for the king in his opera <a href="/wiki/The_Immortal_Hour" title="The Immortal Hour">The Immortal Hour</a> in 1914</li>
<li>In the 2022 video game <i><a href="/wiki/Elden_Ring" title="Elden Ring">Elden Ring</a></i>, "Eochaid" is a mentioned location and former home of the character "Elemer of the Briar". A collectable weapon "Regalia of Eochaid" also hails from the ficticious domain</li></ul>
