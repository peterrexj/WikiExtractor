<p><b>Pagan</b>, also <b>Paganus</b>, <b>Pain</b> or <b>Payn</b>, was a masculine <a href="/wiki/Given_name" title="Given name">given name</a> in use in Europe the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. Other forms include French <i>Payen</i>, <i>Païen</i> or <i>Péan</i>, and Italian <i>Pagano</i>.
</p>
<ul><li>Pagan I, <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_Haifa" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of Haifa">lord of Haifa</a> in 1107–1112</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Pagan_(chancellor)" title="Pagan (chancellor)">Pagan the Chancellor</a> (d. bef. 1129), crusader administrator</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Pain_fitzJohn" title="Pain fitzJohn">Pain fitzJohn</a> (d. 1137), Anglo-Norman administrator</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Pagan_the_Butler" title="Pagan the Butler">Pagan the Butler</a> (d. 1149), crusader baron</li>
<li>Payn, <a href="/wiki/Sheriff_of_Cambridgeshire_and_Huntingdonshire" title="Sheriff of Cambridgeshire and Huntingdonshire">sheriff of Cambridgeshire and Huntingdonshire</a> in 1155–1161</li>
<li>Payn de Beauchamp (d. c. 1157), husband of <a href="/wiki/Rohese_de_Vere,_Countess_of_Essex" title="Rohese de Vere, Countess of Essex">Rohese de Vere, Countess of Essex</a></li>
<li>Payn de Rochefort, <a href="/wiki/Seneschal_of_Anjou" title="Seneschal of Anjou">seneschal of Anjou</a> in 1190</li>
<li>Pagan II, lord of Haifa in 1190s</li>
