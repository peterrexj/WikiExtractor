<p><b>Pope Paschal I</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Paschalis I</i>; died 824) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 25 January 817 to his death in 824.
</p><p>Paschal was a member of an aristocratic Roman family. Before his election to the papacy, he was abbot of St. Stephen's monastery, which served pilgrims. In <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in 823 he crowned <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair I</a> as <a href="/wiki/Coronation_of_the_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Coronation of the Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a>. He rebuilt a number of churches in Rome, including three basilicas.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Paschal_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i>, Paschal was a native of Lazio <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> born Pascale Massimi and son of Bonosus and <a href="/wiki/Episcopa_Theodora" title="Episcopa Theodora">Episcopa Theodora</a>. The <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Censuum" title="Liber Censuum">Liber Censuum</a></i> says that Paschal was from the Massimi branch of the <a href="/wiki/Massimo_family" title="Massimo family">Massimo family</a>, as was his predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_IV" title="Pope Stephen IV">Stephen IV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-g9n13_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g9n13-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_III" title="Pope Leo III">Pope Leo III</a> placed Paschal in charge of the monastery of <a href="/wiki/St_Stephen_of_the_Abyssinians" class="mw-redirect" title="St Stephen of the Abyssinians">St Stephen of the Abyssinians</a>, where his responsibilities included the care of pilgrims visiting Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to early modern accounts, Leo III may have elevated Paschal as the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal priest">cardinal priest</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-g9_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g9-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  Goodson attributes this account to a "desire to explain the attention that the pope so lavishly and prominently paid to that church later in his career."<sup id="cite_ref-g9_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g9-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Accession">Accession</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Paschal_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Accession">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Paschal became <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> on 25 January 817, just one day after the sudden death of Stephen IV.<sup id="cite_ref-g9_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g9-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This decision was made without the sanction of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a>. Paschal began his pontificate apologizing for this slight, stressing that the office had been thrust upon him.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He claimed that the decision had been made to avoid factional strife in Rome. According to the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i>, Pope Paschal's <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legate</a> Theodore returned with a document titled <i>Pactum cum Paschali pontifice</i>, in which the emperor congratulated Paschal, recognized his sovereignty over the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, and guaranteed the free election of future pontiffs.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly1_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly1-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> This document has since been challenged by historians as a forgery.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Paschal_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At first, Emperor Louis confirmed the agreement reached in <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims</a> with Paschal's predecessor, Stephen IV, and detailed in the document <i><a href="/wiki/Pactum_Ludovicanum" class="mw-redirect" title="Pactum Ludovicanum">Pactum Ludovicanum</a></i> about free papal elections and noninterference in Church affairs unless officially asked for help. The two worked together to send Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Ebbo" title="Ebbo">Ebbo</a> of Rheims to evangelize the <a href="/wiki/Danes_(Germanic_tribe)" class="mw-redirect" title="Danes (Germanic tribe)">Danes</a> in 822.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On Easter Sunday of 823, Paschal <a href="/wiki/Coronation" title="Coronation">crowned</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anointed" class="mw-redirect" title="Anointed">anointed</a> Louis's son <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair I</a>. Lothair was less amenable to cooperating with the Papal Curia than his father. He held a court and declared <a href="/wiki/Farfa_Abbey" title="Farfa Abbey">Farfa Abbey</a>, just north of Rome, exempt from papal taxation. Paschal's aristocratic opponents in the papal palace, especially his former legate, Theodore, and his son-in-law, Leo, who turned to the young leader of the Franks for support in their opposition to Paschal.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The decision outraged the Roman nobility and led to an uprising against the authority of the Roman Curia in northern Italy led by Theodore and Leo. The revolt was quickly suppressed, and two of its leaders were seized, blinded, and afterwards beheaded by members of the papal household. Paschal denied any involvement, but the Emperor remained suspicious and sent two commissioners to investigate. Paschal refused to submit to the authority of the imperial court, but he did take an oath of purgation before a synod of thirty-four bishops.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The commissioners returned to <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a>, and Emperor Louis let the matter drop.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Construction_projects">Construction projects</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Paschal_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Construction projects">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Paschal gave shelter to exiled monks from the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> who had fled persecution for their opposition to <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_iconoclasm" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine iconoclasm">iconoclasm</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He both offered the exiled Byzantine mosaic artists work decorating churches in Rome<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly1_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly1-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and wrote to Louis the Pious<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and the Byzantine emperor <a href="/wiki/Leo_the_Armenian" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo the Armenian">Leo the Armenian</a> in support of those who opposed iconoclasm.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paschal rebuilt three basilicas of Rome:  <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a>, <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Domnica" title="Santa Maria in Domnica">Santa Maria in Domnica</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Santa_Cecilia_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Cecilia in Trastevere">Santa Cecilia in Trastevere</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> These churches contain mosaics with lifelike portraits of Paschal.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Paschal is credited with finding the body of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cecilia" title="Saint Cecilia">Saint Cecilia</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb_of_Callixtus" title="Catacomb of Callixtus">Catacomb of Callixtus</a> and translating it to the rebuild the basilica of Santa Cecilia in Trastevere. Paschal also undertook significant renovations on <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_Santa_Maria_Maggiore" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore">Basilica di Santa Maria Maggiore</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In addition, Paschal  added two oratories to <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">Old St. Peter's Basilica</a>, SS. Processus et Martinianus and SS. Xistus et Fabianus, which did not survive the 16th century renovation of St. Peter's.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paschal is also sometimes credited with the renovation of <a href="/wiki/Santo_Stefano_del_Cacco" title="Santo Stefano del Cacco">Santo Stefano del Cacco</a> in early modern sources, but this renovation was actually undertaken by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Goodson, Paschal "used church-building to express the authority of the papacy as an independent state."<sup id="cite_ref-g14_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g14-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Paschal_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_(FindID_69063).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_%28FindID_69063%29.jpg/220px-Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_%28FindID_69063%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="155" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_%28FindID_69063%29.jpg/330px-Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_%28FindID_69063%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_%28FindID_69063%29.jpg/440px-Detailed_image_of_Papal_bulla_of_Paschal_I_817-824_%28FindID_69063%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="709" data-file-height="500" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Papal_bulla" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal bulla">Papal bulla</a> of Paschal I</figcaption></figure>
<p>Only six known letters written by Paschal remain.<sup id="cite_ref-g8n11_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g8n11-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  The first (Jaffé 2546) confirms the possessions of the <a href="/wiki/Territorial_Abbey_of_Farfa" class="mw-redirect" title="Territorial Abbey of Farfa">Territorial Abbey of Farfa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-g8n11_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g8n11-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  The second and third (Jaffé 2547 and Jaffé 2548) were written to a Frankish abbot prior to and after his elevation as <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Vienne" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Diocese of Vienne">archbishop of Vienne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-g8n11_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g8n11-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  The fourth (Jaffé 2550) was written to <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-g8n11_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g8n11-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  The fifth (Jaffé 2551, preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Biblioteca_Ambrosiana" title="Biblioteca Ambrosiana">Biblioteca Ambrosiana</a>) confirms the privileges of the church of <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-g8n11_17-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g8n11-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  The last (Jaffé 2553) was written to <a href="/wiki/Ebbo" title="Ebbo">Ebbo</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Reims" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Reims">archbishop of Reims</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-g8n11_17-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-g8n11-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Paschal_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Paschal died on 11 February 824. The Roman Curia refused him the honour of burial within <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> because of his harsh government of the Roman people.<sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was instead buried in the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Prassede" title="Santa Prassede">Santa Prassede</a>, which also contains the famous <i><a href="/wiki/Episcopa_Theodora" title="Episcopa Theodora">Episcopa Theodora</a></i> mosaic of his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paschal was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in the late sixteenth century. His feast day in the Roman calendar prior to 1963 was 14 May.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-O&#39;Brien_Lives_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-O&#39;Brien_Lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It is currently celebrated on 11 February.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope and Patriarch of Rome (817–824),<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> confessor is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">honored</a> in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/May_14" title="May 14">14 May</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
