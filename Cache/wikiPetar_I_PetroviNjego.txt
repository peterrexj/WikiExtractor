<p><b>Petar I <a href="/wiki/Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1" class="mw-redirect" title="Petrović-Njegoš">Petrović-Njegoš</a></b> (<a href="/wiki/Serbian_Cyrillic" class="mw-redirect" title="Serbian Cyrillic">Serbian Cyrillic</a>: Петар I Петровић Његош; 1748 – 31 October 1830) was the ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Montenegro" title="Prince-Bishopric of Montenegro">Prince-Bishopric of Montenegro</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> (<i>vladika</i>) of <a href="/wiki/Cetinje" title="Cetinje">Cetinje</a>, and Exarch (legate) of the Serbian Orthodox Church in Montenegro. He was the most popular spiritual and military leader from the <a href="/wiki/Petrovi%C4%87_dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Petrović dynasty">Petrović dynasty</a>. During his long rule, Petar strengthened the state by uniting the often quarreling <a href="/wiki/Tribes_of_Montenegro" title="Tribes of Montenegro">tribes</a>, consolidating his control over <a href="/wiki/Old_Montenegro" title="Old Montenegro">Montenegrin lands</a>, introducing the first laws in Montenegro (<i>Zakonik Petra I</i>). His rule prepared Montenegro for the subsequent introduction of modern institutions of the state: taxes, schools and larger commercial enterprises. He was canonized by the Serbian Orthodox Church as <b>Saint Peter of Cetinje</b> (<span title="Serbian-language text"><i lang="sr">Sveti Petar Cetinjski</i></span>).
</p><p>He was described as "a man of uncommon size, handsome features, considerable talent, and a highly respected character" by <a href="/wiki/Therese_Albertine_Luise_Robinson" title="Therese Albertine Luise Robinson">Therese Albertine Luise Robinson</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-TalvjRobinson1801_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TalvjRobinson1801-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The son of Marko and Anđelija (née Martinović), Petar followed the footsteps of his relatives, becoming a monk and a deacon. He spent four years in <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial Russia">Imperial Russia</a>, finishing the Military School (1765–69). In 1778, archimandrite Petar was in Russia with <i>guvernadur</i> Jovan Radonjić and <i>serdar</i> Ivan Petrović.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Metropolitan <a href="/wiki/Sava_Petrovi%C4%87_(prince-bishop)" title="Sava Petrović (prince-bishop)">Sava Petrović</a> (s. 1735–1781), who died in 1781, chose his nephew and co-adjutor <a href="/wiki/Arsenije_Plamenac" title="Arsenije Plamenac">Arsenije Plamenac</a> the successor,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKostić2000351_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKostić2000351-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which was met with opposition from the Montenegrin tribes at the beginning, later switching in favour after Sava gained the support of <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0%C4%87epan_Mali" title="Šćepan Mali">Šćepan Mali</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-JCEA-1959-81_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JCEA-1959-81-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> the false tsar and ruler of Montenegro. Plamenac was inactive and had little power,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> serving as metropolitan between 1781 and his death in 1784.<sup id="cite_ref-JCEA-1959-81_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JCEA-1959-81-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <i>guvernadur</i> family of Radonjić, which had Venetian support and then Austrian, increased their power and sought to push aside the Petrović metropolitans.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The chieftains had suggested Petar the bishop seat already in 1783, and he was then sent to the Habsburg Monarchy to be ordained.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Office">Office</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Office">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="1784.E2.80.9399"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="1784–99">1784–99</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: 1784–99">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Petar was made a bishop (ordained) by <a href="/wiki/Mojsije_Putnik" title="Mojsije Putnik">Mojsije Putnik</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitanate_of_Karlovci" title="Metropolitanate of Karlovci">Metropolitanate of Karlovci</a> at <a href="/wiki/Sremski_Karlovci" title="Sremski Karlovci">Sremski Karlovci</a> on 13 October 1784. He then returned to Vienna where he met Russian chancellor Potemkin, and also Austrian general Zorić and Ragusan emigre Frano Dolci.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Petar was, under unclear circumstances, kicked out of St. Petersburg in November 1785.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During his trip, Montenegro was threatened by <a href="/wiki/Kara_Mahmud_Pasha" title="Kara Mahmud Pasha">Kara Mahmud Pasha</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Pasha_of_Scutari" class="mw-redirect" title="Pasha of Scutari">Pasha of Scutari</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mahmud Pasha attacked in June 1785.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStamatović1999-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Montenegrin army of 8000 was reduced by 3,000 <a href="/wiki/Crmnica" title="Crmnica">Crmničani</a>, and they were followed by many more surrenders.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESrbsko_Učeno_Društvo1891262_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESrbsko_Učeno_Društvo1891262-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Mahmud Pasha crossed <a href="/wiki/Bjelice" title="Bjelice">Bjelice</a>, and burnt down <a href="/wiki/Njegu%C5%A1i" title="Njeguši">Njeguši</a>, and received help from <a href="/wiki/Nik%C5%A1i%C4%87_(tribe)" class="mw-redirect" title="Nikšić (tribe)">Nikšići</a>, then crossed <a href="/wiki/Pa%C5%A1trovi%C4%87i" title="Paštrovići">Paštrovići</a> to return to Scutari.<sup id="cite_ref-PlamenacBojović1997_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PlamenacBojović1997-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Metropolitan Petar I and <i><a href="/wiki/Guvernadur" title="Guvernadur">guvernadur</a></i> <a href="/wiki/Jovan_Radonji%C4%87" title="Jovan Radonjić">Jovan Radonjić</a> were the two head chiefs of Montenegro, one by title, the other according to actual position.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Jovan sought to rule Montenegro by himself; he appropriated secular rights for himself and wanted the Metropolitan to exercise only his spiritual leadership;<sup id="cite_ref-Brastvo32-91_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brastvo32-91-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> that the <i>guvernadur</i> was the master of the people and the Metropolitan the master of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESrbsko_Učeno_Društvo1891227_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESrbsko_Učeno_Društvo1891227-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The two clashed in international politics: the Metropolitan held to Russia, while Jovan relied on Austria.<sup id="cite_ref-Brastvo32-91_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brastvo32-91-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStanojevićVasić1975460_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStanojevićVasić1975460-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Hence, there were two parties in the land, one "Russophile" and the other "Austrophile", led by the Metropolitan and Jovan, respectively.<sup id="cite_ref-Brastvo32-91_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brastvo32-91-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> On the question whether to support Austria or not, the two sides conflicted during the <a href="/wiki/Austro-Turkish_War_(1787%E2%80%9391)" class="mw-redirect" title="Austro-Turkish War (1787–91)">Austro-Turkish War (1787–91)</a> and <a href="/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_(1787%E2%80%9392)" class="mw-redirect" title="Russo-Turkish War (1787–92)">Russo-Turkish War (1787–92)</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> During this period, Montenegro was divided into the following districts: <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Katunska_nahija&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Katunska nahija (page does not exist)">Katunska nahija</a></i>, <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rije%C4%8Dka_nahija&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Riječka nahija (page does not exist)">Riječka nahija</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Crmnica" title="Crmnica">Crmnička nahija</a></i>, <i><a href="/wiki/Lje%C5%A1anska_nahija" title="Lješanska nahija">Lješanska nahija</a></i> and <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pje%C5%A1iva%C4%8Dka_nahija&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pješivačka nahija (page does not exist)">Pješivačka nahija</a></i>. These were governed by the officials, Jovan Radonjić and the Metropolitan, with the help of 5 serdars, 9 vojvodas and 34 knezes (a synthesis of secular and theocratic government which will cause strife and struggle for supremacy until 1832–33).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In July 1788, Jovan Radonjić asked Empress Catherine II of Russia to send <a href="/wiki/Sofronije_Jugovi%C4%87-Markovi%C4%87" title="Sofronije Jugović-Marković">Sofronije Jugović-Marković</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Đ1900_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Đ1900-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> whom he promised the throne of Montenegro; Jovan sought to bring him to the land and replace Petrović, then get rid of him too, securing the rule for himself.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESrbsko_Učeno_Društvo1891228_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESrbsko_Učeno_Društvo1891228-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> He sent another letter in 1789,<sup id="cite_ref-Đ1900_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Đ1900-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> then made a trip to Austria<sup id="cite_ref-ICG-442_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICG-442-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> seeking to retrieve his reputation with the help of the Austrian court.<sup id="cite_ref-ICG-442_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICG-442-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Radonjić requested that the Austrian army be sent into Montenegro, which was declined.<sup id="cite_ref-ICG-442_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICG-442-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> On Radonjić's re-request, the Austrian Emperor decided to send munition to Montenegro in February 1790, provided that the Montenegrins "come under the wings of the Emperor in war-time, as much as in peace-time, with the Ottoman Empire".<sup id="cite_ref-ICG-442_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICG-442-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Austrian support looked unpromising.<sup id="cite_ref-ICG-442_15-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ICG-442-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1794, the <a href="/wiki/Ku%C4%8Di" class="mw-redirect" title="Kuči">Kuči</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rov%C4%8Dani" title="Rovčani">Rovčani</a> (tribes outside Montenegro) were devastated by the Ottomans.<sup id="cite_ref-Barjaktarovic-28_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barjaktarovic-28-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> In 1796, Kara Mahmud Pasha was defeated at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Martini%C4%87i" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Martinići">Battle of Martinići</a>. Mahmud Pasha later returned and was defeated and killed at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Krusi" title="Battle of Krusi">Battle of Krusi</a> on 22 September. Half of the Montenegrin army was led by Metropolitan Petar I, the other by Jovan.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Petar I's army was assisted by the <a href="/wiki/Piperi_(tribe)" title="Piperi (tribe)">Piperi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Barjaktarovic-28_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barjaktarovic-28-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Mahmud Pasha's army, allegedly made up of 30,000, including seven French officers, fought a Montenegrin force of 6,000, and had heavy casualties. The Montenegrin victory resulted in territorial expansion, with the tribes of <a href="/wiki/Bjelopavli%C4%87i" title="Bjelopavlići">Bjelopavlići</a> and Piperi being joined into the Montenegrin state.<sup id="cite_ref-Čulinović1954_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Čulinović1954-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Rov%C4%8Dani" title="Rovčani">Rovčani</a>, as other highlander tribes, subsequently turned more and more towards Montenegro.<sup id="cite_ref-Barjaktarovic-29_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barjaktarovic-29-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The tribe of <a href="/wiki/Njegu%C5%A1i" title="Njeguši">Njeguši</a> to which the <a href="/wiki/Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1_dynasty" title="Petrović-Njegoš dynasty">Petrović-Njegoš</a> belonged was involved in the one of the most long-standing blood feuds in Montenegro against the tribe of <a href="/wiki/Ceklin" title="Ceklin">Ceklin</a> in the 18th century. The feud lasted for 32 years and ended in 1797 in a joint meeting of all tribes of Old Montenegro under Petar Petrović. The pacification of the feud between two of the strongest tribes of the region is considered as an important step in Petar Petrović's strategy of uniting Old Montenegro.<sup id="cite_ref-Boehm_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boehm-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Metropolitan Petar I sent letters in 1799 to the <a href="/wiki/Mora%C4%8Dani" class="mw-redirect" title="Moračani">Moračani</a> and Rovčani, advising them to live peacefully and in solidarity.<sup id="cite_ref-Barjaktarovic-29_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barjaktarovic-29-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>  In 1799, Montenegro was guaranteed a subsidy by Russia, which assured that it would defend its interest.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirályRothenberg198265_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirályRothenberg198265-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="1800.E2.80.9309"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="1800–09">1800–09</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: 1800–09">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/First_Serbian_Uprising" title="First Serbian Uprising">First Serbian Uprising</a> Petar I began cooperating with <a href="/wiki/Kara%C4%91or%C4%91e" title="Karađorđe">Karađorđe</a>, the Serbian rebel leader. Petar I had by that time distinguished himself in international relations, as the bishop and ruler of Montenegro. It was known that Petar was ready to revolt as soon as a favourable opportunity came along. Russian ambassador in Vienna, Razumovski, informed Interior Minister <a href="/wiki/Adam_Jerzy_Czartoryski" title="Adam Jerzy Czartoryski">Czartoryski</a> of a secret message received on 13 December 1803 that Petar I had 2,000 armed men, and that he "had taken off his bishop clothes and dressed in military clothing, a general uniform" and that he planned to raise his army to 12,000 men. Knowing that his army would not be able to fight the stronger Ottomans, he sought to unite with the "rebels of <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0umadija" title="Šumadija">Šumadija</a>", and together, with the help of the Russian, turn on the Turks. He messaged <a href="/wiki/Visoki_De%C4%8Dani" title="Visoki Dečani">Visoki Dečani</a> of his intentions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBogunović_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBogunović-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Petar I was a <a href="/wiki/Pen_pal" title="Pen pal">pen pal</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dositej_Obradovi%C4%87" title="Dositej Obradović">Dositej Obradović</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
 <figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87_Njego%C5%A1,_French_drawing.png" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Petar I Petrovic as a warrior (by French drawing)" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87_Njego%C5%A1%2C_French_drawing.png/220px-Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87_Njego%C5%A1%2C_French_drawing.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="253" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8b/Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87_Njego%C5%A1%2C_French_drawing.png 1.5x" data-file-width="316" data-file-height="364" /></a><figcaption>Petar I Petrović as a warrior (by French drawing)</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1806, the troops of <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_I_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleon I of France">Napoleonic</a> <a href="/wiki/First_French_Empire" title="First French Empire">France</a> advanced toward the <a href="/wiki/Bay_of_Kotor" title="Bay of Kotor">Bay of Kotor</a> in Montenegro. The Montenegrin army led by Petar I, aided by several Russian battalions and the fleet of <a href="/wiki/Admiral" title="Admiral">Admiral</a> <a href="/wiki/Dmitry_Senyavin" title="Dmitry Senyavin">Dmitry Senyavin</a> pushed them back to <a href="/wiki/Dubrovnik" title="Dubrovnik">Dubrovnik</a>. But soon after, Russian <a href="/wiki/Tsar" title="Tsar">Tsar</a> <a href="/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia" title="Alexander I of Russia">Alexander I</a> asked Montenegrins to relinquish control of <a href="/wiki/Boka_Kotorska" class="mw-redirect" title="Boka Kotorska">Boka</a> to <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>. However, after Montenegrins retreated to <a href="/wiki/Herceg_Novi" title="Herceg Novi">Herceg Novi</a>, Alexander changed his mind again, and with a help of Montenegrins conquered <a href="/wiki/Bra%C4%8D" title="Brač">Brač</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kor%C4%8Dula" title="Korčula">Korčula</a>. In the meantime, France encouraged Turkey to attack Russia, which withdrew its fleet from the <a href="/wiki/Adriatic" class="mw-redirect" title="Adriatic">Adriatic</a> to defend the <a href="/wiki/Ionian_islands" class="mw-redirect" title="Ionian islands">Ionian islands</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Tilsit" class="mw-redirect" title="Treaty of Tilsit">Treaty of Tilsit</a> (1807) between Russia and France granted the control of the Bay of Kotor to France. In early 1807, Petar had plans to unite several Serb-inhabited regions into <a href="#Pan-Serbism">a renewed Serbian Empire</a>. In February 1807, Petar I planned an invasion of Herzegovina and asked for Karađorđe's aid.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirályRothenberg198265_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirályRothenberg198265-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> After the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Suvodol" title="Battle of Suvodol">Battle of Suvodol</a> and Serbian rebel advance, Karađorđe managed connecting the rebel forces to Montenegro (1809).<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> However, Karađorđe was unable to hold lasting ties with the Serbs of Montenegro and Bosnia and Herzegovina, as after 1809 the uprising waned.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="1810.E2.80.9320"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="1810–20">1810–20</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: 1810–20">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Petar I waged a successful campaign against the <a href="/wiki/Bosnia_Eyalet" title="Bosnia Eyalet">Bosnia Eyalet</a> in 1819. In 1820, in the north of Montenegro, the highlanders from <a href="/wiki/Mora%C4%8Da" title="Morača">Morača</a> led by <i><a href="/wiki/Field_marshal" title="Field marshal">serdar</a></i> Mrkoje Mijušković won a major battle against the Ottoman Bosnian forces. The repulse of an Ottoman invasion from Albania during the <a href="/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_(1828%E2%80%9329)" class="mw-redirect" title="Russo-Turkish War (1828–29)">Russo-Turkish War (1828–29)</a> led to the recognition of Montenegrin sovereignty over Piperi.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiller2012142_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiller2012142-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Petar I had managed to unite the Piperi and Bjelopavlići with Montenegro,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMiller2012142_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiller2012142-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> and when Bjelopavlići and the rest of <i>the Hills</i> (Seven hills) were joined into the Montenegrin state, the polity was officially called "Black Mountain (Montenegro) and the Hills".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pan-Serbism">Pan-Serbism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Pan-Serbism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Petar I conceived a plan in 1807 to revive a Serbian Empire ("Slaveno–Serb empire"), which he informed the Russian court.<sup id="cite_ref-JM_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JM-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Oslobodjenje_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oslobodjenje-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Earlier, in June 1804, Habsburg Serb metropolitan <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Stratimirovi%C4%87" title="Stefan Stratimirović">Stefan Stratimirović</a> informed the Russian court of the same plan.<sup id="cite_ref-Поповић1933_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Поповић1933-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Petar I's plan was to unite <a href="/wiki/Podgorica" title="Podgorica">Podgorica</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spu%C5%BE" title="Spuž">Spuž</a>, <a href="/wiki/%C5%BDabljak" title="Žabljak">Žabljak</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bay_of_Kotor" title="Bay of Kotor">Bay of Kotor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bosnia_(region)" title="Bosnia (region)">Bosnia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Herzegovina" title="Herzegovina">Herzegovina</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dubrovnik" title="Dubrovnik">Dubrovnik</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatia</a> with Montenegro.<sup id="cite_ref-JM_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JM-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The title of Serbian emperor would be held by the Russian emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-JM_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JM-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The French–Russian peace treaty thwarted the plan.<sup id="cite_ref-JM_28-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JM-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> After the French conquered Dalmatia, they offered Petar I the title of "Patriarch of all of the Serb nation or all Illyricum" under the condition that he stop cooperation with Russia and accept a French protectorate, which he declined, fearing eventual Papal jurisdiction.<sup id="cite_ref-Oslobodjenje_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oslobodjenje-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The Metropolitanate of Cetinje began exerting influence towards <a href="/wiki/Brda,_Montenegro" class="mw-redirect" title="Brda, Montenegro">Brda</a> and <a href="/wiki/Old_Herzegovina" title="Old Herzegovina">Old Herzegovina</a>, which considered Montenegro as the leader for liberation.<sup id="cite_ref-Oslobodjenje_29-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oslobodjenje-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> While his reputation and influence reached the surrounding lands, he increasingly directed himself to <a href="/wiki/Revolutionary_Serbia" title="Revolutionary Serbia">Revolutionary Serbia</a> as the backbone for liberation and unification.<sup id="cite_ref-Oslobodjenje_29-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oslobodjenje-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The project is included in several historiographical works.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonisation">Canonisation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Petar_I_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Canonisation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg/220px-Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="311" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg/330px-Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg/440px-Saint_Peter_of_Cetinje.jpg 2x" data-file-width="518" data-file-height="732" /></a><figcaption>Orthodox icon of Petar as a saint.</figcaption></figure>
<p>He was canonised as <b>Saint Peter of Cetinje</b> by his successor <a href="/wiki/Petar_II_Petrovi%C4%87-Njego%C5%A1" title="Petar II Petrović-Njegoš">Petar II Petrović-Njegoš</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church" title="Serbian Orthodox Church">Serbian Orthodox Church</a> celebrate him on October 31, <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_calendar" title="Gregorian calendar">Gregorian calendar</a>, which is October 18th in the <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a>.
</p>
