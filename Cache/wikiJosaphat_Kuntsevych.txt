<p><b>Josaphat Kuntsevych</b>, OSBM (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1580</span>&#160;– 12 November 1623) was a <a href="/wiki/Basilian_monks" title="Basilian monks">Basilian monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eparch" class="mw-redirect" title="Eparch">archeparch</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ruthenian_Uniate_Church" title="Ruthenian Uniate Church">Ruthenian Uniate Church</a> who on 12 November 1623 was killed by an angry mob in <a href="/wiki/Vitebsk" title="Vitebsk">Vitebsk</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> in the eastern peripheries of the <a href="/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth" title="Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth">Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a>. He remains one of the best-known victims of <a href="/wiki/Anti-Catholicism" title="Anti-Catholicism">anti-Catholic</a> violence related to implementing the <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Kempa2010_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kempa2010-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 57">&#58;&#8202;57&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Subtelny2009_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Subtelny2009-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and has been declared a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His death reflects the conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a> that had intensified after several members of the hierarchy of the Ruthenian Orthodox Church (Kiev Metropolitanate) rejected the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate">Ecumenical Patriarchate</a> and instead entered communion with the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> under the terms laid down by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Florence" title="Council of Florence">Council of Florence</a> in the 1596 <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Historical_and_religious_background">Historical and religious background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Historical and religious background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>King <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_III_Vasa" title="Sigismund III Vasa">Sigismund III Vasa</a>'s policy for the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation_in_the_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth" class="mw-redirect" title="Counter-Reformation in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth">Counter-Reformation in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a> was to reunite, "through missions to non-Catholics, both Protestant and Orthodox," all Christians into the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Crummey2006_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crummey2006-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 302–303">&#58;&#8202;302–303&#8202;</span></sup> After preliminary negotiations with Sigismund III and with <a href="/wiki/Grand_Chancellor_of_the_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Chancellor of the Crown">Grand Chancellor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Great_Hetman_of_the_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Hetman of the Crown">Great Hetman of the Crown</a> <a href="/wiki/Jan_Zamoyski" title="Jan Zamoyski">Jan Zamoyski</a>, a delegation of bishops from the Eastern Orthodox <a href="/w/index.php?title=Metropolitanate_of_Kiev_(1458%E2%80%931596)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Metropolitanate of Kiev (1458–1596) (page does not exist)">Metropolitanate of Kiev (1458–1596)</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%97%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%96%D1%8F_(1458%E2%80%941596)" class="extiw" title="uk:Київська митрополія (1458—1596)">uk</a>&#93;</span> was sent to Rome in 1595 to accede to the <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a> on condition that their rituals and discipline were left intact.<sup id="cite_ref-Bain1908_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bain1908-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 202–203">&#58;&#8202;202–203&#8202;</span></sup> Most Eastern Orthodox bishops within the Commonwealth, including <a href="/wiki/Michael_Rohoza" title="Michael Rohoza">Michael Rohoza</a>, <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Kiev">metropolitan of Kiev</a> – but at <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Voivodeship" title="Vilnius Voivodeship">Vilnius Voivodeship</a>, the capital city of the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania" title="Grand Duchy of Lithuania">Grand Duchy of Lithuania</a>, in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth – were signatories of the Union of Brest in 1596 which brought the Metropolitanate of Kiev into communion with the pope. Two ecclesiastical factions, those Eastern Orthodox bishops who were signatories and those Eastern Orthodox bishops who were not signatories, met and excommunicated each other, but those who did not assent were in a much worse position than before, because they were no longer officially recognized.<sup id="cite_ref-Bain1908_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bain1908-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 204">&#58;&#8202;204&#8202;</span></sup> The Union resulted in two sectarian groups:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a></div>
<ul><li>Ruthenian Orthodox Christians who accepted the Union of Brest became <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Catholic">Eastern Catholics</a> and were known as "<a href="/wiki/Uniates" class="mw-redirect" title="Uniates">Uniates</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-Davies1982a_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davies1982a-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 174">&#58;&#8202;174&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup>  "<i>(<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">unici</i>)</i>", a term which, while once widely used and considered benign, is now considered derogatory and insulting.<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_Uniate_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA_Uniate-14">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CNEWA2011_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CNEWA2011-15">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Eastern Catholic Ruthenians were detested, and considered "schismatics and traitors" by those who remained Orthodox and under the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate">Ecumenical Patriarchate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Suziedelis2011_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Suziedelis2011-17">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 69">&#58;&#8202;69&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> "About two-thirds of the Ruthenian population", however, had become Greek-Catholics by 1620.<sup id="cite_ref-MedlinPatrinelis1971_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MedlinPatrinelis1971-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 88">&#58;&#8202;88&#8202;</span></sup> The northeastern <a href="/wiki/Voivodeships" class="mw-redirect" title="Voivodeships">voivodeships</a> became predominantly "Uniate".<sup id="cite_ref-Wilson2011_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilson2011-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 42">&#58;&#8202;42&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Skinner2009_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Skinner2009-20">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 42">&#58;&#8202;42&#8202;</span></sup></li>
<li>Eastern Orthodox adherents who did not assent to the Union of Brest articles remained Eastern Orthodox and were known as "<a href="/w/index.php?title=Disuniates&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Disuniates (page does not exist)">Disuniates</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/dysunici" class="extiw" title="pl:dysunici">pl</a>&#93;</span>", or "<i>(<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">dysunici</i>)</i>"; they were also called "dissidents" by both Roman and Byzantine Catholics.<sup id="cite_ref-Davies1982a_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davies1982a-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 174">&#58;&#8202;174&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;f&#93;</a></sup> The southeastern voivodeships became predominantly Disuniate.<sup id="cite_ref-Wilson2011_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilson2011-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 42">&#58;&#8202;42&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Skinner2009_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Skinner2009-20">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 42">&#58;&#8202;42&#8202;</span></sup> Disuniates were subjected to varying degrees of <a href="/wiki/Religious_discrimination" title="Religious discrimination">religious discrimination</a> by the Government of the Commonwealth, at the insistence of some Byzantine and Roman Catholic clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-Suziedelis2011_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Suziedelis2011-17">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 95–97">&#58;&#8202;95–97&#8202;</span></sup> The Disuniates were leaderless until a reestablished Eastern Orthodox <a href="/w/index.php?title=Metropolitanate_of_Kiev_(1620%E2%80%931685)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Metropolitanate of Kiev (1620–1685) (page does not exist)">Metropolitanate of Kiev</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%B8%D1%97%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%96%D1%8F_(1620%E2%80%941685)" class="extiw" title="uk:Київська митрополія (1620—1685)">uk</a>&#93;</span> was consecrated in 1620, which the government legalized in 1632.<sup id="cite_ref-Subtelny2009_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Subtelny2009-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was born Ioann Kuntsevych in 1580 or 1584 in <a href="/wiki/Volodymyr-Volynskyi" class="mw-redirect" title="Volodymyr-Volynskyi">Volodymyr</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;g&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Volhynian_Voivodeship_(1569%E2%80%931795)" title="Volhynian Voivodeship (1569–1795)">Volhynian Voivodeship</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Province_of_the_Polish_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Lesser Poland Province of the Polish Crown">Lesser Poland Province of the Polish Crown</a> (now in <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>). He was baptized into a family associated with the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.
</p><p>Although descended from <a href="/wiki/Ruthenian_nobility" title="Ruthenian nobility">Ruthenian nobility</a> (<a href="/wiki/Szlachta" title="Szlachta">szlachta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kuncewicz" class="mw-redirect" title="Kuncewicz">Kuncewicz family</a>), his father had embarked in business, and held the office of town-<a href="/wiki/Councilor" class="mw-redirect" title="Councilor">councilor</a>. Both of Kuntsevych's parents encouraged religious participation and Christian piety in the young John. In the school at Volodymyr he gave evidence of unusual talent; he studied <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic" title="Church Slavonic">Church Slavonic</a> and memorized most of the <i><a href="/wiki/Horologion" title="Horologion">Horologion</a></i>, which from this period he began to read daily. From this source he drew his early <a href="/wiki/Religious_education" title="Religious education">religious education</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;h&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Owing to his parents' poverty, Kuntsevych was <a href="/wiki/Apprenticed" class="mw-redirect" title="Apprenticed">apprenticed</a> to a merchant named Papovič in Vilnius. In Vilnius, divided through the contentions of the various religious <a href="/wiki/Sect" title="Sect">sects</a>, he became acquainted with men such as <a href="/wiki/Josyf_Veliamyn_Rutsky" class="mw-redirect" title="Josyf Veliamyn Rutsky">Josyf Veliamyn Rutsky</a>, a former <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinist</a> who converted to Catholicism and transferred from the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Western</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a>. Rutsky supported the recent union with Rome, and under his influence Kuntsevych grew interested in the Catholic Church.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monk_and_archbishop">Monk and archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Monk and archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1604, in his early 20s, Kuntsevych entered the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a> (<a href="/wiki/Church_and_monastery_of_Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Church and monastery of Holy Trinity">Church and monastery of Holy Trinity</a>) of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Basil_the_Great" title="Order of Saint Basil the Great">Order of Saint Basil the Great</a> in Vilnius, at which time he was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of Josaphat. Seeking to emulate the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Rite" title="Coptic Rite">Coptic Rite</a> <a href="/wiki/Desert_Fathers" title="Desert Fathers">Desert Fathers</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Egypt" title="Roman Egypt">Roman Egypt</a>, Brother Josaphat repeated the <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Prayer" title="Jesus Prayer">Jesus Prayer</a> so constantly that he was heard to still murmur it during his sleep. He also engaged in many acts of <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification of the flesh</a>, such as praying overnight in the parish cemetery during the winter, while barefoot and deliberately underdressed, in order to offer up his suffering towards the salvation of souls.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> According the Beatification testimony of his fellow monks, Brother Josaphat regularly prayed while disciplining himself, "Lord, God, grant unity to the Holy Church and the conversion of the Dissidents."<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stories that the young monk was a Greek Catholic <i><a href="/wiki/Starets" title="Starets">Starets</a></i> spread rapidly and many distinguished people began to visit the Monastery to speak with him for <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">Spiritual direction</a>. Many other young men joined the Monastery, which only recently had been on the verge of closure, due to Brother Josaphat's reputation for holiness.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Brother Josaphat's believed, based on his own studies of history, the liturgical books, and many other sources, that the <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a> represented a return to the true roots and origins of the Christian East. He was so successful at arguing in favor of this point and persuading both Orthodox Christians and Ruthenian and Lithuanian <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinists</a> to convert to Eastern Catholicism, that his theological opponents dubbed him, "The soul-snatcher".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his beatification process, Dorotheus Akhrymovych, a Polotsk city councilman who had known Josaphat in Vilna, testified, "Though the parents of the young boys complained publicly and privately about him, calling him the 'Soul-snatcher' of their sons, later, after their sons returned home from their studies, they were happy with the results and thanked the holy man."<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Father Gennadius Khmelnytsky later testified, "Because of the numerous souls he was converting to God and the numerous boys he was drawing into the religious life from among the townspeople and the <a href="/wiki/Ruthenian_nobility" title="Ruthenian nobility">nobility</a>, the Dissidents called him 'Soul-snatcher', instead of his own name Josaphat. They painted a <a href="/wiki/Icon_(art)" class="mw-redirect" title="Icon (art)">picture</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Last_Judgement" class="mw-redirect" title="Last Judgement">Last Judgement</a> which they placed in the vestibule of the Orthodox church, in which Josaphat was depicted as one of the devils who was dragging souls to <a href="/wiki/Hell" title="Hell">Hell</a> with a hook. Below the painting were written the words: 'Soul-snatcher.'"<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Whenever Brother Josaphat, however, was called this name, however, he would chuckle and respond, "God grant me the grace to snatch all your souls and lead them to Heaven."<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After a notable life as a <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">layman</a>, Rutsky also joined the Order in 1607.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Josaphat was ordained to the <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">diaconate</a>, his regular services and labor for the Church had already begun. As a result of his efforts, the number of <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novices</a> to the Order steadily increased, and under Rutsky – who had meanwhile been <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> – a revival of Eastern Catholic monastic life began among the <a href="/wiki/Ruthenians" title="Ruthenians">Ruthenians</a> (Belarusians and Ukrainians).<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> In 1609, after private study under <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> Valentin Groza Fabricy, Josaphat was ordained a priest by a Catholic bishop. He subsequently became the <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a> (<a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a>) of several monasteries. On November 12, 1617, he was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_archeparch" class="mw-redirect" title="Coadjutor archeparch">coadjutor archeparch</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Archeparchy_of_Polotsk" class="mw-redirect" title="Archeparchy of Polotsk">Archeparchy of Polotsk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;i&#93;</a></sup> He succeeded as archeparch in March 1618.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> During his episcopacy, the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral_in_Polotsk" title="Saint Sophia Cathedral in Polotsk">Saint Sophia Cathedral in Polotsk</a> was rebuilt in 1618–1620.
</p><p>Kuntsevych faced the daunting task of bringing the local populace to accept union with Rome. He faced stiff opposition from the monks, who feared <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_Latinisation" title="Liturgical Latinisation">liturgical Latinisation</a> of the Byzantine Rite. As archeparch he: restored the churches: issued a <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to the clergy, with instructions that it should be memorized; composed rules for priestly life, and entrusted deacons the task of superintending their observance; assembled synods in various towns in the dioceses; and firmly opposed the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Chancellor_of_the_Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Chancellor of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania">Grand Chancellor of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lew_Sapieha" title="Lew Sapieha">Lew Sapieha</a>, who wished to make what Josaphat saw as too many concessions to the Eastern Orthodox.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:AUDIENCE" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:AUDIENCE"><span title="An editor has requested that an example be provided. (December 2018)"><span style="padding-right:0.15em;">examples  needed</span></span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Throughout all his strivings and all his occupations, he continued his religious devotion as a monk, and never abated his desire for <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification of the flesh</a>. Through all this he was successful in winning over a large portion of the people.<sup id="cite_ref-AC_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-36">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Discontent increased among the inhabitants of the eastern voivodeships. In 1618 a Disuniate at <a href="/wiki/Mohilev" class="mw-redirect" title="Mohilev">Mohilev</a><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;j&#93;</a></sup>, Vitebsk Voivodeship, who apparently assented to the Union of Brest, openly resisted its implementation and replaced Uniate clergy with Disuniate clergy. They substituted the names of <a href="/wiki/Timothy_II_of_Constantinople" title="Timothy II of Constantinople">Timothy II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">patriarch of Constantinople</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Osman_II" title="Osman II">Osman II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sultan_of_the_Ottoman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Sultan of the Ottoman Empire">sultan of the Ottoman Empire</a>, in the liturgy for those of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a> and Sigismund III.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;k&#93;</a></sup> The resistance at Mohilev led to increased government intervention against Disuniates, and a 1619 judicial decree condemned the leaders of the insurrection to death and devolved all the previously Eastern Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Church_building" class="mw-redirect" title="Church building">church buildings</a> at Mohilev to the Eastern Catholic Archeparchy of Polotsk.<sup id="cite_ref-Krasinski1840_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Krasinski1840-38">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 190–191">&#58;&#8202;190–191&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;l&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Norman_Davies" title="Norman Davies">Norman Davies</a> wrote in <i><a href="/wiki/God%27s_Playground" title="God&#39;s Playground">God's Playground</a></i> that Kuntsevych "was no man of peace, and had been involved in all manner of oppressions, including that most offensive of petty persecutions – the refusal to allow the Orthodox peasants to bury their dead in consecrated ground;"<sup id="cite_ref-Davies1982a_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davies1982a-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 174–175">&#58;&#8202;174–175&#8202;</span></sup> in other words, he is said to have prohibited burial of Disuniates in Uniate cemeteries.<sup id="cite_ref-Wilson2011_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilson2011-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 42">&#58;&#8202;42&#8202;</span></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="What were 17th century practices for burial of schismatic Christians? Was it considered a desecration of a cemetery to inter schismatic Christians? (December 2014)">further explanation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The Disuniates did not collapse, however. In 1620 they assembled in synod at Kiev, protected by <a href="/wiki/Petro_Konashevych-Sahaidachny" title="Petro Konashevych-Sahaidachny">Petro Konashevych-Sahaidachny</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hetmans_of_Zaporizhian_Cossacks" class="mw-redirect" title="Hetmans of Zaporizhian Cossacks">hetman of Zaporizhian Cossacks</a>, and elected new Eastern Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Eastern_Orthodox_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Eastern Orthodox Church)">bishops</a> including <a href="/wiki/Meletius_Smotrytsky" title="Meletius Smotrytsky">Meletius Smotrytsky</a> as archbishop-elect of Polotsk, all of whom were consecrated "in great secrecy" at Kiev by <a href="/wiki/Theophanes_III_of_Jerusalem" title="Theophanes III of Jerusalem">Theophanes III</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Jerusalem">Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Jerusalem</a>, Neophyte, metropolitan of Sofia, and Avramios, bishop of Stagoi. Thus a rival Disuniate hierarchy was established.<sup id="cite_ref-Crummey2006_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crummey2006-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 305">&#58;&#8202;305&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-MedlinPatrinelis1971_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MedlinPatrinelis1971-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 90">&#58;&#8202;90&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Krasinski1840_38-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Krasinski1840-38">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 191">&#58;&#8202;191&#8202;</span></sup> Sigismund III accused Theophanes III of being a <a href="/wiki/Covert_agent" title="Covert agent">covert agent</a> working on behalf of the Ottoman Empire and ordered his arrest and arrest of those consecrated by him.<sup id="cite_ref-MedlinPatrinelis1971_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MedlinPatrinelis1971-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 89–90">&#58;&#8202;89–90&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>That changed in 1620, when, with <a href="/wiki/Cossack" class="mw-redirect" title="Cossack">Cossack</a> aid, a rival Eastern Orthodox hierarchy was set up by <span class="clarify-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">the Orthodox Church,</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Clarify" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="By which Orthodox Church? (November 2014)">clarify</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-Subtelny2009_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Subtelny2009-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (December 2014)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-Himka1999_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Himka1999-42">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> with Smotrytsky (who later himself entered into communion with the see of Rome)<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HDU_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HDU-44">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> being appointed the Orthodox Archeparch of Polotsk.<sup id="cite_ref-Subtelny2009_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Subtelny2009-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (December 2014)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Smotrytsky publicly claimed that Kuntsevych was preparing a <span class="clarify-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">total Latinization of the Church and its rituals.</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Clarify" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="What did Smotrytsky argue against before his conversion from Eastern Orthodox confession to Eastern Catholic confession? (November 2014)">clarify</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-AC_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-36">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability"><span title="He may have done it but it is not in this source. (December 2014)">failed verification</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>After 1620, according to Orest Subtelny, in <i>Ukraine</i>, sectarian violence over ownership of church property increased and "hundreds of clerics on both sides died in confrontations that often took the form of pitched battles."<sup id="cite_ref-Subtelny2009_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Subtelny2009-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (December 2014)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The government imposed a settlement on the "unsettling and destructive" conflict in 1632 by legalizing the Disuniate hierarchy and redistributing church property between Uniates and Disuniates.<sup id="cite_ref-Subtelny2009_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Subtelny2009-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (December 2014)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;m&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg/220px-Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="288" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg/330px-Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg/440px-Simmler_Martyrdom_of_Josaphat_Kuntsevych.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1907" data-file-height="2500" /></a><figcaption><i>Martyrdom of Josaphat Kuntsevych</i> (c. 1861) by <a href="/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Simmler" title="Józef Simmler">Józef Simmler</a>, <a href="/wiki/National_Museum,_Warsaw" title="National Museum, Warsaw">National Museum</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a></figcaption></figure><p> In October 1623 Kuntsevych ordered the arrest of the last priest who was clandestinely holding Orthodox services at Vitebsk,<sup id="cite_ref-HDU_44-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HDU-44">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> where Kuntsevych had a residence.<sup id="cite_ref-HoU_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HoU-46">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> Enraged at this, some Orthodox townspeople lynched Kuntsevych on 12 November.<sup id="cite_ref-HDU_44-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HDU-44">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Witnesses of the event described it as follows:
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The ringing of cathedral bells and the bells of other churches spread. This was the signal and call to insurrection. From all sides of town masses of people – men, women, and children – gathered with stones and attacked the archbishop's residence. The masses attacked and injured the servants and assistants of the archbishop, and broke into the room where he was alone. One hit him on the head with a stick, another split it with an axe, and when Kuntsevych fell, they started beating him. They looted his house, dragged his body to the plaza, cursed him – even women and children. ...They dragged him naked through the streets of the city all the way to the hill overlooking the river <a href="/wiki/Daugava_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Daugava River">Dvina</a>. Finally, after tying stones to the dead body, they threw him into the Dvina at its deepest.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 121">&#58;&#8202;121&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HoU_46-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HoU-46">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p><a href="/wiki/John_Szlupas" class="mw-redirect" title="John Szlupas">John Szlupas</a> wrote, in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Princeton_Theological_Review" title="The Princeton Theological Review">The Princeton Theological Review</a></i>, that the Lithuanian Protestants were also the secret instigators in the murder of Kuntsevych, and Smotrytsky, the chief agent in the murder, was in constant communication with them.<sup id="cite_ref-Szlupas1907_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Szlupas1907-49">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 263">&#58;&#8202;263&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>In January 1624, a commission presided over by Sapieha investigated Kuntsevych's murder and sentenced 93 people to death for their involvement in the <a href="/wiki/Conspiracy_(criminal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Conspiracy (criminal)">conspiracy</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Kempa2010_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kempa2010-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 57">&#58;&#8202;57&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;n&#93;</a></sup> and many were banished and their property confiscated. The townhall and the disuniate churches were destroyed, and the franchises of the city abolished, but restored under the subsequent reign.<sup id="cite_ref-Krasinski1840_38-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Krasinski1840-38">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 193–194">&#58;&#8202;193–194&#8202;</span></sup> With Kuntsevych's death the Disuniates were completely broken up in Lithuania, and their leaders were severely punished. The Disuniates lost their churches in Vitebsk, Polotsk, <a href="/wiki/Orsha" title="Orsha">Orsha</a>, Mogilev, and other places. Smotrytsky joined the Uniates in order to escape punishment, and turned his pen against the Disuniates whose weaknesses were not secrets from him.<sup id="cite_ref-Szlupas1907_49-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Szlupas1907-49">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 263">&#58;&#8202;263&#8202;</span></sup>
The body was recovered from the river and lay in state in the cathedral of Polatsk. <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatification</a> followed in 1643, but <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> did not take place until 1867, more than two centuries later.<sup id="cite_ref-Himka1999_42-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Himka1999-42">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The body is now in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> in Rome, placed under the altar of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Basil_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Basil the Great">Saint Basil the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-AC_36-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-36">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>As a boy Kuntsevych was said to have shunned the usual games of childhood, prayed much, and lost no opportunity to assist at the Church services. Children especially regarded him with affection. As an apprentice, he devoted every leisure hour to prayer and study. At first Papovič viewed this behavior with displeasure, but Josaphat gradually won such a position in his esteem that Papovič offered him his entire fortune and his daughter's hand. But Josaphat's love for the religious life never wavered.
</p><p>Kuntsevych's favourite devotional exercise was the traditional Eastern monastic practice of <a href="/wiki/Zemnoy_poklon" class="mw-redirect" title="Zemnoy poklon">prostrations</a>, in which the head touches the ground, while saying the <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Prayer" title="Jesus Prayer">Jesus Prayer</a>. Never eating meat, he <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasted</a> much, wore a <a href="/wiki/Cilice" title="Cilice">hair shirt</a> and a chain around his waist. He slept on the bare floor, and <a href="/wiki/Chastisement" title="Chastisement">chastised</a> his body until the blood flowed. The Jesuits frequently urged him to set some bounds to his <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">austerities</a>.
</p><p>From Kuntsevych's zealous study of the Church Slavonic Byzantine Rite <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_book" title="Liturgical book">liturgical books</a> he drew many proofs of Catholic doctrine and wrote several original works.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;o&#93;</a></sup> Throughout his adult life, he was distinguished by his extraordinary zeal in performing the Church services and by extraordinary devotion during the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">Divine Liturgy</a>. Not only in the church did he preach and hear <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confessions</a>, but likewise in the fields, hospitals, prisons, and even on his personal journeys. This zeal, united with his kindness for the poor, led great numbers of Eastern Orthodox confession Ruthenians to a <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">religious conversion</a> to the Eastern Catholic confession and Catholic unity. Among his converts were many important personages such as deposed <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Moscow" title="Ignatius of Moscow">Patriarch Ignatius</a>, of Moscow, and <span class="clarify-content" style="padding-left:0.1em; padding-right:0.1em; color:#595959; border:1px solid #DDD;">Manuel Kantakouzenos, who belonged to the imperial family of the Byzantine Emperor <a href="/wiki/Palaeologus" class="mw-redirect" title="Palaeologus">Palaeologus</a>.</span><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Clarify" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="Who was this? Catholic Encyclopedia used the name Emmanuel Cantacuzenus. (November 2014)">clarify</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<p>After numerous <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> attributed to Kuntsevych were reported to Church officials, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> appointed a commission, in 1628, to inquire into his possible <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>, which examined 116 witnesses under oath. Josaphat's body was claimed to be <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a> five years after his death. In 1637, a second commission investigated his life and, in 1643, Josaphat was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a>. He was canonized on June 29, 1867, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HKV_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HKV-52">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church">Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church</a> celebrates his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on the first Sunday after the <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a> <span class="date-julian">November 12</span> (<a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a> November 25). When, in 1867, Pius IX inserted his feast into the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, it was assigned to November 14, which was the first free day after November 12, which was then occupied by the feast of "<a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_I" title="Pope Martin I">Saint Martin I</a>, Pope and Martyr." In the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1969" title="General Roman Calendar of 1969">General Roman Calendar of 1969</a>, this latter feast was moved to Pope Saint Martin's <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">dies natalis</a></i></span> ('birthday to heaven'), and Saint Josaphat's feast was moved to that date, his own <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">dies natalis</i></span>.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholics">Traditionalist Catholics</a> continue to observe the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1954" title="General Roman Calendar of 1954">General Roman Calendar of 1954</a>, the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_Pope_Pius_XII" title="General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII">General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII</a>, or the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>, in which the feast day is on November 14.
</p><p>Kuntsevych's canonization process began in the interval of the <a href="/wiki/January_Uprising" title="January Uprising">January Uprising</a> of 1863–1865 against the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a> and was "understood in many circles, including Polish, Russian, and Ruthenian circles, as a papal gesture of moral support for the insurgent Poles."<sup id="cite_ref-Himka1999_42-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Himka1999-42">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 31">&#58;&#8202;31&#8202;</span></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Russophile_Ruthenian" class="mw-redirect" title="Russophile Ruthenian">Russophile Ruthenian</a> newspaper, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Slovo_(Ukrainian_newspaper)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Slovo (Ukrainian newspaper) (page does not exist)"><i>Slovo</i></a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BE_(%D1%87%D0%B0%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%BF%D0%B8%D1%81,_1861)" class="extiw" title="uk:Слово (часопис, 1861)">uk</a>&#93;</span>, published several negative articles about Kuntsevych.<sup id="cite_ref-Himka1999_42-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Himka1999-42">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Pages: 29–30">&#58;&#8202;29–30&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;p&#93;</a></sup> This antagonism to his canonization "makes sense within the context of the Russophile <a href="/wiki/Hegemony" title="Hegemony">hegemony</a> in Ruthenian public opinion" and was seen as an insult to Imperial Russia.<sup id="cite_ref-Himka1999_42-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Himka1999-42">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 31">&#58;&#8202;31&#8202;</span></sup> The Russian government responded, in 1875, with further <a href="/wiki/Russification" title="Russification">Russification</a> and forced <a href="/wiki/Conversion_of_Che%C5%82m_Eparchy" title="Conversion of Chełm Eparchy">conversion of the Eastern Catholic Chełm Eparchy</a>, the last Eastern Catholic eparchy in the Russian Empire.<sup id="cite_ref-Himka1999_42-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Himka1999-42">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 32">&#58;&#8202;32&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Society_of_St._Josaphat">Society of St. Josaphat</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Society of St. Josaphat">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>A group of Ukrainian Catholics who oppose the changes made in the Ruthenian Rite to reduce Roman influence have formed the <a href="/wiki/Priestly_Society_of_Saint_Josaphat" title="Priestly Society of Saint Josaphat">Priestly Society of Saint Josaphat</a>. They are linked to the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_St._Pius_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of St. Pius X">Society of St. Pius X</a>, which has not recognized the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Vatican II">Second Vatican Council</a>.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Relic">Relic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Relic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<ul><li>There is a relic of the saint in the "catacombs" of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Roman_Catholic_Church_(Chicago)" title="Holy Trinity Roman Catholic Church (Chicago)">Holy Trinity Roman Catholic Church</a> in Chicago.</li>
<li>There are two relics of Saint Josaphat located in the Basilica of St. Josaphat, in Milwaukee Wisconsin. One is located inside the High Altar and the other is located in the lower Chapel. Vatican documentation is presented in the lower Chapel of the authenticity of the relics.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Controversy">Controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kuntsevych&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Josaphat's canonization has been highly controversial among Ukrainian Orthodox population, mostly due to persecution of Orthodox practices incited by Josaphat. These practices include the arrest of Orthodox priests for holding <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">liturgies</a><sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> and inciting the burning of Orthodox faithful for celebrating a thanksgiving liturgy.<sup id="cite_ref-perepiolkina_59-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-perepiolkina-59">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> Such actions led the Roman Catholic chancelor <a href="/wiki/Lew_Sapieha" title="Lew Sapieha">Lew Sapieha</a> to write a letter to Josaphat in behalf of the King, condemning him for his actions and claiming that his persecution was his own fault.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
