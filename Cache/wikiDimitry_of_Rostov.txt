<p><b>Demetrius of Rostov</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Димитрий Ростовский</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Russian-language romanization"><i lang="ru-Latn">Dmitri Rostovsky</i></span>, <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Димитрій Ростовський</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian" title="Romanization of Ukrainian">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Ukrainian-language romanization"><i lang="uk-Latn">Dymytrii Rostovskyi</i></span>, secular name <b>Daniil Savvich Tuptalo</b>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Даниил Саввич Туптало</span>, or <b>Tuptalenko</b>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Тупталенко</span>, according to some sources; 11 December 1651&#160;&#8211;&#32; 28 October 1709) was a leading opponent of the <a href="/wiki/Caesaropapist" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesaropapist">Caesaropapist</a> reform of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox church">Russian Orthodox church</a> promoted by <a href="/wiki/Theophan_Prokopovich" title="Theophan Prokopovich">Theophan Prokopovich</a>. He is representative of the strong <a href="/wiki/Cossack_Baroque" class="mw-redirect" title="Cossack Baroque">Cossack Baroque</a> influence upon the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> at the turn of the 17th and 18th centuries.
</p><p>Demetrius is sometimes credited as composer or compiler of the first <a href="/wiki/Russian_opera" title="Russian opera">Russian opera</a>, the lengthy <i>Rostov Mysteries</i> of 1705, though the exact nature of this work, as well as its place in history, is open to debate.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is the author of several written works, out of which the most famous is <i>The Lives of Saints</i> (<i>Четьи-Минеи</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Demetrius_of_Rostov&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born into a <a href="/wiki/Cossack" class="mw-redirect" title="Cossack">Cossack</a> family in 1651. Soon thereafter his family moved to <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a>, and he entered the <a href="/wiki/National_University_of_Kyiv-Mohyla_Academy" title="National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy">Kievo-Mohyla Academy</a> at the age of 11. On 9 July 1668 he took his <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> at St. Cyril's Monastery in Kiev and was given the monastic name of Demetrius (after <a href="/wiki/Saint_Demetrius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Demetrius">Saint Demetrius</a> of <a href="/wiki/Thessalonika" class="mw-redirect" title="Thessalonika">Thessalonika</a>). After a brief period in <a href="/wiki/Chernigov" class="mw-redirect" title="Chernigov">Chernigov</a>, Demetrius went to venerate the Byzantine Slavic Christian <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrines</a> of <a href="/wiki/Belarus" title="Belarus">Belarus</a> (at the time property of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Byzantine Rite Belarusian and Ukrainian Catholic metropolitans of the Uniate churches</a>), still located in the <a href="/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth" title="Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth">Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a> at that time. In 1678 he returned from <a href="/wiki/Vilno" class="mw-redirect" title="Vilno">Vilno</a> to <a href="/wiki/Baturyn" title="Baturyn">Baturyn</a> and settled at the court of the <a href="/wiki/Hetman" title="Hetman">hetman</a> <a href="/wiki/Ivan_Samoylovych" title="Ivan Samoylovych">Ivan Samoylovych</a>.
</p><p>During the 1680s, Demetrius lived mostly at the <a href="/wiki/Kiev_Pechersk_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev Pechersk Lavra">Kiev Pechersk Lavra</a>, while his <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a> against hard drinking and lax morals made his name known all over <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>. He was appointed <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a> (<a href="/wiki/Superior_(hierarchy)" title="Superior (hierarchy)">superior</a>) of several major <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate" title="Cossack Hetmanate">Ukraine</a>, but concentrated his attention upon the ambitious project of integrating all the lives of Russian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> into a single work, which he published as <i>Monthly Readings</i> (Четьи-минеи) or <i><a href="/wiki/Menologion" class="mw-redirect" title="Menologion">Menologion</a></i> in 1684-1705. He also found time to study ecclesiastical history of the Russian Orthodox Church.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:277px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg/275px-Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg" decoding="async" width="275" height="206" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg/413px-Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg/550px-Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1200" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sobor_and_Monument_Dmitry_Rostovsky_Rostov_on_Don.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Demetrius of Rostov in front of the <a href="/wiki/Rostov-on-Don" title="Rostov-on-Don">Rostov-on-Don</a> cathedral.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1701 Demetrius was appointed <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> of <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberia</a> but, pleading ill health, preferred to stay in <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a> until he was invested with the archbishopric of <a href="/wiki/Rostov" title="Rostov">Rostov</a>. During his life in Russia, Demetrius opposed both the <a href="/wiki/Old_Believer" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Believer">Old Believers</a>' and <a href="/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter I of Russia">Peter the Great</a>'s ecclesiastical policies, gradually drifting towards the party of <a href="/wiki/Eudoxia_Lopukhina" title="Eudoxia Lopukhina">Eudoxia Lopukhina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tsarevich_Alexei_Petrovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Tsarevich Alexei Petrovich of Russia">Tsarevich Alexis</a>. Shortly before his death he forged <a href="/wiki/The_synodic_act_on_the_heretic_of_Armenia,_the_monk_Martin" class="mw-redirect" title="The synodic act on the heretic of Armenia, the monk Martin">a document</a> to undermine the Old Believers by portraying them as adherents of heresy. He also made invaluable contributions to the Russian education, opening a school and a small theatre in Rostov, where his own plays could be staged.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Work_as_composer">Work as composer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Demetrius_of_Rostov&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Work as composer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Demetrius was also active as a composer, although his musical education is undocumented aside from the standard music curriculum established by Feofan Prokopovich at the Kyiv-Mohyla Academy. Many of his <i>Penitential Psalms</i> achieved wide circulation, not only in the Ukraine but in the Balkans too, and many have become an integral part of Ukrainian folk-song tradition through the <a href="/wiki/Kobzar" title="Kobzar">kobzari</a>, itinerant blind singers.
</p><p>Demetrius is credited as composer or compiler of the first Russian opera, the six-hour-long <i>Rostov Mysteries</i> of 1705. Though this has been staged, notably by <a href="/wiki/Boris_Pokrovsky" title="Boris Pokrovsky">Boris Pokrovsky</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Moscow_Chamber_Musical_Theatre" title="Moscow Chamber Musical Theatre">Moscow Chamber Musical Theatre</a>, in Moscow and at the Brighton Festival (1993), it may best be judged an oratorio on the lives of Russian saints. Its basis is the "Cheti-Minei" (Четьи-Минеи), published in four volumes in 1689, 1690, 1700 and 1705<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> — the same source that inspired <a href="/wiki/Pushkin" class="mw-redirect" title="Pushkin">Pushkin</a> in 1825 to write <i>Boris Godunov</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Demetrius_of_Rostov&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon Demetrius' death, on 28 October 1709, his relics were placed at St. Jacob's Monastery, which his followers would rebuild as Demetrius' shrine. A fortress on the <a href="/wiki/Don_River,_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Don River, Russia">Don River</a> was named after him; today it is known as <a href="/wiki/Rostov-on-the-Don" class="mw-redirect" title="Rostov-on-the-Don">Rostov-on-the-Don</a>.
</p>
