<p><b>Varvara Alexeyevna Yakovleva</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Варвара Алексеевна Яковлева</span>; c. 1880 – July 18, 1918), called <b>Nun Barbara</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Инокиня Варвара</span>), was a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> in the convent of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Elizabeth_Fyodorovna" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duchess Elizabeth Fyodorovna">Grand Duchess Elizabeth Fyodorovna</a>. She was killed by the <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks">Bolsheviks</a> along with the grand duchess and <a href="/wiki/Prince_Ioann_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Ioann Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Ioann Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_Konstantin_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_Igor_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Igor Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Igor Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Sergei_Mikhailovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Sergei Mikhailovich of Russia">Grand Duke Sergei Mikhailovich of Russia</a>, Fyodor Remez, Grand Duke Sergei's secretary, and <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Pavlovich_Paley" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir Pavlovich Paley">Prince Vladimir Pavlovich Paley</a> at <a href="/wiki/Alapaevsk" class="mw-redirect" title="Alapaevsk">Alapaevsk</a>.
</p><p>She was later canonized as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by both the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> within Russia.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Barbara_(Yakovleva)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is very little reliable information about her life before entering the <a href="/wiki/Martha_and_Mary_Convent" class="mw-redirect" title="Martha and Mary Convent">Martha and Mary Convent</a>. According to documentary evidence, she came from <a href="/wiki/Tver" title="Tver">Tver</a>. She arrived at the Convent from <a href="/wiki/Yalta" title="Yalta">Yalta</a> on August 20, 1910. In 1911, she was 31 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-sedmitza_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sedmitza-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Varvara Alexeyevna Yakovleva, small in stature and deeply pious, served as Grand Duchess Elizabeth's maid before taking the veil.<sup id="cite_ref-Mager_1998,_p._331_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mager_1998,_p._331-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her nickname was Varya.<sup id="cite_ref-Mager_1998,_p._244_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mager_1998,_p._244-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Grand Duchess and other women also took vows on that date. As sisters of Grand Duchess Elizabeth's convent, the women were well known throughout Moscow for performing acts of charity. They took food to the homes of the poor, set up a home for women suffering from <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>, established a hospital to care for the sick, as well as establishing homes for the physically disabled, pregnant women and the elderly. They also established an orphanage. Their charitable efforts later spread to other cities in Russia.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Exile_and_death">Exile and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Barbara_(Yakovleva)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Exile and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Yakovleva voluntarily accompanied Grand Duchess Elizabeth when she was arrested following the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Revolution of 1917">Russian Revolution of 1917</a> and sent into exile.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The group was confined at Yekaterinburg and later at Alapaevsk. On the afternoon of July 18, 1918 they were herded into the woods outside Alapaevsk at gunpoint, clubbed on the back of the head, and thrown one by one into a mineshaft in the woods.<sup id="cite_ref-Mager_1998,_p._331_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mager_1998,_p._331-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> All but Grand Duke Sergei Mikhailovich, who had been shot in the head, survived the fall. They could be heard singing hymns from the bottom of the shaft. One by one they lost consciousness and died.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Barbara_(Yakovleva)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG/170px-Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="257" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG/255px-Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG/340px-Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2285" data-file-height="3457" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Church_of_Mary_Magdalene_IMG_3539.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of St Barbara (Yakovleva) at the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Mary_Magdalene" title="Church of Mary Magdalene">Church of Mary Magdalene</a>, Jerusalem</div></div></div>
<p>In 1981, Yakovleva was canonized as a "new martyr" by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Abroad" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Abroad">Russian Orthodox Church Abroad</a> as a victim of Soviet oppression along with the other members of the group. She was in April 1992 also canonized as a martyr by the Russian Orthodox Church inside Russia. In May 1982, the bodies of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Elizabeth_Feodorovna_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duchess Elizabeth Feodorovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Elizabeth Feodorovna of Russia</a> and Barbara (Varvara) were moved from the crypt of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Mary_Magdalene" title="Church of Mary Magdalene">Church of Mary Magdalene</a>, Gethsemane, where only private veneration was possible, to the upper church of St. Mary Magdalene.
</p>
