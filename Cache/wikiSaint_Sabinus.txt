<p><b>Saint Sabinus of Spoleto</b> (died c. 303) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> in the early <a href="/wiki/Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian">Christian</a> church who resisted the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Traditional_narrative">Traditional narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabinus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Traditional narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Venustian, governor of <a href="/wiki/Etruria" title="Etruria">Etruria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, had Sabinus and his <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacons</a> arrested in <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>. Diocletian's order required all Christians to sacrifice to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_mythology" title="Roman mythology">Roman gods</a> or be put to death; their estates were then to be seized for the state. 
</p><p>Venustian mocked Sabinus's faith, accusing him of leading the people to the worship of a dead man. When Sabinus said that <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> rose on the third day, Venustian invited him to do the same thing. The deacons were in great fear, but Sabinus encouraged them to hold to their faith, and they died after being torn apart by iron hooks. Venustian had Sabinus's hands cut off. 
</p><p>In prison after the martyrdom of his deacons, he was tended by a woman named Serena.  While in prison, he healed a man born blind. Venustian heard of the cure and sought a cure for his own eyes from Sabinus. Sabinus healed the governor and converted him to Christianity.  Venustian then sheltered Sabinus. <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximianus Herculius</a>, hearing of this, ordered the <a href="/wiki/Tribune" title="Tribune">tribune</a> Lucius to address the matter. Lucius had Venustian, his wife, and his two sons beheaded at Assisi, and he had Sabinus beaten to death at <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabinus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sabinus's feast day is 30 December in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and in the <i>Roman Martyrology</i> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is depicted in the <i><a href="/wiki/Maest%C3%A0_(Duccio)" title="Maestà (Duccio)">Maestà</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Duccio" title="Duccio">Duccio</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_saints_of_the_name">Other saints of the name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabinus_of_Spoleto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Other saints of the name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> lists six saints called Sabinus, three of whom were bishops. The other two are <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabinus_of_Canossa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sabinus of Canossa (page does not exist)">Sabinus of Canossa</a> (died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;566</span>; feast day, 9 February) and Sabinus of Piacenza (died late 4th century; feast day, 11 December).
</p>
