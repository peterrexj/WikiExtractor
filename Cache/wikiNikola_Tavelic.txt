<p><b>Nicholas Tavelic</b>, OFM (<a href="/wiki/Croatian_language" title="Croatian language">Croatian</a>: <i lang="hr">Nikola Tavelić</i>) was a Croatian <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> who died a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>'s death in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> on November 14, 1391. He was beatified with his companions, who included friars from <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> and <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. All four members of his group have been declared <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, making Tavelic the first Croatian saint.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Tavelic&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg/250px-Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg/375px-Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg/500px-Arheolo%C5%A1ka_zona_Bribirska_glavica_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a><figcaption>Ruins of church and Franciscan monastery of St. Mary in <a href="/wiki/Bribir,_%C5%A0ibenik-Knin_County" title="Bribir, Šibenik-Knin County">Bribir</a>, where Tavelic became a monk.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Most sources mention <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0ibenik" title="Šibenik">Šibenik</a> as Tavelic's birthplace, but another possible location is Velim near <a href="/wiki/Stankovci" title="Stankovci">Stankovci</a>. In 1365, Tavelic became a friar in <a href="/wiki/Bribir,_%C5%A0ibenik-Knin_County" title="Bribir, Šibenik-Knin County">Bribir</a>, the seat of the <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0ubi%C4%87" class="mw-redirect" title="Šubić">Šubić</a>, a powerful Croatian noble family.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tavelic was among 60 friars from various Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">provinces</a> who answered an appeal by the <a href="/wiki/Bosnia_(region)" title="Bosnia (region)">Bosnian</a> <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">guardian</a>, motivated by a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a>, <i>Prae cunctis</i>, issued in 1291 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV" title="Pope Nicholas IV">Pope Nicholas IV</a>, himself a Friar Minor, to work as missionaries in Bosnia, combating the perceived <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresies</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Bosnian_Church" title="Bosnian Church">Bosnian Church</a>. Tavelic spread Catholicism around Bosnia for 12 years. In his report to the pope, the Bosnian guardian later said that the missionaries converted around 50,000 members of that Church.
</p><p>In 1384, Tavelic went to serve in the <a href="/wiki/Custody_of_the_Holy_Land" title="Custody of the Holy Land">Custody of the Holy Land</a> where he met the friars <a href="/w/index.php?title=Deodatus_Aribert&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Deodatus Aribert (page does not exist)">Deodatus Aribert</a> of <a href="/wiki/Rodez" title="Rodez">Rodez</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Narbonne_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter of Narbonne (saint) (page does not exist)">Peter of Narbonne</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrus_von_Narbonne" class="extiw" title="de:Petrus von Narbonne">de</a>&#93;</span> and <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_da_Cuneo" class="extiw" title="it:Stefano da Cuneo">Stephen</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cuneo" title="Cuneo">Cuneo</a>. The four lived at the Monastery of Mount Zion, the ancient friary maintained by the Friars Minor in the city, where they spent several years learning Arabic and serving at the holy sites connected to Jesus' life, which had been entrusted to the care of the Order of Friars Minor and which still drew pilgrims from Christian Europe.
</p><p>After having seen few, if any, conversions from the Muslim populace of the city resulting from their quiet pastoral work at the holy sites of Christianity, Tavelic and his colleagues decided to take the option given to them in the Rule of Life of their founder, <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, and to preach openly the Christian faith to the Muslim populace. They went to the regular gathering before the <a href="/wiki/Qadi" title="Qadi">Qadi</a> of Jerusalem and began to preach.  Following their arrest for this, they refused the option to convert to <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a> and were imprisoned. After again refusing to convert several days later, the group were all sentenced to death. These missionaries were executed near the <a href="/wiki/Jaffa_Gate" title="Jaffa Gate">Jaffa Gate</a> on November 14, 1391, and their remains completely burned.<sup id="cite_ref-TBC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TBC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Tavelic&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Zion_Franciscan_Monastery1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/Zion_Franciscan_Monastery1.jpg/250px-Zion_Franciscan_Monastery1.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="333" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Zion_Franciscan_Monastery1.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="300" data-file-height="399" /></a><figcaption>The courtyard of the former <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mount_Zion_Monastery&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Mount Zion Monastery (page does not exist)">Mount Zion Monastery</a> of the Franciscan friars in Jerusalem, where St. Nikola lived.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Friar Gerard Chalvet, O.F.M., was the guardian of the Jerusalem friary and saw their executions. Together with Friar Martin of <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0ibenik" title="Šibenik">Šibenik</a>, he sent a detailed report to Europe: the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, <a href="/wiki/Leipzig" title="Leipzig">Leipzig</a>, Šibenik and elsewhere. Tavelic and his companions were soon celebrated as  martyrs by Franciscans all over Europe, especially in Šibenik.
</p><p>Five centuries later, in 1880, Antun Josip Fosco, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">Bishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_%C5%A0ibenik" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Šibenik">Roman Catholic Diocese of Šibenik</a>, started the procedure for the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> for the formal <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> of Tavelic. By a special decree, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> recognized his ancient <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a></i> for the Šibenik Diocese in 1889 and for the entire Franciscan Order in 1898. Two years later, the same thing was done for the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.
</p><p>The cult of the friars, especially that of Tavelic, grew between the World Wars. The <i>cultus</i> of the other three martyrs was recognized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1966, with a separate feast day of 17 November. The four friars were <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> together by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in front of 20,000 <a href="/wiki/Croats" title="Croats">Croats</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>, on June 21, 1970, with their conjoined feast day being 14 November.<sup id="cite_ref-TBC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TBC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Churches">Churches</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Tavelic&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Churches">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg/250px-Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="333" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg/375px-Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg/500px-Nikola_Taveli%C4%87_side-altar.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1728" data-file-height="2304" /></a><figcaption>Side-altar dedicated to Saint Nicholas Tavelic in the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> Church of <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0ibenik" title="Šibenik">Šibenik</a>, Croatia.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Most churches dedicated to Tavelić are located in Croatia. Some of them are in <a href="/w/index.php?title=Banjevci&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Banjevci (page does not exist)">Banjevci</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cerovac,_Po%C5%BEega-Slavonia_County" title="Cerovac, Požega-Slavonia County">Cerovac</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Li%C5%A1ani&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Lišani (page does not exist)">Lišani</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perkovi%C4%87,_Croatia" title="Perković, Croatia">Perković</a>, <a href="/wiki/Split_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Split (city)">Split</a>, <a href="/wiki/Donji_Vinjani" title="Donji Vinjani">Vinjani</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zagreb" title="Zagreb">Zagreb</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rijeka" title="Rijeka">Rijeka</a> and <a href="/wiki/%C5%BDupanja" title="Županja">Županja</a>. Churches abroad can be found in <a href="/wiki/Hurlingham,_Buenos_Aires" title="Hurlingham, Buenos Aires">Hurlingham, Buenos Aires</a> in Argentina, <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winnipeg" title="Winnipeg">Winnipeg</a> in Canada, <a href="/wiki/Melbourne" title="Melbourne">Melbourne</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney</a> in Australia and <a href="/wiki/Tomislavgrad" title="Tomislavgrad">Tomislavgrad</a> in Bosnia-Herzegovina
</p>
