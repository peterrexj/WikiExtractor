<p><b>Julius of Novara</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it"><b>Giulio di Orta</b></i>), also <b>Julius of Aegina</b><sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (died 401 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> priest to <a href="/wiki/Northern_Italy" title="Northern Italy">northern Italy</a>.
</p><p>His cult is centred at <a href="/wiki/Lake_Orta" title="Lake Orta">Lake Orta</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Novara" title="Province of Novara">Novarese</a> highlands, and in particular on the island which has been named for him since at least the eighth century,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Isola_San_Giulio" class="mw-redirect" title="Isola San Giulio">Isola San Giulio</a>, and where his presumed <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> are preserved in the crypt, called <i>scurolo</i>, of a basilica dedicated to him.
</p><p>Few facts are known about his career. In the earliest <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a>, which dates from no earlier than the eighth century and is of a character as much legendary as historical, the account of his life is interlaced with that of his brother <b>Julian (Giuliano)</b>, a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> whose name is similar enough to suggest that they may have been the same person, but now we know (thanks to recent archaeological finds in Gozzano's previous parish church, S. Lorenzo) that they both existed.  The <i>Roman <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Martyrology</a></i> commemorates only Julius.  It has been said that Julius' name was recited as part of the <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite" title="Ambrosian Rite">Ambrosian Rite</a> during the fifth and sixth centuries; however, it has also been claimed that this Julius referred to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_I" title="Pope Julius I">Pope Julius I</a>.
</p><p>Julius and Julian may have been <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greeks</a> who came to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> before establishing themselves at Lake Orta.  Their legend states that they were educated in the Christian faith by their parents.  They are said to have been ordered by <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_I" title="Theodosius I">Theodosius I</a> to destroy <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altars</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sacred_grove" title="Sacred grove">sacred woods</a> and to build Christian churches.  They built one hundred churches, according to their tradition.  The ninety-ninth church is said to have been built at <a href="/wiki/Gozzano,_Piedmont" title="Gozzano, Piedmont">Gozzano</a>, and dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lawrence" title="Saint Lawrence">Saint Lawrence</a>.  Julian was buried there.  The hundredth church was built by Julius on the island that bears his name; he dedicated it to Saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul</a>.
</p>
