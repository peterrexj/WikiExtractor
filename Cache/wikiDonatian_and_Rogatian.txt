<p><b>Donatian and Rogatian</b> were two brothers, <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nantes" title="Nantes">Nantes</a> during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>, around 288–290, for refusing to <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">deny their faith</a>. They are also known as <i>les enfants nantais</i>. Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 24 May.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Donatian_and_Rogatian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg/220px-Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="391" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg/330px-Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg/440px-Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1080" data-file-height="1920" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Nantes_Cathedral_chapel_of_St._Donatian_ad_St._Rogatian.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Chapel of St. Donatian and St. Rogatian, <a href="/wiki/Nantes_Cathedral" title="Nantes Cathedral">Cathédrale de Nantes</a>.</div></div></div><p>The story of St. Donatian and his brother St. <a href="/wiki/Rogatian" class="mw-redirect" title="Rogatian">Rogatian</a> is known from a 5th-century document, "the Passion of Children of Nantes". This is the basis for all later works, which also added some extra information to the legend,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> for example the tradition that they were related to a "illustrious" <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armorican</a> family.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Donatian and St. Rogatian were, it seems, the sons of the first <a href="/wiki/Magistrate" title="Magistrate">magistrate</a> of the city. Donatian, the youngest, was baptized (probably by St. <a href="/wiki/Similien_of_Nantes" title="Similien of Nantes">Similien</a>, third <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Nantes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Nantes">bishop of Nantes</a>, who outlived them). Donatian then evangelized his older brother, Rogatian. The family property, a <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Villa" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Villa">villa</a>, also home to the first Christian church built in Nantes, stood on the site of the current <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Donatien-et-Saint-Rogatien" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Donatien-et-Saint-Rogatien">Basilica of St. Donatien-et-Saint-Rogatien</a>. According to tradition, the church was built at the location of their graves, which was in their home, as was customary.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the story, after being denounced as Christians, they were arrested and appeared before the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">imperial</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Prefect" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Prefect">prefect</a>, the provincial governor, who asked them to sacrifice to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Pantheon" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Pantheon">idols</a>. When they refused, they were tortured and spent their last night praying together. That night, Rogatian regretted that he was going to die without being <a href="/wiki/Baptize" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptize">baptized</a>, but his brother reassured him, telling him that the blood of his <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a> would take the place of baptism.
</p><p>They were pierced by the spear of a <a href="/wiki/Lictor" title="Lictor">lictor</a> and then beheaded on the morning of 24 May 304. According to tradition,<sup id="cite_ref-Berranger_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Berranger-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> their martyrdom took place outside the city walls,<sup id="cite_ref-Berranger_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Berranger-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> at the site of the current no. 63 rue Dufour on the old road from <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, near the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eugene-Livet_High_School&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Eugene-Livet High School (page does not exist)">Eugene-Livet High School</a> and not far from the basilica dedicated to them.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Donatian_and_Rogatian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>They are commemorated throughout the <a href="/wiki/Loire_Valley" title="Loire Valley">Loire Valley</a>, as far as <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>, where their relics were displaced at the time of the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> invasions, and then deposited in the ninth or tenth century in their namesake basilica in a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gold" title="Gold">gold</a>. These relics were scattered during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, a wooden shrine<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> replacing the previous reliquary.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Both have statues on either side of the main portal in the <a href="/wiki/Narthex" title="Narthex">narthex</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Nantes_Cathedral" title="Nantes Cathedral">Cathedral Saint-Pierre and Saint-Paul de Nantes</a>. There is also a <a href="/wiki/Painting" title="Painting">painting</a> in the cathedral by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9ophile_Vauchelet&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Théophile Vauchelet (page does not exist)">Théophile Vauchelet</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q2126562#sitelinks-wikipedia" class="extiw" title="d:Special:EntityPage/Q2126562"><span title="&quot;Théophile Vauchelet&quot; in other languages">Wikidata</span></a>&#93;</span>. A church in Orléans is also named after St. Donatian.
</p>
