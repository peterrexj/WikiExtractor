<p><b>Оmelyan Hryhorovych Kovch</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Омелян Григорович Ковч</span>; August 20, 1884, <a href="/wiki/Kosmach" title="Kosmach">Kosmach</a> — March 25, 1944) was a <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek-Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek-Catholic">Ukrainian Greek-Catholic</a> priest murdered in <a href="/wiki/Majdanek_concentration_camp" title="Majdanek concentration camp">Majdanek concentration camp</a>.
</p><p>He was born in a peasant family in the town of Tlumach in the <a href="/wiki/Kosiv" title="Kosiv">Kosiv</a> region of Western <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>, and was ordained in 1911 by <a href="/wiki/Hryhory_Khomyshyn" class="mw-redirect" title="Hryhory Khomyshyn">Hryhorij Chomyszyn</a> after graduating from the <a href="/wiki/Santi_Sergio_e_Bacco" title="Santi Sergio e Bacco">Sts. Sergius and Bacchus College</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1919, he was a field chaplain for the Ukrainian Galician Army.<sup id="cite_ref-:022_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:022-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He served as a parish priest from 1921 to 1943 at the church of St. Nicholas in the village of <a href="/wiki/Peremyshliany" title="Peremyshliany">Peremyshliany</a>. Prior to his imprisonment, Fr. Kovch conducted his priestly ministry in Przemysl, while attending to his parishioners' social and cultural life.<sup id="cite_ref-:022_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:022-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He fathered six children, and devoted himself to helping the poor and orphans.<sup id="cite_ref-:022_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:022-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the spring of 1943 he was arrested by the <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a> for harboring <a href="/wiki/Jews" title="Jews">Jews</a>, specifically for providing Jews with more than 600 baptismal certificates (alternative date from another source: He was arrested by the Gestapo on December 20, 1942<sup id="cite_ref-:022_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:022-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>). On March 25, 1944 he died in the infirmary of Majdanek concentration camp near <a href="/wiki/Lublin" title="Lublin">Lublin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>.
</p><p>On September 9, 1999, the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jewish_Council_of_Ukraine&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jewish Council of Ukraine (page does not exist)">Jewish Council of Ukraine</a> awarded him the title of "Ukraine's righteous."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> took place on June 27, 2001 in <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>, during the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine rite">Byzantine rite</a> liturgy conducted by Pope <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quote">Quote</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Omelyan_Kovch&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Quote">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In a letter, Fr. Kovch wrote: "I understand that you are trying to free me. But I am asking you not to do anything. Yesterday they killed 50 people here. If I were not here, who would help them to endure these sufferings? I thank God for his kindness to me. Apart from Heaven, this is the only place I'd like to be. Here we are all equal: Poles, Jews, Ukrainians, Russians, Latvians, and Estonians. I am the only priest here. I couldn't even imagine what would happen here without me. Here I see God, Who is the same for everybody, regardless of the religious distinctions which exist among us. Maybe our Churches are different, but they are ruled by the same all-powerful God. When I'm celebrating the holy Mass, everyone prays... Don't worry and don't despair about my fate. Instead of this, rejoice with me. Pray for those who created this concentration camp and this system. They are the only ones who need prayers... May God have Mercy on them.".<sup id="cite_ref-:022_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:022-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
