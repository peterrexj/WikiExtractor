<p>The <b>Forty Martyrs of Sebaste</b> or the <b>Holy Forty</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Ancient</a>/<a href="/wiki/Katharevousa" title="Katharevousa">Katharevousa</a> <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> <i>Ἅγιοι Τεσσεράκοντα</i>; <a href="/wiki/Demotic_Greek" title="Demotic Greek">Demotic</a>: <i>Άγιοι Σαράντα</i>) were a group of Roman soldiers in the <a href="/wiki/Legio_XII_Fulminata" title="Legio XII Fulminata">Legio XII <i>Fulminata</i></a> (Armed with Lightning) whose <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a> in 320 for the Christian faith is recounted in traditional martyrologies.
</p><p>They were killed near the city of Sebaste, in <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Lesser Armenia</a> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Sivas" title="Sivas">Sivas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>), victims of the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Licinius" title="Licinius">Licinius</a> who, after 316, persecuted the Christians of the East. The earliest account of their existence and martyrdom is given by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a> (370–379) in a homily he delivered on their feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The Feast of the Forty Martyrs is thus older than Basil himself, who eulogised them only fifty or sixty years after their deaths.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fortymartyrs.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Fortymartyrs.jpg/220px-Fortymartyrs.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="301" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Fortymartyrs.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="285" data-file-height="390" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fortymartyrs.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A miniature from the <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> <a href="/wiki/Gospel_Book" title="Gospel Book">Gospel Lectionary</a>, created c. 1220 near <a href="/wiki/Mosul" title="Mosul">Mosul</a> and exhibiting a strong Muslim-Mongol influence.</div></div></div>
<p>According to Basil, forty soldiers who had openly confessed themselves Christians were condemned by the prefect to be exposed naked upon a frozen <a href="/wiki/Pond" title="Pond">pond</a> near Sebaste on a bitterly cold night, that they might freeze to death. Among the confessors, one yielded and, leaving his companions, sought the warm baths near the lake which had been prepared for any who might prove inconstant.  Upon immersion into the cauldron, the one who yielded went into shock and immediately died. One of the guards, Aglaius, was set to keep watch over the martyrs and beheld at this moment a supernatural brilliancy overshadowing them. He at once proclaimed himself a Christian, threw off his garments, and joined the remaining thirty-nine.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Thus the number of forty remained complete. At daybreak, the stiffened bodies of the confessors, which still showed signs of life, were burned and the ashes cast into a river. Christians, however, collected the precious remains, and the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were distributed throughout many cities; in this way, veneration of the Forty Martyrs became widespread, and numerous churches were erected in their honour.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early">Early</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A church was built at <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Caesarea</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>, and it was in this church that Basil publicly delivered his homily. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa" title="Gregory of Nyssa">Gregory of Nyssa</a> was especially devoted to the Forty Martyrs; two discourses in praise of them, preached by him in the church dedicated to them, are still preserved<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and upon the death of his parents, he laid them to rest beside the relics of the confessors. <a href="/wiki/Ephrem_the_Syrian" title="Ephrem the Syrian">Ephrem the Syrian</a> has also eulogized the Forty Martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sozomen" title="Sozomen">Sozomen</a>, who was an eye-witness, has left an interesting account of the finding of the relics in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, in the shrine of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thyrsus" title="Saint Thyrsus">Saint Thyrsus</a> built by <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_(consul_397)" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarius (consul 397)">Caesarius</a>, through the instrumentality of Empress <a href="/wiki/Pulcheria" title="Pulcheria">Pulcheria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_East">In the East</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In the East">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG/220px-Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG/330px-Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6d/Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG/440px-Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2736" data-file-height="3648" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Chapel_of_the_Forty_Martyrs_IMG_0507.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Chapel of the Forty Martyrs in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Church of the Holy Sepulchre">Church of the Holy Sepulchre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>The cult of the Forty Martyrs is widespread all over the East. The <a href="/wiki/Forty_Saints_Monastery,_Sarand%C3%AB" class="mw-redirect" title="Forty Saints Monastery, Sarandë">Forty Saints Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sarand%C3%AB" title="Sarandë">Sarandë</a>, modern day <a href="/wiki/Albania" title="Albania">Albania</a>, which gave its name in Greek to the city itself (Άγιοι Σαράντα, <i>Hagioi Saranda</i>), was built in the 6th century AD, and is thought to have been an important pilgrimage site.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The Churches of <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Sophia,_Ohrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Sophia, Ohrid">St. Sophia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a> (modern-day <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a> (<a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>) contain their depictions, datable to the 11th and 12th centuries, respectively. A number of auxiliary chapels were dedicated to the Forty, and there are several instances when an entire <a href="/wiki/Temple" title="Temple">temple</a> (church building) is dedicated to them: for example <a href="/wiki/Xeropotamou_Monastery" title="Xeropotamou Monastery">Xeropotamou Monastery</a> on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> and the 13th-century <a href="/wiki/Holy_Forty_Martyrs_Church,_Veliko_Tarnovo" title="Holy Forty Martyrs Church, Veliko Tarnovo">Holy Forty Martyrs Church</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Veliko_Tarnovo" title="Veliko Tarnovo">Veliko Tarnovo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>. <a href="/wiki/Anna_Komnene" title="Anna Komnene">Anna Komnene</a> speaks of a Church of the 40 saints located in Constantinople, in the <i><a href="/wiki/Alexiad" title="Alexiad">Alexiad</a></i>. In 2013 the Feast of the Holy Forty Martyrs in <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0tip" title="Štip">Štip</a> was inscribed in <a href="/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists" title="UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists">UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists</a> of <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_Cathedral" title="Forty Martyrs Cathedral">Armenian Cathedral of Aleppo</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_Cathedral,_Homs" title="Forty Martyrs Cathedral, Homs">Greek Orthodox Cathedral of Homs</a> are dedicated to the Forty Martyrs.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of the Forty Martyrs falls on March 9. In Orthodox Churches that follow the <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a>, this day is intentionally placed so that it will fall during <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a>. There is an intentional play on the number forty being both the number of martyrs and the days in the fast. Their feast also falls during Great Lent so that the endurance of the martyrs will serve as an example to the faithful to persevere to the end (i.e., throughout the forty days of the fast) in order to attain heavenly reward (participation in <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Pascha</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus" title="Resurrection of Jesus">Resurrection of Jesus</a>).
</p><p>There is a pious custom of baking “skylarks” (pastries shaped like skylarks) on this day, because people believed that birds sing at this time to announce the arrival of spring.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A prayer mentioning the Forty Holy Martyrs of Sebaste is also placed in the Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Wedding" title="Wedding">Wedding</a> Service (referred to as a "crowning") to remind the bride and groom that spiritual crowns await them in Heaven also if they remain as faithful to Christ as these saints of long ago.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Orthodox_art">In Orthodox art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: In Orthodox art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg/220px-Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="304" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg/330px-Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg/440px-Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1700" data-file-height="2346" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Relieftafel_40_M%C3%A4rtyrer_von_Sebaste_Bodemuseum.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ivory relief <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> from <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, 10th century (<a href="/wiki/Bode_Museum" title="Bode Museum">Bode Museum</a>, <a href="/wiki/Berlin" title="Berlin">Berlin</a>).</div></div></div>
<p>Byzantine artists were fascinated with the subject that allowed them to graphically show human despair. The Martyrs were typically represented at the point when they were about to freeze to death, "shivering from the cold, hugging themselves for warmth, or clasping hands to their faces or wrists in pain and despair".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> This is particularly evident in the large 10th-century ivory plaque from the <a href="/wiki/Bode_Museum" title="Bode Museum">Bode Museum</a> and the Palaiologan portable mosaic set in wax, from <a href="/wiki/Dumbarton_Oaks" title="Dumbarton Oaks">Dumbarton Oaks</a>.
</p><p>The subject remains popular among Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Iconographer" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconographer">iconographers</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_West">In the West</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In the West">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Special devotion to the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste was introduced at an early date into the West. Bishop <a href="/wiki/Gaudentius_of_Brescia" title="Gaudentius of Brescia">Gaudentius of Brescia</a> (d. about 410 or 427) received particles of the ashes of martyrs during a voyage in the East, and placed them with other relics in the altar of the basilica which he had erected, at the <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecration</a> of which he delivered a discourse, still extant. 
</p><p>The Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Antiqua" title="Santa Maria Antiqua">Santa Maria Antiqua</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Forum" title="Roman Forum">Roman Forum</a>, built in the fifth century, contains a chapel, built like the church itself on an ancient site, and consecrated to the Forty Martyrs. A sixth or seventh-century mural there depicts their martyrdom. The names of the confessors, as we find them also in later sources, were formerly inscribed on this <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a>.
</p><p>Acts of these martyrs, written subsequently, in <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> and <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, are yet extant, also a "Testament" of the Forty Martyrs.
</p>
