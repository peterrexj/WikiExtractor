<p><b>Saint Moluag</b> (c. 510 – 592; also known as <i>Lua</i>, <i>Luan</i>, <i>Luanus</i>, <i>Lugaidh</i>, <i>Moloag</i>, <i>Molluog</i>, <i>Molua</i>, <i>Murlach</i>, <i>Malew</i><sup id="cite_ref-saintpatrickdc_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saintpatrickdc-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-IrishSaintsinGreatBritain_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IrishSaintsinGreatBritain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>) was a Scottish missionary, and a contemporary of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Columba" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Columba">Saint Columba</a>, who evangelized the <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a> of <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> in the sixth century.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Saint Moluag was the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Argyll" title="Argyll">Argyll</a> as evidenced by a charter in 1544, from the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Argyll" title="Duke of Argyll">Earl of Argyll</a>, which states "in honour of God Omnipotent, the blessed Virgin, and Saint Moloc, our patron".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The House of Lorne became the kings of Dalriada and eventually united with the Picts to become the kings of Scots.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Moluag&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Lughaidh, better known by his pet name of Moluag, was an Irish noble of the <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_nAraide" class="mw-redirect" title="Dál nAraide">Dál nAraide</a><sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> (one of the main tribes of the <i>Ulaid</i> in what is now called Ulster). There are various Irish forms of the name, such as <i>Lughaidh</i> (or <i>Lugaid</i>), <i>Luoc</i> and <i>Lua</i>. Latinized they become <i>Lugidus</i>, <i>Lugidius,</i> <i>Lugadius, Lugacius</i> and <i>Luanus</i>. 
</p><p>The name, as it has come down the centuries, Moluag or Moluoc, is made up of the honorific <i>mo</i>, plus the original name <i>Lughaidh</i>, pronounced Lua, plus the endearing suffix <i>–oc</i>. Other variants include <i>Lugdach, Malew, Molonachus, Moloc and Molucus</i>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Moluag&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>MacDonald suggests that there must have been a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography"><i>vita</i></a> of Moluag that is lost because of his prominent appearance in <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard</a>'s <i>Life of Malachy</i>.  He writes <i>"Further support for this occurs in the Life of Patrick by the Cistercian monk Jocelin of Furness written in circa 1185, where Mo-Luóc (“Lugacius”) is described as one of the six Irish priests whom Patrick prophesied would become bishops"</i>.
</p><p>In a footnote he adds that the five other priests were Columbanus (Cólman), Meldanus (Mellán), Lugadius (Mo Lua), Cassanus (Cassán) and Creanus (Ciarán).<sup id="cite_ref-ClericsandClansmen_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ClericsandClansmen-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Moluag was a bishop active during the period of the First Order of Celtic Saints and known as ‘The Clear and Brilliant, The Sun of Lismore in Alba’.<sup id="cite_ref-TheMartyrologyofÓengus_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TheMartyrologyofÓengus-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Moluag&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Moluag was born between 500 and 520. He may have been the Lugaid mentioned in The Life of St <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">Comgall</a> who ordained him<sup id="cite_ref-Vitae_Sanctorum_Hiberniae_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vitae_Sanctorum_Hiberniae-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and to whom he may have had links of kinship.<sup id="cite_ref-Aberdeen_Breviary_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aberdeen_Breviary-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He left with twelve followers to lead the life of a missionary. Tradition states that the rock on which Moluag stood detached itself from the Irish coast and he drifted across to the island of the Lyn of Lorn in Argyll now called the Isle of <a href="/wiki/Lismore,_Scotland" title="Lismore, Scotland">Lismore</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Loch_Linnhe" title="Loch Linnhe">Loch Linnhe</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> where, in 562, he founded his community. (<i>Lios mor</i> is ancient Gaelic for ‘great courtyard’ in reference to the monastery). This had been the sacred island of the Western Picts whose capital was at Beregonium, across the water at <a href="/wiki/Benderloch" title="Benderloch">Benderloch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SkeneCelticScotland_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SkeneCelticScotland-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Lismore was the most important religious spot to the pagan kings of the area. Their kings were cremated on the ancient man-made "burial mound" of <i>Cnoc Aingeil</i> (Gaelic for ‘Hill of Fire’) at Bachuil, about three miles from the north of the island, near to the site that Moluag chose for his first centre. It was therefore the most desirable site for a missionary. Irish missionaries had learnt to focus heavily on the similarity and continuity between early Christianity and Paganism rather than the differences between them. The conversion process was, therefore, one of gradual education rather than outright confrontation and there were few martyrs in the area.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>MacDonald describes Lismore as being "hugely important, being closely tied with one of the earliest and most important Christian Saints in Northern Britain: Mo Luóc, or Moluag."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After founding a monastery on the Isle of Lismore,<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Moluag went on to found two other great centres in the land of the Picts at <a href="/wiki/Rosemarkie" title="Rosemarkie">Rosemarkie</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dufftown" title="Dufftown">Mortlach</a>. These were his three centres of teaching, and all three were to become the seats of the Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">sees</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_the_Isles" title="Diocese of the Isles">Isles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Ross_(Scotland)" title="Diocese of Ross (Scotland)">Ross</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Aberdeen" title="Diocese of Aberdeen">Aberdeen</a>. W. Douglas Simpson noted that Moluag laboured in Argyll, <a href="/wiki/Ross-shire" title="Ross-shire">Ross</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Banff,_Aberdeenshire" title="Banff, Aberdeenshire">Banff</a>. He remains best-remembered for his work in <a href="/wiki/Aberdeenshire" title="Aberdeenshire">Aberdeenshire</a>, where he established three churches in the valley of the <a href="/wiki/River_Dee,_Aberdeenshire" title="River Dee, Aberdeenshire">River Dee</a>—<a href="/wiki/Tarland" title="Tarland">Tarland</a>, Migvie, and <a href="/wiki/Kirkton_of_Durris" title="Kirkton of Durris">Durris</a>. However, Simpson regarded the most important of Moluag's establishments to be the Clova Monastery in <a href="/wiki/Kildrummy" title="Kildrummy">Kildrummy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his life of the Irish <a href="/wiki/Saint_Malachy" title="Saint Malachy">Saint Malachy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> wrote of Moluag, “One of the sons of that sacred family (Bangor) Lua by name, is said himself alone to have been the founder of a hundred monasteries,” Michael Barrett clarifying this as a reference to monastic houses in Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Moluag lived to extreme old age and died on 25 June 592 in <a href="/wiki/Garioch" title="Garioch">the Garioch</a> and was buried at his monastery in <a href="/wiki/Rosemarkie" title="Rosemarkie">Rosemarkie</a>, Ross-shire, Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a> record the death of <i>Lugaid of Les Mór</i> in 592:<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> <i>Obitus Lugide Lis Moer</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Moluag&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Moluag is said to have been buried at Rosemarkie on the <a href="/wiki/Moray_Firth" title="Moray Firth">Moray Firth</a>, though his remains were later transported to Lismore, and honoured in the cathedral which bore his name.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The feast day of Saint Moluag (25 June<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) was restored in 1898 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He is one of the 48 saints referred to in the Lorrha ("Stowe") Missal used by churches of Ireland, Scotland, Britain, France, Germany, Switzerland, and northern Italy: "Saint Lua of Lismore, Pray for us".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Coarb" title="Coarb">Coarb</a>, or successor, of Saint Moluag, is the Livingstone chief of the <a href="/wiki/Clan_MacLea" title="Clan MacLea">Clan MacLea</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> This Livingstone family of Lismore had long been the hereditary abbots of Lismore and, hence, possessors of the <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> of the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The bell of Saint Moluag was in existence until the sixteenth century when it disappeared during the <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Reformation" title="Scottish Reformation">Reformation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> An ancient bell found at Kilmichael Glassary, Argyll was thought to have been the lost bell.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_and_dedications">Legacy and dedications</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Moluag&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy and dedications">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Several churches were dedicated to Saint Moluag, including: 
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St_Moluag%27s_Cathedral,_Lismore" title="St Moluag&#39;s Cathedral, Lismore">St Moluag's Cathedral</a> (Kilmoluag), Lismore;<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Teampull_Mholuaidh" class="mw-redirect" title="Teampull Mholuaidh">Teampull Mholuaidh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Lewis" title="Isle of Lewis">Lewis</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Kirk <a href="/wiki/Malew" title="Malew">Malew</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Man" title="Isle of Man">Isle of Man</a>.</li></ul>
<p>Other sites include churches at <a href="/wiki/Clatt" title="Clatt">Clatt</a> and <a href="/wiki/Tarland" title="Tarland">Tarland</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Aberdeenshire" title="Aberdeenshire">Aberdeenshire</a>; and also churches on <a href="/wiki/Skye" class="mw-redirect" title="Skye">Skye</a>, <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Mull" title="Isle of Mull">Mull</a>, <a href="/wiki/Raasay" title="Raasay">Raasay</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tiree" title="Tiree">Tiree</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Pabay" title="Pabay">Pabay</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> At <a href="/wiki/Alyth" title="Alyth">Alyth</a> in <a href="/wiki/Perth_and_Kinross" title="Perth and Kinross">Perth and Kinross</a> there remain the ruins, known as "The Alyth Arches",<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> of a church which was built on the site of an older sixth-century church dedicated to the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-alythvoice_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alythvoice-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>  It has been suggested that the concentration of dedications to Moluag in North-East Scotland, and particularly in the vicinity of <a href="/wiki/Rhynie,_Aberdeenshire" title="Rhynie, Aberdeenshire">Rhynie</a>, may be a legacy of a saint cult promoted during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Nechtan_mac_Der-Ilei" title="Nechtan mac Der-Ilei">Nechtan mac Der-Ilei</a> and contemporaneous with the ascendancy of the <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_Loairn" title="Cenél Loairn">Cenél Loairn</a>, with whom his Pictish kingdom appears to have enjoyed good relations.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Mortlach in <a href="/wiki/Banffshire" title="Banffshire">Banffshire</a>, where some of his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were preserved, an abbey was founded in 1010 by <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A1el_Coluim_II_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="Máel Coluim II of Scotland">Máel Coluim II of Scotland</a>, in thanks for a victory in which the Scots had invoked the aid of Saint Moluag.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On Lewis, Saint Moluag was invoked for cures from madness.<sup id="cite_ref-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OxfordDictionaryofSaints343-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Clatt there was held annually "St Mallock's Fair", which lasted eight days.<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> At Tarland there was a "Luoch Fair" which is thought to have been in honour of Saint Molaug,<sup id="cite_ref-ACalendarofScottishSaints_11-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ACalendarofScottishSaints-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and at Alyth "Simmalogue Fair" was celebrated.<sup id="cite_ref-alythvoice_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alythvoice-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
