<p><b>Leodegar of Poitiers</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Leodegarius</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Léger</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 615 – October 2, 679 AD) was a martyred Burgundian <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Autun" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Autun">Bishop of Autun</a>. He was the son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sigrada" title="Saint Sigrada">Saint Sigrada</a> and the brother of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Warinus" title="Saint Warinus">Saint Warinus</a>.
</p><p>Leodegar was an opponent of <a href="/wiki/Ebroin" title="Ebroin">Ebroin</a>, the Frankish <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Mayor of the Palace">Mayor of the Palace</a> of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>, and the leader of the faction of Burgundian nobles. His torture and death made him a martyr and saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Lives_of_the_Saints_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lives_of_the_Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leodegar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Leodegar was the son of a high-ranking <a href="/wiki/Burgundy_(historical_region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy (historical region)">Burgundian</a> nobleman, Bodilon, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Poitiers" title="Count of Poitiers">Count of Poitiers</a> and <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Paris" title="Count of Paris">Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sigrada" title="Saint Sigrada">St. Sigrada of Alsace</a>, who later became a nun in the convent of Sainte-Marie at <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>. His brother was Warinus.<sup id="cite_ref-Lives_of_the_Saints_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lives_of_the_Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He spent his childhood in Paris at the court of <a href="/wiki/Clotaire_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotaire II">Clotaire II</a>, King of the Franks and was educated at the palace school. When he was older he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>, where there was a long-established cathedral school, to study under his maternal uncle, Desiderius (Dido), <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Poitiers">Bishop of Poitiers</a>. When he was twenty, his uncle made him an archdeacon.<sup id="cite_ref-Lives_of_the_Saints_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lives_of_the_Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly afterwards Leodegar became a priest, and in 650, with the bishop's permission, became a monk at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maixent" title="Saint-Maixent">St Maxentius</a> in <a href="/wiki/Poitou" title="Poitou">Poitou</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was soon elected abbot, and initiated reforms including the introduction of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine rule">Benedictine rule</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lives_of_the_Saints_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lives_of_the_Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leodegar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Around 656, Leodegar was called to the Neustrian court by the widowed Queen <a href="/wiki/Balthild_of_Chelles" title="Balthild of Chelles">Bathilde</a> to assist in the government of the united kingdoms and in the education of her children. Then in 659, he was installed at the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Autun" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Autun">see of Autun</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Burgundy_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy (region)">Burgundy</a>; he again undertook the work of reform and held a council at Autun in 661. The council denounced <a href="/wiki/Manichaeism" title="Manichaeism">Manichaeism</a>. He made reforms among the secular clergy and in the religious communities, and had three <a href="/wiki/Baptistery" title="Baptistery">baptisteries</a> erected in the city. The church of Saint-Nazaire was enlarged and embellished, and a refuge established for the indigent. Leodegar also caused the public buildings to be repaired and the old Roman walls of Autun to be restored.<sup id="cite_ref-MacErlean_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MacErlean-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His authority at Autun placed him as a leader among the Franco-Burgundian nobles.
</p><p>Leodegar became one of the leaders of the opposition against <a href="/wiki/Ebroin" title="Ebroin">Ebroin</a>, mayor of the palace in Neustria. Ebroin accused Leodegar before King Clothar III. but the king then died in 673, while the trial was still going on. Ebroin now installed Clothar's youngest brother, <a href="/wiki/Theoderic_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Theoderic III">Theoderic III</a>, as king. As the mayor denied the nobles of Neutria and Burgundy access to the king, they called in the middle brother, <a href="/wiki/Childeric_II" title="Childeric II">Childeric II</a>, who had been king of Austrasia since 662 and now assumed rule over Neustria and Burgundy as well. Ebroin was interned at <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil-les-Bains" title="Luxeuil-les-Bains">Luxeuil</a> and Theoderic sent to St. Denis.<sup id="cite_ref-MacErlean_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MacErlean-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ewig_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ewig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Leodegar, who had supported this change, soon ran into conflict with the new king as the latter surrounded himself with advisors from Austrasia. The bishop also criticized the king for his uncanonical marriage to his first cousin Bilichild. After finding himself on the losing side in a hereditary dispute, the king banished Leodegar to Luxeuil as well.<sup id="cite_ref-Ewig_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ewig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-bbkl_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbkl-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>When Childeric II was murdered at Bondi in 675, by a disaffected Frank, <a href="/wiki/Theoderic_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Theoderic III">Theoderic III</a> was installed as king in Neustria, making <a href="/wiki/Leudesius" title="Leudesius">Leudesius</a> his mayor. Ebroin took advantage of the chaos to make his escape from Luxeuil and hasten to the court. In a short time Ebroin had Leudesius murdered and became mayor once again, still Leodegar's implacable enemy.<sup id="cite_ref-MacErlean_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MacErlean-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Leger_dutchms_500.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Leger_dutchms_500.jpg/220px-Leger_dutchms_500.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Leger_dutchms_500.jpg/330px-Leger_dutchms_500.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Leger_dutchms_500.jpg/440px-Leger_dutchms_500.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="750" /></a><figcaption>The martyrdom of St. Leger</figcaption></figure>
<p>About 675 the Duke of Champagne, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ch%C3%A2lons-sur-Marne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Châlons-sur-Marne">Bishop of Châlons-sur-Marne</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Valence" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Valence">Bishop of Valence</a>, stirred up by Ebroin, attacked Autun, and Leodegar fell into their hands. At Ebroin's instigation, Leodegar's eyes were gouged out and the sockets cauterized, and his tongue was cut out. Some years later Ebroin persuaded the king that Childeric had been assassinated at the instigation of Leodegar. The bishop was seized again, and, after a mock trial, was degraded and condemned to further exile, at <a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9camp" title="Fécamp">Fécamp</a>, in Normandy. Near Sarcing he was led out into a forest on Ebroin's order and beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A dubious<sup id="cite_ref-bbkl_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbkl-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> testament drawn up at the time of the council of Autun has been preserved as well as the Acts of the council. A letter which he caused to be sent to his mother after his mutilation is likewise extant.
</p><p>In 782, his relics were translated from the site of his death, Sarcing in Artois, to the site of his earliest hagiography – the Abbey of St Maxentius (Saint-Maixent) near Poitiers. Later they were removed to Rennes and thence to Ebreuil, which place took the name of Saint-Léger in his honour. Some relics are still kept in the cathedral of Autun and the Grand Séminaire of Soissons. In 1458 Cardinal Rolin caused his feast day to be observed as a holy day of obligation.
</p><p>For sources to his biography, there are two early Lives,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> drawn from the same lost source (Krusch 1891), and also two later ones (one of them in verse).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_significance">Cultural significance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leodegar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cultural significance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Historically there was a custom among wealthy <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">British</a> merchants to sell in May, spend the summer outside of London, then to return on St Leger's Day. This gave rise to the saying used in regards to financial trading markets, "Sell in May and go away, and come on back on St. Leger's Day".
</p>
