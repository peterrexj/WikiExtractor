<p><b>Nicholas Pieck, O.F.M.</b>, "Nicolaas" or "Claes Pieck" in Dutch, was a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who was one of a group of Catholic clergy and <a href="/wiki/Lay_brothers" class="mw-redirect" title="Lay brothers">lay brothers</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Gorkum" title="Martyrs of Gorkum">Martyrs of Gorkum</a>, who were executed for refusal to renounce their faith in 1572.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Pieck&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in the town of Gorkum (now <a href="/wiki/Gorinchem" title="Gorinchem">Gorinchem</a>), the son of Jan Pieck and Henriea Clavia, devout Catholics. He was sent to college at <a href="/wiki/%27s-Hertogenbosch" title="&#39;s-Hertogenbosch">'s-Hertogenbosch</a>, and as soon as he had completed his classical studies he received the habit of the Friars Minor at the <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> in that town. Nicholas was ordained a priest in 1558, devoting himself to the apostolic ministry. He was appointed <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Guardian</a> of the friary in <a href="/wiki/Gorinchem" title="Gorinchem">Gorkum</a>, his native town.<sup id="cite_ref-heckmann_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heckmann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pieck preached against <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinism</a>. In particular, he preached the dogma of the <a href="/wiki/Real_Presence" class="mw-redirect" title="Real Presence">Real Presence</a>. In June 1572, the citadel of Gorkum was taken by the <a href="/wiki/Les_Gueux" class="mw-redirect" title="Les Gueux">Watergeuzen</a>, who retained 19 of the clergy as prisoners although they had promised to let the inhabitants depart from the town without being molested.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> For reprisals, because of the city's determined defense, they gathered all the members of the clergy in Gorkum into one prison and took out their grievances against the Spanish crown on the priests and religious.<sup id="cite_ref-schaeffer_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaeffer-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pieck and eight other Franciscan friars were confined in a dark and foul dungeon<sup id="cite_ref-heckmann_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heckmann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> where they were tortured. Taking the cord which Pieck wore around his waist and putting it around his neck, they first suspended him from a beam and then let him fall heavily to the ground. This torture was continued until the cord broke, and Father Nicholas fell to the ground unconscious.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Pieck's two brothers tried to obtain his release, but the guardian would not leave the others.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Other priests were captured, bringing the total to 19.
</p><p>On 6 July they were thrown half-naked into the hold of a ship and removed to Brielle.<sup id="cite_ref-schaeffer_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaeffer-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Stopping at Dordrecht they were exhibited for money to the curious. At Brielle the commander of the Watergeuzen, <a href="/wiki/William_II_de_la_Marck" class="mw-redirect" title="William II de la Marck">William II de la Marck</a>, promised them freedom if they would renounce the authority of the pope and belief in the <a href="/wiki/Real_Presence" class="mw-redirect" title="Real Presence">Real Presence</a>. None did. Despite instructions from Prince <a href="/wiki/William_the_Silent" title="William the Silent">William the Silent</a> to spare them, and protests from the magistrates of Gorkum,<sup id="cite_ref-schaeffer_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaeffer-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the members of the group were hanged on 9 July 1572 in an old barn at the deserted Ruggen Monastery on the outskirts of Briel. The execution was clumsily handled; it took two hours for some of them to strangle.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They became known as the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Gorkum" title="Martyrs of Gorkum">Martyrs of Gorkum</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicholas_Pieck&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Nicholas and his companions were beatified by <a href="/wiki/Clement_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement X">Clement X</a>, 24 November 1675, and canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pius_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius IX">Pius IX</a>, 29 June 1867.<sup id="cite_ref-heckmann_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heckmann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> For many years the place of their martyrdom in Brielle has been the scene of numerous pilgrimages and processions.<sup id="cite_ref-albers_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
