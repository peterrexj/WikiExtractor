<p><b>Saint Laura of Cordoba</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Santa Laura de Córdoba</i>; died 864) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> who lived in <a href="/wiki/Muslim_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim Spain">Muslim Spain</a> during the 9th century. She was born in <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a>, and became a nun at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuteclara&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cuteclara (page does not exist)">Cuteclara</a> after her husband died, eventually rising to become an <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>. She was martyred by Muslims, who took her captive and scalded her to death by placing her in a vat of boiling <a href="/wiki/Pine_pitch" class="mw-redirect" title="Pine pitch">pitch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is on 19 October; she is one of the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Martyrs of Córdoba">Martyrs of Córdoba</a>.
</p><p>She is commemorated by the <a href="/wiki/Estadio_Santa_Laura" class="mw-redirect" title="Estadio Santa Laura">Estadio Santa Laura</a> ("Saint Laura Stadium") in <a href="/wiki/Santiago" title="Santiago">Santiago</a>, Chile and the <a href="/wiki/Humberstone_and_Santa_Laura_Saltpeter_Works" title="Humberstone and Santa Laura Saltpeter Works">Humberstone and Santa Laura Saltpeter Works</a> in northern Chile.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Thomas_Love_Peacock" title="Thomas Love Peacock">Thomas Love Peacock</a> wrote a ballad about Saint Laura in his work <a href="/wiki/Gryll_Grange" title="Gryll Grange">Gryll Grange</a>.
