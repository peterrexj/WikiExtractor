					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Anglo-Saxon pagan king and Christian saint</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Arwald</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Arwald.png" class="image"><img alt="Arwald.png" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Arwald.png/220px-Arwald.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="353" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Arwald.png/330px-Arwald.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Arwald.png/440px-Arwald.png 2x" data-file-width="617" data-file-height="989" /></a><div class="infobox-caption">Image of King Arwald by local Isle of Wight artist Paul A.T. Wilson</div></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">The <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">The <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a> and The <a href="/wiki/New_Forest" title="New Forest">New Forest</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Redbridge,_Southampton" title="Redbridge, Southampton">Redbridge, Southampton</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data">Roman Catholicism, Anglican Communion, Orthodox Church</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">22 April</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>King Arwald</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 686 AD) was the last King of the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a> and last <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">pagan</a> king in Anglo-Saxon England. <b>Saint Arwald</b> is the name collectively given to King Arwald's sons or brothers who, being baptised before their execution, were later canonised and are considered in some Christian traditions to be martyr saints.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="King_Arwald">King Arwald</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: King Arwald">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nearly all that is known of him is from Bede's <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum</a></i>, which describes the invasion of the Isle of Wight by <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A6dwalla_of_Wessex" title="Cædwalla of Wessex">Cædwalla</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>. A <a href="/wiki/Jutes" title="Jutes">Jutish</a> King of the Isle of Wight and the <a href="/wiki/Wihtwara" title="Wihtwara">Wihtwara</a> tribe, he claimed descent from the legendary founders of the island, Stuf and Whitgar. He was killed in battle during the invasion.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Arwald">Saint Arwald</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Saint Arwald">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Though King Arwald was killed in battle, his two sons (or in some versions his brothers) escaped to the Great Ytene Forest (now called the <a href="/wiki/New_Forest" title="New Forest">New Forest</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They were betrayed to <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A6dwalla_of_Wessex" title="Cædwalla of Wessex">Cædwalla</a>, and in some versions were then taken to a place where he "was in hiding with his wounds" at <a href="/wiki/South_Stoneham" title="South Stoneham">Stoneham</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Southampton" title="Southampton">Southampton</a>. Shortly before they were put to the sword they allegedly converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by the intervention of Abbot Cynibert of Hreutford,<sup id="cite_ref-Stanton_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stanton-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> being described by Bede as "the first fruits" of the massacre because of this conversion. Cædwalla later died of wounds sustained during his invasion.
</p><p>Though venerated as saints, their names are unknown, but they are called collectively "St. Arwald" after their father (or brother). Their feast day is 22 April.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Folklore">Folklore</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Folklore">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A version of the story of Arwald survives in the folklore of the nearby <a href="/wiki/New_Forest" title="New Forest">New Forest</a>, a neighbouring area with similarly <a href="/wiki/Jutes" title="Jutes">Jutish</a> heritage to the Isle of Wight. In this tradition the two brothers escape to the Ytene, where they encounter various mysterious figures (which have been theorised by some as representing gods of <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a>), before finally being betrayed by a wealthy <a href="/wiki/Ealdorman" title="Ealdorman">Ealdorman</a>. They are then forced to convert to Christianity, before being killed in the forest by <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A6dwalla_of_Wessex" title="Cædwalla of Wessex">Cædwalla</a>, but the elder brother is able to impale himself on a tree before being murdered, dedicating his death to <a href="/wiki/Woden" class="mw-redirect" title="Woden">Woden</a> and thus redeeming himself to the old gods. The ghost of the younger brother is said to walk the forest still, and on April 22nd has been seen with the ghosts of his brother, King Arwald and Woden himself, feasting on the site of their deaths.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Arwald's unnamed sister survived, as the wife of the king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kent" title="Kingdom of Kent">Kent</a>, who went on to become a direct ancestor of Alfred the Great.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p><i>Caedwalla</i> by  <a href="/wiki/Frank_Cowper" title="Frank Cowper">Frank Cowper</a> (1888) portrays Arwald as the pagan antagonist to the Christian Caedwalla.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The Quay Arts Centre in <a href="/wiki/Newport,_Isle_of_Wight" title="Newport, Isle of Wight">Newport</a> exhibited an Isle of Wight Hidden Heroes exhibition which included a sculpture of a mask of Arwald by Nigel George. This is now on permanent display at Newport Roman Villa, and local celebrations of Arwald recur on the Isle of Wight annually on his Saints Day.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><i>Arwald's Kingdom; Tales from the Isle of Wight</i> (2018) by Mark Francis is a book of poetry, stories and hiking around the Isle of Wight.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
