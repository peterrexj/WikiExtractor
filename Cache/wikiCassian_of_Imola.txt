<p><b>Cassian</b>, or <b>Saint Cassian of Imola</b>, or <b>Cassius</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> saint of the 4th century. His feast day is August 13.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cassian_of_Imola&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:CassianusofImola.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/CassianusofImola.jpg/220px-CassianusofImola.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="194" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/CassianusofImola.jpg/330px-CassianusofImola.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/CassianusofImola.jpg/440px-CassianusofImola.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="705" /></a><figcaption>Cassian being killed by his pupils. From the Dutch <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrs_Mirror" title="Martyrs Mirror">Martyrs Mirror</a></i>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Little is known about his life, although the traditional accounts converge on some of the details of his martyrdom. He was a schoolmaster at <a href="/wiki/Imola" title="Imola">Imola</a>, but rather than sacrifice to the Roman gods, as so ordered by the current emperor, <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>, he was condemned to death and turned over to his own pupils.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Since they were eager for revenge for the many punishments he had inflicted on them, they bound him to a stake and tortured him to death by stabbing him with their pointed iron <a href="/wiki/Stylus" title="Stylus">styli</a>, the devices then used to mark wooden or wax writing tablets. Cassian suffered in one of the persecutions of the fourth century, but in which cannot be assigned with any certainty.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was interred by the Christians at Imola, where afterwards his relics were honoured with a rich mausolæum.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His traditional date of martyrdom is August 13, 363, hence August 13 is his feast day on the Roman calendar. Cassian is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mexico_City" title="Mexico City">Mexico City</a>, <a href="/wiki/Imola" title="Imola">Imola</a> (Italy), <a href="/wiki/Suko%C5%A1an" title="Sukošan">Sukošan</a> (<a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>), and of parish clerks. <a href="/wiki/Comacchio_Cathedral" title="Comacchio Cathedral">Comacchio Cathedral</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Brixen" title="Cathedral of Brixen">Cathedral of Brixen</a> are dedicated to him. He is also the patron saint of the localities of <a href="/wiki/San_Casciano_in_Val_di_Pesa" title="San Casciano in Val di Pesa">San Casciano in Val di Pesa</a> and <a href="/wiki/San_Casciano_dei_Bagni" title="San Casciano dei Bagni">San Casciano dei Bagni</a> (both Italy) and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Las_Galletas&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Las Galletas (page does not exist)">Las Galletas</a> (<a href="/wiki/Tenerife" title="Tenerife">Tenerife</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>). <a href="/wiki/St_Cassian%27s_Centre" title="St Cassian&#39;s Centre">St Cassian's Centre</a> is named for him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_references">Cultural references</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cassian_of_Imola&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cultural references">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are at least two references in modern literature to Cassian.  In the novel <i><a href="/wiki/A_Confederacy_of_Dunces" title="A Confederacy of Dunces">A Confederacy of Dunces</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/John_Kennedy_Toole" title="John Kennedy Toole">John Kennedy Toole</a>, protagonist Ignatius Reilly informs one of his professors that "St. Cassian of Imola was stabbed to death by his students with their styli," a holy death that his professor would not merit.&#160;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Annie_Dillard" title="Annie Dillard">Annie Dillard</a> also makes a reference to him in her 1992 novel <i>The Living.</i>  <a href="/wiki/Bethel_College_(Kansas)" title="Bethel College (Kansas)">Bethel College</a> had a <i>Cassianus Lounge</i> in the faculty offices area.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The famous German jurist <a href="/wiki/Carl_Schmitt" title="Carl Schmitt">Carl Schmitt</a>, named the place in which he spent his late years (in Plettenberg) as "San Casciano". According to the common opinion (for the own Schmitt, the most obvious interpretation), this referred to some place near <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence, Italy</a> (called San Casciano) in which <a href="/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Machiavelli" title="Niccolò Machiavelli">Machiavelli</a> spent the time during which he wrote some of his most influential works. Schmitt acknowledges this view but addresses as "exoteric". On the other hand, the "esoteric" interpretation of the choice would be actually related to the very Saint Cassian of Imola. Carl Schmitt identified with the Catholic martyr insofar as he also felt as some sort of teacher assassinated or stabbed (i.e. betrayed) by his own students (e.g. by <a href="/wiki/Otto_Kirchheimer" title="Otto Kirchheimer">Otto Kirchheimer</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Ride_the_Cyclone" title="Ride the Cyclone">Ride the Cyclone</a>, a 2008 musical with a 2016 <a href="/wiki/Off-Broadway" title="Off-Broadway">Off-Broadway</a> run, the cast of deceased students are all members of the fictional St. Cassian High School Chamber Choir.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
