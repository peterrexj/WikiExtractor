<p><b>Walstan</b> (recorded as <b>Walston</b> in some sources) was an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon">Anglo-Saxon</a> prince, known for the miracles which occurred during and after his life after he became a farm worker. He is a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of  farm animals and agricultural workers, who once visited his <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> at the church at <a href="/wiki/Bawburgh" title="Bawburgh">Bawburgh</a>, in the English county of <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a>. Two sources for his life exist: the&#160;<i>De Sancto Walstano Confessore</i>&#160;in the&#160;<i>Nova Legenda Angliæ</i>, printed by <a href="/wiki/Wynkyn_de_Worde" title="Wynkyn de Worde">Wynkyn de Worde</a> in 1516, and known as the <i>English Life</i>; and a later <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>&#160;manuscript copied in 1658&#160;from a now lost medieval&#160;<a href="/wiki/Triptych" title="Triptych">triptych</a>, now in the&#160;<a href="/wiki/Lambeth_Palace" title="Lambeth Palace">Lambeth Palace</a>&#160;library in London.
</p><p>Walstan is associated with Norfolk, but the&#160;<i>Latin Life</i> gives his birthplace as&#160;<a href="/wiki/Blythburgh" title="Blythburgh">Blythburgh</a> in <a href="/wiki/Suffolk" title="Suffolk">Suffolk</a> and not Bawburgh, as stated in the <i>English Life</i>. Described as the son of Benedict and Blida, he is said to have "received a pious education". At the age of 12 he determined to devote his life to one of prayer, and became a&#160;farm worker for a man at&#160;<a href="/wiki/Taverham" title="Taverham">Taverham</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Norwich" title="Norwich">Norwich</a>. After being told of his forthcoming death, he made his&#160;<a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a>, stopped working and instructed that his two bulls were to pull his body in a cart wherever God willed. After his death on 30 May 1016, his hearse left Taverham and passed through <a href="/wiki/Costessey" title="Costessey">Costessey</a> before reaching Bawburgh, where he was buried; along the route springs miraculously appeared.&#160;A small&#160;<a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a>&#160;dedicated to Walstan was built at Bawburgh.
</p><p>He is represented by a crown and&#160;<a href="/wiki/Sceptre" title="Sceptre">sceptre</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Scythe" title="Scythe">scythe</a> in his hand and cattle near him. St Walstan's Day is still celebrated each year in Bawburgh, when a special <a href="/wiki/Church_service" title="Church service">service</a> takes place on the nearest Sunday to 30 May, his day in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">calendar of saints</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiographical_and_medieval_sources">Hagiographical and medieval sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walstan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiographical and medieval sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png" class="image"><img alt="page from mediaeval book" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png/220px-First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="336" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png/330px-First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png/440px-First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png 2x" data-file-width="530" data-file-height="809" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:First_part_of_the_Latin_Life_of_St_Walstan.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The beginning of Walstan's <i>Latin Life</i>, from the <i>Nova Legenda Angliæ</i> (1516)</div></div></div>
<p>Walstan is supposed to have been born in 975. He is associated with the English county of Norfolk,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShortt1914301_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShortt1914301-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but uncertainty surrounds his actual identity and any details of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill200233_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill200233-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Information that he existed comes from two sources, the earliest of which is <i>De Sancto Walstano Confessore</i> in the <i>Nova Legenda Angliæ</i>, written by the English historian <a href="/wiki/John_Capgrave" title="John Capgrave">John Capgrave</a>, and known as the <i>Latin Life</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch20151_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch20151-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Capgrave's <i>Nova Legenda Angliæ</i> was printed by Wynkyne de Worde in 1516; Walstan was included as one of 15 new saints in a compilation that did not form part of the main text. The source for Wynkyne de Worde's information is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch20151_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch20151-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Walstan's story was also described in a <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscript</a> now known as the Lambeth <i>Life</i> (or <i>English Life</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which was copied on 29 September 1658<sup id="cite_ref-NA_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> by a <a href="/wiki/Scribe" title="Scribe">scribe</a> from a now lost medieval <a href="/wiki/Triptych" title="Triptych">triptych</a> from <a href="/wiki/Bawburgh" title="Bawburgh">Bawburgh</a> church in <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Now part of manuscript Lambeth MSS 935 at <a href="/wiki/Lambeth_Palace" title="Lambeth Palace">Lambeth Palace</a> library in London, it is recorded as 'The History of St. Walston taken out of an ancient parchment MS, enclosed in a case of 3 pieces of Wainscott about a yard long each of ym', with a drawing of the case. The triptych was owned by a man named Clarke, a Norfolk <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusant</a>, who claimed it once came from Bawburgh.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The two <i>Lives</i> were written for two different audiences; the <i>Latin Life</i> was intended for <a href="/wiki/Monastic" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic">monastic</a> readership, and the later <i>English Life</i> was meant to be read by <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a> readers, as indicated when stresses the need to commit to working hard for a master.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200535_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200535-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite being declared a saint prior to the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman conquest of England</a>, no images of Walstan or versions of his life are known to have existed before the <i>Latin Life</i> was written centuries later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200533_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200533-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Domesday_Book" title="Domesday Book">Domesday Book</a>, completed in 1086,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGalbraith196190_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGalbraith196190-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> provides details of Bawburgh, the site of Walstan's shrine during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004146_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004146-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> but does not state that the church there was important in any way.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004146_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004146-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> According to the historian Tim Pestell, the discovery in 2002 of a lead <a href="/wiki/Commemorative_plaque" title="Commemorative plaque">plaque</a> near to the church—possibly a kind of burial inscription—raises the possibility that "devotion to Walstan had already taken hold at Bawburgh by the time of the Norman conquest".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004146–147_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004146–147-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Walstan has been compared with <a href="/wiki/Godric_of_Finchale" title="Godric of Finchale">Godric of Finchale</a>, a Norfolk man who was known for his kindness towards animals.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill200233_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill200233-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walstan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walstan was born in the <a href="/wiki/Vill" title="Vill">vill</a> of Bawburgh. Descended from royalty, his parents were named as being Benedict and Blithe (or Blida) of <a href="/wiki/Martham" title="Martham">Martham</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch2015148_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch2015148-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> a kinswoman of the English king <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_the_Unready" title="Æthelred the Unready">Æthelred the Unready</a> and his son <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Ironside" title="Edmund Ironside">Edmund Ironside</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBlair2002515_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlair2002515-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup> As a boy he "received a pious education",<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> showed great devotion to God and was full of divine grace. At the age of 12 he renounced his life of privilege and with his parents' permission determined to devote his life to one of prayer,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch20155_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch20155-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> although not as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Walstan%27s,_Bawburgh.jpg" class="image"><img alt="photograph of church at Bawburgh" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/St_Walstan%27s%2C_Bawburgh.jpg/220px-St_Walstan%27s%2C_Bawburgh.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="229" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/St_Walstan%27s%2C_Bawburgh.jpg/330px-St_Walstan%27s%2C_Bawburgh.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/St_Walstan%27s%2C_Bawburgh.jpg/440px-St_Walstan%27s%2C_Bawburgh.jpg 2x" data-file-width="898" data-file-height="933" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Walstan%27s,_Bawburgh.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Mary and St Walstan&#39;s, <a href="/wiki/Bawburgh" title="Bawburgh">Bawburgh</a></div></div></div>
<p>Walstan became a <a href="/wiki/Serf" class="mw-redirect" title="Serf">serf</a> and worked as a farm hand for a man at <a href="/wiki/Taverham" title="Taverham">Taverham</a>. He distributed his belongings amongst the needy and gave away his shoes to a poor man. This act of kindness was witnessed by his master's wife, who then cruelly ordered him to load prickly plants onto a cart. Walstan was miraculously saved from injuring his feet, whereby the woman begged his forgiveness. His master then declared he wished him to be his heir, an offer rejected by Walstan, who instead accepted a gift of a pregnant cow. The animal delivered two calves, which Walstan then cared for, not knowing that one day they would transport his body to where he was to be buried.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch20155–6_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch20155–6-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One Friday, when he was <a href="/wiki/Scythe" title="Scythe">scything</a> in a field, an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> appeared, saying, "Brother Walstan, on the third day from today you will enter paradise." Walstan then went to make his <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a> to a priest. The following day he stopped working and on the Monday he instructed his master and others around him that his two bulls were to pull his hearse wherever God ordained. He died that day, on 30 May 1016, and those present witnessed a white dove flying from his mouth to heaven. His hearse left Taverham and passed through <a href="/wiki/Costessey" title="Costessey">Costessey</a> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˌ/: secondary stress follows">ˌ</span><span title="&#39;k&#39; in &#39;kind&#39;">k</span><span title="/ɒ/: &#39;o&#39; in &#39;body&#39;">ɒ</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span></span>/</a></span>&#32;<span class="nowrap" style="font-size:85%">(<span class="unicode haudio"><span class="fn"><span style="white-space:nowrap;margin-right:.25em;"><a href="/wiki/File:En-uk-Costessey.wav" title="About this sound"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/11px-Loudspeaker.svg.png" decoding="async" width="11" height="11" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/17px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/22px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20" /></a></span><a href="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3b/En-uk-Costessey.wav" class="internal" title="En-uk-Costessey.wav">listen</a></span></span>)</span></span>), where the bulls pulled the cart over a deep pool without sinking, and a <a href="/wiki/Spring_(hydrosphere)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spring (hydrosphere)">spring</a> appeared. Near Bawburgh, another spring was made which was later found to have the power to cure the infirm. Walstan was placed inside the church at Bawburgh with the <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Monks" class="mw-redirect" title="Monks">monks</a> in attendance.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch20156–8_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch20156–8-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFarmer2011_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarmer2011-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;note 2&#93;</a></sup> In the following years, people came for themselves and their animals to be cured.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch20158_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch20158-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The anonymous <i>English Life</i> gives Walstan's birthplace as "Blyborow town" or <a href="/wiki/Blythburgh" title="Blythburgh">Blythburgh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Suffolk" title="Suffolk">Suffolk</a>, and <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch2015148_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch2015148-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> relates that he was the son of a king; as a young prince he might suddenly return home, which would have threatened the contract established between himself and the farmer in Taverham. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200535_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200535-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Life</i> contains 11 miracles that occurred from the 14th century onwards, including the miracle of the thorns and the vision of the angel, both also described in the <i>Latin Life</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch201541–42_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch201541–42-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_and_veneration">Legacy and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walstan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Walstan%27s_Well,_Bawburgh,_Norfolk.jpg" class="image"><img alt="photograph of well at Bawburgh" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/St._Walstan%27s_Well%2C_Bawburgh%2C_Norfolk.jpg/220px-St._Walstan%27s_Well%2C_Bawburgh%2C_Norfolk.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="233" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/St._Walstan%27s_Well%2C_Bawburgh%2C_Norfolk.jpg/330px-St._Walstan%27s_Well%2C_Bawburgh%2C_Norfolk.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/St._Walstan%27s_Well%2C_Bawburgh%2C_Norfolk.jpg/440px-St._Walstan%27s_Well%2C_Bawburgh%2C_Norfolk.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1073" data-file-height="1138" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Walstan%27s_Well,_Bawburgh,_Norfolk.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Walstan's Well, Bawburgh</div></div></div>
<p>A small <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> was built off the existing church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Mary</a>, which was given a new dedication of Mary and Walstan.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> He is venerated as a saint of farms and farm workers,<sup id="cite_ref-stick_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stick-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> and his <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> and the wells associated with him were visited by local farmers and farm laborers throughout the Middle Ages.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242_15-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStanton1887242-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In 1309 the revenues generated at Bawburgh by pilgrims could provide an income for six <a href="/wiki/Priests" class="mw-redirect" title="Priests">priests</a> and the church <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a>, in addition to providing the finances needed to rebuild the <a href="/wiki/Chancel" title="Chancel">chancel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill200233_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill200233-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBlomefield1805387–393_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlomefield1805387–393-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> In later decades the shrine became ruinous; it was  rebuilt in the 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200532–33_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200532–33-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> After the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">Dissolution of the Monasteries</a>, the church once again fell into disrepair.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBlomefield1805387–393_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlomefield1805387–393-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> During the 19th century, the village church regained its prosperity when miracles were associated with the water obtained from the well. In 1913 the <i><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Daily_Press" title="Eastern Daily Press">Eastern Daily Press</a></i> named it the "Lourdes of Norfolk". The well at Bawburgh can still be seen.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Costessey, the second place where Walstan's hearse rested, the well there had dried up by 1750. In April 1978 it was recorded as being a deep circular pit about 4 metres (13&#160;ft) across, with lumps of flint wall at the base, and filled with fallen trees.<sup id="cite_ref-Explorer_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Explorer-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> By January 2015 the depression was still visible, and a plan was proposed to fence off the site of the well and place a plaque there.<sup id="cite_ref-Explorer_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Explorer-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The site of the well, now located on the grounds of a golf course, has been marked with a sign.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Walstan is represented in <a href="/wiki/Sacred_art" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred art">religious art</a> by a crown and <a href="/wiki/Sceptre" title="Sceptre">sceptre</a> (his generic emblems)—at least five figures from medieval <a href="/wiki/Rood_screen" title="Rood screen">rood screens</a> depict him in this way<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames1916238–239_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames1916238–239-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFarmer2011_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarmer2011-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>—and with a scythe in his hand and cattle near him (his specific emblems).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBond1914299,_303_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBond1914299,_303-30">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;note 3&#93;</a></sup> Churches dedicated to Walstan dating from before the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a> are found in Norfolk. Uniquely outside England, a church in <a href="/wiki/Rongai" title="Rongai">Rongai</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kenya" title="Kenya">Kenya</a>, is dedicated to Walstan.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETwinch2015133,136–137_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETwinch2015133,136–137-34">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The historian Kellie Robertson has said that during the Middle Ages, Walstan was a "good example of a localized cult exemplifying the vibrancy of lay popular piety" and "an exemplar of filial love, charity, devotion to work, and obedience to the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200532_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200532-35">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> His absence from medieval <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_year" title="Liturgical year">liturgical calendars</a> is thought to be a reflection of the local nature of the <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> that appeared after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPestell2004147-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Robertson connects Walstan's popularity in the mid 15th century with the popular unrest in <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a> during this period, caused by restrictive laws on agricultural workers in the aftermath of the <a href="/wiki/Black_Death" title="Black Death">Black Death</a> that swept across the country in 1349.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEByrne200428_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEByrne200428-36">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200533_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200533-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> That he only appears as a saint in the 16th century—and in a revision of an earlier version of <i>Sanctilogium angliae</i>—implies he was relatively unimportant at the time it was first compiled, and his cult revived in the mid 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobertson200533_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobertson200533-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St Walstan's Day is celebrated each year in Bawburgh, when a special Patronal Service takes place on the nearest Sunday to 30 May, his feast date.<sup id="cite_ref-stick_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stick-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESociété_des_Bollandistes18981269_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESociété_des_Bollandistes18981269-37">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>  St. Walstan's Day occurred on 28 December in some 17th and 18th century <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">saints' calendars</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames1916238–267_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames1916238–267-38">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
