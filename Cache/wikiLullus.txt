<p><b>Saint Lullus</b> (Lull or Lul) (born about 710 AD in <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>, died 16 October 786 in <a href="/wiki/Bad_Hersfeld" title="Bad Hersfeld">Hersfeld</a>) was the first permanent <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">archbishop of Mainz</a>, succeeding Saint <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a>, and first abbot of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld_Abbey" title="Hersfeld Abbey">Hersfeld Abbey</a>. He is historiographically considered the first official sovereign of the <a href="/wiki/Electorate_of_Mainz" title="Electorate of Mainz">Electorate of Mainz</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monk_to_archbishop">Monk to archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lullus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Monk to archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lullus was born in Wessex around 710 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-athelstan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-athelstan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a monk in the Benedictine monastery of <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury_Abbey" title="Malmesbury Abbey">Malmesbury Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>. It is possible that his earlier name was "Rehdgerus" (possible in a multitude of spellings including Ratkar, Hredgar, Raedgar, etc.).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During a pilgrimage to Rome in 737 he met Saint Boniface and decided to join him in his missionary work in northern Germany. In 738, Lullus joined the Benedictine monastery of <a href="/wiki/Fritzlar" title="Fritzlar">Fritzlar</a>, founded by Boniface in 732, where his teacher was abbot Saint <a href="/wiki/Wigbert" class="mw-redirect" title="Wigbert">Wigbert</a> who had also come from England.
</p><p>In 741, Charles Martel died, and in this year the most important phase of Boniface's career started, with Lullus as his closest assistant. Many of the biographical facts about Lullus derive from the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface#Correspondence" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface Correspondence</a>: he is attested as a deacon in 745-46, as Boniface's archdeacon in 746-47, and as priest in 751 though he was probably ordained before that. The correspondence evidences that Lullus was trusted enough to be Boniface's messenger (he went to Rome twice on his behalf) and this includes the secret negotiations over Boniface's successor at <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a>. Lullus exchanges letters (and gifts) with <a href="/wiki/Edburga_of_Minster-in-Thanet" title="Edburga of Minster-in-Thanet">Edburga of Minster-in-Thanet</a> and <a href="/wiki/Leoba" title="Leoba">Leoba</a>, among others; as the youngest of  Boniface's associates and not yet tied to a specific place, he grew to be his closest associate. Moreover, a study by <a href="/wiki/Michael_Tangl" title="Michael Tangl">Michael Tangl</a>, cited by <a href="/wiki/Theodor_Schieffer" title="Theodor Schieffer">Theodor Schieffer</a>, suggests that Boniface, whose eyesight had begun to fail him early in the 740s, may have used Lullus's services in reading and writing the letters that were such an important part of his work, and Tangl suspected that Lullus likely cooperated with Boniface on some of the most important letters.<sup id="cite_ref-schieffer_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schieffer-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>King <a href="/wiki/Pippin_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin the Younger">Pippin</a> confirmed him in 753 as bishop of Mainz and in 754 he became archbishop, as Saint Boniface resigned and appointed Lullus his successor.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Lullus became the first regular archbishop of Mainz when Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_I" title="Pope Adrian I">Hadrian I</a> granted him the pallium in about 781. He then greatly expanded his bishopric by absorbing those of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCraburg" title="Büraburg">Büraburg</a> (near Fritzlar) and <a href="/wiki/Erfurt" title="Erfurt">Erfurt</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carolingian_era">Carolingian era</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lullus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Carolingian era">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From 769 onward, Lullus promoted the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_architecture" title="Carolingian architecture">Carolingian style</a> monastery of <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld_Abbey" title="Hersfeld Abbey">Hersfeld Abbey</a>, which he succeeded in having placed under <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty" title="Carolingian dynasty">Carolingian dynasty</a> protection in 775.
</p><p>Lullus's chief accomplishment was the completion of Saint Boniface's reform of the church in the Frankish <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian Empire</a> and the successful conclusion of the <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianization</a> of the Germans in <a href="/wiki/Hesse" title="Hesse">Hesse</a>-<a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-athelstan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-athelstan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> But while Boniface had looked for a close link to Rome, Lullus sought a better understanding with the Frankish kings.
</p><p>Lullus died on 16 October 786 in Hersfeld Abbey at <a href="/wiki/Bad_Hersfeld" title="Bad Hersfeld">Bad Hersfeld</a>, and is buried in the church.<sup id="cite_ref-athelstan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-athelstan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lullus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg/220px-Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg/330px-Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg/440px-Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2446" data-file-height="1463" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hersfelder_lullusfest_entrance.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Hersfelder lullusfest entrance</div></div></div>
<p>Lullus was canonized on 7 April 852.
</p><p>The <i>Vita Lulli</i>, written by Lampert of Hersfeld (probably between 1063 and 1073) led to Lullus being venerated as a saint<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and becoming the main patron of the abbey along with Wigbert.
</p><p>Lullusfest, the oldest folk festival in Germany, marked its 1,160th birthday in 2012. The festival celebrates the founding of the city of Bad Hersfeld. Founded more than 1,275 years ago, the city still reveres St Lullus, who left Malmesbury in the 730s on a mission to convert the German tribes to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
