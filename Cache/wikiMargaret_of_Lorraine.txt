<p><b>Margaret of Lorraine, Duchess of Alençon</b> (1463 at the castle of <a href="/wiki/Vaud%C3%A9mont" title="Vaudémont">Vaudémont</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lorraine_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorraine (province)">Lorraine</a> – 2 November 1521 in <a href="/wiki/Argentan" title="Argentan">Argentan</a>, Normandy) was a French <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">noblewoman</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> of the order of <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> (<i>Ordre des Clarisses</i>). She was beatified in 1921.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Lorraine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret was born in 1463 in Vaudémont Castle, <a href="/wiki/Lorraine" title="Lorraine">Lorraine</a> as the youngest daughter of <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Count_of_Vaud%C3%A9mont" title="Frederick II, Count of Vaudémont">Frederick II, Count of Vaudémont</a> and <a href="/wiki/Yolande_d%27Anjou" class="mw-redirect" title="Yolande d&#39;Anjou">Yolande d'Anjou</a>. She lost her father when she was the age of seven, and was brought up at <a href="/wiki/Aix-en-Provence" title="Aix-en-Provence">Aix-en-Provence</a> by her grandfather <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Anjou" title="René of Anjou">René of Anjou</a>. When latter died in 1480, she was sent back to Lorraine to her brother, <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_II" class="mw-redirect" title="René II">René II</a>. He arranged her marriage to <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Alen%C3%A7on" class="mw-redirect" title="René of Alençon">René, Duke of Alençon</a>, whom she wed in <a href="/wiki/Toul" title="Toul">Toul</a> on 14 May 1488.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Alençon and Margaret had three children:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Charles_IV_of_Alen%C3%A7on" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles IV of Alençon">Charles IV of Alençon</a> (1489–1525), married <a href="/wiki/Marguerite_de_Navarre" title="Marguerite de Navarre">Marguerite of Angoulême</a> as her first husband.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Fran%C3%A7oise_of_Alen%C3%A7on" class="mw-redirect" title="Françoise of Alençon">Françoise of Alençon</a>, Duchess of Beaumont (1490- 14 September 1550), married firstly in 1505, François, Duke of Longueville; married secondly in 1513, <a href="/wiki/Charles_de_Bourbon,_Duke_of_Vend%C3%B4me" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme">Charles de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme</a>, by whom she had thirteen children.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Alen%C3%A7on" title="Anne of Alençon">Anne, Lady of La Guerche</a> (30 October 1492- 18 October 1562), married in 1508, <a href="/wiki/William_IX,_Marquess_of_Montferrat" class="mw-redirect" title="William IX, Marquess of Montferrat">William IX Palaeologos</a>, Marquis of <a href="/wiki/Montferrat" title="Montferrat">Montferrat</a> by whom she had three children.</li></ul>
<p>Margaret died on November 2, 1521 in <a href="/wiki/Argentan" title="Argentan">Argentan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> from natural causes. 
</p><p>Almost 400 years after her death, she was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on March 10, 1921 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Widowhood">Widowhood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Lorraine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Widowhood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Left a widow in 1492, she busied herself in the administration of her duchy and the education of her children. When she was relieved of the duties imposed upon her by her position, she decided to renounce the world and retired to Mortagne, to a monastery of religious women who followed the rule of Saint Elizabeth. Later, having brought with her to Argentan some of these nuns, she founded there another monastery which she placed, with the authorization of the pope, under the rule of <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Saint Clare</a>, modified by the Minor Observants.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She herself took the religious habit in this house and made her vows on 11 October 1520. On 2 November 1521, after having lived an austere life for a year, she died in her modest cell, at the age of fifty-eight. Her body, preserved in the monastery of the Poor Clares, and when that monastery was suppressed, was transferred to the church of Saint Germain d'Argentan. In 1793, during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, it was profaned and thrown into the common burial place.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The memory of Margaret of Lorraine is preserved in the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrologium_franciscanum&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Martyrologium franciscanum (page does not exist)">Martyrologium franciscanum</a></i> and in the <i>Martyrologium gallicanum</i>. After an invitation made by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_S%C3%A9ez" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Séez">bishop of Séez</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacques_Camus_de_Pontcarr%C3%A9&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jacques Camus de Pontcarré (page does not exist)">Jacques Camus de Pontcarré</a>, <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIII" title="Louis XIII">Louis XIII</a> asked <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> to order a canonical inquiry into the virtues and the miracles of the Duchess.
</p>
