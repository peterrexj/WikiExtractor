<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Paul of Narbonne</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">3rd century AD</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">March 22<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Paul of Narbonne</b> (3rd century AD) was one of the "apostles to the <a href="/wiki/Gauls" title="Gauls">Gauls</a>" sent out (probably under the direction of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Fabian" title="Pope Fabian">Pope Fabian</a>, 236–250) during the consulate of <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> and Gratus (250-251 AD) to Christianize <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> after the persecutions under <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Decius" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Decius">Emperor Decius</a> had all but dissolved the small <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> communities. According to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a>, Fabian sent out seven bishops from <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to Gaul to preach the Gospel: <a href="/wiki/Gatianus_of_Tours" title="Gatianus of Tours">Gatien</a> to <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trophimus_of_Arles" title="Trophimus of Arles">Trophimus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a>, <b>Paul</b> to <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saturnin" title="Saturnin">Saturnin</a> to <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris" title="Saint Denis of Paris">Denis</a> to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austromoine" title="Austromoine">Austromoine</a> to <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Martial" title="Saint Martial">Martial</a> to Limoges.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> (<i>Historia Francorum</i> I, 30), using the <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/acta" class="extiw" title="wikt:acta">acta</a></i> of Saturninus, affirms that Paul was among those priests consecrated at Rome and sent to replant the Christian communities in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>.  Saturninus of Toulouse and Dionysius (Denis) of Paris were martyred, but Paul survived to establish the church at Narbonne as its first bishop and die in peace. The claim of <a href="/wiki/Prudentius" title="Prudentius">Prudentius</a> that Paul's association with the city of Narbonne had made it famous may be read as literary <a href="/wiki/Hyperbole" title="Hyperbole">hyperbole</a>. There is a brief <i>Vita Antiqua</i>  perhaps of the 6th century, which has been edited by the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a>. It tells that Paul converted the inhabitants of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9ziers" title="Béziers">Béziers</a>, setting over them a bishop, <a href="/wiki/Aphrodisius" title="Aphrodisius">Aphrodisius</a>, before turning his attention to Narbonne, where he founded two churches. An anecdote recounts how two of his acolytes set a woman's slippers at the foot of his bed, to accuse him of improprieties, but Paul was able miraculously to confound and pardon them.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Identification_with_Sergius_Paulus">Identification with Sergius Paulus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_of_Narbonne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Identification with Sergius Paulus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Medieval <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">legends</a> moved the seven apostles of Gaul back in time to the apostolic generation (see especially <a href="/wiki/Saint_Martial" title="Saint Martial">Martial of Limoges</a>), in order to strengthen local traditions with apostolic connections; such a legend identified third-century Paul with the Roman proconsul <a href="/wiki/Sergius_Paulus" title="Sergius Paulus">Sergius Paulus</a>, who was converted by <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> (<a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Acts#13:6" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Acts">Acts 13:6–13</a>). The historical Paul is still venerated in Narbonne as <b>Saint Paul-Serge</b> through this connection.  He is said to have been accompanied by Aphrodisius, who later became the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_B%C3%A9ziers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Béziers">bishop of Béziers</a> and whom developing tradition identified as the man who sheltered the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Family" title="Holy Family">Holy Family</a> during their <a href="/wiki/Flight_into_Egypt" title="Flight into Egypt">flight into Egypt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a> the <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> of Saint-Paul-Serge is a collegiate church built over the burial site of Paul, the first bishop. The site is at the border of an extensive <a href="/wiki/Necropolis" title="Necropolis">necropolis</a> of early Christian burials. The early shrine was a small basilica, 12 meters by 6.5 meters, built in the 4th century, destroyed by fire in the 5th century, then occupied by a monastery. As a place of pilgrimage first mentioned in 782 it was rebuilt more than once, most recently between 1180<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and 1200, during a period of renewed urban prosperity, reusing old materials and always retaining its ancient foundations. After a fire, work on rebuilding the choir resumed from 1224. The paleochristian <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypts</a> survive, with 2nd-3rd century mosaic flooring and <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagi</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Grenou-1-.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Grenou-1-.jpg/150px-Grenou-1-.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="113" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Grenou-1-.jpg/225px-Grenou-1-.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Grenou-1-.jpg/300px-Grenou-1-.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Grenou-1-.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Paul's Frog. Basilica of Saint-Paul, Narbonne.</div></div></div>
<p>The basilica became the center of the <i>Bourg Saint Paul</i> sited somewhat apart from the Roman citadel of Narbonne, protected by its own walls and retaining its own separate consuls. "Saint Paul's frog", recognizable in the veinings of a marble <a href="/wiki/Baptismal_font" title="Baptismal font">stoup</a>, has given rise to fanciful anecdotes.
</p>
