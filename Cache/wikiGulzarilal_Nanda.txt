<p><b>Gulzarilal Nanda</b> (4 July 1898 – 15 January 1998)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was an Indian politician and economist who specialised in <a href="/wiki/Labor_economics" class="mw-redirect" title="Labor economics">labour issues</a>. He was the Interim <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_India" title="Prime Minister of India">Prime Minister of India</a> for two 13-day tenures following the deaths of <a href="/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru" title="Jawaharlal Nehru">Jawaharlal Nehru</a> in 1964 and <a href="/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri" title="Lal Bahadur Shastri">Lal Bahadur Shastri</a> in 1966 respectively. Both his terms ended after the ruling <a href="/wiki/Indian_National_Congress" title="Indian National Congress">Indian National Congress</a>'s parliamentary party elected a new prime minister. He was awarded the <a href="/wiki/Bharat_Ratna" title="Bharat Ratna">Bharat Ratna</a>, India's highest civilian award, in 1997.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth">Birth</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Birth">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Nanda was born on 4 July 1898 in <a href="/wiki/Sialkot" title="Sialkot">Sialkot</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Punjab_Province_(British_India)" title="Punjab Province (British India)">Punjab</a>, <a href="/wiki/British_India" class="mw-redirect" title="British India">British India</a> in a <a href="/wiki/Punjabi_Hindu" class="mw-redirect" title="Punjabi Hindu">Punjabi Hindu</a> <a href="/wiki/Khatri" title="Khatri">Khatri</a> family.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Sialkot later became a part of the <a href="/wiki/Punjab_(Pakistan)" class="mw-redirect" title="Punjab (Pakistan)">Punjab Province of Pakistan</a> in 1947, after the partition of <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>. Nanda received his education in <a href="/wiki/Lahore" title="Lahore">Lahore</a>, <a href="/wiki/Amritsar" title="Amritsar">Amritsar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Agra" title="Agra">Agra</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Allahabad" class="mw-redirect" title="Allahabad">Allahabad</a>.
</p><p>He met Mahatma Gandhi in 1921, where he settled in Gujarat on his request.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2011)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Research_worker">Research worker</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Research worker">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Nanda worked as a research scholar on labour problems at <a href="/wiki/Allahabad_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Allahabad University">Allahabad University</a> (1920–1921), and became a professor of economics at National College in <a href="/wiki/Bombay" class="mw-redirect" title="Bombay">Bombay</a> (<a href="/wiki/Mumbai" title="Mumbai">Mumbai</a>) in 1921.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2013)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The same year, he joined the Indian <a href="/wiki/Non-Cooperation_Movement" class="mw-redirect" title="Non-Cooperation Movement">Non-Cooperation Movement</a> against the British Raj. In 1922, he became secretary of the Ahmedabad <a href="/wiki/Textile_Labour_Association" class="mw-redirect" title="Textile Labour Association">Textile Labour Association</a> where he worked until 1946. He was imprisoned for <a href="/wiki/Satyagraha" title="Satyagraha">Satyagraha</a> in 1932, and again from 1942 to 1944.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2013)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>. He was honoured with "Proud Past Alumni" in the list of 42 members, from "Allahabad University Alumni Association", NCR, Ghaziabad (Greater Noida) Chapter 2007–2008 registered under society act 1860 with registration no. 407/2000.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He married Lakshmi, with whom he had two sons and a daughter.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Lok_Sabha_member">Lok Sabha member</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Lok Sabha member">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nanda was elected to the Lok Sabha in the 1957 elections, and was appointed Union Minister for Labour, Employment and Planning, and later, as Deputy Chairman of the Planning Commission. He visited the <a href="/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Federal Republic of Germany">Federal Republic of Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/Yugoslavia" title="Yugoslavia">Yugoslavia</a>, and Austria in 1959.
</p><p>Nanda was re-elected to the Lok Sabha in the 1962 elections from the <a href="/wiki/Sabarkantha" class="mw-redirect" title="Sabarkantha">Sabarkantha</a> constituency in <a href="/wiki/Gujarat" title="Gujarat">Gujarat</a>. He initiated the Congress Forum for Socialist Action in 1962. He was Union Minister for Labour and Employment in 1962–1963, and <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Home_Affairs" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Home Affairs">Minister for Home Affairs</a> in 1963–1966.
</p><p>Nanda was re-elected to the Lok Sabha in the 1967 and 1971 elections from the <a href="/wiki/Kaithal_(Lok_Sabha_Constituency)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kaithal (Lok Sabha Constituency)">Kaithal (Lok Sabha Constituency)</a> in <a href="/wiki/Haryana" title="Haryana">Haryana</a>. He was a principled man. In 1971, he resigned from the Congress saying that he did not like the politics of that era. <sup id="cite_ref-5-ls_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5-ls-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template noprint noexcerpt Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:NOTRS" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:NOTRS"><span title="This claim needs references to better sources. (January 2022)">better&#160;source&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Interim_Prime_Minister">Interim Prime Minister</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Interim Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nanda was the Interim Prime Minister of India twice for thirteen days each: the first time after the death of first Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru" title="Jawaharlal Nehru">Jawaharlal Nehru</a> in 1964, and the second time after the death of Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri" title="Lal Bahadur Shastri">Lal Bahadur Shastri</a> in 1966.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He was the Home Minister of India during both these periods, and this is the reason why he was chosen as Prime Minister. The Indian Constitution has no provision of Acting Prime Minister as is widely circulated but incorrectly. Both his terms were uneventful, yet they came at sensitive times because of the potential danger to the country following Nehru's death soon after a war with China in 1962 and Shastri's death after a war with Pakistan in 1965.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Nanda died on 15 January 1998 at the age of 99; from 25 November 1997 when former <a href="/wiki/Malawian" class="mw-redirect" title="Malawian">Malawian</a> President <a href="/wiki/Hastings_Banda" title="Hastings Banda">Hastings Banda</a> died; until his own death Nanda was the oldest living former state leader.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> At his death, Nanda was the last surviving member of the second and third Nehru cabinets and the last living state leader to have been born in the 19th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Gandhian_life">Gandhian life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Gandhian life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He lived a very simple life, with no personal property at the time of his death. He never allowed politics to influence his family life. Yet he always had enough time for his family. He once engaged his government provided driver for a car that was used by his family. He never allowed his family to use his official vehicle. He once got angry with his staff when he learned that his grandson, Tejas used his office paper and drew a picture. He immediately purchased paper from market and gave his grandson to draw on it.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was also concerned about rising corruption in the country and suggested to decrease wasteful consumption by officials and people in general. He had also opposed Indira Gandhi's Emergency, as he felt that the sacrifices to bring democracy to India became meaningless due to the tyranny.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gulzarilal_Nanda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
