<p><b>Martha</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>: מָרְתָא‎) is a <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">biblical</a> figure described in the <a href="/wiki/Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospels">Gospels</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke" title="Gospel of Luke">Luke</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">John</a>. Together with her siblings <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany" title="Lazarus of Bethany">Lazarus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Bethany" title="Mary of Bethany">Mary of Bethany</a>, she is described as living in the village of <a href="/wiki/Bethany" title="Bethany">Bethany</a> near <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. She was witness to <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a> resurrecting her brother, <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany" title="Lazarus of Bethany">Lazarus</a>. 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r886046785">.mw-parser-output .toclimit-2 .toclevel-1 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-3 .toclevel-2 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-4 .toclevel-3 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-5 .toclevel-4 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-6 .toclevel-5 ul,.mw-parser-output .toclimit-7 .toclevel-6 ul{display:none}</style><div class="toclimit-2"><meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" /></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Etymology_of_the_name">Etymology of the name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Etymology of the name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name <i>Martha</i> is a <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> transliteration of the <a href="/wiki/Koine_Greek" title="Koine Greek">Koine Greek</a> Μάρθα, itself a transliteration of the <a href="/wiki/Aramaic" title="Aramaic">Aramaic</a> מָרְתָא‎ <i>Mârtâ,</i> "the mistress" or "the lady", from מרה "mistress," feminine of מר "master." The Aramaic form occurs in a <a href="/wiki/Nabatean" class="mw-redirect" title="Nabatean">Nabatean</a> inscription found at <a href="/wiki/Puteoli" class="mw-redirect" title="Puteoli">Puteoli</a>, and now in the <a href="/wiki/Naples_Museum" class="mw-redirect" title="Naples Museum">Naples Museum</a>; it is dated AD 5 (Corpus Inscr. Semit., 158); also in a <a href="/wiki/Tadmor,_Syria" class="mw-redirect" title="Tadmor, Syria">Palmyrene</a> inscription, where the Greek translation has the form <i>Marthein.</i>
<sup id="cite_ref-Pope_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pope-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biblical_references">Biblical references</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Biblical references">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke" title="Gospel of Luke">Gospel of Luke</a>, Jesus <a href="/wiki/Jesus_at_the_home_of_Martha_and_Mary" title="Jesus at the home of Martha and Mary">visits the home of two sisters named Mary and Martha</a>. The two sisters are contrasted: Martha was "encumbered about many things" while Jesus was their guest, while Mary had chosen "the better part", that of listening to the master's discourse.<sup id="cite_ref-Easton_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Easton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The name of their village is not recorded, nor (unlike in John 11:18) is there any mention of whether Jesus was near Jerusalem. Biblical commentator <a href="/wiki/Heinrich_August_Wilhelm_Meyer" title="Heinrich August Wilhelm Meyer">Heinrich Meyer</a> notes that "Jesus cannot yet be in <a href="/wiki/Bethany" title="Bethany">Bethany</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> where Martha and Mary dwelt [according to John's Gospel]".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> But the <a href="/wiki/Cambridge_Bible_for_Schools_and_Colleges" title="Cambridge Bible for Schools and Colleges">Cambridge Bible for Schools and Colleges</a> claims that it was "undoubtedly Bethany".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>As Jesus and his disciples were on their way, he came to a village where a woman named Martha opened her home to him. She had a sister called Mary, who sat at the Lord's feet listening to what he said. But Martha was distracted by all the preparations that had to be made. She came to him and asked, "Lord, don’t you care that my sister has left me to do the work by myself? Tell her to help me!"<br />
"Martha, Martha," the Lord answered, "you are worried and upset about many things, but only one thing is needed. Mary has chosen what is better, and it will not be taken away from her."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg/300px-Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="189" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg/450px-Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg/600px-Semiradsky_Christ_Martha_Maria.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1740" data-file-height="1099" /></a><figcaption>Christ in the house of Martha and <a href="/wiki/Mary,_sister_of_Lazarus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary, sister of Lazarus">Mary</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Henryk_Siemiradzki" title="Henryk Siemiradzki">Henryk Siemiradzki</a>, 1886</figcaption></figure>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">Gospel of John</a>, Martha and Mary appear in connection with two incidents: the <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany#Raising_of_Lazarus" title="Lazarus of Bethany">raising from the dead</a> of their brother Lazarus (John 11) and the <a href="/wiki/Anointing_of_Jesus" title="Anointing of Jesus">anointing of Jesus</a> in Bethany (John 12:3).
</p><p>In the account of the raising of Lazarus, Jesus meets with the sisters in turn: Martha followed by Mary. Martha goes immediately to meet Jesus as he arrives, while Mary waits until she is called. As one commentator notes, "Martha, the more aggressive sister, went to meet Jesus, while quiet and contemplative Mary stayed home. This portrayal of the sisters agrees with that found in Luke 10:38–42."<sup id="cite_ref-Zondervan_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Zondervan-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In speaking with Jesus, both sisters lament that he did not arrive in time to prevent their brother's death: "Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> But where Jesus' response to Mary is more emotional, his response to Martha is one of teaching, calling her to hope and faith:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>When Martha heard that Jesus was coming, she went out to meet him, but Mary stayed at home. "Lord", Martha said to Jesus, "if you had been here, my brother would not have died. But I know that even now God will give you whatever you ask."
</p><p><br />Jesus said to her, "Your brother will rise again."
<br />Martha answered, "I know he will rise again in the resurrection at the last day."
<br />Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will live, even though he dies; and whoever lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?"
</p>
<p><br />"Yes, Lord", she told him, "I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who was to come into the world."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>As the narrative continues, Martha calls her sister Mary to see Jesus. Jesus has Mary bring him to Lazarus' tomb where he commands the stone to be removed from its entrance. Martha here objects, "But, Lord, by this time there is a bad odor, for he has been there four days", to which Jesus replies, "Did I not tell you that if you believed, you would see the glory of God?".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> They then take away the stone and Jesus prays and calls Lazarus forth alive from the tomb.
</p><p>Martha appears again in John 12:1–8, where she serves at a meal held in Jesus' honor at which her brother is also a guest. The narrator only mentions that the meal takes place in Bethany, while the apparently parallel accounts in the Gospels of <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew" title="Gospel of Matthew">Matthew</a><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark" title="Gospel of Mark">Mark</a><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> specify that it takes place at the home of one <a href="/wiki/Simon_the_Leper" title="Simon the Leper">Simon the Leper</a>. As the Catholic Encyclopedia notes, "We are surely justified in arguing that, since Matthew and Mark place the scene in the house of Simon, St. John must be understood to say the same; it remains to be proven that Martha could not 'serve' in Simon's house."<sup id="cite_ref-Pope_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pope-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It is at this meal that a woman (Martha's sister Mary, according to John) <a href="/wiki/Anointing_of_Jesus" title="Anointing of Jesus">anoints Jesus</a> with expensive perfume.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Western_traditions">Western traditions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Western traditions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In medieval <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western Christianity</a>, Martha's sister Mary was often equated with <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>. This identification led to additional information being attributed to Martha as well:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Mary, Martha, and Lazarus are represented by St. John as living at Bethania, but St. Luke would seem to imply that they were, at least at one time, living in <a href="/wiki/Galilee" title="Galilee">Galilee</a>; he does not mention the name of the town, but it may have been <a href="/wiki/Magdala" title="Magdala">Magdala</a>, and we should thus, supposing Mary of Bethania and Mary Magdalene to be the same person, understand the appellative "Magdalene". The words of St. John (11:1) seem to imply a change of residence for the family. It is possible, too, that St. Luke has displaced the incident referred to in Chapter 10. The likeness between the pictures of Martha presented by Luke and John is very remarkable. The familiar intercourse between the Saviour of the world and the humble family which St. Luke depicts is dwelt on by St. John when he tells us that "Jesus loved Martha, and her sister Mary, and Lazarus" (11:5). Again the picture of Martha's anxiety (John 11:20–21, 39) accords with the picture of her who was "busy about much serving" (Luke 10:40); so also in John 12:2: "They made him a supper there: and Martha served." But St. John has given us a glimpse of the other and deeper side of her character when he depicts her growing faith in Christ's Divinity (11:20–27), a faith which was the occasion of the words: "I am the resurrection and the life." The Evangelist has beautifully indicated the change that came over Martha after that interview: "When she had said these things, she went and called her sister Mary secretly, saying: The Master is come, and calleth for thee."<sup id="cite_ref-Pope_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pope-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Eastern_Orthodox_tradition">Eastern Orthodox tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Eastern Orthodox tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> tradition, though not specifically named as such in the gospels, Martha and Mary were among the <a href="/wiki/Myrrh-bearers" class="mw-redirect" title="Myrrh-bearers">Myrrh-bearing Women</a>. These faithful followers of Jesus stood at <a href="/wiki/Golgotha" class="mw-redirect" title="Golgotha">Golgotha</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus" title="Crucifixion of Jesus">Crucifixion of Jesus</a> and later came to his tomb early on the morning following <a href="/wiki/Biblical_Sabbath" title="Biblical Sabbath">Sabbath</a> with <a href="/wiki/Myrrh" title="Myrrh">myrrh</a> (expensive oil), according to the Jewish tradition, to anoint their Lord's body. The <a href="/wiki/Myrrhbearers" title="Myrrhbearers">Myrrhbearers</a> became the first witnesses to the <a href="/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus" title="Resurrection of Jesus">Resurrection of Jesus</a>, finding the empty tomb and hearing the joyful news from an angel.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Orthodox tradition also relates that Martha's brother Lazarus was cast out of Jerusalem in the persecution against the Jerusalem Church following the martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Stephen">St. Stephen</a>. His sister Martha fled Judea with him, assisting him in the proclaiming of the Gospel in various lands,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> while Mary Magdalene remained with <a href="/wiki/John_the_Apostle" title="John the Apostle">John the Apostle</a> and assisted him with the Church of Jerusalem. 
According to Cyprian tradition, Lazarus and Martha later came to <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>, where Lazarus became the first Bishop of Kittim (modern <a href="/wiki/Larnaca" title="Larnaca">Larnaca</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> All three died in <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Martha is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and commemorated by the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran Church">Lutheran Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>.
Through time, as the reverence for St. Martha developed, the images of maturity, strength, common sense, and concern for others predominated.<sup id="cite_ref-antigonish_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antigonish-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_days">Feast days</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Feast days">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a> celebrates the feast day Martha, <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Bethany" title="Mary of Bethany">Mary of Bethany</a> and her brother <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany" title="Lazarus of Bethany">Lazarus</a> of 29 July.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The feast of Martha, classified as a "semi-double" in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>, became a "simple" one in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_Pope_Pius_XII" title="General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII">General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII</a>, a "third class feast" in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a> in the present <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a> <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a> commemorate Martha and her sister Mary on 4 June. They also commemorate them collectively among the <a href="/wiki/Myrrhbearers" title="Myrrhbearers">Myrrh-bearing Women</a> on the Sunday of the Myrrhbearers (the Third Sunday of <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Pascha</a> – i.e., the second Sunday after Easter Sunday). Martha also figures in the commemorations of <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_Saturday" title="Lazarus Saturday">Lazarus Saturday</a> (the day before <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a>).
</p><p>Martha is commemorated on 29 July in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints_(Lutheran)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints (Lutheran)">Calendar of Saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran Church">Lutheran Church</a>, together with Mary and Lazarus and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">Calendar of saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Martha is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> (with <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Bethany" title="Mary of Bethany">Mary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_of_Bethany" title="Lazarus of Bethany">Lazarus</a>) in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/July_29" title="July 29">29 July</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends_and_myths">Legends and myths</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Legends and myths">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Golden_Legend">Golden Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Golden Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to legend, St. Martha left <a href="/wiki/Judea" title="Judea">Judea</a> after <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>' resurrection, around AD 48, and went to <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a> with her sister <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Bethany" title="Mary of Bethany">Mary</a> (conflated with <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>) and her brother Lazarus. With them, Martha first settled in <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> (now in France). The <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i>, compiled in the 13th century, records the Provençal tradition:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Saint Martha, hostess of our Lord Jesus Christ, was born of a royal kindred. Her father was named Syro and her mother Encharia. The father of her was duke of <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a> and places maritime, and Martha with her sister possessed by the heritage of their mother three places, that was, the castle Magdalen, and Bethany and a part of Jerusalem. It is nowhere read that Martha had ever any husband nor fellowship of man, but she as a noble hostess ministered and served our Lord, and would also that her sister should serve him and help her, for she thought that all the world was not sufficient to serve such a guest.<br /><br />After the ascension of our Lord, when the disciples were departed, she with her brother Lazarus and her sister Mary, also Saint <a href="/wiki/Maximin_of_Trier" title="Maximin of Trier">Maximin</a> [actually a 3rd-century figure] which baptized them, and to whom they were committed of the Holy Ghost, and many others, were put into a ship without sail, oars, or rudder governail, of the paynims, which by the conduct of our Lord they came all to <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a>, and after came to the territory of Aquense or <a href="/wiki/Aix-en-Provence" title="Aix-en-Provence">Aix</a>, and there converted the people to the faith. Martha was right facound of speech, and courteous and gracious to the sight of the people.<sup id="cite_ref-Golden_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Golden-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The <i>Golden Legend</i> also records the grand lifestyle imagined for Martha and her siblings in its entry on Mary Magdalene:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Mary Magdalene had her surname of Magdala, a castle, and was born of right noble lineage and parents, which were descended of the lineage of kings. And her father was named Cyrus, and her mother Eucharis. She with her brother Lazarus, and her sister Martha, possessed the castle of Magdala, which is two miles from Nazareth, and Bethany, the castle which is nigh to Jerusalem, and also a great part of Jerusalem, which, all these things they departed among them. In such wise that Mary had the castle Magdala, whereof she had her name Magdalene. And Lazarus had the part of the city of Jerusalem, and Martha had to her part Bethany. And when Mary gave herself to all delights of the body, and Lazarus entended all to knighthood, Martha, which was wise, governed nobly her brother's part and also her sister's, and also her own, and administered to knights, and her servants, and to poor men, such necessities as they needed. Nevertheless, after the ascension of our Lord, they sold all these things.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Martha_in_Tarascon">St. Martha in Tarascon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: St. Martha in Tarascon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Taming_the_Tarasque,_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Taming_the_Tarasque%2C_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg/220px-Taming_the_Tarasque%2C_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="159" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Taming_the_Tarasque%2C_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg/330px-Taming_the_Tarasque%2C_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Taming_the_Tarasque%2C_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg/440px-Taming_the_Tarasque%2C_from_Hours_of_Henry_VIII.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2072" data-file-height="1500" /></a><figcaption>Martha with the Tarasque, from the <a href="/wiki/Hours_of_Henry_VIII" title="Hours of Henry VIII">Hours of Henry VIII</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>A further legend relates that Martha then went to <a href="/wiki/Tarascon" title="Tarascon">Tarascon</a>, France, where a <a href="/wiki/Monster" title="Monster">monster</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Tarasque" title="Tarasque">Tarasque</a>, was a constant threat to the population. The <i>Golden Legend</i> describes it as a beast from Galatia; a great <a href="/wiki/Dragon" title="Dragon">dragon</a>, half beast and half fish, greater than an ox, longer than a horse, having teeth sharp as a sword, and horned on either side, head like a lion, tail like a serpent, that dwelt in a certain wood between <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a> and <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a>. Holding a cross in her hand, Martha sprinkled the beast with holy water. Placing her sash around its neck, she led the tamed dragon through the village.<sup id="cite_ref-antigonish_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antigonish-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg/220px-Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg/330px-Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg/440px-Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon_04.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4160" data-file-height="3120" /></a><figcaption>Gothic Tomb of Saint Martha in Tarascon</figcaption></figure>
<p>There Martha lived, daily occupied in prayers and in fastings. Martha eventually died in Tarascon, where she was buried. Her tomb is located in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> of the <a href="/wiki/%C3%89glise_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon" title="Église Sainte-Marthe de Tarascon">local Collegiate Church</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_(Tarascon).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_%28Tarascon%29.jpg/170px-%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_%28Tarascon%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_%28Tarascon%29.jpg/255px-%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_%28Tarascon%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_%28Tarascon%29.jpg/340px-%C3%89glise_Coll%C3%A9giale_Sainte_Marthe_%28Tarascon%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="614" data-file-height="819" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/%C3%89glise_Sainte-Marthe_de_Tarascon" title="Église Sainte-Marthe de Tarascon">St Martha's Collegiate Church in Tarascon</a></figcaption></figure><p>The dedication of the Collegiate Church at Tarascon to St. Martha is believed to date from the 9th century or earlier. <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">Relics</a> found in the church during a reconstruction in 1187 were identified as hers, and reburied in a new shrine at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> In the Collegiate Church crypt is a late 15th-century <a href="/wiki/Cenotaph" title="Cenotaph">cenotaph</a>, also known as the Gothic Tomb of Saint Martha. It is the work of <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Laurana" title="Francesco Laurana">Francesco Laurana</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatian</a> sculptor of the Italian School, commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_of_Anjou" title="René of Anjou">King René</a>. At its base are two openings through which the relics could be touched. It bears three low reliefs separated by fluted pilasters representing: on the left, Saint Martha and the Tarasque; in the center, Saint Mary Magdalene borne aloft by the angels; on the right, Lazarus as Bishop of Marseille with his mitre and staff. There are two figures on either side: on the left, Saint Front, Bishop of Perrigueux, present at the funeral of Saint Martha, and on the right, Saint Marcelle, Martha's servant.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h3><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Martha_and_Villajoyosa">St. Martha and Villajoyosa</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: St. Martha and Villajoyosa">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The town of <a href="/wiki/Villajoyosa" title="Villajoyosa">Villajoyosa</a>, Spain, honors St. Martha as its patron saint and celebrates The Festival of <a href="/wiki/Moros_y_Cristianos" class="mw-redirect" title="Moros y Cristianos">Moors and Christians</a> annually in her honor. The 250-year-old festival commemorates the attack on Villajoyosa by Berber pirates led by Zalé-Arraez in 1538, when, according to legend, St. Martha came to the rescue of the townsfolk by causing a flash flood which wiped out the enemy fleet, thus preventing the corsairs from reaching the coast.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Gnostic_tradition">Gnostic tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Gnostic tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Martha appears in the sacred <a href="/wiki/Gnostic" class="mw-redirect" title="Gnostic">gnostic</a> text <i><a href="/wiki/Pistis_Sophia" title="Pistis Sophia">Pistis Sophia</a></i>. She is instructed by the risen Christ on several of the repentances that must be made in order to have salvation. She also makes several prophetic interpretations of different <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Depictions_in_art_and_literature">Depictions in art and literature</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Depictions in art and literature">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa,_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/220px-Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="128" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/330px-Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/440px-Cristo_en_casa_de_Marta_y_Mar%C3%ADa%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg 2x" data-file-width="7290" data-file-height="4226" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Christ_in_the_House_of_Martha_and_Mary_(Vel%C3%A1zquez)" title="Christ in the House of Martha and Mary (Velázquez)"><i>Christ in the House of Martha and Mary</i></a>, <a href="/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez" title="Diego Velázquez">Diego Velázquez</a>, 1618</figcaption></figure>
<p>The subject of Martha is mostly found in art from the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a> onwards, especially in the 17th century, when the domestic setting is usually given a realistic depiction. However it appears in some <a href="/wiki/Ottonian_art" title="Ottonian art">Ottonian</a> cycles of the <i><a href="/wiki/Life_of_Christ_in_art" title="Life of Christ in art">Life of Christ</a></i>.
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Christ_in_the_House_of_Martha_and_Mary_(Vel%C3%A1zquez)" title="Christ in the House of Martha and Mary (Velázquez)"><i>Christ in the House of Martha and Mary</i> (Velázquez)</a>, a 1618 oil-on-canvas painting by the Spanish painter Velázquez.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Christ_in_the_House_of_Martha_and_Mary_(Vermeer)" title="Christ in the House of Martha and Mary (Vermeer)"><i>Christ in the House of Martha and Mary</i> (Vermeer)</a>, a 1655 painting by <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Vermeer" title="Johannes Vermeer">Johannes Vermeer</a>.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Martha_and_Mary_Magdalene_(Caravaggio)" title="Martha and Mary Magdalene (Caravaggio)"><i>Martha and Mary Magdalene</i> (Caravaggio)</a>, a 1598-9 painting by the Italian Baroque master <a href="/wiki/Michelangelo_Merisi_da_Caravaggio" class="mw-redirect" title="Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio">Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio</a>.</li></ul>
<p>Literary works about Martha include: 
</p>
<ul><li><i>Martha and Mary</i>, a story in <a href="/wiki/Karel_%C4%8Capek" title="Karel Čapek">Karel Čapek</a>'s <i>Apocryphal Tales</i> (1932)<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"Saint Martha and the Dragon", a poem by <a href="/wiki/Charles_Causley" title="Charles Causley">Charles Causley</a> in his collected works.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i><a href="/wiki/Sons_of_Martha" class="mw-redirect" title="Sons of Martha">The Sons of Martha</a></i> (1907), a poem by <a href="/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling" title="Rudyard Kipling">Rudyard Kipling</a>.</li>
<li>In <i><a href="/wiki/The_Handmaid%27s_Tale" title="The Handmaid&#39;s Tale">The Handmaid's Tale</a></i>, the dystopian novel of <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Atwood" title="Margaret Atwood">Margaret Atwood</a>, infertile women forced to be servants for the ruling class are called "Marthas", as their service is considered imitating Martha.</li></ul>
