<p><b>Bruno of Querfurt</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 974 – 14 February or 9/14 March 1009), also known as <i>Brun</i> and <i>Boniface</i>, was a Christian <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a>, who was beheaded near the border of <a href="/wiki/Kievan_Rus" class="mw-redirect" title="Kievan Rus">Kievan Rus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a> for trying to spread Christianity. He is also called the second "Apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Old_Prussians" title="Old Prussians">Prussians</a>".
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Querfurt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Querfurt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bruno was from a noble family of <a href="/wiki/Querfurt" title="Querfurt">Querfurt</a> (now in  <a href="/wiki/Saxony-Anhalt" title="Saxony-Anhalt">Saxony-Anhalt</a>). He is rumored to have been a relative of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Otto_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto III</a>. At the age of six, he was sent to be educated at the cathedral school in <a href="/wiki/Magdeburg" title="Magdeburg">Magdeburg</a>, seat of <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Magdeburg" title="Adalbert of Magdeburg">Adalbert of Magdeburg</a>, the teacher and namesake of <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Adalbert of Prague</a>. While still a youth, he was made a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Magdeburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Magdeburg">Cathedral of Magdeburg</a>. The fifteen-year-old Otto III made Bruno a part of his royal court. In 995 Otto III appointed Bruno as his court chaplain.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for Otto's imperial coronation, Bruno met <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Adalbert of Prague</a>, the first "Apostle of the Prussians", killed a year later, which inspired Bruno to write a biography of Adalbert when he reached the <a href="/wiki/Christianization#Hungary" title="Christianization">recently Christianized</a> and consolidated <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a> himself. Bruno spent much time at the monastery where Adalbert had become a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and where abbot <a href="/wiki/John_Canaparius" title="John Canaparius">John Canaparius</a> may have written a life of Adalbert. In 998, Bruno entered a Benedictine monastery near <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> that Otto had founded, and later underwent strict <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> training under the guidance of <a href="/wiki/Romuald" title="Romuald">Romuald</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_life">Missionary life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Querfurt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Missionary life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:452px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg/450px-Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg" decoding="async" width="450" height="353" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg/675px-Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg/900px-Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3009" data-file-height="2363" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Pomeraniae_Ducatus_Tabula.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The lands given Bruno in 1003 were in the region shown on left bank of the Oder, <a href="/wiki/Hither_Pomerania" class="mw-redirect" title="Hither Pomerania">Hither Pomerania</a> along the coast, or the future <a href="/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg" title="Margraviate of Brandenburg">Margraviate of Brandenburg</a> to its south.</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Otto_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto III</a> hoped to establish a monastery between the <a href="/wiki/Elbe" title="Elbe">Elbe</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Oder" title="Oder">Oder</a> (somewhere in the  <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> lands that became <a href="/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg" title="Margraviate of Brandenburg">Brandenburg</a> or Western <a href="/wiki/Pomerania" title="Pomerania">Pomerania</a>) to help convert the local population to Christianity and colonize the area. In 1001, two monks from his monastery traveled to Poland, while Bruno was with Otto in Italy, studying the language and awaiting the Apostolic appointment by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Pope Sylvester II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1003 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Pope Sylvester II</a> appointed Bruno, at the age of 33, to head a mission amongst the pagan peoples of Eastern Europe. Bruno left Rome in 1004, and having been named an archbishop was consecrated in February of that year by Archbishop Tagino of Magdeburg.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Owing to a regional conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor Henry II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">Duke Boleslaus I of Poland</a>, Bruno could not go directly to Poland so he set out for Hungary. There he went to the places that Adalbert of Prague had attended.
</p><p>Bruno tried to persuade <a href="/wiki/Ahtum" class="mw-redirect" title="Ahtum">Ahtum</a>, the Duke of <a href="/wiki/Banat" title="Banat">Banat</a>, who was under jurisdiction of <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Constantinople">Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> to accept the jurisdiction of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a>, but this precipitated a large controversy leading to organized opposition from local monks. Bruno elected to gracefully exit the region after he first finished his book, the famous <i>"Life of <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Adalbert of Prague</a>,"</i> a literary memorial giving a history of the (relatively recent) conversion of the Hungarians.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After this diplomatic failure, Bruno went to <a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>, where Grand Duke <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_I,_Prince_of_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir I, Prince of Kiev">Vladimir I</a> authorized him to make Christian converts among the <a href="/wiki/Pechenegs" title="Pechenegs">Pechenegs</a>, semi-nomadic <a href="/wiki/Turkic_peoples" title="Turkic peoples">Turkic peoples</a> living between the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Don_River,_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Don River, Russia">Don</a> rivers.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bruno spent five months there and baptized some thirty adults. He helped to bring about a peace treaty between them and the ruler of Kyiv.
</p><p>Before leaving for Poland, Bruno <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> a bishop for the <a href="/wiki/Pechenegs" title="Pechenegs">Pechenegs</a>. While in Poland he consecrated the first Bishop of Sweden and is said to have sent emissaries to baptize the king of Sweden,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> whose mother had come from Poland. Bruno found out that his friend Benedict and four companions had been killed by robbers in 1003.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bruno took eyewitness accounts and wrote down a touching history of the so-called Five Martyred Brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mission_to_Prussia_and_death">Mission to Prussia and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Querfurt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Mission to Prussia and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Polska_992_-_1025.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Polska_992_-_1025.png/220px-Polska_992_-_1025.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="197" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Polska_992_-_1025.png/330px-Polska_992_-_1025.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Polska_992_-_1025.png/440px-Polska_992_-_1025.png 2x" data-file-width="4215" data-file-height="3780" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Polska_992_-_1025.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prussia_(Baltic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Prussia (Baltic)">Prussia</a> during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus I of Poland">Bolesław the Brave</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>In the autumn or at the end of 1008 Bruno and eighteen companions set out to found a mission among the <a href="/wiki/Old_Prussians" title="Old Prussians">Old Prussians</a>; they succeeded in converting Netimer and then traveled to the east, heading very likely towards <a href="/wiki/Yotvingians" title="Yotvingians">Yotvingia</a>.
</p><p>Bruno met opposition in his efforts to evangelize the borderland and when he persisted in disregarding their warnings he was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on 14 February (or 9 or 14 March) 1009, and most of his eighteen companions were hanged by Zebeden, brother of Netimer.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Duke <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_the_Brave" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus the Brave">Boleslaus the Brave</a> bought the bodies and brought them to Poland. (It was supposed that they were laid to rest in <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemyśl</a>, where some historians place Bruno's diocese; such localization of Bruno's burial place is hardly probable because Przemyśl then belonged to <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> Kievan Rus through 1018.) The <i>Annals of Magdeburg,</i> <a href="/wiki/Thietmar_of_Merseburg" title="Thietmar of Merseburg">Thietmar of Merseburg</a>'s <i>Chronicle</i>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Quedlinburg" title="Annals of Quedlinburg">Annals of Quedlinburg</a></i>, various works of <a href="/wiki/Magdeburg" title="Magdeburg">Magdeburg</a> Bishops, and many other written sources of 11th–15th centuries record this story.
</p><p>Soon after his death, Bruno and his companions were venerated as martyrs and Bruno was soon after <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>. It was said that <a href="/wiki/Braunsberg" class="mw-redirect" title="Braunsberg">Braunsberg</a> was named after Bruno.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
