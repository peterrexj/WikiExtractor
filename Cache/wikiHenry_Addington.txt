<p><b>Henry Addington, 1st Viscount Sidmouth</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a></span> (30 May 1757&#160;&#8211;&#32;15 February 1844) was a <a href="/wiki/British_Tory" class="mw-redirect" title="British Tory">British Tory</a> statesman who served as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of the United Kingdom</a> from 1801 to 1804.
</p><p>Addington is best known for obtaining the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Amiens" title="Treaty of Amiens">Treaty of Amiens</a> in 1802, an unfavourable peace with <a href="/wiki/First_French_Empire" title="First French Empire">Napoleonic France</a> which marked the end of the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Second_Coalition" title="War of the Second Coalition">Second Coalition</a> during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars" title="French Revolutionary Wars">French Revolutionary Wars</a>. When that treaty broke down he resumed the war, but he was without allies and conducted relatively weak defensive hostilities, ahead of what would become the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Third_Coalition" title="War of the Third Coalition">War of the Third Coalition</a>. He was forced from office in favour of <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a>, who had preceded Addington as Prime Minister. Addington is also known for his reactionary crackdown on advocates of democratic reforms during a ten-year spell as <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a> from 1812 to 1822. He is the longest continuously serving holder of that office since it was created in 1782.
</p>

<p>Henry Addington was the son of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Addington" title="Anthony Addington">Anthony Addington</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pitt_the_Elder" class="mw-redirect" title="Pitt the Elder">Pitt the Elder</a>'s physician; and Mary Addington, the daughter of the Rev. Haviland John Hiley, headmaster of <a href="/wiki/Reading_School" title="Reading School">Reading School</a>. As a consequence of his father's position, Addington was a childhood friend of <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a>. Addington studied at <a href="/wiki/Reading_School" title="Reading School">Reading School</a>, <a href="/wiki/Winchester_College" title="Winchester College">Winchester</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Brasenose_College,_Oxford" title="Brasenose College, Oxford">Brasenose College, Oxford</a>, and then studied law at <a href="/wiki/Lincoln%27s_Inn" title="Lincoln&#39;s Inn">Lincoln's Inn</a>. He married Ursula Mary Hammond in 1781; she brought an income of £1,000 a year into the marriage. The couple had eight children, of whom six survived to adulthood. Ursula Addington died in 1811; in 1823 Addington married a widow, Marianne Townsend, daughter of <a href="/wiki/William_Scott,_1st_Baron_Stowell" title="William Scott, 1st Baron Stowell">William Scott, 1st Baron Stowell</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_career">Political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was elected to the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Great_Britain" title="House of Commons of Great Britain">House of Commons</a> in 1784 as one of the Members of Parliament for <a href="/wiki/Devizes_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Devizes (UK Parliament constituency)">Devizes</a>, and became <a href="/wiki/Speaker_of_the_House_of_Commons_(United_Kingdom)" title="Speaker of the House of Commons (United Kingdom)">Speaker of the House of Commons</a> in 1789. In March 1801, <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a> resigned from office, ostensibly over the refusal of <a href="/wiki/George_III" title="George III">King George III</a> to remove some of the existing political restrictions on Roman Catholics in Ireland (<a href="/wiki/Catholic_Emancipation" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Emancipation">Catholic Emancipation</a>), but poor health, failure in war, economic collapse, alarming levels of social unrest due to famine, and irreconcilable divisions within the Cabinet also played a role. Both Pitt and the King insisted that Addington take over as Prime Minister, despite his own objections, and his failed attempts to reconcile the King and Pitt.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Addington_ministry" title="Addington ministry">Addington ministry</a></div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Napoleonic_Wars" title="United Kingdom in the Napoleonic Wars">United Kingdom in the Napoleonic Wars</a></div>
<p>Foreign policy was the centrepiece of his term in office. Some historians have been highly critical and said that it was ignorant and indifferent to Britain's greatest needs. However, Thomas Goldsmith argues that Addington and Hawkesbury conducted a logical, consistent and eurocentric balance-of-power policy, rooted in rules and assumptions governing their conduct, rather than a chaotic free-for-all approach.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoldsmith2016_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoldsmith2016-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Addington's domestic reforms doubled the efficiency of the <a href="/wiki/Income_tax" title="Income tax">income tax</a>. In foreign affairs, he secured the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Amiens" title="Treaty of Amiens">Treaty of Amiens</a> in 1802. While the treaty's terms were the bare minimum that the British government could accept, <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleon Bonaparte">Napoleon Bonaparte</a> would not have agreed to any terms more favourable to the British, and the British government had reached a state of financial collapse from war expenditure, the loss of Continental markets for British goods and two successive failed harvests that had led to widespread famine and social unrest, rendering peace a necessity.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>By early 1803, Britain's financial and diplomatic positions had recovered sufficiently to allow Addington to declare war on France, when it became clear that the French would not allow a settlement for the defences of Malta that would have been secure enough to fend off a French invasion that appeared imminent.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>At the time and ever since, Addington has been criticised for his lacklustre conduct of the war and his defensive posture. However, without allies, Britain's options were limited to defence. He increased the forces, provided a tax base that could finance an enlarged war and seized several French possessions. To gain allies, Addington cultivated better relations with Russia,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFeldbæk1978_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeldbæk1978-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Austria, and <a href="/wiki/Prussia" title="Prussia">Prussia</a>, which later culminated in the <a href="/wiki/Third_Coalition" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Coalition">Third Coalition</a> shortly after he left office. Addington also strengthened British defences against a French invasion through the building of <a href="/wiki/Martello_tower" title="Martello tower">Martello towers</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Southern_England" title="Southern England">south coast</a> and the raising of more than 600,000 men at arms.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHall1988_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHall1988-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundling_Hospital">Foundling Hospital</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Foundling Hospital">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In 1802, Addington accepted an honorary position as vice-president for life on the Court of Governors of London's <a href="/wiki/Foundling_Hospital" title="Foundling Hospital">Foundling Hospital</a> for abandoned babies.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Loss_of_office">Loss of office</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Loss of office">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg/260px-Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg" decoding="async" width="260" height="176" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg/390px-Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg/520px-Britannia-between-Death-and-the-Doctors-Gillray.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="4241" data-file-height="2871" /></a><figcaption>In <i>Britannia between Death and the Doctor's</i> (1804), <a href="/wiki/James_Gillray" title="James Gillray">James Gillray</a> caricatured Pitt as a doctor kicking Addington (the previous doctor) out of Britannia's sickroom.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Although the King stood by him, it was not enough, because Addington did not have a strong enough hold on both Houses of Parliament. By May 1804, partisan criticism of Addington's war policies provided the pretext for a parliamentary putsch by the three major factions (<a href="/wiki/William_Wyndham_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville" class="mw-redirect" title="William Wyndham Grenville, 1st Baron Grenville">Grenvillites</a>, <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox" title="Charles James Fox">Foxites</a>, and Pittites), who had decided that they should replace Addington's ministry. Addington's greatest failing was his inability to manage a parliamentary majority by cultivating the loyal support of MPs beyond his own circle and the friends of the King. That, combined with his mediocre speaking ability, left him vulnerable to Pitt's mastery of parliamentary management and his unparallelled oratory skills. Pitt's parliamentary assault against Addington in March 1804 led to the slimming of his parliamentary majority to the point that defeat in the House of Commons was imminent.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcCahill1987_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcCahill1987-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lord_President_and_Lord_Privy_Seal">Lord President and Lord Privy Seal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Lord President and Lord Privy Seal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Addington remained an important political figure because he had gained a large following of MPs who supported him loyally in the Commons. He was reconciled with Pitt in December 1804, with the help of <a href="/wiki/Lord_Hawkesbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Hawkesbury">Lord Hawkesbury</a> as an intermediary. As a result, Pitt arranged for him to join the Cabinet as <a href="/wiki/Lord_President_of_the_Council" title="Lord President of the Council">Lord President of the Council</a> in January 1805 but insisted for Addington to accept a <a href="/wiki/Peerage" title="Peerage">peerage</a> to avoid the inconvenience of them sitting together in the Commons and Addington was created <a href="/wiki/Viscount_Sidmouth" title="Viscount Sidmouth">Viscount Sidmouth</a>, of <a href="/wiki/Sidmouth" title="Sidmouth">Sidmouth</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">County of Devon</a> on 12 January 1805.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In return for the support of the government by Addington's loyal supporters, Pitt agreed to include Addington's colleague the <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Buckinghamshire" title="Earl of Buckinghamshire">Earl of Buckinghamshire</a> as <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Duchy_of_Lancaster" title="Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster">Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster</a> with a promise to elevate him to the first vacancy of a more senior position in the Cabinet. However, when <a href="/wiki/Henry_Dundas,_1st_Viscount_Melville" title="Henry Dundas, 1st Viscount Melville">Melville</a> resigned as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty" title="First Lord of the Admiralty">First Lord of the Admiralty</a> in July 1805, Pitt broke his promise by having <a href="/wiki/Sir_Charles_Middleton" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Charles Middleton">Sir Charles Middleton</a> appointed instead of Buckinghamshire. As a result of the betrayal, Addington and Buckinghamshire resigned and took all of their supporters into opposition. Addington was appointed <a href="/wiki/Lord_Privy_Seal" title="Lord Privy Seal">Lord Privy Seal</a> in 1806 in the <a href="/wiki/Ministry_of_All_the_Talents" title="Ministry of All the Talents">Ministry of All the Talents</a> that succeeded Pitt. Later that year he returned to the position of Lord President to 1807. His resignation, in opposition to a limited measure of Catholic Emancipation, which the Cabinet was considering despite the opposition of <a href="/wiki/King_George_III" class="mw-redirect" title="King George III">King George III</a>, precipitated the fall of the Talents Ministry.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Home_Secretary">Home Secretary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Home Secretary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He returned to government again as Lord President in March 1812, and, in June of the same year, became <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a>. As Home Secretary, Addington countered revolutionary opposition, being responsible for the temporary <a href="/wiki/Habeas_Corpus_Suspension_Act_1817" title="Habeas Corpus Suspension Act 1817">suspension</a> of <i><a href="/wiki/Habeas_corpus" title="Habeas corpus">habeas corpus</a></i> in 1817 and the passage of the <a href="/wiki/Six_Acts" title="Six Acts">Six Acts</a> in 1819. His tenure also saw the <a href="/wiki/Peterloo_Massacre" title="Peterloo Massacre">Peterloo Massacre</a> of 1819. He left office in 1822, succeeded as Home Secretary by Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a>, but Addington remained in the Cabinet as <a href="/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio_(United_Kingdom)" title="Minister without portfolio (United Kingdom)">Minister without Portfolio</a> for the next two years, opposing, along with the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Wellington" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a>, other members of Cabinet, and <a href="/wiki/King_George_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="King George IV">King George IV</a>, British recognition of the South American republics. He remained active in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> for the next few years, making his final speech in opposition to Catholic Emancipation in 1829 and casting his final vote against the <a href="/wiki/Reform_Act_1832" title="Reform Act 1832">Reform Act 1832</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Residences_and_land">Residences and land</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Residences and land">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mortlake,_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church,_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Mortlake%2C_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church%2C_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg/220px-Mortlake%2C_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church%2C_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="151" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Mortlake%2C_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church%2C_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg/330px-Mortlake%2C_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church%2C_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Mortlake%2C_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church%2C_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg/440px-Mortlake%2C_St_Mary_the_Virgin%27s_church%2C_memorial_for_Henry_Addington.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="2062" /></a><figcaption>Memorial in Mortlake</figcaption></figure>
<p>Addington maintained homes at Upottery, <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bulmershe_Court" title="Bulmershe Court">Bulmershe Court</a>, in what is now the <a href="/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire" title="Reading, Berkshire">Reading</a> suburb of <a href="/wiki/Woodley,_Berkshire" title="Woodley, Berkshire">Woodley</a>, but moved to the <a href="/wiki/White_Lodge" class="mw-redirect" title="White Lodge">White Lodge</a> in <a href="/wiki/Richmond_Park" title="Richmond Park">Richmond Park</a> when he became Prime Minister. However, he maintained links with Woodley and the Reading area as commander of the Woodley Yeomanry Cavalry and High Steward of Reading. He also donated to the town of Reading the four acres (1.6&#160;ha) of land that is today the site of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Berkshire_Hospital" title="Royal Berkshire Hospital">Royal Berkshire Hospital</a>, and his name is commemorated in the town's Sidmouth Street and Addington Road as well as in Sidmouth Street in Devizes and Addington Special School in Woodley, Reading.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> In Devizes he paid for the new <a href="/wiki/Market_Cross,_Devizes" title="Market Cross, Devizes">Market Cross</a>, designed by <a href="/wiki/James_Wyatt" title="James Wyatt">James Wyatt</a>, that was constructed in 1814.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Speaker of the House of Commons, from 1795 he had a residence in the <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster" title="Palace of Westminster">Palace of Westminster</a>, to the north-east of the House of Commons.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECooke1987186_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECooke1987186-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Addington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Addington died in London on 15 February 1844 at the age of 86, from <a href="/wiki/Influenza" title="Influenza">influenza</a>, and was buried in the churchyard at <a href="/wiki/St_Mary_the_Virgin,_Mortlake" title="St Mary the Virgin, Mortlake">St Mary the Virgin, Mortlake</a> on <a href="/wiki/Mortlake_High_Street" title="Mortlake High Street">Mortlake High Street</a>, now in Greater London.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
