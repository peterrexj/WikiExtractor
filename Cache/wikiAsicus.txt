<p><b>Saint Assicus</b> <i>(Asicus, Assic)</i> was the first bishop of <a href="/wiki/Elphin,_County_Roscommon" title="Elphin, County Roscommon">Elphin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, and venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of that place. He was also an artisan metalworker.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Tradition">Tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Assicus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Assicus was a friend of St. Patrick, and a skilled metal worker in brass and copper. 
Converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>, he is also said to have been Patrick's worker in <a href="/wiki/Iron" title="Iron">iron</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Elphin, Patrick built a church, called through centuries, "Tempull Phadruig" (Patrick's church). There he established an episcopal monastery, one of the first monasteries founded by him. He appointed Assicus as abbot-bishop, and with him left Bite, son of the brother of Assicus, and Cipia, mother of Bite.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Assicus was of the family of Hono, a druid of wealth and influence, from whom Patrick obtained the land.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy1905_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy1905-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Artisan">Artisan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Assicus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Artisan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Under the <a href="/wiki/Brehon_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Brehon Law">Brehon Law</a>, craftsmen were well respected in ancient Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
The first bishop of Elphin is described in the "Book of Armagh" as the <i>cerd</i>, (the wright or goldsmith) of St. Patrick. Assicus made chalices, patens, and metal book-covers for the churches founded by Patrick.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <i>Tripartite Life of St Patrick</i> is stated:
</p>
<dl><dd><i>Bishop St. Assic was Patrick's <a href="/wiki/Coppersmith" title="Coppersmith">coppersmith</a>, and made altars, tables, and square <a href="/wiki/Bookcase" title="Bookcase">bookcases</a>. Besides, he made our saint's <a href="/wiki/Paten" title="Paten">patens</a> in honour of Bishop Patrick, and of them I have seen three square patens, that is, a paten in the Church of Patrick in <a href="/wiki/Armagh" title="Armagh">Armagh</a>, and another in the Church of Elphin, and a third in the great-church of Donough-patrick (at Carns near <a href="/wiki/Tulsk" title="Tulsk">Tulsk</a>).</i><sup id="cite_ref-grattan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-joyce_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-joyce-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Assicus was an expert metal worker, and was also renowned as a <a href="/wiki/Bellfounder" class="mw-redirect" title="Bellfounder">bellfounder</a>. Following the example of their masters, the successors and spiritual children of St. Assicus founded a school of art and produced beautiful objects of Celtic workmanship in the Diocese of Elphin.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Assicus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Of his last days the following graphic description is given by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/John_Healy_(bishop)" title="John Healy (bishop)">Healy</a>:
</p>
<dl><dd><i>Assicus himself in shame because of a lie told either by him, or, as others say, of him, fled into <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">Donegal</a>, and for seven years abode in the island of <a href="/wiki/Rathlin_O%27Birne" title="Rathlin O&#39;Birne">Rathlin O'Birne</a>. Then his monks sought him out, and after much labour found him in the mountain glens, and tried to bring him home to his own <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at Elphin. But he fell sick by the way and died with them in the wilderness. So they buried the venerable old man in the churchyard of Rath Cunga, now Racoon, in the Barony of <a href="/wiki/Tirhugh" title="Tirhugh">Tirhugh</a>, County Donegal. The old churchyard is there still, though now disused, on the summit of a round hillock close to the left of the road from <a href="/wiki/Ballyshannon" title="Ballyshannon">Ballyshannon</a> to Donegal, about a mile to the south of the village of Ballintra. We sought in vain for any trace of an inscribed stone in the old churchyard. He fled from men during life, and, like <a href="/wiki/Moses" title="Moses">Moses</a>, his grave is hidden from them in death.</i><sup id="cite_ref-grattan_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>His feast is celebrated 27 April, as is recorded in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Tallaght" title="Martyrology of Tallaght">Martyrology of Tallaght</a></i> under that date.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Identities">Identities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Assicus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Identities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Assicus is sometimes thought to be the same man as <a href="/wiki/St._Tassac" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Tassac">St. Tassac</a> and St. Assam / St. Assan, or both.
</p>
