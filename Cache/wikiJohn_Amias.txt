<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">John Amias</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><span class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Frameless"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed-john-amias-and-robert-dalby.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Blessed-john-amias-and-robert-dalby.png/220px-Blessed-john-amias-and-robert-dalby.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="309" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Blessed-john-amias-and-robert-dalby.png/330px-Blessed-john-amias-and-robert-dalby.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Blessed-john-amias-and-robert-dalby.png 2x" data-file-width="365" data-file-height="512" /></a></span><div class="infobox-caption">John Amias and Robert Dalby, "At the place of Execution." <p><b>John Amias</b> (died 16 March 1589) was a  <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">Roman Catholic priest</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> in 1929.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Amias&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is some doubt about his early life and his real name. One story is that he was indeed John Amias or Amyas, born at <a href="/wiki/Wakefield" title="Wakefield">Wakefield</a> in <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, England, where he married and raised a family, exercising the trade of cloth-merchant. On the death of his wife, he divided his property among his children and left for the <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Continent</a> to become a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-ryan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ryan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There is also a possibility that he was really William Anne (surname), youngest son of John and Katherine Anne, of <a href="/wiki/Clayton,_South_Yorkshire" title="Clayton, South Yorkshire">Frickley</a> near Wakefield.
</p><p>Regardless of his actual name, on 22 June 1580, a widower calling himself "John Amias" entered the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Rheims</a> to study for the priesthood. He was ordained a priest in <a href="/wiki/Rheims_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims Cathedral">Rheims Cathedral</a> on 25 March 1581. On 5 June of that year Amias, set out for Paris and then England, as a missionary, in the company of another priest, Edmund Sykes. Of his missionary life we know little. Towards the end of 1588 he was seized at the house of a Mr Murton at <a href="/wiki/Melling-with-Wrayton" title="Melling-with-Wrayton">Melling</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a> and imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/York_Castle" title="York Castle">York Castle</a>. Given the 1585 Act making it a capital offence to be a Catholic priest in England the sentence of <a href="/wiki/Hanging,_drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanging, drawing and quartering">hanging, drawing and quartering</a> was inevitable. It was carried out outside the city of York on 16 March 1589. Amias was beginning to address the assembled people, and explain that it was for religion, and not treason, that he suffered, but was not allowed to proceed.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His fate was shared by a fellow priest, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Dalby_(Blessed)" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Dalby (Blessed)">Robert Dalby</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ryan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ryan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Both were beatified by Pope Pius XI on 15 December 1929.
</p>
