					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">5th century Christian Latin poet</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Orientius</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;"><a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">Martyr</a></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">5th century<br />Rome</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Pre-congregation</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 May</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Orientius</b> was a Christian <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> poet of the fifth century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography_and_work">Biography and work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Orientius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography and work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He wrote the <a href="/wiki/Elegiac" title="Elegiac">elegiac</a> poem <i><a href="/wiki/Commonitorium_(Orientius)" title="Commonitorium (Orientius)">Commonitorium</a></i> of 1036 verses (divided into two books) describing the way to heaven, with warnings against its hindrances. He was a <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> (II, 184), who had been converted after a life of sin (I, 405 sq.), was evidently an experienced pastor, and wrote at a time when his country was being devastated by the <a href="/wiki/Barbarian_invasions" class="mw-redirect" title="Barbarian invasions">invasion of barbarians</a>.  
</p><p>All this points to his identification with Orientius, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Augusta_Ausciorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Augusta Ausciorum">Bishop of Augusta Ausciorum</a> (<a href="/wiki/Auch" title="Auch">Auch</a>), who as a very old man was sent by <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_I" title="Theodoric I">Theodoric I</a>, King of the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a>, as ambassador to the Roman generals <a href="/wiki/Flavius_A%C3%ABtius" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavius Aëtius">Flavius Aëtius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Litorius" title="Litorius">Litorius</a> in 439 ("Vita S. Orientii" in "Acta SS.", I May, 61).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Commonitorium quotes classical Roman poets —<a href="/wiki/Virgil" title="Virgil">Virgil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ovid" title="Ovid">Ovid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catullus" title="Catullus">Catullus</a>— and is perhaps influenced by <a href="/wiki/Prudentius" title="Prudentius">Prudentius</a>. It exists in only one manuscript (Cod. Ashburnham. sæc. X), and is followed by some shorter anonymous poems not by Orientius, and by two prayers in verse attributed to him.
</p><p>The first complete edition was published by <a href="/wiki/Mart%C3%A8ne" class="mw-redirect" title="Martène">Martène</a>, <i>Veterum Scriptorum Monumenta</i>, I (Rouen, 1700); then by <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Gallandi" title="Andrea Gallandi">Andrea Gallandi</a>, <i>Bibliotheca veterum Patrum</i>, X (Venice, 1774), 185–96, reprinted by <a href="/wiki/J.P._Migne" class="mw-redirect" title="J.P. Migne">J.P. Migne</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologia Latina</a></i> 61.977-1006. The best modern edition is by Ellis in the <i><a href="/wiki/Corpus_Scriptorum_Ecclesiasticorum_Latinorum" title="Corpus Scriptorum Ecclesiasticorum Latinorum">Corpus Scriptorum Eccl. Latinorum</a></i> XVI (Vienna, 1888): "Poetæ Christiani minores", I, 191–261.
</p>
