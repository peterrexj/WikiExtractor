<p><b>Onesimus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ὀνήσιμος</span></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">translit.</a></small>&#160;<span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn"><i>Onēsimos</i></i></span>, meaning "useful"; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;68 AD</span>, according to Catholic tradition),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> also called <b>Onesimus of Byzantium</b> and <b>The Holy Apostle Onesimus</b> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was probably a <a href="/wiki/Roman_slavery" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman slavery">slave</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> to <a href="/wiki/Philemon_of_Colossae" class="mw-redirect" title="Philemon of Colossae">Philemon of Colossae</a>, a man of Christian faith. He may also be the same Onesimus named by <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch" title="Ignatius of Antioch">Ignatius of Antioch</a> (died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;107</span>) as bishop in <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which would put Onesimus's death closer to 95. If so, Onesimus went from slave to brother to bishop.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Scripture">In Scripture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Onesimus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: In Scripture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name "Onesimus" appears in two New Testament epistles—in Colossians 4 and in Philemon. In Colossians 4:9<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> a person of this name is identified as a Christian accompanying <a href="/wiki/Tychicus" title="Tychicus">Tychicus</a> to visit the Christians in <a href="/wiki/Colossae" title="Colossae">Colossae</a>; nothing else is stated about him in this context. He may well be the freed Onesimus from the <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_Philemon" title="Epistle to Philemon">Epistle to Philemon</a>.
</p><p>The Epistle to Philemon was written by <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> to Philemon concerning a person believed to be a runaway slave named Onesimus. The traditional designation of Onesimus as a slave is doubted by some modern scholars.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Onesimus found his way to the site of Paul's imprisonment (most probably <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> or <a href="/wiki/Caesarea" title="Caesarea">Caesarea</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> to escape punishment for a theft of which he was accused.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> After hearing the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> from Paul, Onesimus converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. Paul, having earlier converted Philemon to Christianity, sought to reconcile the two by writing the letter to Philemon which today exists in the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The letter reads (in part):
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I appeal to you for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten while in my chains, who once was unprofitable to you, but now is profitable to you and to me. I am sending him back. You therefore receive him, that is, my own heart, whom I wished to keep with me, that on your behalf he might minister to me in my chains for the gospel. But without your consent I wanted to do nothing, that your good deed might not be by compulsion, as it were, but voluntary. For perhaps he departed for a while for this purpose, that you might receive him forever, no longer as a slave but more than a slave—a beloved brother, especially to me but how much more to you, both in the flesh and in the Lord.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite><a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul of Tarsus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Philemon_of_Colossae" class="mw-redirect" title="Philemon of Colossae">Philemon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_Philemon" title="Epistle to Philemon">Epistle to Philemon</a> 1:10–16 (<a href="/wiki/New_King_James_Version" title="New King James Version">NKJV</a>)</cite></div></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_tradition">In tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Onesimus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: In tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although it is doubted by authorities such as <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Fitzmyer" title="Joseph Fitzmyer">Joseph Fitzmyer</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> it may be the case that this Onesimus was the same one consecrated a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> by the Apostles and who accepted the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">episcopal throne</a> in Ephesus<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> following <a href="/wiki/Saint_Timothy" title="Saint Timothy">Timothy</a>. During the reign of <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Domitian" title="Domitian">Domitian</a> and the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a>, Onesimus was imprisoned in Rome and may have been <a href="/wiki/Martyred" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyred">martyred</a> by <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoning</a> (although some sources claim that he was beheaded). However, since the reign of Domitian was from 81 AD to 96 AD, then Onesimus' death would have to fall within these years and not 68 AD as stated above.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_liturgy">In liturgy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Onesimus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In liturgy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Onesimus is regarded as a saint by many Christian denominations. The <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod" title="Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod">Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod</a> commemorates him and <a href="/wiki/Philemon_(New_Testament_character)" class="mw-redirect" title="Philemon (New Testament character)">Philemon</a> on February 15.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eastern Churches remember Onesimus on 15 February. The traditional Western commemoration of Onesimus is on 16 February.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> But in the 2004 edition of the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>, Onesimus is listed under 15 February. There, he is described as "[a] runaway slave, whom the apostle Paul received to the faith of Christ while in prison, regarding him as a son of whom he had become father, as he himself wrote to Philemon, Onesimus's master".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
