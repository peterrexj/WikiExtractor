<p><b>Stanley Francis Rother</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;r&#39; in &#39;rye&#39;">r</span><span title="/oʊ/: &#39;o&#39; in &#39;code&#39;">oʊ</span><span title="/θ/: &#39;th&#39; in &#39;thigh&#39;">θ</span><span title="/ər/: &#39;er&#39; in &#39;letter&#39;">ər</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">ROH</span>-thər</i></a>; March 27, 1935 – July 28, 1981) was an <a href="/wiki/American_people" class="mw-redirect" title="American people">American</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">priest</a> from <a href="/wiki/Oklahoma" title="Oklahoma">Oklahoma</a> who was murdered in <a href="/wiki/Guatemala" title="Guatemala">Guatemala</a> in 1981. He had worked as a missionary priest there since 1968. He held several parish assignments as a priest of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Oklahoma_City" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Oklahoma City">Archdiocese of Oklahoma City</a> from 1963 to 1968 before being assigned to Guatemala.
</p><p>On December 1, 2016, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> confirmed that Rother had died a martyr, murdered for his faith, and Rother was beatified on September 23, 2017, in <a href="/wiki/Oklahoma_City" title="Oklahoma City">Oklahoma City</a>. He is the first U.S.-born priest and martyr to be beatified by the Catholic Church, and the second person to be beatified on American soil after the <a href="/wiki/New_Jersey" title="New Jersey">New Jersey</a>-born nun <a href="/wiki/Miriam_Teresa_Demjanovich" class="mw-redirect" title="Miriam Teresa Demjanovich">Miriam Teresa Demjanovich</a> in 2014.<sup id="cite_ref-rotherrn_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rotherrn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanley_Rother&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education_and_priesthood">Education and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanley_Rother&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg/280px-Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg" decoding="async" width="280" height="210" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg/420px-Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg/560px-Stanley_rother_memorial.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a><figcaption>Memorial plaque to Rother in Santiago Atitlán. It quotes <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=John%2015:13&amp;version=nrsv">John 15:13</a>: “Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends.”</figcaption></figure>
<p>Stanley Francis Rother was born on March 27, 1935, in <a href="/wiki/Okarche" class="mw-redirect" title="Okarche">Okarche</a>, one of four children of Franz Rother and Gertrude Smith, who farmed near that <a href="/wiki/Oklahoma" title="Oklahoma">Oklahoma</a> town. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on March 29, 1935, in Okarche's Holy Trinity Church by Father Zenon Steber. His sister Betty Mae adopted the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> Sister Marita upon taking her vows, and they had two brothers, Tom and Jim.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rother was strong and adept at farm tasks. He decided to become a priest after completing high school at Holy Trinity School. He studied at Saint John Seminary and then <a href="/wiki/Assumption_Seminary" title="Assumption Seminary">Assumption Seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Antonio,_Texas" class="mw-redirect" title="San Antonio, Texas">San Antonio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Texas" title="Texas">Texas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He served as a <a href="/wiki/Sacristan" title="Sacristan">sacristan</a>, groundskeeper, <a href="/wiki/Bookbinder" class="mw-redirect" title="Bookbinder">bookbinder</a>, <a href="/wiki/Plumber" title="Plumber">plumber</a>, and gardener. After almost six years, seminary staff advised him to withdraw.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following consultation with Bishop <a href="/wiki/Victor_Reed" class="mw-redirect" title="Victor Reed">Victor Reed</a> of Oklahoma City, Rother attended <a href="/wiki/Mount_St._Mary%27s_University" title="Mount St. Mary&#39;s University">Mount Saint Mary's Seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Emmitsburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Emmitsburg">Emmitsburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Maryland" title="Maryland">Maryland</a>, from which he graduated in 1963. Bishop Reed <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> him to the priesthood on May 25, 1963. Rother served as an associate <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> in various parishes around Oklahoma: Saint William in <a href="/wiki/Durant,_Oklahoma" title="Durant, Oklahoma">Durant</a>, Saint Francis Xavier and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Family_Cathedral_(Tulsa,_Oklahoma)" title="Holy Family Cathedral (Tulsa, Oklahoma)">Holy Family Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tulsa" class="mw-redirect" title="Tulsa">Tulsa</a>, and Corpus Christi in <a href="/wiki/Oklahoma_City" title="Oklahoma City">Oklahoma City</a>. In 1968, at his own request, Reed assigned him to the diocese's mission to the <a href="/wiki/Tz%27utujil_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Tz&#39;utujil people">Tz'utujil people</a> (also spelt “Tz'utuhil”) of <a href="/wiki/Santiago_Atitl%C3%A1n" title="Santiago Atitlán">Santiago Atitlán</a> in the rural highlands of southwest Guatemala.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Guatemalan_mission">Guatemalan mission</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanley_Rother&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Guatemalan mission">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>To better connect with his congregation, he learned <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Tz%E2%80%99utujil_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Tz’utujil language">Tz’utujil language</a> which was an unwritten <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_language" title="Indigenous language">indigenous language</a> first recorded by the missionary Ramón Carlín. He served in Santiago Atitlán from 1968 until his death. He supported a radio station located on the mission property, which transmitted daily lessons in language and mathematics. In 1973 he noted with pride in a letter: "I am now preaching in Tz'utuhil."<sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During that time, in addition to his pastoral duties, he <a href="/wiki/Bible_translations" title="Bible translations">translated</a> the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a> into Tz'utujil and began regular <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Masses</a> in Tz'utujil. In the late 1960s, Rother founded in <a href="/wiki/Panabaj" title="Panabaj">Panabaj</a> a small hospital, dubbed the "<i>Hospitalito</i>"; Father Carlín was a collaborator in this project.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1975, Rother had become the <i>de facto</i> leader of the Oklahoma-sponsored mission effort in Guatemala as other religious and lay supporters rotated out of the program.<sup id="cite_ref-StAM_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StAM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was a prominent figure in the community, owing to his light complexion as well as his habit of smoking <a href="/wiki/Tobacco" title="Tobacco">tobacco</a> in a <a href="/wiki/Pipe_smoking" title="Pipe smoking">pipe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Since there was no  Tz'utujil equivalent for "Stanley," the people of Rother's mission affectionately called him "<i>Padre Apla's</i>” ("Father Francis”), a nod to his other given name.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Final_months_and_murder">Final months and murder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanley_Rother&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Final months and murder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Within the last year of his life, Rother saw the radio station destroyed and its director murdered. Some of his <a href="/wiki/Catechists" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechists">catechists</a> and parishioners would <a href="/wiki/Enforced_disappearance" title="Enforced disappearance">disappear</a> and later be found dead, their corpses showing signs of beating and torture; Rother knew all this upon returning to Guatemala in May 1981. In December 1980, he had written a letter to the faithful in Oklahoma describing the violent situation: "This is one of the reasons I have for staying in the face of physical harm. The shepherd cannot run at the first sign of danger."<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the beginning of 1981, Rother was <a href="/wiki/Death_threat" title="Death threat">warned</a> that his name was eighth on a hit list of right-wing <a href="/wiki/Death_squad" title="Death squad">death squads</a>, and that he should immediately leave Guatemala to stay alive.<sup id="cite_ref-TM_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TM-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Rother reluctantly returned to Oklahoma in January, and while home in Okarche, said Mass served by <a href="/wiki/Daniel_Henry_Mueggenborg" title="Daniel Henry Mueggenborg">Daniel Henry Mueggenborg</a>, a college student who became inspired by Rother to pursue the priesthood,<sup id="cite_ref-bio-sketch_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-sketch-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> though he later asked the archbishop for permission to return. Another reason for returning was that he wanted to celebrate <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> with them.<sup id="cite_ref-TL_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Rother returned to Santiago Atitlán in April, aware that he was being watched.<sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TM_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TM-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template"><span style="white-space: nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Link_rot" title="Wikipedia:Link rot"><span title="&#160;Dead link tagged January 2020">dead link</span></a></i>&#93;</span></sup>
</p><p>On the morning of July 28, 1981, just after midnight, gunmen broke into Rother's rectory. The assassins forced a teenager named Francisco Bocel (who was in the church) to show them Rother's bedroom. The men threatened to kill Bocel if he did not lead them to Rother, so he led them down a flight of stairs and knocked on a nearby door.<sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Rother opened the door, and a struggle ensued as Bocel fled; he was shot twice in the head.<sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rother was one of 10 priests murdered in Guatemala that year. His remains were flown back to Oklahoma and buried in Holy Trinity Cemetery in his hometown on August 3, 1981. At the request of his former Tz'utujil parishioners, his <a href="/wiki/Heart-burial" title="Heart-burial">heart was removed and buried</a> under the <a href="/wiki/Altar_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Altar (Catholicism)">altar</a> of the church in Santiago Atitlán.<sup id="cite_ref-NO_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Three men were arrested on charges of murder within weeks of Rother's murder; another man and woman were also sought for questioning at that stage. The three men arrested admitted to having entered the church in a robbery attempt, and to having shot Rother dead when the priest tried stopping them.<sup id="cite_ref-TIME_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TIME-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Despite the confessions, many people familiar with the circumstances of the murder considered the three accused men innocent, and the prosecutions a cover-up of <a href="/wiki/Paramilitary" title="Paramilitary">paramilitary</a> involvement in the murder.<sup id="cite_ref-StAM_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StAM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Convictions for all three men were later overturned by a Guatemalan appellate court, under pressure from U.S. authorities.<sup id="cite_ref-StAM_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StAM-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> No other suspects have been prosecuted for Rother’s murder.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanley_Rother&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg/220px-First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="228" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg/330px-First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg/440px-First_Class_Relic_Blessed_Stanley_Rother.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2426" data-file-height="2509" /></a><figcaption>First Class bone Relic of Blessed Stanley Rother</figcaption></figure>
<p>The beatification process was set to open in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Oklahoma_City" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Oklahoma City">Archdiocese of Oklahoma City</a>, but the cause first had to be transferred there from Guatemala; a cause opens in the diocese where the individual died. The forum transfer was granted by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Solol%C3%A1-Chimaltenango" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Sololá-Chimaltenango">Diocese of Sololá-Chimaltenango</a> to the Archdiocese of Oklahoma City on September 3, 2007. The diocesan investigation process opened on October 5, 2007, and closed on July 20, 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The formal start of the cause was on November 25, 2009, when Rother was titled “<a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>”. The diocesan process received validation from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> on March 16, 2012, and later received the <i><a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a></i> dossier from cause officials in 2014. Theologians unanimously approved Rother’s cause in a decision on June 23, 2015,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and by the cardinal and bishops of the CCS on October 18, 2016.
</p><p>On December 1, 2016, his beatification received approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> who confirmed that Rother had been killed "<i>in odium fidei</i>" ("in hatred of the faith"). Rother was beatified on September 23, 2017, at the <a href="/wiki/Cox_Convention_Center" class="mw-redirect" title="Cox Convention Center">Cox Convention Center</a>, with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, prefect of the CCS, presiding on the pope's behalf. The beatification  <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> was attended by 20,000 people.<sup id="cite_ref-rotherblessed_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rotherblessed-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-blessedrother_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blessedrother-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-rotherrn_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rotherrn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Among the bishops who assisted Amato were the Paul S. Coakley, Archbishop of Oklahoma City, and Oklahoma City Archbishop Emeritus <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_J._Beltran" title="Eusebius J. Beltran">Eusebius J. Beltran</a>, who initiated Rother's cause in 2007.<sup id="cite_ref-rotherblessed_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rotherblessed-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for the cause was Dr. Andrea Ambrosi.
</p><p>A mission church has since been named after him in <a href="/wiki/Decatur,_Arkansas" title="Decatur, Arkansas">Decatur, Arkansas</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Little_Rock" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Little Rock">Roman Catholic Diocese of Little Rock</a>; it is the first Catholic church in the world dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrine">Shrine</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanley_Rother&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Shrine">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 2019, the Archdiocese of Oklahoma City broke ground on the Venerable Servant of God Father Stanley Rother Shrine, a new church and ministry complex to be built on archdiocesan property at <a href="/wiki/Interstate_35" title="Interstate 35">I-35</a> and 89th Street in Southern Oklahoma City (the site of the former Brookside Golf Course).  At the time, it was expected to become the largest Catholic Church in the US state of Oklahoma.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On February 17, 2023, a dedication Mass for Rother took place and marked the official opening the Blessed Stanley Rother Shrine in Oklahoma City. A chapel located inside the shrine is the final resting place for Rother, and the shrine also features a museum, sanctuary, gift shop, and visitor center.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Colonial_architecture" title="Spanish Colonial architecture">Spanish Colonial</a> style shrine is fully funded by Catholic donors and is debt-free.
</p>
