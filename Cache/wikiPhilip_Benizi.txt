<p><b>Philip Benizi</b> (sometimes <b>St Philip Benitius</b>, and in Italian <b>Filippo Benizzi</b>) (August 15, 1233 – August 22, 1285) was a <a href="/wiki/General_superior" class="mw-redirect" title="General superior">general superior</a> of the Order of the <a href="/wiki/Servites" class="mw-redirect" title="Servites">Servites</a>, and credited with reviving the order. Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo X">Leo X</a> recognised his cult 24 January 1516 essentially beatifying him (although this was not a formal category at the time); and Pope <a href="/wiki/Clement_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement X">Clement X</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> him as a saint in 1671.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Benizi_de_Damiani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Philip Benizi was born on August 15 in the <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florentine</a> district of <a href="/wiki/Oltrarno" title="Oltrarno">Oltrarno</a>, of the noble family of Benizi. Of his childhood but little is known. He entered the order of the Servites as a lay brother and was sent to a convent three leagues from Florence, where he displayed the utmost diligence. He would retire into a cavern near the church to meditate. Two Dominicans who chanced to visit him were so struck by his piety that they insisted he become a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>When he was elected the <a href="/wiki/General_superior" class="mw-redirect" title="General superior">general superior</a> on June 5, 1267, the order, which had long been the object of attack from enemies, entered into the crisis of its existence. The <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Council of Lyons">Second Council of Lyons</a> in 1274 put into execution the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_ordinances" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecclesiastical ordinances">ordinance</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Lateran_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Fourth Lateran Council">Fourth Lateran Council</a>, forbidding the foundation of new religious orders, and absolutely suppressing all mendicant institutions not yet approved by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. In 1276 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_V" title="Pope Innocent V">Pope Innocent V</a>, in a letter addressed to Philip, declared the order suppressed. Philip then proceeded to Rome, but before his arrival there, Innocent V had died.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The city of <a href="/wiki/Forl%C3%AC" title="Forlì">Forlì</a> was part of the Papal States and, in 1283, the site of strong anti-papal sentiment was placed under interdict. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_IV" title="Pope Martin IV">Pope Martin IV</a> asked Philip to go to Forlì and try to reconcile the divided city. According to certain sixteenth century legende of Philip Benizi, an eighteen-year-old <a href="/wiki/Peregrine_Laziosi" title="Peregrine Laziosi">Peregrine Laziosi</a> (Latiosi), the son of a <a href="/wiki/Ghibelline" class="mw-redirect" title="Ghibelline">Ghibelline</a> leader, was among those abusive towards Philip. However, Philip's meeting with the initially antagonistic Peregrine inspired the young man to later join the Servites, himself being canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> in 1726.<sup id="cite_ref-servite_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-servite-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to Peregrine's earliest legenda, this meeting between Philip and Peregrine never happened.
</p><p>Philip died on August 22, 1285, during the <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">Octave</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption</a> at <a href="/wiki/Todi" title="Todi">Todi</a>, where he is buried.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloister</a> of Santa Maria of the Servites in <a href="/wiki/Vicenza" title="Vicenza">Vicenza</a>, took place his miracles in 1319.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Benizi_de_Damiani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Church of the Servites of Mary in Todi, <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, contains the body of St Philip Benizi, whose statue is the work of <a href="/wiki/Bernini" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernini">Bernini</a>.
</p><p>St Philip's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on August 23. He and Santa Maria Addolorata are the titular co-patrons of the <a href="/wiki/Minor_basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Minor basilica">minor basilica</a> of <a href="/wiki/Monte_Senario" title="Monte Senario">Monte Senario</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Vaglia" title="Vaglia">Vaglia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Florence" title="Metropolitan City of Florence">Metropolitan City of Florence</a>), in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Florence" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Florence">Diocese of Florence</a> (since 1917).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Churches_named_after_St_Philip_Benizi">Churches named after St Philip Benizi</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Benizi_de_Damiani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Churches named after St Philip Benizi">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="United_States">United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Benizi_de_Damiani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There are churches named for Saint Philip Benizi in:
<a href="/wiki/Black_Canyon_City,_Arizona" title="Black Canyon City, Arizona">Black Canyon City, Arizona</a>; <a href="/wiki/Fullerton,_California" title="Fullerton, California">Fullerton, California</a>; <a href="/wiki/Belle_Glade,_Florida" title="Belle Glade, Florida">Belle Glade, Florida</a>; <a href="/wiki/Jonesboro,_Georgia" title="Jonesboro, Georgia">Jonesboro, Georgia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Chicago" title="Chicago">Chicago</a>, Illinois; <a href="/wiki/Grafton,_Massachusetts" title="Grafton, Massachusetts">Grafton, Massachusetts</a>; <a href="/wiki/Viburnum,_Missouri" title="Viburnum, Missouri">Viburnum, Missouri</a>; <a href="/wiki/Darby,_Montana" title="Darby, Montana">Darby, Montana</a>; <a href="/wiki/Creswell,_Oregon" title="Creswell, Oregon">Creswell</a>; <a href="/wiki/Oregon_City,_Oregon" title="Oregon City, Oregon">Oregon City, Oregon</a>; <a href="/wiki/Moncks_Corner,_South_Carolina" title="Moncks Corner, South Carolina">Moncks Corner, South Carolina</a>; <a href="/wiki/Poteet,_Texas" title="Poteet, Texas">Poteet, Texas</a>; <a href="/wiki/Ford,_Washington" title="Ford, Washington">Ford, Washington</a>; and <a href="/wiki/Trapper_Creek,_Alaska" title="Trapper Creek, Alaska">Trapper Creek, Alaska</a>.  
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_references">Cultural references</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_Benizi_de_Damiani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Cultural references">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Five scenes from his life were painted in the early 16th century by the Florentine <a href="/wiki/Andrea_del_Sarto" title="Andrea del Sarto">Andrea del Sarto</a>: <i>"His Charity to a Leper"</i>, <i>"The Smiting of the Blasphemers"</i>, <i>"The Cure of the Woman Possessed with a Devil"</i>, <i>"The Resurrection of a Child before the bier of the Saint"</i>, and <i>"The Veneration of his Relics"</i>. These appear in the atrium of the Servite church of the SS. Annunziata, Florence.
</p><p>There is a <a href="/wiki/Statue_of_Philip_Benizi_de_Damiani,_Charles_Bridge" title="Statue of Philip Benizi de Damiani, Charles Bridge">statue of him</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>. Designed in 1714, this statue was made from <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a> <a href="/wiki/Marble" title="Marble">marble</a> and donated by the Servites convent in Prague. The statue portrays him holding a cross, a book and a <a href="/wiki/Whip" title="Whip">spray</a>. By his legs there is the crown of the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>. A clay model of this statue can be found in the Salzburg museum.
</p><p>There is also a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> dedicated to him in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Marcello_al_Corso" title="San Marcello al Corso">San Marcello al Corso</a> in Rome, and a statue dedicated to him at The National Sanctuary of our Sorrowful Mother, popularly known as <a href="/wiki/The_Grotto" title="The Grotto">The Grotto</a>, in Portland, Fortnite
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Name_day" title="Name day">name days</a> are commonly celebrated, the <a href="/wiki/Name_days_in_Slovakia" title="Name days in Slovakia">name day</a> for “Philip” falls on August 23, his feast day.
</p>
