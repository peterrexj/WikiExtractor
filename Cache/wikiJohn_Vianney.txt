<p><b>John Vianney</b> (born  <b>Jean-Baptiste-Marie Vianney</b>; 8 May 1786 – 4 August 1859), venerated as <b>Saint John Vianney</b>, was a French <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> who is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">saint</a> and as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of parish priests. He is often referred to as the "<i><b>Curé d'Ars</b></i>" (i.e. the parish priest of Ars), internationally known for his priestly and pastoral work in his parish in <a href="/wiki/Ars-sur-Formans" title="Ars-sur-Formans">Ars</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, because of the radical spiritual transformation of the community and its surroundings. Catholics attribute this to his saintly life, <a href="/wiki/Mortification_in_Roman_Catholic_teaching" class="mw-redirect" title="Mortification in Roman Catholic teaching">mortification</a>, persevering ministry in the sacrament of <a href="/wiki/Sacrament_of_Penance_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacrament of Penance (Catholic Church)">confession</a>, and ardent <a href="/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Veneration of Mary in the Catholic Church">devotion to the Blessed Virgin Mary</a>. His feast day is August 4.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Vianney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vianney was born on 8 May 1786, in the French town of <a href="/wiki/Dardilly" title="Dardilly">Dardilly</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> (near <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>), and was baptized the same day. His parents, Matthieu Vianney and his wife Marie (Belize),<sup id="cite_ref-risso_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-risso-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> had six children, of whom John was the fourth. The Vianneys were devout Catholics who helped the poor. Vianney's paternal grandparents once gave hospitality to <a href="/wiki/Benedict_Joseph_Labre" title="Benedict Joseph Labre">Benedict Joseph Labre</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the homeless, who passed through Dardilly on his pilgrimage to Rome in 1770.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1790, the anticlerical Terror phase of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a> forced many loyal priests to hide from the regime in order to carry out the sacraments in their parish. Even though to do so had been declared illegal, the Vianneys traveled to distant farms to attend Masses celebrated  on the run. Realizing that such priests risked their lives day by day, Vianney began to look upon them as heroes. He received his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> catechetical instruction in a private home from two nuns whose communities had been dissolved during the Revolution. He made his first communion at the age of 13<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> in a neighbor's kitchen;<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> during the Mass, the windows were covered so that the light of the candles could not be seen from outside.
</p><p>The Catholic Church was re-established in France in 1802 by <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleon Bonaparte">Napoleon Bonaparte</a>, resulting in religious peace throughout the country, culminating in a <a href="/wiki/Concordat" title="Concordat">Concordat</a>. By this time, Vianney was concerned about his future vocation and longed for an education. He was 20 when his father allowed him to leave the farm to be taught at a "presbytery-school" in the neighboring village of <a href="/wiki/%C3%89cully" title="Écully">Écully</a>, conducted by the <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A9" title="Abbé">Abbé</a> Balley.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The school taught arithmetic, history, geography and Latin. Vianney struggled with school, especially with Latin, since his past education had been interrupted by the French Revolution. Only because of Vianney's deepest desire to be a priest—and Balley's patience—did he persevere.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Vianney's studies were interrupted in 1809 when he was drafted into Napoleon's armies.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He would have been exempt, as an ecclesiastical student, but Napoleon had withdrawn the exemption in certain dioceses because of his need for soldiers in his <a href="/wiki/Peninsular_War" title="Peninsular War">fight against Spain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-otten_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otten-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Two days after he had to report at Lyons, he became ill and was hospitalized, during which time his draft left without him. Once released from the hospital, on 5 January, he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Roanne" title="Roanne">Roanne</a> for another draft.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991236-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He went into a church to pray, and fell behind the group. He met a young man who volunteered to guide him back to his group, but instead led him deep into the mountains of Le Forez, to the village of <a href="/wiki/Les_Noes" class="mw-redirect" title="Les Noes">Les Noes</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Desertion" title="Desertion">deserters</a> had gathered.<sup id="cite_ref-otten_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otten-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Vianney lived there for fourteen months,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> hidden in the byre attached to a farmhouse, and under the care of Claudine Fayot, a widow with four children. He assumed the name Jerome Vincent, and under that name, he opened a school for village children.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Since the harsh weather isolated the town during the winter, the deserters were safe from <a href="/wiki/Gendarme_(historical)" title="Gendarme (historical)">gendarmes</a>. However, after the snow melted, gendarmes came to the town constantly, searching for deserters. During these searches, Vianney hid inside stacks of fermenting hay in Fayot's barn.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>An imperial proclamation in March 1810 granted amnesty to all deserters,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> enabling Vianney to go back legally to Écully, where he resumed his studies. He was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> in 1811, and in 1812 he went to the <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> at <a href="/wiki/Verri%C3%A8res-en-Forez" title="Verrières-en-Forez">Verrières-en-Forez</a>. In autumn of 1813, he was sent to the major seminary at Lyons. Considered too slow, he was returned to Balley. However, Balley persuaded the <a href="/wiki/Vicar_general" title="Vicar general">vicar general</a> that Vianney's piety was great enough to compensate for his ignorance, and the seminarian received <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Subdiaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Subdiaconate">subdiaconate</a> on 2 July 1814, was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> in June 1815, and was ordained priest on 12 August 1815 in the <a href="/wiki/Couvent_des_Minimes_de_Grenoble" title="Couvent des Minimes de Grenoble">Couvent des Minimes de Grenoble</a>. He said his first Mass the next day,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and was appointed the assistant to Balley in Écully.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Cur.C3.A9_.28priest.29_of_Ars"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Curé_(priest)_of_Ars">Curé (priest) of Ars</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Vianney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Curé (priest) of Ars">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1818, shortly after the death of Balley, Vianney was appointed <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> of the parish of Ars, a town of 230 inhabitants.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> When Vianney's <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">bishop</a> first assigned him to Ars, Vianney got lost trying to find the town. Two young men tending flocks in the fields pointed him in the right direction.<sup id="cite_ref-heffernan_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heffernan-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> With Catherine Lassagne and Benedicta Lardet, he established <i>La Providence</i>, a home for girls.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As parish priest, Vianney realized that the Revolution's aftermath had resulted in religious ignorance and indifference, due to the devastation wrought on the Catholic Church in France. At the time, Sundays in rural areas were spent working in the fields, or dancing and drinking in taverns. Vianney spent time in the confessional and gave homilies against <a href="/wiki/Blasphemy" title="Blasphemy">blasphemy</a> and profane dancing.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> If his parishioners did not give up this dancing, he refused them <a href="/wiki/Absolution" title="Absolution">absolution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237–238_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991237–238-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> His stern sermons were later collected together in the famous "<a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Sermons_of_the_Cur%C3%A9_of_Ars" class="extiw" title="s:The Sermons of the Curé of Ars">Sermons of the Curé of Ars</a>," along with his moral <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Blessed_Cur%C3%A9_of_Ars_in_His_Catechetical_Instructions" class="extiw" title="s:The Blessed Curé of Ars in His Catechetical Instructions">Catechetical Instructions</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years">Later years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Vianney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg/150px-St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="84" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg/225px-St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg/300px-St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2047" data-file-height="1152" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_John_Vianney_at_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Church_Trumbull_CT.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A statue of Saint John Vianney was installed in the newly renovated sanctuary at St. Catherine of Siena Parish Church, Trumbull CT on March 24, 2019.</div></div></div><p>
Vianney came to be known internationally, and people from distant places began travelling to consult him as early as 1827. </p><blockquote><p>"By 1855, the number of pilgrims had reached 20,000 a year. During the last ten years of his life, he spent 16 to 18 hours a day in the confessional. Even the bishop forbade him to attend the annual retreats of the diocesan clergy because of the souls awaiting him yonder".<sup id="cite_ref-otten_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otten-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote><p>He spent at least 11 or 12 hours a day in the confessional during winter, and up to 16 in the summer.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991238_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991238-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
In his article "How does the Church Respond to Suicide?" Shaun McAfee references an incident described in the book <i>Cure of Ars:</i> </p><blockquote><p>"...a woman...told....Vianney that she was devastated because her husband had committed suicide. She wanted to approach the great priest but his line often lasted for hours and she could not reach him. She was ready to give up and in a moment of mystical insight that only a great saint can receive,...Vianney exclaimed through the crowd, “He is saved!” The woman was incredulous so the saint repeated, stressing each word, “I tell you he is saved. He is in Purgatory, and you must pray for him. Between the parapet of the bridge and the water he had time to make an act of contrition.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>”</p></blockquote><p>Vianney had a great devotion to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Philomena" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Philomena">Saint Philomena</a>. Vianney regarded her as his guardian and erected a chapel and shrine in honor of the saint. During May 1843, Vianney fell so ill he thought that his life was coming to its end. Vianney attributed his cure to her intercession.
</p><p>Vianney yearned for the contemplative life of a monk, and four times ran away from Ars, the last time in 1853.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991238_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991238-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> He was a champion of the poor as a <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Franciscan tertiary</a> and was a recipient of the coveted <a href="/wiki/French_Legion_of_Honour" class="mw-redirect" title="French Legion of Honour">French Legion of Honour</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-heffernan_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heffernan-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Vianney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1e/Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg/220px-Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="141" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1e/Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg/330px-Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1e/Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg/440px-Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg 2x" data-file-width="831" data-file-height="533" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Jean-Marie_Vianney.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The body of Saint John Mary Vianney, entombed above the main altar in the Basilica at <a href="/wiki/Ars-sur-Formans" title="Ars-sur-Formans">Ars</a>, France. The face is covered with a wax mask.</div></div></div>
<p>On 4 August 1859, Vianney died at the age of 73.<sup id="cite_ref-sisters_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sisters-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The bishop presided over his funeral with 300 priests and more than 6,000 people in attendance. Before he was buried, Vianney's body was fitted with a wax mask.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sermentizon_vianney.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Sermentizon_vianney.jpg/150px-Sermentizon_vianney.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="335" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Sermentizon_vianney.jpg/225px-Sermentizon_vianney.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Sermentizon_vianney.jpg/300px-Sermentizon_vianney.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1489" data-file-height="3329" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sermentizon_vianney.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Jean-Marie Vianney in the church of a small village in France</div></div></div>
<p>On 3 October 1874 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> proclaimed him "venerable"; on 8 January 1905, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> declared him <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> and proposed him as a model to the parochial clergy. In 1925 John Mary Vianney was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-sisters_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sisters-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> who in 1929 made him patron saint of parish priests.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991239_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1991239-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In 1928 his feast day was inserted into the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration on 9 August. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Code_of_Rubrics" title="Code of Rubrics">1960 revision</a>, in which the Vigil of Saint Lawrence had a high rank, moved the feast to 8 August. Finally, the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> placed it on 4 August, the day of his death.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 1959, to commemorate the centenary of John Vianney's death, Pope John XXIII issued the encyclical letter <i><a href="/wiki/Sacerdotii_nostri_primordia" title="Sacerdotii nostri primordia">Sacerdotii nostri primordia</a></i>. <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> visited Ars in person in 1986 in connection with the bicentenary of Vianney's birth and referred to him as a "rare example of a pastor acutely aware of his responsibilities … and a sign of courage for those who today experience the grace of being called to the priesthood."<sup id="cite_ref-heffernan_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heffernan-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In honor of the 150th anniversary of Vianney's death, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> declared a Year of the Priest, running from the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Feast of the Sacred Heart">Feast of the Sacred Heart</a> 2009–2010.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Postal_Service" class="mw-redirect" title="Vatican Postal Service">Vatican Postal Service</a> issued a set of stamps to commemorate the 150th Anniversary. With the following words on 16 June 2009, Benedict XVI officially marked the beginning of the year dedicated to priests, "…On the forthcoming Solemnity of the Most Sacred Heart of Jesus, Friday 19 June 2009 – a day traditionally devoted to prayer for the sanctification of the clergy –, I have decided to inaugurate a ‘Year of the Priest’ in celebration of the 150th anniversary of the <i>dies natalis</i> of John Mary Vianney, the Patron Saint of parish priests worldwide..."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope Benedict XVI declared 19 June 2009 – 19 June 2010 the Year of the Priests to encourage priests to strive for spiritual perfection.<sup id="cite_ref-Benedict_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benedict-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> In the Pope's words the Curé d'Ars is "a true example of a pastor at the service of Christ's flock."<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are statues of Vianney in many French churches and in Catholic churches throughout the world. Also, many parishes founded in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries are named after him. Some relics are kept in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Notre-Dame_de_la_Salette_in_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Notre-Dame de la Salette in Paris">Church of Notre-Dame de la Salette in Paris</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>John Vianney is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/August_4" title="August 4">4 August</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id=".22Heart_of_a_Priest.22_relic_tour"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Heart_of_a_Priest&quot;_relic_tour">"Heart of a Priest" relic tour</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Vianney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: &quot;Heart of a Priest&quot; relic tour">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church,_Trumbull,_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg" class="image"><img alt="A statue of St. John Vianney is visible on the left side of the photo" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church%2C_Trumbull%2C_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg/220px-Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church%2C_Trumbull%2C_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="159" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church%2C_Trumbull%2C_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg/330px-Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church%2C_Trumbull%2C_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church%2C_Trumbull%2C_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg/440px-Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church%2C_Trumbull%2C_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4500" data-file-height="3246" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Heart_of_St_John_Vianney_on_altar_of_St_Catherine_of_Siena_Parish_Church,_Trumbull,_CT_29_IV_2019.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A statue of Saint John Vianney is visible on the left side of the photo</div></div></div>
<p>In November 2018, Vianney's heart was transported to the United States for a 6-month nationwide tour.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> As Joe Bollig explained in his article detailing the relic's unexpected stop in Kansas City, "The heart receives special veneration because in Scripture it is considered to be a person’s hidden center of emotional, intellectual and moral activity. Saint John Vianney is said to have lived his life according to the heart of Christ and united his heart to Christ’s."<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> By the end of the tour, "the relic [had] traveled almost 36,000 miles — almost one and a half times the circumference of the Earth — and was available for over 1,200 hours of public veneration."<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The tour of the incorrupt heart of John Vianney came to the Parish of St. Catherine of Siena in historic Nichols, CT, on April 29, 2019,  with a liturgy celebrated by Daniel A. Cronin, Archbishop Emeritus of Hartford, and concelebrated by Joseph A. Marcello, pastor of St. Catherine of Siena.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> (Photos of the event available <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/stcathtrumbull/albums/72157706851708481">here</a>.)
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notable_institutions_carrying_his_name">Notable institutions carrying his name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Vianney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Notable institutions carrying his name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are dozens of institutions, including schools, seminaries, and churches named after Vianney in countries including Belize, Brazil, Canada, Guatemala, Haiti, Ireland, India, Pakistan, Philippines, England, United States, Nigeria, South Africa, New Zealand and Australia.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
