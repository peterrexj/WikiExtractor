					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Adelchis" redirects here. For other people named Adelchis, see <a href="/wiki/Adelchis_(given_name)" title="Adelchis (given name)">Adelchis (given name)</a>.</div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI,_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI%2C_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg/220px-ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI%2C_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="306" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI%2C_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg/330px-ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI%2C_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI%2C_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg/440px-ADALGISO_DE_PLATONI%2C_REY_DE_ITALIA.jpg 2x" data-file-width="460" data-file-height="640" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Emanuele_Tesauro" title="Emanuele Tesauro">Emanuele Tesauro</a>'s depiction of <i>Adalgisus Desiderii filius, Italiae rex, Romanus patritius</i> ("Adalgis, son of Desiderius, king of Italy, Roman patrician") from his <i>Del Regno d'Italia sotto i barbari</i> (1664).</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Adalgis</b> or <b>Adelchis</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;740</span> – 788) was an associate <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Lombards" class="mw-redirect" title="King of the Lombards">king of the Lombards</a> from August 759, reigning with his father, <a href="/wiki/Desiderius" title="Desiderius">Desiderius</a>, until their deposition in June 774.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His mother was <a href="/wiki/Ansa,_Queen_of_the_Lombards" title="Ansa, Queen of the Lombards">Ansa</a>. He is also remembered today as the hero of the play <i><a href="/wiki/Adelchi" title="Adelchi">Adelchi</a></i> (1822) by <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni" title="Alessandro Manzoni">Alessandro Manzoni</a>.
</p><p>In Desiderius' attempts to rekindle an alliance between the Lombards and Carolingians he proposed that Adalgis should marry Charlemagne's sister Gisela. Bachrach has suggested that this proposal was to undermine the Carolingian's relationship with the papacy.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When in 773 the Lombard kingdom was invaded by <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, the <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="King of the Franks">king of the Franks</a>, Desiderius stayed in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, the capital, where <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Pavia_(773%E2%80%9374)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Pavia (773–74)">he unsuccessfully resisted a siege</a>. Adalgis instead took refuge in <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a>, where he sheltered <a href="/wiki/Gerberga,_Queen_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="Gerberga, Queen of the Franks">the widow</a> and children of Charlemagne's younger brother, <a href="/wiki/Carloman_I" title="Carloman I">Carloman I</a>, who had entered an Italian monastery after abdicating the kingship.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Even before the fall of Pavia, when the Frankish army approached Verona, Adalgis did not resist. He escaped to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, where he was received by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman emperor">Eastern Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Constantine_V" title="Constantine V">Constantine V</a>, who raised him to the <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(ancient_Rome)" title="Patrician (ancient Rome)">patriciate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalgis hoped to return to re-conquer Italy, and solicited help from Duke <a href="/wiki/Arechis_II_of_Benevento" title="Arechis II of Benevento">Arechis II of Benevento</a> for this purpose. Many Lombards refused to submit to Frankish rule, believing that Adalgis's return was imminent.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The historian <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Deacon" title="Paul the Deacon">Paul the Deacon</a> reflected a widespread belief among the Lombards when he wrote, as part of his poetic epigraph for the tomb of Ansa, that "in her, by Christ, the greatest hope of the Lombards spent a time."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Einhard" title="Einhard">Einhard</a>, Charlemagne's biographer, also records that "on [Adalgis] all hope seemed to incline" (<i>in quem spes omnium inclinatae videbantur</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Only in 787, after the efforts of the <a href="/wiki/Empress_Irene" class="mw-redirect" title="Empress Irene">Empress Irene</a> to obtain the hand in marriage of Charlemagne's daughter <a href="/wiki/Rotrude" title="Rotrude">Rotrude</a> for her son, <a href="/wiki/Constantine_VI" title="Constantine VI">Constantine VI</a>, did the Romans move to give Adalgis the military assistance he required.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> An expeditionary corps was placed under the command of the <i><a href="/wiki/Saccellarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saccellarius">saccellarius</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Logotheta" class="mw-redirect" title="Logotheta">logotheta</a></i> Ioannes and augmented by troops from Sicily under the <i><a href="/wiki/Patrikios" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrikios">patrikios</a></i> Theodoros. The Roman army landed in Calabria towards the end of 788, but was met by the united armies of the Lombard dukes <a href="/wiki/Hildeprand_of_Spoleto" title="Hildeprand of Spoleto">Hildeprand of Spoleto</a> and <a href="/wiki/Grimoald_III_of_Benevento" title="Grimoald III of Benevento">Grimoald III of Benevento</a>, who had succeeded his father, Arechis, and made peace with the Franks. These Lombard forces were accompanied by Frankish troops under <a href="/wiki/Winiges_of_Spoleto" title="Winiges of Spoleto">Winiges</a>. In the ensuing battle the Romans were defeated, but there is no further record of the fate of Adalgis.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
