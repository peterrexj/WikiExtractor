<p><b>Saint Avoye of Sicily</b>, also known as Saint Auré or <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ewe" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ewe">Saint Ewe</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian martyr</a> from the 3rd century, who was originally from <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily, Italy</a>. She died in <a href="/wiki/Boulogne-sur-Mer" title="Boulogne-sur-Mer">Boulogne-sur-Mer, France</a> around <a href="/wiki/234" title="234">234</a>, according to tradition. She is recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Avoye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The most common interpretation of her life is that Saint Avoye was born in Sicily, to her father, Quintien, who was a Sicilian <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a>, and to her mother, Gérasine, a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a>, who was originally from the province of <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Avoye had eight siblings, both brothers and sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After praying for her beauty to be taken away so that she could remain a virgin, Avoye became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> so as to avoid temptation and potential suitors.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the year of her death, Avoye went to <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a> to assist her aunt <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ursula" title="Saint Ursula">Saint Ursula</a> at her wedding.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> There, Avoye discovered that Ursula had also consecrated herself to God and intended to remain a virgin.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The two fled to <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne, Germany</a>, where Ursula and her companions were martyred by a band of barbarians.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Avoye was spared from death by a barbarian who intended to marry her; however, Avoye refused, and was then imprisoned and deprived of food. She began to see weekly apparitions of the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Virgin Mary</a>, who brought her food which was "kneaded by the angels themselves."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Avoye was liberated from prison in an unknown manner, where she went to Boulogne-sur-Mer and was rediscovered by the barbarians, tortured, and martyred via decapitation.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There exist numerous other versions of the tradition of Saint Avoye, one of which alleges she was from England and escaped to Pluneret to become a hermit, whilst fleeing a Saxon whom her father wanted her to marry. Her father pursued her across the ocean, but she arrived in France safely.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cult">Cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Avoye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Avoye's cult can be found in the church of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Chapelle_Sainte-Avoye_de_Pluneret&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Chapelle Sainte-Avoye de Pluneret (page does not exist)">Chapelle Sainte-Avoye de Pluneret</a> which is in <a href="/wiki/Morbihan" title="Morbihan">Morbihan</a>, and also in <a href="/wiki/St_Ewe" title="St Ewe">St. Ewe, Cornwall</a>, a village which is named after her.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At the chapel in Pluneret, parents frequently bring toddlers and children with developmental delays in motor skills such as speaking and walking, so that their children can be sat in the stone boat of Avoye and blessed.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This stone boat is a relic which is relayed in tradition to have carried Avoye across the ocean from France to Cornwall.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her feast day is celebrated on 6 May,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> however, some sources state that Avoye's feast day is on 2 May<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> or other various days in early May.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
