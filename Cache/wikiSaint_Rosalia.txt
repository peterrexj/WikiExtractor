<p><b>Rosalia</b> (<small>Italian:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="it-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian" title="Help:IPA/Italian">[rozaˈliːa]</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Sicilian_language" title="Sicilian language">Sicilian</a>: <i lang="scn">Rusulìa</i>; 1130–1166), nicknamed <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">la Santuzza</i></span> ("the Little Saint"), is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> in Italy, <a href="/wiki/Camargo,_Chihuahua" title="Camargo, Chihuahua">Camargo</a> in Chihuahua, and three towns in <a href="/wiki/Venezuela" title="Venezuela">Venezuela</a>: <a href="/wiki/El_Hatillo_Municipality" title="El Hatillo Municipality">El Hatillo</a>,  <a href="/w/index.php?title=Zuata&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Zuata (page does not exist)">Zuata</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuata" class="extiw" title="es:Zuata">es</a>&#93;</span>, and <a href="/wiki/El_Play%C3%B3n,_Venezuela" title="El Playón, Venezuela">El Playón</a>. She is especially important internationally as a saint invoked in times of <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a>. From 2020 onwards she has been invoked by some citizens of Palermo to protect the city from <a href="/wiki/COVID-19" title="COVID-19">COVID-19</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Rosalia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Rosalia_statue_(Monterey,_California).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Saint_Rosalia_statue_%28Monterey%2C_California%29.jpg/120px-Saint_Rosalia_statue_%28Monterey%2C_California%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="120" height="193" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Saint_Rosalia_statue_%28Monterey%2C_California%29.jpg/180px-Saint_Rosalia_statue_%28Monterey%2C_California%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Saint_Rosalia_statue_%28Monterey%2C_California%29.jpg/240px-Saint_Rosalia_statue_%28Monterey%2C_California%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2115" data-file-height="3394" /></a><figcaption>A statue of Saint Rosalia in <a href="/wiki/Monterey,_California" title="Monterey, California">Monterey, California</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Rosalia was born of a <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> noble family that claimed descent from <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>. Devoutly religious, she retired to live as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in a cave on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Pellegrino" title="Mount Pellegrino">Mount Pellegrino</a>, where she died alone in 1166. Tradition says that she was led to the cave by two <a href="/wiki/Angels" class="mw-redirect" title="Angels">angels</a>. On the cave wall she wrote "I, Rosalia, daughter of Sinibald, Lord of <a href="/wiki/Monte_delle_Rose" title="Monte delle Rose">[Monte] delle Rose</a>, and Quisquina, have taken the resolution to live in this cave for the love of my Lord, Jesus Christ."<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="1624_plague">1624 plague</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Rosalia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: 1624 plague">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1624, a <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a> beset Palermo. During this hardship Rosalia reportedly appeared first to a sick woman, then to a hunter, to whom she indicated where her remains were to be found. She ordered him to bring her bones to Palermo and have them carried in procession through the city.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The hunter climbed the mountain and found her bones in the cave as described. He did what she had asked in the apparition. After her remains were carried around the city three times, the plague ceased. After this Rosalia was venerated as the patron saint of Palermo, and a sanctuary was built in the cave where her remains were discovered.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her post-1624 iconography is dominated by the work of the Flemish painter <a href="/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck" title="Anthony van Dyck">Anthony van Dyck</a>, who was trapped in the city during the 1624–1625 quarantine, during which time he produced five paintings of Rosalia, now in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rosalia_(Anthony_van_Dyck)" title="Saint Rosalia (Anthony van Dyck)">Madrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rosalia_Crowned_by_Angels_(Houston)" title="Saint Rosalia Crowned by Angels (Houston)">Houston</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rosalia_Crowned_by_Angels_(London)" title="Saint Rosalia Crowned by Angels (London)">London</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rosalie_Interceding_for_the_Plague%E2%80%93Stricken_of_Palermo" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Rosalie Interceding for the Plague–Stricken of Palermo">New York</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rosalia_Crowned_by_Angels_(Palermo)" title="Saint Rosalia Crowned by Angels (Palermo)">Palermo itself</a>. In 1629 he also produced <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Rosalia_Interceding_for_the_City_of_Palermo" title="Saint Rosalia Interceding for the City of Palermo">Saint Rosalia Interceding for the City of Palermo</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Coronation_of_Saint_Rosalia" class="mw-redirect" title="Coronation of Saint Rosalia">Coronation of Saint Rosalia</a></i> to assist Jesuit efforts to spread devotion to her beyond Sicily.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Rosalia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Rosalia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Santa_Rosalia.jpg/200px-Santa_Rosalia.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Santa_Rosalia.jpg/300px-Santa_Rosalia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Santa_Rosalia.jpg/400px-Santa_Rosalia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>A statue of St. Rosalia being carried through the streets of <a href="/wiki/Bivona" title="Bivona">Bivona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The feast of Saint Rosalia is on 4 September.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>, the Festino di Santa Rosalia is held each year on 14 July, and continues into the next day.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It is a major social and religious event in the city.
</p><p>The devotion to Santa Rosalia is widespread among the large and mainly Hindu <a href="/wiki/Tamils" title="Tamils">Tamil</a> community of Sri Lankan origin settled in Palermo.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 4 September, a tradition of walking barefoot from Palermo up to the <a href="/wiki/Santuario_di_Santa_Rosalia,_Palermo" title="Santuario di Santa Rosalia, Palermo">Sanctuary of Santa Rosalia</a> high up on Mount Pellegrino is observed in honor of Rosalia.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Italian-American" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian-American">Italian-American</a> communities in the United States, the July feast is generally dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Mount_Carmel" title="Our Lady of Mount Carmel">Our Lady of Mount Carmel</a><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> while the September feast, beginning in August, brings large numbers of visitors annually to the <a href="/wiki/Bensonhurst" class="mw-redirect" title="Bensonhurst">Bensonhurst</a> section of <a href="/wiki/Brooklyn" title="Brooklyn">Brooklyn</a> in New York City.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_biology">In biology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Rosalia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In biology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg/200px-Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="278" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg/300px-Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg/400px-Theodoor_Boeyermans_-_The_extasy_of_Saint_Rosalie_of_Palermo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1639" data-file-height="2281" /></a><figcaption><i>The ecstasy of Saint Rosalia of Palermo</i> by <a href="/wiki/Theodoor_Boeyermans" title="Theodoor Boeyermans">Theodoor Boeyermans</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Rosalia was proposed as the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Evolutionary_biology" title="Evolutionary biology">evolutionary studies</a> in a paper by <a href="/wiki/G._Evelyn_Hutchinson" title="G. Evelyn Hutchinson">G.E. Hutchinson</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> This was due to a visit he paid to a pool of water downstream from the cave where St. Rosalia's remains were found, where he developed ideas based on observations of <a href="/wiki/Corixidae" title="Corixidae">water boatman</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Rosalia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Rosalia was an important subject in Italian Renaissance and Baroque painting, particularly in sacre conversazioni (group pictures of saints flanking the Virgin Mary) by artists such as <a href="/wiki/Riccardo_Quartararo" title="Riccardo Quartararo">Riccardo Quartararo</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mario_di_Laurito&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Mario di Laurito (page does not exist)">Mario di Laurito</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vincenzo_La_Barbara" class="mw-redirect" title="Vincenzo La Barbara">Vincenzo La Barbara</a>, and possibly <a href="/wiki/Antonello_da_Messina" title="Antonello da Messina">Antonello da Messina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> But it was Flemish master <a href="/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck" title="Anthony van Dyck">Anthony van Dyck</a> (1599–1637), who was caught in Palermo during the 1624 plague, who produced the most paintings of her (see also above). His depictions – a young woman with flowing blonde hair, wearing a Franciscan cowl and reaching down toward the city of Palermo in its peril – became the standard iconography of Rosalia from that time onward. Van Dyck's series of St. Rosalia paintings have been studied by <a href="/wiki/Gauvin_Alexander_Bailey" title="Gauvin Alexander Bailey">Gauvin Alexander Bailey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Xavier_F._Salomon" title="Xavier F. Salomon">Xavier F. Salomon</a>, both of whom curated or co-curated exhibitions devoted to the theme of Italian art and the plague.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In March 2020, <i>The New York Times</i> published an article about the Metropolitan Museum of Art's painting of Saint Rosalia by Van Dyck in the context of <a href="/wiki/COVID-19" title="COVID-19">COVID-19</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
