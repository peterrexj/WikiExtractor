<p><b>Frederick I</b> was <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Utrecht" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Utrecht">Utrecht</a> between 815/816 and 834/838 AD, and is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>Frederick was born around 780 in <a href="/wiki/Friesland" title="Friesland">Friesland</a> and was a grandson of the Frisian <a href="/wiki/Radbod,_King_of_the_Frisians" class="mw-redirect" title="Radbod, King of the Frisians">King Radboud</a>. According to Church records, he died on 18 July 838 but other sources give dates between 834 and 838. In any case it is certain that he was murdered.
</p><p>At a young age he was taught at <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a> by the clergy, including Bishop <a href="/wiki/Ricfried" title="Ricfried">Ricfried</a>. After completing his studies he was ordained <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and put in charge of converting the remaining <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">heathens</a> in the northern areas of the diocese, but also in areas outside of the diocese. It is known that he preached at <a href="/wiki/Walcheren" title="Walcheren">Walcheren</a> and together with <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Odulfus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Odulfus (page does not exist)">St. Odulfus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Stavoren" title="Stavoren">Stavoren</a> and its surroundings.
</p><p>After the death of Ricfried in 815/816, Frederick was chosen as Bishop of Utrecht. He was known for his piety and erudition. He maintained a correspondence with <a href="/wiki/Rabanus_Maurus" title="Rabanus Maurus">Rabanus Maurus</a>. He was praised for his knowledge and understanding during the synod of <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a> in 829. The <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a><i>Vita S. Bonifacii</i> has been attributed to him.
</p><p>It is unclear exactly how Frederick came to an end. It has been established that he was murdered, but by whom and why is unclear. <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">Legend</a> tells that he was stabbed by two men after the offering of the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> on 18 July 838. According to the 11th and 12th century writers Bishop <a href="/wiki/Otbert_of_Li%C3%A8ge" title="Otbert of Liège">Otbert of Liège</a> (<i>Passio Frederici</i>) and <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>, the killers were hired by Empress <a href="/wiki/Judith_of_Bavaria_(died_843)" title="Judith of Bavaria (died 843)">Judith</a>, because of Frederick's regular criticism of her dissolute way of life. Later writers like <a href="/wiki/Cesare_Baronio" class="mw-redirect" title="Cesare Baronio">Cesare Baronio</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jean_Mabillon" title="Jean Mabillon">Jean Mabillon</a> write that the inhabitants of Walcheren, who were hostile to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, sent them as a response to Frederick's preaching there.
</p><p>The latter seems the most plausible. There are no sources of writers of the time showing that the empress was unchaste or immoral, or that Frederick had made that allegation. Moreover, Walcheren was quite hostile to the <a href="/wiki/Missionaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Missionaries">missionaries</a> from Utrecht.
</p><p>Shortly after his death, Frederick was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>. His feast day is 18 July and he is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the deaf. He was buried in <a href="/wiki/St._Salvator%27s_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Salvator&#39;s Church">St. Salvator's Church</a> in Utrecht.
</p>
