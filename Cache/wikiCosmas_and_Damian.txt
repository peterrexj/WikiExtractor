<p><b>Cosmas and Damian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">قُزما ودميان</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic" title="Romanization of Arabic">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Arabic-language romanization"><i lang="ar-Latn">Qozma wa Demyān</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Κοσμᾶς καὶ Δαμιανός</span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Cosmas et Damianus</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;3rd century</span>&#160;– c.<span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201; 287 or 303</span> AD) were two <a href="/wiki/Arabs" title="Arabs">Arab</a> physicians and early <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian martyrs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Elgood2010_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elgood2010-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> They practised their profession in the seaport of <a href="/wiki/Yumurtal%C4%B1k" title="Yumurtalık">Aegeae</a>, then in the Roman <a href="/wiki/Province" title="Province">province</a> of <a href="/wiki/Syria_(Roman_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Syria (Roman province)">Syria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Stracke_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stracke-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cosmas and Damian were third century Arabian-born twin brothers who embraced Christianity and practised medicine and surgery without a fee. This led them to being named <i>anargyroi</i> (from the Greek <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἀνάργυροι</span></span>, 'the silverless' or '<a href="/wiki/Holy_Unmercenaries" title="Holy Unmercenaries">unmercenaries</a>'); by this, they attracted many to the Christian faith.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> They reputedly cured blindness, fever, paralysis and reportedly expelled a breast serpent. They were arrested by Lysias, governor of Cilicia (modern day Çukurova, Turkey) during the Diocletian persecution because of their faith and fame as healers. Emperor Diocletian was a religious fanatic and favoured the pantheism of the Olympian Gods. He issued a series of edicts that condemned the Christians in his attempt to wipe out Christianity from his empire.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Lives">Lives</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Lives">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Gerard_Seghers,_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_(17th_century).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Gerard_Seghers%2C_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_%2817th_century%29.jpg/220px-Gerard_Seghers%2C_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_%2817th_century%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="185" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Gerard_Seghers%2C_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_%2817th_century%29.jpg/330px-Gerard_Seghers%2C_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_%2817th_century%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Gerard_Seghers%2C_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_%2817th_century%29.jpg/440px-Gerard_Seghers%2C_Saint_Cosmas_and_Saint_Damian_%2817th_century%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="449" data-file-height="377" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Cosmas and Saint Damian</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Gerard_Seghers" title="Gerard Seghers">Gerard Seghers</a>, 17th century, Private collection, U.S.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Nothing is known of their lives except that they suffered martyrdom in Syria during the persecution of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. 
According to Christian traditions, the twin brothers were born in Arabia and became skilled doctors.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saladino_d%27Ascoli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saladino d&#39;Ascoli (page does not exist)">Saladino d'Ascoli</a>, a 15th century Italian physician, claims that the medieval electuary, a pasty mass consisting of a drug mixed with sugar and water or honey suitable for oral administration,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> known as opopira,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> a complex compound medicine used to treat diverse maladies including paralysis, was invented by Cosmas and Damian.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocletian Persecution">persecution</a> under Diocletian, Cosmas and Damian were arrested by order of the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">Prefect</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cilicia_(Roman_province)" title="Cilicia (Roman province)">Cilicia</a>, one Lysias who is otherwise unknown, who ordered them under <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">torture</a> to recant. However, according to legend they stayed true to their faith, enduring being hung on a cross, stoned and shot by arrows and finally suffered execution by <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheading</a>. Anthimus, Leontius and Euprepius, their younger brothers, who were inseparable from them throughout life, shared in their martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-Stracke_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stracke-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg/220px-Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="365" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg/330px-Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg/440px-Beinwunder_Cosmas_und_Damian.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1658" data-file-height="2751" /></a><figcaption>Cosmas and Damian miraculously transplant the black leg of the Ethiopian onto the white body of the patient.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg/240px-Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="180" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg/360px-Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg/480px-Holy_Cosmas_and_holy_Damian_shrine_in_St._Michael_in_Munich.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a><figcaption>Reliquary (1400/1420) in <a href="/wiki/St._Michael%27s_Church,_Munich" title="St. Michael&#39;s Church, Munich">St. Michael's Church, Munich</a> containing the alleged skulls of Cosmas and Damian. The convent of the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a> in Madrid also has two skulls alleged to be of Cosmas and Damian.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pope_Felix_presents_church_to_Cosmas_and_Damian.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Pope_Felix_presents_church_to_Cosmas_and_Damian.jpg/220px-Pope_Felix_presents_church_to_Cosmas_and_Damian.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="303" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Pope_Felix_presents_church_to_Cosmas_and_Damian.jpg/330px-Pope_Felix_presents_church_to_Cosmas_and_Damian.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a3/Pope_Felix_presents_church_to_Cosmas_and_Damian.jpg 2x" data-file-width="399" data-file-height="549" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Pope_Felix_IV" title="Pope Felix IV">Pope Felix IV</a> presents Saints Cosmas and Damian with <a href="/wiki/Santi_Cosma_e_Damiano" title="Santi Cosma e Damiano">the basilica</a> he <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">rededicated</a> to them.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The veneration of Cosmas and Damian quickly spread beyond Constantinople; accounts of their martyrdom were rewritten by various authors such as <a href="/wiki/Andrew_of_Crete" title="Andrew of Crete">Andrew of Crete</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peter_the_Wonderworker" title="Peter the Wonderworker">Peter of Argos</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theodore_II_Laskaris" title="Theodore II Laskaris">Theodore II Laskaris</a>, and a certain Maximus around 1300. The legends are preserved also in Syriac, Coptic, Georgian, Armenian, and Latin.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As early as the 4th century, churches dedicated to the twin saints were established at <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Mesopotamia" title="Mesopotamia">Mesopotamia</a>. Devotion to the two saints spread rapidly in both East and West. <a href="/wiki/Theodoret" title="Theodoret">Theodoret</a> records the division of their reputed relics. Their relics, deemed miraculous, were buried in the city of <a href="/wiki/Cyrrhus" title="Cyrrhus">Cyrrhus</a> in Syria. Churches were built in their honor by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Proclus_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Proclus of Constantinople">Proclus</a> and by Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a> (527–565), who sumptuously restored the city of Cyrrhus and dedicated it to the twins, but brought their purported relics to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There, following his cure, ascribed to the intercession of Cosmas and Damian, Justinian, in gratitude also built and adorned their church at Constantinople,<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and it became a celebrated place of pilgrimage. At Rome, Pope <a href="/wiki/Felix_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix IV">Felix IV</a> (526–530) rededicated the Library of Peace (<i>Bibliotheca Pacis</i>) as a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Cosma_e_Damiano" title="Santi Cosma e Damiano">Santi Cosma e Damiano</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_forums" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial forums">Forum of Vespasian</a> in their honour. The church is much rebuilt but still famed for its sixth-century mosaics illustrating the saints.
</p><p>What are said to be their skulls are venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Convent_of_Las_Descalzas_Reales" title="Convent of Las Descalzas Reales">Convent of Las Descalzas Reales</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Poor_Ladies" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Poor Ladies">Clares</a> in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, where they have been since 1581, the gift of Maria, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Charles_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Charles V">Emperor Charles V</a>. They had previously been removed from Rome to <a href="/wiki/Bremen" title="Bremen">Bremen</a> in the tenth century, and thence to <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a>. 
Other skulls said to be theirs were discovered in 1334 by <a href="/wiki/Burchard_Grelle" title="Burchard Grelle">Burchard Grelle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Erzstift_Bremen" class="mw-redirect" title="Erzstift Bremen">Archbishop of Bremen</a>. He "personally 'miraculously' retrieved the relics of the holy physicians Cosmas and Damian, which were allegedly immured and forgotten in the choir of the <a href="/wiki/Bremen_Cathedral" title="Bremen Cathedral">Bremen Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In celebration of the retrieval Archbishop and <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">Chapter</a> arranged a feast at Pentecost 1335, when the relics were translated from the wall to a more dignified place.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Grelle claimed the relics were those Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Adaldag" title="Adaldag">Adaldag</a> brought from Rome in 965. The cathedral master-builder Johann Hemeling made a shrine for the relics, which was finished around 1420. The shrine, made from carved oak wood covered with gilt and rolled silver is considered an important mediaeval gold work.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1649 Bremen's Chapter, Lutheran by this time, sold the shrine without the heads to <a href="/wiki/Maximilian_I,_Elector_of_Bavaria" title="Maximilian I, Elector of Bavaria">Maximilian I of Bavaria</a>. The two heads remained in Bremen and came into the possession of the small Roman Catholic community. They were shown from 1934 to 1968 in the Church of St. Johann and in 1994 they were buried in the crypt.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The shrine is now shown in the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/St._Michael%27s_Church,_Munich" title="St. Michael&#39;s Church, Munich">church of St Michael</a> in <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>. At least since 1413 another supposed pair of skulls of the saints has been stored in <a href="/wiki/Stephansdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephansdom">St Stephens's Cathedral</a> in Vienna. Other relics are claimed by the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_San_Giorgio_Maggiore" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of San Giorgio Maggiore">Church of San Giorgio Maggiore</a> in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:CosmasDamianfraangelico.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/CosmasDamianfraangelico.jpg/220px-CosmasDamianfraangelico.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="176" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/CosmasDamianfraangelico.jpg/330px-CosmasDamianfraangelico.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8c/CosmasDamianfraangelico.jpg 2x" data-file-width="420" data-file-height="336" /></a><figcaption>The martyrdom of Saints Cosmas and Damian by <a href="/wiki/Fra_Angelico" title="Fra Angelico">Fra Angelico</a> (<a href="/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre" class="mw-redirect" title="Musée du Louvre">Musée du Louvre</a>, Paris).</figcaption></figure>
<p>The martyr twins are invoked in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a><sup id="cite_ref-Foley_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> in the prayer known as the <i>Communicantes</i> (from the first Latin word of the prayer): "In communion with the whole Church, they venerate above all others the memory of the glorious ever-virgin Mary, Mother of our God and Lord, Jesus Christ, then of blessed Joseph, husband of the Virgin, your blessed Apostles and Martyrs, Peter and Paul, Andrew, James, ...John and Paul, <i>Cosmas and Damian</i> and all your Saints: grant through their merits and prayers that in all things we may be defended by the help of your protection." They are also invoked in the <a href="/wiki/Litany_of_the_Saints" title="Litany of the Saints">Litany of the Saints</a>, and in the older form of the Roman rite, in the <a href="/wiki/Collect" title="Collect">Collect</a> for Thursday in the Third Week of Lent, as the <a href="/wiki/Station_days" title="Station days">station church</a> for this day is <a href="/wiki/Santi_Cosma_e_Damiano" title="Santi Cosma e Damiano">Santi Cosma e Damiano</a>.
</p><p>Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, which had been on 27 September, was moved in 1969 to 26 September because 27 September is the <i>dies natalis</i> ("day of birth" into Heaven) of <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a>, now more widely venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research" title="Wikipedia:No original research"><span title="The material near this tag possibly contains original research. (February 2023)">original research?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> In Canada it has been moved to 25 September (as 26 September is the Feast of the Canadian Martyrs in Canada).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sts Cosmas and Damian are regarded as the patrons of physicians, surgeons, and pharmacists and are sometimes represented with medical emblems. They are also regarded as the patron saints of twins.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_(Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons)_-_Walters_27572.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_%28Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons%29_-_Walters_27572.jpg/220px-French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_%28Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons%29_-_Walters_27572.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_%28Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons%29_-_Walters_27572.jpg/330px-French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_%28Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons%29_-_Walters_27572.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_%28Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons%29_-_Walters_27572.jpg/440px-French_-_Double_Capital_Depicting_Saints_Cosmas_and_Damian_%28Guild_of_Barber-Surgeons%29_-_Walters_27572.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1799" data-file-height="1353" /></a><figcaption>Cosmas and Damian are depicted as <a href="/wiki/Supporter" title="Supporter">supporters</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Escutcheon_(heraldry)" title="Escutcheon (heraldry)">arms</a> of the guild of barber-surgeons carved into a <a href="/wiki/Capital_(architecture)" title="Capital (architecture)">capital</a>, 15th century, from the Carmes monastery in <a href="/wiki/Trie-sur-Ba%C3%AFse" title="Trie-sur-Baïse">Trie-sur-Baïse</a> in southwestern France.  The inscription reads, "Saints Cosmas and Damian pray for us".</figcaption></figure>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>, the twin saints are regarded as protectors of children, and 27 September is commemorated, especially in <a href="/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro" title="Rio de Janeiro">Rio de Janeiro</a>, by giving children bags of candy with the saints' effigy printed on them and throughout the entire state of Bahia where Catholics and adepts of <a href="/wiki/Candomble" class="mw-redirect" title="Candomble">Candomblé</a> religion offer typical food such as <a href="/wiki/Caruru_(food)" title="Caruru (food)">caruru</a>.
The ritual consists of first offering the food to seven children that are no older than seven years old and then having them feast while sitting on the floor and eating with their hands. The Church of Saints Cosmas and Damian, in <a href="/wiki/Igarassu,_Pernambuco" title="Igarassu, Pernambuco">Igarassu</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pernambuco" title="Pernambuco">Pernambuco</a> is Brazil's oldest church, built in 1535.
</p><p>In the UK Damian is the <a href="/wiki/Dexter_and_sinister" title="Dexter and sinister">dexter</a> side supporter in the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> of the <a href="/wiki/British_Dental_Association" title="British Dental Association">British Dental Association</a>.
</p><p>Cosmas and Damian are venerated every year in <a href="/wiki/Utica,_New_York" title="Utica, New York">Utica, New York</a>, at St. Anthony's Parish during the annual pilgrimage which takes place on the last weekend of September (close to the 27 September feast day). There are thousands of pilgrims who come to honor the saints. Over 80 busloads come from Canada and other destinations. The 2-day festival includes music (La Banda Rosa), much Italian food, masses and processions through the streets of East Utica. It is one of the largest festivals honoring saints in the northeast USA.
</p>

<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox Churches</a>, Cosmas and Damian are venerated as a type of saint known as <a href="/wiki/Unmercenary_Physicians" class="mw-redirect" title="Unmercenary Physicians">Unmercenary Physicians</a> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">ἀνάργυροι</span>, <i>anargyroi,</i> <i>"without money"</i>). This classification of saints is unique to the Eastern Church and refers to those who heal purely out of love for God and man, strictly observing the command of Jesus: "Freely have you received, freely give." («Δωρεὰν ἐλάβετε, δωρεὰν δότε...» <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Matthew#10:8" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Matthew">Matthew 10:8</a>) While each of the Unmercenaries has his own <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast days</a>, all are commemorated together on the first Sunday in November, in a feast known as the <a href="/wiki/Synaxis" title="Synaxis">Synaxis</a> of the Unmercenary Physicians.
</p><p>The Orthodox celebrate no less than three different sets of saints by the name of Cosmas and Damian, each with their own distinct <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>:
</p>
<ul><li><b>Saints Cosmas and Damian of Cilicia (Arabia)</b> (<a href="/wiki/October_17_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="October 17 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">17 October</a>) Brothers, according to Christian legend they were beaten and beheaded together with three other Christians: Leontius, Anthimus, and Eutropius.</li>
<li><b>Saints Cosmas and Damian of Asia Minor</b> — alternately, <b>of Mesopotamia</b> (<a href="/wiki/November_1_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="November 1 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">1 November</a>) Twin sons of <a href="/wiki/Theodota_of_Philippi" title="Theodota of Philippi">Theodota of Philippi</a>. Died peacefully and were buried together at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Thereman&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Thereman (page does not exist)">Thereman</a> in Mesopotamia.</li>
<li><b>Saints Cosmas and Damian of Rome</b> (<a href="/wiki/July_1_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="July 1 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">1 July</a>) Brothers, according to Christian tradition they were <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> outside Rome by a jealous pagan physician during the reign of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Carinus" title="Carinus">Carinus</a> (283–284).</li></ul>
<p>Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icons</a> of the saints depict them vested as laymen holding medicine boxes. Often each will also hold a spoon with which to dispense medicine. The handle of the spoon is normally shaped like a cross to indicate the importance of spiritual as well as physical healing, and that all cures come from God.
</p>
