					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">5th century British saint</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Kea</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Confessor</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">probably <a href="/wiki/Lothian" title="Lothian">Lothian</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">early 6th century<br /><a href="/wiki/Cleder" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleder">Cleder</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>; <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican Communion</a>;<a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>&#160;;</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Cleder" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleder">Cleder</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">5 November</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data">hermit with a stag</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saint Kea</b> (<a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cornish_language" title="Cornish language">Cornish</a>: <i>Ke</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Ké</i>) was a late 5th-century <a href="/wiki/Britons_(historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (historical)">British</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> from the <i><a href="/wiki/Hen_Ogledd" title="Hen Ogledd">Hen Ogledd</a></i> ("Old North")—the <a href="/wiki/Brythonic_languages" class="mw-redirect" title="Brythonic languages">Brythonic</a>-speaking parts of what is now <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Lowlands" title="Scottish Lowlands">southern Scotland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Northern_England" title="Northern England">northern England</a>. According to tradition he was chiefly active in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, and his <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> was popular in those regions as well as throughout <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and the <a href="/wiki/West_Country" title="West Country">West Country</a>. St Fili or Filius, to whom the parish church of <a href="/wiki/Philleigh" title="Philleigh">Philleigh</a> is dedicated,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> probably came from Wales and is said to have been a companion of St Kea.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Kea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg/220px-Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="292" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg/330px-Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg/440px-Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="590" data-file-height="783" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Kea_assumed_travels.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Kea assumed travels</div></div></div>
<p>Kea is chiefly known through a <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> summary of a lost <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> written by Maurice of Cleder in the 17th century, as well as <i><a href="/wiki/Beunans_Ke" class="mw-redirect" title="Beunans Ke">Beunans Ke</a></i>, an incomplete 16th-century <a href="/wiki/Cornish-language" class="mw-redirect" title="Cornish-language">Cornish-language</a> <a href="/wiki/Mystery_play" title="Mystery play">play</a> rediscovered in 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to these, he was the son of King <a href="/wiki/King_Lot" title="King Lot">Lleuddun Luyddog</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lothian" title="Lothian">Lothian</a>, and served as <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> in North Britain before moving on to become a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>. He first went to <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and then moved south, founding churches at <a href="/wiki/Street,_Somerset" title="Street, Somerset">Street</a>, <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a> and <a href="/wiki/Landkey" title="Landkey">Landkey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> (holy place of St Kea). He finally settled at <a href="/wiki/Old_Kea" title="Old Kea">Old Kea</a> in Cornwall, which was subsequently named for him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was harassed by the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cornwall" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Cornwall">Cornish king</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tewdwr_Mawr" title="Tewdwr Mawr">Teudar</a>, when he sheltered a deer that Teudar was hunting. Having his oxen confiscated, he used the deer to plow the soil instead. He later travelled over the <a href="/wiki/English_Channel" title="English Channel">Channel</a> to <a href="/wiki/Cleder" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleder">Cleder</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, where he eventually died. In Brittany, he is known as "St. Quay".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The work also describes Kea's dealings with <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">King Arthurs</a> passage probably explains the Arthurian section in <i>Beunans Ke</i>, which describes Arthur's conflict with the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> emperor <a href="/wiki/Lucius_Tiberius" title="Lucius Tiberius">Lucius Hiberius</a> and Mordred's subsequent treachery.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg/220px-Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg/330px-Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg/440px-Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="427" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Plaque_at_Playing_Place_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1058923.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Plaque at Playing Place</div></div></div>
<p>An alternate legend describes Kea as an Irish monk, who, standing on the shore watching Christian missionaries depart for Cornwall, prayed that he not be left behind. At that point, the granite slab upon which he stood began to float and carried him across.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A similar tale is told of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran" title="Saint Piran">Saint Piran</a>, who is said to have floated safely over the water to land upon the sandy beach of Perranzabuloe in Cornwall.<sup id="cite_ref-st_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-st-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Plays featuring St Kea were performed at <a href="/wiki/Playing_Place" title="Playing Place">Playing Place</a>, where a plaque marks the <a href="/wiki/Plain-an-gwarry_(theatre)" class="mw-redirect" title="Plain-an-gwarry (theatre)">plain-an-gwarry</a> field in which they were staged.
</p>
