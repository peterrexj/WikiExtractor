<p><b>William Hart</b> (born at <a href="/wiki/Wells,_Somerset" title="Wells, Somerset">Wells</a>, 1558; executed at <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>, 15 March 1583) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1886. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Hart_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Elected Trappes Scholar at <a href="/wiki/Lincoln_College,_Oxford" title="Lincoln College, Oxford">Lincoln College</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oxford_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford University">Oxford</a>, 25 May 1571, he supplicated <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts" title="Bachelor of Arts">B.A.</a>, 18 June 1574. The same year he followed the rector, <a href="/wiki/John_Bridgewater" title="John Bridgewater">John Bridgewater</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Douai_College" class="mw-redirect" title="Douai College">Douai College</a>. He accompanied the college to <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, and returned there after a serious operation for kidney stones on 22 November 1578 at <a href="/wiki/Namur_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Namur (city)">Namur</a>. He took the college oath at the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Rome" title="English College, Rome">English College, Rome</a>, on 23 April 1579, where he was ordained priest. On 26 March 1581, he left Rome, arriving at Reims on 13 May, and resuming his journey on 22 May.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On reaching England he worked on a ministry in <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a>, frequently visiting imprisoned Catholics, providing comfort and whatever assistance he was able. He was present at the Mass at <a href="/wiki/York_Castle" title="York Castle">York Castle</a> at which <a href="/wiki/William_Lacy_(Catholic_priest)" title="William Lacy (Catholic priest)">William Lacy</a> was captured, and escaped by getting down the wall and wading through the moat up to his chin.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Betrayed by an <a href="/wiki/Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostate">apostate</a> Catholic on Christmas Day, 1582, and thrown into an underground dungeon, he was put into double irons. After examination before the Dean of York and the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_the_North" title="Council of the North">Council of the North</a>, he was arraigned at the Lent Assizes.
</p><p>The account of his trial states that he was arraigned on two counts. He might have been on trial on three, namely:
</p>
<ol><li>under the <a href="/wiki/Bulls,_etc.,_from_Rome_Act_1571" title="Bulls, etc., from Rome Act 1571">Bulls, etc., from Rome Act 1571</a>, for having brought papal writings, to wit his certificate of ordination, into the realm;</li>
<li>under <a href="/wiki/13_Eliz._1" class="mw-redirect" title="13 Eliz. 1">13 Eliz. 1</a>. c. 3, for having gone abroad without royal licence; and</li>
<li>under the <a href="/wiki/Religion_Act_1580" title="Religion Act 1580">Religion Act 1580</a>, for having reconciled John Wright and one Couling to the Catholic Church.</li></ol>
<p>On what counts he was found guilty does not clearly appear, but he was certainly guilty of the second.
</p>
