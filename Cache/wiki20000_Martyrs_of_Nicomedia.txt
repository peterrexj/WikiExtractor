<p>The <b>20,000 Martyrs of Nicomedia</b> refers to victims of <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians" title="Persecution of Christians">persecution of Christians</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nicomedia" title="Nicomedia">Nicomedia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a> (modern Izmit, Turkey) by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> Emperors <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> in the early 4th century AD.
</p><p>According to various <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a> and <a href="/wiki/Menologion" class="mw-redirect" title="Menologion">menologion</a>, the persecution included the burning of a church that held numerous Christians on <a href="/wiki/Christmas_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Christmas Day">Christmas Day</a>.
</p><p>This event took place when the emperor Maximian (284-305) returned with victory over Ethiopians in 304 AD. It happened after they had refused to sacrifice to idols during Christmas Mass in order to thank gods for the victory he had acquired.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Later Maximian and his soldiers entered the church and told the Christians they could escape punishment if they renounced Christ. The Christian priest Glycerius answered that the Christians would never "renounce their faith, even under the threat of torture". Maximian ordered him to be burned to death. Those who had not been burned in the church were captured and tortured to death. The bishop <a href="/wiki/Anthimus_of_Nicomedia" title="Anthimus of Nicomedia">Anthimos</a> who had escaped burning in the church was captured and beheaded.
</p><p>The number 20,000 may be apocryphal.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh2007_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh2007-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, the martyrs of Nicomedia continue to be honored with feast days:<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh2007_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh2007-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> they are commemorated on 28 December in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and by the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Catholic">Byzantine Catholic</a> Churches.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, there are separate entries for groups of martyrs of Nicomedia. The martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Anthimus_of_Nicomedia" title="Anthimus of Nicomedia">Anthimus of Nicomedia</a> and companions is commemorated on 24 April and "the commemoration of many holy martyrs of Nicomedia" on June 23.
</p>
