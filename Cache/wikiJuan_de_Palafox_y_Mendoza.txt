<p><b>Juan de Palafox y Mendoza</b> (26 June 1600 – 1 October 1659) was a Spanish politician, administrator, and Catholic clergyman in 17th century <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and a viceroy of <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>.
</p><p>Palafox was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Puebla" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Puebla">Bishop of Puebla</a> (1640−1655), and the interim <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mexico" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mexico">Archbishop of Mexico</a> (1640−1642). He also held political office, from 10 June 1642 to 23 November 1642 as the <a href="/wiki/Viceroy_of_New_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Viceroy of New Spain">Viceroy of New Spain</a>. He lost a high-profile struggle with the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> in New Spain, resulting in a recall to Spain, to the minor <a href="/wiki/See_of_Osma" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Osma">Diocese of Osma</a> in <a href="/wiki/Old_Castile" title="Old Castile">Old Castile</a>. Although a case was opened for his beatification shortly after he died in 1659, he was not designated "Blessed" until 2011.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Navarre" title="Navarre">Navarre</a>, Spain, Don Juan Palafox y Mendoza was the natural son ("a child of transgression") of Jaime de Palafox, the Marquis of Ariaza, of the Aragonese nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His mother became a Carmelite nun. He was taken in by a family of millers who gave him the name "Juan" and raised him for ten years, after which his father recognized him, and had him educated at Alcalá and Salamanca.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1626 he was a deputy of the nobility in the Cortes de <a href="/wiki/Monz%C3%B3n" title="Monzón">Monzón</a>, and later a prosecutor at the Council of War and a member of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_the_Indies" title="Council of the Indies">Council of the Indies</a>, the chief administrative body for administration of the overseas territories of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Empire" title="Spanish Empire">Spanish Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecclesiastical_career">Ecclesiastical career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ecclesiastical career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Palafox was ordained in 1629, and became the chaplain of <a href="/wiki/Maria_of_Austria,_Holy_Roman_Empress" title="Maria of Austria, Holy Roman Empress">Maria of Austria, Holy Roman Empress</a>, the sister of King <a href="/wiki/Philip_IV_of_Spain" title="Philip IV of Spain">Philip IV of Spain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He accompanied her on her various trips around Europe.
</p><p>In 1639 Philip IV nominated him, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> appointed him, as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Puebla" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Puebla">Bishop of Puebla de los Ángeles</a> in viceroyal Mexico. <a href="/wiki/Puebla_de_los_%C3%81ngeles" class="mw-redirect" title="Puebla de los Ángeles">Puebla de los Ángeles</a> was the second largest city in the <a href="/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Viceroyalty of New Spain">Viceroyalty of New Spain</a> (viceroyal México) then, and is the present day <a href="/wiki/Puebla,_Puebla" class="mw-redirect" title="Puebla, Puebla">City of Puebla</a>. He was consecrated as a Bishop in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> on 27 December 1639.
</p><p>He left for America and arrived in <a href="/wiki/Veracruz" title="Veracruz">Veracruz</a> on 24 June 1640. He was in the company of the new <a href="/wiki/Viceroy_of_New_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Viceroy of New Spain">Viceroy of New Spain</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diego_L%C3%B3pez_Pacheco,_7th_Duke_of_Escalona" title="Diego López Pacheco, 7th Duke of Escalona">Diego López Pacheco, 7th Duke of Escalona</a>, whom he had gotten to know during the voyage. That same ship brought an Irishman, <a href="/wiki/William_Lamport" title="William Lamport">William Lamport</a>, known in New Spain as Don Guillén de Lombardo y Guzmán, who played a role in political turmoil during the 1640s. Palafox was also named <i>Visitador</i> (royal inspector, representative of the king), to investigate the two previous viceroys. His retinue further included the Aragonese painter <a href="/wiki/Pedro_Garc%C3%ADa_Ferrer" title="Pedro García Ferrer">Pedro García Ferrer</a> and the Flemish painter <a href="/wiki/Diego_de_Borgraf" title="Diego de Borgraf">Diego de Borgraf</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-diego_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diego-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He served as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Puebla" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Puebla">Bishop of Puebla</a> from 1640 to 1655, and as interim archbishop of Mexico from 1642 to 1643.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jesuits_controversy_and_recall">Jesuits controversy and recall</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Jesuits controversy and recall">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was embroiled in a major controversy with the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> over ecclesiastical jurisdiction that eventually cost him his post as Bishop of Puebla de los Ángeles. The Spanish crown was moving to displace mendicant orders from their populous and lucrative <i>doctrinas</i> in central Mexico, and replace them with parishes staffed by secular (diocesan) clergy with benefices rather than mendicants.  He was largely successful in doing so in Puebla.
</p><p>He then targeted the Jesuits as another entity that did not respect ecclesiastical jurisdiction by paying tithes, essentially a 10% tax on agricultural production, to the Church hierarchy.  In the 1640s when he took on the Jesuits, Palafox pointed out that the Jesuit order was a hugely wealthy landowner in New Spain.  Jesuits claimed that the income from their haciendas went exclusively toward support of their educational institutions (<i>colegios</i>) and their missionary work on the colonial frontiers.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> On principle, Palafox asserted that it was the spiritual duty of all to pay the tithe, which the Jesuits steadfastly refused to do. The tithe transferred wealth from the countryside's landed estates to cities and towns, supporting the cathedral chapter, parish priests, and charitable institutions.
</p><p>Obviously, as a powerful bishop, Palafox would have been interested in increasing the revenue from Jesuit tithes, but also in asserting episcopal authority over that order. In 1647, the diocese of Puebla ordered all Jesuits to produce licenses from the diocese to preach and hear confession, something that was required under canon law and empowered bishops.  The Jesuits asserted they needed no such licenses, that they could exercise such powers without special permission of a bishop. Palafox wrote that if this were true, that the bishop had no power in his own diocese and he would be separated from his own flock by "an alien authority".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Jesuits found an ally against Palafox in the new viceroy, <a href="/wiki/Garc%C3%ADa_Sarmiento_de_Sotomayor,_2nd_Count_of_Salvatierra" title="García Sarmiento de Sotomayor, 2nd Count of Salvatierra">García Sarmiento de Sotomayor, 2nd Count of Salvatierra</a>.  Salvatierra sought to arrest Palafox.  In 1647, rather than be arrested, which might have produced an uprising in Puebla against the viceroy's abuse of authority, Palafox fled to the mountains outside the city.  The move was calculated to show the crown that the situation in New Spain was grave, that the viceroy and the Jesuits were challenging the rightful place of episcopal authority.  In that he failed and was ultimately humiliated by being recalled to Spain.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg/220px-Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="324" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg/330px-Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg/440px-Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2058" data-file-height="3027" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Diego_de_Borgraf_-_Portrait_of_Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Portrait of Juan de Palafox y Mendoza</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Diego_de_Borgraf" title="Diego de Borgraf">Diego de Borgraf</a>, 1643</div></div></div>
<p>Palafox laid formal complaints against the Jesuits at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, however, refused to approve his censures, and all he could obtain was a brief from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_X" title="Pope Innocent X">Pope Innocent X</a> (on 14 May 1648), commanding the Jesuits to respect the episcopal jurisdiction. On 20 May 1655, Palafox and the Jesuits signed an accord, but disagreements continued. In the same year the Jesuits succeeded in securing his transfer to the little <a href="/wiki/See_of_Osma" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Osma">see of Osma</a> in <a href="/wiki/Old_Castile" title="Old Castile">Old Castile</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Although Palafox's ecclesiastical career went into eclipse, his writings against the Jesuits were subsequently published in France and in the eighteenth century, his writings were used to strengthen the case for regalist authority resulting in the expulsion of the Jesuits from Spain and Spanish territories in 1767.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some of Palafox's influential anti-Jesuit writings deals with the <a href="/wiki/Chinese_Rites_controversy" title="Chinese Rites controversy">Chinese Rites controversy</a>. Palafox had jurisdiction as a bishop on certain Asian missions, but - according to Costa Rican scholar Ricardo Martínez Esquivel - the main reason he declared the Jesuit's tolerance for traditional ancestor worship practices among Chinese converts to Christianity as heretic was "his personal conflict" with the Jesuits.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Viceregal_legacy">Viceregal legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Viceregal legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Palafox founded the <a href="/wiki/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana" title="Biblioteca Palafoxiana">Biblioteca Palafoxiana</a>, the first public library in the <a href="/wiki/Americas" title="Americas">Americas</a>, on 5 September 1646, stocking it with five thousand books of science and philosophy. He also founded the Dominican Convent of Santa Inés, the Colleges of San Pedro and San Pablo, and the girls school Purísima Concepción. He amended the by-laws of the seminary of San Juan, and worked diligently on completing the cathedral, which was dedicated 18 April 1649.
</p><p>As bishop, Palafox y Mendoza distinguished himself by his efforts to protect the <a href="/wiki/Native_American_(Americas)" class="mw-redirect" title="Native American (Americas)">Native Americans</a>, forbidding any methods of conversion other than persuasion.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Despite being well disposed toward the indigenous, he showed no interest in their history, which others of that era used to bolster notions of a long and distinguished history that predated the Spanish conquest, nor did he attribute anything spiritually special about the New World.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While bishop, Palafox was the principal consecrator of <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Diego_D%C3%ADaz_de_Quintanilla_y_de_Hev%C3%ADa_y_Vald%C3%A9s" title="Francisco Diego Díaz de Quintanilla y de Hevía y Valdés">Francisco Diego Díaz de Quintanilla y de Hevía y Valdés</a> (1640), <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Durango" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Durango">Bishop of Durango</a>; <a href="/wiki/Fernando_Montero_Espinosa" title="Fernando Montero Espinosa">Fernando Montero Espinosa</a> (1640), <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Nueva_Segovia" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Nueva Segovia">Bishop of Nueva Segovia</a>; <a href="/wiki/Diego_de_Guevara_y_Estrada" title="Diego de Guevara y Estrada">Diego de Guevara y Estrada</a> (1642), <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Santo_Domingo" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Santo Domingo">Archbishop of Santo Domingo</a>; <a href="/wiki/Marcos_de_Torres_y_Rueda" title="Marcos de Torres y Rueda">Marcos de Torres y Rueda</a> (1645), <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Yucat%C3%A1n" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Yucatán">Bishop of Yucatán</a>; <a href="/wiki/Juan_de_Ma%C3%B1ozca_y_Zamora" title="Juan de Mañozca y Zamora">Juan de Mañozca y Zamora</a> (1645), <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Mexico" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Mexico">Archbishop of México</a>; and <a href="/wiki/Diego_L%C3%B3pez_de_la_Vega" title="Diego López de la Vega">Diego López de la Vega</a> (1650), <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_M%C3%A9rida-Badajoz" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Mérida-Badajoz">Bishop of Badajoz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathHierPalafox_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathHierPalafox-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg/220px-Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="329" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg/330px-Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg/440px-Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3872" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Biblioteca_Palafoxiana_de_Puebla.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Biblioteca_Palafoxiana" title="Biblioteca Palafoxiana">Biblioteca Palafoxiana</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Puebla,_Puebla" class="mw-redirect" title="Puebla, Puebla">Puebla, Puebla</a></div></div></div>
<p>Palafox was an enthusiastic patron of the arts, and it was during his tenure in Puebla that the city became the musical center of New Spain. Composers such as <a href="/wiki/Juan_Gutierrez_de_Padilla" class="mw-redirect" title="Juan Gutierrez de Padilla">Juan Gutierrez de Padilla</a>, <i>maestro di capilla</i> of the cathedral under Palafox and the most famous seventeenth century composer in Mexico, brought the latest European music styles to the New World. Palafox also strongly believed in education in general.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_career">Political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As visitador general, Bishop Palafox had powers to inspect practices in the viceroyalty, but the viceroy himself was protected from the inspector-general's inquiries, thus undermining his ability to pursue effective reform.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Palafox's general mission was "to increase efficiency in government, strengthen royal authority, maximize the extraction of resources, and improve the administration of the viceroyalty", especially toward increasing the revenues for the crown.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Blocked from effective reform, Palafox broke with Viceroy <a href="/wiki/Diego_L%C3%B3pez_Pacheco_Cabrera_y_Bobadilla" class="mw-redirect" title="Diego López Pacheco Cabrera y Bobadilla">Diego López Pacheco Cabrera y Bobadilla</a> in 1642, accusing the viceroy of being in league with <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_Restoration_War" title="Portuguese Restoration War">at war with Spain</a> to restore its independence following the <a href="/wiki/Iberian_Union" title="Iberian Union">Iberian Union</a>; the viceroy was a cousin of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Braganza" title="House of Braganza">Duke of Braganza</a>, since acclaimed king as <a href="/wiki/John_IV_of_Portugal" title="John IV of Portugal">John IV of Portugal</a>. One scholar has characterized the suspicion of the viceroy's conspiring with rebels as being based on "slender evidence".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Palafox claimed to have orders from the Crown, although he did not show them. He arrived secretly in the capital, and in the middle of the night of 9/10 June, he met with the <a href="/wiki/Audiencia_Real" class="mw-redirect" title="Audiencia Real">Audiencia</a> (high court) and laid out his suspicions. He then ordered that the viceregal palace be surrounded by guards. The following morning Viceroy López Pacheco was informed that he was under arrest and that the bishop had been named archbishop of Mexico and viceroy of New Spain. His possessions were confiscated and he was held for some time before being allowed to return to Spain. In Spain he was acquitted of the charges against him.
</p><p>During his brief term as viceroy, Palafox established the laws governing the University, the <a href="/wiki/Audiencia_Real" class="mw-redirect" title="Audiencia Real">Audiencia</a>, and the legal profession. Palafox considered a key duty of the viceroy was to serve as president of the Audiencia, the high court, the seat of justice. Palafox wrote that the viceroy as president of the Audiencia should prudently treat the judges (<i>oidores</i>) with the greatest respect, but also "preserve the authority and superiority of the head" i.e., the president/viceroy.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Two members of the Audiencia rejected his reforms, and he suspended them from office. Palafox also raised twelve companies of militia to protect the colony against the spread of revolution from Portugal and <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a>, which was in revolt against the Spanish monarchy. He destroyed the pagan religious statues of the Indians that had been kept in the capital as trophies of the Spanish conquest.
</p><p>He was succeeded as viceroy by <a href="/wiki/Garc%C3%ADa_Sarmiento_de_Sotomayor,_2nd_Count_of_Salvatierra" title="García Sarmiento de Sotomayor, 2nd Count of Salvatierra">García Sarmiento de Sotomayor, 2nd Count of Salvatierra</a> on 23 November 1642, but continued to hold the post of visitador. Having a bishop serve as viceroy was not the usual pattern of appointment, but the extraordinary circumstances that prompted to crown to precipitously remove the previous viceroy meant that Palafox was a useful, if temporary, replacement until the crown appointed his successor.  The high tension between the new viceroy, Salvatierra, and bishop and visitador general Palafox was not unprecedented however.  In 1624 the viceroy the marquis of Gelves had ordered the expulsion of the archbishop from the viceroyalty, in clear terms the civil authority challenging the ecclesiastical.  That earlier conflict had resulted in a huge riot in the main plaza of Mexico City and the ouster of the viceroy himself.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The conflict between Salvatierra and Palafox, who was then acting as visitador, flared over what might seem a trivial matter, whether or not the viceroy could sit on a cushion when seated with the Audiencia.  Palafox said no, since it distinguished the viceroy from the high court judges. However, the practice had been standard with earlier viceroys. Where the performance of power and its prerogatives was important not as minor traditions but as the theater of power, such a conflict was seated in deeper issues.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following the example of an earlier Spanish ecclesiastic in Mexico, <a href="/wiki/Juan_Gonz%C3%A1lez_de_Mendoza" title="Juan González de Mendoza">Juan González de Mendoza</a>, Juan de Palafox y Mendoza authored a book on China. His <i>Historia de la conquista de la China por el Tartaro</i> (<i>History of the Conquest of China by the Tartars</i>) reported on the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_the_Ming_Dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Fall of the Ming Dynasty">conquest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ming_Dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Ming Dynasty">Ming China</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Manchu_people" title="Manchu people">Manchus</a>, based on reports that reached Mexico by the way of the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>. 
The work was first <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k574970">published in Spanish</a> in Paris in 1670; a French translation appeared the same year. An English translation, whose full title was <i><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://luna.folger.edu/luna/servlet/detail/FOLGERCM1~6~6~283291~120756:The-history-of-the-conquest-of-Chin">The History of the Conquest of China by the Tartars together with an Account of Several Remarkable things, Concerning the Religion, Manners, and Customs of Both Nation's, but especially the Latter</a></i>, appeared in London in 1676. Palafox's work, based on hearsay, was generally less informed than <i>De bello tartarico</i>, an eyewitness account by the Chinese-speaking Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Martino_Martini" title="Martino Martini">Martino Martini</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His writings were published in 15 volumes in Madrid in 1762.
</p><p>Recently a bi-lingual edition of his observations on Mexican Indians has been published under the title <i>Virtues of the Indian/Virtudes del Indio</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cause_of_beatification_and_canonization">Cause of beatification and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Palafox_y_Mendoza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Cause of beatification and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1694 <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_Spain" title="Charles II of Spain">Charles II of Spain</a> petitioned for his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>; the decree allowing the introduction of the cause of beatification was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> in 1726, and in 1758, under Pope <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XIV" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XIV">Benedict XIV</a>, the procedure for the approval of Palafox's writings was initiated. The process was continued under the Pontificates of <a href="/wiki/Clement_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement XIII">Clement XIII</a> and <a href="/wiki/Clement_XIV" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement XIV">Clement XIV</a>.
</p><p>However, though the process passed through the preliminary stages, securing for Palafox the title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>, the cause was in effect blocked under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> through the intervention of the Jesuits.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> A vote by the Congregation then responsible for the cause was taken on 28 February 1777 and twenty-six out of forty one prelates favored the continuation of Palafox's cause of beatification with the proclamation of a decree of heroic virtue; the decree was then submitted to Pope Pius VI for approbation; Pius VI, however, decided to suspend the final decision.
</p><p>The cause thus was suspended in 1777 and remained so until 2003, when it was restored under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>; the question of the heroic virtue was returned to the consideration of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints. On 17 January 2009, Pope <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XVI">Benedict XVI</a> approved the decree of the said Congregation recognizing Palafox's heroic virtue, thus granting him the title of <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>. On 8 January 2010, the Congregation of the Causes of Saints accepted a miracle attributed to Palafox's intercession. The decree recognizing the miracle was promulgated by Pope Benedict XVI on 27 March 2010. The cause for his beatification was supported by the Diocese of Osma-Soria in Spain, where he spent the last years of his life and ecclesiastical career, following his unsuccessful struggle with the Jesuits in his Diocese of Puebla in New Spain (Mexico).
</p><p>As the long process for holy recognition of Palafox by Vatican, stretching from the late seventeenth century to the early twenty-first, it is clear that there were authorities opposed to his cause. The cause for his beatification likely found favor with John Paul II and his successor Benedict XVI.  Both popes strongly advocated for the episcopal authority of the Catholic Church against secular authority, the position that Palafox advocated when he served in Mexico. Popes have considerable authority to delay or fast track causes for beatification and canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> While Pope John Paul II often announced beatifications during papal visits, a wildly popular move local holy people are so honored, Benedict XVI discontinued the practice of going to the announcing beatifications in person in the home locale. The ceremony for the beatification of Palafox was overseen by the Papal Legate, Cardinal Angelo Amato, Prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Juan de Palafox was finally proclaimed Blessed on 5 June 2011. The rite of beatification was presided over by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, Prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of the Saints, by mandate of Pope Benedict XVI. Palafox's feast day is 6 October.
</p>
