					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">English Roman Catholic priest and martyr</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Blessed Edward Waterson</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">London</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><span style="display:none">(<span class="dday deathdate">1594-01-07</span>)</span>7 January 1594<br />Newcastle upon Tyne</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Edward Waterson</b> (? – 7 January 1594 (<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">NS</a>)) was an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a> priest and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.  He served the hidden Catholics in England during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a>. Edward was arrested in 1593 and executed at <a href="/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne" title="Newcastle upon Tyne">Newcastle upon Tyne</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1929.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Waterson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in London, Waterson was brought up in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>. As a young man he travelled to Turkey with some English merchants. In 1588, on his return, he stopped in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and was brought into the Catholic Church there by <a href="/wiki/Richard_Smith_(bishop)" title="Richard Smith (bishop)">Richard Smith</a>. The Pilgrim-book of the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> records his stay there, 29 November-11 December, 1588. Waterson proceeded to the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, arriving there 24 January, 1589. He received the tonsure and minor orders on 18 August, 1590, subdiaconate on 21 September, 1591, diaconate on 24 February, 1592, and was ordained priest 11 March 1592.<sup id="cite_ref-Burton_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In summer 1592 Waterson returned to England, where legal restrictions on Catholics were severe, in order to minister to hidden Catholics.  <sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Lambton" title="Joseph Lambton">Joseph Lambton</a>, a young Catholic priest who was on the same ship, was arrested upon landing, but Waterson escaped.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, he was captured by the authorities in midsummer 1593. Lambton was executed 31 July 1592. The sheriff then took part of the quartered remains and showed them to Waterson in an effort to frighten him, but Waterson viewed them as holy relics. Waterson was held until just after Christmas (<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">OS</a>), when he was <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>, as a traitor. When he was tied to the hurdle to be drawn to the place of execution, the horse would not move, so he had to be brought on foot. While incarcerated in the Newgate prison, Newcastle, he had attempted to escape by burning down his cell door.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
