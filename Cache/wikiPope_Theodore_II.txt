<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>

<p><b>Pope Theodore II</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Theodorus II</i>; 840 – December 897) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> for twenty days in December 897. His short reign occurred during a period of partisan strife in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, which was entangled with a period of violence and disorder in <a href="/wiki/Central_Italy" title="Central Italy">central Italy</a>. His main act as pope was to annul the recent <a href="/wiki/Cadaver_Synod" title="Cadaver Synod">Cadaver Synod</a>, therefore reinstating the acts and ordinations of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Pope Formosus</a>, which had themselves been annulled by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_VI" title="Pope Stephen VI">Pope Stephen VI</a>. He also had the body of Formosus recovered from the river <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a> and reburied with honour. He died in office in late December 897.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Theodore_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known of Theodore's background; he is recorded as being born a Roman, and the son of Photios. His brother Theodosius (or Theosius) was also a bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-kw115_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kw115-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Theodore was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> as a priest by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_V" title="Pope Stephen V">Pope Stephen V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In January 897, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_VI" title="Pope Stephen VI">Pope Stephen VI</a> held what is known as the <a href="/wiki/Cadaver_Synod" title="Cadaver Synod">Cadaver Synod</a>. Because his predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Formosus" class="mw-redirect" title="Formosus">Formosus</a>, sided with <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Carinthia" title="Arnulf of Carinthia">Arnulf of Carinthia</a> rather than Stephen's ally, <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Spoleto" class="mw-redirect" title="Lambert of Spoleto">Lambert of Spoleto</a>, in their struggle for the imperial dignity, Stephen had the corpse of Formosus exhumed and tried for "perjury, violating the canons prohibiting the <a href="/wiki/Translation_of_bishops" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation of bishops">translation of bishops</a>, and coveting the papacy".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The dead pope was found guilty, his body thrown in the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a>, and all his acts and ordinations were annulled. Supporters of Formosus rebelled and deposed Stephen VI. His successor, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Romanus" title="Pope Romanus">Romanus</a>, is generally assumed to have been pro-Formosus, but he too was soon deposed.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Theodore_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theodore II was elected to succeed the deposed Romanus as <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>. The exact dates of Theodore II's reign are unknown, but modern sources generally agree that he was pope for twenty days during December 897.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-kw115_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kw115-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Flodoard" title="Flodoard">Flodoard</a>, a tenth-century French chronicler, only credited Theodore with a twelve-day reign,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> while in his history of the popes, <a href="/wiki/Alexis-Fran%C3%A7ois_Artaud_de_Montor" title="Alexis-François Artaud de Montor">Alexis-François Artaud de Montor</a> listed Theodore's reign as being twenty days, from 12 February to 3 March 898.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Like Romanus, Theodore was a supporter of Formosus. Some historians believe that Romanus had been deposed because he had not acted to restore Formosus' honour quickly enough, though others suggest that he was removed by supporters of Stephen VI. In either case, Theodore immediately threw himself into the task of undoing the Cadaver Synod. He called his own synod, which annulled the rulings set out by Stephen VI. In so doing, he restored the acts and ordinations of Formosus, including the restoration of a large number of clergy and bishops to their offices.<sup id="cite_ref-kw115_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kw115-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Theodore also ordered Formosus' body to be recovered from the harbour of <a href="/wiki/Portus" title="Portus">Portus</a>, where it had been secretly buried, and restored to the original grave at <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">Old St. Peter's Basilica</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kw115_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kw115-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Like Romanus before him, Theodore bestowed a <a href="/wiki/Privilege_(canon_law)" class="mw-redirect" title="Privilege (canon law)">privilege</a> upon the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Grado" title="Patriarch of Grado">See of Grado</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and had a coin minted, bearing the name of Lambert on the obverse, and "<a href="/wiki/Scs._Petrus" class="mw-redirect" title="Scs. Petrus">Scs. Petrus</a>" and "Thedr." on the reverse.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Flodoard cast Theodore in a positive light, describing him as "beloved of the clergy, a friend of peace, temperate, chaste, affable and a great lover of the poor."<sup id="cite_ref-mann_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He died in office, though the cause of his death is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-kw115_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kw115-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Because of this, some writers, such as Wendy Reardon,<sup id="cite_ref-reardon_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reardon-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> suggest the possibility of foul play.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Horace Kinder Mann offers a different suggestion in his papal history, noting that it is possible that popes who were "infirm or even older than [...] their predecessors" might have been elected intentionally.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Theodore was buried at St. Peter's Basilica, but <a href="/wiki/List_of_non-extant_papal_tombs" title="List of non-extant papal tombs">his tomb was destroyed</a> during the demolition of the old basilica in the seventeenth century.<sup id="cite_ref-reardon_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-reardon-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Aftermath">Aftermath</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Theodore_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Aftermath">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Theodore's death, both <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_IX" title="Pope John IX">John IX</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_III" title="Pope Sergius III">Sergius III</a> claimed to have been elected pope; the latter was excommunicated and driven from the city, though he did later become pope in 904. John IX held synods reaffirming that of Theodore II, and he further banned the trial of people after their death.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In turn, Sergius III later annulled the synods of Theodore II and John IX, and reinstated the validity of the Cadaver Synod.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
