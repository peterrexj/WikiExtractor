<p><b>Saint Cecilia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Sancta Caecilia</i>), also spelled <b>Cecelia</b>, was a Roman virgin martyr and is venerated in <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican</a>, and some <a href="/wiki/Lutheran" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran">Lutheran</a> churches, such as the Church of Sweden.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She became the patroness of music and musicians, it being written that, as the musicians played at her wedding, Cecilia "sang in her heart to the Lord".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELovewell1898_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELovewell1898-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Musical compositions are dedicated to her, and her feast, on 22 November,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> is the occasion of concerts and musical festivals.
</p><p>Saint Cecilia is one of several virgin martyrs commemorated by name in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Catholic">Latin</a> Church. The church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Cecilia_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Cecilia in Trastevere">Santa Cecilia in Trastevere</a>, founded in the 3rd century by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_I" title="Pope Urban I">Urban I</a>, is believed to be on the site of the house where she lived and died.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:192px;"><a href="/wiki/File:CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg/190px-CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg" decoding="async" width="190" height="222" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg/285px-CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg/380px-CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg 2x" data-file-width="770" data-file-height="900" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:CeciliaValerianTiburtius.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saints Cecilia, Valerian, and Tiburtius</i> by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Botticini" title="Francesco Botticini">Botticini</a></div></div></div>
<p>It is popularly supposed that Cecilia was a noble lady of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a><sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who, with her husband Valerian, his brother Tiburtius, and a Roman soldier named Maximus, suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a> in about 230, under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Severus" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexander Severus">Alexander Severus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMason1917307_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMason1917307-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_de_Rossi" title="Giovanni Battista de Rossi">Giovanni Battista de Rossi</a>, however, argues that instead she perished in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a> between 176 and 180, citing the report of <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Poitiers">Bishop of Poitiers</a> (d. 600).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the story, despite her vow of virginity, her parents forced her to marry a pagan nobleman named <a href="/wiki/Tiburtius,_Valerian,_and_Maximus" title="Tiburtius, Valerian, and Maximus">Valerian</a>. During the wedding, Cecilia sat apart singing to God in her heart, and for that, she was later declared the saint of musicians.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When the time came for her marriage to be consummated, Cecilia told Valerian that watching over her was an <a href="/wiki/Angel_of_the_Lord" title="Angel of the Lord">angel of the Lord</a>, who would punish him if he sexually violated her but would love him if he respected her virginity. When Valerian asked to see the angel, Cecilia replied that he could see the angel if he would go to the third milestone on the <a href="/wiki/Appian_Way" title="Appian Way">Via Appia</a> and be baptized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_I" title="Pope Urban I">Pope Urban I</a>. After following Cecilia's advice, he saw the angel standing beside her, crowning her with a <a href="/wiki/Wreath_(attire)" title="Wreath (attire)">chaplet</a> of roses and lilies.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:192px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_Rafa%C3%ABl_Santi_(1483-1520)_-_Heilige_Cecilia_in_extase_met_Paulus,_Johannes_(evangelist),_Augustinus_en_Maria_Magdalena_-_26-04-2012_9-13-18.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_Rafa%C3%ABl_Santi_%281483-1520%29_-_Heilige_Cecilia_in_extase_met_Paulus%2C_Johannes_%28evangelist%29%2C_Augustinus_en_Maria_Magdalena_-_26-04-2012_9-13-18.jpg/190px-thumbnail.jpg" decoding="async" width="190" height="292" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_Rafa%C3%ABl_Santi_%281483-1520%29_-_Heilige_Cecilia_in_extase_met_Paulus%2C_Johannes_%28evangelist%29%2C_Augustinus_en_Maria_Magdalena_-_26-04-2012_9-13-18.jpg/285px-thumbnail.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_Rafa%C3%ABl_Santi_%281483-1520%29_-_Heilige_Cecilia_in_extase_met_Paulus%2C_Johannes_%28evangelist%29%2C_Augustinus_en_Maria_Magdalena_-_26-04-2012_9-13-18.jpg/380px-thumbnail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2123" data-file-height="3263" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bologna_Pinacoteca_Nazionale_-_Rafa%C3%ABl_Santi_(1483-1520)_-_Heilige_Cecilia_in_extase_met_Paulus,_Johannes_(evangelist),_Augustinus_en_Maria_Magdalena_-_26-04-2012_9-13-18.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i><a href="/wiki/The_Ecstasy_of_St._Cecilia_(Raphael)" title="The Ecstasy of St. Cecilia (Raphael)">The Ecstasy of St. Cecilia</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Raphael" title="Raphael">Raphael</a></div></div></div>
<p>The martyrdom of Cecilia is said to have followed that of her husband Valerian and his brother at the hands of the prefect Turcius Almachius.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The legend about Cecilia's death says that after being struck three times on the neck with a sword, she lived for three days, and asked the pope to convert her home into a church.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Cecilia was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb_of_Callixtus" title="Catacomb of Callixtus">Catacomb of Callixtus</a> and later transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Cecilia_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Cecilia in Trastevere">Church of Santa Cecilia in Trastevere</a>. In 1599, her body was found still <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>, seeming to be asleep.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cecilia is one of the most famous Roman martyrs, although some elements of the stories recounted about her do not appear in the source material.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Johann_Peter_Kirsch" title="Johann Peter Kirsch">Johann Peter Kirsch</a>, the existence of the martyr is a historical fact. At the same time, some details bear the mark of a pious romance, like many other similar accounts compiled in the fifth and sixth centuries. The relation between Cecilia and Valerian, Tiburtius, and Maximus, mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Martyrs" title="Acts of the Martyrs">Acts of the Martyrs</a>, has some historical foundation. Her feast day has been celebrated since about the fourth century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1908_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1908-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> There is no mention of Cecilia in the <i><a href="/wiki/Depositio_martyrum" class="mw-redirect" title="Depositio martyrum">Depositio Martyrum</a></i>, but there is a record of an early Roman church founded by a lady of this name, Santa Cecilia in <a href="/wiki/Trastevere" title="Trastevere">Trastevere</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Santa_Cecilia_in_Trastevere">Santa Cecilia in Trastevere</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Santa Cecilia in Trastevere">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Santa_Cecilia_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Cecilia in Trastevere">Santa Cecilia in Trastevere</a></div>
<p>The church of Santa Cecilia in Trastevere is reputedly built on the site of the house in which she lived. The original church was constructed in the fourth century; during the ninth century, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_I" title="Pope Paschal I">Pope Paschal I</a> had remains that were supposedly hers buried there. In 1599, while leading a renovation of the church, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Paolo_Emilio_Sfondrati" title="Paolo Emilio Sfondrati">Paolo Emilio Sfondrati</a> had the remains, which he reported to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>, excavated and reburied.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Name">Name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name "Cecilia" applied generally to Roman women who belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Plebs" class="mw-redirect" title="Plebs">plebeian</a> <a href="/wiki/Gens" title="Gens">clan</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Caecilia_gens" title="Caecilia gens">Caecilii</a>. Legends and hagiographies, mistaking it for a personal name, suggest fanciful etymologies. Among those cited by <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="Geoffrey Chaucer">Chaucer</a> in "<a href="/wiki/The_Second_Nun%27s_Tale" title="The Second Nun&#39;s Tale">The Second Nun's Tale</a>" are: lily of heaven, the way for the blind, contemplation of <a href="/wiki/Heaven" title="Heaven">heaven</a> and the active life, as if lacking in blindness, and a heaven for people to gaze upon.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patroness_of_musicians">Patroness of musicians</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patroness of musicians">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco,_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel,_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627,_NGA_46172.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco%2C_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel%2C_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627%2C_NGA_46172.jpg/260px-Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco%2C_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel%2C_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627%2C_NGA_46172.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="212" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco%2C_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel%2C_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627%2C_NGA_46172.jpg/390px-Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco%2C_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel%2C_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627%2C_NGA_46172.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco%2C_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel%2C_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627%2C_NGA_46172.jpg/520px-Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco%2C_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel%2C_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627%2C_NGA_46172.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="3255" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Orazio_Gentileschi_and_Giovanni_Lanfranco,_Saint_Cecilia_and_an_Angel,_c._1617-1618_and_c._1621-1627,_NGA_46172.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Orazio_Gentileschi" title="Orazio Gentileschi">Orazio Gentileschi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Lanfranco" title="Giovanni Lanfranco">Giovanni Lanfranco</a>, <i>Saint Cecilia and an Angel</i>, c. 1617–1618 and c. 1621–1627, <a href="/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art" title="National Gallery of Art">National Gallery of Art</a>, Washington, D.C.</div></div></div>
<p>The first record of a music festival in her honour was held at <a href="/wiki/%C3%89vreux" title="Évreux">Évreux</a> in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> in 1570.<sup id="cite_ref-academy_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-academy-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_di_Santa_Cecilia" title="Accademia Nazionale di Santa Cecilia">Accademia Nazionale di Santa Cecilia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> is one of the oldest musical institutions in the world. It was founded by the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a>, <i>Ratione congruit</i>, issued by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V" title="Pope Sixtus V">Sixtus V</a> in 1585, which invoked two saints prominent in Western musical history: <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Gregory the Great</a>, after whom <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">Gregorian chant</a> is named, and Saint Cecilia, the patron saint of music.
</p><p>Her feast day became an occasion for musical concerts and festivals that occasioned well-known poems by <a href="/wiki/John_Dryden" title="John Dryden">John Dryden</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Pope" title="Alexander Pope">Alexander Pope</a><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and music by <a href="/wiki/Henry_Purcell" title="Henry Purcell">Henry Purcell</a> (<i><a href="/wiki/Hail!_Bright_Cecilia" title="Hail! Bright Cecilia">Ode to St. Cecilia</a></i>); 3 different <a href="/wiki/Oratorios" class="mw-redirect" title="Oratorios">oratorios</a> by <a href="/wiki/Marc-Antoine_Charpentier" title="Marc-Antoine Charpentier">Marc-Antoine Charpentier</a>, <i>Caecilia virgo et martyr octo vocibus</i> H.397, for soloists, double Chorus, double string orchestra and bc, <i>Cecilia virgo et martyr</i> H.413, for soloists, chorus, 2 treble instruments and bc, and <i>Caecilia virgo et martyr</i> H.415, for soloists, chorus , 2 treble instruments and bc, to libretti probably written by <a href="/wiki/Philippe_Goibaut" title="Philippe Goibaut">Philippe Goibaut</a>); <a href="/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel" title="George Frideric Handel">George Frideric Handel</a> (<i><a href="/wiki/Ode_for_St._Cecilia%27s_Day_(Handel)" title="Ode for St. Cecilia&#39;s Day (Handel)">Ode for St. Cecilia's Day</a></i>; <i><a href="/wiki/Alexander%27s_Feast_(Handel)" title="Alexander&#39;s Feast (Handel)">Alexander's Feast</a></i>); <a href="/wiki/Charles_Gounod" title="Charles Gounod">Charles Gounod</a> (<i><a href="/wiki/St._Cecilia_Mass" title="St. Cecilia Mass">St. Cecilia Mass</a></i>); as well as <a href="/wiki/Benjamin_Britten" title="Benjamin Britten">Benjamin Britten</a>, who was born on her feast day (<i><a href="/wiki/Hymn_to_St_Cecilia" title="Hymn to St Cecilia">Hymn to St Cecilia</a></i>, based on a poem by <a href="/wiki/W._H._Auden" title="W. H. Auden">W. H. Auden</a>). Herbert Howells' <i>A Hymn to Saint Cecilia</i> has words by Ursula Vaughan Williams; <a href="/wiki/Gerald_Finzi" title="Gerald Finzi">Gerald Finzi</a>'s "For Saint Cecilia", Op. 30, was set to verses written by Edmund Blunden; <a href="/wiki/Michael_Hurd_(composer)" title="Michael Hurd (composer)">Michael Hurd's</a> 1966 composition "A Hymn to Saint Cecilia"<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> sets John Dryden's poem; and <a href="/wiki/Frederik_Magle" title="Frederik Magle">Frederik Magle</a>'s <i>Cantata to Saint Cecilia</i> is based on the history of Cecilia.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> <i>The Heavenly Life</i>, a poem from <i><a href="/wiki/Des_Knaben_Wunderhorn" title="Des Knaben Wunderhorn">Des Knaben Wunderhorn</a></i> (which <a href="/wiki/Gustav_Mahler" title="Gustav Mahler">Gustav Mahler</a> used in his <i><a href="/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Mahler)" title="Symphony No. 4 (Mahler)">Symphony No. 4</a></i>) mentions that "Cecilia and all her relations make excellent court musicians."
</p><p>From the name of Cecilia comes <i>Cecyliada</i>, the name of the festival of sacred, choral, and contemporary music, held from 1994 in <a href="/wiki/Police,_West_Pomeranian_Voivodeship" title="Police, West Pomeranian Voivodeship">Police, Poland</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:192px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg/190px-Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg" decoding="async" width="190" height="261" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg/285px-Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg/380px-Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg 2x" data-file-width="948" data-file-height="1300" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Carlo_Saraceni_-_The_Martyrdom_of_St_Cecilia_-_WGA20831.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Martyrdom of St Cecilia</i> by <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Saraceni" title="Carlo Saraceni">Carlo Saraceni</a> (c. 1610)</div></div></div>
<p>Cecilia symbolizes the central role of music in the liturgy.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Cistercian <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> of the convent nearby Santa Cecilia in Trastevere shear lambs' wool to be woven in the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">palliums</a> of new metropolitan archbishops. The lambs are raised by the <a href="/wiki/Trappist" class="mw-redirect" title="Trappist">Trappists</a> of the Abbey <a href="/wiki/Tre_Fontane" class="mw-redirect" title="Tre Fontane">Tre Fontane</a> in Rome. The Pope blesses the lambs every 21 January, the Feast of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Agnes" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Agnes">Saint Agnes</a>. The pallia are given by the Pope to the new metropolitan archbishops on the <a href="/wiki/Solemnity_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Solemnity of Saints Peter and Paul">Solemnity of Saints Peter and Paul</a>, 29 June.
</p><p>Located on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._Cecilia%27s_Abbey,_Ryde" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Cecilia&#39;s Abbey, Ryde">St. Cecilia's Abbey, Ryde</a> was founded in 1882. The nuns live a traditional monastic life of prayer, work, and study in accordance with the ancient <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia#Rule_of_St._Benedict" title="Benedict of Nursia">Rule of St. Benedict</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The famous luthier <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Vuillaume" title="Jean-Baptiste Vuillaume">Jean-Baptiste Vuillaume</a> produces a line of violin and viola under the name St. Cécile with a decal stamped on the upper back.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cecilia is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemoration</a> on 22 November.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> She is honored on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Rome" title="Agnes of Rome">Agnes of Rome</a> on January 21.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cecilia is frequently depicted playing the viola, a <a href="/wiki/Portative_organ" title="Portative organ">portative organ</a>, or other musical instruments,<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> evidently to express what was often attributed to her, namely that while the musicians played at her nuptials, she sang in her heart to God, though the organ may be misattributed to her,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1908_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1908-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> as the result of a mistranslation.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A miniature Saint Cecilia beneath <a href="/wiki/Worcester_Cathedral" title="Worcester Cathedral">Worcester Cathedral</a> was featured on the reverse side of the Sir <a href="/wiki/Edward_Elgar" title="Edward Elgar">Edward Elgar</a> <a href="/wiki/Bank_of_England_%C2%A320_note" title="Bank of England £20 note">£20 banknote</a>, which was withdrawn by the <a href="/wiki/Bank_of_England" title="Bank of England">Bank of England</a> in 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span id="Renaissance.2C_baroque_and_classical_music"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Renaissance,_baroque_and_classical_music">Renaissance, baroque and classical music</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Renaissance, baroque and classical music">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Marc-Antoine_Charpentier" title="Marc-Antoine Charpentier">Marc-Antoine Charpentier</a> composed four <i>Histoires sacrées</i> using a text thought to have been written by his colleague <a href="/wiki/Philippe_Goibaut" title="Philippe Goibaut">Philippe Goibaut</a> des Bois La Grugère:
<ul><li><i>In honorem Caeciliae, Valeriani et Tiburtij canticum</i> H.394 for three voices, two treble instruments, and continuo (1675&#160;?).</li>
<li><i>Caecilia virgo et martyr</i> octo vocibus H.397 for soloists, double chorus, double orchestra, and continuo (1677–78).</li>
<li><i>Caecilia virgo et martyr</i>, H.413 for soloists, chorus, and two treble instruments  (1683–85).</li>
<li><i>Caecilia virgo et martyr</i> H.415 – H.415 a for soloists, chorus, and two treble instruments (1686).</li></ul></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Henry_Purcell" title="Henry Purcell">Henry Purcell</a>, Laudate Ceciliam (1683), <a href="/wiki/Welcome_to_all_the_pleasures" title="Welcome to all the pleasures">Welcome to all the pleasures</a> (1683), Raise, raise the voice (c1685) and <a href="/wiki/Hail!_Bright_Cecilia" title="Hail! Bright Cecilia">Hail! Bright Cecilia</a> (1692).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9bastien_de_Brossard" title="Sébastien de Brossard">Sébastien de Brossard</a>, "Canticle for Saint Cécila" SdB.9 (1720&#160;?)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Scarlatti" title="Alessandro Scarlatti">Alessandro Scarlatti</a> <i>Il martirio di santa Cecilia</i>, oratorio donné pour la première fois le <abbr>1<sup>er</sup></abbr> mars 1708; <i>Messa di Santa Cecilia</i>(1720).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel" title="George Frideric Handel">Georg Friedrich Haendel</a> composed two works for Saint Cecilia with <a href="/wiki/John_Dryden" title="John Dryden">John Dryden</a>: The Oratorio Alexander's Feast or The Power of Music (1736) and Ode for St. Cecilia's Day (1739).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Joseph_Haydn" title="Joseph Haydn">Joseph Haydn</a>, <i>Missa Sanctae Caeciliae</i> ou <i>Missa Cellensis in honorem Beatissimae Virginis Mariae (1766–67).</i></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_Hiller" title="Ferdinand Hiller">Ferdinand Hiller</a>, <i>St. Cäcilia</i> (1848), cantata for soloists and orchestra to the text by <a href="/wiki/Wolfgang_M%C3%BCller_von_K%C3%B6nigswinter" title="Wolfgang Müller von Königswinter">Wolfgang Müller von Königswinter</a>.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Charles_Gounod" title="Charles Gounod">Charles Gounod</a>, <i>Hymne à Sainte Cécile</i>, CG 557 (1865) for solo violin and orchestra<sup id="cite_ref-IMSLP-Gounod_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IMSLP-Gounod-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Contemporary_music">Contemporary music</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cecilia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Contemporary music">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Judith_Shatin" title="Judith Shatin">Judith Shatin</a> wrote <i>The Passion of Saint Cecilia</i> for piano and orchestra<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> and <i>Fantasy on Saint Cecilia</i><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> for solo piano.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Fred_Momotenko" class="mw-redirect" title="Fred Momotenko">Fred Momotenko</a> composed <i>"Cecilia"</i>, a composition for full mixed choir, "a hymn to the past as well as to the future of the monastic tradition". The world premiere was at <a href="/wiki/Koningshoeven_Abbey" title="Koningshoeven Abbey">Koningshoeven Abbey</a> on Saint Cecilia's feast day, 2014.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Benjamin_Britten" title="Benjamin Britten">Benjamin Britten</a> wrote a <a href="/wiki/Hymn_to_St_Cecilia" title="Hymn to St Cecilia">Hymn to St Cecilia</a>, a setting for the poem by <a href="/wiki/W._H._Auden" title="W. H. Auden">W. H. Auden</a>.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Paul_Simon" title="Paul Simon">Paul Simon</a> wrote the 1970 song "<a href="/wiki/Cecilia_(Simon_%26_Garfunkel_song)" title="Cecilia (Simon &amp; Garfunkel song)">Cecilia</a>" which title refers to the patron saint of music.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Lou_Harrison" title="Lou Harrison">Lou Harrison</a> wrote his <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mass_for_St._Cecilia%27s_Day&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Mass for St. Cecilia&#39;s Day (page does not exist)">Mass for St. Cecilia's Day</a> for choir, harp, and drone (1983–86).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Stalk-Forrest_Group" class="mw-redirect" title="Stalk-Forrest Group">Stalk-Forrest Group</a> (later name changed to Blue Öyster Cult), recorded a song St. Cecilia, the EP was later released under the SFG name as the St.Cecilia sessions.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Arvo_P%C3%A4rt" title="Arvo Pärt">Arvo Pärt</a> was commissioned to compose a work for the <a href="/wiki/Great_Jubilee" title="Great Jubilee">Great Jubilee</a> in Rome in 2000, and wrote <span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it"><a href="/wiki/Cecilia,_vergine_romana" title="Cecilia, vergine romana">Cecilia, vergine romana</a></i></span> (Cecilia, Roman virgin) for mixed choir and orchestra. The Italian text deals with the saint's life and martyrdom. It was first performed on 19 November 2000, close to her feast day, by the <a href="/wiki/Accademia_Nazionale_di_Santa_Cecilia" title="Accademia Nazionale di Santa Cecilia">Accademia Nazionale di Santa Cecilia</a> conducted by <a href="/wiki/Myung-whun_Chung" title="Myung-whun Chung">Myung-whun Chung</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Gerald_Finzi" title="Gerald Finzi">Gerald Finzi</a> composed "For St. Cecilia" for solo tenor, chorus (SATB), and orchestra.  Setting of a work by English poet and author <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Blunden" title="Edmund Blunden">Edmund Blunden</a>. Duration ca 18 minutes.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Herbert_Howells" title="Herbert Howells">Herbert Howells</a> composed his "A Hymn for Saint Cecilia" for choir and organ in 1960, as commissioned by the <a href="/wiki/Worshipful_Company_of_Musicians" title="Worshipful Company of Musicians">Worshipful Company of Musicians</a>, with a text by <a href="/wiki/Ursula_Vaughan_Williams" title="Ursula Vaughan Williams">Ursula Vaughan Williams</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>On the 2015 Feast of Saint Cecilia, <a href="/wiki/Foo_Fighters" title="Foo Fighters">Foo Fighters</a> released their EP "Saint Cecilia" for free download via their website. The five-song EP features a track named after the EP "Saint Cecilia". The EP was recorded during an impromptu studio session at Hotel Saint Cecilia located in Austin, Texas.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>Informator Choristarum</i> (organist and master of the choristers) at <a href="/wiki/Magdalen_College,_Oxford" title="Magdalen College, Oxford">Magdalen College</a>, Oxford (1957–1981), <a href="/wiki/Bernard_Rose_(musician)" title="Bernard Rose (musician)">Bernard Rose</a>'s unaccompanied anthem for SATB choir (with divisions) <i>Feast Song For St. Cecilia</i> (1974) is a setting a poem of the same name by his son, musician Gregory Rose.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/E._Florence_Whitlock" title="E. Florence Whitlock">E. Florence Whitlock</a> composed <i>Ode to St. Cecilia, Opus 5</i>, based on text by John Dryden, in 1958.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Singer/Songwriter/Guitarist, Rik Emmett, composed the song "Calling St. Cecilia" on his 1992 LP Ipso Facto.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Blue_%C3%96yster_Cult" title="Blue Öyster Cult">Blue Öyster Cult</a> release song “The Return of St. Cecilia” on their 2020 album “The Symbol Remains”</li>
<li>The Chicago band Turnt (now known as Everybody All The Time) released a song called Girls which refers to St Cecilia in the lyrics. The song was first performed at Northwestern University's Mayfest Battle of the Bands on Friday 24 May 2013 at 27 Live in downtown Evanston.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>On 2008 <a href="/wiki/Brian_Eno" title="Brian Eno">Brian Eno</a>'s and <a href="/wiki/David_Byrne" title="David Byrne">David Byrne</a>'s album "<a href="/wiki/Everything_That_Happens_Will_Happen_Today" title="Everything That Happens Will Happen Today">Everything That Happens Will Happen Today</a>", Cecilia is referred to in the song "The River".<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Iceage" title="Iceage">Iceage</a> release song “Dear Saint Cecilia” on their 2021 album <a href="/wiki/Seek_Shelter" title="Seek Shelter">Seek Shelter</a></li>
<li>British pop band <a href="/wiki/The_Vamps_(British_band)" title="The Vamps (British band)">The Vamps</a> released song "<a href="/wiki/Oh_Cecilia_(Breaking_My_Heart)" title="Oh Cecilia (Breaking My Heart)">Oh Cecilia (Breaking My Heart)</a>" on their 2014 debut studio album <i><a href="/wiki/Meet_the_Vamps" title="Meet the Vamps">Meet the Vamps</a></i></li>
<li>Welsh rock band <a href="/wiki/Holding_Absence" title="Holding Absence">Holding Absence</a> released song "Saint Cecilia" on split EP "This Is as One" with British metal band <a href="/wiki/Loathe_(band)" title="Loathe (band)">Loathe</a> in 2018.</li>
<li>Canadian band Ellevator released their song St. Cecelia in 2018 on their album Ellevator.</li></ul>

<ul><li>The poem "Moschus Moschiferus", by Australian poet <a href="/wiki/A._D._Hope" title="A. D. Hope">A. D. Hope</a> (1907–2000), is subtitled "A Song for St Cecilia's Day". The poem is of 12 stanzas and was written in the 1960s.</li>
<li>Cecilia is also the subject of Alexander Pope's poem "Ode on St. Cecilia's Day".</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_Chaucer" title="Geoffrey Chaucer">Geoffrey Chaucer</a> retells the story of Cecilia and Valerian and his brother in "<a href="/wiki/The_Second_Nun%27s_Tale" title="The Second Nun&#39;s Tale">The Second Nun's Tale</a>" in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Canterbury_Tales" title="The Canterbury Tales">The Canterbury Tales</a></i>.</li>
<li>Cecilia is a symbol for the divine power of music in <a href="/wiki/Heinrich_von_Kleist" title="Heinrich von Kleist">Heinrich von Kleist</a>'s extended anecdote "<a href="/wiki/St._Cecilia,_or_the_Power_of_Music" title="St. Cecilia, or the Power of Music">St. Cecilia, or the Power of Music</a>".</li>
<li>Saint Cecilia features in the 1979 collection of short stories by <a href="/wiki/Angela_Carter" title="Angela Carter">Angela Carter</a> "The Bloody Chamber" in the story of the same name.</li></ul>
