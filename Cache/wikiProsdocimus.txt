<p><b>Saint Prosdocimus (Prosdecimus) of Padua</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Prosdocimo</i>, <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Prosdozimus</i>) (d. November 7, ca. 100 AD) is venerated as the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Padua">bishop of Padua</a>.  Tradition holds that, being of <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> origin, he was sent from <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter_the_Apostle" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter the Apostle">Saint Peter the Apostle</a>.  He is thus often depicted in art with this Apostle.  The cathedral at <a href="/wiki/Feltre" title="Feltre">Feltre</a> is dedicated to him and Saint Peter the Apostle, and the artist <a href="/wiki/Il_Pordenone" title="Il Pordenone">Il Pordenone</a> (c. 1483 - 1539) created a work depicting Prosdocimus with Peter.
</p><p>He evangelized the region and is said to have founded the parish church at <a href="/wiki/Isola_Vicentina" title="Isola Vicentina">Isola Vicentina</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_(Particolare)-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_%28Particolare%29-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png/220px-Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_%28Particolare%29-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_%28Particolare%29-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png/330px-Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_%28Particolare%29-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_%28Particolare%29-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png/440px-Romanino_-_Pala_Santa_Giustina_%28Particolare%29-_Museo_Eremitani_-_Padova.png 2x" data-file-width="454" data-file-height="682" /></a><figcaption><i>Pala Santa Giustina</i>, Romanino</figcaption></figure>
<p>His tomb is situated at the basilica of <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Santa_Giustina,_Padua" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Santa Giustina, Padua">Santa Giustina</a> at Padua.  The chapel dedicated to him there was built over his tomb outside the walls of Padua. The church also once contained the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of Prosdocimus's <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Daniel_of_Padua" title="Daniel of Padua">Saint Daniel</a>, though these were moved to the Paduan church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sofia_Church_(Padua)" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Sofia Church (Padua)">Santa Sofia</a> in the 11th century.
</p><p>Prosdocimus is depicted in an altarpiece by <a href="/wiki/Romanino" title="Romanino">Romanino</a>, now in the <a href="/wiki/Musei_Civici_di_Padova" title="Musei Civici di Padova">Musei Civici di Padova</a>, Padua. He holds the jug a water with which he baptized Justina.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
