					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Anthimus of Rome</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Sant&#39;Antimo prete e martire.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg/150px-Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="160" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg/225px-Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg/300px-Sant%27Antimo_prete_e_martire.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3055" data-file-height="3251" /></a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">303</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">11 May</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saint Anthimus of Rome</b>, or <b>Sant'Antimo</b> in Italian (died 303), is a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> saint.  His life is largely composed of legend.  He is said to have been born in <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a>.  A Christian <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, he was imprisoned for his beliefs at the time of the Emperors <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  His feast day is May 11.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthimus_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> governor of Bithynia, Pinianus, fell ill, his Christian wife Lucina (Lycinia), well known for her charity to imprisoned Christians and niece of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Gallienus" title="Gallienus">Gallienus</a>, found Anthimus in prison.  Anthimus converted Pinianus, and the governor was cured.  Gratefully, Pinianus liberated all of the Christian prisoners in his province and allowed Anthimus to hide himself in the governor's villa on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Salaria" title="Via Salaria">Via Salaria</a>.  
</p><p>Now based in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, Anthimus converted many to the Christian faith and countless miracles were attributed to him.  He converted a priest of the god <a href="/wiki/Silvanus_(mythology)" title="Silvanus (mythology)">Silvanus</a> and the pagan priest's entire family.  Accused of having destroyed the <a href="/wiki/Simulacrum" title="Simulacrum">simulacrum</a> of Silvanus, he was thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a> with a stone around his neck.  His legend states that he was miraculously rescued by an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a>, later recaptured, and then <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> by order of the <a href="/wiki/Consul" title="Consul">consul</a> Priscus.  He was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> where he habitually prayed.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthimus_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His tomb was first situated at <a href="/wiki/Montemaggiore_al_Metauro" title="Montemaggiore al Metauro">Montemaggiore</a> and was the object of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> and veneration.  His body, during the time of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, was transferred to <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Montalcino" title="Montalcino">Montalcino</a>, where <a href="/wiki/St._Antimo%27s_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Antimo&#39;s Abbey">St. Antimo's Abbey</a> currently stands.  A <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> historian of the 17th century speculated that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hadrian_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Hadrian I">Pope Hadrian I</a> gave the relics of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian" title="Saint Sebastian">Saint Sebastian</a> and Anthimus to <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, who then donated the relics to the abbey when it was founded.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  But this theory has not yet been verified.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
