<p><b>José María Robles Hurtado</b> (May 3, 1888 – June 26, 1927) was a <a href="/wiki/Mexican_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Mexican people">Mexican</a> priest and one of several priests martyred during the <a href="/wiki/Cristero_War" title="Cristero War">Cristero War</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Robles_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born to the devoutly-Catholic family of Antonio Robles and Petronilla Hurtado in <a href="/wiki/Mascota" title="Mascota">Mascota</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jalisco" title="Jalisco">Jalisco</a>. At 12, he entered the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Guadalajara,_Jalisco" class="mw-redirect" title="Guadalajara, Jalisco">Guadalajara</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained to the priesthood at Guadalajara in 1913, at 25. A few years later, he founded the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sisters_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus (page does not exist)">Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus</a>. He wrote a number of works to propagate the Catholic faith and also catechized others in ways contrary to the laws of the country. While serving as the pastor at a parish in <a href="/wiki/Tecolotl%C3%A1n" title="Tecolotlán">Tecolotlán</a>, he began to promote greater devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred Heart of Jesus">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> through his preaching, his personal example, and his great devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>. His fervency was so pronounced that he became known as the "Madman of the Sacred Heart." He was known to work tirelessly for the care of the sick in his parish, and he often spent several hours hearing <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confessions</a> of his parishioners. He also worked for greater reverence to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe" title="Our Lady of Guadalupe">Our Lady of Guadalupe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He was a <a href="/wiki/Knight_of_Columbus" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of Columbus">Knight of Columbus</a> council 1979.<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Persecution">Persecution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Robles_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Persecution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_1917" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitution of 1917">Constitution of 1917</a> prohibited any public <a href="/wiki/Processions" class="mw-redirect" title="Processions">processions</a> or other devotional practices. Hurtado proposed the creation of a huge <a href="/wiki/Cross" title="Cross">cross</a> to be placed in the geographic center of <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>, which he said would be symbolic of how Mexico recognized Christ as its king, and he organized a public ceremony for the laying of the cornerstone of the cross in direct violation of the existing constitution.<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In anticipation of the laying of the cornerstone, signs were placed throughout Mexico proclaiming <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> the "King of Mexico" and declaring the nation's devotion to the Sacred Heart. In 1923, an estimated 40,000 Roman Catholics made their way to the site of the cross to take part in the groundbreaking ceremonies at the hill, which was at the time called "La Loma" and is now called the mountain of Christ the King.  After the open display of defiance, the government decided to intensify its persecution of Catholics in Mexico and to ensure that Robles, in particular, would not engage in such acts again.<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite the increasing persecution of Catholics in general and the explicit invitations to him to leave Mexico personally, Robles remained and continued to minister to his congregation and to offer what solace he could to the survivors and families of Catholics who had been persecuted and killed by the government. Eventually, he even went further, and promoted the idea of armed defense of Catholics who were suffering from the persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Robles_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robles Hurtado recognized the likelihood of being killed for his actions, and he wrote a poem in which he stated, "I want to love you until martyrdom."<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was arrested on June 25, 1927, for saying a prayer in the home of the Agraz family, which was hiding him. He was found guilty and sentenced to be hanged from an oak tree. The next day before <a href="/wiki/Dawn" title="Dawn">dawn</a>, he was led to the tree. In a final display of compassion for his executioners, he offered them a small <a href="/wiki/Votive_candle" title="Votive candle">votive candle</a> that he had in his pocket to help light the path to the tree on which he was to be hanged. Upon arriving there, he forgave the men for what they were about to do. He took the noose into his own hands and said "Don't dirty your hands" to the man who brought it, kissed it, and placed it around his own neck.<sup id="cite_ref-KOC_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-KOC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Robles_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on November 22, 1992<sup id="cite_ref-CF_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and canonized on May 21, 2000, by Pope John Paul II, together with others involved in the Cristero War, including <a href="/wiki/Cristobal_Magallanes_Jara" class="mw-redirect" title="Cristobal Magallanes Jara">Cristobal Magallanes Jara</a>, his 24 companions in martyrdom, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mar%C3%ADa_de_Jes%C3%BAs_Sacramentado_Venegas&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="María de Jesús Sacramentado Venegas (page does not exist)">María de Jesús Sacramentado Venegas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-JP2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JP2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Robles_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On February 27, 2012, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Guadalajara" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Guadalajara">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Guadalajara</a> granted to Bishop <a href="/wiki/James_S._Wall" title="James S. Wall">James S. Wall</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Gallup" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Gallup">Roman Catholic Diocese of Gallup</a>, a first class relic for the altar in Bishop Wall's chapel.<sup id="cite_ref-PM_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This relic was received on April 12, 2012, and is especially dear to the Diocese of Gallup because their patron is the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-PM2_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PM2-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
