<p><b>Saint Gaudentius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Gaudenzio di Brescia</i>; died 410) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Brescia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Brescia">Bishop of Brescia</a> from 387 until 410, and was a <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologian</a> and author of many letters and sermons.  He was the successor of <a href="/wiki/Philastrius" title="Philastrius">Saint Philastrius</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaudentius_of_Brescia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gaudentius had studied under Philastrius, and was a preacher in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a>.  When Philastrius died around 387, the people of Brescia elected Gaudentius as bishop –evidently against his will.  He was on <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> when Philastrius had died.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> states that “the people of <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> bound themselves by an oath that they would accept no other bishop than Gaudentius; and <a href="/wiki/St._Ambrose" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Ambrose">St. Ambrose</a> and other neighboring prelates, in consequence, obliged him to return, though against his will. The Eastern bishops also threatened to refuse him <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Communion</a> if he did not obey.”<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>    
</p><p>Gaudentius was consecrated by Saint Ambrose in 387.  A record of the discourse given made by Gaudentius on the occasion of his consecration survives.  Gaudentius reported upon his consecration that he had brought back with him from the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_the_Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John the Baptist">Saint John the Baptist</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">Apostles</a>, relics of saints from <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, and of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_Sebaste" title="Forty Martyrs of Sebaste">Forty Martyrs of Sebaste</a>.  The nieces of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Basil" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Basil">Saint Basil</a> had given him the relics of the Forty Martyrs at <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_in_Cappadocia" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea in Cappadocia">Caesarea in Cappadocia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>     
</p><p>He deposited these relics in a basilica that he named <i>Concilium Sanctorum</i>, and wrote a sermon upon its dedication.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaudentius_of_Brescia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Twenty-one <a href="/wiki/Tract_(literature)" title="Tract (literature)">tractates</a> attributed to Gaudentius survive.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Gaudentius also wrote many pastoral letters.  Ten of his sermons have survived.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His Easter sermons were written down at the request of the Brescian nobleman Benivolus, who had been too ill to listen to Gaudentius speak.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Defense_of_Saint_John_Chrysostom">Defense of Saint John Chrysostom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaudentius_of_Brescia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Defense of Saint John Chrysostom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gaudentius and the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Constantinople">Archbishop of Constantinople</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_Chrysostom" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John Chrysostom">Saint John Chrysostom</a>, were friends; the two may have met at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gaudentius joined a delegation in 405 sent by <a href="/wiki/Innocent_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent I">Innocent I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Honorius_(emperor)" title="Honorius (emperor)">Honorius</a> to defend Chrysostom.  The delegation was sent to speak with the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Emperor_Arcadius" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Emperor Arcadius">Eastern Emperor Arcadius</a> to defend Chrysostom after the latter had been accused by the member of what was considered a <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretical</a> sect, and exiled.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Gaudentius and his companions, two bishops, encountered many difficulties and never reached their goal of entering <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.  At the start of their journey Gaudentius and his two companions had been seized at <a href="/wiki/Athens" title="Athens">Athens</a>.  They were sent to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, a journey of three days on a ship without food.
</p><p>However, they were not allowed to enter the city, and were imprisoned in the fortress of Athyra, in <a href="/wiki/Thrace" title="Thrace">Thrace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An attempt to bribe them into speaking with <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Atticus_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Atticus of Constantinople">Archbishop Atticus of Constantinople</a>, the man who had replaced Chrysostom, failed, but one of the thumbs of the three travelers was broken during a scuffle in which Byzantine officials had seized the bishops’ credentials by force.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gaudentius and his two companions were then put on board an <a href="/wiki/Unseaworthy" class="mw-redirect" title="Unseaworthy">unseaworthy</a> vessel; it was alleged that the ship's captain had orders to wreck them.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The travelers arrived safely, however, at <a href="/wiki/Lampsacus" title="Lampsacus">Lampsacus</a>.  They departed for Italy and arrived at <a href="/wiki/Otranto" title="Otranto">Otranto</a> after twenty days.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  
Despite the failure of the mission, Chrysostom sent a letter of thanks to Gaudentius.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia" title="Palladius of Galatia">Palladius of Galatia</a> preserved an account by Gaudentius of his four-month adventure (<i>Dialogus</i>, 4).<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaudentius_of_Brescia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gaudentius's relics were kept at Brescia in the church of San Giovanni, built on the site of the ancient <i>Concilium Sanctorum</i>.
</p><p>Gaudentius was depicted in <a href="/wiki/Altar-piece" class="mw-redirect" title="Altar-piece">altar-pieces</a> by painters of Brescia, including <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Bonvicino" class="mw-redirect" title="Alessandro Bonvicino">Moretto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Savoldo" class="mw-redirect" title="Savoldo">Savoldo</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Romanino" title="Romanino">Romanino</a>.
</p>
