<p><b>Fausta of Cyzicus</b>, also known as <b>Saint Fausta</b> (c. 298 – 311), was a 4th-century girl from <a href="/wiki/Cyzicus" title="Cyzicus">Cyzicus</a>. At the age of 13, she was arrested, tortured, and executed for being a Christian.
</p><p>A pagan priest, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Evilasius" title="Saint Evilasius">Evilasius</a>, was responsible for torturing and executing her. According to tradition, Evilasius converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> after watching her courageous resistance, and he was also martyred for this act.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Although Fausta had remained impervious to the initial torture, she and Evilasius perished together in a cauldron of boiling water.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They are also venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Catholic Church">Eastern Catholic Church</a> along with Maximus, the magistrate who condemned Evilasius. Tradition holds that Maximus repented at the last moment and joined the pair in the cauldron.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
