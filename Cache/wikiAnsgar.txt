<p><b>Ansgar</b> (8 September 801 – 3 February 865), also known as <b>Anskar</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <b>Saint Ansgar</b>, <b>Saint Anschar</b> or <b>Oscar</b>, was <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Bremen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Bremen">Archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen</a> in the northern part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_East_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the East Franks">Kingdom of the East Franks</a>. Ansgar became known as the "Apostle of the North" because of his travels and the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Hamburg" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Hamburg">See of Hamburg</a> received the missionary mandate to bring Christianity to Northern Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansgar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ansgar was the son of a noble <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> family, born near <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a> (present day France).<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> After his mother's early death, Ansgar was brought up in <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Corbie_Abbey" title="Corbie Abbey">Corbie</a> in <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Ansgari" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita Ansgari">Vita Ansgarii</a></i> ("Life of Ansgar"), when the little boy learned in a vision that his mother was in the company of <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Mary, mother of Jesus</a>, his careless attitude toward spiritual matters changed to seriousness.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His pupil, successor, and eventual biographer <a href="/wiki/Rimbert" title="Rimbert">Rimbert</a> considered the visions (of which this was the first) to have been Ansgar's main life motivator.
</p><p>Ansgar acted in the context of the phase of <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianization</a> of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony" title="Duchy of Saxony">Saxony</a> (present day <a href="/wiki/Northern_Germany" title="Northern Germany">Northern Germany</a>) begun by <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> and continued by Charlemagne's son and successor, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a>. In 822 Ansgar became one of many missionaries sent to found the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Corvey" class="mw-redirect" title="Corvey">Corvey</a> (New Corbie) in <a href="/wiki/Westphalia" title="Westphalia">Westphalia</a>, where he became a teacher and preacher. A group of monks including Ansgar were sent further north to <a href="/wiki/Jutland" title="Jutland">Jutland</a> with the king <a href="/wiki/Harald_Klak" title="Harald Klak">Harald Klak</a>, who had received baptism during his exile. With Harald's downfall in 827 and Ansgar's companion Autbert having died, their school for the sons of courtiers closed and Ansgar returned to Germany. Then in 829, after the Swedish king <a href="/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_at_Hauge" class="mw-redirect" title="Björn at Hauge">Björn at Hauge</a> requested missionaries for his <a href="/wiki/Swedes" title="Swedes">Swedes</a>, King Louis sent Ansgar, now accompanied by friar Witmar from New Corbie as his assistant. Ansgar preached and made converts, particularly during six months at <a href="/wiki/Birka" title="Birka">Birka</a>, on Lake <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A4laren" title="Mälaren">Mälaren</a>, where the wealthy widow <a href="/wiki/Mor_Frideborg" title="Mor Frideborg">Mor Frideborg</a> extended hospitality. Ansgar organized a small congregation with her and the king's steward, Hergeir, as its most prominent members.
</p><p>In 831 Ansgar returned to Louis' court at <a href="/wiki/Worms,_Germany" title="Worms, Germany">Worms</a> and was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Prince-Archbishopric_of_Bremen" title="Prince-Archbishopric of Bremen">Archbishopric of Hamburg-Bremen</a>. This was a new archbishopric, incorporating the bishoprics of Bremen and <a href="/wiki/Verden,_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Verden, Germany">Verden</a> and with the right to send missions into all the northern lands, as well as to consecrate bishops for them. Ansgar received the mission of evangelizing pagan <a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Denmark</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sweden" title="Sweden">Sweden</a>. The King of Sweden decided to cast lots as to whether to admit the Christian missionaries into his kingdom. Ansgar recommended the issue to the care of God, and the lot was favorable.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Ansgar was consecrated as a bishop in November 831, with the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IV" title="Pope Gregory IV">Gregory IV</a>. Before traveling north once again, Ansgar traveled to Rome to receive the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> directly from the pope's hands, and was formally named legate for the northern lands. <a href="/wiki/Ebbo,_Archbishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Ebbo, Archbishop of Reims">Ebbo, Archbishop of Reims</a> had previously received a similar commission, but would be deposed twice before his death in 851, and never actually traveled so far north, so the jurisdiction was divided by agreement, with Ebbo retaining Sweden for himself. For a time Ansgar devoted himself to the needs of his own diocese, which was still a missionary territory and had few churches. He founded a monastery and a school in Hamburg. Although intended to serve the Danish mission further north, it accomplished little.
</p><p>After <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> died in 840, his empire was divided and Ansgar lost the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Torhout" title="Torhout">Turholt</a>, which Louis had given to endow Ansgar's work. Then in 845, the <a href="/wiki/Danes" title="Danes">Danes</a> unexpectedly raided Hamburg, destroying all the church's treasures and books. Ansgar now had neither see nor revenue, and many helpers deserted him. The new king, Louis' third son, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>, did not re-endow Turholt to Ansgar, but in 847 he named the missionary to the vacant diocese of Bremen, where Ansgar moved in 848. However, since Bremen had been <a href="/wiki/Suffragan" class="mw-redirect" title="Suffragan">suffragan</a> to the Bishop of Cologne, combining the sees of Bremen and Hamburg presented canonical difficulties. After prolonged negotiations, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> would approve the union of the two dioceses in 864.
</p><p>Through this political turmoil, Ansgar continued his northern mission. The Danish civil war compelled him to establish good relations with two kings, <a href="/wiki/Horik_I" title="Horik I">Horik the Elder</a> and his son, <a href="/wiki/Horik_II" title="Horik II">Horik II</a>. Both assisted him until his death; Ansgar was able to secure permission to build a church in <a href="/wiki/Hedeby" title="Hedeby">Sleswick</a> north of Hamburg and recognition of Christianity as a tolerated religion.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Ansgar did not forget the Swedish mission, and spent two years there in person (848–850), averting a threatened pagan reaction. In 854, Ansgar returned to Sweden when king <a href="/wiki/Olof_(I)_of_Sweden" class="mw-redirect" title="Olof (I) of Sweden">Olof</a> ruled in Birka. According to Rimbert, he was well disposed to Christianity. On a Viking raid to <a href="/wiki/Apuole" class="mw-redirect" title="Apuole">Apuole</a> (current village in <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>) in <a href="/wiki/Courland" title="Courland">Courland</a>, the Swedes plundered the <a href="/wiki/Curonians" title="Curonians">Curonians</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansgar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ansgar was buried in <a href="/wiki/Bremen_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bremen (city)">Bremen</a> in 865. His successor as archbishop, <a href="/wiki/Rimbert" title="Rimbert">Rimbert</a>, wrote the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Ansgari" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita Ansgari">Vita Ansgarii</a></i>. He noted that Ansgar wore a rough hair shirt, lived on bread and water, and showed great charity to the poor. Adam of Bremen attributed the <i>Vita et miracula of Willehad</i> (first bishop of Bremen) to Ansgar in <i>Gesta Hammenburgensis ecclesiæ</i>; Ansgar is also the reputed author of a collection of brief prayers <i>Pigmenta</i> (ed. J. M. Lappenberg, Hamburg, 1844).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> declared Ansgar a saint shortly after the missionary's death. The first actual missionary in Sweden and the <a href="/wiki/Nordic_countries" title="Nordic countries">Nordic countries</a> (and organizer of the Catholic church therein), Ansgar was later declared "Patron of <a href="/wiki/Scandinavia" title="Scandinavia">Scandinavia</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Relics are located in <a href="/wiki/Hamburg" title="Hamburg">Hamburg</a> in two places: St. Mary's Cathedral (Ger.: Domkirche St. Marien) and St. Ansgar's and St. Bernard's Church (Ger.: St. Ansgar und St. Bernhard Kirche).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
Statues of Bishop Ansgar stand in <a href="/wiki/Hamburg" title="Hamburg">Hamburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Copenhagen" title="Copenhagen">Copenhagen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ribe" title="Ribe">Ribe</a>, as well as a stone cross at <a href="/wiki/Birka" title="Birka">Birka</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">feast day</a> (<a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a>) is <a href="/wiki/February_3" title="February 3">3 February</a>, as it is in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-teccalendar_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-teccalendar-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Visions">Visions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansgar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Visions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg/170px-Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="403" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg/255px-Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg/340px-Peiffer_-_St._Ansgar_-_Trostbr%C3%BCcke_Hamburg_cropped.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1545" data-file-height="3663" /></a><figcaption>Saint Ansgar statue in Hamburg</figcaption></figure>
<p>Although a historical document and primary source written by a man whose existence can be proven historically, the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Ansgari" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita Ansgari">Vita Ansgarii</a></i> ("The Life of Ansgar") aims above all to demonstrate Ansgar's sanctity. It is partly concerned with Ansgar's visions, which, according to the author Rimbert, encouraged and assisted Ansgar's remarkable missionary feats.
</p><p>Through the course of this work, Ansgar repeatedly embarks on a new stage in his career following a vision.  According to Rimbert, his early studies and ensuing devotion to the ascetic life of a monk were inspired by a vision of his mother in the presence of Mary, mother of Jesus.  Again, when the Swedish people were left without a priest for some time, he begged King Horik to help him with this problem; then after receiving his consent, consulted with Bishop Gautbert to find a suitable man. The two together sought the approval of King Louis, which he granted when he learned that they were in agreement on the issue. Ansgar was convinced he was commanded by heaven to undertake this mission and was influenced by a vision he received when he was concerned about the journey, in which he met a man who reassured him of his purpose and informed him of a prophet that he would meet, the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> <a href="/wiki/Adalard_of_Corbie" title="Adalard of Corbie">Adalhard</a>, who would instruct him in what was to happen.  In the vision, he searched for and found Adalhard, who commanded, "Islands, listen to me, pay attention, remotest peoples", which Ansgar interpreted as God's will that he go to the Scandinavian countries as "most of that country consisted of islands, and also, when 'I will make you the light of the nations so that my salvation may reach to the ends of the earth' was added, since the end of the world in the north was in Swedish territory".<sup id="cite_ref-rimbert_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rimbert-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
