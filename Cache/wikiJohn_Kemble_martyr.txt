<p><b>John Kemble</b> (c. 1599 – 22 August 1679) was an <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. He was one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years_and_ordination">Early years and ordination</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Kemble_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years and ordination">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Kemble was born at Rhydicar Farm, St Weonards, <a href="/wiki/Herefordshire" title="Herefordshire">Herefordshire</a>, in 1599, the son of John and Anne Kemble. They were a prominent local <a href="/wiki/Recusant" class="mw-redirect" title="Recusant">recusant</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a> family,<sup id="cite_ref-walks_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-walks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> which included four other priests. His cousin was another future martyr – <a href="/wiki/David_Lewis_(Jesuit_priest)" title="David Lewis (Jesuit priest)">David Lewis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> John Kemble was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> College, on 23 February 1625. He returned to <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> on 4 June 1625 as a missionary in <a href="/wiki/Monmouthshire_(historic)" title="Monmouthshire (historic)">Monmouthshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Herefordshire" title="Herefordshire">Herefordshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pastoral_work">Pastoral work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Kemble_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pastoral work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pembridge_Castle.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Pembridge_Castle.jpg/220px-Pembridge_Castle.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Pembridge_Castle.jpg/330px-Pembridge_Castle.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Pembridge_Castle.jpg/440px-Pembridge_Castle.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="426" /></a><figcaption>Pembridge Castle</figcaption></figure>
<p>In normal times, despite harsh anti-Catholic laws, the extent of persecution depended upon the sympathies of local landowners. Around Hereford and Monmouth, where the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Worcester" title="Earl of Worcester">Earl of Worcester</a> (from 1642 Marquess) held sway at <a href="/wiki/Raglan_Castle" title="Raglan Castle">Raglan Castle</a>, the old religion was for long periods practised with impunity, even after his own conversion to the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>. From 1622 there was even a Jesuit College at <a href="/wiki/Cwm,_Llanrothal" title="Cwm, Llanrothal">Cwm, Llanrothal</a>, near Welsh Newton, which survived until 1678, though its existence was widely known, and was twice debated in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_England" title="House of Commons of England">House of Commons</a>.
</p><p>Upon Kemble's return to Monmouthshire, he served more than 50 years as an itinerant priest, winning admirers even among Protestants. Little is known of his work caring for his flock during these 53 years. The condition of Catholics had eased but priests still needed to perform their ministry discreetly.<sup id="cite_ref-walks_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-walks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Based at <a href="/wiki/Pembridge_Castle" title="Pembridge Castle">Pembridge Castle</a>, the home of his nephew, Captain Richard Kemble,<sup id="cite_ref-tablet_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tablet-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> he had seemed immune from prosecution.
</p>
<h2><span id="Titus_Oates.27_plot"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Titus_Oates'_plot">Titus Oates' plot</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Kemble_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Titus Oates&#039; plot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The uneasy tolerance within which Kemble had operated was shattered by the <a href="/wiki/Popish_Plot" title="Popish Plot">Popish Plot</a> of 1678. <a href="/wiki/Titus_Oates" title="Titus Oates">Titus Oates</a> was a <a href="/wiki/Perjurer" class="mw-redirect" title="Perjurer">perjurer</a> who concocted a plot in which the <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican</a> <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_England" title="Charles II of England">Charles II</a> would be assassinated and his Catholic brother (later, <a href="/wiki/James_II_of_England" title="James II of England">King James II</a>) installed as king in his place.
</p><p>When Oates' story was examined in detail the whole fraud was exposed, but it gave disgruntled Protestants and ambitious chancers an opportunity. Anti-Catholic politicians made cynical use of this "plot" to implicate English Catholics, particularly priests. Among the many Catholics caught up in the frenzy was John Kemble.<sup id="cite_ref-past_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-past-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/David_Lewis_(martyr)" class="mw-redirect" title="David Lewis (martyr)">David Lewis</a> was apprehended at St. Michael's Church, <a href="/wiki/Llantarnam" title="Llantarnam">Llantarnam</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_and_execution">Arrest and execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Kemble_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Arrest and execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Father Kemble was staying at Pembridge Castle, near <a href="/wiki/Welsh_Newton" title="Welsh Newton">Welsh Newton</a>, when he was arrested on 7 December 1678. He was warned about the impending arrest but declined to leave his flock, saying, "According to the course of nature, I have but a few years to live. It will be an advantage to suffer for my religion and, therefore, I will not abscond".<sup id="cite_ref-past_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-past-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was arrested by Captain John Scudamore of Kentchurch. It is a comment on the tortuous values of the age that Scudamore's own wife and children were parishioners of Kemble. Kemble was kept in Hereford Gaol until the Spring Assizes of 1679. In April 1679 Kemble, now 80, was ordered to be taken to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> to be interviewed about the plot. As the elderly priest had difficulty riding a horse, he was strapped like a pack to his horse on the way there. He was found to have had no connection with the alleged plot but found guilty of the <a href="/wiki/Treason" title="Treason">treasonous</a> crime of being a Catholic priest. He was sentenced to be <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>. He was returned to <a href="/wiki/Hereford" title="Hereford">Hereford</a> for the sentence to be carried out, and allowed to walk most of the way back.
</p><p>Before he was led out to his execution on 22 August 1679 Kemble insisted on saying his prayers and finishing his drink, and the assembled party joined the elderly priest in a final smoke and a cup of sack. The Herefordshire sayings, <i>Kemble pipe</i> and <i>Kemble cup</i>, refer to a parting pipe or cup.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Before his death Kemble addressed the assembled crowd, pointing out that no association with the "plot" had been charged to him. The old priest went on to say: "The failure of the authorities in London to connect me to the plot makes it evident that I die only for profession of the Catholic religion, which was the religion that first made this Kingdom Christian."<sup id="cite_ref-tablet_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tablet-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Consoling his distraught hangman, the priest is said to have whispered, "Honest Anthony, my friend Anthony, be not afraid; do thy office. I forgive thee with all my heart. Thou wilt do me a greater kindness than discourtesy."<sup id="cite_ref-walks_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-walks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:GraveOfStJohnKemble(PhilipHalling)Jan2006.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/GraveOfStJohnKemble%28PhilipHalling%29Jan2006.jpg/192px-GraveOfStJohnKemble%28PhilipHalling%29Jan2006.jpg" decoding="async" width="192" height="288" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/GraveOfStJohnKemble%28PhilipHalling%29Jan2006.jpg/288px-GraveOfStJohnKemble%28PhilipHalling%29Jan2006.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/GraveOfStJohnKemble%28PhilipHalling%29Jan2006.jpg/384px-GraveOfStJohnKemble%28PhilipHalling%29Jan2006.jpg 2x" data-file-width="426" data-file-height="640" /></a><figcaption>Grave of John Kemble</figcaption></figure>
<p>Kemble was allowed to die on the <a href="/wiki/Gallows" title="Gallows">gallows</a> before being drawn and quartered, thus he was spared the agony suffered by many other Catholic martyrs of England, Scotland and Wales. He died on 22 August 1679 at <a href="/wiki/Widemarsh_Common" title="Widemarsh Common">Widemarsh Common</a>, Hereford. His death was greeted with dismay in the locality, Protestants no less than Catholics praising him as "a great gentleman".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of Kemble's hands is still preserved at <a href="/wiki/St_Francis_Xavier_Church,_Hereford" title="St Francis Xavier Church, Hereford">St Francis Xavier Church</a> in Hereford<sup id="cite_ref-tablet_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tablet-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> city centre. His body rests in the (<a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>) churchyard of St Mary the Virgin at <a href="/wiki/Welsh_Newton" title="Welsh Newton">Welsh Newton</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and local Catholics make an annual pilgrimage to his grave.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Kemble_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">Miracles</a> were soon attributed to the saintly priest. Scudamore's daughter was cured of <a href="/wiki/Esophageal_cancer" title="Esophageal cancer">throat cancer</a>, and Scudamore's wife recovered her hearing whilst praying at Kemble's grave.<sup id="cite_ref-past_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-past-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> John Kemble was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1929 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on 25 October 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relatives">Relatives</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Kemble_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Relatives">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Reynolds,_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Reynolds%2C_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/170px-Reynolds%2C_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="273" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Reynolds%2C_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/255px-Reynolds%2C_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Reynolds%2C_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/340px-Reynolds%2C_Sir_Joshua_-_Mrs_Siddons_as_the_Tragic_Muse_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2001" data-file-height="3209" /></a><figcaption>Sarah Siddons as the Tragic Muse by <a href="/wiki/Sir_Joshua_Reynolds" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Joshua Reynolds">Sir Joshua Reynolds</a> (The Huntington, San Marino, California)</figcaption></figure>
<p>The famous <a href="/wiki/Kemble_family" title="Kemble family">Kemble family</a> are relatives of John Kemble. The family became almost a 'Royal Family' of the English stage in the nineteenth century. The most famous was <a href="/wiki/Sarah_Siddons" title="Sarah Siddons">Sarah Siddons</a>, née Kemble. It has been said that she was the finest English tragic actress ever. She was John Kemble's great-great grandniece.
</p><p>Other noteworthy members of the family, though not Catholics, were: <a href="/wiki/Roger_Kemble" title="Roger Kemble">Roger Kemble</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Kemble" title="Stephen Kemble">Stephen Kemble</a>, <a href="/wiki/Charles_Kemble" title="Charles Kemble">Charles Kemble</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Mitchell_Kemble" title="John Mitchell Kemble">John Mitchell Kemble</a>, <a href="/wiki/Frances_Anne_Kemble" class="mw-redirect" title="Frances Anne Kemble">Frances Anne Kemble</a> who after spending time in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> held strong <a href="/wiki/Abolitionism" title="Abolitionism">anti slavery</a> opinions, <a href="/wiki/Adelaide_Kemble" title="Adelaide Kemble">Adelaide Kemble</a> and <a href="/wiki/Henry_Kemble_(actor,_born_1848)" title="Henry Kemble (actor, born 1848)">Henry Kemble</a>. The actor <a href="/wiki/John_Philip_Kemble" title="John Philip Kemble">John Philip Kemble</a> was baptized a Catholic and spent some time at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>.
</p>
