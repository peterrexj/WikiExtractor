<p><b>Manuel Gómez González</b> (May 29, 1877 – May 21, 1924) was a Spanish missionary <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Priesthood in the Catholic Church">priest</a> and a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who lived in Brazil.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Manuel_G%C3%B3mez_Gonz%C3%A1lez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Son of <a href="/wiki/Farmers" class="mw-redirect" title="Farmers">farmers</a>, González was <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Holy orders in the Catholic Church">ordained priest</a> on May 24, 1902 in <a href="/wiki/Tui,_Pontevedra" title="Tui, Pontevedra">Tui</a>, Galicia, Spain.
</p><p>In 1904, after exercising his priestly ministry in his native land, he moved to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Braga" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Braga">Archdiocese of Braga</a>, Portugal, where he was parish priest of the parishes of Our Lady of the Extreme (1905-1911) in <a href="/wiki/Vila_Pouca_de_Aguiar" title="Vila Pouca de Aguiar">Vila Pouca de Aguiar</a>, and of St. Andrew of Taias and St. Michael of Baroque (1911–1913) in <a href="/wiki/Mon%C3%A7%C3%A3o" title="Monção">Monção</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Due to the political persecutions found in the <a href="/wiki/First_Portuguese_Republic" title="First Portuguese Republic">First Portuguese Republic</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which were intensifying more and more, he moved to Brazil in 1913, where he went to work in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Soledade" title="Soledade">Soledade</a>, jurisdiction of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Santa_Maria" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Santa Maria">Diocese of Santa Maria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rio_Grande_do_Sul" title="Rio Grande do Sul">Rio Grande do Sul</a>, and on 7 September 1915 in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Nonoai" title="Nonoai">Nonoai</a>, in the same diocese and state.
</p><p>His social work in the area included the construction of a <a href="/wiki/Pottery" title="Pottery">pottery</a> shop, a hotel and houses for the <a href="/wiki/Homelessness" title="Homelessness">homeless</a> of Nonoai. He also introduced new crops in the <a href="/wiki/Agriculture" title="Agriculture">agriculture</a> of the area.
</p><p>The Brazilian revolution of 1923 made the border region between Uruguay and Brazil very dangerous, with various bands and commandos acting violently for the ownership of lands.
</p><p>Next to his <a href="/wiki/Acolyte" title="Acolyte">acolyte</a> <a href="/wiki/Ad%C3%ADlio_Daronch" title="Adílio Daronch">Adílio Daronch</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> González visited the chapels of the region. In one of the routine visits, both the priest and his auxiliary altar boy were <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a> and <a href="/wiki/Murder" title="Murder">assassinated</a> on May 21, 1924, in the town of Feijão Miúdo, in the current municipality of <a href="/wiki/Tr%C3%AAs_Passos" title="Três Passos">Três Passos</a> by revolutionaries.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Manuel_G%C3%B3mez_Gonz%C3%A1lez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 14 June 1996 began the process of beatification of González and Daronch who were venerated by the faithful Catholics of the region as martyrs and in December 2006 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> recognized the existence of their martyrdom, with which they opened the road to beatification.
</p><p>On 21 October 2007 they were beatified by mandate of the <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in a ceremony presided by <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saraiva_Martins" title="José Saraiva Martins">José Saraiva Martins</a> in Frederico Westphalen, Rio Grande do Sul.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
