<p><b>Maria Antonina Kratochwil</b> (21 August 1881 – 7 October 1942)<sup id="cite_ref-SND_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SND-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Martyrs of World War II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She tried to help the Jews survive during <a href="/wiki/The_Holocaust" title="The Holocaust">the Holocaust</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A member of the <a href="/wiki/School_Sisters_of_Notre_Dame" title="School Sisters of Notre Dame">School Sisters of Notre Dame</a> residing in the <a href="/wiki/Kresy" title="Kresy">Kresy</a> <a href="/wiki/Macroregion" title="Macroregion">region</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Polish_Second_Republic" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish Second Republic">Polish Second Republic</a> before the war began; she was arrested along with her sisters by Nazi Germans a year after <a href="/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa" title="Operation Barbarossa">Operation Barbarossa</a> of 1941, and singled out for anti-Nazi activities. She was severely beaten while in prison, contracted typhus, and died upon her hasty release.<sup id="cite_ref-Kupka2001_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kupka2001-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Antonina_Kratochwil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Maria Antonina was born in Witkowice near <a href="/wiki/Ostrava" title="Ostrava">Ostrava</a> where her parents arrived in 1879 from <a href="/wiki/W%C4%99gierska_G%C3%B3rka" title="Węgierska Górka">Węgierska Górka</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Austrian_Partition" title="Austrian Partition">Austrian Partition</a>. In search of sustenance, her father worked at a foundry.<sup id="cite_ref-DKo_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DKo-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1885 the family returned to her mother's hometown in Węgierska Górka close to <a href="/wiki/%C5%BBywiec" title="Żywiec">Żywiec</a>, and settled in <a href="/wiki/Bielsko-Bia%C5%82a" title="Bielsko-Biała">Bielsko</a> nearby.<sup id="cite_ref-SND_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SND-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1901 Maria Antonina entered the Congregation of <a href="/wiki/School_Sisters_of_Notre_Dame" title="School Sisters of Notre Dame">School Sisters of Notre Dame</a>, a worldwide <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> order devoted to providing primary, secondary, and post-secondary education. She passed her maturation exams in 1906,<sup id="cite_ref-PBr_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PBr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and became a professed <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">sister</a>. Ten years before the <a href="/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic" title="Second Polish Republic">rebirth of sovereign Poland</a> Kratochwil was sent to <a href="/wiki/Karvin%C3%A1" title="Karviná">Karviná</a> near <a href="/wiki/Cieszyn" title="Cieszyn">Cieszyn</a> to teach at a Polish elementary school,<sup id="cite_ref-DKo_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DKo-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> twice: between 1906–09, and 1910–17. Already in independent Poland she settled in <a href="/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Lwów">Lwów</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kresy" title="Kresy">Kresy</a> region where she taught until 1925. She served as director of a Catholic boarding school there in 1925–32; relocated to the town of Tłumacz to train other sisters as teachers, and returned to Lwów, where she was appointed director of a school for candidates in 1931–39.<sup id="cite_ref-SND_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SND-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="World_War_II">World War II</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Antonina_Kratochwil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: World War II">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Following the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland" title="Soviet invasion of Poland">Soviet invasion of Poland</a> at the beginning of World War II, the <a href="/wiki/NKVD" title="NKVD">NKVD</a> authorities closed down the Polish schools in Lwów and dismissed the sisters. Maria Antonina relocated with her sisters to Mikuliczyn in December 1939 (or February 1940).<sup id="cite_ref-SND_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SND-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-PBr_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PBr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The Soviets raided the convent in Mikuliczyn, nationalized it, and expelled the sisters; they were prohibited from wearing their <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habits</a> ever again. Lwów was taken over by the Germans in June 1941 at the onset of <a href="/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa" title="Operation Barbarossa">Operation Barbarossa</a>. A year later, Sister Maria Antonia was arrested by the <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a> on 9 July 1942, along with six other religious sisters, and thrown into prison in <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk" title="Ivano-Frankivsk">Stanisławów</a> (present-day Ivano-Frankivsk, <a href="/wiki/Western_Ukraine" title="Western Ukraine">western Ukraine</a>), a provincial capital with a large <a href="/wiki/Polish_Jewish" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish Jewish">Polish Jewish</a> population entrapped in the <a href="/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw%C3%B3w_Ghetto" title="Stanisławów Ghetto">Stanisławów Ghetto</a>. The sisters were kept in a cell with dozens of other women.<sup id="cite_ref-DKo_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DKo-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Sister Kratochwil intervened against the brutal treatment of Jewish female prisoners by the Gestapo office run by the notorious Holocaust perpetrator <i>SS</i>-<i><a href="/wiki/Hauptsturmf%C3%BChrer" title="Hauptsturmführer">Hauptsturmführer</a></i> <a href="/wiki/Hans_Krueger" title="Hans Krueger">Hans Krueger</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-yadvashem-Pohl_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-yadvashem-Pohl-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> As punishment for her audacity, Kratochwil was subjected to a torturous beating. Returned to the cell all bloodied, she could no longer lie on her back.<sup id="cite_ref-DKo_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DKo-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The six sisters were released at the end of September 1942, after weeks of interrogations.<sup id="cite_ref-PBr_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PBr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Maria Antonina died from her injuries on 2 October 1942 in a hospital,<sup id="cite_ref-PBr_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PBr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> five days after her release from prison. She was buried in (the no-longer existing) Sapieżyński Cemetery in Stanisławów.<sup id="cite_ref-PBr_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PBr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SND_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SND-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Sister Kratochwil was declared patron of <i>Shalom</i> at the 3rd <i>International Shalom</i> meeting held in <a href="/wiki/El_Salvador" title="El Salvador">El Salvador</a> in August 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-shalom_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shalom-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> A short book was published about her life in 2001.<sup id="cite_ref-Kupka2001_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kupka2001-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

Five days after release from prison, she died from the consequence of tortures and the illness of typhus.<sup id="cite_ref-SND_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SND-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ASi_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASi-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
