<p><b>Prince Constantine Constantinovich of Russia</b> (<i>Константин Константинович</i>; 1 January 1891 &#8211; 18 July 1918), nicknamed Kostya by the family, was the third son and fourth child of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Constantine_Constantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duke Constantine Constantinovich of Russia">Grand Duke Constantine Constantinovich of Russia</a> by his wife <a href="/wiki/Princess_Elisabeth_of_Saxe-Altenburg_(1865%E2%80%931927)" title="Princess Elisabeth of Saxe-Altenburg (1865–1927)">Grand Duchess Elizabeth Mavrikievna of Russia</a>.
</p><p>The Prince was a silent, shy person who fancied theatre and was educated in the <a href="/wiki/Corps_des_Pages" class="mw-redirect" title="Corps des Pages">Corps des Pages</a>, a military academy in Saint Petersburg. He served in the army during the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a>. A priest who met him at the front, Hegumen Seraphim, wrote: "He was an extremely modest officer of the Guard of the <a href="/wiki/Izmaylovsky_Regiment" class="mw-redirect" title="Izmaylovsky Regiment">Izmaylovsky Regiment</a>, much beloved by officers and soldiers alike; along with them he was a brave soldier who distinguished himself. I personally remember seeing him in the trenches among the soldiers, risking his life."
</p><p>After seeing the happiness of his two elder siblings, <a href="/wiki/Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince John Constantinovich of Russia">John</a> and <a href="/wiki/Princess_Tatiana_Konstantinovna_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Tatiana Konstantinovna of Russia">Tatiana</a>, Konstantin was keen to start his own family. He was interested in the <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia" title="Nicholas II of Russia">Tsar's</a> eldest daughter, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna of Russia">Olga</a>, but was also drawn to <a href="/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Romania" title="Elisabeth of Romania">Princess Elisabeth of Romania</a>. Elisabeth's grandmother, the former <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Maria_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Maria Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Maria Alexandrovna of Russia</a>, wrote to her daughter, the <a href="/wiki/Princess_Marie_of_Edinburgh" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Marie of Edinburgh">Crown Princess Marie of Romania</a> in 1911, saying, "The young Kostya is seized now with terror that she will be snatched away, as he says, before he has even made her acquaintance. The young man seems really quite nice, is much liked in his regiment, and they have really been very well brought up. This one is full of life...if you have nothing against this youth coming on a passing visit, will you telegraph to me." However, the request was denied on political grounds, as Prince John was married to <a href="/wiki/Princess_Helen_of_Serbia" title="Princess Helen of Serbia">Helen of Serbia</a>, and Konstantin never did find the marriage he longed for.
</p><p>In March 1918 he was exiled to the <a href="/wiki/Urals" class="mw-redirect" title="Urals">Urals</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks">Bolsheviks</a> and murdered in a mineshaft near <a href="/wiki/Alapayevsk" title="Alapayevsk">Alapayevsk</a>, along with his brothers <a href="/wiki/Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince John Constantinovich of Russia">Prince John Constantinovich</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prince_Igor_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Igor Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Igor Konstantinovich</a>, his cousin <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Paley" title="Vladimir Paley">Prince Vladimir Pavlovich Paley</a> and other relatives and friends. His body was eventually buried in the Russian Orthodox Church cemetery in <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a>, which was destroyed in 1986 to build a park.
