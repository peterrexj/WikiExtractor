<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Canice" and "Saint Canice" redirect here. <p><b>Cainnech of Aghaboe</b> (515/16–600), also known as <b>Saint Canice</b> in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, <b>Saint Kenneth</b> in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, <b>Saint Kenny</b> and in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> <b>Sanctus Canicus</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> abbot, monastic founder, priest and missionary during the <a href="/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages" title="Early Middle Ages">early medieval period</a>. Cainnech is one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a><sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and preached <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> across Ireland and to the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Picts" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the Picts">Picts</a> in Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-catholiconline_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholiconline-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He wrote a commentary on the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospels</a>, which for centuries was known as the <i>Glas-Choinnigh</i> or <i>Kenneth's Lock</i> or the <i>Chain of Cainnech</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Most of what is written about Cainnech's life is based on tradition, however he was considered a man of virtue, great eloquence and learning.  His feast day is commemorated on 11 October in the Roman Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church according to their respective calendars (Gregorian or Church Julian) with additional feast days on 1st or 14 August in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Introduction">Introduction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Introduction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Canice_window.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/St_Canice_window.jpg/130px-St_Canice_window.jpg" decoding="async" width="130" height="472" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/St_Canice_window.jpg/195px-St_Canice_window.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/41/St_Canice_window.jpg 2x" data-file-width="248" data-file-height="900" /></a><figcaption>Depicted in stained glass, <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Cathedral,_Kilkenny" title="St Mary&#39;s Cathedral, Kilkenny">St Mary's Cathedral, Kilkenny</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>A lot of what is known of Cainnech comes from legend. However, he is documented by <a href="/wiki/Adomn%C3%A1n" title="Adomnán">Adomnán</a> (also known as <a href="/wiki/Eunan" title="Eunan">Eunan</a>), the ninth abbot of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> who died in 704. Adomnán was a hagiographer and his greatest work <i>Vita Columbae</i> or <i>Life of St. <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a></i> contains references to Cainnech.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-sabine_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Cainnech.27s_background"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Cainnech's_background">Cainnech's background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cainnech&#039;s background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg/150px-San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg/225px-San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/79/San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg/300px-San_canizio_kilkenny.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3888" /></a><figcaption>Statue at St. Canice's Catholic Church, Kilkenny</figcaption></figure>
<p>Cainnech was born in 515 or 516, at Glengiven, near <a href="/wiki/Dungiven" title="Dungiven">Dungiven</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a>, the northern <a href="/wiki/Provinces_of_Ireland" title="Provinces of Ireland">province</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabine_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  His full name was Cainnech moccu Dalánn.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cainnech's father Lughadh Leithdhearg was descended from the CorcoDalann or Ui Dalainn, a tribe whose ancestor, Dalann, is traced back to Fergus (<a href="/wiki/Kings_of_Ulster" class="mw-redirect" title="Kings of Ulster">King of Ulster</a>), son of Ross, son of Rudhraighe. The Corco-Dalann were from an island referred to as "Insula Nuligi", and is usually identified with Inis-Doimhle or Inis-Uladh, which is now the Little Island, in the River <a href="/wiki/Suir" class="mw-redirect" title="Suir">Suir</a>, south-east of <a href="/wiki/Waterford" title="Waterford">Waterford</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lughadh was a distinguished <a href="/wiki/Bard" title="Bard">bard</a>, a highly trained, professional itinerant poet. Lughadh settled at Glengiven, in what is now <a href="/wiki/County_Londonderry" title="County Londonderry">County Londonderry</a>. Lughadh ended up under the favour and protection of the chief of Cianachta, and became the tutor of the chieftain's son, Geal Breagach.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cainnech's mother was called Maul or Mella.<sup id="cite_ref-Smith_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> She attained an eminent degree of sanctity. The church of Thompleamoul or Capella Sanctae Maulae seu Mellae, beside Kilkenny city, was dedicated to God under her invocation.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg/150px-Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="215" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg/225px-Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg/300px-Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_07_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg 2x" data-file-width="621" data-file-height="892" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian of Clonard</a> imparting his blessing to the Twelve Apostles of Ireland</figcaption></figure>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Early_Christian_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christian Ireland">early Christian Ireland</a> the druid tradition collapsed, with the spread of the new faith. The study of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and Christian theology flourished in monasteries.
</p><p>Cainnech spent his early years watching his chieftain's flocks. In 543 Cainnech became a pupil at <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian</a>'s monastic school at <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard</a>. During the sixth century, some of the most significant names in the history of Irish <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> studied at the Clonard monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Twelve students who studied under <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian of Clonard</a> became known as the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>, Cainnech was one of these. It was at Clonard that Cainnech became a friend and companion of <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Colmcille</a> (Columba).
</p><p>In 544 he studied under <a href="/wiki/Mobh%C3%AD_Cl%C3%A1rainech" title="Mobhí Clárainech">Mobhí Clárainech</a> at the school of <a href="/wiki/Glasnevin" title="Glasnevin">Glasnevin</a>, with <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Kieran of Clonmacnoise</a> and <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">Comgall</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a>. When plague scattered that community, he went to <a href="/wiki/Cadoc" title="Cadoc">Cadoc</a>'s monastery of <a href="/wiki/Llancarfan" title="Llancarfan">Llancarfan</a> in <a href="/wiki/Glamorganshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Glamorganshire">Glamorganshire</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, where he was ordained a priest in 545.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He left for <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to obtain the blessing of the reigning pontiff. In 550 he had returned to Glengiven, where he converted his foster-brother, Geal-Breagach, who afterwards assisted him in founding Drumachose, in nearby <a href="/wiki/Limavady" title="Limavady">Limavady</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Scotland">Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 565 Cainnech joined Columba in Scotland, where he is known as Kenneth. Adamnan tells of the arrival of Cainnech, on <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>. <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a> had a prophecy of a "certain holy and excellent man, who will arrive here among us before evening." According to Adamnan, God provided Cainnech with a safe and calm crossing, even though the sea was perilous and stormy that day. Columba received him that evening with all honour and hospitality.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated2_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cainnech built a church in the place now known as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrews" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrews">Saint Andrews</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He built monastic cells on the island of Ibdon, possibly <a href="/wiki/South_Uist" title="South Uist">South Uist</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and Eninis, an oratory called Lagan-Kenny on the shores of <a href="/wiki/Loch_Laggan" title="Loch Laggan">Loch Laggan</a> (the remains of which are marked on the OS map), and a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a> on the banks of the Eden. The saint may have been an important saint in converting <a href="/wiki/South_Uist" title="South Uist">South Uist</a> to <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Scottish_mission" title="Hiberno-Scottish mission">Christianity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Cainnech's name is still recalled in the ruins of an ancient church, Kil-Chainnech on <a href="/wiki/Tiree" title="Tiree">Tiree</a>, in a burial ground, Kil-Chainnech, in <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> and <a href="/wiki/Inch_Kenneth" title="Inch Kenneth">Inch Kenneth</a> off <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Mull" title="Isle of Mull">Mull</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kilchenzie_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kilchenzie-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_Ireland">Return to Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Return to Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cainnech spent a good deal of his time in <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Osraige" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Osraige">Ossory</a> in what is now <a href="/wiki/County_Laois" title="County Laois">County Laois</a>. In Ossory he had a good repute with the king, Colmann son of Feradach. Colman gave him grants of land including <a href="/wiki/Aghaboe" title="Aghaboe">Aghaboe</a> ("the field of the Ox") which became his principal monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-sabine_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Aghaboe grew in importance, and in the 7th century sent <a href="/wiki/Vergilius_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Vergilius of Salzburg">Feargal</a> as a missionary to the church of <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>. Aghaboe was for a time the site of the bishop's see until under Norman influence in the twelfth century the see transferred from Aghaboe to Kilkenny.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In 1346 Diarmaid Mac Giollaphádraig burned the town of Aghaboe, and completely destroyed Cainnech's shrine along with his relics.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg/226px-Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg" decoding="async" width="226" height="170" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg/339px-Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg/452px-Kilkenny_St._Canice_Cathedral.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2848" data-file-height="2136" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/St._Canice%27s_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Canice&#39;s Cathedral">St. Canice's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kilkenny" title="Kilkenny">Kilkenny</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Kilkenny" title="Kilkenny">Kilkenny</a> (Irish: Cill Chainnigh "The Church of Cainnech") was originally the name of a church erected by or dedicated to Cainnech, but was afterwards extended to the townland and parish.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Donovan1839_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Donovan1839-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Kilkenny was one of the last parts of Ireland to be converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. Tradition asserts that in 597, Cainnech led a Christian force to Kilkenny to eliminate the last bastion of Druidic rule in Ireland. The last Archdruid of Ireland had retired with his Council to a mound in Kilkenny for safety. Cainnech led an army there and overcame them. He founded a monastery near what is now the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland's</a> St. Canice's Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He died and was interred at <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Aghaboe" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Aghaboe">Abbey of Aghaboe</a> in 599/600.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Chain_of_Cainnech">Chain of Cainnech</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Chain of Cainnech">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In his old age Cainnech retired to an island in what was once Loch Cree, and wrote a commentary on all four Gospels. This became known as <i>Glass Kinnich</i> (<i>Glas-Chainnigh</i>) or the <i>Chain of Cainnech</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabine_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This was long preserved in his church and became a continuous commentary in the Middle Ages.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cainnech is the patron of <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Aghaboe" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Aghaboe">Aghaboe</a> and together with <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir" title="Ciarán of Saigir">Ciarán of Saigir</a>, is one of the patrons of Kilkenny and the historic <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ossory" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Ossory">kingdom of Osraige</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Smith_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Cainnech is also the patron saint of the shipwrecked.<sup id="cite_ref-kilchenzie_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kilchenzie-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span id="Troparion_of_St_Cainnech_.28tone_8.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Troparion_of_St_Cainnech_(tone_8)">Troparion of St Cainnech (tone 8)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cainnech_of_Aghaboe&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Troparion of St Cainnech (tone 8)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>This is a <a href="/wiki/Troparion" title="Troparion">Troparion</a> of St Cainnech. <br />
In honour thou dost rank with Ireland's Enlightener, <br />
O Lover of the Desert, Composer of sacred verse, <br />
Father of Monks and Founder of Monasteries, O Father Cainnech. <br />
Labouring for Christ, both in thy native land and in Scotland, <br />
thou art a tireless intercessor for the faithful. <br />
Pray for us who hymn thee, that despite our frailty we may be granted great mercy.
