<p>Saint <b>Marie-Adolphine Dierkx</b> (1866–1900, born <b>Anna Catharina</b> or <b>Kaatje Dierkx</b>) was a Dutch nun who died for her faith in China during the <a href="/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion" title="Boxer Rebellion">Boxer Rebellion</a> and was canonised in 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-csi_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-csi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-vanv_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vanv-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She is one of the group known as the <a href="/wiki/Martyr_Saints_of_China" title="Martyr Saints of China">Martyr Saints of China</a> who were canonised by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 1 October 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-martyrs_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrs-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-vatican_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-hagiog_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hagiog-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-caspers_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-caspers-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her birthplace has been converted into a chapel.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marie-Adolphine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born Anna Catharina Dierkx on 8 March 1866 in <a href="/wiki/Ossendrecht" title="Ossendrecht">Ossendrecht</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Brabant" title="North Brabant">North Brabant</a>, Netherlands.<sup id="cite_ref-cdr_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cdr-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> She was one of six children in a poor family, and her mother died while she was young. She worked in a <a href="/wiki/Chicory" title="Chicory">chicory</a>-processing factory and in domestic service before joining the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Missionaries_of_Mary" title="Franciscan Missionaries of Mary">Franciscan Missionaries of Mary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a> in 1893 and taking the name Marie-Adolphine. In 1899 she was one of seven sisters from the Franciscan order who went to China to help at an orphanage. The orphanage, soon cared for 200 children and Marie-Adolphine was placed in charge of the laundry.<sup id="cite_ref-caspers_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-caspers-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-fmm_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fmm-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 9 July the nuns were murdered as part of the <a href="/wiki/Taiyuan_massacre" title="Taiyuan massacre">Taiyuan massacre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fmm_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fmm-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Marie-Adolphine was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on 24 November 1946. She was canonised joyfully by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 1 October 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-pilgrims_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pilgrims-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>There are two statues of Marie-Adolphine in St Gertrude's church <a href="/wiki/Ossendrecht" title="Ossendrecht">Ossendrecht</a>, one in stone<sup id="cite_ref-statue-stone_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-statue-stone-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and one in wood.<sup id="cite_ref-statue-wood_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-statue-wood-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The house where she was born has been converted into a chapel, the Adolphinekapel, to which pilgrimages are made. The converted chapel was first opened in <a href="/wiki/1954" title="1954">1954</a> and in <a href="/wiki/1966" title="1966">1966</a> the neighbouring building was purchased and demolished so that the Adolphineplein could be extended. In <a href="/wiki/1983" title="1983">1983</a> the first chapel was replaced by a new chapel. The new chapel still hinted that it had been converted from her birthplace. It was opened by the Bishop of Breda <a href="/wiki/Hubertus_Ernst" title="Hubertus Ernst">Hubertus Ernst</a> on 29 May <a href="/wiki/1983" title="1983">1983</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bedevaarten_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bedevaarten-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Her_name">Her name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marie-Adolphine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Her name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her names appear in sources in many versions. Some of this appears to be confusion caused when her name was registered.<sup id="cite_ref-name_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Her religious name appears as Maria, Marie or Mary, with or without a hyphen, Adolphine or Adolfine; her birth surname is spelled Dierkx, Diercks, Dierks, Dirks; she was Anne or Anna, Catharina or Catherine, known as Kaatje, or Judoca (her mother's name).<sup id="cite_ref-bedevaarten_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bedevaarten-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Her father further complicated the records when he registered her as dead when she was three, but it was her sister who had died, and the records remained in error.<sup id="cite_ref-name_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-name-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
