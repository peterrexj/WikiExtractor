<p><b>Louis I, Duke of Anjou</b> (23 July 1339 – 20 September 1384) was a French prince, the second son of <a href="/wiki/John_II_of_France" title="John II of France">John II of France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bonne_of_Luxembourg" title="Bonne of Luxembourg">Bonne of Bohemia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeane201617_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeane201617-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  His career was markedly unsuccessful.  Born at the <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Vincennes" title="Château de Vincennes">Château de Vincennes</a>, Louis was the first of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Valois-Anjou" title="House of Valois-Anjou">Angevin branch</a> of the French royal house. His father appointed him <a href="/wiki/List_of_Counts_and_Dukes_of_Anjou" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Counts and Dukes of Anjou">Count of Anjou</a> and <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Maine" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Maine">Count of Maine</a> in 1356, and then raised him to the title Duke of Anjou in 1360 and <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Touraine" title="Duke of Touraine">Duke of Touraine</a> in 1370.
</p><p>He fought in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Poitiers_(1356)" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Poitiers (1356)">Battle of Poitiers (1356)</a>, in which his father the king was captured by the English. In 1360, he was one of a group of hostages the French surrendered to the English in exchange for the king. He escaped from England, after which his father felt bound in honour to return to English custody, where he later died.
</p><p>In 1382, as the adopted son of <a href="/wiki/Joanna_I_of_Naples" title="Joanna I of Naples">Joanna I of Naples</a>, he succeeded to the counties of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Provence" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Provence">Provence</a> and <a href="/wiki/County_of_Forcalquier" title="County of Forcalquier">Forcalquier</a>. He also inherited from her a claim to the kingdoms of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Naples</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem" title="Kingdom of Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. He was already a veteran of the <a href="/wiki/Hundred_Years%27_War" title="Hundred Years&#39; War">Hundred Years' War</a> against the English when he led an army into Italy to claim his Neapolitan inheritance. He died on the march and his claims and titles fell to his son and namesake, <a href="/wiki/Louis_II_of_Anjou" title="Louis II of Anjou">Louis II</a>, who succeeded in ruling Naples for a time.
</p>

<h2><span id="Hundred_Years.27_War"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hundred_Years'_War">Hundred Years' War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_I_of_Anjou&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hundred Years&#039; War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Louis was present at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Poitiers_(1356)" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Poitiers (1356)">Battle of Poitiers (1356)</a>, in the battalion commanded by his brother <a href="/wiki/Charles_V_of_France" title="Charles V of France">Charles</a>, the Dauphin. They hardly fought and the whole group escaped in the middle of the confrontation. Although humiliating, their flight allowed them to avoid capture by the English, who won the battle decisively. King <a href="/wiki/John_II_of_France" title="John II of France">John II</a> and Louis' younger brother <a href="/wiki/Philip_II,_Duke_of_Burgundy" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip II, Duke of Burgundy">Philip</a> were not so fortunate and were captured by the English, commanded by <a href="/wiki/Edward,_the_Black_Prince" class="mw-redirect" title="Edward, the Black Prince">Edward, the Black Prince</a>. Their ransom and peace conditions between <a href="/wiki/France_in_the_Middle_Ages" title="France in the Middle Ages">France</a> and <a href="/wiki/England_in_the_Middle_Ages" title="England in the Middle Ages">England</a> were agreed in the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Br%C3%A9tigny" title="Treaty of Brétigny">Treaty of Brétigny</a>, signed in 1360. Amongst the complicated items of the treaty was a clause that determined the surrender of 40 high-born hostages as guarantee for the payment of the king's ransom. Louis, already Duke of Anjou, was in this group and sailed to England in October 1360. However, France was not in good economic condition and further installments of the debt were delayed. As consequence, Louis was in English custody for much more than the expected six months. He tried to negotiate his freedom in a private negotiation with <a href="/wiki/Edward_III_of_England" title="Edward III of England">Edward III of England</a> and, when this failed, decided to escape. On his return to France, he met his father's disapproval for his unknightly behavior. John II considered himself dishonored and this, combined with the fact that his ransom payments agreed to in the Treaty of Brétigny were in arrears, caused John to return to captivity in England to redeem his honor.
</p><p>From 1380 to 1382 Louis served as <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">regent</a> for his nephew, King <a href="/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France" title="Charles VI of France">Charles VI of France</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="King_of_Naples">King of Naples</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_I_of_Anjou&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: King of Naples">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1382 Louis left France in the latter year to claim the throne of Naples following the death of Queen <a href="/wiki/Joanna_I_of_Naples" title="Joanna I of Naples">Joanna I</a>. She had adopted him to succeed her, as she was childless and did not wish to leave her inheritance to any of her close relatives, whom she considered enemies. He was also able to succeed her as count of Provence and Forcalquier. Despite his coronation at <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> as King of Naples by <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_VII" title="Antipope Clement VII">Antipope Clement VII</a>, Louis was forced to remain in France and Joan's troops were defeated by <a href="/wiki/Charles_III_of_Naples" title="Charles III of Naples">Charles of Durazzo</a>, her second cousin and previous heir. Joanna was killed in her prison in <a href="/wiki/San_Fele" title="San Fele">San Fele</a> in 1382; Louis, with support of the Antipope, France, <a href="/wiki/Bernab%C3%B2_Visconti" title="Bernabò Visconti">Bernabò Visconti</a> of Milan and <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_VI_of_Savoy" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus VI of Savoy">Amadeus VI of Savoy</a>, and using the money he had been able to obtain during the regency, launched an expedition to regain the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a> from Charles.
</p><p>The expedition, counting to some 40,000 troops,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was however unsuccessful. Charles, who counted on the mercenary companies under <a href="/wiki/John_Hawkwood" title="John Hawkwood">John Hawkwood</a> for a total of some 14,000 men, was able to divert the French from Naples to other regions of the kingdom and to harass them with guerrilla tactics. Amadeus fell ill and died in <a href="/wiki/Molise" title="Molise">Molise</a> on 1 March 1383 and his troops abandoned the field. Louis asked for help from his king nephew in France, who sent him an army under <a href="/wiki/Enguerrand_VII,_Lord_of_Coucy" class="mw-redirect" title="Enguerrand VII, Lord of Coucy">Enguerrand of Coucy</a>. The latter was able to conquer <a href="/wiki/Arezzo" title="Arezzo">Arezzo</a> and then invade the Kingdom of Naples, but midway was reached by the news that Louis had suddenly died at <a href="/wiki/Bisceglie" title="Bisceglie">Bisceglie</a> on 20 September 1384. He soon sold Arezzo to <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Florence" title="Republic of Florence">Florence</a> and returned to France.
</p>
