<p><b>Jean-François Régis</b>, SJ, commonly known as <b>Saint John Francis Regis</b> and <b>Saint Regis</b> (31 January 1597 &#8211; 31 December 1640), was a French <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in 1737. A tireless preacher, Regis is best known for his work with at-risk women and orphans.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Francis_Regis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jean-François Régis was born 31 January 1597, in <a href="/wiki/Fontcouverte,_Aude" title="Fontcouverte, Aude">Fontcouverte, Aude</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Languedoc" title="Languedoc">Languedoc</a> region of southern France. His father, Jean Régis, had recently been ennobled as a result of service rendered during the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_League_(French)" title="Catholic League (French)">Wars of the League</a>. His mother, Marguerite de Cugunhan, was of a noble family.<sup id="cite_ref-van_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was educated at the Jesuit College of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9ziers" title="Béziers">Béziers</a>. On 8 December 1616, in his nineteenth year, he entered the Jesuit novitiate at <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> and he took his vows two years later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHoever1955229_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoever1955229-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After finishing his course in <a href="/wiki/Rhetoric" title="Rhetoric">rhetoric</a> at <a href="/wiki/Cahors" title="Cahors">Cahors</a>, Regis was sent to teach <a href="/wiki/Grammar" title="Grammar">grammar</a> at several colleges: <a href="/wiki/Billom" title="Billom">Billom</a> (1619–22), <a href="/wiki/Puy-en-Velay" class="mw-redirect" title="Puy-en-Velay">Puy-en-Velay</a> (1625–27), and <a href="/wiki/Auch" title="Auch">Auch</a> (1627–28). While he was teaching, he also pursued his studies in <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> at the scholasticate at <a href="/wiki/Tournon-d%27Agenais" title="Tournon-d&#39;Agenais">Tournon</a>. Noted for an intense love of preaching and <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">teaching the Faith</a>, as well as the desire to save souls,<sup id="cite_ref-van_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Regis began his study of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> in 1628. Less than two years later, in 1630, he was ordained a priest at 31. The following year, having completed his studies, Regis made his <a href="/wiki/Tertianship" title="Tertianship">tertianship</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHoever1955230_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoever1955230-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Regis was now fully prepared for his lifework and entered upon his apostolic career in the summer of 1631. He was a tireless worker who spent most of his life serving the marginalized.<sup id="cite_ref-regisu_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-regisu-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As a newly ordained priest, he worked with <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a> victims in Toulouse. From May 1632 until September 1634, his headquarters was at the Jesuit College of <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>. Here he labored for the conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Huguenots</a>, visited hospitals, assisted the needy, withdrew from vice wayward women and girls, and preached Catholic <a href="/wiki/Doctrine" title="Doctrine">doctrine</a> with tireless zeal to children and the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Regis is best known for his work with at-risk women and orphans. He established safe houses and found jobs for them.<sup id="cite_ref-regisu_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-regisu-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Regis established the <i>Confraternity of the Blessed Sacrament</i>, which organized charitable collections of money and food from the wealthy. He also established several <a href="/wiki/Hostels" class="mw-redirect" title="Hostels">hostels</a> for prostitutes, and helped many become trained lace makers, which provided them with a stable income and an opportunity to avoid the threat of exploitation.<sup id="cite_ref-regisu_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-regisu-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1633, Regis went to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Viviers" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Viviers">Diocese of Viviers</a> at the invitation of the local bishop, <a href="/wiki/Monsignor" title="Monsignor">Monsignor</a> Louis II de la Baume de Suze, giving missions throughout the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHoever1955230_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoever1955230-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> From 1633 to 1640 he <a href="/wiki/Evangelism" title="Evangelism">evangelized</a> more than fifty districts in le <a href="/wiki/Vivarais" title="Vivarais">Vivarais</a>, le <a href="/wiki/Forez" title="Forez">Forez</a>, and le <a href="/wiki/Velay" title="Velay">Velay</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-van_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Regis labored diligently on behalf of both priests and laypersons. His preaching style was said to have been simple and direct.  He appealed to the uneducated peasantry and numerous conversions resulted.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHoever1955230_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoever1955230-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Regis's labors reaped a harvest of conversions. However, his boldness – perceived as arrogance in some cases – led to a conflict with certain other priests, a period of tension with the local bishop, and even threats of violence from those whose vices he condemned.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Although he longed to devote himself to the conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">indigenous inhabitants</a> of Canada,<sup id="cite_ref-Native_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Native-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> he remained in France all his life.
</p><p>Regis walked from town to town, in rough mountainous areas where travel was difficult, especially in the winter.<sup id="cite_ref-regisu_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-regisu-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He died of <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> at age forty-three on 31 December 1640,<sup id="cite_ref-foley_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> at <a href="/wiki/Lalouvesc" title="Lalouvesc">Lalouvesc</a> (<a href="/wiki/Ard%C3%A8che" title="Ardèche">Ardèche</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Francis_Regis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Francis Regis was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a> on 18 May 1716,<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> on 5 April 1737.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Francis_Regis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>John Francis Regis is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Lace" title="Lace">lacemakers</a>, medical social workers, and illegitimate children.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Francis_Regis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In a 1997 letter to the Bishop of Viviers, Pope John Paul II commemorated the fourth centenary of St. John Francis Regis' birth, honoring him as a "lofty figure of holiness" and an example for the Church in the modern world.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Today, Regis' name lives on across the world. There are lakes, mountains, hotels, apartment complexes, swimming pools, and streets with his namesake.<sup id="cite_ref-regisu_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-regisu-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Parishes are dedicated to St. John Francis Regis in <a href="/wiki/Arnaudville,_Louisiana" title="Arnaudville, Louisiana">Arnaudville, Louisiana</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Hollywood,_Maryland" title="Hollywood, Maryland">Hollywood, Maryland</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri" title="Kansas City, Missouri">Kansas City, Missouri</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Regis_University" title="Regis University">Regis University</a>, located in Colorado, along with the Regis Campus of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ignatius%27_College,_Riverview" title="Saint Ignatius&#39; College, Riverview">Saint Ignatius' College, Riverview</a>, are both named in his honor, as well as numerous elementary and high schools worldwide, including <a href="/wiki/Regis_High_School_(New_York_City)" title="Regis High School (New York City)">Regis High School</a> (<a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>), <a href="/wiki/Regis_School_of_the_Sacred_Heart" class="mw-redirect" title="Regis School of the Sacred Heart">Regis School of the Sacred Heart</a> (<a href="/wiki/Houston,_Texas" class="mw-redirect" title="Houston, Texas">Houston, Texas</a>), <a href="/wiki/Regis_High_School_(Oregon)" title="Regis High School (Oregon)">Regis High School</a> (<a href="/wiki/Stayton,_Oregon" title="Stayton, Oregon">Stayton, Oregon</a>), <a href="/wiki/Regis_High_School_(Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin)" class="mw-redirect" title="Regis High School (Eau Claire, Wisconsin)">Regis High School</a> (<a href="/wiki/Eau_Claire,_Wisconsin" title="Eau Claire, Wisconsin">Eau Claire, Wisconsin</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Regis_Jesuit_High_School" title="Regis Jesuit High School">Regis Jesuit High School</a> (<a href="/wiki/Aurora,_Colorado" title="Aurora, Colorado">Aurora, Colorado</a>).
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Regis_Mohawk_Reservation" title="St. Regis Mohawk Reservation">St. Regis Mohawk Reservation</a>, where a Roman Catholic church named for him stands, is also named in his honor, due to his respect for the native inhabitants of North America.<sup id="cite_ref-Native_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Native-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Jesuit mission at <a href="/wiki/Conewago_Chapel" class="mw-redirect" title="Conewago Chapel">Conewago</a>, Pennsylvania, was named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-hbgdiocese_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hbgdiocese-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
