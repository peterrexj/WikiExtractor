					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Indract</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Confessor and martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">unknown<br />Ireland</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">unknown, but traditionally either <a href="/wiki/Huish_Episcopi" title="Huish Episcopi">Huish Episcopi</a> or <a href="/wiki/Shapwick,_Somerset" title="Shapwick, Somerset">Shapwick</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">5 February</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><div style="display: inline-block; line-height: 1.2em; padding: .1em 0;"><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)#In_Christianity" title="Cult (religious practice)">Catholic cult</a> suppressed</div></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">Dissolution of the Monasteries</a>, 1539</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Indract</b> or <b>Indracht</b> was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> who, along with his companions, was venerated at <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a>, a monastery in the <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">county of Somerset</a> in south-western England. In the <a href="/wiki/High_Middle_Ages" title="High Middle Ages">High Middle Ages</a> Glastonbury tradition held that he had been an Irish <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrim</a> — a king's son – on his way back from Rome who was molested and killed by a local <a href="/wiki/Thegn" title="Thegn">thegn</a> after he had stopped off to visit the <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> of <a href="/wiki/St_Patrick" class="mw-redirect" title="St Patrick">St Patrick</a>. This tradition synchronised his life with that of <a href="/wiki/Ine_of_Wessex" title="Ine of Wessex">King Ine</a> (688–726), though historian <a href="/wiki/Michael_Lapidge" title="Michael Lapidge">Michael Lapidge</a> has argued that he is most likely to represent a 9th-century <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Iona" title="Abbot of Iona">abbot of Iona</a> named <a href="/wiki/Indrechtach_ua_F%C3%ADnnachta" class="mw-redirect" title="Indrechtach ua Fínnachta">Indrechtach ua Fínnachta</a>.
</p><p>The cult seems to date from the late 10th or early 11th century, though this is uncertain. There is one main extant account, the anonymous 12th century <i>Passio sancti Indracti</i>. An earlier text written in <a href="/wiki/Old_English" title="Old English">Old English</a> is said to have existed and been used by the writer of the <i>Passio</i>. There is also evidence that the 12th-century historian <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a> wrote his own saint's life, and although now lost it may also have used the Old English text. In the 14th century a <a href="/wiki/St_Alban%27s_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St Alban&#39;s Abbey">St Alban's</a> monk added significant new material of probable <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornish</a> origin, mentioning a sister named Dominica (for whom <a href="/wiki/St_Dominic,_Cornwall" title="St Dominic, Cornwall">St Dominic, Cornwall</a> is named) and some miracles.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_evidence">Early evidence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Indract_of_Glastonbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early evidence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The body of Indract supposedly lay in a stone shrine, with <a href="/wiki/St_Patrick" class="mw-redirect" title="St Patrick">St Patrick</a>'s, in the Old Church of St Mary at <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The historical identity of the Indract resting in this shrine is obscure, but it is unlikely that he can be identified with any known figure of the 7th or 8th centuries, the period of his life according to later Glastonbury sources.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is however a strong similarity between the story of the Glastonbury Indract and that of a 9th-century <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Iona" title="Abbot of Iona">abbot of Iona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Indrechtach_ua_F%C3%ADnnachta" class="mw-redirect" title="Indrechtach ua Fínnachta">Indrechtach ua Fínnachta</a>, whom several contemporary Irish sources report as being "martyred among the English (<i>apud Saxones</i>)" in 854.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> These sources give his death date as 12 March, which differs from the Canterbury date of 8 May.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A plausible explanation is that later monks at Glastonbury, possessing the body and only a bare story, invented the rest.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The cult, although never widespread outside Glastonbury, became known in Ireland: the  <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Tallaght" title="Martyrology of Tallaght">Martyrology of Tallaght</a></i> in the 12th century <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Leinster" title="Book of Leinster">Book of Leinster</a> has a marginal note about the Glastonbury Indract and also lists his feast day as 8 May.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is no evidence however that Indract's cult existed at Glastonbury before the 11th century.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">calendar</a> produced at Glastonbury around 970 (from the <i><a href="/wiki/Leofric_Missal" title="Leofric Missal">Leofric Missal</a></i>) omits his name, yet in a <a href="/wiki/Leominster" title="Leominster">Leominster</a> <a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">litany</a> (BL <a href="/wiki/Cotton_Galba" class="mw-redirect" title="Cotton Galba">Cotton Galba</a> A xiv) dated by historian Michael Lapidge to the second quarter of the 11th century his name is listed as "confessor", and is placed next to St Patrick's, hinting at a Glastonbury base for the cult.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> This litany is the earliest evidence of the cult for Indract, in England at least.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-424_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-424-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He is named as a "martyr" in a late 11th-century litany from <a href="/wiki/Winchester" title="Winchester">Winchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Passio_sancti_Indracti"><i>Passio sancti Indracti</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Indract_of_Glastonbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Passio sancti Indracti">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His story in its earliest form is told in the 12th century <i>Passio sancti Indracti</i> or "Passion of St Indract" (Oxford Bodleian Library MS Digby 112).<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-424_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-424-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Passio'</i>s anonymous author claims that he used an earlier life in <a href="/wiki/Old_English" title="Old English">Old English</a> as his source.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-425_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-425-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This earlier work has not survived.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-425_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-425-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i>Passio</i>, Indract was a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and the son of an Irish king.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He and his nine companions had gone to Rome on pilgrimage and on their return journey they decided to visit Glastonbury and the shrine of St Patrick there, staying for a night at a place called <a href="/wiki/Huish_Episcopi" title="Huish Episcopi">Huish Episcopi</a> (<i>Hywisc</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> As it happened, the ruler of the region, <a href="/wiki/Ine_of_Wessex" title="Ine of Wessex">King Ine</a>, was staying nearby at <a href="/wiki/South_Petherton" title="South Petherton">South Petherton</a> (<i>Pedred</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> A king's <a href="/wiki/Thegn" title="Thegn">thegn</a> named Husa, with some followers, attacked and killed the Irish pilgrims, believing they possessed gold.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> After a posthumous miracle, King Ine had the bodies of most of the martyrs buried in the church of St Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The body of one companion is said not to have been found, but on their feast day, 8 May, a column of light is said to emanate from his place of burial.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The text proceeds to recount some more posthumous miracles, including a vision by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Guthlac_of_Glastonbury&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guthlac of Glastonbury (page does not exist)">Guthlac of Glastonbury</a>, a future <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Glastonbury" title="Abbot of Glastonbury">abbot of Glastonbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-426_12-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-426-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="William_of_Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Indract_of_Glastonbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: William of Malmesbury">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The 16th century antiquarian <a href="/wiki/John_Leland_(antiquary)" title="John Leland (antiquary)">John Leland</a> wrote that among the various <a href="/wiki/Saint_lives" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint lives">saint lives</a> at Glastonbury was a <i>Vita Indracti</i> by William of Malmesbury.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-434-35_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-434-35-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> William of Malmesbury, in his <i>De Antiquitate Glastoniensis Ecclesiae</i>, claimed to have discussed the saint in another work.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-434_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-434-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> William's <i>Vita Indracti</i>, though once believed to have been the <i>Passio</i>, has not survived.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Indract is however mentioned by William of Malmesbury in three surviving works.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-435_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-435-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Notable "discrepancies" [Lapidge] between the <i>Passio</i> and William's assertions in these works include his failure to associate Indract with Abbot Guthlac (despite making mention of this abbot in other contexts), failure to name the location of the martyring, and giving the number of Indract's companions as seven.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-435-36_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-435-36-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Historian Michael Lapidge believed that the source for William of Malmesbury's work was Old English text, and that differences between the <i>Passio</i> and William of Malmesbury can be accounted for by embellishments added by the <i>Passio</i> author.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_evidence">Later evidence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Indract_of_Glastonbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later evidence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Seen of Glastonbury, writing around 1342, is the next important source of information about Indract and his cult.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-436_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-436-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> He repeats more or less the same story as the <i>Passio</i>, but his account differs in various details.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-436_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-436-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The martyrdom takes place at <a href="/wiki/Shapwick,_Somerset" title="Shapwick, Somerset">Shapwick</a> (<i>Schapwik</i>), not Huish Episcopi (<i>Hywisc</i>), and he follows William of Malmesbury in giving the number of companions as seven.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-436_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-436-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Michael Lapidge suggested, on the basis of similarities with William of Malmesbury, that John Seen had probably consulted William's lost work.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-436_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-436-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/St_Alban%27s_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="St Alban&#39;s Abbey">St Alban's</a> monk <a href="/wiki/John_of_Tynemouth_(chronicler)" title="John of Tynemouth (chronicler)">John of Tynemouth</a>, another mid 14th century author, adds information regarding Indract in his <i>Sanctilogium Angliae</i>. Although otherwise summarising the <i>Passio</i> account, he relates a new tradition about Indract and a sister of his named Dominica.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-437_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-437-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Indract with his (nine) companions and his sister Dominica, on their way to Rome, stopped at a place called <i>Tamerunta</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-437_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-437-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> There Indract drove his staff into the ground, causing an oak tree to grow, and there he caused a pond to provide a plentiful supply of fish.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-437_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-437-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The place <i>Tamerunta</i> (see "<a href="/wiki/Tamerton_Foliot" title="Tamerton Foliot">Tamerton Foliot</a>") lies along the <a href="/wiki/River_Tamar" title="River Tamar">river Tamar</a> on the Cornish border, and suggests that this new information came from a Cornish source, perhaps the church of <a href="/wiki/St_German%27s_Priory" title="St German&#39;s Priory">St Germans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The chronicler <a href="/wiki/William_Worcester" title="William Worcester">William Worcester</a>, writing in 1478, claimed that Indract and his companions lay at <a href="/wiki/Shepton_Mallet" title="Shepton Mallet">Shepton Mallet</a>, five miles from Glastonbury.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> This may be a misunderstanding, perhaps based on a commemoration stone at Shapwick, which William has confused with Shepton.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-438_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-438-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Indract's relics are listed in two 14th century Glastonbury lists of relics (BL Cotton Titus D vii fols. 2r–13v and Cambridge Trinity College MS R.5.33 (724) fols. 104r–105v).<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-438_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-438-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As an indication of the local nature of his cult, his name occurs in only one English calendar of saints, a 15th-century manuscript probably written at Glastonbury (Up Holland College, MS 98).<sup id="cite_ref-Lapidge-438_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapidge-438-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> A chapel at <a href="/wiki/St_Dominick" class="mw-redirect" title="St Dominick">St Dominick</a> in Cornwall was dedicated to him, though the suggestion that <a href="/wiki/Landrake" title="Landrake">Landrake</a> was named after him has been deemed "impossible" [Lapidge].<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> This chapel was licensed in 1405 and 1418 and may have been at a place called Chapel where there is a holy well.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  There is a small modern chapel dedicated to St Indract at Halton Quay near St Dominic.
</p>
