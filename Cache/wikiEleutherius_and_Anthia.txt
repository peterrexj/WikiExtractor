<p><b>Eleutherius</b> (or <b>Eleut(h)erus</b> or <b>Eleftherios</b>; sometimes called <b>Liberalis</b> or <b>Liberator</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the former transliterations and the latter translations of his (Albanian: <i>Shën Lefter,</i> <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἐλευθέριος</span></span>) and his mother <b>Antia</b> (or <b>Anthia</b>) (Albanian: <i>Shën Anthi,</i><a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἀνθία</span></span>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Santi Eleuterio e Anzia</i>) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> in Greece and Albania.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eleutherius_and_Antia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, Eleutherius's  father died when he was a young child and his mother, Anthia, took him to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Anicetus" title="Pope Anicetus">Anicetus</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a>, who taught him in the divine scriptures.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Eleutherius is venerated as a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Pannonia" title="Diocese of Pannonia">Illyricum</a>; according to tradition, Antia was his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  According to a source in Greek dating from before the 5th century, Antia was the widow of a <a href="/wiki/Consul" title="Consul">consul</a> named Eugenius.  Her son Eleutherius was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and then consecrated as bishop by a man named Anicetus.  This tradition may have originated through confusion with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eleutherius" title="Pope Eleutherius">Pope Eleutherius</a>, who may have been a deacon of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Anicetus" title="Pope Anicetus">Pope Anicetus</a> (c. 154–164).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The tradition states that Eleutherius was appointed bishop of Messina and Illyricum at the age of twenty and apparently settled in Valona. He was imprisoned by a <i><a href="/wiki/Comes" title="Comes">comes</a></i> named Felix; Eleutherius and Antia were taken to Rome to be judged by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a>. According to this source, Eleutherius and Antia were both condemned to death on December 15.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> According to tradition, Eleutherius was <a href="/wiki/Club_(weapon)" title="Club (weapon)">clubbed</a> to death, while Antia was beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> translation of this Greek text, dating from around the 8th century, states that Anicetus, after consecrating Eleutherius, assigned him to the see of <i>Apuliam Aecanam civitatem</i> (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Aeca&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Aeca (page does not exist)">Aeca</a>).  Eleutherius and Antia were then taken to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and killed on April 18. The source states that the citizens of Aeca retrieved the bodies of the two martyrs from Rome and returned to their city with them.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Baronius" class="mw-redirect" title="Baronius">Baronius</a> uses the descriptive <i>Episcopi Illyrici</i> (bishop of Illyricum) in his <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>, since he consulted the Greek source. <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> believed the association with Aeca was erroneous, and centuries earlier, Florus had believed <i>Apuliam Aecanam</i> was an error for <i>Apuliam Messenam</i> (<a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a>), but the association with Messina may also be erroneous. The confusion is increased when it is taken into account the fact that Eleutherius' name, which means "one who is free," was translated into Latin as <i>Liberator</i> or <i>Liberalis</i>; he may have been confused with <a href="/wiki/St._Liberalis" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Liberalis">other saints named Liberalis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Messina still claims Eleutherius and Antia as natives, stating that he was born in this <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicilian</a> city on April 18, 121, and that later Eleutherius became a bishop of Illyricum.<sup id="cite_ref-granmirci_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granmirci-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  They were tortured with hot boiling oil, <a href="/wiki/Resin" title="Resin">resin</a>, and heated irons, and then thrown to the lions; none had the desired effect and finally the two were executed.
</p><p>Their bodies were then, according to tradition, buried in the Roman church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Santa Sabina</a>, in the altar of San Lorenzo, and then moved to <a href="/wiki/San_Giovanni_della_Pigna" class="mw-redirect" title="San Giovanni della Pigna">San Giovanni della Pigna</a>, near the <a href="/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome" title="Pantheon, Rome">Pantheon</a>, with the relics of <a href="/wiki/Genesius_of_Rome" title="Genesius of Rome">Genesius of Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-granmirci_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granmirci-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The association with San Giovanni della Pigna may also be a result of confusion with Pope Eleuterus, whose relics were also said to have been translated to San Giovanni della Pigna.  Christians from <a href="/wiki/Rieti" title="Rieti">Rieti</a> then may have carried their relics to their city, which still claims them.<sup id="cite_ref-enrosadira_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-enrosadira-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eleutherius_and_Antia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg/250px-Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="333" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg/375px-Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg/500px-Serramonacesca_chiesa_benedettina_03.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/San_Liberatore_a_Maiella" title="San Liberatore a Maiella">San Liberatore a Maiella</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Despite this confusion, Eleutherius' cult is ancient and widespread, and his name appears not only in ancient Greek calendars, but also in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> (under April 18, September 5, November 24, where it states “in civitate Riatensi”, a reference to <a href="/wiki/Rieti" title="Rieti">Rieti</a>, where he was also venerated);<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> the Marble Calendar of <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> (under April 18), and in <a href="/wiki/Mozarabic" class="mw-redirect" title="Mozarabic">Mozarabic</a> calendars.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Rieti">Rieti</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eleutherius_and_Antia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Rieti">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The church of Sant'Eleuterio was built outside of the city walls of Rieti around the 5th century. According to tradition, the church claimed the relics of Eleutherius and Antia, carried from Rome by Bishop Primus of Rieti.<sup id="cite_ref-enrosadira_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-enrosadira-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Around the 5th or 6th centuries, the Benedictine <a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_of_Rieti&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Stephen of Rieti (page does not exist)">Stephen of Rieti</a> founded a monastic community near the tomb of the two martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-rieti_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rieti-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Devotion to Eleutherius increased after a legend associated with the bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Probus_of_Rieti&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Probus of Rieti (page does not exist)">Probus of Rieti</a>: that before his death a vision appeared of the two saints, Eleutherius and <a href="/wiki/Juvenal_of_Narni" title="Juvenal of Narni">Juvenal</a> (<i>Giovenale</i>), to accompany the bishop into heaven.<sup id="cite_ref-rieti_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rieti-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The church of Sant'Eleuterio acquired importance during the age of the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a>, with its foundation confirmed with solemn honors by <a href="/wiki/Liutprand,_King_of_the_Lombards" title="Liutprand, King of the Lombards">Liutprand</a>. It acquired greater splendor after 1000 AD, when Peter, a local abbot, restored the church and its monastery, which lay near a stream and the city cemetery. In 1122, Count Grimald granted Sant'Eleuterio, the monastery, and its lands to the Cathedral of Santa Maria of Rieti. On August 13, 1198, Bishop Adolphus Secenari and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> the relics of the two saints to the cathedral, elevating the church to the status of a <a href="/wiki/Collegiate_church" title="Collegiate church">collegiate church</a> with twelve <a href="/wiki/Canon_priests" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon priests">canon priests</a> and an abbot-rector.<sup id="cite_ref-enrosadira_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-enrosadira-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_places_in_Italy">Other places in Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eleutherius_and_Antia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Other places in Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Numerous churches rose in Italy in honor of this saint.  There was a church dedicated to him at Rome, on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Labicana" title="Via Labicana">Via Labicana</a>. He was venerated on April 18 at <a href="/wiki/Nepi" title="Nepi">Nepi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vasto" title="Vasto">Vasto</a>, and at <a href="/wiki/Pore%C4%8D" title="Poreč">Poreč</a> (Parenzo) in <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a>.  At <a href="/wiki/Chieti" title="Chieti">Chieti</a>, <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a>, <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Sulmona" title="Sulmona">Sulmona</a>, he was venerated on May 21; at <a href="/wiki/Terracina" title="Terracina">Terracina</a> on May 13; on May 23 at <a href="/wiki/Arce,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Arce, Italy">Arce</a>, and on 31 December at <a href="/wiki/Canne,_Apulia" class="mw-redirect" title="Canne, Apulia">Canne</a>, where he described as a bishop of that town and a son of Evantia (a corruption of <i>Antia</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  There were other churches dedicated to him at <a href="/wiki/Mugnano_del_Cardinale" title="Mugnano del Cardinale">Mugnano del Cardinale</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ariano_Irpino" title="Ariano Irpino">Ariano Irpino</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The monastery of <a href="/wiki/San_Liberatore_a_Maiella" title="San Liberatore a Maiella">San Liberatore a Maiella</a> is dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
