<p><b>Catherine of Sweden</b>, <b>Katarina av Vadstena</b>, <b>Catherine of Vadstena</b> or <b>Katarina Ulfsdotter</b> (c. 1332&#160;– 24 March 1381) was a Swedish noblewoman. She is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church. Her father was Ulf Gudmarsson, Lord of Ulvåsa, and her mother was Saint <a href="/wiki/Bridget_of_Sweden" title="Bridget of Sweden">Bridget of Sweden</a> (known as Birgitta Birgersdotter of Finsta in her lifetime).<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Vadstena&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the age of twelve or thirteen she married Lord Eggert van Kyren, a religious young nobleman of German descent, whom she persuaded to take a vow of absolute chastity, and both lived in a state of virginity.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Catherine accompanied her mother to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in 1349 and soon upon arrival heard news of her husband's death.
</p><p>She stayed on with her mother, accompanied her on several journeys, including one to the Holy Land. At the death of Bridget, Catherine returned to Sweden with her mother's body, which was buried at the monastery of Vadstena.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Catherine became head of the <a href="/wiki/Brigittines" class="mw-redirect" title="Brigittines">Brigittine convent</a> at <a href="/wiki/Vadstena_Abbey" title="Vadstena Abbey">Vadstena Abbey</a>, founded by her mother.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Catherine took on the task of forming the community in the rule her mother had written and directing the Order of the Holy Savior, or <a href="/wiki/Bridgettines" title="Bridgettines">Bridgettines</a>. Later, she returned to Rome to work for her mother's canonization. She stayed there five years and formed a close friendship with <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was spurred by a vision to visit the Holy Land in 1372, and she died soon after her return to Rome.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Vadstena&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1484, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_VIII" title="Pope Innocent VIII">Pope Innocent VIII</a> gave permission for Catherine's veneration as a saint and her feast was assigned to 22 March in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>. Catherine is generally represented with a hind (female <a href="/wiki/Red_deer" title="Red deer">red deer</a>) at her side, which is said to have come to her aid "when unchaste youths sought to ensnare her".<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1488, Pope <a href="/wiki/Innocent_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent VIII">Innocent VIII</a> gave permission for the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation</a> of her relics in <a href="/wiki/Vadstena" title="Vadstena">Vadstena</a>. The formal <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> process, which also documented the required miracles,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> was never completed because of the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NF_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NF-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Sweden" title="Church of Sweden">Church of Sweden</a> the feast of St. Catherine (Katarinamäss) is celebrated on 2 August.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
