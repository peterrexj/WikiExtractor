<p><b>Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna of Russia</b> (<b>Olga Nikolaevna Romanova</b>; Russian&#58; <span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Великая Княжна Ольга Николаевна</span></span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">tr.</a></small> <span title="Russian-language text"><i lang="ru-Latn">Velikaya Knyazhna Ol'ga Nikolaevna</i></span>, <small>IPA:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="ru-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian" title="Help:IPA/Russian">[vʲɪˈlʲikəjə knʲɪˈʐna ˈolʲɡə nʲɪkɐˈla(j)ɪvnə]</a></span>&#32;<span class="nowrap" style="font-size:85%">(<span class="unicode haudio"><span class="fn"><span style="white-space:nowrap;margin-right:.25em;"><a href="/wiki/File:RU-Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia.ogg" title="About this sound"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/11px-Loudspeaker.svg.png" decoding="async" width="11" height="11" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/17px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/22px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20" /></a></span><a href="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/ba/RU-Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia.ogg" class="internal" title="RU-Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna of Russia.ogg">listen</a></span></span>)</span>; 15 November&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 3 November&#93;&#160;1895 – 17 July 1918) was the eldest child of the last <a href="/wiki/Tsar" title="Tsar">Tsar</a> of the Russian Empire, <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Nicholas_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Nicholas II">Emperor Nicholas II</a>, and of <a href="/wiki/Empress_Alexandra_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Empress Alexandra of Russia">Empress Alexandra of Russia</a>.
</p><p>During her lifetime, Olga's future marriage was the subject of great speculation within Russia. Matches were rumored with <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Dmitri_Pavlovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia">Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania" title="Carol II of Romania">Crown Prince Carol of Romania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Edward_VIII" title="Edward VIII">Edward, Prince of Wales</a>, eldest son of Britain's <a href="/wiki/George_V" title="George V">George V</a>, and with <a href="/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Yugoslavia" title="Alexander I of Yugoslavia">Crown Prince Alexander of Serbia</a>. Olga herself wanted to marry a Russian and remain in her home country. During <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, she nursed wounded soldiers in a <a href="/wiki/Military_hospital" title="Military hospital">military hospital</a> until her own nerves gave out and, thereafter, oversaw administrative duties at the hospital.
</p><p>Olga's <a href="/wiki/Execution_of_the_Romanov_family" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution of the Romanov family">murder</a> following the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Revolution of 1917">Russian Revolution of 1917</a> resulted in her <a href="/wiki/Canonization_of_the_Romanovs" title="Canonization of the Romanovs">canonization</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Passion_bearer" title="Passion bearer">passion bearer</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>. In the 1990s, her remains were identified through <a href="/wiki/DNA_testing" class="mw-redirect" title="DNA testing">DNA testing</a> and were buried in a funeral ceremony at <a href="/wiki/Saints_Peter_and_Paul_Cathedral,_Saint_Petersburg" title="Saints Peter and Paul Cathedral, Saint Petersburg">Peter and Paul Cathedral</a> in St. Petersburg along with those of her parents and two of her sisters.
</p>

<p>Olga had chestnut-blonde hair, bright blue eyes, a broad face, and an upturned nose. When she was 10, her tutor <a href="/wiki/Pierre_Gilliard" title="Pierre Gilliard">Pierre Gilliard</a> reflected that she was "very fair" with "sparkling, mischievous eyes and a slightly retrousee nose."<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Baroness <a href="/wiki/Sophie_Buxhoeveden" title="Sophie Buxhoeveden">Sophie Buxhoeveden</a>, her mother's lady-in-waiting, reflected that "[she] was fair and tall, with smiling blue eyes, a somewhat short nose, which she called 'my humble snub,' and lovely teeth."<sup id="cite_ref-alexanderpalace.org_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alexanderpalace.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was considered less pretty than her sisters, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Tatiana_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Tatiana Nikolaevna of Russia">Tatiana</a> and <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Maria_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna of Russia">Maria</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> though her appearance improved as she grew older. "As a child she was plain, at fifteen she was beautiful", wrote her mother's friend <a href="/wiki/Lili_Dehn" title="Lili Dehn">Lili Dehn</a>. "She was slightly above the medium height, with a fresh complexion, deep blue eyes, quantities of light chestnut hair, and pretty hands and feet."<sup id="cite_ref-Dehn_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dehn-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In her memoirs, <a href="/wiki/Meriel_Buchanan" title="Meriel Buchanan">Meriel Buchanan</a> described the physical appearance of the 17-year-old Olga at an imperial ball in 1912: "She had not the regular features, the almost mystical beauty of her sister, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Tatiana_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Tatiana Nikolaevna of Russia">Tatiana Nikolaevna</a>, but with her rather tip-tilted nose, her wide laughing mouth, her sparkling blue eyes, she had a charm, a freshness, an enchanting exuberance that made her irresistible."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga was compassionate and sought to help others. As a child, she saw a little girl crying in the road. She threw her doll out of her carriage, saying, "Don't cry, little girl, here's a doll for you."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Baroness Sophie Buxhoeveden remembered that "she was generous, and an appeal to her met with immediate response. 'Oh, one must help poor so-and-so. I must do it somehow,' she would say."<sup id="cite_ref-alexanderpalace.org_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alexanderpalace.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When she was 20, she took control of a portion of her sizable fortune and began to respond independently to requests for charity. One day when she was out for a drive she saw a young child using crutches. She asked about the child and learned that the youngster's parents were too poor to afford treatment. She set aside an allowance to cover the child's medical bills.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A court official, Alexander Mossolov, recalled that Olga's character was "even, good, with an almost angelic kindness" by the time she was a young woman.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga was famous for her quick temper and moodiness. As a small child, she told a portrait painter, "You are a very ugly man and I don't like you one bit!"<sup id="cite_ref-Eagar_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eagar-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Her mother's friend <a href="/wiki/Anna_Vyrubova" title="Anna Vyrubova">Anna Vyrubova</a> wrote that Olga's "chief characteristics&#160;… were a strong will and a singularly straightforward habit of thought and action... Admirable qualities in a woman, these same characteristics are often trying in childhood, and Olga as a little girl sometimes showed herself willful and even disobedient."<sup id="cite_ref-Vyrubova_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vyrubova-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> On 11 January 1909, Alexandra admonished the 13-year-old Olga for rudeness and bad behavior. She told the teenager that she must be polite to the servants, who looked after her well and did their best for her, and she should not make her nurse "nervous" when she was tired and not feeling well.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Olga responded on 12 January 1909 that she would try to do better but it wasn't easy because her nurse became angry and cross with her for no good reason.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> However, <a href="/wiki/Elizaveta_Ersberg" title="Elizaveta Ersberg">Elizaveta Ersberg</a>, one of the maids, told her niece that the servants sometimes had good reason to be cross with Olga because the eldest grand duchess could be spoiled, capricious, and lazy.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> On 24 January 1909, Alexandra scolded the active teenager, who once signed another of her letters with the nickname "Unmounted Cossack", again: "You are growing very big — don't be so wild and kick about and show your legs, it is not pretty. I never did so when your age or when I was smaller and younger even."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> According to Anna, Olga was 'extremely pretty, with brilliant blue eyes and a lovely complexion, Olga resembled her father in the fineness of her features, especially in her delicate, slightly tipped nose.'<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> and measured 165 centimetres tall.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga's governess and tutors noted that she had some autocratic impulses. On a visit to a museum where state carriages were on display, Olga ordered one of the servants to prepare the largest and most beautiful carriage for her daily drive. Her wishes were not honored, much to the relief of her governess, <a href="/wiki/Margaretta_Eagar" title="Margaretta Eagar">Margaretta Eagar</a>.
</p><p>Olga was highly intelligent and enjoyed studying. "The eldest, Olga Nicolaevna, possessed a remarkably quick brain," recalled her Swiss tutor, <a href="/wiki/Pierre_Gilliard" title="Pierre Gilliard">Pierre Gilliard</a>. "She had good reasoning powers as well as initiative, a very independent manner, and a gift for swift and entertaining repartee."<sup id="cite_ref-Gilliard_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gilliard-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> She enjoyed reading about politics and read newspapers. She reportedly enjoyed choosing from her mother's book selection. When she was caught taking a book before her mother read it, Olga would jokingly tell her mother that Alexandra must wait to read the novel until Olga had determined whether it was an appropriate book for her to read.<sup id="cite_ref-Massie-1967_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Massie-1967-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga was musically gifted. Her teachers said that she had "an absolutely correct ear."<sup id="cite_ref-alexanderpalace.org_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alexanderpalace.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Baroness <a href="/wiki/Sophie_Buxhoeveden" title="Sophie Buxhoeveden">Sophie Buxhoeveden</a> reflected that, "She could play by ear anything she had heard, and could transpose' complicated pieces of music, play the most difficult accompaniments at sight, and her touch on the piano was delightful. She sang prettily in a mezzosoprano. She was lazy at practising, but when the spirit moved her she would play by the hour."<sup id="cite_ref-alexanderpalace.org_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alexanderpalace.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga felt the rights of eldest children should be protected. When she was told the Biblical story of <a href="/wiki/Joseph_(Hebrew_Bible)" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseph (Hebrew Bible)">Joseph</a> and his coat of many colors, she sympathized with the eldest brothers rather than Joseph. She also sympathized with Goliath rather than David in the Biblical story of <a href="/wiki/David_and_Goliath" class="mw-redirect" title="David and Goliath">David and Goliath</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Eagar_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eagar-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> When her French tutor, Pierre Gilliard, was teaching her the formation of French verbs and the use of auxiliaries, ten-year-old Olga responded, "I see, monsieur. The auxiliaries are the servants of the verbs. It's only poor 'avoir' which has to shift for itself."<sup id="cite_ref-Gilliard_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gilliard-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Due to her sheltered life, Olga had little experience with the world. She and her sisters had little understanding of money, because they had not had an opportunity to shop in stores or to see money exchange hands. Young Olga once thought that a hat maker who came to the palace had given her a new hat as a present. Olga was once frightened when she witnessed a policeman arresting someone on the street. She thought the policeman would come to arrest her because she had behaved badly for Miss Eagar. When reading a history lesson, she remarked that she was glad she lived in current times, when people were good and not as evil as they had been in the past.
</p><p>Olga was fascinated with heaven and the afterlife. In November 1903, the 8-year-old Olga learned about death when her first cousin, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Elisabeth_of_Hesse_and_by_Rhine_(1895%E2%80%931903)" title="Princess Elisabeth of Hesse and by Rhine (1895–1903)">Princess Elisabeth of Hesse and by Rhine</a>, died of <a href="/wiki/Typhoid_fever" title="Typhoid fever">typhoid fever</a> while on a visit to the Romanovs at their Polish estate. "My children talked much of cousin Ella and how God had taken her spirit, and they understood that later God would take her body also to heaven," wrote Eagar. "On Christmas morning when Olga awoke, she exclaimed at once, 'Did God send for cousin Ella's body in the night?' I felt startled at such a question on Christmas morning, but answered, 'Oh, no, dear, not yet.' She was greatly disappointed, and said, 'I thought He would have sent for her to keep Christmas with Him.'"<sup id="cite_ref-Eagar_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eagar-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Olga was born on 15 November 1895. She was the oldest child and daughter of Emperor Nicholas II and Empress Alexandra. The birth was difficult, and Alexandra was in labour for 13 hours. Ultimately, Dr. Ott used forceps to deliver Olga.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> She weighed 4.5&#160;kg at birth, and she was so robust that Nicholas said that she didn't look like a newborn.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Her parents were overjoyed and insisted that they were not disappointed that their first child was not a boy. When he was congratulated by the court chamberlain, Nicholas declared, "I am glad that our child is a girl. Had it been a boy he would have belonged to the people, being a girl she belongs to us."<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._33_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._33-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Alexandra agreed: "For us there is no question of sex. Our child is simply a gift from God."<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._33_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._33-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1896, Nicholas and Alexandra brought Olga on a visit to Scotland, France, and Darmstadt. In Balmoral, <a href="/wiki/Victoria,_Queen_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Victoria, Queen of the United Kingdom">Victoria, Queen of the United Kingdom</a>, Alexandra's grandmother and Olga's great-grandmother, met Olga for the first time. Queen Victoria approved of Olga and declared "the baby is magnificent."<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> In France, Olga was very popular. Nicholas told his mother that "our daughter made a great impression everywhere."<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._41_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._41-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Olga was greeted by shouts of "Vive la bebe!"<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._41_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._41-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga and her siblings were raised as simply as possible. They slept on hard camp cots unless they were ill, and they took cold baths every morning.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Servants called Olga and her siblings by their first names and <a href="/wiki/Patronymic" title="Patronymic">patronyms</a> rather than by their imperial titles.<sup id="cite_ref-Massie_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Massie-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga had four siblings: <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Tatiana_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Tatiana Nikolaevna of Russia">Grand Duchesses Tatiana</a>, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Maria_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna of Russia">Maria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Anastasia_Nikolaevna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna of Russia">Anastasia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Tsarevich_Alexei_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Tsarevich Alexei of Russia">Tsarevich Alexei of Russia</a>. Her great-grandmother <a href="/wiki/Queen_Victoria" title="Queen Victoria">Queen Victoria</a> was one of her godmothers.
</p><p>Olga's Russian title (<i>Velikaya Knyazhna</i> Великая Княжна) is most precisely translated as "Grand Princess," meaning that Olga, as an "imperial highness," was higher in rank than other princesses in Europe who were "royal highnesses". However, "Grand Duchess" is the usual English translation.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Olga's friends and family generally called her simply Olga Nikolaevna or nicknamed her "Olishka," "Olenka," or "Olya."
</p><p>Olga was most often paired with her sister Tatiana. The two girls shared a room, dressed alike, and were known as "The Big Pair."<sup id="cite_ref-Massie_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Massie-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1900, before her brother <a href="/wiki/Alexei_Nikolaevich,_Tsarevich_of_Russia" title="Alexei Nikolaevich, Tsarevich of Russia">Alexei</a> was born, her father fell seriously ill. During this period, the empress attempted to persuade her husband to change the succession laws in Russia to allow females to inherit the throne in the absence of any male heirs in order for Olga to inherit the empire, as opposed to her uncle, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Michael_Alexandrovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Michael Alexandrovich of Russia">Grand Duke Michael Alexandrovich</a>. Ultimately, these changes did not take place.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relationships_with_family">Relationships with family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Relationships with family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Olga idolized her father and wore a necklace with an icon of St. Nicholas on her chest.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> She, like her siblings, enjoyed games of tennis and swimming with her father during their summer holidays and often confided in him when she went with him on long walks.<sup id="cite_ref-Massie-1967_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Massie-1967-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although she loved Alexandra, Olga had a strained relationship with her mother. "Olga is always most unamiable about every proposition, though may end by doing what I wish", wrote Alexandra to Nicholas on 13 March 1916. "And when I am severe – sulks me."<sup id="cite_ref-Bokhanov124_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bokhanov124-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> In another letter to Nicholas during World War I, Alexandra complained that Olga's grumpiness, bad humor and general reluctance to make an official visit to the hospital where she usually worked as a Red Cross nurse made things difficult.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Parlormaid <a href="/wiki/Elizaveta_Nikolaevna_Ersberg" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizaveta Nikolaevna Ersberg">Elizaveta Nikolaevna Ersberg</a> told her niece that Nicholas II paid closer attention to the children than Alexandra did and Alexandra often was ill with a migraine or quarreled with the servants.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> In 1913, Olga complained in a letter to her grandmother, <a href="/wiki/Maria_Feodorovna_(Dagmar_of_Denmark)" title="Maria Feodorovna (Dagmar of Denmark)">Dowager Empress Maria Feodorovna</a> about her mother's invalidism. "As usual her heart isn't well", Olga wrote. "It's all so unpleasant."<sup id="cite_ref-king_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-king-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Marie_of_Edinburgh" class="mw-redirect" title="Marie of Edinburgh">Queen Marie of Romania</a>, who met Olga and her sisters when they visited Romania on a state trip in 1914, commented in her memoirs that the girls were natural and confided in her when Alexandra wasn't present, but when she appeared "they always seemed to be watching her every expression so as to be sure to act according to her desires."<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaAnastasia.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/OlgaAnastasia.jpg/180px-OlgaAnastasia.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="180" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/OlgaAnastasia.jpg/270px-OlgaAnastasia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/OlgaAnastasia.jpg/360px-OlgaAnastasia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1152" data-file-height="1152" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaAnastasia.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchess Olga reads a book as her sister Grand Duchess Anastasia looks on. Courtesy: Beinecke Library</div></div></div></div>
<p>Alexandra frequently reminded Olga to be a good example for her younger siblings and make sure that they behaved. In many letters, Alexandra told Olga "remember above all to always be a good example to the little ones"<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._119_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._119-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> and warned her that "you must have a positive influence over them."<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._119_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._119-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> Olga struggled to keep her siblings in line. Alexandra blamed sixteen-year-old Olga, who was sitting beside her seven-year-old brother, for failing to control the misbehaving Tsarevich Alexei during a family dinner. The spoiled Alexei teased others at the table, refused to sit up in his chair, wouldn't eat his food and licked his plate. The Tsarina's expectation was unreasonable, said <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Konstantin_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia">Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, a distant cousin of the imperial family. "Olga cannot deal with him", he wrote in his diary on 18 March 1912.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Court official A. A. Mossolov wrote that Olga was already seventeen, but still "she had the ways of a <a href="/wiki/Flapper" title="Flapper">flapper</a>", referring to her rough manners and liking for exuberant play.<sup id="cite_ref-Mossolov_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mossolov-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga was especially close with her paternal aunt and namesake, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Olga_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna</a>. Later, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Olga_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna</a> reflected that "[Olga] resembled me in character, and that was perhaps why we understood each other so well."<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga was close to her younger brother, Alexei. Alexei adored Olga and considered her his favorite sister. Whenever his parents reprimanded him, Alexei would "declare that he was Olga's boy, pick up his toys, and go to her apartment."<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relationship_with_Grigori_Rasputin">Relationship with Grigori Rasputin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Relationship with Grigori Rasputin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Despite this occasional misbehavior, Olga, like all her family, doted on the long-awaited heir Tsarevich Alexei, or "Baby". The little boy suffered frequent attacks of <a href="/wiki/Hemophilia" class="mw-redirect" title="Hemophilia">hemophilia</a> and nearly died several times. Like their mother, Olga and her three sisters were also potentially carriers of the hemophilia gene. Olga's younger sister Maria reportedly hemorrhaged in December 1914 during an operation to remove her tonsils, according to her paternal aunt <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Olga_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia</a>, who was interviewed later in her life. The doctor performing the operation was so unnerved that he had to be ordered to continue by Tsarina Alexandra. Olga Alexandrovna said she believed all four of her nieces bled more than was normal and believed they were carriers of the <a href="/wiki/Hemophilia" class="mw-redirect" title="Hemophilia">hemophilia</a> gene like their mother, who inherited the trait from her maternal grandmother <a href="/wiki/Queen_Victoria" title="Queen Victoria">Queen Victoria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Symptomatic carriers of the gene, while not haemophiliacs themselves, can have symptoms of hemophilia including a lower than normal blood clotting factor that can lead to heavy bleeding.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaAlexei.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/OlgaAlexei.jpg/220px-OlgaAlexei.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="215" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/OlgaAlexei.jpg/330px-OlgaAlexei.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/OlgaAlexei.jpg/440px-OlgaAlexei.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1496" data-file-height="1464" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaAlexei.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchess Olga with her brother, Tsarevich Alexei, ca. 1910.</div></div></div></div>
<p>Olga's mother relied on the counsel of <a href="/wiki/Grigori_Rasputin" title="Grigori Rasputin">Grigori Rasputin</a>, a Russian peasant and wandering <i><a href="/wiki/Starets" title="Starets">starets</a></i> or "holy man", and credited his prayers with saving the ailing Tsarevich on numerous occasions. Olga and her siblings were also taught to view Rasputin as "Our Friend" and to share confidences with him. In the autumn of 1907, Olga's aunt <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Olga_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia</a> was escorted to the nursery by the Tsar to meet Rasputin. Olga, her sisters and brother, were all wearing their long white nightgowns. "All the children seemed to like him", Olga Alexandrovna recalled. "They were completely at ease with him."<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, one of the girls' governesses, Sofia Ivanovna Tyutcheva, was horrified in 1910 that Rasputin was permitted access to the nursery when the four girls were in their nightgowns and wanted him barred. Although Rasputin's contacts with the children were completely innocent, Nicholas asked Rasputin to avoid going to the nurseries in the future to avoid further scandal. Alexandra eventually had the governess fired. Tyutcheva took her story to other members of the family.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> Nicholas's sister <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Xenia_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Xenia Alexandrovna of Russia</a> was horrified by Tyutcheva's story. She wrote on 15 March 1910 that she could not understand:
</p>
<dl><dd>"…the attitude of Alix and the children to that sinister Grigory (whom they consider to be almost a saint, when in fact he's only a <a href="/wiki/Khlysts" title="Khlysts"><i>khlyst</i></a>!) He's always there, goes into the nursery, visits Olga and Tatiana while they are getting ready for bed, sits there talking to them and <i>caressing</i> them. They are careful to hide him from Sofia Ivanovna, and the children don't dare talk to her about him. It's all quite unbelievable and beyond understanding."<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Maria Ivanovna Vishnyakova, another nurse for the royal children, was at first a devotee of Rasputin, but later was disillusioned by him. She claimed that she was raped by Rasputin in the spring of 1910.<sup id="cite_ref-Radzinsky_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Radzinsky-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> The Empress refused to believe her and said that everything Rasputin did was holy. Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna was told that Vishnyakova's claim had been immediately investigated, but "they caught the young woman in bed with a Cossack of the Imperial Guard." Vishnyakova was dismissed from her post in 1913.<sup id="cite_ref-Radzinsky_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Radzinsky-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was whispered in society that Rasputin had seduced not only the Tsarina but also the four Grand Duchesses.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Rasputin had released ardent, though by all accounts completely innocent in nature, letters written by the Tsarina and the four grand duchesses to him. They circulated throughout society, fueling rumors. Pornographic cartoons circulated depicting Rasputin having relations with the empress, with her four daughters and Anna Vyrubova nude in the background.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Nicholas ordered Rasputin to leave St. Petersburg for a time, much to Alexandra's displeasure, and Rasputin went on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> Despite the rumors, the imperial family's association with Rasputin continued until Rasputin was murdered on 17 December 1916. "Our Friend is so contented with our girlies, says they have gone through heavy 'courses' for their age and their souls have much developed", Alexandra wrote to Nicholas on 6 December 1916, a few weeks before Rasputin was killed.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> However, as she grew older, Olga was less inclined to see Rasputin as her friend and was more aware of how his friendship with her parents affected the stability of her country. Olga wrote in her diary the day after the murder that she suspected <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Dmitri_Pavlovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia">Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia</a>, her first cousin once removed and the man she had at one time been expected to marry, was the murderer of "Father Grigory."<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> Dmitri and <a href="/wiki/Felix_Yussupov" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix Yussupov">Felix Yussupov</a>, the husband of her first cousin <a href="/wiki/Princess_Irina_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Irina of Russia">Princess Irina of Russia</a>, were among the murderers. In his memoirs, A. A. Mordvinov reported that the four grand duchesses appeared "cold and visibly terribly upset" by Rasputin's death and sat "huddled up closely together" on a sofa in one of their bedrooms on the night they received the news. Mordvinov reported that the young women were in a gloomy mood and seemed to sense the political upheaval that was about to be unleashed.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> Rasputin was buried with an icon signed on the reverse side by Olga, her sisters and mother. However, Olga was the only member of the family who did not attend Rasputin's funeral, according to the diary of her first cousin once removed <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Andrei_Vladimirovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Andrei Vladimirovich of Russia">Grand Duke Andrei Vladimirovich of Russia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> Olga's own diary however shows that she did attend his funeral. She wrote on 21 December (Old Style) 1916 "At 9 o' clock, we 4, Papa and Mama went to the place where Ania's construction is for the service of Litia and burial of Father Grigory."<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> According to the memoirs of <a href="/wiki/Valentina_Ivanovna_Chebotareva" class="mw-redirect" title="Valentina Ivanovna Chebotareva">Valentina Ivanovna Chebotareva</a>, a woman who nursed with Olga during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, Olga said in February 1917, about a month after the murder, that while it might have been necessary for Rasputin to be killed, it should never have been done "so terribly." She was ashamed that the murderers were her relatives.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> After Olga and her sisters had been killed, the Bolsheviks found that each was wearing an amulet bearing Rasputin's image and a prayer around their necks.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_prospects">Marriage prospects</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Marriage prospects">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Prince_John_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" title="Prince John Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince John Konstantinovich of Russia</a> fell in love with Olga. When he was 16, he attended Alexei's christening in 1904 and met the 9-year-old Olga. He reflected that “I was so enraptured by her I can’t even describe it. It was like a wildfire fanned by the wind. Her hair was waving, her eyes were sparkling, well, I can’t even begin to describe it!!”<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup> In 1908, he traveled to the Crimea "only out of hunger to see Olga." He admitted his feelings to Olga's parents, but they rejected him. He told his father, "They won't let me marry Olga Nikolaevna."<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1911, there were rumors that Olga would marry <a href="/wiki/George,_Crown_Prince_of_Serbia" title="George, Crown Prince of Serbia">George, Crown Prince of Serbia</a> or <a href="/wiki/Boris_III_of_Bulgaria" title="Boris III of Bulgaria">Prince Boris of Bulgaria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup> The article claimed that Nicholas intended to make his four daughters the "Queens of the Balkans" to keep the Balkan states faithful to Russia. After the coronation of <a href="/wiki/King_George_V" class="mw-redirect" title="King George V">King George V</a>, there was speculation that either Olga or Tatiana would marry <a href="/wiki/Edward_VIII" title="Edward VIII">Prince Edward of Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There were rumors that Olga and her first cousin once removed <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Dmitri_Pavlovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia">Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia</a> were romantically involved. As an orphan, Dmitri was very close to Olga's parents, which provoked more speculation that he would marry Olga. Arthur Cherep-Spiridovich wrote, "Such was the emperor's affection for him that all the entourage already saw in him the future fiancé of one of the grand duchesses.<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._172_58-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._172-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup> Alexandra Bogdanova, the wife of a general and hostess of a monarchist salon, wrote in her diary on 7 June 1912 that Olga had been betrothed the previous night to Grand Duke.<sup id="cite_ref-Helen_Rappaport_p._172_58-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Helen_Rappaport_p._172-58">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup> The Washington Post reported that Olga had refused <a href="/wiki/Prince_Adalbert_of_Prussia_(1884%E2%80%931948)" title="Prince Adalbert of Prussia (1884–1948)">Prince Adalbert</a> because "she had given her heart to her cousin Grand Duke Dmitri Paulovitch."<sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup> In August 1912, <a href="/wiki/Meriel_Buchanan" title="Meriel Buchanan">Meriel Buchanan</a>, the British ambassador's daughter, wrote in her diary, "I heard a rumour yesterday that a certain person is going to marry the Emperor's eldest daughter. I can't quite believe it considering all the high and mighty people who are panting to marry her. Of course she may have a coup de foudre for him and insist on having her own way."<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup> In his book <i>The Rasputin File</i>, Edvard Radzinsky speculates that the betrothal was broken off due to Dmitri's dislike for <a href="/wiki/Grigori_Rasputin" title="Grigori Rasputin">Grigori Rasputin</a>, his association with <a href="/wiki/Felix_Yussupov" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix Yussupov">Felix Yussupov</a> and rumors that Dmitri was bisexual.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, there was some discussion of a marriage between Olga and <a href="/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania" title="Carol II of Romania">Prince Carol of Romania</a>. In 1914, Foreign Minister <a href="/wiki/Sergey_Sazonov" title="Sergey Sazonov">Sergey Sazonov</a> advocated the match because he wanted to ensure that the Romanian royal family would support Russia in case of a future conflict.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup> Nicholas and Alexandra saw the benefits of the match, but they insisted that "the grand duchess's marriage&#160;… should take place only as the result of a much closer acquaintance between the young people and on the absolute condition of their daughter's voluntary agreement to it."<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup> In March 1914, <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Romania" title="Ferdinand I of Romania">Crown Prince Ferdinand of Romania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Queen_Marie_of_Romania" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Marie of Romania">Crown Princess Marie of Romania</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Carol_II_of_Romania" title="Carol II of Romania">Prince Carol of Romania</a> visited the Romanovs in St. Petersburg. Despite going on walks and dinners with each other, Olga and Carol seemed uninterested in each other. During a visit to Romania in the spring of 1914, Olga and Carol were uninterested in each other and did not speak to each other.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Queen_Marie_of_Romania" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Marie of Romania">Crown Princess Marie of Romania</a> noted that Carol was "not enamored of Olga's broad, plain face and brusque manner."<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup> She judged that Olga's face "was too broad, her cheekbones too high,"<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup> and she told her mother that all of the grand duchesses "were not found very pretty."<sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Marthe_Bibesco" title="Marthe Bibesco">Marthe Bibesco</a>, who was with the Romanian party, heard a rumor that the grand duchesses had "decided... to make themselves as ugly as they could.... so that Carol should not fall in love with any of them."<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Olga told Gilliard that she wanted to marry a Russian and remain in her own country. She said her parents would not force her to marry anyone she could not like.<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">&#91;69&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1913, <a href="/wiki/Duchess_Marie_of_Mecklenburg-Schwerin" title="Duchess Marie of Mecklenburg-Schwerin">Duchess Marie of Mecklenburg-Schwerin</a> asked Alexandra about a potential marriage between the 18-year-old Olga and her own 38-year-old son, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Boris_Vladimirovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Boris Vladimirovich of Russia">Grand Duke Boris Vladimirovich of Russia</a>. Alexandra was horrified, because "the idea of Boris is too unsympathetic." She refused to allow "a pure, fresh girl" to marry "a well-used, half worn out, blasé young man" and "live in a house in which many a woman has shared his life."<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">&#91;70&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Romances">Romances</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Romances">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg/220px-OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="185" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg/330px-OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg/440px-OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg 2x" data-file-width="646" data-file-height="543" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaTatianaMariaofficer.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchesses Olga, left, Tatiana, right, and Maria Nikolaevna, center, ca. 1911, with Pavel Voronov, the officer Olga fell in love with in 1913.</div></div></div></div>
<p>Olga and her younger sisters were surrounded by young men assigned to guard them at the palace and on the imperial yacht <i>Standart</i> and were used to mingling with them and sharing holiday fun during their annual summer cruises. When Olga was fifteen, a group of officers aboard the imperial yacht gave her a portrait of <a href="/wiki/Michelangelo" title="Michelangelo">Michelangelo</a>'s nude <a href="/wiki/David_(Michelangelo)" title="David (Michelangelo)">David</a>, cut out from a newspaper, as a present for her <a href="/wiki/Name_day" title="Name day">name day</a> on 11 July 1911. "Olga laughed at it long and hard", her indignant fourteen-year-old sister Tatiana wrote to her aunt <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Olga_Alexandrovna_of_Russia" title="Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia">Grand Duchess Olga Alexandrovna of Russia</a>. "And not one of the officers wishes to confess that he has done it. Such swine, aren't they?"<sup id="cite_ref-71" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-71">&#91;71&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaVoronov.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/OlgaVoronov.jpg/220px-OlgaVoronov.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="169" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/OlgaVoronov.jpg/330px-OlgaVoronov.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/OlgaVoronov.jpg/440px-OlgaVoronov.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1488" data-file-height="1144" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaVoronov.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna and Pavel Voronov, ca. 1912. Olga fell in love with Voronov and was distressed when he married, though she wished him well. Courtesy: Beinecke Library.</div></div></div></div>
<p>In November 1911 a full dress ball was held at <a href="/wiki/Livadia_Palace" title="Livadia Palace">Livadia</a> to celebrate her sixteenth birthday and her entry into society. Her hair was put up for the first time and her first ballgown was pink. Her parents gave her a diamond ring and a diamond and pearl necklace as a birthday present and symbol that she had become a young woman.<sup id="cite_ref-72" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-72">&#91;72&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg/220px-OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="178" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg/330px-OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg/440px-OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2848" data-file-height="2304" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OlgaAlexandraAnastasia1916.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna in her nursing uniform, Tsarina Alexandra, and Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna in the Tsarina's sitting room in about 1916. Courtesy: Beinecke Library.</div></div></div></div>
<p>While society was discussing matches with the princess, Olga fell in love with a succession of officers. In late 1913, Olga fell in love with Pavel Voronov, a junior officer on the imperial yacht <i>Standart</i>, but such a relationship would have been impossible due to their differing ranks. Voronov was engaged a few months later to one of the ladies in waiting. "God grant him good fortune, my beloved", a saddened Olga wrote on his wedding day, "It's sad, distressing."<sup id="cite_ref-73" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-73">&#91;73&#93;</a></sup> Later, in her diaries of 1915 and 1916, Olga frequently mentioned a man named Mitya with great affection.<sup id="cite_ref-Bokhanov124_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bokhanov124-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the diary of Valentina Chebotareva, a woman who nursed with Olga during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, Olga's "golden Mitya" was Dmitri Chakh-Bagov, a wounded soldier she cared for when she was a <a href="/wiki/Red_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Red Cross">Red Cross</a> nurse. Chebotareva wrote that Olga's love for him was "pure, naive, without hope" and that she tried to avoid revealing her feelings to the other nurses. She talked to him regularly on the telephone, was depressed when he left the hospital, and jumped about exuberantly when she received a message from him. Dmitri Chakh-Bagov adored Olga and talked of killing Rasputin for her if she only gave the word, because it was the duty of an officer to protect the imperial family even against their will. However, he also reportedly showed other officers the letters Olga had written to him when he was drunk. Another young man, Volodia Volkomski, appeared to have affection for her as well. "(He) always has a smile or two for her", wrote Alexandra to Nicholas on 16 December 1916.<sup id="cite_ref-74" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-74">&#91;74&#93;</a></sup> Chebotareva also noted in her diary Olga's stated "dreams of happiness: "To get married, [to] always live in the countryside [in] winter and summer, [to] see only good people [and] no one official."<sup id="cite_ref-75" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-75">&#91;75&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div style="clear:both;"></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_adulthood_and_World_War_I">Early adulthood and World War I</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Early adulthood and World War I">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:OTMA1915-2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/OTMA1915-2.jpg/220px-OTMA1915-2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="280" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/OTMA1915-2.jpg/330px-OTMA1915-2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/OTMA1915-2.jpg/440px-OTMA1915-2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1656" data-file-height="2104" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:OTMA1915-2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchesses Anastasia, Tatiana, Maria, and Olga with <a href="/wiki/Alexandra_Feodorovna_(Alix_of_Hesse)" title="Alexandra Feodorovna (Alix of Hesse)">Empress Alexandra</a> on an official visit to Sevastopol in 1916. Courtesy: Beinecke Library.</div></div></div></div>
<p>Olga experienced her first brush with violence at age fifteen, when she witnessed the assassination of the government minister <a href="/wiki/Pyotr_Stolypin" title="Pyotr Stolypin">Pyotr Stolypin</a> during a performance at the <a href="/wiki/National_Opera_of_Ukraine" title="National Opera of Ukraine">Kiev Opera House</a>. "Olga and Tatiana had followed me back to the box and saw everything that happened", Tsar Nicholas II wrote to his mother, <a href="/wiki/Maria_Feodorovna_(Dagmar_of_Denmark)" title="Maria Feodorovna (Dagmar of Denmark)">Dowager Empress Maria</a>, on 10 September 1911. "…It had made a great impression on Tatiana, who cried a lot, and they both slept badly."<sup id="cite_ref-76" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-76">&#91;76&#93;</a></sup> Three years later, she saw gunshot wounds close up when she trained to become a <a href="/wiki/Red_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Red Cross">Red Cross</a> nurse. Olga, her sister Tatiana, and her mother Tsarina Alexandra treated wounded soldiers at a hospital on the grounds of <a href="/wiki/Tsarskoye_Selo" title="Tsarskoye Selo">Tsarskoye Selo</a>.
</p><p>Olga was disdainful of her cousin <a href="/wiki/Princess_Irina_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Irina of Russia">Princess Irina of Russia</a>'s husband <a href="/wiki/Felix_Yussupov" class="mw-redirect" title="Felix Yussupov">Felix Yussupov</a>, the man who eventually murdered Rasputin in December 1916. Yussupov had taken advantage of a law permitting men who were only sons to avoid military service. He was in civilian dress at a time when many of the Romanov men and the wounded soldiers Olga cared for were fighting. "Felix is a 'downright civilian', dressed all in brown, walked to and fro about the room, searching in some bookcases with magazines and virtually doing nothing; an utterly unpleasant impression he makes – a man idling in such times", Olga wrote to her father, Tsar Nicholas, on 5 March 1915 after paying a visit to the Yussupovs.<sup id="cite_ref-77" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-77">&#91;77&#93;</a></sup> She was also strongly patriotic. In July 1915, while discussing the wedding of an acquaintance with fellow nurses, Olga said she understood why the ancestry of the groom's German grandmother was being kept hidden. "Of course he has to conceal it", she burst out. "I quite understand him, she may perhaps be a real bloodthirsty German."<sup id="cite_ref-Tschebotarioff_78-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tschebotarioff-78">&#91;78&#93;</a></sup> Olga's unthinking comments hurt her mother, who had been born in Germany, reported fellow nurse Valentina Ivanovna Chebotareva.<sup id="cite_ref-Tschebotarioff_78-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tschebotarioff-78">&#91;78&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nursing during the war provided Olga and her sister Tatiana with exposure to experiences they had not previously had. The girls enjoyed talking with fellow nurses at the hospital, women they would never have met if not for the war, and knew the names of their children and their family stories.<sup id="cite_ref-Tschebotarioff-60_79-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tschebotarioff-60-79">&#91;79&#93;</a></sup> On one occasion, when a lady in waiting who usually picked up the girls from the hospital was detained and sent a carriage without an attendant, the two girls decided to go shopping in a store when they had a break. They ordered the carriage driver to stop in a shopping district and went into a store where they were not recognized because of their nursing uniforms. However, they discovered that they did not know how to buy anything because they had never used money. The next day they asked Chebotareva how to go about purchasing an item from a store.<sup id="cite_ref-Tschebotarioff-60_79-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tschebotarioff-60-79">&#91;79&#93;</a></sup> Yet other stories tell of a regular salary of nine dollars the girls received each month, and how they used it to purchase such items as perfume and notepaper.<sup id="cite_ref-80" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-80">&#91;80&#93;</a></sup> They had also been shopping with their Aunt, Olga Alexandrovna<sup id="cite_ref-81" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-81">&#91;81&#93;</a></sup> and Olga had visited shops on a trip to Germany with her sister Tatiana.<sup id="cite_ref-82" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-82">&#91;82&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Olga_%C3%A1pol%C3%B3n%C5%91.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Olga_%C3%A1pol%C3%B3n%C5%91.jpg/220px-Olga_%C3%A1pol%C3%B3n%C5%91.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="313" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/Olga_%C3%A1pol%C3%B3n%C5%91.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="318" data-file-height="452" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Olga_%C3%A1pol%C3%B3n%C5%91.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna in her nursing uniform in a formal portrait taken ca. 1915.</div></div></div></div>
<p>Olga cared for and pitied the soldiers she helped to treat. However, the stress of caring for wounded, dying men eventually also took its toll on the sensitive, moody Olga's nerves. Her sister Maria reported in a letter that Olga broke three panes of a window on a "caprice" with her umbrella on 5 September 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-83" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-83">&#91;83&#93;</a></sup> On another occasion, she destroyed items in a cloakroom when she was "in a rage", according to the memoirs of Valentina Chebotareva. On 19 October 1915 she was assigned office work at the hospital because she was no longer able to bear the gore of the operating theater. She was given <a href="/wiki/Arsenic" title="Arsenic">arsenic</a> injections in October 1915, at the time considered a treatment for depression or nervous disorders.<sup id="cite_ref-84" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-84">&#91;84&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sophie_Buxhoeveden" title="Sophie Buxhoeveden">Baroness Sophie Buxhoeveden</a>, one of her mother's ladies in waiting, recalled that Olga had to give up nursing and instead only supervised the hospital wards because she had "overtired herself" and became "nervous and anaemic."<sup id="cite_ref-Buxhoeveden_85-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Buxhoeveden-85">&#91;85&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the accounts of courtiers, Olga knew the financial and political state of the country during the war and revolution. She reportedly also knew how much the Russian people disliked her mother and father. "She was by nature a thinker", remembered <a href="/wiki/Gleb_Botkin" title="Gleb Botkin">Gleb Botkin</a>, the son of the family's physician, <a href="/wiki/Eugene_Botkin" title="Eugene Botkin">Yevgeny Botkin</a>, "and as it later seemed to me, understood the general situation better than any member of her family, including even her parents. At least I had the impression that she had little illusions in regard to what the future held in store for them, and in consequence was often sad and worried."<sup id="cite_ref-king_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-king-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Baroness Sophie Buxhoeveden recalled that "the horror of the Revolution told on her more keenly than on any of the others. She changed completely, and all her bright spirits disappeared."<sup id="cite_ref-alexanderpalace.org_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alexanderpalace.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Captivity_and_death">Captivity and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Captivity and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Execution_of_the_Romanov_family" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution of the Romanov family">Execution of the Romanov family</a></div>
<p>The family were arrested during the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Revolution of 1917">Russian Revolution of 1917</a> and were imprisoned first at their home in Tsarskoye Selo and later at private residences in <a href="/wiki/Tobolsk" title="Tobolsk">Tobolsk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Yekaterinburg" title="Yekaterinburg">Yekaterinburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberia</a>. "Darling, you must know how awful it all is", Olga wrote in a letter to a friend from Tobolsk.<sup id="cite_ref-86" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-86">&#91;86&#93;</a></sup> During the early months of 1917, the children caught measles. Olga contracted <a href="/wiki/Pleurisy" title="Pleurisy">pleurisy</a>, as well.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg/260px-Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="195" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg/390px-Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg/520px-Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Otmaincaptivity1917.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">From left to right: Grand Duchesses Maria, Olga, Anastasia and Tatiana Nikolaevna in captivity at <a href="/wiki/Tsarskoe_Selo" class="mw-redirect" title="Tsarskoe Selo">Tsarskoe Selo</a> in the spring of 1917</div></div></div></div>
<p>Olga tried to draw comfort from her faith and her proximity to her family. To her "beloved mama", with whom she had sometimes had a difficult relationship, she wrote a poem in April 1917, while the family was still imprisoned at Tsarskoye Selo: "You are filled with anguish for the sufferings of others. And no one's grief has ever passed you by. You are relentless, only towards yourself, forever cold and pitiless. But if only you could look upon your own sadness from a distance, just once with a loving soul — Oh, how you would pity yourself, how sadly you would weep."<sup id="cite_ref-Olga_87-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Olga-87">&#91;87&#93;</a></sup> In another letter from Tobolsk, Olga wrote: "Father asks to&#160;… remember that the evil which is now in the world will become yet more powerful, and that it is not evil which conquers evil, but only love …"<sup id="cite_ref-Christopher_88-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christopher-88">&#91;88&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A poem copied into one of her notebooks prays for patience and the ability to forgive her enemies:
</p>
<dl><dd>"Send us, Lord, the patience, in this year of stormy, gloom-filled days, to suffer popular oppression, and the tortures of our hangmen. Give us strength, oh Lord of justice, Our neighbor's evil to forgive, And the Cross so heavy and bloody, with Your humility to meet, In days when enemies rob us, To bear the shame and humiliation, Christ our Savior, help us. Ruler of the world, God of the universe, Bless us with prayer and give our humble soul rest in this unbearable, dreadful hour. At the threshold of the grave, breathe into the lips of Your slaves inhuman strength — to pray meekly for our enemies."<sup id="cite_ref-89" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-89">&#91;89&#93;</a></sup></dd></dl>
<p>Also found with Olga's effects, reflecting her own determination to remain faithful to the father she adored, was <a href="/wiki/Edmond_Rostand" title="Edmond Rostand">Edmond Rostand</a>'s <i>L'Aiglon,</i> the story of <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleon Bonaparte">Napoleon Bonaparte</a>'s son, who remained loyal to his deposed father until the end of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-90" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-90">&#91;90&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There was one report that her father gave Olga a small <a href="/wiki/Revolver" title="Revolver">revolver</a>, which she concealed in a boot while in captivity at Tsarskoe Selo and at Tobolsk. Colonel <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eugene_Kobylynsky&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Eugene Kobylynsky (page does not exist)">Eugene Kobylynsky</a>, their sympathetic jailer, pleaded with the grand duchess to surrender her revolver before she, her sisters, and brother were transferred to Yekaterinburg. Olga reluctantly gave up her gun and was left unarmed.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The family had been briefly separated in April 1918 when the Bolsheviks moved Nicholas, Alexandra, and Maria to Yekaterinburg. Alexei and the three other young women remained behind because Alexei had suffered another attack of hemophilia. The Empress chose Maria to accompany her because "Olga's spirits were too low" and level-headed Tatiana was needed to take care of Alexei.<sup id="cite_ref-91" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-91">&#91;91&#93;</a></sup> In May 1918 the remaining children and servants boarded the ship <i>Rus</i> that ferried them from Tobolsk to Yekaterinburg. Aboard ship, Olga was distressed when she saw one of the guards slip from a ladder and injure his foot. She ran to the man and explained that she had been a nurse during the war and wanted to look at his foot. He refused her offer of treatment. All through the afternoon, Olga fretted over the guard, whom she called "her poor fellow."<sup id="cite_ref-92" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-92">&#91;92&#93;</a></sup> At Tobolsk Olga and her sisters had sewn jewels into their clothing in hopes of hiding them from the Bolsheviks, since Alexandra had written to warn them that upon arrival in Ekaterinburg, she, Nicholas and Maria had been aggressively searched and belongings confiscated.<sup id="cite_ref-93" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-93">&#91;93&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana,_last_photo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana%2C_last_photo.jpg/220px-Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana%2C_last_photo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="259" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana%2C_last_photo.jpg/330px-Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana%2C_last_photo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana%2C_last_photo.jpg/440px-Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana%2C_last_photo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="710" data-file-height="837" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Empress_Alexandra_with_Olga_and_Tatiana,_last_photo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Grand Duchesses Tatiana (left) and Olga (far right) with their mother, Tsarina Alexandra (center), in captivity at Tobolsk in the spring of 1918</div></div></div></div><p>
Pierre Gilliard later recalled his last sight of the imperial children at Yekaterinburg:<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"The sailor Nagorny, who attended to Alexei Nikolaevitch, passed my window carrying the sick boy in his arms, behind him came the Grand Duchesses loaded with valises and small personal belongings. I tried to get out, but was roughly pushed back into the carriage by the sentry. I came back to the window. Tatiana Nikolayevna came last carrying her little dog and struggling to drag a heavy brown valise. It was raining and I saw her feet sink into the mud at every step. Nagorny tried to come to her assistance; he was roughly pushed back by one of the commissars …"<sup id="cite_ref-94" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-94">&#91;94&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>At the Ipatiev House, Olga and her sisters were eventually required to do their own laundry and learned how to make bread. The girls took turns keeping Alexandra company and amusing Alexei, who was still confined to bed and suffering from pain after his latest injury. Olga was reportedly deeply depressed and lost a great deal of weight during her final months. "She was thin, pale, and looked very sick", recalled one of the guards, Alexander Strekotin, in his memoirs. "She took few walks in the garden, and spent most of her time with her brother." Another guard recalled that the few times she did walk outside, she stood there "gazing sadly into the distance, making it easy to read her emotions."<sup id="cite_ref-95" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-95">&#91;95&#93;</a></sup> Later, Olga appeared angry with her younger sister Maria for being too friendly to the guards, reported Strekotin.<sup id="cite_ref-96" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-96">&#91;96&#93;</a></sup> After late June, when a new command was installed, the family was forbidden from fraternizing with the guards and the conditions of their imprisonment became even more stringent.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg/220px-AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg/330px-AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg/440px-AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1914" data-file-height="1077" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:AlexeiOlgaRus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">Tsarevich Alexei (left) and Grand Duchess Olga Nikolaevna in May 1918 aboard the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rus_(ship)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Rus (ship) (page does not exist)">Rus</a></i>, the ship that ferried them to their deaths at Yekaterinburg. This is the last known photo of Olga and Alexei</div></div></div></div>
<p>On 14 July 1918, local priests at Yekaterinburg conducted a private church service for the family and reported that Olga and her family, contrary to custom, fell on their knees during the prayer for the dead.<sup id="cite_ref-97" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-97">&#91;97&#93;</a></sup> The following day, on 15 July, Olga and her sisters appeared in good spirits as they joked with one another and moved the beds in their room so visiting cleaning women could scrub the floor. They got down on their hands and knees to help the women and whispered to them when the guards weren't looking. All four young women wore long black skirts and white silk blouses, the same clothing they had worn the previous day. Their short hair was "tumbled and disorderly." They told the women how much they enjoyed physical exertion and wished there was more of it for them to do in the Ipatiev House. Olga appeared sickly.<sup id="cite_ref-98" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-98">&#91;98&#93;</a></sup> As the family was eating dinner that night, <a href="/wiki/Yakov_Yurovsky" title="Yakov Yurovsky">Yakov Yurovsky</a>, the head of the detachment, came in and announced that the family's kitchen boy and Alexei's playmate, 14-year-old <a href="/wiki/Leonid_Sednev" title="Leonid Sednev">Leonid Sednev</a>, must gather his things and go to a family member. The boy had actually been sent to a hotel across the street because the guards did not want to kill him along with the rest of the Romanov party. The family, unaware of the plan to kill them, was upset and unsettled by Sednev's absence. Dr. Eugene Botkin and Tatiana went that evening to Yurovsky's office, for what was to be the last time, to ask for the return of the kitchen boy who kept Alexei amused during the long hours of captivity. Yurovsky placated them by telling them the boy would return soon, but the family was unconvinced.<sup id="cite_ref-99" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-99">&#91;99&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Late that night, on the night of 16 July, the family was awakened and told to come down to the lower level of the house because there was unrest in the town at large and they would have to be moved for their own safety. The family emerged from their rooms carrying pillows, bags, and other items to make Alexandra and Alexei comfortable. The family paused and crossed themselves when they saw the <a href="/wiki/Taxidermy" title="Taxidermy">stuffed</a> mother bear and cubs that stood on the landing, perhaps as a sign of respect for the dead. Nicholas told the servants and family "Well, we're going to get out of this place." They asked questions of the guards but did not appear to suspect they were going to be killed. Yurovsky, who had been a professional photographer, directed the family to take different positions as a photographer might. Alexandra, who had complained about the lack of chairs for herself and Alexei, sat to her son's left. The Tsar stood behind Alexei, Dr. Botkin stood to the Tsar's right, Olga and her sisters stood behind Alexandra along with the servants. They were left for approximately half an hour while further preparations were made. The group said little during this time, but Alexandra whispered to the girls in English, violating the guard's rules that they must speak in Russian. Yurovsky came in, ordered them to stand, and read the sentence of execution. Olga and her mother attempted to make the sign of the cross and the rest of the family had time only to utter a few incoherent sounds of shock or protest before the death squad under Yurovsky's command began shooting. It was the early hours of 17 July 1918.<sup id="cite_ref-100" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-100">&#91;100&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The initial round of gunfire killed only the Tsar, the Empress and two male servants, and wounded Grand Duchess Maria, Dr Botkin and the Empress' maidservant, Demidova. At that point the gunmen had to leave the room because of smoke and toxic fumes from their guns and plaster dust their bullets had released from the walls. After allowing the haze to clear for several minutes, the gunmen returned. Dr Botkin was killed, and a gunman named Ermakov repeatedly tried to shoot Tsarevich Alexei, but failed because jewels sewn into the boy's clothes shielded him. Ermakov tried to stab Alexei with a bayonet but failed again, and finally Yurovsky fired two shots into the boy's head. Yurovsky and Ermakov approached Olga and Tatiana, who were crouched against the room's rear wall, clinging to each other and screaming for their mother. Ermakov stabbed both young women with his 8-inch bayonet, but had difficulty penetrating their torsos because of the jewels that had been sewn into their chemises. The sisters tried to stand, but Tatiana was killed instantly when Yurovsky shot her in the back of her head. A moment later, Olga too died when Ermakov shot her in the jaw.<sup id="cite_ref-101" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-101">&#91;101&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-102" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-102">&#91;102&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For decades (until all the bodies were found and identified, see below) conspiracy theorists suggested that one or more of the family somehow survived the slaughter. Several people claimed to be surviving members of the Romanov family following the assassinations. A woman named <a href="/wiki/Marga_Boodts" title="Marga Boodts">Marga Boodts</a> claimed to be Grand Duchess Olga. Boodts lived in a villa on <a href="/wiki/Lake_Como" title="Lake Como">Lake Como</a> in Italy and it was claimed that she was supported by the former kaiser, <a href="/wiki/William_II,_German_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="William II, German Emperor">Wilhelm II</a> and by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>. Prince <a href="/wiki/Prince_Sigismund_of_Prussia_(1896%E2%80%931978)" title="Prince Sigismund of Prussia (1896–1978)">Sigismund of Prussia</a>, son of Alexandra's sister, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Irene_of_Hesse_and_by_Rhine" title="Princess Irene of Hesse and by Rhine">Irene</a>, said he accepted her as Olga, and Sigismund also supported Anastasia claimant Anna Anderson.<sup id="cite_ref-103" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-103">&#91;103&#93;</a></sup> His mother, Irene, did not believe either woman. Michael Goleniewski, an Alexei pretender, even claimed that the entire family had escaped.<sup id="cite_ref-104" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-104">&#91;104&#93;</a></sup> Most historians discounted the claims and consistently believed that Olga died with her family.<sup id="cite_ref-105" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-105">&#91;105&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Romanov_graves_and_DNA_proof">Romanov graves and DNA proof</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Romanov graves and DNA proof">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg/220px-Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="303" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg/330px-Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg/440px-Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg 2x" data-file-width="466" data-file-height="641" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Forensic_rec._Romanov_03.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;">A forensic facial reconstruction of Grand Duchess Olga by S.A. Nikitin, 1994</div></div></div></div><p> Remains later identified through DNA testing as the Romanovs and their servants were discovered in the woods outside Yekaterinburg in 1991. Two bodies, Alexei and one of his sisters, generally thought to be either Maria or Anastasia, were missing. On 23 August 2007, a Russian archaeologist announced the discovery of two burned, partial skeletons at a bonfire site near Yekaterinburg that appeared to match the site described in Yurovsky's memoirs. The archaeologists said the bones are from a boy who was roughly between the ages of twelve and fifteen years at the time of his death and of a young woman who was roughly between the ages of fifteen and nineteen years old.<sup id="cite_ref-106" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-106">&#91;106&#93;</a></sup> Anastasia was seventeen years, one month old at the time of the assassination, while her sister Maria was nineteen years, one month old and her brother Alexei was two weeks shy of his fourteenth birthday. Olga and Tatiana were twenty-two and twenty-one years old at the time of the assassination. Along with the remains of the two bodies, archaeologists found "shards of a container of sulfuric acid, nails, metal strips from a wooden box, and bullets of various caliber." The bones were found using metal detectors and metal rods as probes.<sup id="cite_ref-Bones_found_107-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bones_found-107">&#91;107&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Preliminary testing indicated a "high degree of probability" that the remains belong to the Tsarevich Alexei and to one of his sisters, Russian forensic scientists announced on 22 January 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-Study_108-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Study-108">&#91;108&#93;</a></sup> The Yekaterinburg region's chief forensic expert Nikolai Nevolin indicated the results would be compared against those obtained by foreign experts.<sup id="cite_ref-Russia_109-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Russia-109">&#91;109&#93;</a></sup> On 30 April 2008, Russian forensic scientists announced that DNA testing confirms that the remains belong to the Tsarevich Alexei and to one of his sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-Eckel_110-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Eckel-110">&#91;110&#93;</a></sup> In March 2009, Dr. Michael Coble of the US Armed Forces DNA Identification Lab published the final, peer reviewed results of the tests on the 2007 remains, comparing them with the 1991 remains, concluding the entire family died together in 1918. Russian and Austrian scientists got the same results.<sup id="cite_ref-111" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-111">&#91;111&#93;</a></sup> This finding confirms that all of the Tsar's family were accounted for.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Grand_Duchess_Olga_Nikolaevna_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Romanov_sainthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Romanov sainthood">Romanov sainthood</a></div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Olga Romanova</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Saint, Grand Duchess and Passion bearer</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honored&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data">Russian Orthodox Church</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1981 and 2000 by Russian Orthodox Church Abroad and the Russian Orthodox Church</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data">Church on Blood, Yekaterinburg, Russia</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">17 July</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>In 2000, Olga and her family were <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as <a href="/wiki/Passion_bearer" title="Passion bearer">passion bearers</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>. The family had previously been canonized in 1981 by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Abroad" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox Church Abroad">Russian Orthodox Church Abroad</a> as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">holy martyrs</a>.
</p><p>The bodies of Tsar Nicholas II, Tsarina Alexandra, and three of their daughters were finally interred at <a href="/wiki/Saints_Peter_and_Paul_Cathedral,_Saint_Petersburg" title="Saints Peter and Paul Cathedral, Saint Petersburg">St. Peter and Paul Cathedral</a> in St. Petersburg on 17 July 1998, eighty years after they were murdered.<sup id="cite_ref-112" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-112">&#91;112&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
