<p><b>Saint Corbinian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Corbinianus</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Corbinien</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Korbinian</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 670 – 8 September <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;730 AD</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. After living as a hermit near <a href="/wiki/Chartres" title="Chartres">Chartres</a> for fourteen years, he made a pilgrimage to Rome. Pope Gregory II sent him to Bavaria. His opposition to the marriage of Duke Grimoald to his brother's widow, Biltrudis, caused Corbinian to go into exile for a time. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 8 September. The commemoration of the translation of his relics is 20 November.<sup id="cite_ref-index_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-index-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Corbinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Corbinian was born and baptized as <b>Waldegiso</b> at <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2tres,_Seine-et-Marne" title="Châtres, Seine-et-Marne">Châtres</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Melun" title="Melun">Melun</a>, in Frankish territory. He was named after his father, who may have died when Corbinian was an infant. Soon after his father's death, his mother Corbiniana<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> renamed Waldegiso to "Corbinian", after herself.<sup id="cite_ref-compass_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-compass-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Nothing else is known of his childhood. The early source for Corbinian's life is the <i>Vita Corbiniani</i> of <a href="/wiki/Arbeo_of_Freising" title="Arbeo of Freising">Bishop Arbeo of Freising</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He lived in Châtres on the road to <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> for fourteen years, near a church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Germain_of_Paris" title="Germain of Paris">Saint Germain</a>. His reputation attracted students to him, which distracted him from his hermitage. His devotion to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter the Apostle</a> prompted a decision to make a journey to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, accompanied by some of the disciples. While in Rome, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_II" title="Pope Gregory II">Pope Gregory II</a> admonished him to use his talents to <a href="/wiki/Evangelize" class="mw-redirect" title="Evangelize">evangelize</a> <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria" title="Duchy of Bavaria">Bavaria</a>. Corbinian, who may already have been a bishop or who was so consecrated by Gregory, was sent to minister to <a href="/wiki/Grimoald_of_Bavaria" title="Grimoald of Bavaria">Grimoald</a>, the Frankish <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bavaria">Duke of Bavaria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Corbinian probably arrived in Bavaria in 724.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On a mountain near <a href="/wiki/Freising" title="Freising">Freising</a>, where there was already a sanctuary, the saint erected a <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> and a school, which came to be governed by his brother Erembert, after his death. In 738, when <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Saint Boniface</a> regulated the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ecclesial" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:ecclesial">ecclesial</a> structure in the Duchy of Bavaria by creating four <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">dioceses</a> to be governed by the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">archbishop of Mainz</a>, Erembert was chosen first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Freising" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Freising">Bishop of Freising</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathmun_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathmun-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg/220px-Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="151" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg/330px-Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg/440px-Saint_Corbinian_of_Freising_and_the_Bear_by_Cosmas_Damian_Asam.jpg 2x" data-file-width="817" data-file-height="560" /></a><figcaption>Saint Corbinian of Freising and the Bear, by <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_Damian_Asam" title="Cosmas Damian Asam">Cosmas Damian Asam</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1725</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>Soon after settling, Corbinian denounced Grimoald's marriage to his brother's widow, Biltrudis, though Grimoald had already repented of his <a href="/wiki/Incest" title="Incest">incest</a>. This incited his anger and the chagrin of his wife, who <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/excoriate" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:excoriate">excoriated</a> Corbinian, labeling him a foreign interloper. Finally, she arranged to have him murdered. Corbinian fled Freising until Grimoald was killed and Biltrudis carried off by invaders in 725.<sup id="cite_ref-compass_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-compass-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Corbinian returned on the invitation of Grimoald's successor, Huebert,<sup id="cite_ref-cathcor_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathcor-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and continued his apostolic labors at Freising until his own death in 730.
</p><p>Corbinian's body, buried at Merano, was translated to Freising in 769 by the aforementioned Bishop Arbeo, author of Corbinian's <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i>, and is now entombed in <a href="/wiki/Freising_Cathedral" title="Freising Cathedral">Freising Cathedral</a>.
</p>
<h2><span id="Corbinian.27s_bear"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Corbinian's_bear">Corbinian's bear</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Corbinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Corbinian&#039;s bear">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Corbinian's symbol is the saddled <a href="/wiki/Bear" title="Bear">bear</a>.  According to his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, a bear killed Corbinian's pack horse on the way to Rome and so the saint commanded it to carry his load. Once he arrived in Rome, however, he let the bear go, and it lumbered back to its native forest.<sup id="cite_ref-bear_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bear-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  Both the heraldic element and the legend itself carry significant <a href="/wiki/Symbol" title="Symbol">symbolism</a>. One interpretation is that the bear tamed by <a href="/wiki/Divine_grace" title="Divine grace">God's grace</a> is the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Freising" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Freising">Bishop of Freising</a> himself and the pack saddle is the burden of his <a href="/wiki/Episcopate" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopate">episcopate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The bear's submission and retreat can also be interpreted as Christianity's "taming" and "domestication" of the ferocity of <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">paganism</a> and, consequentially, the laying of a "[foundation] for a great civilization in the Duchy of Bavaria."<sup id="cite_ref-bear_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bear-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Catholic_iconography">In Catholic iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Corbinian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In Catholic iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg/160px-Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg.png" decoding="async" width="160" height="214" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg/240px-Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg/320px-Coat_of_Arms_of_Benedictus_XVI.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="762" data-file-height="1021" /></a><figcaption>Papal Arms of Pope Benedict XVI</figcaption></figure>
<p>Corbinian's Bear is used as the symbol of Freising in both civic and ecclesiastical <a href="/wiki/Heraldry" title="Heraldry">heraldry</a>. It appeared on the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Coat of arms of Pope Benedict XVI">arms</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>, who first adopted the symbol when, still known as Joseph Ratzinger, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Munich_and_Freising" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Munich and Freising">Archbishop of Freising-Munich</a> in March 1977.<sup id="cite_ref-cnews_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cnews-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  He retained the bear in his revised coat of arms when he was elevated to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a> in June of the same year, and again on his papal coat of arms when he was elected in 2005.
</p><p>The scallop shell is a traditional reference to pilgrimage. For Pope Benedict XVI, it also reminded  him of the legend according to which one day St. Augustine, pondering the mystery of the Trinity, saw a child at the seashore playing with a shell, trying to put the water of the ocean into a little hole. Then he heard the words: This hole can no more contain the waters of the ocean than your intellect can comprehend the mystery of God. The crowned Moor is a regional motif in heraldry often seen in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, Benedict's German homeland.  Benedict has been quoted saying that, in addition to the obvious reference back to Saint Corbinian, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Munich_and_Freising" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Munich and Freising">founder of the diocese</a> where Benedict would become bishop in 1977, the bear represents Benedict himself being "tamed by God" to bear the spiritual burdens of Benedict's own ministries first as bishop, then as cardinal, and now as pope.<sup id="cite_ref-Milestones_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Milestones-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
