<p>Saint <b>Ceolwulf</b> was <a href="/wiki/King_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Northumbria">King of Northumbria</a> from 729 until 737, except for a short period in 731 or 732 when he was deposed and quickly restored to power. Ceolwulf abdicated and entered the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Lindisfarne" title="Lindisfarne">Lindisfarne</a>. He was the "most glorious king" to whom <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> dedicated his <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum</a></i>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ceolwulf_of_Northumbria&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ceolwulf was born around 695 in Northumbria.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His ancestry is thus given by the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i>: "Ceolwulf was the son of Cutha, Cutha of Cuthwin, Cuthwin of Leoldwald, Leoldwald of Egwald, Egwald of Aldhelm, Aldhelm of Ocga, Ocga of Ida, Ida of Eoppa."<sup id="cite_ref-hind_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hind-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Ceolwulf's brother, Coenred, seized the Northumbrian throne in 716.<sup id="cite_ref-Britannia_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Coenred ruled for two years when Osric, the last of the House of Aethelric, claimed the throne and ruled for ten years. In 729, shortly before his death, Osric nominated Ceolwulf as his successor.
</p><p>He consulted the Venerable Bede for advice on important matters.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  While praising Ceolwulf's piety, Bede also expressed some reservations regarding Ceolwulf's ability to rule. Ceolwulf was a man with deep monastic interests and perhaps little suited to affairs of state. Bede dedicated his <i>Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum</i> (<i>History of the English Church</i>) to Ceolwulf in 731.<sup id="cite_ref-Britannia_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The beginning of his reign was disturbed by factions and rebellion, and that same year he was forcibly seized by his enemies and compelled to receive the monastic tonsure.<sup id="cite_ref-Stanton_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stanton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was deposed for a short period but quickly restored. The details of the attempted coup are unclear. Bishop <a href="/wiki/Acca_of_Hexham" title="Acca of Hexham">Acca of Hexham</a> is said to have been deprived of his see, which suggests he may have supported Ceolwulf's opponents.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Ceolwulf named his cousin <a href="/wiki/Ecgbert_of_York" title="Ecgbert of York">Ecgbert</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> of <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> around 732<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> (other sources date the appointment to 734).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It has been suggested that Ceolwulf had spent time in Ireland, perhaps studying to enter into religion. As king, he had endowed the monastery at Lindisfarne with many gifts. He obtained a special dispensation for the monks which allowed the consumption of beer and wine, contrary to the established Celtic practice which limited beverages to water and milk.<sup id="cite_ref-Britannia_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 737, Ceolwulf abdicated in favor of his first cousin <a href="/wiki/Eadberht_of_Northumbria" title="Eadberht of Northumbria">Eadberht</a>, to retire to Lindisfarne.<sup id="cite_ref-Stanton_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stanton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His death is recorded in the winter of 764&#8211;765. Bishop <a href="/wiki/Ecgred_of_Lindisfarne" title="Ecgred of Lindisfarne">Ecgred of Lindisfarne</a> translated Ceolwulf's relics to the rebuilt Church of Saint Peter, Cuthbert, and Ceolwulf at <a href="/wiki/Norham" title="Norham">Norham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Stanton_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stanton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast day is 15 January.<sup id="cite_ref-A_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-A-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
