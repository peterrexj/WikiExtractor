<p><b>Saint Judicael</b> or <b>Judicaël</b> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;590</span>&#160;&#8211; 16 December 647 or 652) (<a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>:<i>Ithel</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-EBK_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EBK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> also spelled <b>Judhael</b> (with many other variants),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was the King of <a href="/wiki/Domnon%C3%A9e" title="Domnonée">Domnonée</a>, part of <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, in the mid-7th century and later revered as a Roman Catholic saint.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Judicael&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, the Bretons were divided into various <i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">regna</i></span></i> (minor kingdoms) during the sixth century, of which <a href="/wiki/Domnon%C3%A9e" title="Domnonée">Domnonée</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cornouaille" title="Cornouaille">Cornouaille</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gwened_(kingdom)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gwened (kingdom)">Gwened</a> are the best known. They initially pledged themselves to <a href="/wiki/Childebert_I" title="Childebert I">Childebert I</a> in exchange for legitimacy. They attempted to escape <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Empire">Frankish</a> rule during the time of <a href="/wiki/Chilperic_I" title="Chilperic I">Chilperic I</a>, who subdued <a href="/wiki/Waroch_II" title="Waroch II">Waroch II</a> and at least the eastern realms of the region. <a href="/wiki/Guntram" title="Guntram">Guntram</a>, Chilperic's brother, retained his lordship over <a href="/wiki/Browaroch" class="mw-redirect" title="Browaroch">Waroch</a> and the <i>Brittani</i> formed a Frankish tributary-vassal state through the reign of <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref--Smith_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note--Smith-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiographic_life">Hagiographic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Judicael&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiographic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Judicael was born around the year 590, the eldest son of Judael or Judhael, King of Domnonée, and Queen Prizel, the daughter of Ausoch, Count of Léon. He was the eldest of fifteen brothers and five sisters, several of whom, such as Judoc and Guinien, were revered as saints.
</p><p>When Judhael died around 605, although Judicael was his eldest son and heir, the Throne was usurped by his younger brothers, Haeloc, while Judicael preferred to retire to <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St_M%C3%A9en" title="Abbey of St Méen">St John's Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl" title="Gaël">Gaël</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DLB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DLB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Haeloc in about 615, Judicael finally left the monastic life behind in order to rule Domnonée. For twenty years, he ruled the kingdom with authority and wisdom. He married in Morone around 630.[[<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>|citation needed]]
</p><p>Around 642, Judicael retired again to St John's Abbey at <a href="/wiki/Ga%C3%ABl" title="Gaël">Gaël</a> or possibly to the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Paimpont" title="Paimpont">Paimpont</a> which he had founded. He left the throne to his brother, Judoc (aka Josse), but he also embraced the monastic life instead and the subsequent kings of Domnonée are unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-DLB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DLB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Judicael died on Sunday 16 December in either 647 or 652.<sup id="cite_ref-EBK_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EBK-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was buried next at Gaël Abbey, next to the founder and his abbot, Saint <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A9en" class="mw-redirect" title="Méen">Méen</a>, and was later declared a saint.He is traditionally said to have been the brother of Saints <a href="/wiki/Judoc" title="Judoc">Judoc</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winnoc" title="Winnoc">Winnoc</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Judicael&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg/200px-Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="205" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg/300px-Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg/400px-Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg 2x" data-file-width="497" data-file-height="510" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Triens_de_Judica%C3%ABl_frapp%C3%A9_%C3%A0_Redonis.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A gold coin minted by Judicael in the 630s.</div></div></div>
<p>Bishop Ouen of Rouen, in his 'Life of Éloi of Noyon' and the pseudo-Fredegar in his '<a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Fredegar" title="Chronicle of Fredegar">Chronicle</a>' relate that in 635/636 during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>, the Bretons attacked the borders of the Franks. Threatened by the intervention of the Burgundian army which had just defeated the Basques of Soule, King Judicael agreed to come and meet the Frankish king in his palace in <a href="/wiki/Clichy,_Hauts-de-Seine" title="Clichy, Hauts-de-Seine">Clichy</a>. Judicael exchanged presents with Dagobert, recognised his suzerainty and concluded peace. However, he was "a very religious man and had a great fear of God" and fearful of the irreligious ways of the royal court, he refused further hospitality.<sup id="cite_ref--Smith_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note--Smith-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Judicael is known to have minted his own coins.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_interpretations">Later interpretations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Judicael&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later interpretations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the Cartulary of Redon, it is recorded that a noblewoman called Roiantdreh adopted King Solomon of Brittany as heir to her lands in AD 869, her son Owain having predeceased her. At the end of the document, she details her paternal ancestry over eight generations: "Jedechael begat Urbien, Urbien begat Judon, Judon begat Custentin, Custentin begat Argant, Argant begat Judwal, Judwal begat Louenan, Louenan begat Roiantdreh". Some historians, including recently Alan J. Raude, believe that, due to the presence of names from the family of the kings of Domnonée, Roiantrdreh's ancestor 'Jedechael' is King Judicael of the early 7th century.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Arthur de la Borderie, however, doubted this identification because there was no mention of him being 'king and saint' as was customary.<sup id="cite_ref-DLB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DLB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The 'Life' of Saint Judicael written in the 11th century by a monk called Ingomar states that "all the princes who reigned in Brittany since Judicael were descended from this king" and Dom Morice uses this to postulate that he was an ancestor of a pseudo-Erispoe, Count of Rennes, and of the later kings of Brittany, designating the latter as the father of King <a href="/wiki/Nominoe" title="Nominoe">Nominoe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-PHM_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PHM-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1514, Alain Bouchart, in his 'Grandes Chroniques' constructed a complete list of 'Kings of Brittany' largely based on the fictional work of <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth" title="Geoffrey of Monmouth">Geoffrey of Monmouth</a> and claimed they descended from the legendary King <a href="/wiki/Conan_Meriadoc" title="Conan Meriadoc">Conan Meriadoc</a>. To the 10th king in the list, he gives the name, Judicael, taken from the historical king of Domnonée.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This fictional character's existence was accepted well into the eighteenth century in the works of Pierre-Hyacinthe Morice de Beaubois.<sup id="cite_ref-PHM_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PHM-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
