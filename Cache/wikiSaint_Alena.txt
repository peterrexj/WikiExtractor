<p><b>Saint Alena</b> (also written Alène<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or Alina<sup id="cite_ref-Ebobas_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ebobas-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) is a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> who, if historical, was martyred around the year 640.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She is sometimes referred to as Alena of Forest,<sup id="cite_ref-Ebobas_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ebobas-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or Alena of Brussels,<sup id="cite_ref-StPat_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StPat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> having died in <a href="/wiki/Forest,_Belgium" title="Forest, Belgium">Forest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a> which is now one of the nineteen municipalities of <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_of_Saint_Alena">Life of Saint Alena</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Alena&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life of Saint Alena">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The traditional account of Saint Alena's life, dating to the twelfth century,<sup id="cite_ref-Denis_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denis-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> states that she was born in <a href="/wiki/Dilbeek" title="Dilbeek">Dilbeek</a>, just outside Brussels, Belgium, to pagan parents, the nobleman Levold and his wife, Hildegaart.<sup id="cite_ref-Brewiarz_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brewiarz-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Legend_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Legend-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Alena chose to be <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> without the knowledge of her parents. As a <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a>, she had to attend <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> secretly.<sup id="cite_ref-StPat_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StPat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When her father discovered that she was worshipping as a Christian, he came to the conclusion that Christians had bewitched her into conversion. He sent guards to bring her home; she resisted, and during the fighting she lost one arm.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She subsequently died in prison due to her injuries.<sup id="cite_ref-Brewiarz_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brewiarz-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Various (unspecified) miracles were claimed at the burial site of Alena's body, and one of Levold's subjects, Duke Omundus, had his sight restored by invoking Alena's prayers.<sup id="cite_ref-Legend_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Legend-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An angel appeared and took the severed arm to the chapel where she worshipped. Alena's parents were shocked, but the miracles, and the witness given by her determined faith, led them to examine Christianity, and they themselves were converted.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_of_Saint_Alena">Veneration of Saint Alena</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Alena&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration of Saint Alena">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg/220px-Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="137" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg/330px-Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg/440px-Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2054" data-file-height="1282" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Dilbeek_town_house_0001.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sint-Alenatoren (left) with <a href="/wiki/De_Viron_Castle" title="De Viron Castle">De Viron Castle</a> on the background</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg/220px-%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg/330px-%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg/440px-%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="2700" data-file-height="1520" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%87%D0%B0_%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BF%D1%8B%D1%85.jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Blind Leading the Blind</i> by <a href="/wiki/Pieter_Breughel_the_Elder" class="mw-redirect" title="Pieter Breughel the Elder">Pieter Breughel the Elder</a> In the background is the church of Saint Alena of Dilbeek Belgium</div></div></div>
<p>The Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Cambrai" title="Cambrai">Cambrai</a> gave permission for Abbot Godeschalk of <a href="/wiki/Affligem_Abbey" title="Affligem Abbey">Affligem Abbey</a> to enshrine Alena's remains in the <a href="/wiki/Forest_Abbey" title="Forest Abbey">Priory of Forest</a>, then a dependency of Affligem, as the relics of a saint on Pentecost Sunday, 19 May 1193. This was effectively a <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">canonisation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Denis_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denis-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Legend_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Legend-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The chapel with Alena's relics became a popular place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. Her shrine became incorporated into the parish church of Forest as a chapel which held her <a href="/wiki/Cenotaph" title="Cenotaph">cenotaph</a> and <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Denis_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Denis-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> By around 1600, her jawbone and upper arm were preserved in the parish church, each in its own jewel-studded <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> dating from the 15th century. The rest of the bones, with the exception of the collarbones, were preserved in the abbey church in a <a href="/wiki/Baroque" title="Baroque">Baroque</a> reliquary (1644) commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Fran%C3%A7oise_de_Bette" title="Françoise de Bette">Françoise de Bette</a>, abbess 1637–1666. When the abbey was suppressed in 1796 the last abbess, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Juana_Francisca_de_Rueda_de_Conteras&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Juana Francisca de Rueda de Conteras (page does not exist)">Juana Francisca de Rueda de Conteras</a> (1785–1818), removed this reliquary to a monastery near <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg" title="Würzburg">Würzburg</a>. It was returned to Forest only in 1812. The collarbones are still kept in a reliquary in the church of St Ambrosius in <a href="/wiki/Dilbeek" title="Dilbeek">Dilbeek</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Alena is depicted in art as a <a href="/wiki/Princess" title="Princess">princess</a> with one arm torn off. She might also be portrayed healing a blind man, or with an angel helping her.<sup id="cite_ref-StPat_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StPat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is venerated in Brussels, and is invoked for eye troubles and toothache.<sup id="cite_ref-StPat_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StPat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her feast day is given as 17 June by the Polish <a href="/wiki/Breviary" title="Breviary">Breviary</a><sup id="cite_ref-Brewiarz_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brewiarz-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> but other Polish-language sources also recognise 16 June, 28 July and 16 December;<sup id="cite_ref-Ebobas_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ebobas-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> with 16 June and 16 December often being kept as the <a href="/wiki/Name_day" title="Name day">name day</a> in Poland.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Her feast is cited as 19 June in Italy,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and 24 June by English-language sources.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-StPat_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StPat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It is kept locally on 16 June in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Brabant" title="Province of Brabant">Brabant</a> region of Belgium.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Antiochian_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiochian Orthodox Church">Antiochian Orthodox</a> Diocese of North America commemorates her on 18 June.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> She does not appear in the index of the 2004 edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
