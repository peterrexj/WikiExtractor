<p><b>Saint Sabina</b> was a saint and <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> of the early church. Her feast day is 29 August. She lived and suffered martyrdom at the beginning of the 2nd century in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sabina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG/220px-Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG/330px-Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG/440px-Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4288" data-file-height="2848" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Sabina2013-08-27.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">Reliquary</a> of St Sabina in the local parish of Santi Pietro e Paolo in Ascona.</div></div></div>
<p>Sabina was the daughter of Herod Metallarius and the wealthy widow of Senator Valentinus,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> originally from <a href="/wiki/Avezzano" title="Avezzano">Avezzano</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Abruzzo" title="Abruzzo">Abruzzo</a> region of Italy.
</p><p>Sabina converted to Christianity due to the example of her Syrian slave <a href="/wiki/Saint_Serapia" title="Saint Serapia">Serapia</a>. The widow then withdrew with a few devout friends to one of her country seats, where she spent her time doing good works. Serapia was denounced and beheaded in the city of Vindena in the state of <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>. Sabina rescued her remains and had them interred in the family mausoleum where she also expected to be buried. Sabina was initially discharged out of regard to her quality and friends;<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> but some time later was denounced as well, and accused of being a Christian by Elpidio the Prefect. She was thereupon martyred in Rome around the year 126 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 430 her <a href="/wiki/Translation_of_relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation of relics">relics were brought</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Aventine_Hill" title="Aventine Hill">Aventine Hill</a>, to the newly erected <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Santa Sabina</a> on the site of her <a href="/wiki/Domus" title="Domus">house</a>, originally situated near a temple of <a href="/wiki/Juno_(mythology)" title="Juno (mythology)">Juno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  This house may also have formed an early Christian <a href="/wiki/Titular_church" title="Titular church">titular church</a>. The church was initially dedicated to both Sabina and Serapia.<sup id="cite_ref-Löffler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Löffler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commentary">Commentary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sabina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Commentary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Klemens Löffler, writing for the <i>Catholic Encyclopedia</i>, the Acts of the martyrdom have no historic value.<sup id="cite_ref-Löffler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Löffler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Maya Maskarinec suggests that "'Sabina'...was most plausibly the donor who had provided the <a href="/wiki/Titular_church" title="Titular church"><i>titulus</i></a> with property on the Aventine."<sup id="cite_ref-maya_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maya-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Often this was a private home to be used as a church. When someone donated property or money, the resulting foundation bore their name. The <i>passio</i> that developed during the sixth to eighth century becomes attached to the memory regarding a late fourth or early fifth century philanthropist. "Gradually, however, throughout Rome, many of the <i>tituli's</i> donors metamorphosed into their communities' patron saints."<sup id="cite_ref-maya_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maya-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
