<p><b>Quirinus of Neuss</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Quirin, Quirinus</i>), sometimes called <i>Quirinus of Rome</i> (which is the name shared by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quirinus_of_Tegernsee" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Quirinus of Tegernsee">another martyr</a>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches. His <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> was centered at <a href="/wiki/Neuss" title="Neuss">Neuss</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, though he was a <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_early_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Persecution of early Christians in the Roman Empire">Roman martyr</a>.
</p><p>According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>, a Roman martyr named Quirinus was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb">Catacomb</a> of Prætextatus on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Appia" class="mw-redirect" title="Via Appia">Via Appia</a>. The <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> (ed. De Rossi-Duchesne, 52) mentions Quirinus' name and place of burial. The <i>Itineraries</i> to the graves of the Roman martyrs (<a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_De_Rossi" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni Battista De Rossi">Giovanni Battista De Rossi</a>, "Roma sotterranea", I, 180–1) also mention these two pieces of information.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Martyrologium Hieronymianum </i> assigns him under the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> of April 30, the date that appears in the catalogue of Roman martyrs of the 4th century.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Quirinus_of_Neuss&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Quirinus is introduced into the legendary Acts of Sts. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_I" title="Pope Alexander I">Pope Alexander I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Balbina" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Balbina">Balbina</a>, where it is said he was a <a href="/wiki/Tribune" title="Tribune">tribune</a> (Dufourcq, loc. cit., 175). He is said to have been <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a> in 116. Legends make him a Roman tribune who was ordered with executing Alexander, Eventius, and Theodolus, who had been arrested by order of <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Quirino_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Quirino-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> However, after witnessing miracles performed by these three saints, Quirinus converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and baptized by Alexander,<sup id="cite_ref-Baring_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> along with his daughter Balbina. <sup id="cite_ref-San_Quirino_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Quirino-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Quirin was condemned to have his tongue, hands and feet cut off.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to the popular legend, which is often represented in art, his tongue was offered to a falcon, but the bird refused to eat it: the Acts say nothing of it. The hands and feet were in like manner cast to dogs, and popular tradition adds that they refused to devour them. Afterwards he was drawn by oxen to the place of final execution where he was decapitated.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It is believed he was martyred on March 30, before being buried in the catacomb of Prætextatus on the Via Appia.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Quirinus_of_Neuss&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Quirinus_Schrein.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Quirinus_Schrein.jpg/250px-Quirinus_Schrein.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Quirinus_Schrein.jpg/375px-Quirinus_Schrein.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Quirinus_Schrein.jpg/500px-Quirinus_Schrein.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3142" data-file-height="2023" /></a><figcaption>Reliquary holding Quirinus' relics. Neuss.</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado</a> took the name from these Acts and put it in his Martyrology under date of March 30, on which day it used to be found in the <i>Roman Martyrology</i> (Quentin, "Les martyrologes historiques", 490). The latest edition of the Roman Martyrology commemorates Saint Quirinus on April 30.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>According to a document from <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> dating from 1485, Quirinus' body was donated in 1050 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo IX</a> to an abbess of Neuss named Gepa (who is called a sister of the pope).<sup id="cite_ref-San_Quirino_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Quirino-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In this way the <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> came to the <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a> Church of St. Quirinus at Neuss (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Quirinus-M%C3%BCnster&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Quirinus-Münster (page does not exist)">Quirinus-Münster</a>) which still exists. A statue of Quirinus sits atop the church (which <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Bernadotte" class="mw-redirect" title="Jean-Baptiste Bernadotte">Jean-Baptiste Bernadotte</a> attempted to plunder during the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars" title="Napoleonic Wars">Napoleonic Wars</a><sup id="cite_ref-QUIRINUS_von_Neuss_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-QUIRINUS_von_Neuss-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>).
</p><p>Inhabitants of that city invoked him for aid during <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Neuss" title="Siege of Neuss">Siege of Neuss</a> by <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bold" title="Charles the Bold">Charles the Bold</a> that occurred in 1474–5.<sup id="cite_ref-heiligenlexikon.de_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligenlexikon.de-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-San_Quirino_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Quirino-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His cult spread to Cologne, <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scandinavia" title="Scandinavia">Scandinavia</a>, western <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/The_Netherlands" class="mw-redirect" title="The Netherlands">the Netherlands</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, where he became the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Correggio,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Correggio, Italy">Correggio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Quirino_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Quirino-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Numerous wells and springs were dedicated to him, and he was invoked against the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a>, <a href="/wiki/Smallpox" title="Smallpox">smallpox</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gout" title="Gout">gout</a>; he was also considered a patron saint of animals.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Quirino_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Quirino-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Pilgrims to Neuss sought the <i>Quirinuswasser</i> (Quirinus water) from the <i>Quirinusbrunnen</i> (Quirinus spring or pump-room).<sup id="cite_ref-QUIRINUS_von_Neuss_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-QUIRINUS_von_Neuss-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Weather_lore" title="Weather lore">farmers' saying</a> associated with Quirinus' former feast day of March 30 was "Wie der Quirin, so der Sommer" ("As St. Quirinus' Day goes, so will the summer").<sup id="cite_ref-heiligenlexikon.de_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligenlexikon.de-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Quirinus, along with <a href="/wiki/Hubertus" title="Hubertus">Hubertus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cornelius_the_Centurion" title="Cornelius the Centurion">Cornelius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony</a>, was venerated as one of the <a href="/wiki/Four_Holy_Marshals" title="Four Holy Marshals">Four Holy Marshals</a> ('<i>Vier Marschälle Gottes</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Rhineland" title="Rhineland">Rhineland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-heiligenlexikon.de_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-heiligenlexikon.de-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  Portraits of Quirinus and of <a href="/wiki/St._Valentine" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Valentine">St. Valentine</a> appear at the top of the <a href="/wiki/Recto" class="mw-redirect" title="Recto">recto</a> of the <i><a href="/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicles" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuremberg Chronicles">Nuremberg Chronicles</a></i> (<i>Folio CXXII [Geneva]</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
