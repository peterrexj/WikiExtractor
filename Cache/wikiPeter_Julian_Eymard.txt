<p><b>Peter Julian Eymard</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament">S.S.S.</a></span> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Pierre-Julien Eymard</i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[pjɛʁ ʒyljɛ̃ ɛjmɑʁ]</a></span>; 4 February 1811 – 1 August 1868) was a French <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> and founder of two <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institutes</a>: the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament">Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament</a> for men and the <a href="/wiki/Servants_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Servants of the Blessed Sacrament">Servants of the Blessed Sacrament</a> for women.
</p><p>Eymard entered the novitiate of the <a href="/wiki/Oblates_of_Mary_Immaculate" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblates of Mary Immaculate">Oblates of Mary Immaculate</a> in June 1829. His first attempt as a seminarian ended because of serious illness. Throughout his life, Eymard suffered from poor health, particularly ‘weakness of the lungs’ and <a href="/wiki/Migraine" title="Migraine">migraine</a> headaches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Julian_Eymard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eymard was born 4 February 1811 at <a href="/wiki/La_Mure" title="La Mure">La Mure</a>, Isère, in the French Alps. His father was a smith whose second wife was Julian's mother.<sup id="cite_ref-marist_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marist-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> All his life Peter Julian (or Pierre-Julien in French) had an intense devotion to Mary, the Mother of Jesus. Before his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on 16 March 1823, he went on foot to the shrine of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Laus" title="Our Lady of Laus">Notre-Dame du Laus</a>. Later, he learned of the apparition of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_La_Salette" title="Our Lady of La Salette">Notre-Dame de La Salette</a> and enjoyed traveling to various Marian shrines throughout France.<sup id="cite_ref-congregation_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-congregation-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When his mother died in 1828 Julian resolved to enter the novitiate of the <a href="/wiki/Oblates_of_Mary_Immaculate" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblates of Mary Immaculate">Oblates of Mary Immaculate</a> and, despite his father's opposition, did so in June 1829. His first attempt as a seminarian ended because of serious illness. Throughout his life, Eymard suffered from poor health, particularly ‘weakness of the lungs’ and migraine headaches.
</p><p>After his father's death in 1831, he succeeded – with the help of his former superior – in gaining admission to the major seminary of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Grenoble-Vienne" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Grenoble-Vienne">Diocese of Grenoble</a>. On 20 July 1834, he was ordained a priest for the Diocese of Grenoble.<sup id="cite_ref-Benziger_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benziger-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was assigned as assistant pastor at the town of <a href="/wiki/Chatte" title="Chatte">Chatte</a>, and three years later appointed pastor of Mount Saint-Eynard.
</p><p>On his second assignment at Monteynard, the parish, which had a dilapidated church and poor rectory, consisted of a farming community with few people attending Mass. There had not been a regular pastor there for some time. The bishop urged Eymard's two sisters to move with him to the <a href="/wiki/Rectory" class="mw-redirect" title="Rectory">rectory</a>, which they did. In fact, they furnished the rectory, for the parish was very poor. Although Eymard is known to have revitalized the place, he was dissatisfied with parish work, and decided to join the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Mary_(Marists)" title="Society of Mary (Marists)">Marists</a> (the Society of Mary). His two sisters were quite devastated as they had dedicated their lives to serving him.<sup id="cite_ref-grace_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grace-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On August 20, 1837, he entered the Society of Mary seminary at Lyon and made his profession in February 1840. He worked with lay organizations promoting devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> and to the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>, particularly in the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Hours" class="mw-redirect" title="Forty Hours">Forty Hours</a>. He rose to the position of <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Provincial</a> of the Society at <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a> in 1844. His new responsibilities included being in charge of the Third Order of Mary, a lay group dedicated to Marist spirituality and to the promotion of the Christian family. <a href="/wiki/John_Vianney" title="John Vianney">John Vianney</a> was a member.<sup id="cite_ref-missmag_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-missmag-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His eucharistic spirituality did not spring full-grown from some mystical experience, but progressively.<sup id="cite_ref-pelletier_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelletier-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
As visitor-general, Eymard travelled throughout France to inspect the various Marist communities. He became familiar with the practice of sustained eucharistic worship during a visit to Paris in 1849, when he met with members of the Association of Nocturnal Adorers who had established exposition and perpetual adoration of the Blessed Sacrament at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Victories,_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Our Lady of Victories, Paris">Basilica of Our Lady of Victories</a>. After praying at the shrine of <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Fourvi%C3%A8re" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Fourvière">Our Lady of Fourviere</a> on 21 January 1851, Eymard moved to establish a Marist community dedicated to eucharistic adoration. However, his desire to establish a separate fraternity promoting adoration of the Blessed Sacrament was not seen as part of the <a href="/wiki/Charism" class="mw-redirect" title="Charism">charism</a> of the Marists.<sup id="cite_ref-grace_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grace-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His superiors disapproved, transferring him to the Marist College at <a href="/wiki/La_Seyne-sur-Mer" title="La Seyne-sur-Mer">La Seyne-sur-Mer</a>. Eventually, Eymard resolved to leave the Society of Mary to begin his new religious congregation with the diocesan priest Raymond de Cuers.<sup id="cite_ref-province_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-province-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Congregation_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament">Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Julian_Eymard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG/220px-L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="331" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG/330px-L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG/440px-L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3056" data-file-height="4592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:L55_-_Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin_-_Le_P%C3%A8re_Eymard.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Le Père Eymard, by Rodin</div></div></div>
<p>On 13 May 1856, the Paris bishops consented to Eymard's plans for a ‘Society of the Blessed Sacrament’. After many trials, Eymard and de Cuers established public exposition of the Blessed Sacrament in Paris on 6 January 1857 in a run-down building at 114 rue d’Enfer (which literally meant ‘street of hell’).<sup id="cite_ref-province_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-province-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament">Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament</a> began working with children in Paris to prepare them to receive their <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a>. It also reached out to non-practicing Catholics, inviting them to repent and begin receiving Communion again. Eymard established a common rule for the members of the society and worked toward papal approval.<sup id="cite_ref-grace_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grace-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A second community was established in Marseille in 1859, and a third in Angers in 1862. Pius IX granted a Decree of Approbation in June 1863. Eymard was a tireless proponent of frequent Holy Communion, an idea given more authoritative backing by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> in 1905.
</p><p>On 10 January 1969 Pope Paul VI issued a <b>Letter</b> to the Superior General, Roland Huot, of the Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament, lauding the function of adoration of the Blessed Sacrament Outside Mass declaring all those who do so make their Eucharistic adoration "in the name of the Church".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> This concession is included in the revised Roman Ritual, Holy Communion and the Worship of the Eucharist Outside Mass, No. 90 in the <i>editio typica</i>.
</p><p>By DECREE of the Congregation for the Sacraments and Divine Worship, dated 9 December 1995, SAINT PETER JULIAN EYMARD, PRIEST, was inserted in the General Roman Calendar with the rank of optional memorial.
</p><p><i>Font and fullness of all evangelization and striking expression of the infinite love of our divine Redeemer for mankind, the Holy Eucharist clearly marked the life and pastoral activity of Peter Julian Eymard. He truly deserves to be called an outstanding apostle of the Eucharist. In fact, his mission in the Church consisted in promoting the centrality of the Eucharistic Mystery in the whole life of the Christian community.</i>
</p><p>The French sculptor <a href="/wiki/Auguste_Rodin" title="Auguste Rodin">Auguste Rodin</a> received counsel from Eymard when Rodin entered the Congregation as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> in 1862, having given up art after the death of his sister. Eymard recognized Rodin's talent and advised him to return to his vocation. Rodin later produced a <a href="/wiki/Bust_(sculpture)" title="Bust (sculpture)">bust</a> of Eymard.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Servants_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament">Servants of the Blessed Sacrament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Julian_Eymard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Servants of the Blessed Sacrament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg/220px-St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg/330px-St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg/440px-St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Peter_Julian_bust.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bust of Eymard in a church in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a></div></div></div>
<p>In 1858, together with <a href="/wiki/Marguerite_Guillot" class="mw-redirect" title="Marguerite Guillot">Marguerite Guillot</a>, he founded the <a href="/wiki/Servants_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" title="Servants of the Blessed Sacrament">Servants of the Blessed Sacrament</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Contemplative" class="mw-redirect" title="Contemplative">contemplative</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> for women. He is quoted as saying, "You take Communion to become holy, not because you already are."<sup id="cite_ref-province_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-province-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Contemporaries">Contemporaries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Julian_Eymard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Contemporaries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eymard was a friend and contemporary of <a href="/wiki/John_Vianney" title="John Vianney">John Marie Vianney</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peter_Chanel" title="Peter Chanel">Peter Chanel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marcellin_Champagnat" title="Marcellin Champagnat">Marcellin Champagnat</a>, <a href="/wiki/Basil_Moreau" title="Basil Moreau">Basil Moreau</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pauline-Marie_Jaricot" class="mw-redirect" title="Pauline-Marie Jaricot">Pauline-Marie Jaricot</a> . He died at the age of fifty-seven<sup id="cite_ref-Benziger_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benziger-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/La_Mure" title="La Mure">La Mure</a> on 1 August 1868, of complications from a brain haemorrhage. His remains were buried in the cemetery at La Mure until 1877, when they were moved to the Blessed Sacrament Congregation's Corpus Christi Chapel in Paris, which had been consecrated in September 1876.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Julian_Eymard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eymard was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a> in 1908, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by Pope Pius XI on 12 July 1925, and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> on 9 December 1962.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Also canonized with Eymard were <a href="/wiki/Servite" class="mw-redirect" title="Servite">Servite</a> priest Anthony Mary Pucci (1819–92) and the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> lay brother <a href="/wiki/Francis_Mary_of_Camporosso" class="mw-redirect" title="Francis Mary of Camporosso">Francis Mary of Camporosso</a> (1804–66). His feast day is celebrated in the Roman Catholic Church on 2 August. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> named Eymard "Apostle of the Eucharist".<sup id="cite_ref-missmag_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-missmag-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-pelletier_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pelletier-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a statue of Eymard in Saint Jean Baptiste Catholic Church in New York City. Below the statue is a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> containing his right <a href="/wiki/Humerus" title="Humerus">humerus</a> bone.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Eymard is recognised as a major contributor to nineteenth-century French spirituality.
</p><p>Catholic churches dedicated to Eymard are located in:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Mooroolbark,_Victoria" title="Mooroolbark, Victoria">Mooroolbark, Victoria, Australia</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Sydney" title="Sydney">Sydney, New South Wales, Australia</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/La_Mure" title="La Mure">La Mure, Isère, France</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Thi%C3%A8s_Region" title="Thiès Region">Koudiadiène, Thiès, Senegal</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/New_Orleans" title="New Orleans">New Orleans, Louisiana, United States</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Holiday,_Florida" title="Holiday, Florida">Holiday, Florida, United States</a></li></ul>
<p>The following landmarks have been named to honor Eymard:
</p>
<ul><li>Rue Julien-Eymard (Julien Eymard Street), located in <a href="/wiki/Shawinigan" title="Shawinigan">Shawinigan, Quebec, Canada</a>;</li>
<li>Eymard Drive (formerly Sunnyside Drive), located along E. Rodriguez St., <a href="/wiki/Quezon_City" title="Quezon City">Quezon City, Philippines</a>, where the provincial house of the Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament, Province of our Lady of the Assumption is located;</li>
<li>Peter Julian Eymard Street, located in Sto. Nino Homes Phase 3-C, Brgy. Perez, <a href="/wiki/Meycauayan_City" class="mw-redirect" title="Meycauayan City">Meycauayan City, Bulacan, Philippines</a>.</li></ul>
