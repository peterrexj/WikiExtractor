<p><b>Henry Walpole</b> (1558 – 7 April 1595) was an <a href="/wiki/English_people" title="English people">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> martyr, executed at York for refusing to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Walpole&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walpole was born at <a href="/wiki/Docking,_Norfolk" title="Docking, Norfolk">Docking, Norfolk</a>, in 1558, the eldest son of Christopher Walpole, by Margery, heiress of Richard Beckham of Narford, and was educated at <a href="/wiki/Norwich_School_(educational_institution)" class="mw-redirect" title="Norwich School (educational institution)">Norwich School</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge" title="Peterhouse, Cambridge">Peterhouse, Cambridge</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gray%27s_Inn" title="Gray&#39;s Inn">Gray's Inn</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> While at Gray's Inn, he came to the attention of government spies by his frequent association with known <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusant</a> gentry.
</p><p>He attended the discussions that <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> held with Anglican divines, and was present at the execution of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> in 1581: his clothes were sprinkled with Campion's blood. Heretofore somewhat lukewarm in religious matters, Walpole then gave up his law practice and followed in Campion's footsteps.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He wrote a small book of poetry honouring Campion which was secretly printed and circulated in London. The authorities sought to discover the parties involved. The printer, a friend of Walpole named Valenger, was fined and suffered the loss of his ears, but did not betray Walpole, who was nonetheless under suspicion. Walpole fled London for his father's home in Norfolk, and from there escaped to France.<sup id="cite_ref-jesuits_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jesuits-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Jesuit">Jesuit</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Walpole&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Jesuit">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He went by way of <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> and Paris, to <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, where he arrived on 7 July 1582.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On 28 April 1583, he was admitted into the English College, Rome, and in October he received minor orders. On 2 February 1584, he became a probationer of the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>, and soon afterwards he returned to France, where he continued his studies, chiefly at the Scots College at Pont-à-Mousson. He was ordained subdeacon and deacon at <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>, and priest at Paris on 17 December 1588. She was then sent to Brussels. Walpole was fluent in Italian, French, Latin, English, and Spanish.
</p><p>After staying in Brussels for a year, he was assigned military chaplain to the English and Irish Catholic refugees serving in the Spanish forces in the Netherlands. He was captured and taken to the English fort at Flushing,<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> where he was tortured before being ransomed by his brother Michael and his Jesuit superiors. He then went to Tournai for his third year of probation, after which he was sent to help with the founding of the new English seminaries at Seville and <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Valladolid" title="English College, Valladolid">Valladolid</a>. In 1593 he travelled to Philip II of Spain to obtain permission to found St. Omers. now Stonyhurst College in Lancashire.
</p><p>Walpole, his youngest brother Thomas, and an English soldier sailed from Dunkirk on a French semi-pirate ship headed for Scotland because the southern ports of England were closed because of the plague. After ten days of stormy seas, they were put ashore at Flamborough Head, Yorkshire, on 4 December 1593, and immediately split up. Walpole was arrested at an inn in <a href="/wiki/Bridlington" title="Bridlington">Bridlington</a>, having been betrayed by a fellow passenger who was earning money to buy his way out of prison. Walpole was imprisoned for the next sixteen months.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Walpole spent about three months at York Castle before priest hunter <a href="/wiki/Richard_Topcliffe" title="Richard Topcliffe">Richard Topcliffe</a> had him transferred to the Tower of London in February 1594.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There Walpole was tortured on the rack and suspended by his wrists for hours, fourteen sessions spaced out so as not to cause his accidental death under interrogation. His father was in failing health, and, as Henry was his heir, the estate would <a href="/wiki/Escheat" title="Escheat">escheat</a> to the crown if Henry were condemned for treason.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG/220px-Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="84" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG/330px-Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG/440px-Henry_Walpole_graffiti.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4250" data-file-height="1614" /></a><figcaption>While incarcerated in the Salt Tower, Jesuit priest Henry Walpole carved his name in the plaster along with those of saints Peter, Paul, Jerome, Ambrose, Augustine, and Gregory the Great.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In the spring of 1595, he was sent back to York for trial, where he was joined by <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Rawlins" title="Alexander Rawlins">Alexander Rawlins</a>, who was also awaiting trial. Both were tried on 3 April on the charge of being Catholic priests. Walpole, a former lawyer, argued that the law only applied to priests who had not given themselves up to officials within three days of arrival. He himself had been arrested less than a day after landing in England, so he had not violated that law. The judges demanded that he take the Oath of Supremacy, acknowledging the queen's complete authority in religion. He refused to do so and was convicted of high treason. Both he and Rawlins were found guilty and condemned, and on 7 April 1595, they were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>. Rawlins died first; Walpole was allowed to hang until he was dead.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commemoration">Commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Henry_Walpole&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walpole was beatified in 1929 and canonized in 1970 as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>. His feast day is celebrated on 7 April.
</p><p>A Spanish account of Walpole's life and martyrdom was brought out by Joseph Creswell as <i>Historia de la Vida y Martyrio que padecio en Inglaterra, este año de .1595. el P. Henrique Valpolo sacerdote de la Compañia de Iesus, que fue embiado del Colegio de los Ingleses de Valladolid, y ha sido el primer martyr de los Seminarios de España. Con el martyrio de otros quatro Sacerdotes: los dos de la misma Compañia, y los otros dos de los Seminarios</i> (En Madrid, en casa de Pedro Madrigal, 1596).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Augustus_Jessopp" title="Augustus Jessopp">Augustus Jessopp</a> wrote a biography of Walpole under the title <i>One generation of a Norfolk house</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and edited his letters, which were printed at Norwich in 1873 under the title <i>Letters of Fa. Henry Walpole, S.J.</i>.
</p><p>St. Henry Walpole Catholic Church, <a href="/wiki/Burnham_Market" title="Burnham Market">Burnham Market</a>, Norfolk is named in his honour.
</p>
