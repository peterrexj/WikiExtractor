<p><b>Andrei Rublev</b> (Russian&#58; <span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Андре́й Рублёв</span></span>, <small>IPA:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="ru-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Russian" title="Help:IPA/Russian">[ɐnˈdrʲej rʊˈblʲɵf]</a></span> <span class="unicode haudio"><span class="fn"><span style="white-space:nowrap;margin-right:.25em;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ru-Andrey_Rublev.ogg" title="About this sound"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/11px-Loudspeaker.svg.png" decoding="async" width="11" height="11" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/17px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/22px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20" /></a></span><a href="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/21/Ru-Andrey_Rublev.ogg" class="internal" title="Ru-Andrey Rublev.ogg">listen</a></span>&#160;<small class="metadata audiolinkinfo" style="cursor:help;">(<a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Media_help" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:Media help"><span style="cursor:help;">help</span></a>·<a href="/wiki/File:Ru-Andrey_Rublev.ogg" title="File:Ru-Andrey Rublev.ogg"><span style="cursor:help;">info</span></a>)</small></span>, also transliterated as <i>Andrey Rublyov</i>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow" title="Grand Duchy of Moscow">Muscovite</a> <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon painter</a> born in the 1360s who died between 1427 and 1430<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>. He is considered to be one of the greatest <a href="/wiki/Medieval_art" title="Medieval art">medieval</a> <a href="/wiki/Russian_culture#Visual_arts" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian culture">Russian</a> <a href="/wiki/Painting" title="Painting">painters</a> of <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> Christian <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icons</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">frescos</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrei_Rublev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little information survives about his life; even where he was born is unknown. He probably lived in the <a href="/wiki/Trinity-St._Sergius_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra">Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>, under Nikon of <a href="/wiki/Radonezh" title="Radonezh">Radonezh</a>, who became <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a> after the death of <a href="/wiki/Sergius_of_Radonezh" title="Sergius of Radonezh">Sergius of Radonezh</a> in 1392. The first mention of Rublev is in 1405, when he decorated <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icons</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">frescos</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_the_Annunciation,_Moscow" title="Cathedral of the Annunciation, Moscow">Cathedral of the Annunciation</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Moscow_Kremlin" class="mw-redirect" title="Moscow Kremlin">Moscow Kremlin</a>, in company with <a href="/wiki/Theophanes_the_Greek" title="Theophanes the Greek">Theophanes the Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prokhor_of_Gorodets" title="Prokhor of Gorodets">Prokhor of Gorodets</a>. His name was the last of the list of masters, as the junior both by rank and by age. Theophanes was an important <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_art" title="Byzantine art">Byzantine</a> master, who moved to Russia and is considered to have trained Rublev.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrei_Rublev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chronicles tell us that together with <a href="/wiki/Daniel_Chorny" title="Daniel Chorny">Daniel Chorny</a> he painted the <a href="/wiki/Dormition_Cathedral,_Vladimir" title="Dormition Cathedral, Vladimir">Assumption Cathedral in Vladimir</a> in 1408 as well as the <a href="/wiki/Old_Katholikon_of_the_Trinity_Lavra" title="Old Katholikon of the Trinity Lavra">Trinity Cathedral</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Trinity_Lavra_of_St._Sergius" title="Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius">Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius</a> between 1425 and 1427. After Daniel's death, Andrei came to Moscow's <a href="/wiki/Andronikov_Monastery" title="Andronikov Monastery">Andronikov Monastery</a> where he painted his last work, the frescoes of the Saviour Cathedral. He is also believed to have painted at least one of the <a href="/wiki/Miniature_(illuminated_manuscript)" title="Miniature (illuminated manuscript)">miniatures</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Khitrovo_Gospels" title="Khitrovo Gospels">Khitrovo Gospels</a>.
</p><p>The only work authenticated as entirely his is the icon of the <a href="/wiki/Trinity_(Andrei_Rublev)" title="Trinity (Andrei Rublev)">Trinity</a> (c. 1410, currently in the <a href="/wiki/Tretyakov_Gallery" title="Tretyakov Gallery">Tretyakov Gallery</a>, <a href="/wiki/Moscow" title="Moscow">Moscow</a>). It is based on an earlier icon known as the "Hospitality of Abraham" (illustrating <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0118.htm#1">Genesis 18</a>). Rublev removed the figures of <a href="/wiki/Abraham" title="Abraham">Abraham</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sarah" title="Sarah">Sarah</a> from the scene, and through a subtle use of composition and symbolism changed the subject to focus on the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Mysteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred Mysteries">Mystery</a> of the Trinity.
</p><p>In Rublev's art two traditions are combined: the highest <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> and the classic harmony of <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_art" title="Byzantine art">Byzantine</a> mannerism. The characters of his paintings are always peaceful and calm. After some time his art came to be perceived as the ideal of Eastern Church painting and of Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrei_Rublev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rublev died at Andronikov Monastery between 1427 and 1430. Rublev's work influenced many artists including <a href="/wiki/Dionisy" class="mw-redirect" title="Dionisy">Dionisy</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Stoglavi_Sobor" class="mw-redirect" title="Stoglavi Sobor">Stoglavi Sobor</a> (1551) promulgated Rublev's icon style as a model for church painting. Since 1959 the Andrei Rublev Museum at the <a href="/wiki/Andronikov_Monastery" title="Andronikov Monastery">Andronikov Monastery</a> has displayed his and related art.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Rublev as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in 1988, celebrating his <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> on 29 January<sup id="cite_ref-SHC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and/or on 4 July.<sup id="cite_ref-SHC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1966, <a href="/wiki/Andrei_Tarkovsky" title="Andrei Tarkovsky">Andrei Tarkovsky</a> made a film <i><a href="/wiki/Andrei_Rublev_(film)" title="Andrei Rublev (film)">Andrei Rublev</a></i>, loosely based on the artist's life. This became the first (and perhaps only) film produced in the Soviet era to treat the artist as a world-historic figure and Christianity as an axiom of Russia's historical identity,<sup id="cite_ref-jhoberman_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jhoberman-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> during a <a href="/wiki/List_of_conflicts_in_Eastern_Europe_during_Turco-Mongol_rule" title="List of conflicts in Eastern Europe during Turco-Mongol rule">turbulent period</a> in the <a href="/wiki/History_of_Russia" title="History of Russia">history of Russia</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrei_Rublev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>29 January – commemoration of his death anniversary (<a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox Church</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>12/13 June – feast day, Synaxis of All of <a href="/wiki/Andronikov_Monastery" title="Andronikov Monastery">Andronikov Monastery</a> (with Andronicus, Sabbas, Alexander, Abbots of Moscow and <a href="/wiki/Daniel_Chorny" title="Daniel Chorny">Daniel the Black</a>, the icon painter)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>4 July – main feast day from the list of "Russian saints of Moscow and Vladimir" by Nikodim (Kononov),</li>
<li>6 July – Synaxis of All Saints of Radonezh</li>
<li><b>Synaxis of all saints of Moscow</b> – movable holiday on the Sunday before 26 August (<a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">ROC</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Selected_works">Selected works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrei_Rublev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Selected works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
