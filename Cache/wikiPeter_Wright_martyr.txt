<p><b>Peter Wright</b> (1603 &#8211; 19 May 1651) was an English <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> priest and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who has been beatified by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wright_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter Wright was born in <a href="/wiki/Slipton" title="Slipton">Slipton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Northamptonshire" title="Northamptonshire">Northamptonshire</a>, one of twelve children. Peter was still young when his father died. He had to work in a country solicitor's office at <a href="/wiki/Thrapston" title="Thrapston">Thrapston</a> in his home area. After spending ten years with the solicitor he enlisted in the English army in the <a href="/wiki/Low_Countries" title="Low Countries">Low Countries</a> in 1627 or 1628, but finding that he did not care for military life, he deserted after a month and went to <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant" title="Duchy of Brabant">Brabant</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-jesuits_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jesuits-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wright_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Having drifted away from his <a href="/wiki/Christian_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian faith">faith</a> in his youth, he visited the English <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> in <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a> and asked to be reconciled to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Church</a>. He then visited <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> and for two years attended the college of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a>. In 1629 he entered the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> novitiate at <a href="/wiki/Watten,_Nord" title="Watten, Nord">Watten</a>. After studying <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> and then <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at Liège, he was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">priest</a> there in 1639<sup id="cite_ref-jesuits_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jesuits-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and after a further period at Liège was sent to serve at the English <a href="/wiki/College_of_St._Omer" class="mw-redirect" title="College of St. Omer">College of St. Omer</a>. Having no aptitude in supervising young boys, he was then sent to serve as chaplain to Colonel Sir <a href="/wiki/Henry_Gage_(soldier)" title="Henry Gage (soldier)">Henry Gage</a>'s English regiment in the service of <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, based near <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest">Arrest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wright_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Arrest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When Gage returned to England in the spring of 1644 to aid King <a href="/wiki/Charles_I_of_England" title="Charles I of England">Charles I</a>, Wright went with him, first to <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> and then to the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Basing_House" title="Siege of Basing House">relief of Basing House</a>, the seat of <a href="/wiki/John_Paulet,_5th_Marquess_of_Winchester" title="John Paulet, 5th Marquess of Winchester">John Paulet, 5th Marquess of Winchester</a>. He administered the sacraments to the dying Gage on 11 January 1645. After this Wright became the marquess's chaplain, first in Hampshire and later in the <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> house. Wright was seized there by a <a href="/wiki/List_of_Roman_Catholic_handed_over_to_the_judiciary_in_the_London_area_during_the_1640s" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Roman Catholic handed over to the judiciary in the London area during the 1640s">band of pursuivants</a> who burst in on <a href="/wiki/Candlemas" title="Candlemas">Candlemas</a> day, 2 February 1650, as he was about to say Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-Bassett_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bassett-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Trial">Trial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wright_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Trial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Committed to <a href="/wiki/Newgate" title="Newgate">Newgate</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he was brought to trial before <a href="/wiki/Henry_Rolle" title="Henry Rolle">Henry Rolle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chief_Justice" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chief Justice">Lord Chief Justice</a>, sitting with justices Philip Jermyn and Richard Aske and others, at the Old Bailey on 14–16 May. Something of the atmosphere of the times should be clear when it is recalled that Charles I had been put on trial and subsequently been executed on 30 January 1649. The evidence at Wright's trial was provided by the informer <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Gage_(clergyman)" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Gage (clergyman)">Thomas Gage</a>, apostate brother of the late Sir Henry and a renegade <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> priest. Thomas Gage had met Wright in the years when he was a military chaplain and testified against him.<sup id="cite_ref-jesuits_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jesuits-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Wright was condemned under the statute 27 Eliz., c. 2. for being a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> in England, and sentenced on Saturday 17 May to being <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Execution">Execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Wright_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His execution at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn, London</a> on a hot Whit Monday, 19 May 1651, took place before over twenty thousand spectators,<sup id="cite_ref-Singpore_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Singpore-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> including a number of Jesuits in disguise, on hand to give their friend absolution.<sup id="cite_ref-Bassett_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bassett-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In the period of the trial and the days after his execution, Wright was if not popular, at least a respected figure in public opinion. The sheriff's officers also seem to have been relatively well disposed to him and he was allowed to hang until he was dead, being thus spared the agonies of being eviscerated alive. His friends were allowed to take his body away and later it was taken to the Jesuit college in Liege where it was buried.<sup id="cite_ref-Singpore_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Singpore-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter Wright was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_XI" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius XI">Pius XI</a> on 15 December 1929. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 19 May.
</p>
