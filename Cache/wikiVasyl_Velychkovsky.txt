<p><b>Vasyl Vsevolod Velychkovsky</b> (June 1, 1903 &#8211; June 30, 1973) was a priest, and later <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>, of the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church">Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a> in <a href="/wiki/Communion_with_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Communion with Rome">communion with Rome</a>.  He is a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, dying in 1973 of his injuries sustained while imprisoned by the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet Union</a> for his <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> faith.
</p><p>Velychkovsky was born in <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk" title="Ivano-Frankivsk">Stanislaviv</a>, in then-<a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austria-Hungary</a>. In 1920 he entered the seminary in <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>, then in Poland. In 1925 he took his first religious vows in the village of Holosko near Lviv in the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Most_Holy_Redeemer" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer">Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer</a> (better known as the Redemptorists) and was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>. As a priest-monk Vasyl Velychkovsky taught and preached in <a href="/wiki/Volyn" class="mw-redirect" title="Volyn">Volyn</a>, a Ukrainian-majority province in interwar Poland. In 1942 he became <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the monastery in German-occupied <a href="/wiki/Ternopil" title="Ternopil">Ternopil</a>. Because of <a href="/wiki/Religious_persecution" title="Religious persecution">religious persecution</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Communist" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist">Communist</a> Soviet Union he was arrested in 1945 by the <a href="/wiki/NKVD" title="NKVD">NKVD</a> and sent to <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Execution" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution">punishment of death</a> was commuted to 10 years of <a href="/wiki/Penal_labour" title="Penal labour">hard labor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ugcc_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ugcc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On release in 1955 he went back to Lviv, and was ordained a bishop in 1963. In 1969 he was imprisoned again for three years for his religious activities.<sup id="cite_ref-ugcc_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ugcc-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Released in 1972, he was <a href="/wiki/Exile" title="Exile">exiled</a> outside the USSR. He died of his injuries from prison in <a href="/wiki/Winnipeg,_Manitoba" class="mw-redirect" title="Winnipeg, Manitoba">Winnipeg, Manitoba</a>, Canada on June 30, 1973, aged 70.<sup id="cite_ref-shrine_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrine-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thirty years after his death, Vasyl Velychkovsky's body was found to be almost <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a> (his toes had fallen off and were subsequently divided to be used as holy relics).<sup id="cite_ref-shrine_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrine-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a> in 2001, the intact remains of Vasyl Velychkovsky are enshrined at <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Joseph%27s_Ukrainian_Catholic_Church&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Joseph&#39;s Ukrainian Catholic Church (page does not exist)">St. Joseph's Ukrainian Catholic Church</a> in Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada.  Today, his shrine is located at 250 Jefferson Avenue, Winnipeg, Manitoba.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
