<p><b>Saint Maurice</b> (also <b>Moritz</b>, <b>Morris</b>, or <b>Mauritius</b>; <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop"><b>Ⲁⲃⲃⲁ Ⲙⲱⲣⲓⲥ</b></span>) was an <a href="/wiki/Egyptians" title="Egyptians">Egyptian</a> military leader who headed the legendary <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Rome</a> in the 3rd century, and is one of the favorite and most widely venerated saints of that <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> group. He is the patron saint of several professions, locales, and kingdoms.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographical</a> material, Maurice was an Egyptian, born in AD 250 in <a href="/wiki/Thebes,_Egypt" title="Thebes, Egypt">Thebes</a>, an ancient city in <a href="/wiki/Upper_Egypt" title="Upper Egypt">Upper Egypt</a> that was the capital of the <a href="/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt" title="New Kingdom of Egypt">New Kingdom of Egypt</a> (1575-1069 BC). He was brought up in the region of Thebes (<a href="/wiki/Luxor" title="Luxor">Luxor</a>).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Maurice became a soldier in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a>. He rose through the ranks until he became the commander of the Theban legion, thus leading approximately a thousand men. He was an acknowledged Christian at a time when <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christianity">early Christianity</a> was considered to be a threat to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>. Yet, he moved easily within the <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> society of his day.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The legion, entirely composed of Christians, had been called from Thebes in Egypt to <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> to assist Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> in defeating a revolt by the <a href="/wiki/Bagaudae" title="Bagaudae">bagaudae</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Theban Legion was dispatched with orders to clear the <a href="/wiki/Great_St_Bernard_Pass" title="Great St Bernard Pass">Great St Bernard Pass</a> across the <a href="/wiki/Alps" title="Alps">Alps</a>. Before going into battle, they were instructed to offer sacrifices to the pagan gods and pay homage to the emperor. Maurice pledged his men's military allegiance to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. He stated that service to God superseded all else. He said that to engage in wanton slaughter was inconceivable to Christian soldiers. He and his men refused to worship <a href="/wiki/List_of_Roman_deities" title="List of Roman deities">Roman deities</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dania_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dania-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>However, when Maximian ordered them to harass some local Christians, they refused. Ordering the unit to be punished, Maximian had every tenth soldier killed, a military punishment known as <a href="/wiki/Decimation_(Roman_army)" class="mw-redirect" title="Decimation (Roman army)">decimation</a>. More orders followed, the men refused compliance as encouraged by Maurice, and a second decimation was ordered. In response to the Theban Christians' refusal to attack fellow Christians, Maximian ordered all the remaining members of his legion to be executed. The place in <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a> where this occurred, known as <a href="/wiki/Agaunum" title="Agaunum">Agaunum</a>, is now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maurice,_Switzerland" title="Saint-Maurice, Switzerland">Saint-Maurice, Switzerland</a>, site of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Maurice,_Agaunum" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Maurice, Agaunum">Abbey of St. Maurice</a>.
</p><p>So reads the earliest account of their <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a>, contained in the public letter which Bishop <a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyon" title="Eucherius of Lyon">Eucherius of Lyon</a> (c. 434–450), addressed to his fellow bishop, Salvius. Alternative versions<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2008)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> have the <a href="/wiki/Roman_legion" title="Roman legion">legion</a> refusing Maximian's orders only after discovering innocent Christians had inhabited a town they had just destroyed, or that the emperor had them executed when they refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Maurice became a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the German <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperors</a>. In 926, <a href="/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler" title="Henry the Fowler">Henry the Fowler</a> (919–936), even ceded the present <a href="/wiki/Swiss_canton" class="mw-redirect" title="Swiss canton">Swiss canton</a> of <a href="/wiki/Aargau" title="Aargau">Aargau</a> to the abbey, in return for Maurice's lance, sword and spurs. The <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Sword" title="Imperial Sword">sword</a> and spurs of Saint Maurice were part of the regalia used at coronations of the <a href="/wiki/Austro-Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Austro-Hungary">Austro-Hungarian</a> emperors until 1916, and among the most important insignia of the imperial throne (although the actual sword dates from the 12th Century). In addition, some of the emperors were anointed before the Altar of Saint Maurice at <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 929, Henry the Fowler held a royal court gathering (<i>Reichsversammlung</i>) at <a href="/wiki/Magdeburg" title="Magdeburg">Magdeburg</a>. At the same time the Mauritius Kloster in honor of Maurice was founded. In 961, <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, was building and enriching <a href="/wiki/Magdeburg_Cathedral" title="Magdeburg Cathedral">Magdeburg Cathedral</a>, which he intended for his own tomb. To that end,
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>in the year 961 of the Incarnation and in the 25th year of his reign, in the presence of all of the nobility, on the <a href="/wiki/Christmas_Eve" title="Christmas Eve">vigil of Christmas</a>, the body of St. Maurice was conveyed to him at Regensburg along with the bodies of some of the saint's companions and portions of other saints. Having been sent to Magdeburg, these relics were received with great honour by a gathering of the entire populace of the city and of their fellow countrymen. They are still venerated there, to the salvation of the homeland.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Maurice is traditionally depicted in full armor, in Italy emblazoned with a red cross. In folk culture he has become connected with the legend of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Lance" title="Holy Lance">Holy Lance</a>, which he is supposed to have carried into battle; his name is engraved on the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Lance" title="Holy Lance">Holy Lance</a> of <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, one of several relics claimed as the spear that pierced <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>' side on the cross. Saint Maurice gives his name to the town <a href="/wiki/St._Moritz" title="St. Moritz">St. Moritz</a> as well as to numerous places called <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maurice_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint-Maurice (disambiguation)">Saint-Maurice</a> in French speaking countries. The <a href="/wiki/Indian_Ocean" title="Indian Ocean">Indian Ocean</a> island state of <a href="/wiki/Mauritius" title="Mauritius">Mauritius</a> was named after <a href="/wiki/Maurice,_Prince_of_Orange" title="Maurice, Prince of Orange">Maurice, Prince of Orange</a>, and not directly after Maurice himself.
</p><p>Over 650 religious foundations dedicated to Saint Maurice can be found in France and other European countries. In Switzerland alone, seven churches or altars in <a href="/wiki/Aargau" title="Aargau">Aargau</a>, six in the <a href="/wiki/Canton_of_Lucerne" title="Canton of Lucerne">Canton of Lucerne</a>, four in the <a href="/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn" title="Canton of Solothurn">Canton of Solothurn</a>, and one in <a href="/wiki/Appenzell_Innerrhoden" title="Appenzell Innerrhoden">Appenzell Innerrhoden</a> can be found (in fact, his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is a cantonal <a href="/wiki/Holiday" title="Holiday">holiday</a> in Appenzell Innerrhoden).<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Particularly notable among these are the Church and Abbey of Saint-Maurice-en-Valais, the Church of Saint Moritz in the <a href="/wiki/Engadin" title="Engadin">Engadin</a>, and the Monastery Chapel of <a href="/wiki/Einsiedeln_Abbey" title="Einsiedeln Abbey">Einsiedeln Abbey</a>, where his name continues to be greatly revered. Several <a href="/wiki/Order_of_chivalry" title="Order of chivalry">orders of chivalry</a> were established in his honor as well, including the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Golden_Fleece" title="Order of the Golden Fleece">Order of the Golden Fleece</a>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saints_Maurice_and_Lazarus" title="Order of Saints Maurice and Lazarus">Order of Saints Maurice and Lazarus</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Maurice" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Maurice">Order of Saint Maurice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Additionally, fifty-two towns and villages in France have been named in his honor.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler&#39;s_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler&#39;s-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maurice was also the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of a Catholic parish and church in the <a href="/wiki/9th_Ward_of_New_Orleans" title="9th Ward of New Orleans">9th Ward of New Orleans</a> and including part of the town of <a href="/wiki/Arabi,_Louisiana" title="Arabi, Louisiana">Arabi</a> in <a href="/wiki/St._Bernard_Parish,_Louisiana" title="St. Bernard Parish, Louisiana">St. Bernard Parish</a>. The church was constructed in 1856, but was devastated by the winds and flood waters of Hurricane Katrina on 29 August 2005; the copper-plated steeple was blown off the building. The church was subsequently deconsecrated in 2008, and the local diocese put it up for sale in 2011.<sup id="cite_ref-nola2014_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nola2014-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-nola2011_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nola2011-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> By 2014, a local attorney had purchased the property for a local arts organization, after which the building served as both an arts venue and the worship space for a Baptist church that had been displaced following the hurricane.<sup id="cite_ref-nola2014_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nola2014-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-nola2015_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nola2015-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 19 July 1941, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> declared Saint Maurice to be patron Saint of the <a href="/wiki/Italian_Army" title="Italian Army">Italian Army</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Alpini" title="Alpini">Alpini</a> (mountain infantry corps).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The Alpini have celebrated Maurice's feast every year since then.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">Synaxarium</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria</a> does not mention Saint Maurice, although there are several Coptic churches named for him.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Apparition">Apparition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Apparition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Laus" title="Our Lady of Laus">Our Lady of Laus</a> apparitions included an apparition of Saint Maurice.  He appeared in an antique episcopal vestment and told <a href="/wiki/Beno%C3%AEte_Rencurel" title="Benoîte Rencurel">Benoîte Rencurel</a> that he was the one to whom the nearby chapel was dedicated, that he would fetch her some water (before drawing some water out of a well she had not seen), that she should go down to a certain valley to escape the local guard and see the Blessed Virgin <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Mary, mother of Jesus</a>, and that Mary was both <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">in Heaven</a> and could <a href="/wiki/Marian_apparition" title="Marian apparition">appear on Earth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-magnificat_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnificat-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Maurice is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Savoy" title="Duchy of Savoy">Duchy of Savoy</a> (France) and of the Valais (Switzerland) as well as of soldiers, swordsmiths, armies, and infantrymen. In 1591 <a href="/wiki/Charles_Emmanuel_I,_Duke_of_Savoy" title="Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy">Charles Emmanuel I, Duke of Savoy</a> arranged the triumphant return of part of the relics of Saint Maurice from the monastery of Agaune in Valais.<sup id="cite_ref-Merle_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Merle-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is also the patron saint of weavers and dyers. <a href="/wiki/Manresa" title="Manresa">Manresa</a> (Spain), <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> (Italy), <a href="/wiki/Montalbano_Jonico" title="Montalbano Jonico">Montalbano Jonico</a> (Italy), <a href="/wiki/Schiavi_di_Abruzzo" title="Schiavi di Abruzzo">Schiavi di Abruzzo</a> (Italy), <a href="/wiki/Bad_Sulza" title="Bad Sulza">Stadtsulza</a> (Germany) and <a href="/wiki/Coburg" title="Coburg">Coburg</a> (Germany) have chosen St. Maurice as their patron saint as well. St Maurice is also the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Brotherhood_of_Blackheads" title="Brotherhood of Blackheads">Brotherhood of Blackheads</a>, a historical military order of unmarried merchants in present-day <a href="/wiki/Estonia" title="Estonia">Estonia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Latvia" title="Latvia">Latvia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In September 2008, certain relics of Maurice were transferred to a new <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> and rededicated in <a href="/wiki/Schiavi_di_Abruzzo" title="Schiavi di Abruzzo">Schiavi di Abruzzo</a> (Italy).
</p><p>He is also the patron saint of the town of <a href="/wiki/Coburg" title="Coburg">Coburg</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>. He is shown there as a man of colour especially on <a href="/wiki/Manhole_cover" title="Manhole cover">manhole covers</a> as well as on the city coat of arms. There he is called "Coburger Mohr" (engl.: "Coburg Moor").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Portrayal">Portrayal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Portrayal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The earliest surviving work portraying St. Maurice as a dark-complexioned African dates from the 12th century.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The oldest surviving image that depicts Saint Maurice as a <a href="/wiki/Black_African" class="mw-redirect" title="Black African">black African</a> in knight's armor<sup id="cite_ref-Robert_Suckale_page_19_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robert_Suckale_page_19-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> was sculpted in mid-13th century for Magdeburg Cathedral; there it is displayed next to the grave of Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor. Jean Devisse, <i>The Image of the Black in Western Art</i>, laid out the documentary sources for the saint's popularity and documented it with illustrative examples.<sup id="cite_ref-JSOTR_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JSOTR-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-JNH_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JNH-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the new cathedral was built under Archbishop Albert II of Käfernberg (served 1205–32), a relic said to be the head of Maurice was procured from the Holy Land.
</p><p>The image of Saint Maurice has been examined in detail by Gude Suckale-Redlefsen,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> who demonstrated that this image of Maurice has existed since Maurice's first depiction in Germany between the <a href="/wiki/Weser" title="Weser">Weser</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Elbe" title="Elbe">Elbe</a>, and spread to <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>, where it became associated with the imperial ambitions of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Luxembourg" title="House of Luxembourg">House of Luxembourg</a>. According to Suckale-Redlefsen, the image of Maurice reached its apogee during the years 1490 to 1530.
</p><p>Images of the saint died out in the mid-sixteenth century, undermined, Suckale-Redlefsen suggests, by the developing <a href="/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade" title="Atlantic slave trade">Atlantic slave trade</a>. "Once again, as in the early Middle Ages, the color black had become associated with spiritual darkness and cultural 'otherness'".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
There is an oil on wood painting of Maurice by <a href="/wiki/Lucas_Cranach_the_Elder" title="Lucas Cranach the Elder">Lucas Cranach the Elder</a> (1472–1553) in the New York Metropolitan Museum of Art.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The city of Coburg's <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> honoured the town's patron saint Saint Maurice, since they were granted in 1493. In 1934, the Nazi government forbade any glorification of the "Black" race, and they replaced the coat of arms with one depicting a vertical sword with a Nazi <a href="/wiki/Swastika" title="Swastika">swastika</a> on the pommel.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The original coat of arms was restored in 1945 at the end of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>. Today, the silhouette of Saint Maurice can be found mainly on manhole covers as well as the city coat of arms.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maurice&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a></div>
<p>There is a difference of opinion among researchers as to whether or not the story of the Theban Legion is based on historical fact, and if so, to what extent. The account by <a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyon" title="Eucherius of Lyon">Eucherius of Lyon</a> is classed by <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> among the historical romances.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Donald F. O'Reilly, in <i>Lost Legion Rediscovered</i>, argues that evidence from <a href="/wiki/Coin" title="Coin">coins</a>, <a href="/wiki/Papyrus" title="Papyrus">papyrus</a>, and Roman army lists support the story of the Theban Legion.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Denis Van Berchem, of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Geneva" title="University of Geneva">University of Geneva</a>, proposed that Eucherius' presentation of the legend of the Theban legion was a literary production, not based on a local tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The monastic accounts themselves do not specifically state that all the soldiers were collectively executed;  the twelfth century bishop <a href="/wiki/Otto_of_Freising" title="Otto of Freising">Otto of Freising</a> wrote in his <i><a href="/wiki/Otto_of_Freising#Chronica_de_duabus_civitatibus" title="Otto of Freising">Chronica de duabus civitatibus</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> that many of the legionaries escaped and only some were executed at Agaunum, though the others were later apprehended and put to death at <a href="/wiki/Bonn" title="Bonn">Galliae Bonna</a> and <a href="/wiki/K%C3%B6ln" class="mw-redirect" title="Köln">Colonia Aggripina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Donald2_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donald2-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
