<p>

<b>Thomas Marshall (the Blessed John Beche)</b>, also known as <b>John Beche</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-Ashdown_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ashdown-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (died 1 December 1539) was the last Abbot of <a href="/wiki/Colchester_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Colchester Abbey">Colchester Abbey</a>. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Marshall_(Abbot_of_Colchester)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Marshall, commonly known as John Beche, was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Colchester" title="Colchester">Colchester</a> Beche family, who were a dynasty of renowned <a href="/wiki/Pewtersmith" class="mw-redirect" title="Pewtersmith">pewtersmiths</a> in the town.<sup id="cite_ref-Ashdown_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ashdown-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was educated at <a href="/wiki/Oxford_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford University">Oxford University</a> (probably <a href="/wiki/Gloucester_Hall" class="mw-redirect" title="Gloucester Hall">Gloucester Hall</a> now <a href="/wiki/Worcester_College" class="mw-redirect" title="Worcester College">Worcester College</a>) where he took his degree of Doctor of Divinity in 1515. He then became the twenty-sixth <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_St._Werburgh%27s" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of St. Werburgh&#39;s">Abbot of St. Werburgh's</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chester" title="Chester">Chester</a> (now Chester Cathedral) and went on to become Abbot of St. John the Baptist's, Colchester on 10 June 1530. 
</p><p>On 30 March 1534, Abbot Beche took his seat in the House of Lords. In that year, the <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Supremacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Act of Supremacy">Act of Supremacy</a> was passed by which Henry VIII made himself Head of the <a href="/wiki/Church_in_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Church in England">Church in England</a>, and on 7 July he, the Prior, and the community of 14, signed their agreement to the Act. Many clergymen considered the break with Rome of a temporary nature, and that it was possible to distinguish between the king as head of the Church in temporal matters, though not in matters spiritual.<sup id="cite_ref-fitzpatrick_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fitzpatrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Abbot was a strong opponent of the King's new policy, and a friend and admirer of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More" title="Thomas More">Thomas More</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Fisher" title="John Fisher">John Fisher</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Following the execution of three Carthusian priors, Fisher and More during 1535, his expressions of reverence for them was reported to the authorities.<sup id="cite_ref-macpherson_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macpherson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In November, 1538, Beche denied the legal right of Henry VIII's royal commission to confiscate his abbey. He was then committed to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> on a charge of <a href="/wiki/Treason" title="Treason">treason</a>; despite being discharged, he was rearrested and taken back to Colchester.
</p><p>The Abbot's servant said that his master denied that the king could suppress the Abbey because it was above the yearly value of 300 marks specified in the statute.<sup id="cite_ref-fitzpatrick_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fitzpatrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Other witnesses testified that Beche had said that God would "take vengeance for the putting down of these houses of religion", that Fisher and More "died like good men and it was pity of their deaths", and he claimed that the king had broken with the Catholic Church because he wanted to marry <a href="/wiki/Anne_Boleyn" title="Anne Boleyn">Anne Boleyn</a>. Beche denied these charges but at his trial in Colchester, in November, 1539, he no longer pleaded against the charges. He was convicted and executed.<sup id="cite_ref-macpherson_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macpherson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The execution occurred on the Abbot's <i>demesne</i> lands, probably at the Abbey's gallows at Greenstead.<sup id="cite_ref-Ashdown_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ashdown-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Pectoral_cross" title="Pectoral cross">pectoral cross</a> was rescued by the <a href="/wiki/Mannock_baronets" title="Mannock baronets">Mannock family</a> of <a href="/wiki/Giffords_Hall,_Stoke-by-Nayland" title="Giffords Hall, Stoke-by-Nayland">Giffords Hall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stoke-by-Nayland" title="Stoke-by-Nayland">Stoke-by-Nayland</a>, who entrusted it to <a href="/wiki/Buckfast_Abbey" title="Buckfast Abbey">Buckfast Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>, where it still remains.<sup id="cite_ref-Ashdown_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ashdown-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Marshall_(Abbot_of_Colchester)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a> decreed the beatification of Abbot John Beche on 13 May 1895.<sup id="cite_ref-macpherson_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macpherson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His feast is kept on 1 December in the diocese of Brentwood.<sup id="cite_ref-fitzpatrick_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fitzpatrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
