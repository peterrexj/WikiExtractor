<p><b>Saint Publius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Maltese_language" title="Maltese language">Maltese</a>: <i lang="mt">San Publju</i>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Πούπλιος</span></span>) is a first century <a href="/wiki/Maltese_people" title="Maltese people">Maltese</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. He is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as the first <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Malta" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Malta">Bishop of Malta</a> and one of the first <a href="/wiki/List_of_archbishops_of_Athens" title="List of archbishops of Athens">Bishops of Athens</a>. 
</p><p>Publius is <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a>'s first acknowledged <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, the prince of the island (<a href="/wiki/Maltese_language" title="Maltese language">Maltese</a>: <i lang="mt">il-prinċep tal-gżira</i>). According to Maltese <a href="/wiki/Christian_tradition" title="Christian tradition">Christian tradition</a>, Publius' conversion led to <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a> being the first <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> nation in the <a href="/wiki/West" title="West">West</a>. His feast day is celebrated by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>, of which the traditions related and the day of celebration differ.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History_and_tradition">History and tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Publius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History and tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Christian_tradition" title="Christian tradition">Christian tradition</a>, it was Publius who received <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> during his <a href="/wiki/Shipwreck" title="Shipwreck">shipwreck</a> on the island as recounted in the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a>. According to the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a>, Paul cured Publius' <a href="/wiki/Dysentery" title="Dysentery">dysentery</a>-afflicted father.
</p>
<dl><dd><i>"In the vicinity of that place were lands belonging to a man named Publius, the chief of the island. He welcomed us and received us cordially as his guests for three days. It so happened that the father of Publius was sick with a fever and dysentery. Paul visited him and, after praying, laid his hands on him and healed him. After this had taken place, the rest of the sick on the island came to Paul and were cured. They paid us great honor and when we eventually set sail they brought us the provisions we needed."</i> — Acts 28:7–10, New American Bible</dd></dl>
<p>Apart from being <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Floriana" title="Floriana">Floriana</a>, Publius is also one of the two patron saints of Malta beside Paul. He was martyred c. 125,<sup id="cite_ref-CO_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> during the persecution of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a>, and canonised in the year 1634.
</p><p>His feast is celebrated on January 22, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, which places him as the successor of <a href="/wiki/Dionysius_the_Areopagite" title="Dionysius the Areopagite">Dionysius the Areopagite</a> (Denis the Areopagite), dating his martyrdom to c. 112 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;note 2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, however, his feast day is observed on <a href="/wiki/March_13_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="March 13 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">March 13</a>, and according to an epistle of Saint <a href="/wiki/Dionysius,_Bishop_of_Corinth" class="mw-redirect" title="Dionysius, Bishop of Corinth">Dionysius, Bishop of Corinth</a>, he is placed as the successor of Saint <a href="/wiki/Narcissus_of_Athens" title="Narcissus of Athens">Narcissus of Athens</a>, dating his martyrdom to the period of the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_early_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Persecution of early Christians in the Roman Empire">persecution under Marcus Aurelius</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;161–180</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-synax_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-synax-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
