<p><b>Benedict</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Benedictus</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Benedetto</i>) was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> from c. 685 to c. 732. He is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_(bishop_of_Milan)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benedict was archbishop of Milan from c. 685 to c. 732. Among the few information about his life, it is known that he wrote the <a href="/wiki/Epitaph" title="Epitaph">epitaph</a> for <a href="/wiki/Caedwalla" class="mw-redirect" title="Caedwalla">Caedwalla</a>, the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Wessex">king of Wessex</a> who was buried in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DCA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benedict returned in Rome at the time of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Constantine" title="Pope Constantine">Pope Constantine</a> where he opposed the claim of the bishop of Pavia, <a href="/wiki/Armentarius_of_Pavia" title="Armentarius of Pavia">Armentarius</a>, that Pavia were immediately subject to Rome. Benedict reminded to the Pope that Pavia historically was under the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_See" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan See">Metropolitan See</a> of Milan and that the bishop of Pavia was used to be consecrated by the one of Milan. The pope however ruled in favour of Armentarius, perhaps due to a privilege granted at the times of <a href="/wiki/Evodius" title="Evodius">Evodius</a> or because Pavia was the capital of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards" title="Kingdom of the Lombards">Kingdom of the Lombards</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BS_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To Benedict was due the construction of a church and a monastery in Milan, of which no trace remains. He probably was 
influential in settling the <a href="/wiki/Schism_of_the_Three_Chapters" title="Schism of the Three Chapters">Schism of the Three Chapters</a>. <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Deacon" title="Paul the Deacon">Paul the Deacon</a>, about fifty years after his death, remember him as a saint venerated all over Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-DCA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The day of his death was between 9 and 11 March 732.<sup id="cite_ref-DCA_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A poetry written about ten years after his death, <i>De laudibus Mediolani</i>, praises him and informs us that he was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Ambrogio" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Ambrogio">Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is 11 March in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> and 6 September in the <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite" title="Ambrosian Rite">Ambrosian Rite</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A late tradition, with no historical basis, associates Benedict with the Milan's family of the Crespi.
</p>
