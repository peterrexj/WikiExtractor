<p><b>Saint Godelieve</b> (also known as <b>Godeleva</b>, <b>Godeliève</b>, and <b>Godelina</b>; <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>: <i lang="nl">Sint-Godelieve</i>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1052<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>&#160;&#8211;&#32;6 July 1070) is a <a href="/wiki/Flemish_people" title="Flemish people">Flemish</a> saint.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill1999157_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill1999157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She behaved with charity &amp; gentleness to all, accepting an arranged marriage as was the custom, but her husband and family turned out to be abusive. Eventually he had her strangled by his servants. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Every year, on the Sunday following 5 July, a procession celebrating Saint Godelieve takes place in <a href="/wiki/Gistel" title="Gistel">Gistel</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Godelieve&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition, as recorded in her <i>Vita</i>, states that she was pious as a young girl, and became much sought after by suitors as a beautiful young woman. Godelieve, however, wanted to become a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>. A nobleman named Bertolf (Berthold) of <a href="/wiki/Gistel" title="Gistel">Gistel</a>, however, determined to marry her, successfully invoked the help of her father's overlord, <a href="/wiki/Eustace_II,_Count_of_Boulogne" title="Eustace II, Count of Boulogne">Eustace II, Count of Boulogne</a>, along with her parents. She accepted the betrothal obediently &amp; went to Bertolf’s family home. There she was badly treated by him &amp; his mother, while she continued to live an obedient daughter-in-law &amp; managed the household well &amp; with Christian charity. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill1999157_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill1999157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Bertold became more dis-satisfied with her &amp; he ordered his servants to provide only bread and water to the young bride.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Godelieve shared this food with the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200150_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200150-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Godelieve managed to escape to the home of her father, Hemfrid, seigneur of <a href="/wiki/Wierre-Effroy" title="Wierre-Effroy">Wierre-Effroy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill1999157_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarper-Bill1999157-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  Hemfrid, appealing to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tournai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tournai">Bishops of Tournai</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Soissons" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Soissons">Soissons</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Flanders" title="Count of Flanders">Count of Flanders</a>, they concluded the marriage to be indissoluble and managed to have Bertolf restore Godelieve to her rightful position as his wife, which signaled a renewal of persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-Rudge_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rudge-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In July 1070, Godelieve returned to Gistel and soon after, at the order of Bertolf, was <a href="/wiki/Strangled" class="mw-redirect" title="Strangled">strangled</a> by two servants and thrown into a pool,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> causing it to appear she died a <a href="/wiki/Natural_death" class="mw-redirect" title="Natural death">natural death</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Rudge_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rudge-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Godelieve&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Bertolf married again, and had a daughter Edith, who was born <a href="/wiki/Blindness" class="mw-redirect" title="Blindness">blind</a>: the legend  states that Edith was cured through the intercession of Saint Godelieve.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMulder-Bakker200269_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMulder-Bakker200269-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Bertolf, now repentant of his crimes, went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to obtain <a href="/wiki/Absolution" title="Absolution">absolution</a>. He went on a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, and became a monk at St. Winnoc's Abbey at <a href="/wiki/Bergues" title="Bergues">Bergues</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMulder-Bakker200269_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMulder-Bakker200269-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Edith founded a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Gistel" title="Gistel">Gistel</a>, which was dedicated to Saint Godelieve, which she joined herself as a nun.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMulder-Bakker200269_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMulder-Bakker200269-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Godelieve&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Godelieve's body was <a href="/wiki/Exhumation" class="mw-redirect" title="Exhumation">exhumed</a> in 1084 by the Bishops of Tournai and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Noyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Noyon">Noyon</a>, in the presence of <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Saxony" title="Gertrude of Saxony">Gertrude of Saxony</a>, the wife of <a href="/wiki/Robert_I,_Count_of_Flanders" title="Robert I, Count of Flanders">Robert I, Count of Flanders</a>, the Abbot of St. Winnoc's and a number of clergymen. It was Radbod II, bishop of Noyon-Tournai, that consecrated Godelieve's relics in 1084,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200146_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200146-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and Godelieve's popular cult developed thereafter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145-46_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKienzleNienhuis200145-46-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Drogo, a monk of St. Winnoc's Abbey, wrote Godelieve's biography, the <i>Vita Godeliph</i>, about ten years after her death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHead2001359_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHead2001359-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The abbey of <a href="/wiki/Ten_Putte_Abbey" title="Ten Putte Abbey">Ten Putte Abbey</a> in Bruges was dedicated to her.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEde_Vries200744_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_Vries200744-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Every year, on the Sunday following 5 July, a <a href="/wiki/Procession" title="Procession">procession</a> celebrating Saint Godelieve takes place in Gistel. In 2017, the Godelieve procession was recognized as an Intangible Cultural Heritage.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Godelieve's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>, 6 July, was, like that of <a href="/wiki/Swithun" title="Swithun">Saint Swithun</a> in England and <a href="/wiki/Medardus" title="Medardus">Saint Medard</a> in France, connected with the <a href="/wiki/Weather" title="Weather">weather</a>. She is thus considered one of the "weather saints".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
A monastery of Benedictine nuns was established on the site of her home, belonging to the Subiaco Congregation. It was closed due to falling numbers about 2020; the building is currently under review by the city/church authorities.
</p>
