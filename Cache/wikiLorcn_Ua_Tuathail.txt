<p><b>Lorcán Ua Tuathail</b>, known in English as <b>Laurence O'Toole</b> and in French as <b>Laurent d'Eu</b> (1128  – 14 November 1180),  was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Dublin" title="Archbishop of Dublin">Archbishop of Dublin</a> at the time of the <a href="/wiki/Norman_invasion_of_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman invasion of Ireland">Norman invasion of Ireland</a>. Lorcán played a prominent role in the Irish Church Reform Movement of the 12th century and mediated between the parties during and after the invasion. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> in 1225 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lorcán was born at <a href="/wiki/Kilkea" title="Kilkea">Kilkea</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Kildare" title="County Kildare">County Kildare</a>, Ireland, the youngest<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> of four sons of King Muirchertach Ua Tuathail of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=U%C3%AD_Muiredaig&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Uí Muiredaig (page does not exist)">Uí Muiredaig</a>, a branch of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_D%C3%BAnlainge" title="Uí Dúnlainge">Uí Dúnlainge</a> dynasty. His mother was an O'Byrne princess of the Uí Fáelán branch of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_D%C3%BAnlainge" title="Uí Dúnlainge">Uí Dúnlainge</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Uí Tuathail (O'Toole) take their surname from their ancestor Tuathal mac Augaire, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Leinster" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Leinster">King of Leinster</a>, who died in 958. They resided at Maistiu (<a href="/wiki/Mullaghmast" title="Mullaghmast">Mullaghmast</a>) in what is now <a href="/wiki/County_Kildare" title="County Kildare">County Kildare</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>However, by the time of his son's birth King Muirchertach was subordinate to the new over-kings from South Leinster, of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Ceinnselaig" title="Uí Ceinnselaig">Uí Ceinnselaig</a>. The king from 1126 was <a href="/wiki/Diarmait_Mac_Murchada" class="mw-redirect" title="Diarmait Mac Murchada">Diarmait Mac Murchada</a> (Dermot MacMurrough). At the age of 10, Lorcán was sent to Diarmait as a hostage for his father.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, at one point Muirchertach's loyalty to Diarmait must have become suspect as Lorcán was imprisoned for some two years in extreme austerity and barely given enough to live on. Due to the intercession of the abbot of <a href="/wiki/Glendalough" title="Glendalough">Glendalough</a> – members of Lorcán's family had been buried at one of its churches for generations – relations were amicably restored between Diarmait and Muirchertach.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>One result of his confinement was the strengthening of Lorcán's wish to enter the religious life. The story goes that when Muirchertach arrived at Glendalough for Lorcán, he stated that he would draw lots to have one of his sons made a priest, at which Lorcán laughed as he had long thought of doing so. No lots were drawn, and Lorcán stayed at Glendalough.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Glendalough">Abbot of Glendalough</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Abbot of Glendalough">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg/220px-Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg/330px-Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg/440px-Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4288" data-file-height="2412" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wicklow_Way_Glendalough.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Glendalough, Co. Wicklow, Ireland</div></div></div>
<p>In time he rose to become <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Glendalough" title="Abbot of Glendalough">Abbot of Glendalough</a> at the age of 26 in 1154.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Lorcán was a religious reformer. He wished that the Irish Church would reflect the Catholic Church and strengthen the bonds between the Irish Church and Rome. Through his own example Lorcán brought his spiritual renewal to the church in Ireland and married the best in the Gaelic monastic movement with the best in the Frankish-European liturgical monastic movement. 
</p><p>Lorcán began a spiritual renewal programme amongst the monks of the Abbey bringing the Gaelic Abbey of Glendalough in line with the Frankish Abbeys of Continental Europe. He invited the Canons of St Augustine to come and assist in the reform of the Abbey and he became a member of the Augustinian Order himself.<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A great famine raged during the first four months of his administration, and brigandage beset his community, even undertaken by noblemen. In the early thirteenth-century <i>Life of St Lorcán</i>, it was claimed Lorcán protected his community from brigandage through his solemn prayer, fasting, and miraculous curse.<sup id="cite_ref-Harrington2018_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Harrington2018-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was well regarded by both the community in Glendalough and its secular neighbours for sanctity and charity to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Dublin">Archbishop of Dublin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Dublin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg/220px-Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="802" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg/330px-Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg/440px-Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg 2x" data-file-width="856" data-file-height="3120" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Dublin_Christ_Church_Cathedral_Baptistery_Window_Saint_Laurence_O%27Toole_2012_09_26.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Baptistery window of Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin</div></div></div>
<p>When he was 32, following the death of Archbishop Gregory in 1162, he was elected unanimously <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Dublin" title="Archbishop of Dublin">Archbishop of Dublin</a><sup id="cite_ref-cna_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> at the Synod of <a href="/wiki/Clane" title="Clane">Clane</a>, and was consecrated in by Gelasius, Archbishop of Armagh and successor of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Malachy" title="Saint Malachy">Máel Máedóc</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the first Irishman to be appointed to the See of this town then ruled by Danes and Norwegians; it is notable that his nomination was backed not only by the High King <a href="/wiki/Ruaidr%C3%AD_Ua_Conchobair" title="Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair">Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair</a> (Rory O'Connor), King Diarmait Mac Murchada (who had by then been married to Lorcán's sister, Mór); and the community at Glendalough, but also by the clergy and population of <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> itself. He played a prominent part in the Irish Church Reform Movement of the 12th century, as well as rebuilding several parish churches and emphasising the use of <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">Gregorian chant</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG/220px-Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG/330px-Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG/440px-Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG 2x" data-file-width="5184" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wicklow_Mountains_National_Park_Glendalough_Valley_St_Kevins_Bed_02.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Kevins Bed, Glendalough</div></div></div>
<p>As Archbishop of Dublin, Lorcán began a policy of church building and laid the foundation stone for the Cathedral of the Holy Trinity (now Christ Church). To assist in the spiritual formation of the priests and people of the Diocese Lorcán invited the Augustinian monks to become part of the Cathedral Chapter of the Holy Trinity. In a city that was in the middle of an economic boom, Lorcán, as Archbishop, was seen as the one who stretched out his hand to care for the poor and the neglected. There was appalling poverty in the city at the time and each day Lorcán fed the poor of the city in his home. He also established care centres for the children who had been abandoned by their parents or who were orphaned in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lorcán frequently made choice of Glendalough for his retreats; but he usually hid himself in a solitary cave at some distance from the monastery, between a rock and a deep lake, which <a href="/wiki/Kevin_of_Glendalough" title="Kevin of Glendalough">Kevin of Glendalough</a> had used.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_coming_of_the_Normans">The coming of the Normans</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: The coming of the Normans">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Norman_invasion_of_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman invasion of Ireland">Norman invasion of Ireland</a></div>
<p>In 1166, Lorcán's brother-in-law Diarmait was deposed as King of Leinster by an alliance of Irish kings and princes, led by <a href="/wiki/High_King" class="mw-redirect" title="High King">High King</a> <a href="/wiki/Ruaidr%C3%AD_Ua_Conchobair" title="Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair">Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair</a> (Rory O'Connor) and King <a href="/wiki/Tigern%C3%A1n_Ua_Ruairc" title="Tigernán Ua Ruairc">Tigernán Ua Ruairc</a> (Tiernan O'Rourke) of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Breifne" title="Kingdom of Breifne">Breifne</a>. Diarmait had in 1152 abducted Dervorguilla, Ua Ruairc's wife and on the death of Diarmait's protector, <a href="/wiki/High_King" class="mw-redirect" title="High King">High King</a> <a href="/wiki/Muirchertach_Mac_Lochlainn" title="Muirchertach Mac Lochlainn">Muirchertach Mac Lochlainn</a> in 1166, he paid the price. Exiled and with only a half-hearted promise of help from <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II of England</a>, after much wandering in Wales, England and France, he returned to Ireland with a group of penniless and down-on-their-luck <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a>, <a href="/wiki/Flemish_people" title="Flemish people">Flemish</a> and Welsh allies to help him regain his kingdom.
</p><p>Dublin was a walled city, but the citizens were terrified by the Norman knights and men-at-arms, as well as by the stories that were being told of their fierceness and cruelty. Lorcán O'Toole's house was besieged by people imploring him to save them and to make a treaty with the Anglo-Normans. The Archbishop went out to the foreigners' camp. While he appealed to them, two of the Norman knights with their followers made a breach in the walls of the city and entered the streets. They burned houses and killed the unarmed people. The noise and tumult reached the camp and so Lorcán heard of the massacre. He hurried back to Dublin and succeeded in stopping the slaughter.<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The expedition succeeded beyond their wildest dreams; Diarmait was reinstated as King of Leinster, the <a href="/wiki/Norsemen" title="Norsemen">Norse</a> towns of <a href="/wiki/Wexford" title="Wexford">Wexford</a>, <a href="/wiki/Waterford" title="Waterford">Waterford</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> captured, and the Irish under the High King defeated. To seal the alliance, Diarmait offered his daughter, <a href="/wiki/Aoife_MacMurrough" title="Aoife MacMurrough">Aoife</a> — who was also Lorcán's niece – in marriage to the leader of the Normans, <a href="/wiki/Richard_de_Clare,_2nd_Earl_of_Pembroke" title="Richard de Clare, 2nd Earl of Pembroke">Strongbow</a>.
</p><p>The last years of Lorcán's life were defined by these events and those that were consequent upon it. He had been in negotiations with Diarmait when he and his allies laid siege to Dublin after a band of Norman <a href="/wiki/Knights" class="mw-redirect" title="Knights">knights</a> seized the town. He acted again as mediator when the <a href="/wiki/Ascall_mac_Ragnaill" title="Ascall mac Ragnaill">deposed King of Dublin</a> unsuccessfully tried to recapture his town and again when Ua Conchobair laid siege.
</p><p>Lorcán was now a national figure greatly in demand as a mediator by all sides. No greater tribute can be paid to him than the fact that he was the one man in Ireland whom everybody trusted. Irish, Vikings, Normans, all had equal respect for him as a man of total honour and integrity.<sup id="cite_ref-diocese_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-diocese-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Synod_of_Cashel">Synod of Cashel</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Synod of Cashel">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Cashel" title="Synod of Cashel">Synod of Cashel</a></div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1134653256">.mw-parser-output .side-box{margin:4px 0;box-sizing:border-box;border:1px solid #aaa;font-size:88%;line-height:1.25em;background-color:#f9f9f9;display:flow-root}.mw-parser-output .side-box-abovebelow,.mw-parser-output .side-box-text{padding:0.25em 0.9em}.mw-parser-output .side-box-image{padding:2px 0 2px 0.9em;text-align:center}.mw-parser-output .side-box-imageright{padding:2px 0.9em 2px 0;text-align:center}@media(min-width:500px){.mw-parser-output .side-box-flex{display:flex;align-items:center}.mw-parser-output .side-box-text{flex:1}}@media(min-width:720px){.mw-parser-output .side-box{width:238px}.mw-parser-output .side-box-right{clear:right;float:right;margin-left:1em}.mw-parser-output .side-box-left{margin-right:1em}}</style><div class="side-box side-box-right plainlinks sistersitebox"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1126788409">.mw-parser-output .plainlist ol,.mw-parser-output .plainlist ul{line-height:inherit;list-style:none;margin:0;padding:0}.mw-parser-output .plainlist ol li,.mw-parser-output .plainlist ul li{margin-bottom:0}</style>
<div class="side-box-flex">
<div class="side-box-image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Wikisource-logo.svg/38px-Wikisource-logo.svg.png" decoding="async" width="38" height="40" class="noviewer" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Wikisource-logo.svg/57px-Wikisource-logo.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Wikisource-logo.svg/76px-Wikisource-logo.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="410" data-file-height="430" /></div>
<div class="side-box-text plainlist"><a href="/wiki/Wikisource" title="Wikisource">Wikisource</a> has original text related to this article:
<div style="margin-left: 10px;"><b><a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Privilege_of_Pope_Alexander_III_to_Henry_II" class="extiw" title="wikisource:Privilege of Pope Alexander III to Henry II">Privilege of Pope Alexander III to Henry II</a></b></div></div></div>
</div>
<p>The arrival of Henry II of England as Lord of Ireland in Dublin on 11 November 1171 served a number of purposes: first, to rein in his erstwhile Norman subjects before they established a rival Norman kingdom of their own; second, to receive the submission of the Irish kings and princes; third, to arrange a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> at <a href="/wiki/Cashel,_Tipperary" class="mw-redirect" title="Cashel, Tipperary">Cashel</a>. This was to bring Ireland in line with Catholic Church observances as practised in Henry's other domains in England and France. Two of the statutes proclaimed concerned the marriage laws of the Irish clergy and the granting of the <a href="/wiki/Rock_of_Cashel" title="Rock of Cashel">Rock of Cashel</a> to the church. It was also used to try to bring the Church of Ireland under the jurisdiction of <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> and in the process <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> confirmed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_IV" title="Pope Adrian IV">Pope Adrian IV</a>'s donation of Ireland to Henry in 1172. The implications of all this only seems to have sunk in after Henry's departure in April 1172 and to this end Ua Conchobair sent Ua Tuathail – accompanied by Catholicus, <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Clonfert" title="Abbot of Clonfert">Abbot of Clonfert</a> — to London to negotiate a settlement with Henry.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Treaty_of_Windsor">Treaty of Windsor</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Treaty of Windsor">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Windsor_(1175)" title="Treaty of Windsor (1175)">Treaty of Windsor (1175)</a></div>
<p>The Treaty of Windsor was a pact between Ua Conchobair and Henry II which acknowledged Henry's right to the Lordship of Leinster, Meath and such areas then occupied by his Norman subjects. Lorcán was able to get Henry to acknowledge Ua Conchobair's right to the High Kingship and to his lands. However, in so doing, Lorcán had to cede to Henry Ua Conchobair's tribute to him.
</p><p>During the negotiations, Lorcán was saying mass at the shrine of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a> at Canterbury when he was attacked by a madman. The man had heard of the archbishop's reputation and had the idea of giving the church another martyr; he struck Lorcán on the head, before the altar, with a club. Unlike Becket, Ua Tuathail, though knocked to the ground, was able to recover and finish the mass.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Last_years_and_death_in_Normandy">Last years and death in Normandy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Last years and death in Normandy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Archbishop Lorcán left Ireland in 1179 to attend the <a href="/wiki/Third_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Third Council of the Lateran">Third Council of the Lateran</a> in Rome, accompanied by five other bishops. From <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> he received a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a>, confirming the rights and privileges of the See of Dublin. Alexander also named him as <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a>. On his return to Ireland he kept up the pace of reform to such an extent that as many as 150 clerics were withdrawn from their offices for various abuses and sent to Rome.
</p><p>Lorcán was described as tall and graceful in figure.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was well known as an ascetic, wore a hair shirt, never ate meat, and fasted every Friday on bread and water. In contrast to this, it is said that when he entertained, his guests lacked for nothing while he drank water coloured to look like wine so as not to spoil the feast. Each <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> he returned to Glendalough to make a forty days' retreat in <a href="/wiki/St._Kevin" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Kevin">St. Kevin</a>'s Cave on a precipice of Lugduff Mountain over the <a href="/wiki/Glendalough_Upper_Lake" title="Glendalough Upper Lake">Upper Lake</a>.
</p><p>In 1180, he left Ireland for the last time, taking with him a son of Ua Conchobair's as a hostage to Henry. He meant to admonish Henry for incursions against Ua Conchobair, contrary to the Treaty of Windsor. After a stay at the Monastery of <a href="/wiki/Abingdon,_Oxfordshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Abingdon, Oxfordshire">Abingdon</a> south of <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> – necessitated by a closure of the ports – he landed at <a href="/wiki/Le_Tr%C3%A9port" title="Le Tréport">Le Tréport</a>, Normandy, at a cove named after him, Saint-Laurent. He fell ill and was conveyed to the Abbey of St. Victor at <a href="/wiki/Eu,_Seine-Maritime" title="Eu, Seine-Maritime">Eu</a>.  Mortally ill, it was suggested that he should make his will, to which he replied: <i>"God knows, I have not a penny under the sun to leave anyone."</i> His last thoughts were of his people in Dublin: <i>"Alas, you poor, foolish people, what will you do now? Who will take care of you in your trouble? Who will help you?"</i> He died at Eu, Normandy on 14 November 1180, and was buried there.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_relics">Veneration and relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lorc%C3%A1n_Ua_Tuathail&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Veneration and relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG/220px-Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG/330px-Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG/440px-Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3264" data-file-height="2448" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Laurence_O%27Toole_heart_Christ_Church.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ua Tuathail's heart in Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin</div></div></div>
<p>Due to the claimed great number of miracles that rapidly occurred either at his tomb or through his intercession, Lorcán was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> by Pope Honorius III only 45 years after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-Pogatchnik20120304_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pogatchnik20120304-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Early devotees of Lorcán compared him with his <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> contemporary <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a>, as a man who had suffered physical attacks while defending the independence of the church and his people from oppression, and who had held out the threat of divine curse or <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunication</a> against the church's oppressors.<sup id="cite_ref-Harrington2018_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Harrington2018-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg/220px-Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="220" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg/330px-Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg/440px-Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Coll%C3%A9giale_Notre-Dame_et_Saint-Laurent_d%27Eu-7838.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relic of St Laurent O'Toole (Lorcán Ua Tuathail) in Collégiale Notre-Dame et Saint-Laurent, Eu, Normandy. It includes the skull of the Saint</div></div></div>
<p>In Eu, the crowd of pilgrims coming to pray before his relics led to the construction of a magnificent new church there, the Collégiale Notre-Dame-et-Saint-Laurent d'Eu (Collegiate Church of Our Lady &amp; St. Lorcán of Eu) where the recumbent figure (<i>gisant</i>) of Lorcán is the oldest preserved in the crypt. He is represented there with bearded face, mitre on his head, dressed in his vestments and priestly insignia. The <i>gisant</i> dates from the late 12th century and is among the oldest of its kind in France.
</p><p>His skull was reportedly brought to England in 1442 by a nobleman named Sir Rowland Standish (relation of <a href="/wiki/Myles_Standish" title="Myles Standish">Myles Standish</a>) who had fought at <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt" title="Battle of Agincourt">Agincourt</a>. The bones were supposedly interred at the parish church of <a href="/wiki/Chorley" title="Chorley">Chorley</a> in England, now named <a href="/wiki/St_Laurence%27s_Church,_Chorley" title="St Laurence&#39;s Church, Chorley">St. Laurence's</a>, until they disappeared in the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>. However the Collégiale Notre-Dame-et-Saint-Laurent d'Eu still possesses a reliquary containing his skull.
</p><p>Lorcán's heart was returned to Ireland and is preserved in a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church_Cathedral,_Dublin" title="Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin">Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin</a>. Lorcán's heart remains in Christ Church Cathedral despite the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Reformation">Irish Reformation</a>, although devotion to saints is more prominent <a href="/wiki/Catholic_devotions" title="Catholic devotions">in Roman Catholicism</a> than <a href="/wiki/Anglican_devotions" title="Anglican devotions">in the Anglicanism</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> which owns the cathedral. The reliquary was stolen in 2012, with the <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_Christ_Church_Cathedral,_Dublin" title="Dean of Christ Church Cathedral, Dublin">Dean of Christ Church</a> saying "It has no economic value, but it is a priceless treasure that links our present foundation with its founding father".<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Pogatchnik20120304_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pogatchnik20120304-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It was recovered in <a href="/wiki/Phoenix_Park" title="Phoenix Park">Phoenix Park</a> in 2018 after a tip-off to the <a href="/wiki/Garda_S%C3%ADoch%C3%A1na" title="Garda Síochána">Garda Síochána</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Media reported that the unidentified thieves thought it was cursed and caused family members' illnesses.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> At a special <a href="/wiki/Evensong" title="Evensong">evensong</a> ceremony in Christ Church on 26 April 2018, archbishop <a href="/wiki/Michael_Jackson_(bishop)" title="Michael Jackson (bishop)">Michael Jackson</a> received the heart from a senior Garda.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
