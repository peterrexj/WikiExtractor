					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">7th- and 8th-century missionary to Britain and saint</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p><p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">James the Deacon</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">after 671</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-PSI_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PSI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><br /><a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican Church">Anglican Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-Church_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Church-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints#Pre-Congregation" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Pre-Congregation</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">17 August or 11 October</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>James the Deacon</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died after 671) was a Roman <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> who accompanied <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_York" title="Paulinus of York">Paulinus of York</a> on his mission to <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>. He was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian mission</a>, which went to England to <a href="/wiki/Christianise" class="mw-redirect" title="Christianise">Christianise</a> the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a> from their native <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a>. However, when he arrived in England is unknown. After Paulinus left Northumbria, James stayed near Lincoln and continued his missionary efforts, dying sometime after 671, according to the medieval chronicler <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_the_Deacon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James was presumably an <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards" title="Kingdom of the Lombards">Italian</a>, like the other members of the Gregorian mission.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The dates of his birth and his arrival in Britain are unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-World98_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-World98-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He went with Paulinus to Northumbria accompanying <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelburg_of_Kent" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelburg of Kent">Æthelburh</a>, sister of King <a href="/wiki/Eadbald_of_Kent" title="Eadbald of Kent">Eadbald of Kent</a>, who went to <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> to marry King <a href="/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria" title="Edwin of Northumbria">Edwin</a>. Traditionally this event is dated to 625, but the historian D. P. Kirby argues that the mission to Northumbria probably happened before 619.<sup id="cite_ref-Coming66_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coming66-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Edwin died in battle at <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Hatfield_Chase" title="Battle of Hatfield Chase">Hatfield</a> fighting against <a href="/wiki/Penda_of_Mercia" title="Penda of Mercia">Penda of Mercia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cadwallon_ap_Cadfan" title="Cadwallon ap Cadfan">Caedwalla</a> in 633.<sup id="cite_ref-Coming68_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coming68-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Edwin had been the main supporter of Paulinus' mission, and with his death, a pagan backlash set in. Paulinus fled to Kent, along with  Æthelburg and Edwin and Æthelburg's daughter <a href="/wiki/Eanfl%C3%A6d" title="Eanflæd">Eanflæd</a>. James, however, remained behind in Northumbria and continued missionary efforts.<sup id="cite_ref-Coming68_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coming68-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> James' efforts were centred in Lincoln, at a church that Paulinus had built there, the remains of which may lie under the church of St. Paul-in-the-Bail.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks22_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks22-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This was in the dependent <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lindsey" title="Kingdom of Lindsey">kingdom of Lindsey</a>, where Paulinus had preached before Edwin's death,<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby65_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby65-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and it was reconquered by one of Edwin's successors, <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria" title="Oswald of Northumbria">Oswald of Northumbria</a> in the 640s.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby74_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby74-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bede writes that James lived in a village near <a href="/wiki/Catterick,_North_Yorkshire" title="Catterick, North Yorkshire">Catterick</a>, which "bears his name to this day".<sup id="cite_ref-Bede139_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede139-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He reports that James undertook missionary work in the area and lived to a great age.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During the reign of King <a href="/wiki/Oswiu_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Oswiu of Northumbria">Oswiu of Northumbria</a>, James attended the royal court, for he celebrated Easter with Oswiu's queen, Eanflæd, Edwin's daughter. James and Eanflæd celebrated Easter on the date used by the Roman church, which led to conflicts with Oswiu, who celebrated Easter on the date calculated by the Irish church. These dates did not always agree and were one of the reasons that Oswiu called the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a> in 664 to decide which system of Easter calculation his kingdom would use.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby87_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby87-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Bede's account of events, James was present at the Synod of Whitby.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> Bede states that after the synod, and the return of Roman customs, James, as a trained singing master in the Roman and Kentish style, taught many people <a href="/wiki/Plainsong" title="Plainsong">plainsong</a> or <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">Gregorian chant</a> in the Roman manner.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>James' date of death is unknown, but Bede implies that he was still alive during Bede's lifetime, which presumably means that he died after Bede's birth, sometime around 671 or 672. This would mean he was at least 70 years old at his death.<sup id="cite_ref-World98_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-World98-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It has been suggested that James was Bede's informant for the life of Edwin, the works of Paulinus, and perhaps the Synod of Whitby.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The historian <a href="/wiki/Frank_Stenton" title="Frank Stenton">Frank Stenton</a> calls James "the one heroic figure in the Roman mission".<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> This reflects the fact that many of the Gregorian missionaries had a habit of fleeing when things went wrong.<sup id="cite_ref-Coming75_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Coming75-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his death, James was venerated as a saint. His feast day is 17 August (Catholic <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-PSI_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PSI-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or 11 October (<a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
