<p><b>Ia of Cornwall</b> (also known as <i>Eia</i>, <i>Hia</i> or <i>Hya</i>) was an evangelist and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the 5th or 6th centuries in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>. She is said to have been an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> princess, the sister of <a href="/wiki/Erc_of_Slane" title="Erc of Slane">Erc of Slane</a> and a student of Baricus.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ia_of_Cornwall&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ia went to the seashore to depart for Cornwall from her native Ireland along with other saints. Finding that they had gone without her, fearing that she was too young for such a hazardous journey, she was grief-stricken and began to pray. As she prayed, she noticed a small leaf floating on the water and touched it with a rod to see if it would sink. As she watched, it grew bigger and bigger. Trusting God, she embarked upon the leaf and was carried across the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Sea" title="Irish Sea">Irish Sea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ives_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She reached Cornwall before the others, where she joined <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gwinear" title="Saint Gwinear">Gwinear</a> and <a href="/wiki/Felec_of_Cornwall" title="Felec of Cornwall">Felec of Cornwall</a>.
</p><p>Legend holds that they had up to 777 companions.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She is said to have founded an oratory in a clearing in a wood on the site of the existing Parish Church that is dedicated to her.<sup id="cite_ref-ives_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Ia was martyred under "King Teudar"<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (i.e., <a href="/wiki/Tewdwr_Mawr" title="Tewdwr Mawr">Tewdwr Mawr</a> of <a href="/wiki/Penwith" title="Penwith">Penwith</a>) on the <a href="/wiki/River_Hayle" title="River Hayle">River Hayle</a> and buried at what is now <a href="/wiki/St_Ives,_Cornwall" title="St Ives, Cornwall">St Ives</a>, where <a href="/wiki/St_Ia%27s_Church,_St_Ives" title="St Ia&#39;s Church, St Ives">St Ia's Church</a>—of which she is now the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>—was erected over her grave; the town of St Ives grew up around the site. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ia_of_Cornwall&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Like many Cornish saints, public and liturgical veneration of Ia is mostly limited to the area surrounding St Ives. Ia has two churches dedicated to her, both in St Ives: <a href="/wiki/St_Ia%27s_Church,_St_Ives" title="St Ia&#39;s Church, St Ives">St Ia's Church</a>, which is of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>, and a Catholic church dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a> and Saint Ia.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A now ruined chapel near <a href="/wiki/Troon,_Cornwall" title="Troon, Cornwall">Troon</a> was also dedicated to her.<sup id="cite_ref-troon_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-troon-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The church of <a href="/wiki/Plouy%C3%A9" title="Plouyé">Plouyé</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> was probably dedicated originally to this saint as well.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/John_Leland_(antiquary)" title="John Leland (antiquary)">John Leland</a> gives details from a Latin <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> of Ia, which is no longer extant. <a href="/wiki/St_Olaf%27s_Church,_Poughill" title="St Olaf&#39;s Church, Poughill">St Olaf's Church, Poughill</a> and <a href="/wiki/St_Uny%27s_Church,_Lelant" title="St Uny&#39;s Church, Lelant">St Uny's Church, Lelant</a> both have stained glass windows depicting her. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast Day">feast day</a> is 3 February. 
</p>
