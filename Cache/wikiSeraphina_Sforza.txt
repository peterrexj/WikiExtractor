					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SforzaSerafina.JPG" class="image"><img alt="SforzaSerafina.JPG" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/SforzaSerafina.JPG/220px-SforzaSerafina.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="325" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cb/SforzaSerafina.JPG 1.5x" data-file-width="300" data-file-height="443" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SforzaSerafina.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div></div></div></div>
<p><b>Seraphina Sforza</b> (1434 – 8 September 1478), born <b>Sveva da Montefeltro</b>, was an Italian noblewoman and nun, a <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clare">Poor Clare</a> after her husband <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Sforza" title="Alessandro Sforza">Alessandro Sforza</a> discarded her.
</p><p>She was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a> in 1754, and her feast is kept on 9 September by <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seraphina_Sforza&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born in <a href="/wiki/Urbino" title="Urbino">Urbino</a> to <a href="/wiki/Guido_Antonio_of_Montefeltro" class="mw-redirect" title="Guido Antonio of Montefeltro">Guido Antonio of Montefeltro</a>, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Urbino" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Urbino">Count of Urbino</a>, and Caterina Colonna in the first half of 1434.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her father having died in 1438, and her mother in 1443, she was cared for in Urbino first by her elder brother <a href="/wiki/Oddantonio_da_Montefeltro" title="Oddantonio da Montefeltro">Oddantonio da Montefeltro</a>, who had succeeded their father as Count, and then by her half-brother <a href="/wiki/Federico_da_Montefeltro" title="Federico da Montefeltro">Federico da Montefeltro</a>, who succeeded Oddantonio after his assassination in 1444.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In March 1446, aged around 12, she moved to Rome where she stayed for more than a year, under the protection of her maternal uncle Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Prospero_Colonna_(cardinal)" title="Prospero Colonna (cardinal)">Prospero Colonna (cardinal)</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1448 Seraphina married <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Sforza" title="Alessandro Sforza">Alessandro Sforza</a>, Lord of <a href="/wiki/Pesaro" title="Pesaro">Pesaro</a>. Some time after, her husband left to support his brother <a href="/wiki/Francesco_I_Sforza" title="Francesco I Sforza">Francesco I Sforza</a> as Duke of Milan. During his six-year absence, Seraphina was entrusted with governing Pesaro.<sup id="cite_ref-Habig_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Habig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  Upon his return, her husband gave himself up to a dissolute life, and ill-treated her. He forced her to enter the convent of Poor Clares at Pesaro.
</p><p>In 1475 Seraphina was elected abbess of the monastery at Pesaro, where she died in 1478. Her body, exhumed some years after her death, was found incorrupt, and is preserved in <a href="/wiki/Pesaro_Cathedral" title="Pesaro Cathedral">Pesaro Cathedral</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
