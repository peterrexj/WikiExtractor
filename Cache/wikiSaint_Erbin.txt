<p><b>Erbin of Dumnonia</b> (Latin: Urbanus; c. 427 &#8211; c. 480) was a 5th-century King of <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a> and <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monarch">Monarch</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Erbin_of_Dumnonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Monarch">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Traditionally, Erbin was a King of Dumnonia, the son of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Corneu" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine Corneu">Constantine Corneu</a> and the father of <a href="/wiki/Geraint" title="Geraint">Geraint</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was the brother of <a href="/wiki/Digain" title="Digain">Saint Digain</a>, founder of the church at <a href="/wiki/Llangernyw" title="Llangernyw">Llangernyw</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Erbin succeeded his father as King of Dumnonia around 443. 
</p><p>Erbin chiefly appears in <i><a href="/wiki/Geraint_and_Enid" class="mw-redirect" title="Geraint and Enid">Geraint and Enid</a></i>, one of the Three <a href="/wiki/Welsh_Romances" class="mw-redirect" title="Welsh Romances">Welsh Romances</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Mabinogion" title="Mabinogion">Mabinogion</a>. In the romance of <a href="/wiki/Culhwch_and_Olwen" title="Culhwch and Olwen">Culhwch and Olwen</a> he is the father of  Gereint, <a href="/wiki/Dywel_fab_Erbin" title="Dywel fab Erbin">Dywel</a>, and Ermid, the latter two knights at Arthur's court at <a href="/wiki/Celliwig" title="Celliwig">Celliwig</a>. According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Bonedd_y_Saint" title="Bonedd y Saint">Bonedd y Saint</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Cybi" title="Cybi">Saint Cybi</a> is Erbin's great-grandson through Cybi's father <a href="/wiki/Salomon_of_Cornwall" title="Salomon of Cornwall">Salomon of Cornwall</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-odden_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the Mabinogion he appears as an old king whose realm is subject to attacks from his enemies. He recalls his son from the court of King Artu to aid him in the war and restores the Dumnonian power, then abdicates in his son's favour. About 480 Erbin dies.<sup id="cite_ref-odden_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The common <a href="/wiki/Calamintha" title="Calamintha">calamint</a> (an aromatic herb) is called in Welsh erbin.<sup id="cite_ref-odden_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odden-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Erbin_of_Dumnonia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Erbin appears as a saint in the <i>Bonedd y Saint</i> and is traditionally associated with <a href="/wiki/Erbistock" title="Erbistock">Erbistock</a> in <a href="/wiki/Denbighshire" title="Denbighshire">Denbighshire</a>. His feast day is 13 January. <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Hunter_Doble" title="Gilbert Hunter Doble">Gilbert Hunter Doble</a> suggested he might be the same as Saint Ervan/Erme of <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Erbyn or Ervan is said to have been the father of Selevan. Erbyn is the original patron saint of <a href="/wiki/St_Ervan" title="St Ervan">St Ervan</a> but in more modern times he has become confused with the Greek <a href="/wiki/Saint_Hermes" title="Saint Hermes">saint Hermes</a> who is now regarded as such.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was probably the founder of the church of Saint Ervan in Cornwall. In ancient Welsh calendars he is commemorated as a saint on 13 January or 29 May.
</p>
