<p>In the Christian tradition, Gamaliel is recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Pharisee" class="mw-redirect" title="Pharisee">Pharisee</a> doctor of <a href="/wiki/Halakha" title="Halakha">Jewish Law</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEncy_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEncy-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Acts_5" title="Acts 5">Acts of the Apostles, 5</a>  speaks of Gamaliel as a man held in great esteem by all Jews and as the Jewish law teacher of <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> in <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Acts#22:3" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Acts">Acts 22:3</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gamaliel <a href="/wiki/Gamaliel%27s_principle" title="Gamaliel&#39;s principle">encouraged his fellow Pharisees to show leniency</a> to the apostles of Jesus Christ in <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Acts#5:34" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Acts">Acts 5:34</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Jewish_tradition">In Jewish tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamaliel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: In Jewish tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:194px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gamliel.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Gamliel.jpg/192px-Gamliel.jpg" decoding="async" width="192" height="288" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Gamliel.jpg/288px-Gamliel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Gamliel.jpg/384px-Gamliel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="725" data-file-height="1089" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gamliel.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;"> Rabban Gamaliel<br />(רבן גמליﭏ)</div></div></div></div>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Talmud" title="Talmud">Talmud</a>, Gamaliel is described as bearing the titles <a href="/wiki/Nasi_(Hebrew_title)" title="Nasi (Hebrew title)">Nasi</a> <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">(Hebrew:</a> נָשִׂיא‎ <i>Nāśīʾ</i>) "prince" and <i>Rabban</i> "our master", as the president of the Great Sanhedrin in Jerusalem; although some dispute this, it is not doubted that he held a senior position in the highest court in Jerusalem.<sup id="cite_ref-JE_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
Gamaliel holds a reputation in the <a href="/wiki/Mishnah" title="Mishnah">Mishnah</a> for being one of the greatest teachers in all the annals of Judaism: "Since Rabban Gamaliel the Elder died, there has been no more reverence for the law, and purity and piety died out at the same time".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gamaliel's authority on questions of religious law is suggested by two Mishnaic anecdotes in which "the king and queen" ask for his advice about rituals.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The identity of the king and queen in question is not given, but is generally thought to either be <a href="/wiki/Herod_Agrippa" title="Herod Agrippa">Herod Agrippa</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Cypros" class="mw-redirect" title="Cypros">Cypros</a> the <a href="/wiki/Nabataeans" title="Nabataeans">Nabataean</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Herod_Agrippa_II" title="Herod Agrippa II">Herod Agrippa II</a> and his sister <a href="/wiki/Berenice_(daughter_of_Herod_Agrippa)" title="Berenice (daughter of Herod Agrippa)">Berenice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-JE_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As <a href="/wiki/Rabbinic_literature" title="Rabbinic literature">rabbinic literature</a> always contrasts the school of <a href="/wiki/Hillel_the_Elder" title="Hillel the Elder">Hillel the Elder</a> to that of <a href="/wiki/Shammai" title="Shammai">Shammai</a> and only presents the collective opinions of each of these opposing schools of thought without mentioning the individual nuances and opinions of the rabbis within them, these texts do not portray Gamaliel as being knowledgeable about the Jewish scriptures, nor do they portray him as a teacher.<sup id="cite_ref-JE_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> For this reason, Gamaliel is not listed as part of the chain of individuals who perpetuated the Mishnaic tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Instead the chain is listed as passing directly from Hillel to <a href="/wiki/Yohanan_ben_Zakkai" title="Yohanan ben Zakkai">Yohanan ben Zakkai</a>.
</p><p>Nevertheless, the Mishnah mentions Gamaliel's authorship of a few laws about community welfare and conjugal rights. He argued that the law should protect women during divorce, and that, for the purpose of remarriage, a single witness was sufficient evidence for the death of a husband.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Various pieces of classical rabbinic literature additionally mention that Gamaliel sent out three <a href="/wiki/Epistle" title="Epistle">epistles</a>, designed as notifications of new religious rulings, and which portray Gamaliel as the head of the Jewish body for religious law.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Two of these three were sent, respectively, to the inhabitants of <a href="/wiki/Galilee" title="Galilee">Galilee</a> and "the Darom" (southern Judea), and were on the subject of the <a href="/wiki/First_tithe" title="First tithe">first tithe</a>. The third epistle was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Jewish_diaspora" title="Jewish diaspora">Jews of the diaspora</a> and argued for the introduction of an <a href="/wiki/Intercalation_(timekeeping)" title="Intercalation (timekeeping)">intercalary month</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Plum%C3%A9liau_(56)_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Plum%C3%A9liau_%2856%29_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG/220px-Plum%C3%A9liau_%2856%29_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Plum%C3%A9liau_%2856%29_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG/330px-Plum%C3%A9liau_%2856%29_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Plum%C3%A9liau_%2856%29_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG/440px-Plum%C3%A9liau_%2856%29_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1786" data-file-height="2381" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Plum%C3%A9liau_(56)_Chapelle_Saint-Nicod%C3%A8me_Retable_de_la_Descente_de_Croix_20.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Gamaliel in the Chapelle Saint-Nicodème de Pluméliau.</div></div></div>
<p>Since the Hillel school of thought is presented collectively, there are very few other teachings which are clearly identifiable as Gamaliel's. There is only a cryptic dictum, comparing his students to classes of fish:
</p>
<dl><dd>A <a href="/wiki/Kosher_animals" title="Kosher animals">ritually impure fish</a>: one who has memorized everything by study, but has no understanding, and is the son of poor parents</dd>
<dd>A ritually pure fish: one who has learnt and understood everything, and is the son of rich parents</dd>
<dd>A fish from the <a href="/wiki/Jordan_River" title="Jordan River">Jordan River</a>: one who has learnt everything, but doesn't know how to respond</dd>
<dd>A fish from the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean_Sea" title="Mediterranean Sea">Mediterranean Sea</a>: one who has learnt everything, and knows how to respond</dd></dl>
<p>In some manuscripts of <a href="/wiki/Dunash_ibn_Tamim" title="Dunash ibn Tamim">Dunash ibn Tamim</a>'s tenth-century Hebrew commentary on the <i><a href="/wiki/Sefer_Yetzirah" title="Sefer Yetzirah">Sefer Yetzirah</a></i>, the author identifies Gamaliel with the physician <a href="/wiki/Galen" title="Galen">Galen</a>. He claims to have seen an Arabic medical work translated from Hebrew entitled <i>The Book of Gamaliel the Prince (Nasi), called Galenos among the Greeks</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> However, since Galen lived in the second century and Gamaliel died during the mid-first century, this is unlikely.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Quotes">Quotes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamaliel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Quotes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Provide yourself with a <a href="/wiki/Rabbi" title="Rabbi">rabbi</a>, and eschew doubtful matters, and tithe not overmuch by guesswork.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Christian_tradition">In Christian tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamaliel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In Christian tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg/220px-Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg/330px-Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg/440px-Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg 2x" data-file-width="483" data-file-height="680" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gamaliel_erscheint_dem_Lucianus_im_Traum.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gamaliel appearing to the priest Lucianus in a dream. 15th century painting.</div></div></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a> introduces Gamaliel as a <a href="/wiki/Pharisee" class="mw-redirect" title="Pharisee">Pharisee</a> and celebrated doctor of the <a href="/wiki/613_Mitzvot" class="mw-redirect" title="613 Mitzvot">Mosaic Law</a> in <span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+5%3A34%E2%80%9340&amp;version=9">Acts 5:34–40</a></span>. In the larger context (vs.<span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+5%3A17%E2%80%9342&amp;version=9">17–42</a></span>), <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and the other <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Twelve Apostles">apostles</a> are described as being prosecuted before the Sanhedrin for continuing to preach the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">gospel</a> despite the Jewish authorities having previously prohibited it. The passage describes Gamaliel as presenting an argument against killing the apostles, reminding them about the previous revolts of <a href="/wiki/Theudas" title="Theudas">Theudas</a> and <a href="/wiki/Judas_of_Galilee" title="Judas of Galilee">Judas of Galilee</a>, which had collapsed quickly after the deaths of those individuals. Gamaliel's advice was accepted after his concluding argument:
</p>
<dl><dd>"And now I say unto you, Refrain from these men, and let them alone: for if this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought: But if it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it; lest haply ye be found even to fight against God." —<span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+5%3A38%E2%80%9339&amp;version=9">Acts 5:38–39</a></span></dd></dl>
<p>The Book of Acts later goes on to describe <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> recounting that although "born in Tarsus", he was brought up in Jerusalem "at the feet of Gamaliel, <i>[and]</i> taught according to the perfect manner of the law of the fathers" (<a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Acts#22:3" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Acts">Acts 22:3</a>). No details are given about which teachings Paul adopted from Gamaliel, as it is assumed that as a Pharisee, Paul was already recognized in the community at that time as a devout Jew. Also, how much Gamaliel influenced aspects of Christianity is unmentioned. However, there is no other record of Gamaliel ever having taught in public,<sup id="cite_ref-JE_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but the Talmud does describe Gamaliel as teaching a student who displayed "impudence in learning", which a few scholars identify as a possible reference to Paul.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="what scholars (February 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The relationship of <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle_and_Judaism" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul the Apostle and Judaism">Paul the Apostle and Judaism</a> continues to be the subject of scholarly debate. <a href="/wiki/Helmut_Koester" title="Helmut Koester">Helmut Koester</a>, Professor of Divinity and of Ecclesiastical History at <a href="/wiki/Harvard_University" title="Harvard University">Harvard University</a>, questions if Paul studied under this famous rabbi, arguing that there is a marked contrast in the tolerance that Gamaliel is said to have expressed about Christianity with the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_early_Christians_by_the_Jews" class="mw-redirect" title="Persecution of early Christians by the Jews">"murderous rage" against Christians</a> that Paul is described as having prior to his conversion (<span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+8%3A1%E2%80%933&amp;version=9">Acts 8:1–3</a></span>).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2013)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Alleged_Gospel_of_Gamaliel">Alleged Gospel of Gamaliel</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamaliel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Alleged Gospel of Gamaliel">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The "Gospel of Gamaliel" is a hypothetical book speculated to exist by some scholars, perhaps a part of <a href="/wiki/Pilate_apocrypha" class="mw-redirect" title="Pilate apocrypha">Pilate apocrypha</a>.  While no ancient sources directly refer to such a gospel, <a href="/wiki/Paulin_Ladeuze" title="Paulin Ladeuze">Paulin Ladeuze</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carl_Anton_Baumstark" title="Carl Anton Baumstark">Carl Anton Baumstark</a> first proposed that such a book existed in 1906.  Scholars who believe such a book once existed have reconstructed it from a homily, the "Lament of Mary" (<i>Laha Maryam</i>) by a bishop named Cyriacus. They believe <i>Laha Maryam</i> extensively quotes the Gospel of Gamaliel; the Lament includes a section that leads with "I, Gamaliel" which caused the speculation that these sections were actually quoting an existing gospel.  Other scholars believe that such inference that the author was "plagiarizing" a lost gospel is unwarranted, and these sections are simply written by Cyriacus from the perspective of Gamaliel.  The hostile view derisively calls it an "imaginary work".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Reasonably complete manuscripts of <i>Laha Maryam</i> exist in both <a href="/wiki/Ge%27ez" class="mw-redirect" title="Ge&#39;ez">Ethiopian (Ge'ez)</a> and <a href="/wiki/Garshuni" title="Garshuni">Karshuni (Arabic)</a> language versions.  Regardless of whether <i>Laha Maryam</i> is quoting a lost gospel or not, Gamaliel does feature in it.  He is a witness to a miracle of healing in the raising of a dead man at <a href="/wiki/Empty_tomb" title="Empty tomb">Jesus's tomb</a>; Jesus's abandoned grave-cloths have miraculous powers.  Gamaliel also talks with <a href="/wiki/Pontius_Pilate" title="Pontius Pilate">Pontius Pilate</a>, who is portrayed highly positively as a Christian himself.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gamaliel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:352px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg/350px-Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="255" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg/525px-Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg/700px-Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1167" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Stephengamalielnicodemus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Stephen Mourned by Saints Gamaliel and Nicodemus</i>, follower of Carlo Saraceni, c. 1615, Museum of Fine Arts, Boston</div></div></div>
<p>Ecclesiastical tradition claims that Gamaliel had embraced the <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian faith</a> and his tolerant attitude toward <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christianity">early Christians</a> is explained by this. According to <a href="/wiki/Photios_I_of_Constantinople" title="Photios I of Constantinople">Photios I of Constantinople</a>, he was baptised by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_the_Apostle" title="John the Apostle">John the Apostle</a>, together with his son <a href="/wiki/Abibon" title="Abibon">Abibon</a> (Abibo, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Abibas" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Abibas">Abibas</a>, Abibus) and <a href="/wiki/Nicodemus" title="Nicodemus">Nicodemus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Clementine_Literature" class="mw-redirect" title="Clementine Literature">Clementine Literature</a> suggested that he maintained secrecy about the conversion and continued to be a member of the Sanhedrin for the purpose of covertly assisting his fellow Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Some scholars consider the traditions to be spurious,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> and the passage in which Gamaliel is mentioned does not state that he became a Christian either implicitly or explicitly.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> venerates Gamaliel as a saint, and he is commemorated on August 2,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> the date when <a href="/wiki/Sacred_tradition" title="Sacred tradition">tradition</a> holds that his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were found, along with those of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen" title="Saint Stephen">Stephen</a> the <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">Protomartyr</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abibon" title="Abibon">Abibon</a> (Gamaliel's son), and <a href="/wiki/Nicodemus" title="Nicodemus">Nicodemus</a>. The traditional liturgical calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> celebrates the same feast day of the finding of the relics on August 3. It is said that in the fifth century, by a miracle, his body had been discovered and taken to <a href="/wiki/Piazza_dei_Miracoli#Duomo" title="Piazza dei Miracoli">Pisa Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gamaliel is referred to in the 15th-century <a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a> document, <i><a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Ll%C3%A0tzer" class="mw-redirect" title="Acts of Llàtzer">Acts of Llàtzer</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
