<p><b>Amadeus of Portugal</b> (<a href="/wiki/Campo_Maior,_Portugal" title="Campo Maior, Portugal">Campo Maior, Portugal</a> ca. 1420 – <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Milan" title="Duchy of Milan">Duchy of Milan</a>, 10 August 1482), born <b>João de Menezes da Silva</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_people" title="Portuguese people">Portuguese</a> <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">nobleman</a> who became first a <a href="/wiki/Hieronymites" title="Hieronymites">Hieronymite</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, then left that life to become a <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan Order</a>. Later he became a reformer of that <a href="/wiki/Religious_order_(Catholic)" title="Religious order (Catholic)">religious order</a>, which led to his founding of a distinct branch of the Friars Minor that was named after him, but later suppressed by the Pope in order to unite them into one great family of <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Friars Minor Observants</a> (1568).
</p><p>His <i><b>Apocalypsis nova</b></i>, which contained prophecies of a pope, the "Angelic Pastor", who would work with an emperor to restore harmony in the church and the world, was influential well into the next century, in Rome and the monarchies of <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_church">Life and church</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and church">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>He was born <a href="/wiki/De_Silva_Fern%C3%A1ndez_de_H%C3%ADjar_Portugal_family" title="De Silva Fernández de Híjar Portugal family">João de Menezes da Silva</a> in 1420 in <a href="/wiki/Campo_Maior,_Portugal" title="Campo Maior, Portugal">Campo Maior</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal" title="Kingdom of Portugal">Portugal</a>, the youngest of the eleven children of <a href="https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_Gomes_da_Silva,_1.%C2%BA_alcaide_de_Campo_Maior" class="extiw" title="pt:Rui Gomes da Silva, 1.º alcaide de Campo Maior">Rui Gomes da Silva</a>, the first <a href="/wiki/Castellan#Portugal" title="Castellan">alcaide</a> of Campo Maior,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> on the border of Castile and Portugal, and of Isabel de Menezes, an illegitimate daughter of <a href="/wiki/Don_(honorific)" title="Don (honorific)">Dom</a> <a href="/wiki/Pedro_de_Meneses,_1st_Count_of_Vila_Real" title="Pedro de Meneses, 1st Count of Vila Real">Pedro de Meneses, 1st Count of Vila Real</a> and 2nd <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Viana_do_Alentejo" title="Count of Viana do Alentejo">Count of Viana do Alentejo</a>, under whom Silva served in <a href="/wiki/Ceuta" title="Ceuta">Ceuta</a>. One of his sisters was <a href="/wiki/Beatrice_of_Silva" title="Beatrice of Silva">Beatrice of Silva</a>, a noted <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Marian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a> and the foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism#Roman_Catholicism" title="Christian monasticism">monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Conceptionists" title="Conceptionists">Order of the Immaculate Conception</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Silva married at eighteen, but left his bride the instant he was married, and went into Spain, where he fought against the Moors under John II. He then decided to become a monk, beginning his religious life in the <a href="/wiki/Hieronymite" class="mw-redirect" title="Hieronymite">Hieronymite</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_de_Guadalupe" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa María de Guadalupe">Santa María de Guadalupe</a>, where he spent about ten years.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Desirous of joining the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>, he abandoned that life and went to <a href="/wiki/%C3%9Abeda" title="Úbeda">Úbeda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Castile" title="Crown of Castile">Castile</a>, where he was received into the order in 1452, entering as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>. He chose to seek <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> after a few years, and was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> in 1459. After that, while living in various friaries, chiefly in <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, he attracted attention by his virtue and purported miracles. Under the protection of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a>, he established the friary of Our Lady of Peace (1469) which became the center of a Franciscan reform. The <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of the Order, <a href="/wiki/Francesco_della_Rovere" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco della Rovere">Francesco della Rovere</a>, extended his protection to him. When later the Minister General became <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a> he called Amadeus to Rome to be his <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Other foundations were then made in Italy, among them one at Rome. He returned to Milan, where he was taken sick, and died in August 1482.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Apocalypsis_nova_(Veneto,_c._1500).png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Apocalypsis_nova_%28Veneto%2C_c._1500%29.png/220px-Apocalypsis_nova_%28Veneto%2C_c._1500%29.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="324" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Apocalypsis_nova_%28Veneto%2C_c._1500%29.png/330px-Apocalypsis_nova_%28Veneto%2C_c._1500%29.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Apocalypsis_nova_%28Veneto%2C_c._1500%29.png/440px-Apocalypsis_nova_%28Veneto%2C_c._1500%29.png 2x" data-file-width="712" data-file-height="1049" /></a><figcaption>The opening page of an illuminated manuscript of Amadeus's <i>Apocalypsis Nova</i>, made in <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a>, c. 1500</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Supernatural favors attributed to his intercession aided in the spread of his <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)#Roman_Catholic_&#39;&#39;cultus&#39;&#39;" title="Cult (religious practice)"><i>cultus</i></a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> testify to the authenticity of the title "Blessed" bestowed on him.
</p><p>The friaries he founded continued, after his death, to form a distinct branch of the Minorites. These friars were called the Amadeans or Amadists, and they had twenty-eight houses in Italy, the chief one being Saint Peter de Montorio in Rome. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_VIII" title="Pope Innocent VIII">Pope Innocent VIII</a> gave them the friary of Saint Genesto near <a href="/wiki/Cartagena,_Spain" title="Cartagena, Spain">Cartagena</a>, in Castile (1493). The successors of  Amadeus: Georges de Val-Camonique, Gilles de Montferrat, Jean Allemand and Bonaventura de Cremona, preserved his foundation in its original spirit until <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> suppressed it, along with similar branches of the Franciscan Order, uniting them into one great family of Friars Minor Observants (1568).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He composed a treatise entitled <i>De revelationibus et prophetiis</i>, two copies of which are mentioned by Nicholas Antonio. The work of another Amadeus, <i>Homilies on the Blessed Virgin</i>, has been erroneously attributed to him. His <i>Apocalypsis nova</i> is a dialogue with the <a href="/wiki/Archangel" title="Archangel">Archangel</a> <a href="/wiki/Gabriel" title="Gabriel">Gabriel</a> about Christian doctrines, which, in some parts, is a commentary on the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Revelation" title="Book of Revelation">Book of Revelation</a>.
</p>

<p>In <a href="/wiki/Ecstasy_(emotion)" title="Ecstasy (emotion)">ecstasy</a> with the Archangel Gabriel, Amadeus revealed and prayed the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Archangels" title="Seven Archangels">Seven Archangels</a> with the names that are not recognized by the Roman Catholic Church (<a href="/wiki/Jegudiel" title="Jegudiel">Jegudiel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gabriel" title="Gabriel">Gabriel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Selaphiel" title="Selaphiel">Selaphiel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Michael_(archangel)" title="Michael (archangel)">Michael</a>, <a href="/wiki/Uriel" title="Uriel">Uriel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Raphael_(archangel)" title="Raphael (archangel)">Raphael</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Barachiel" title="Barachiel">Barachiel</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Alvino_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alvino-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
