<p><b>Francis-Xavier de Montmorency-Laval</b>,  commonly referred to as <b>François de Laval</b> (30 April 1623 – 6 May 1708), was a French prelate of the Catholic Church. Consecrated a bishop in 1658, he led the Apostolic Vicariate of New France from 1658 to 1674 and then became the first bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Quebec" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Quebec">Diocese of Quebec</a> from its erection in 1674 until he retired because of poor health in 1688. He continued to work in New France until his death in 1708. Among his accomplishments was the founding of the <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9minaire_de_Qu%C3%A9bec" title="Séminaire de Québec">Séminaire de Québec</a> in 1663. Laval was a member of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Montmorency" title="House of Montmorency">Montmorency family</a>, but renounced his rights as heir so he could pursue his ecclesiastical career.
</p><p>He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 22 June 1980 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> declared him a saint on 3 April 2014.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Laval was born on 30 April 1623 in Montigny-Sur-Avre in the ancient Province of <a href="/wiki/Perche" title="Perche">Perche</a>, now the <a href="/wiki/French_departments" class="mw-redirect" title="French departments">Department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Eure-et-Loir" title="Eure-et-Loir">Eure-et-Loir</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBégin195925_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBégin195925-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father Hugues de Laval, a member of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Laval" title="House of Laval">House of Laval</a>, was the <a href="/wiki/Fief" title="Fief">Seigneur</a> of <a href="/wiki/Montigny-sur-Avre" title="Montigny-sur-Avre">Montigny</a>, Montbaudry, Alaincourt and <a href="/wiki/Revercourt" title="Revercourt">Revercourt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190617_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190617-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His mother Michelle de Péricard was from a family of hereditary officers of the Crown in Normandy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190617_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190617-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Despite his family's heritage, his parents were not considered to be wealthy. Montigny was considered equivalent to a good-sized market-town.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Laval had five brothers and two sisters. His youngest brother Henri entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine Order</a> and his sister Anne Charlotte entered the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Sisters_of_the_Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament">Congregation of Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Laval's mother served as an example of piety and encouraged him to be charitable to those who were less fortunate.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190617_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190617-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Often described as destined for an ecclesiastical lifestyle, Laval was recognized as a clear-sighted and intelligent boy and he was admitted into the "privileged ranks of those who comprised the Congregation of the Holy Virgin."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190620_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This was a society founded by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>, who aimed to inspire young people to adopt religious lifestyles, and encouraged regular prayer and spiritual practices. At the age of eight, Laval received the <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> and took <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a>, which then allowed him to enter the College of La Flèche in 1631,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> an institution that educated the sons of the elite families and guaranteed him a good education. There Laval came into contact with reports of the Jesuit missions to the Huron in Canada, which inspired him to become a missionary, like his patron saint <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1637, Laval was appointed a canon of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_%C3%89vreux" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Évreux">Cathedral of Évreux</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_%C3%89vreux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Évreux">Bishop of Évreux</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190620_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The death of Laval's father in September 1636 left his family in a precarious financial situation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBégin195915_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBégin195915-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> François received revenue from the <a href="/wiki/Prebend" class="mw-redirect" title="Prebend">prebend</a> attached to his position as a canon, which allowed him to continue his education.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBégin195915_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBégin195915-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Once he completed his classical education at the age of nineteen, Laval left La Flèche to study philosophy and theology at the College de Clermont in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However the death of his two eldest brothers in the battles at <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_N%C3%B6rdlingen_(1634)" title="Battle of Nördlingen (1634)">Nördlingen</a> in 1634 and <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Freiburg" title="Battle of Freiburg">Freiburg</a> in 1644 made him the head of the family. His mother, the Bishop of Évreux, and his cousin all argued he should abandon his ecclesiastical career.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190621_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190621-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Instead Laval helped his mother settle the family's affairs and renounced his rights of primogeniture, which then passed to his brother Jean-Louis.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190622_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190622-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Laval returned to his studies in Paris and on 1 May 1647 he was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBégin195919_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBégin195919-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The Bishop of Évreux, regretting his earlier attempt to persuade Laval to change his career path, appointed him <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">archdeacon</a> of his diocese in December 1647.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In this post Laval oversaw 155 parishes and four chapels. Laval was said to approach this task with fervour and enthusiasm.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In the following years, he devoted himself to establishing order in the parishes, providing relief for the poor, caring for the sick, and engaging in various charitable activities. In 1654, presented with the possibility of serving as a missionary in Asia, he resigned as archdeacon,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190623_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190623-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> but the project was abandoned and he was left without a clerical position.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Freed of all responsibility, Laval spent three years at a <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_retreat" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual retreat">spiritual retreat</a> near <a href="/wiki/Caen" title="Caen">Caen</a> known as the Hermitage, founded by Jean de Bernières de Louvigny, a lay leader in the reform of the Catholic Church in France.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190624_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190624-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> There he also made the acquaintance of the founder's nephew, <a href="/wiki/Henri_de_Berni%C3%A8res" title="Henri de Bernières">Henri de Bernières</a>, who became an invaluable assistant in later years. In addition to prayer and charitable work, he took on the responsibility of reforming a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> whose morals were thought to be too lax and became the administrator of two monasteries of nuns.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  His dedication to these projects earned him commendation from François de Servien, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bayeux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bayeux">Bishop of Bayeux</a>, who described him as a priest "of great piety, prudent and of unusually great competence in business matters, [who had set] fine examples of virtue".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="New_France">New France</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: New France">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval.jpg/220px-Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="250" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval.jpg/330px-Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cd/Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval.jpg 2x" data-file-width="383" data-file-height="435" /></a><figcaption>François de Laval</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/New_France" title="New France">New France</a> had no bishop for the first 50 years of its European settlement. Colonial religious officials, either Recollects or Jesuits, managed spiritual affairs. In 1646, under pressure from Rome, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">Archbishop of Rouen</a> was recognized as the authority over the Church in New France, but his authority was limited to granting faculties to clergy traveling to the colony.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004105_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004105-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Yet no one denied the need for an established  ecclesiastical presence.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966102_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966102-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Appointing a bishop was a contentious issue disputed between the Jesuits and the newly arrived <a href="/wiki/Sulpicians" class="mw-redirect" title="Sulpicians">Sulpicians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966102_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966102-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The former were quite accustomed to working independently<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004106_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004106-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and they believed a Sulpician bishop would make the Church in the colony subordinate to the Crown.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966102_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966102-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The Sulpicians proposed one of their own, <a href="/wiki/Gabriel_Thubi%C3%A8res_de_Levy_de_Queylus" title="Gabriel Thubières de Levy de Queylus">Gabriel Thubières de Levy de Queylus</a>, as bishop, while the Jesuits supported Laval and gained royal approval easily with the support of the <a href="/wiki/Queen_mother" title="Queen mother">queen mother</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Austria" title="Anne of Austria">Anne of Austria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966104–105_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966104–105-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Securing papal confirmation was another challenge.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966105_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966105-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The Holy See remained reserved regarding Laval's nomination, as the Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith wanted to assert papal authority in the colony<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> rather than allow the Jesuits to exercise control in a new diocese that was part of the ecclesiastical structure of the Catholic Church in France. Instead of naming Laval bishop of the diocese of New France, he was made a bishop of a newly created <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_vicariate" title="Apostolic vicariate">apostolic vicariate</a>, a type of jurisdiction directly managed by the Holy See through the Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith: the Apostolic Vicariate of New France.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004108Leblond_de_Brumath190626_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004108Leblond_de_Brumath190626-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Vicariate_of_New_France">Vicariate of New France</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Vicariate of New France">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 3 June 1658, the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bulls</a> were signed appointing Laval vicar apostolic of New France and titular bishop of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Petra" title="Archbishop of Petra">Petra in Palestina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966105_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966105-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> On 8 December 1658, in the church of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Germain-des-Pr%C3%A9s" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés">Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés</a> in Paris, Laval received his episcopal consecration from Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Celio_Piccolomini" title="Celio Piccolomini">Celio Piccolomini</a>, <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Nunciature_to_France" title="Apostolic Nunciature to France">Apostolic Nuncio to France</a>, assisted by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Hardouin_de_P%C3%A9r%C3%A9fixe_de_Beaumont" title="Hardouin de Péréfixe de Beaumont">Hardouin de Péréfixe de Beaumont</a> of Rodez and Bishop André du Saussay of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Toul" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Toul">Toul</a> as co-consecrators.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190626_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath190626-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Laval took an oath of loyalty to the French king and sailed from <a href="/wiki/La_Rochelle" title="La Rochelle">La Rochelle</a> for New France on 13 April 1659.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 16 June he arrived at Quebec.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He began work immediately and on the day he arrived he baptized a young <a href="/wiki/Wyandot_people" title="Wyandot people">Huron</a> and gave a dying man his last sacraments.
</p><p>While small in size, the colony still provided a number of challenges to Laval.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He found himself having to make concessions where he never thought to before to a population that, while scarce, was spread out, and was less inclined to continue under strict church discipline.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966133_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966133-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Additionally, he had to deal with the Sulpician de Queylus, who had been managing Church affairs in the colony as <a href="/wiki/Vicar_general" title="Vicar general">vicar general</a> under the authority of the Archbishop of Rouen, who continued to claim ecclesiastical jurisdiction over New France. De Queylus continued to assert his authority for nearly two years, while Laval repeatedly appealed to the king and the Holy See for support.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Laval struggled a great deal throughout his career to defend the church's power against state intrusion. Upon his arrival, Laval was adamant in asserting his primacy over the governor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>  He was immediately in opposition with Governor d’Argenson, particularly regarding ceremony and protocol.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Also, the issue of selling alcohol to the natives further fueled their feud. Laval believed that intoxicated natives were an embarrassment to the colony and endangered the lives of those around them.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> He quickly imposed the threat of excommunication on those who continued to deal in this trade. Governor D’Argenson abhorred this action, deeming it an intrusion of church into state affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  D’Argenson soon resigned and was replaced by d’Avaugour, who, in order to avoid any conflict with Laval, decreed harsh penalties against anyone caught selling alcohol to the natives.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134_22-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  Again, Laval was displeased, believing that excommunication was a far more humane consequence. When alcohol was again being sold freely to natives, in a moment of despair over the state of New France, Laval departed for France in August 1662 to consult with Louis XIV on the matter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134_22-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  Laval succeeded in bringing about d’Avaugour's recall the following year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134–135_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966134–135-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Laval returned to New France he had increased powers. King Louis XIV had assured Laval that he would have a future appointment as bishop, requested that he establish a Sovereign Council in Quebec, and even asked Laval to choose New France's next governor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966135_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966135-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> For governor, Laval chose Chevalier de Mézy, a friend from his time at the Hermitage of Caen.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966135_24-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966135-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>  In the developing Sovereign Council, which held its first session 18 September 1663, Mézy represented the first figure of authority, followed by Laval, and Gaudais-Dupon, commissioner.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECampeau1973327_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECampeau1973327-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Laval appointed Mézy hoping to have an ally among high-ranking state official. In the trade of alcohol to the natives he did find in Mézy an ally; together the two forbade the trading of alcohol.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECampeau1973327_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECampeau1973327-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>  However, constituting the Sovereign Council revealed that the two represented conflicting interests in matters of church and state. Soon, another conflict between Laval and governor ensued, leading Laval to take to the streets with drums to tell his version of the feud.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966136_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966136-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Upon Mézy's death, the Sovereign council was reorganized. Intendant Jean Talon was added, and immediately assumed the functions previously exercised by Laval.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966136_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966136-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>  With this change in the council Laval began to attend the council's meetings less frequently; from then on Laval retreated somewhat from state affairs and focused purely on ecclesiastical matters.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966136_26-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966136-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of Laval's principal projects as apostolic vicar was the education and training of priests and church administrators.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966137_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966137-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9minaire_de_Quebec" class="mw-redirect" title="Séminaire de Quebec">Major Seminary of Quebec</a> opened on 26 March 1663. Affiliated with the <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9minaire_des_Missions_%C3%89trang%C3%A8res" class="mw-redirect" title="Séminaire des Missions Étrangères">Séminaire des Missions Étrangères</a>, in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPlouffe_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlouffe-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> In October 1668, Laval added a minor seminary to train boys It was meant to train boys who might have priestly vocation<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966137_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966137-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> both of French and indigenous origins.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPlouffe_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlouffe-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> It opened with eight French students and six Huron, but grew quickly as French missionaries, especially Sulpicians, arrived to serve as teachers.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath1906105_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath1906105-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Laval wanted these teachers to spread the word that his institution was to establish a sense of charity and love for religion in the colony and not another source of law or authority.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECampeau1973323_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECampeau1973323-30">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Laval's view of the Grand Séminaire was greater than a mere teaching academy. He hoped that it would become a home for all parochial priests. Laval encouraged them to see it as their true home and as a place to which they may turn to in sickness or old age.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966137_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966137-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>  Furthermore, he wanted the seminary to become a paymaster for all priests and parishes, which meant that it had to be well funded. In order to accomplish this feat, Laval donated most of his own fortune to the seminary since it had now become his home as well.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECampeau1973319_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECampeau1973319-31">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> He also convinced the king, <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIV_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XIV of France">Louis XIV</a>, to give him the income of three different abbeys in France. Moreover, since his institution was expected to pay off all priests, Laval thought it would be normal to receive the incomes levied by their parishes. This idea was however met with a lot of resistance from the population, which was not accustomed to contributing to the upkeep of religious institutions. His original goal of demanding a tax worth one-thirteenth of the produce of farms was met with violent resistance, which forced him to reduce it to one-twenty-sixth.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966138_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966138-32">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Laval shared a large part of his administrative work with other clerics to develop their experience. To lead the seminary he appointed his companion from France, Henri de Bernière, linking this role with Bernière's other assignment a pastor of the parish of Quebec. Furthermore, he also appointed five other directors who would form the bishop's advisory body.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966138_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966138-32">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> In 1668 he appointed the previously troublesome de Queylus as the first Superior of a new seminary in Ville-Marie.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers"><span title="The time period mentioned near this tag is ambiguous. (July 2022)">when?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Laval also took interest in practical education for craftsmen and farmers, founding a school of arts and crafts at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Joachim,_Quebec" title="Saint-Joachim, Quebec">Saint-Joachim</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966138_32-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966138-32">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers"><span title="The time period mentioned near this tag is ambiguous. (July 2022)">when?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Diocese_of_Quebec">Diocese of Quebec</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Diocese of Quebec">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1674, fifteen years after his arrival in New France, Laval asked that the vicariate be promoted to a diocese. The Holy See established the Diocese of Quebec on 1 October 1674, naming Laval as its first bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109_21-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChoquette2004109-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He revisited the issue of alcohol sales to the indigenous peoples. In 1675, Laval, over the objections of Governor <a href="/wiki/Louis_de_Buade_de_Frontenac" title="Louis de Buade de Frontenac">Louis de Buade de Frontenac</a>, excommunicated all who sold alcohol to them.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966150_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966150-33">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> On 24 May 1679 Laval succeeded in obtaining a royal decree banning the trade.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVachon_3-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVachon-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1678, he obtained a royal edict stating that permanent curacies would be established in the colony. In 1681, he drew up the boundaries of parishes.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Retirement">Retirement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Laval&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Retirement">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Laval eventually realised that his health was declining and that he could no longer run his large diocese, which extended from Acadia to Lake Michigan. In 1688, he passed on his responsibilities as bishop to <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_de_La_Croix_de_Chevri%C3%A8res_de_Saint-Vallier" title="Jean-Baptiste de La Croix de Chevrières de Saint-Vallier">Jean-Baptiste de La Croix de Chevrières de Saint-Vallier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966151–52_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh1966151–52-34">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Laval continued to collaborate with the colony's high religious authorities, until his last days. He helped the poor with his presence and his gifts of charity. He never missed a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> or a day of <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasting</a>, despite his ever declining health. By 1707, he had developed an ulcer which eventually took his life on 6 May 1708.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath1906261_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath1906261-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> His body was placed in a coffin in the cathedral; however his heart was kept in the chapel of the seminary to which he had dedicated most of his life and fortune.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath1906265_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELeblond_de_Brumath1906265-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Laval's remains have been entombed in a shrine for personal veneration in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral-Basilica_of_Notre-Dame_de_Qu%C3%A9bec" title="Cathedral-Basilica of Notre-Dame de Québec">Cathedral-Basilica of Notre-Dame de Québec</a>. He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1980. He was granted <a href="/wiki/Equivalent_canonization" title="Equivalent canonization">Equivalent canonization</a> on 3 April 2014 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Laval" title="Université Laval">Université Laval</a>, founded 1852, was named in his honor. The city of <a href="/wiki/Laval,_Quebec" title="Laval, Quebec">Laval, Quebec</a>, north of <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, is also named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
