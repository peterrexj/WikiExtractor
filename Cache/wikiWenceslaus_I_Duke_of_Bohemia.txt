<p><b>Wenceslaus I</b> (<a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Václav</i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="cs-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Czech" title="Help:IPA/Czech">[ˈvaːtslaf]</a></span>&#32;<span class="nowrap" style="font-size:85%">(<span class="unicode haudio"><span class="fn"><span style="white-space:nowrap;margin-right:.25em;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cs-svaty_Vaclav.ogg" title="About this sound"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/11px-Loudspeaker.svg.png" decoding="async" width="11" height="11" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/17px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Loudspeaker.svg/22px-Loudspeaker.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="20" data-file-height="20" /></a></span><a href="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/34/Cs-svaty_Vaclav.ogg" class="internal" title="Cs-svaty Vaclav.ogg">listen</a></span></span>)</span>; c. 907 – 28 September 935 or 929), <b>Wenceslas I</b> or <i>Václav the Good</i><sup id="cite_ref-Christiansen_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christiansen-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> was the Duke (<i><a href="/wiki/Knyaz" title="Knyaz">kníže</a></i>) of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Bohemia" title="Duchy of Bohemia">Bohemia</a> from 921 until his death, probably in 935.  According to the legend, he was assassinated by his younger brother, <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" title="Boleslaus I, Duke of Bohemia">Boleslaus the Cruel</a>.
</p><p>His martyrdom and the popularity of several biographies gave rise to a reputation for heroic virtue that resulted in his sainthood. He was posthumously declared to be a king and patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech state</a>. He is the subject of the well-known "<a href="/wiki/Good_King_Wenceslas" title="Good King Wenceslas">Good King Wenceslas</a>", a carol for <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen%27s_Day" title="Saint Stephen&#39;s Day">Saint Stephen's Day</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wenceslaus was the son of <a href="/wiki/Vratislaus_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Vratislaus I">Vratislaus I</a>, Duke of Bohemia from the <a href="/wiki/P%C5%99emyslid_dynasty" title="Přemyslid dynasty">Přemyslid dynasty</a>. His grandfather, <a href="/wiki/Bo%C5%99ivoj_I_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bořivoj I of Bohemia">Bořivoj I of Bohemia</a>, was converted to Christianity by <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Cyril and Methodius</a>. His mother, <a href="/wiki/Drahom%C3%ADra" title="Drahomíra">Drahomíra</a>, was the daughter of a <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> tribal chief of the <a href="/wiki/Havelli" class="mw-redirect" title="Havelli">Havelli</a>, but was baptized at the time of her marriage. His paternal grandmother, <a href="/wiki/Ludmila_of_Bohemia" title="Ludmila of Bohemia">Ludmila of Bohemia</a>, saw to it that he was educated in the Old Slavonic language and, at an early age, Wenceslaus was sent to the college at <a href="/wiki/Bude%C4%8D_(Kladno_District)" title="Budeč (Kladno District)">Budeč</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 921, when Wenceslaus was about thirteen, his father died and his grandmother became regent. Jealous of the influence that Ludmila wielded over Wenceslaus, Drahomíra arranged to have her killed. Ludmila was at <a href="/wiki/Tet%C3%ADn_(Beroun_District)" title="Tetín (Beroun District)">Tetín</a> Castle near <a href="/wiki/Beroun" title="Beroun">Beroun</a> when assassins murdered her on 15 September 921. She is said to have been strangled by them with her veil. She was at first buried in the church of St. Michael at Tetín, but her remains were later removed, probably by Wenceslaus,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> to the <a href="/wiki/St._George%27s_Basilica,_Prague" title="St. George&#39;s Basilica, Prague">church of St. George in Prague</a>, which had been built by his father.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Drahomíra then assumed the role of regent and immediately initiated measures against Christians. When Wenceslaus was 18, those Christian nobles who remained rebelled against Drahomira. The uprising was successful, and Drahomira was sent into exile to Budeč.
</p>

<p>With the support of the nobles, Wenceslaus took control of the government.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He "...reined in the dependent dukes who had become restive under the regency and used Christianity to strengthen his state."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the fall of <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a>, the rulers of the Bohemian Duchy had to deal both with continuous raids by the <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Hungarian people">Magyars</a> and the forces of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony" title="Duchy of Saxony">Saxon</a> and <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler" title="Henry the Fowler">Henry the Fowler</a>, who had started several eastern campaigns into the adjacent lands of the <a href="/wiki/Polabian_Slavs" title="Polabian Slavs">Polabian Slavs</a>, homeland of Wenceslaus's mother. To withstand Saxon overlordship, Wenceslaus's father Vratislaus had forged an alliance with the <a href="/wiki/History_of_Bavaria" title="History of Bavaria">Bavarian</a> duke <a href="/wiki/Arnulf,_Duke_of_Bavaria" title="Arnulf, Duke of Bavaria">Arnulf</a>, a fierce opponent of King Henry at that time. The alliance became worthless, however, when Arnulf and Henry reconciled at <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a> in 921.
</p><p>Early in 929, the joint forces of Duke Arnulf of Bavaria and King Henry I the Fowler reached <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> in a sudden attack that forced Wenceslaus to resume the payment of a tribute first imposed by the <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Carinthia" title="Arnulf of Carinthia">Arnulf of Carinthia</a> in 895.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Henry had been forced to pay a huge tribute to the Magyars in 926 and needed the Bohemian tribute, which Wenceslaus probably refused to pay after the reconciliation between Arnulf and Henry.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Another possible reason for the attack was the formation of the anti-Saxon alliance between Bohemia, the Polabian Slavs, and the Magyars.
</p><p>Wenceslaus introduced German priests into his realm and favoured the Latin rite instead of the old Slavic, which had gone into disuse in many places for want of priests.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He also founded a rotunda consecrated to <a href="/wiki/St._Vitus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Vitus">St. Vitus</a> at <a href="/wiki/Prague_Castle" title="Prague Castle">Prague Castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> that was the basis of present-day <a href="/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral" title="St. Vitus Cathedral">St. Vitus Cathedral</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Assassination">Assassination</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Assassination">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ermordung_Wenzels_(cropped).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Ermordung_Wenzels_%28cropped%29.jpg/220px-Ermordung_Wenzels_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="125" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Ermordung_Wenzels_%28cropped%29.jpg/330px-Ermordung_Wenzels_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Ermordung_Wenzels_%28cropped%29.jpg/440px-Ermordung_Wenzels_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="618" data-file-height="351" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ermordung_Wenzels_(cropped).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Wenceslaus flees from his brother who is wielding a sword, but the priest closes the door of the church (from Gumpold's Codex)</div></div></div>
<p>In September 935, a group of nobles allied with Wenceslaus's younger brother Boleslav plotted to kill him. After Boleslav invited Wenceslaus to a celebration of the feast of <a href="/wiki/Sts._Cosmas_and_Damian" class="mw-redirect" title="Sts. Cosmas and Damian">Saints Cosmas and Damian</a> in <a href="/wiki/Star%C3%A1_Boleslav" class="mw-redirect" title="Stará Boleslav">Stará Boleslav</a>, three of Boleslav's companions (Tira, Česta, and Hněvsa) fell on the duke and stabbed him to death.<sup id="cite_ref-Newton_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Newton-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> As the duke fell, Boleslav ran him through with a lance.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_of_Prague" title="Cosmas of Prague">Cosmas of Prague</a>, in his <i>Chronica Boëmorum</i> of the early 12th century, one of Boleslav's sons was born on the day of Wenceslaus's death. Because of the ominous circumstance of his birth, the infant was named <a href="/wiki/Strachkvas" title="Strachkvas">Strachkvas</a>, which means "a dreadful feast".<sup id="cite_ref-Newton_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Newton-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also a tradition that Wenceslaus's loyal servant <a href="/wiki/Podevin" title="Podevin">Podevin</a> avenged his death by killing one of the chief conspirators, an act for which he was executed by Boleslav.<sup id="cite_ref-gibbs_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gibbs-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The assassination of Wenceslaus has been characterized as an important turning point in early Bohemian history, as the rule of Boleslav I saw him renounce the Franks, centralize power in Bohemia and expand the territory of the polity.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Wenceslaus I, Duke of Bohemia</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:Wenzeslaus_by_Peter_Parler.JPG" class="image"><img alt="Wenzeslaus by Peter Parler.JPG" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Wenzeslaus_by_Peter_Parler.JPG/200px-Wenzeslaus_by_Peter_Parler.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="290" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Wenzeslaus_by_Peter_Parler.JPG/300px-Wenzeslaus_by_Peter_Parler.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/13/Wenzeslaus_by_Peter_Parler.JPG 2x" data-file-width="310" data-file-height="450" /></a><div class="infobox-caption">Statue of Saint Wenceslaus in <a href="/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral" title="St. Vitus Cathedral">St. Vitus Cathedral</a>, Prague. The head of the statue apparently fits the measurements of Wenceslaus's skull.</div></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;"><a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">Martyr</a></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 907<br /><a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">28 September 935<br /><a href="/wiki/Star%C3%A1_Boleslav" class="mw-redirect" title="Stará Boleslav">Stará Boleslav</a>, Bohemia</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral" title="St. Vitus Cathedral">St. Vitus Cathedral</a>, Prague</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">28 September</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Crown_(headgear)" class="mw-redirect" title="Crown (headgear)">Crown</a>, dagger, burning eagle on a banner</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Wenceslaus was considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> immediately after his death, when a cult of Wenceslaus grew up in <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a> and in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Within a few decades, four biographies of him were in circulation.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> These <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> had a powerful influence on the <a href="/wiki/High_Middle_Ages" title="High Middle Ages">High Middle Ages</a> concept of the <i>rex justus</i> (righteous king), a <a href="/wiki/Monarch" title="Monarch">monarch</a> whose power stems mainly from his great piety as well as his princely vigor.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Referring approvingly to these hagiographies, the chronicler <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_of_Prague" title="Cosmas of Prague">Cosmas of Prague</a>, writing in about the year 1119, states:<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>But his deeds I think you know better than I could tell you; for, as is read in his <i>Passion</i>, no one doubts that, rising every night from his noble bed, with bare feet and only one chamberlain, he went around to God’s churches and gave alms generously to widows, orphans, those in prison and afflicted by every difficulty, so much so that he was considered, not a prince, but the father of all the wretched.</p></blockquote>
<p>Several centuries later this legend was asserted as fact by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_II" title="Pope Pius II">Pope Pius II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although Wenceslaus was only a duke during his lifetime, Holy Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto I</a> posthumously "conferred on [Wenceslaus] the regal dignity and title", which is why he is referred to as "king" in legend and song.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymn</a> "Svatý Václave" (Saint Wenceslaus) or "<a href="/wiki/Saint_Wenceslas_Chorale" title="Saint Wenceslas Chorale">Saint Wenceslas Chorale</a>" is one of the oldest known Czech songs. Traceable to the 12th century AD, it is still among the most popular religious songs in the Bohemian lands. In 1918, at the founding of the modern Czechoslovak state, the song was discussed as a possible choice for the national anthem. During the <a href="/wiki/German_occupation_of_Czechoslovakia" class="mw-redirect" title="German occupation of Czechoslovakia">Nazi occupation</a>, it was often played along with the Czech anthem.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Wenceslaus's feast day is celebrated on <a href="/wiki/September_28_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="September 28 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">September 28</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> On this day, celebrations and a pilgrimage are held in the city of <a href="/wiki/Brand%C3%BDs_nad_Labem-Star%C3%A1_Boleslav" title="Brandýs nad Labem-Stará Boleslav">Stará Boleslav</a>, whereas the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translation of his relics</a>, which took place in 938, is commemorated on <a href="/wiki/March_4_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="March 4 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">4 March</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Since 2000, the September 28 feast day has been a <a href="/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Czech_Republic" title="Public holidays in the Czech Republic">public holiday in the Czech Republic</a>, celebrated as Czech Statehood Day.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_legend">In legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Lebka_vaclavova.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Lebka_vaclavova.JPG/220px-Lebka_vaclavova.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Lebka_vaclavova.JPG/330px-Lebka_vaclavova.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Lebka_vaclavova.JPG/440px-Lebka_vaclavova.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Lebka_vaclavova.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Miloslav_Vlk" title="Miloslav Vlk">Miloslav Vlk</a> with the skull of Saint Wenceslaus during a procession on 28 September 2006</div></div></div>
<p>According to legend, one Count Radislas rose in rebellion and marched against King Wenceslaus. The latter sent a deputation with offers of peace, but Radislas viewed this as a sign of cowardice. The two armies were drawn up opposite each other in battle array, when Wenceslaus, to avoid shedding innocent blood, challenged Radislas to single combat. As Radislas advanced toward the king, he saw by Wenceslaus's side two angels, who cried: "Stand off!" Thunderstruck, Radislas repented his rebellion, threw himself from his horse at Wenceslaus's feet, and asked for pardon. Wenceslaus raised him and kindly received him again into favor.
</p><p>A second enduring <a href="/wiki/King_asleep_in_mountain" title="King asleep in mountain">legend</a> claims an army of knights sleeps under <a href="/wiki/Blan%C3%ADk" title="Blaník">Blaník</a>, a mountain in the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech Republic</a>. They will awake and, under the command of Wenceslaus, bring aid to the Czech people in their ultimate danger. There is a similar legend in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> which says that when the Motherland is in danger or in its darkest times and close to ruin, the equestrian statue of King Wenceslaus in Wenceslaus Square will come to life, raise the army sleeping in Blaník, and upon crossing the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a> his horse will stumble and trip over a stone, revealing the legendary sword of Bruncvík. With this sword, King Wenceslaus will slay all the enemies of the Czechs, bringing peace and prosperity to the land.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Wenceslaus is the subject of the popular <a href="/wiki/St._Stephen%27s_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Stephen&#39;s Day">Saint Stephen's Day</a> (celebrated on December 26 in the West) carol "<a href="/wiki/Good_King_Wenceslas" title="Good King Wenceslas">Good King Wenceslas</a>". It was published by <a href="/wiki/John_Mason_Neale" title="John Mason Neale">John Mason Neale</a> in 1853, and may be a translation of a poem by Czech poet Václav Alois Svoboda<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>.
The usual American English spelling of the duke's name, "Wenceslaus", is occasionally encountered in later textual variants of the carol, although it was not used by Neale in his version.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Wenceslaus is not to be confused with King <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_I_of_Bohemia" title="Wenceslaus I of Bohemia">Wenceslaus I of Bohemia</a> (Wenceslaus I Premyslid), who lived more than three centuries later.
</p>

<p>An <a href="/wiki/Statue_of_Saint_Wenceslas,_Wenceslas_Square" title="Statue of Saint Wenceslas, Wenceslas Square">equestrian statue of Saint Wenceslaus</a> and other patrons of Bohemia (St. Adalbert, St. Ludmila, St. Prokop and St. Agnes of Bohemia) are located on <a href="/wiki/Wenceslaus_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Wenceslaus Square">Wenceslaus Square</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>. The statue is a popular meeting place in Prague. Demonstrations against the Communist regime were held there.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His helmet and armour are on display inside Prague Castle.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The lavish 1930 silent film <a href="/wiki/St._Wenceslas_(film)" title="St. Wenceslas (film)"><i>St. Wenceslas</i></a> was at the time the most expensive Czech film ever made.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Ogden_Nash" title="Ogden Nash">Ogden Nash</a> wrote a comic epic poem, "The Christmas that Almost Wasn't" (1957), in which a boy awakens Wenceslaus and his knights to save the kingdom of Lullapat from usurpers who have outlawed Christmas, with elements from the legend of Wenceslas.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The 1994 television film, <i>Good King Wenceslas</i>, is a highly fictional account of his early life. The film stars <a href="/wiki/Jonathan_Brandis" title="Jonathan Brandis">Jonathan Brandis</a> in the title role, supported by <a href="/wiki/Leo_McKern" title="Leo McKern">Leo McKern</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stefanie_Powers" title="Stefanie Powers">Stefanie Powers</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Joan_Fontaine" title="Joan Fontaine">Joan Fontaine</a> as Ludmila.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
