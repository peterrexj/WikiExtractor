<p><b>John of Beverley</b> (died 7 May 721) was an English bishop active in the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>. He was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hexham" title="Bishop of Hexham">bishop of Hexham</a> and then the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of York">bishop of York</a>, which was the most important religious designation in the area. He went on to found the town of <a href="/wiki/Beverley" title="Beverley">Beverley</a> by building the first structure there, a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>. John was associated with miracles during and after his lifetime and was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in 1037. As this is prior to the Great East–West <a href="/wiki/Schism_of_1054" class="mw-redirect" title="Schism of 1054">Schism of 1054</a>, he is also recognised as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Beverley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was said to have been born of noble parents at <a href="/wiki/Harpham" title="Harpham">Harpham</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/East_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="East Riding of Yorkshire">East Riding of Yorkshire</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh316_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh316-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is said to have received his education at <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> under <a href="/wiki/Adrian_of_Canterbury" title="Adrian of Canterbury">Adrian</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh316_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh316-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and not <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a> as per some sources. However, all these statements are first recorded after his canonization in 1037, and may not be reliable.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was for a time a member of the <a href="/wiki/Whitby_Abbey" title="Whitby Abbey">Whitby</a> community, under <a href="/wiki/St_Hilda" class="mw-redirect" title="St Hilda">St Hilda</a>, a fact recorded by his friend <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He won renown as a preacher, displaying marked erudition in expounding Scripture.<sup id="cite_ref-macauley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macauley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 687 he was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hexham" title="Bishop of Hexham">bishop of Hexham</a> by <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore of Tarsus</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-CH_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Archbishop of Canterbury (602–690), and in 705 was promoted to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of York">bishopric of York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook224_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook224-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Of his new activity little is known beyond that he was diligent in visitation, considerate towards the poor, and attentive to the training of students whom he maintained under his personal charge.<sup id="cite_ref-macauley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macauley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He ordained Bede as a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and as a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh316_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh316-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He resigned perhaps about 717 and retired to a monastery which he had founded at Beverley, where he died on 7 May 721.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh316_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh316-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Beverley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was canonised in 1037,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and his feast is celebrated annually on 7 May. His translation is also celebrated on 25 October in the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS278_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS278-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Many miracles of healing are ascribed to John, whose pupils were numerous and devoted to him, and the popularity of his cult was a major factor in the prosperity of Beverley during the Middle Ages. He was celebrated for his scholarship as well as for his virtues. Around 1066, <a href="/wiki/Folcard" title="Folcard">Folcard</a> was commissioned to write a Life of John.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> An account of John's miracles was written by <a href="/wiki/William_Ketel" title="William Ketel">William Ketel</a> in the 11th or 12th century which contains the first mention of King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelstan" title="Æthelstan">Æthelstan</a>'s visit to Beverley.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The earliest existing mention of John's banner is in 1138 when <a href="/wiki/Thurstan" title="Thurstan">Thurstan</a> incorporated it into the standard which gave its name to the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Standard" title="Battle of the Standard">Battle of the Standard</a>. By 1266 it was accepted that when levies were made in Yorkshire for the royal army, it was sufficient for Beverley to send one man with the banner of John.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1292 a new shrine was commissioned. An extant contract between Roger de Faringdon and the canons of <a href="/wiki/Beverley_Minster" title="Beverley Minster">Beverley Minster</a> states:
</p>
<blockquote><p>For a <a href="/wiki/Silver-gilt" title="Silver-gilt">silver-gilt</a> shrine, made from gold and silver supplied by the Chapter, 5ft long, 1ft wide. Of proportionate height, beautiful, and adorned with plates and columns in architectural style with figures everywhere of size and number as the Chapter determine, and canopies and pinnacles before and behind, and other proper ornaments. Roger to remake any figure at the whim of The Chapter. The pay to be silver equal to the weight used before gilding. Roger may not undertake any other work before completion.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England">Edward I</a> was a devotee of John and furthered the cult. In 1295 Edward established a <a href="/wiki/Chantry" title="Chantry">chantry</a> in Beverley Minster in the saint's honour.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1301, he gave 50 marks towards the building of the shrine and diverted half of a fine owed by the town to the same purpose and ceded the remaining half.
</p><p>On 25 October 1307 John's relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to the new tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh316_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh316-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
Edward I visited the Minster in 1296, 1297, and 1300 on his way north to fight with the Scottish and took the banner of John to aid him. <a href="/wiki/Edward_II_of_England" title="Edward II of England">Edward II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Edward_III_of_England" title="Edward III of England">Edward III</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_England" title="Henry IV of England">Henry IV</a> also used the banner in military campaigns.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the Middle Ages his name was also attached to the legend of a hermit who commits grave sins but nonetheless enjoys God's grace. This text survives in the Dutch <a href="/wiki/Chapbook" title="Chapbook">chapbook</a> <i><a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/nl:Jan_van_Beverley" class="extiw" title="s:nl:Jan van Beverley">Historie van Jan van Beverley</a></i>, printed by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_van_der_Noot" title="Thomas van der Noot">Thomas van der Noot</a> in Brussels in c.1512.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Henry_V_of_England" title="Henry V of England">Henry V</a> gave the credit for his victory at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt" title="Battle of Agincourt">Battle of Agincourt</a> to the miraculous intervention of John,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> the battle having been fought on the anniversary of John's translation.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh316_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh316-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On the day of the battle, blood and oil were seen running from the tomb. Henry made John one of the patrons of the royal household and ordered that his feast was to be celebrated throughout England. Henry and his queen came to Beverley in 1420 to make offerings at the saint's shrine.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Beverley_minster.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Beverley_minster.jpg/300px-Beverley_minster.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="450" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Beverley_minster.jpg/450px-Beverley_minster.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Beverley_minster.jpg/600px-Beverley_minster.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1440" data-file-height="2160" /></a><figcaption>The West front of <a href="/wiki/Beverley_Minster" title="Beverley Minster">Beverley Minster</a> today. The relics of John of Beverley are housed there, covered by a marble slab.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1541, the shrine was destroyed on the orders of <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a> as part of the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Its contents disappeared from records. In 1664, workmen discovered a vault under the floor of the minster's <a href="/wiki/Nave" title="Nave">nave</a>. Made of stone, it was 15&#160;ft long (4.6&#160;m) and 2&#160;ft wide (0.61&#160;m) at the head and 1&#160;ft wide (0.30&#160;m) at the base. Encased in lead, were found ashes, six beads, three great brass pins and four large iron nails. The lead had the following inscription:
</p>
<blockquote><p>In the year from the incarnation of our Lord, 1188, this church was burnt in the month of September, the night after the feast of St Matthew the Apostle and in the year 1197, the 6th of the ides of March, there was an inquisition made of the relics of the Blessed John in this place, and these bones were found in the east part of this sepulchre, and redeposited; dust mixed with mortar was found likewise and re-interred.</p></blockquote>
<p>In 1738, when the present minster floor was laid, the same relics were dug up and replaced in the same position with an arched brick vault over them. This was covered by a marble slab, similar to others in the nave. On the roof of the church, over the tomb, <i>Reliquas beati Johanis Beverlacenic his undicat</i>.
</p><p>The inscription on the tomb now reads:
</p><p>HERE LIES <br />THE BODY OF SAINT JOHN OF BEVERLEY<br />FOUNDER OF THIS CHURCH<br /> BISHOP OF HEXHAM AD 687–705<br /> BISHOP OF YORK A.D. 705–718<br /> HE WAS BORN AT HARPHAM<br /> AND DIED AT BEVERLEY<br /> AD 721
</p><p>In recent times, the feast of St John has been marked in Beverley in two ways. On the Thursday nearest 7 May the choir and members of the congregation of Beverley Minster go the church at Harpham and process to the well. The well is decorated (before the event) with flowers. After singing an <a href="/wiki/Anthem" title="Anthem">anthem</a> and praying, the procession returns to the church for choral <a href="/wiki/Evening_Prayer_(Anglican)" class="mw-redirect" title="Evening Prayer (Anglican)">evensong</a>.
</p><p>On the Sunday nearest 7 May the civic dignitaries process in full <a href="/wiki/Regalia" title="Regalia">regalia</a> with <a href="/wiki/Ceremonial_mace" title="Ceremonial mace">mace</a> bearers to the minster. The procession enters by the Great West Door. During the following service, children from Harpham present <a href="/wiki/Primula_vulgaris" title="Primula vulgaris">primroses</a> gathered from the woods around the village. The flowers are placed around John's tomb.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Julian_of_Norwich" title="Julian of Norwich">Julian of Norwich</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Fisher" title="John Fisher">John Fisher</a> were devotees of John of Beverley.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS278_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS278-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
