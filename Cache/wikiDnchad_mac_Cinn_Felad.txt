					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
<b>Dúnchad mac Cinn Fáelad</b> (also called <b>Dunichad</b>, <b>Duncad</b>, and <b>Donatus</b>; died 717) was the eleventh <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> (707–717). He was the son of Cenn Fáelad, and grandson of <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A1el_Coba_mac_%C3%81edo" title="Máel Coba mac Áedo">Máel Coba</a>, of the <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_Conaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenél Conaill">Cenél Conaill</a>. While most early abbots of Iona were members of Cenél Conaill they came from minor branches of the kindred, but Dúnchad came from the ruling line, grandson of one <a href="/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland" title="High King of Ireland">High King of Ireland</a> and the nephew of two others, <a href="/wiki/Cellach_mac_M%C3%A1ele_Coba" title="Cellach mac Máele Coba">Cellach</a> and <a href="/wiki/Conall_C%C3%B3el" title="Conall Cóel">Conall</a>.
</p><p>He is first heard of as Abbot of Killochuir on the coast of southeastern Ulster (perhaps <a href="/wiki/Killough" title="Killough">Killough</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Down" title="County Down">County Down</a>). He later become Abbot of Iona, although there is considerable dispute about this matter. The <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a></i> first mention him in that capacity in the year 707; but <a href="/wiki/Conamail_of_Iona" class="mw-redirect" title="Conamail of Iona">Conamail of Iona</a> is said to have been the abbot of Iona from 704 through 710. It is possible that Dúnchad served as a <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor" title="Coadjutor">coadjutor</a> (or principatum tenuit) of Conamail of Iona. He himself may have been elected in opposition to Abbot Conamail, while Dorbbéne in 713 and <a href="/w/index.php?title=F%C3%A1elch%C3%BA_of_Iona&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fáelchú of Iona (page does not exist)">Fáelchú</a> in 716 may have been elected to oppose Dúnchad. It has also been suggested that at least some of these people may have coadjutors, <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">priors</a>, or possibly even <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishops</a> at Iona at the time. The final agreement about the dating of Easter on Iona took place at the instance of St. Ecgberht of Northumbria, a priest who had been educated in Ireland, who was successful in persuading the community to abandon the Celtic Easter and tonsure.
</p><p>When Dúnchad died in 717, Fáelchú continued in his position. In the same year of Dúnchad's death, King <a href="/wiki/Nechtan_mac_Derile" class="mw-redirect" title="Nechtan mac Derile">Nechtan mac Derile</a>, the Gaelic ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a>, allegedly expelled the Ionan clergy from Pictland. His feast day is 25 May.
</p>
<h2><span id="Dorbb.C3.A9ne"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Dorbbéne">Dorbbéne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=D%C3%BAnchad_mac_Cinn_F%C3%A1elad&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Dorbbéne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_the_Four_Masters" title="Annals of the Four Masters">Annals of the Four Masters</a></i> (713.5), records the death of St. Dorbaine Foda, Abbot of Ia. Dorbbéne became abbot in 713, but died five months later on 25 October 713. Leslie Toke suggests that Dorbbéne may have been a coadjutors to St. Dunchadh, prior, or even bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Owen_Clancy" title="Thomas Owen Clancy">Thomas Owen Clancy</a> suggests that this interruption in Dúnchad's abbacy may reflect the politics between factions of monks at Iona at the time who disagreed about the dating of Easter.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
