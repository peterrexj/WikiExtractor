<p><b>Guillaume-Joseph Chaminade</b>, <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Mary_(Marianists)" title="Society of Mary (Marianists)">SM</a> (also known as <b>William Joseph Chaminade;</b> <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigueux" title="Périgueux">Périgueux</a>, 8 April 1761 – <a href="/wiki/Bordeaux" title="Bordeaux">Bordeaux</a>, 22 January 1850) was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_France" title="Catholic Church in France">French Catholic</a> priest who survived persecution during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a> and later founded the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Mary_(Marianists)" title="Society of Mary (Marianists)">Society of Mary</a>, usually called the Marianists, in 1817. He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 3 September 2000. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 22 January.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Marianist_Family" title="Marianist Family">Marianist Family</a>'s other three branches — the <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sisters</a> known as the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Mary_Immaculate" class="mw-redirect" title="Daughters of Mary Immaculate">Daughters of Mary Immaculate</a>, the married and single men and women of the Marianist Lay Communities, and the consecrated laywomen of the Alliance Mariale  — also look to Chaminade as a founder or inspiration.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chaminade was born in 1761 in <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigueux" title="Périgueux">Périgueux</a> to Catherine Bethon and Blaise Chaminade, in the former province of <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigord" title="Périgord">Périgord</a>, now the <a href="/wiki/French_departments" class="mw-redirect" title="French departments">Department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dordogne" title="Dordogne">Dordogne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was the 14th child of deeply religious parents. Three of his brothers became priests. Feeling called to serve in this way as well, he entered a <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Mussidan" title="Mussidan">Mussidan</a> at the age of ten. He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest in 1785 for the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_P%C3%A9rigueux" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Périgueux">local diocese</a>. <sup id="cite_ref-VNS_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Revolutionary_era">Revolutionary era</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Revolutionary era">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1790, after the start of the French Revolution, Chaminade moved to <a href="/wiki/Bordeaux" title="Bordeaux">Bordeaux</a>. There he became an enemy of the state by defying the <a href="/wiki/Civil_Constitution_of_the_Clergy" title="Civil Constitution of the Clergy">Civil Constitution of the Clergy</a>, which would have required him to take an oath affirming the Revolution's secular values and disclaiming the authority of the Roman Catholic Church. He secretly continued to work as a priest, risking a possible death penalty.  One of his allies in this work was <a href="/wiki/Marie-Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Charlotte_de_Lamourous" title="Marie-Thérèse Charlotte de Lamourous">Marie-Thérèse Charlotte de Lamourous</a>, whom he later assisted in founding Bordeaux's <i>Miséricorde</i> (House of Mercy) for "fallen women".<sup id="cite_ref-VNS_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1795, when the national government sought to work with the non-juring clergy, Chaminade accepted responsibility for supervising the reconciliation of the clergy of Bordeaux who had taken the Constitutional Oath but wanted to make peace with the Catholic Church; about fifty such priests completed their reconciliation with his help. After the <a href="/wiki/Coup_of_18_Fructidor" title="Coup of 18 Fructidor">Coup of 18 Fructidor</a> by the <a href="/wiki/French_Directory" title="French Directory">French Directory</a> in 1797, he fled the country and found refuge for three years in <a href="/wiki/Zaragoza,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Zaragoza, Spain">Zaragoza, Spain</a><sup id="cite_ref-VNS_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While living there, he would regularly visit the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Pillar" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar">Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar</a> out of his strong devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Mary,_the_mother_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary, the mother of Jesus">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>. As a result of his prayer he developed a vision for restoring the Catholic faith to France. To this end, he decided to build an organization of both lay and religious Order members, taking the Virgin Mary as the model of a perfect disciple of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-VNS_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When he returned to Bordeaux in November 1800, he re-established the Marian <a href="/wiki/Sodality" title="Sodality">Sodality</a>, which he hoped would promote the <a href="/wiki/Desecularization" title="Desecularization">desecularization</a> of France by offering "the spectacle of a people of saints". He saw the development of the young lay movement as the prime focus of his mission. In this he was opposed by the traditionalist forces in the Church, both clergy and lay, who saw the re-creation of the privileges and institutions of the pre-Revolutionary Church as the true goal of their restoration of the faith in France. In 1824 Chaminade published a reply to that line of thinking in which he stated, "The levers that move the moral world somehow need a new fulcrum."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sodality spread to other cities, and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> recognized his efforts by appointing him <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Administrator" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Administrator">Apostolic Administrator</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Bazas" title="Ancient Diocese of Bazas">Diocese of Bazas</a> and later, in 1801, naming him an "Apostolic Missionary" to the region, confirming its trust in him.<sup id="cite_ref-VNS_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VNS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some sodalitists wanted to make a more complete commitment to the Church, so Chaminade, along with the <a href="/wiki/Ad%C3%A8le_de_Batz_de_Trenquell%C3%A9on" title="Adèle de Batz de Trenquelléon">Adèle de Batz de Trenquelléon</a>, founded the Institute of the Daughters of Mary Immaculate in <a href="/wiki/Agen" title="Agen">Agen</a> in 1816. A year later, he founded the Society of Mary at Bordeaux. Both religious institutes devoted themselves to teaching. Chaminade sought to establish a network of schools to train Catholic teachers, but this effort was checked by the <a href="/wiki/1830_Revolution" class="mw-redirect" title="1830 Revolution">1830 Revolution</a>. However, both of Chaminade's <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institutes</a> continued to grow: the Daughters of Mary founded schools in south-western France to educate rural women and the Society of Mary expanded in France and spread to Switzerland (1839) and the United States of America (1849).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The last ten years of Chaminade's life were filled with problems of health, finances and obstacles to his vision in the administration of the Society. He was replaced in January 1846 as <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> by a <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a>, which he considered illegitimate, called by members of the General Council of the Society, with the approval of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. Partially paralyzed, he thereafter was left in virtual isolation by the government of the Society.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chaminade died in Bordeaux in 1850, surrounded by members of the Society he had founded. His tomb is located in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg/220px-Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg/330px-Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg/440px-Procesi%C3%B3n_Beato_Guillermo_Jos%C3%A9_Chaminade.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4160" data-file-height="3120" /></a><figcaption>Procession</figcaption></figure>
<p>The process of inquiry for the cause of Chaminade's <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> was opened in 1909, with testimony taken in until 1912 in France and Spain, where he had lived. The cause was submitted to Rome by the Marianists in 1918. Study on the matter continued until 1973, when Chaminade was declared Venerable by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracle_worker">Miracle worker</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Miracle worker">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Argentina">Argentina</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Argentina">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In 1995 the healing from lung cancer of Elena Otera, a resident of <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a>, was studied as a possible miracle to be attributed to the <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercession</a> of Chaminade. After a positive conclusion in the local inquiry conducted by the Marianist <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">Postulator</a> of the cause, the matter was referred to the Vatican for investigation. The medical boards consulted by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> finally declared her healing as "scientifically inexplicable" in January 1999. A review of the cause then took place by the theologians and bishops of the Congregation. They voted unanimously in favor of declaring a miracle that following October. This was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, who beatified Chaminade in 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="United_States">United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Joseph_Chaminade&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_(University_of_Dayton)_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_%28University_of_Dayton%29_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG/250px-Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_%28University_of_Dayton%29_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="333" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_%28University_of_Dayton%29_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG/375px-Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_%28University_of_Dayton%29_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_%28University_of_Dayton%29_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG/500px-Chapel_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_%28University_of_Dayton%29_-_Bl._William_Joseph_Chaminade_statue.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="4000" /></a><figcaption>A statue of Chaminade adorns the Chapel of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Dayton" title="University of Dayton">University of Dayton</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Rachel Baumgartner, then a high school student in <a href="/wiki/St._Louis" title="St. Louis">St. Louis</a>, <a href="/wiki/Missouri" title="Missouri">Missouri</a>, in the United States, was diagnosed in December 1998 with Askin's tumor, a kind of <a href="/wiki/Sarcoma" title="Sarcoma">sarcoma</a>. She underwent emergency surgery to have the tumor removed, followed by <a href="/wiki/Chemotherapy" title="Chemotherapy">chemotherapy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Radiation" title="Radiation">radiation</a>. She was a member of the Parish of Our Lady of the Pillar, founded and staffed by the Marianists, and was chosen to attend the ceremony for Chaminade's beatification in Rome in 2000. Early the next year the tumor was found to have re-appeared in her <a href="/wiki/Bone_marrow" title="Bone marrow">bone marrow</a>, for which she underwent a <a href="/wiki/Hematopoietic_stem_cell_transplantation" title="Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation">stem cell transplant</a>, which had a severe negative impact on her health. The tumor was found to have re-developed between her heart, lung and spine in November 2002.
</p><p>Baumgartner was advised that no one had ever survived when this occurred after such a transplant, and that she had only a few weeks to live. After a year and a half, however, the tumor (which was the size of a small Nerf football) stopped growing and had not damaged the organs around it.  In 2004 a noted surgeon removed the tumor, finding that it had died with almost no treatment, which was medically unexplainable. Baumgartner, now married and named Rachel Lozano, attributed this to the intercession of Chaminade. The Marianist pastor of the parish referred this matter to the Superior General of the Society of Mary, who in turn requested that the local bishop, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_St._Louis" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of St. Louis">Archbishop of St. Louis</a>, conduct a formal inquiry. The investigation was concluded in July 2010 and forwarded by the Archdiocese and the Marianists to Rome for judgment.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
