<p><b>Saint Liberalis of <a href="/wiki/Treviso" title="Treviso">Treviso</a></b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Liberale</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the 4th century.  Tradition states that he was a priest who opposed <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a> and that he was persecuted at <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Liberalis_of_Treviso&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A legend, composed around the 10th century and incorporating elements from other saints’ <a href="/wiki/Hagiographies" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographies">hagiographies</a>, states that Liberalis was a native of <a href="/wiki/Altinum" title="Altinum">Altinum</a>, born to a noble <a href="/wiki/Equestrian_(Roman)" class="mw-redirect" title="Equestrian (Roman)">equestrian</a> family.  According to the legend, he was educated in the Christian faith by <a href="/wiki/Heliodorus_of_Altino" title="Heliodorus of Altino">Heliodorus of Altino</a> (Eliodoro), first Christian bishop of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
Liberalis practiced extreme <a href="/wiki/Mortifications" class="mw-redirect" title="Mortifications">mortifications</a> and fasts after his conversion.
</p><p>Faced with growing opposition from both Arianism and paganism in the see, Heliodorus retired as bishop and lived as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> on a <a href="/wiki/Desert_island" title="Desert island">desert island</a> in the lagoons near Altino, entrusting the see to a man named Ambrose. Worried about Ambrose's ability to handle the rise of Arianism in the see, Liberalis decided to look for Heliodorus and attempt to convince him to come back to his see.  On the way, Liberalis fell asleep in a church and experienced a strange dream, in which an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a>, resplendent in white, encouraged him in his mission and also announced his imminent death.  Liberalis traveled onward to Castrazone where there was a church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lawrence" title="Saint Lawrence">Saint Lawrence</a>.  He was unable to find a way to reach Heliodorus’ island and decided instead to lead the life of a hermit.  However, he fell ill and died on April 27.  He was buried in the Castrazone church in a marble <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Liberale_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Liberale-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Liberalis_of_Treviso&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His relics were carried to Treviso from Altinum around 452 to safeguard them against the <a href="/wiki/Hunnish" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunnish">Hunnish</a> invasions, or perhaps later, when the threat came from the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a> rather than the Huns.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Liberale_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Liberale-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Liberalis' relics may have been enshrined with those associated with the martyrs <a href="/wiki/Theonistus,_Tabra,_and_Tabratha" class="mw-redirect" title="Theonistus, Tabra, and Tabratha">Theonistus, Tabra, and Tabratha</a> (<i>Teonisto, Tabra e Tabrata</i>) at the cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Torcello" title="Torcello">Torcello</a> after 639 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Liberale_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Liberale-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, the presence of these martyrs’ relics in Treviso is also attested, and a strong cultus took hold there, with documentation attesting to the cultus dating from 1082 onwards. In the twelfth century, Liberalis was proclaimed patron saint of Treviso, and was patron of the cathedral, along with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Saint Paul</a>. Liberalis became patron of <a href="/wiki/Castelfranco_Veneto" title="Castelfranco Veneto">Castelfranco Veneto</a> as a result of the fact that citizens of Treviso founded that town in 1199.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Liberale_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Liberale-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In art he is either depicted with a <a href="/wiki/Dalmatic" title="Dalmatic">dalmatic</a> or in a soldier's cloak.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Liberale_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Liberale-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
