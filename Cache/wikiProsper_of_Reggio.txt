<p><b>Saint Prosper of Reggio</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Prospero</i>; died 25 June c. 466) is an Italian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  Tradition holds that he was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Reggio_Emilia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Reggio Emilia">bishop of Reggio Emilia</a> for twenty-two years.  Little is known of his life, but documents attest that he was indeed bishop of Reggio Emilia in the fifth century.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Prospero_di_Reggio_Emilia_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Prospero_di_Reggio_Emilia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Remembered for his sense of <a href="/wiki/Charity_(virtue)" class="mw-redirect" title="Charity (virtue)">charity</a>, he is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Reggio Emilia, although its <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> is not dedicated to him.  Instead, the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Prospero_(Reggio_Emilia)" class="mw-redirect" title="San Prospero (Reggio Emilia)">San Prospero</a>, which Prosper himself had built and dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Apollinaris_of_Ravenna" title="Apollinaris of Ravenna">Saint Apollinaris</a>, commemorates his episcopate.  He died at Reggio Emilia.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Prosper_of_Reggio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition holds that the Bishop Prosper was able to deflect the onslaught of <a href="/wiki/Attila_the_Hun" class="mw-redirect" title="Attila the Hun">Attila the Hun</a> by miraculously calling for a fog to hide the city. The patron saint of Modena, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Geminianus" title="Geminianus">Geminianus</a> is also said to have performed the same miracle.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg/250px-PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg/375px-PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg/500px-PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1960" data-file-height="1476" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:PiazzaprosperoRE.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Piazza <a href="/wiki/San_Prospero_(Reggio_Emilia)" class="mw-redirect" title="San Prospero (Reggio Emilia)">San Prospero</a>, Reggio Emilia, with patron saint's basilica.</div></div></div> 
<p>His cult was ancient and was diffused during the eleventh to fourteenth centuries.  Prosper was venerated in <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lucca" title="Lucca">Lucca</a>, and other cities beyond Reggio.  Some thirty-one churches and chapels were dedicated to him in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Prospero_di_Reggio_Emilia_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Prospero_di_Reggio_Emilia-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  However, after the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>, his cult once again was confined only to Reggio.  The Reggio diocese celebrates his cult locally on 24 November but 25 June is his feast day on general calendars.
</p><p>The painting by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Soncini" title="Giovanni Soncini">Giovanni Soncini</a> of Saints Bernard and Prosper is the only known painting by this artist.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Prosper_of_Reggio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were translated in 703 to a new church built in his honor by Thomas, a subsequent bishop of Reggio.  They lay alongside those of <a href="/wiki/Venerius_the_Hermit" title="Venerius the Hermit">Venerius the Hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cosmas_and_Damian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cosmas and Damian">Cosmas and Damian</a>.
</p><p>In the tenth century, Reggio being vulnerable to attacks from the sea, Bishop Ermenald (Ermenaldo) transported his relics to the Cathedral of Santa Maria in the center of the city while the church dedicated to Prosper was being built. This was completed by his successor Tenzo (Tenzone) (979).  <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_V" title="Pope Gregory V">Pope Gregory V</a> consecrated the church in 997.  In the 16th century, the church was rebuilt; Prosper's body lies under the great altar.
</p>
