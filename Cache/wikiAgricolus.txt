<p><b>Saint Agricola (Agricol, Agricolus) of Avignon</b> (c. 630&#8211;c. 700) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Avignon" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Avignon">bishop of Avignon</a>. According to tradition, Agricola ("farmer") was the son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Magnus_of_Avignon" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Magnus of Avignon">Saint Magnus</a>, also a bishop of the city.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agricola_of_Avignon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the age of sixteen, he was professed a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_L%C3%A9rins" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Lérins">Abbey of Lérins</a>.  However, at the age of thirty, he was summoned by his father Magnus to Avignon, where he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor" title="Coadjutor">coadjutor</a>.  When his father died in 660, he succeeded as bishop.  He built a church in the city that was staffed by the monks of Lérins. He built a convent for <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>. He was a well-known <a href="/wiki/Preacher" title="Preacher">preacher</a>, and famous for his charity and defense of the poor and sick against civil authorities.
</p><p>He died of <a href="/wiki/Natural_causes" class="mw-redirect" title="Natural causes">natural causes</a> and was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Veredemus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Veredemus">Saint Veredemus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-gardon_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gardon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agricola_of_Avignon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A charter of 919 mentions that Saint Agricola had been buried in the Avignon church dedicated to Saint Peter (Saint-Pierre).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the end of the 11th century, Bishop Arbert of Avignon made a donation that referred to the abbey of Saint-Agricol; the church of Saint-Agricol (Saint Agricola) that seems to have been built in the 12th century was made collegial in 1321 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXII" title="Pope John XXII">Pope John XXII</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Avignon_Papacy" title="Avignon Papacy">Avignon-based popes</a>, who equipped it with a statute and income.  It is possible that on this occasion that the transfer of the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of St. Agricola from the church of Saint-Pierre to Saint-Agricol occurred. They are still preserved in this church.
</p><p>The cult of Saint Agricola increased in the 14th and 15th centuries. 15th-century documents record that he prevented an invasion of <a href="/wiki/Stork" title="Stork">storks</a> by his blessing. He is thus <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> of storks and is depicted with them as his emblem. In 1647, he was declared <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the city of Avignon by Archbishop César Argelli. Devotees prayed to the saint whose name signifies "cultivator of fields" for <a href="/wiki/Weather" title="Weather">good weather</a>, fine harvests, and <a href="/wiki/Rain" title="Rain">rain</a>  during times of <a href="/wiki/Drought" title="Drought">drought</a>. He is also invoked against the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a> and misfortune of all kinds. His feast day is September 2.
</p>
