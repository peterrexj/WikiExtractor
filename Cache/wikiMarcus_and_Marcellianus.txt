<p><b>Mark and Marcellian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Marcus et Marcellianus</i>) are <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> venerated as <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Their cult is sometimes associated with that of Saints Tranquillinus, Martia, Nicostratus, Zoe, Castulus and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Tiburtius" title="Saint Tiburtius">Tiburtius</a>, though not in the official <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_book" title="Liturgical book">liturgical books</a> of the Church, which mention only Mark and Marcellianus (in first place) among the saints for 18 June.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Their mention in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> on that date from before the time of the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> was removed in the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a>, because nothing is known about them except their names, the fact of their martyrdom, and that they were buried on 18 June in the cemetery of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Balbina" title="Santa Balbina">Santa Balbina</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their legend states that they were martyred at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> towards the end of the third century, most likely in the year 286. They are mentioned in most of the ancient <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a>, including the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, and their martyrdom is described in the <i>Acts of St Sebastian</i>, which, though ancient, is largely legendary.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mark_and_Marcellian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, Mark and Marcellian were <a href="/wiki/Twin" title="Twin">twin</a> brothers from a distinguished family. They lived in Rome and became <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacons</a> in the early Church. When they refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods, they were arrested. Their parents, Tranquillinus and Martia, visited them in prison, urging them to renounce their being Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian" title="Saint Sebastian">Sebastian</a> convinced them not to abandon their faith. Sebastian converted Tranquillinus and Martia, as well as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Tiburtius" title="Saint Tiburtius">Tiburtius</a>, the son of <a href="/wiki/Chromatius_of_Rome" title="Chromatius of Rome">Agrestius Chromatius</a>, allegedly <a href="/wiki/Praefectus_urbis" class="mw-redirect" title="Praefectus urbis">prefect of Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-allegedly_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allegedly-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Nicostratus, another official, and his wife Zoe, were also converted. According to the legend, Zoe had been a mute for six years. However, she made known to Sebastian her desire to convert to the Church. As soon as she had, her speech returned to her. Nicostratus then brought the rest of the prisoners; these were sixteen people who were also converted by Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-Ebenezer_Cobham_Brewer_1901_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ebenezer_Cobham_Brewer_1901-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chromatius and Tiburtius became converts, and Chromatius set free all his prisoners, resigned his position, and retired to <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a>.
</p><p>Mark and Marcellian were concealed by <a href="/wiki/Castulus" title="Castulus">Castulus</a>, a Christian officer, but they were betrayed by an <a href="/wiki/Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostate">apostate</a>, Torquatus. The twins were again taken into custody. Chromatius's alleged successor Fabianus<sup id="cite_ref-allegedly_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allegedly-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> condemned them to be bound head downwards to two pillars with their feet nailed to them. Mark and Marcellian hung there for a full day until they were pierced with <a href="/wiki/Lance" title="Lance">lances</a>. The twins were buried in the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> near the <a href="/wiki/Cemetery" title="Cemetery">cemetery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Domitilla_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavia Domitilla (saint)">Domitilla</a>.
</p><p>Meanwhile, Zoe was hung to the branch of a tree and a fire was kindled underneath her feet, and she was killed. Nicostratus and five others were drowned in the <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a>.  Tiburtius was thrown into a ditch and buried alive.<sup id="cite_ref-Ebenezer_Cobham_Brewer_1901_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ebenezer_Cobham_Brewer_1901-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mark_and_Marcellian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The bodies of Marcus and Marcellianus were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">moved</a>, probably during the ninth century, to the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Cosma_e_Damiano" title="Santi Cosma e Damiano">Santi Cosma e Damiano</a>. They were discovered there in 1583 during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII" title="Pope Gregory XIII">Pope Gregory XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bodies remain there in a <a href="/wiki/Tomb" title="Tomb">tomb</a>, near an ancient painting of the two martyrs with a third person, who appears to be the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>. In 1902, their <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> in the catacombs of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Balbina" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Balbina">Saint Balbina</a> was rediscovered.
</p>
