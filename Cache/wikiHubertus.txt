<p><b>Hubertus</b> or <b>Hubert</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 656 – 30 May 727 <small>A.D.</small>) was a Christian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> who became the first bishop of <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a> in 708 <small>A.D.</small><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of hunters, mathematicians, opticians and metalworkers. Known as the "Apostle of the <a href="/wiki/Ardennes" title="Ardennes">Ardennes</a>", he was called upon, until the early 20th century, to cure <a href="/wiki/Rabies" title="Rabies">rabies</a> through the use of the traditional <a href="/wiki/Saint_Hubert%27s_Key" title="Saint Hubert&#39;s Key">Saint Hubert's Key</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hubert was widely venerated during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. The iconography of his legend is entangled with the legend of the martyr <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eustace" title="Saint Eustace">Saint Eustace</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> published seven early lives of Hubertus (<i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i>, November 3, 759 – 930 <small>A.D.</small>); the first of these was the work of a contemporary, although it offers few details.
</p><p>Hubertus died 30 May 727 <small>A.D.</small> in or near a place called (in Latin) <i>Fura</i>. In the later Middle Ages, this location was claimed to have been identified as <a href="/wiki/Tervuren" title="Tervuren">Tervuren</a> near <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>; recent scholarship, however, considers <a href="/wiki/Voeren" title="Voeren">Voeren</a> (Fourons), a location much closer to Liège than Brussels, to be the saint's likelier resting place.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 3 November.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hubert was born about the year 656, the eldest son of a Frankish duke.<sup id="cite_ref-Brown_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brown-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At the age of 10 he nearly died from a fever. As a youth, Hubert was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustrian</a> court of <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III" title="Theuderic III">Theuderic III</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;673–691</span>) at Paris, where his charm and agreeable address led to his investment with the dignity of <a href="/wiki/Count_palatine" title="Count palatine"> "count of the palace"</a>. Like many nobles of the time, Hubert was a hunting enthusiast.
</p><p>About this time (682), Hubert married Floribanne, daughter of Dagobert, <a href="/wiki/Counts_of_Louvain" title="Counts of Louvain"> Count of Leuven</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Brown_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brown-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (Their son <a href="/wiki/Floribert_of_Li%C3%A8ge" title="Floribert of Liège">Floribert of Liège</a> later would succeed his father as Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a> - in office: 727 to <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;737</span>; bishoprics were all but accounted as fiefs heritable in the great families of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty"> Merovingian kingdoms</a>.) Hubert moved to the Austrasian court where he was warmly welcomed by <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Herstal" title="Pepin of Herstal">Pepin of Herstal</a>, Mayor of the palace, who entitled him almost immediately Grand Master of the household.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spiritual_conversion">Spiritual conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Spiritual conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg/300px-Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="178" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg/450px-Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg/600px-Wilhelm_R%C3%A4uber_Hubertus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2407" data-file-height="1432" /></a><figcaption>"The Conversion of Holy Hubertus", Wilhelm Räuber (1849–1926)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Hubert's wife died giving birth to their son Floribert, and his grief prompted him to retreat from the court. He withdrew into the forests of the Ardennes and gave himself up entirely to hunting.<sup id="cite_ref-cds_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cds-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>Legend holds that on a Good Friday morning, while the faithful were in church, Hubert was hunting in the forest. As he pursued a magnificent stag or <a href="/wiki/Hart_(deer)" title="Hart (deer)">hart</a>, the animal turned and Hubert was astounded to see a crucifix floating between its antlers. He heard a voice saying: "Hubert unless thou turnest to the Lord and leadest a holy life, thou shalt quickly go down into Hell." Hubert dismounted and prostrated himself, and after asking "Lord, what wouldst Thou have me do?" is told, "Go and seek <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Maastricht" title="Lambert of Maastricht">Lambert</a>, and he will instruct you."<sup id="cite_ref-cds_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cds-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The story of the stag first appears in one of the later legendary <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> (<i>Bibliotheca hagiographic Latina</i>, nos. 3994–4002) and has been appropriated from the legend of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eustace" title="Saint Eustace">Saint Eustace</a> or Placidus.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It was only attributed to Saint Hubert in the 15th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_career">Religious career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg/220px-St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg/330px-St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg/440px-St_Hubert_of_Li%C3%A8ge_is_consecrated_bishop_by_Pope_Sergius.jpg 2x" data-file-width="649" data-file-height="435" /></a><figcaption>Saint Hubertus being consecrated Bishop by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Pope Sergius&#160;I</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Hubert set out immediately for <a href="/wiki/Maastricht" title="Maastricht">Maastricht</a>, to meet Lambert, a bishop who received him kindly and became his spiritual director. Hubert renounced all his very considerable honors and gave up his birthright to the Aquitaine to his younger brother, <a href="/wiki/Odo_of_Aquitaine" class="mw-redirect" title="Odo of Aquitaine">Odo</a>, whom he made guardian of his infant son, Floribert. Having distributed all his personal wealth among the poor, Lambert sent him to the Ardennes to live amongst the people and the forest creatures. Hubert studied for the priesthood, was ordained, and soon afterward became one of Lambert's chief associates in the administration of his diocese. At the request of Lambert, Hubert made a pilgrimage to Rome in 708 in Lambert's name, but during his absence, Lambert was assassinated by the followers of Pepin. According to the hagiographies of Hubert, this act was simultaneously revealed to the pope in a vision, together with an injunction to appoint Hubert <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Maastricht" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Maastricht">bishop of Maastricht</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hubert distributed his episcopal revenues among the poor, was diligent in fasting and prayer, and became well known for his eloquence in the pulpit. In 720, in obedience to a vision, Hubert <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> St. Lambert's remains from Maastricht to Liège with great pomp and ceremony, with several neighboring bishops assisting. A basilica for the relics was built upon the site of Lambert's martyrdom, and was consecrated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lambert%27s_Cathedral,_Li%C3%A8ge" title="Saint Lambert&#39;s Cathedral, Liège">cathedral</a> the following year, the see being removed from Maastricht to Liège, then only a small village. This laid the foundation of the future greatness of Liège, of which Lambert is honored as patron, and Hubert as founder and first bishop.
</p><p>Hubert actively evangelized among pagans in the extensive <a href="/wiki/Ardennes" title="Ardennes">Ardennes</a> forests and in <a href="/wiki/Toxandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Toxandria">Toxandria</a>, a district stretching from near Tongeren to the confluence of the rivers Waal and the Rhine. He gained the trust (and the faith) of its people through the outdoors-man skills he acquired in his hunting life. He became a sought authority whenever matters of the forest came up.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hubertus died peacefully in a place called <i>Fura</i>, located 30 miles from Liège, 30 May 727 or 728. Initially he was buried in the collegiate St. Peter's Church, Liège, but his bones were translated to the Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Hubert" title="Abbey of Saint-Hubert">Abbey of Amdain</a> in the Ardennes in 825. The abbey became a locus for pilgrimages, until Hubert's coffin disappeared during the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a>. His feast day is 3 November, probably the date of the translation of his relics to Amdain.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hubert was widely venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> and partly because of his noble birth, several military orders were named after him: the <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavarian</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemian</a> <a href="/wiki/International_Order_of_St._Hubertus" title="International Order of St. Hubertus">International Order of St. Hubertus</a> and that of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">Archbishop-Elector of Cologne</a>.
</p><p>Hubertus, along with <a href="/wiki/Quirinus_of_Neuss" title="Quirinus of Neuss">Quirinus of Neuss</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cornelius_the_Centurion" title="Cornelius the Centurion">Cornelius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony</a>, was venerated as one of the <a href="/wiki/Four_Holy_Marshals" title="Four Holy Marshals">Four Holy Marshals</a> (<i>Vier Marschälle Gottes</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Rhineland" title="Rhineland">Rhineland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The <i>St. Hubertus Orden</i> (<a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Hubert" title="Order of Saint Hubert">Order of Saint Hubert</a>), a <a href="/wiki/Chivalric_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Chivalric order">chivalric order</a>, was founded in 1444 by Gerhard V of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_J%C3%BClich" title="Duchy of Jülich">Jülich and Berg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>, at least two <a href="/wiki/Church_(building)" title="Church (building)">churches</a> were dedicated to Saint Hubertus within the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer2004_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer2004-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>St. Hubert of Liège is patron of archers; dogs; forest workers; trappers; hunting and huntsmen;<sup id="cite_ref-Brown_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brown-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> mathematicians;<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> metal workers; smelters and the city of Liège.
</p><p>St. Hubert has been described as the patron saint of hunters and is honored by sportsmen as the originator of ethical hunting behavior. However, he renounced hunting after having his vision encountering the stag, as it was believed that God had seen his hunting life as an unholy, idolatrous one which would lead him to Hell. When Hubert became a priest, clergy were subsequently forbidden to hunt and if they did, would be required to do penance.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hubertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hubert is honored among sport hunters as the originator of ethical hunting behavior. In some versions of the story, the stag is said to have lectured Hubert to hold animals in higher regard and have compassion for them as God's creatures with value in their own right. For example, the hunter ought to only shoot when a clean, quick, and therefore humane kill is assured. He ought to shoot only old stags which are past their prime breeding years and forego a much-anticipated shot on a trophy to instead euthanize a sick or injured animal that might appear on the scene. Further, one ought never to shoot a female with young in tow, to assure the young deer have a mother to guide them to food during the winter.<sup id="cite_ref-cds_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cds-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Such is the legacy of Hubert, which is still taught today and who held in high regard in the extensive, rigorous German and Austrian hunter education courses.
</p><p>His legacy is also followed by the French <i><a href="/wiki/Hunting_with_hounds" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunting with hounds">chasse à courre</a></i> (riding to hounds) masters, huntsmen, and followers who hunt deer, boar, and roe on horseback and are the last direct heirs of Hubert in Europe. <i>Chasse à course</i> is currently enjoying a revival in France. These hunters apply a specific set of ethics, rituals, rules, and tactics that date from the early Middle Ages. Hubert is venerated every year by the hunts in formal ceremonies.
</p><p>The St. Hubert Club of Great Britain is dedicated to responsible deer management.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The St. Hubertus Club Chicago is a German cultural organization that promotes German heritage and hunting in the German tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Sv%C3%A4t%C3%BD_Anton_manor_house" title="Svätý Anton manor house">Svätý Anton manor house</a>, which houses a Forestry, Wood and Hunting Museum, hosts a festival, St. Hubertus Days each September.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
