<p><a href="/wiki/Dom_(title)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dom (title)">D</a>. <b>Nuno Álvares Pereira</b>, <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">O. Carm.</a> (<span style="font-size:85%;">Portuguese pronunciation:&#160;</span><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="pt-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese" title="Help:IPA/Portuguese">[ˈnunu ˈaɫvɐɾɨʃ pɨˈɾɐjɾɐ]</a></span>; 24 June 1360 &#8211; 1 November 1431) was a Portuguese general of great success who had a decisive role in the <a href="/wiki/1383-1385_Crisis" class="mw-redirect" title="1383-1385 Crisis">1383-1385 Crisis</a> that assured <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>'s independence from <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Castile" title="Crown of Castile">Castile</a>. He later became a mystic and was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a>, in 1918, and <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in 2009.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nuno Álvares Pereira is often referred to as the <b>Saint Constable</b> (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Santo Condestável</i>) or as <b>Saint Nuno of Saint Mary</b> (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">São Nuno de Santa Maria</i>), his <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Barcelos" title="Count of Barcelos">count of Barcelos</a>, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Our%C3%A9m" title="Count of Ourém">Ourém</a> and <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Arraiolos" title="Count of Arraiolos">Arraiolos</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:102px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Armas_pereira.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Armas_pereira.svg/100px-Armas_pereira.svg.png" decoding="async" width="100" height="117" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Armas_pereira.svg/150px-Armas_pereira.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Armas_pereira.svg/200px-Armas_pereira.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="716" data-file-height="841" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Armas_pereira.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Nun'Álvares Pereira coat of arms</div></div></div>
<p>Nuno Álvares Pereira was born on 24 June 1360 in Flor da Rosa, near Crato, central Portugal, the illegitimate son of Dom Álvaro Gonçalves Pereira, <a href="/wiki/Prior_of_Crato" title="Prior of Crato">prior of Crato</a> and Iria Gonçalves do Carvalhal.<sup id="cite_ref-ocarm_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocarm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His grandfather was Dom <a href="/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Pereira" title="Gonçalo Pereira">Gonçalo Pereira</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Braga" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Braga">archbishop of Braga</a> from 1326 until 1349. He was descended from the oldest Portuguese and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia" title="Kingdom of Galicia">Galician</a> nobility.
</p><p>About a year after his birth, the child was legitimized by royal decree<sup id="cite_ref-cro_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cro-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and so was able to receive a knightly education typical of the offspring of the noble families of the time.
</p><p>At 13 years of age he became page to Queen <a href="/wiki/Leonor_Telles_de_Meneses" class="mw-redirect" title="Leonor Telles de Meneses">Leonor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ocarm_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocarm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At age 16, he married <a href="/wiki/Leonor_de_Alvim" title="Leonor de Alvim">Leonor de Alvim</a>, a rich young widow.<sup id="cite_ref-ocarm_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocarm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Three children were born to the union, two boys who died early in life, and a girl, <a href="/wiki/Beatriz_Pereira_de_Alvim" title="Beatriz Pereira de Alvim">Beatriz</a>, who married <a href="/wiki/Afonso_I,_Duke_of_Braganza" title="Afonso I, Duke of Braganza">Afonso</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/John_I_of_Portugal" title="John I of Portugal">King John I</a> and founder of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Braganza" title="House of Braganza">House of Braganza</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_life">Military life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Military life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Álvares Pereira began military service in 1373, when he was only 13, and helped stop an invasion from Castile. However, according to his own words, his first military campaigns were no more than skirmishes on the borders of Portugal. He was an impetuous and brave young man who soon showed himself to be an excellent leader.
</p><p>When King <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Portugal" title="Ferdinand I of Portugal">Ferdinand I of Portugal</a> died in 1383, his only heir was <a href="/wiki/Beatrice_of_Portugal" title="Beatrice of Portugal">Beatrice</a>, married to king <a href="/wiki/John_I_of_Castile" title="John I of Castile">John I of Castile</a>. In order to preserve Portuguese independence, the nobles supported the claim of King Ferdinand's half-brother <a href="/wiki/John_I_of_Portugal" title="John I of Portugal">John, Master of Aviz</a> to the throne. After his first victory over the Castilians, in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Atoleiros" title="Battle of Atoleiros">Battle of Atoleiros</a> (April 1384), John of Aviz named Nuno Álvares Pereira protector and <a href="/wiki/Constable_of_Portugal" title="Constable of Portugal">constable of Portugal</a>, in practice supreme commander of Portugal's armies, and <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Our%C3%A9m" title="Count of Ourém">count of Ourém</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-society_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was only 24 years old.
</p><p>Álvares Pereira used guerilla tactics trying to dislodge the Castilian army besieging Lisbon in 1384 but plague finally drove them away.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In April 1385, John of Aviz was recognized as king by the <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_Cortes" title="Portuguese Cortes">Cortes</a>. This triggered an invasion of the country by King John I of Castile, in support of his wife's rights to the throne. Nuno Álvares Pereira was engaged against the northern cities loyal to the Castilians. During this time of war, he fed the hungry populations of his Castilian opposition at his own expense.<sup id="cite_ref-society_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg/300px-Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="175" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg/450px-Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg/600px-Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5574" data-file-height="3250" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Batalha_de_Aljubarrota_02.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Aljubarrota" title="Battle of Aljubarrota">The Battle of Aljubarrota</a></div></div></div>
<p>On 14 August 1385, at <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Aljubarrota" title="Battle of Aljubarrota">Aljubarrota</a> he led 6,500 volunteers to victory against a Castilian force of over 30,000, thus ending the threat of annexation. He attributed the victory to the Blessed Virgin, whose name, Maria, was inscribed on his sword.<sup id="cite_ref-society_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Mary</a>, he fasted on Wednesdays, Fridays and Saturdays. The banner he chose as his personal standard bore the image of the cross, of Mary and of the saintly knights James and George. At his own expense he built numerous churches and monasteries, among which was the Carmelite church in Lisbon and the church of Our Lady of Victories at Batalha.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/1383-1385_Crisis" class="mw-redirect" title="1383-1385 Crisis">1383-1385 Crisis</a>, Álvares Pereira was made the <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Arraiolos" title="Count of Arraiolos">count of Arraiolos</a> and <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Barcelos" title="Count of Barcelos">Barcelos</a>, which, along with the previous one, were the only three countships existing at the time and which had been taken from nobles who had taken the part of Castile. He was also made the major <a href="/wiki/Majordomo" title="Majordomo">majordomo</a> of the realm.
</p><p>Not wanting to give the enemy room to manoeuvre, the king of Portugal and his supreme general took the offensive and raided several Castilian towns, defeating once again a much larger Castilian army at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Valverde_(1385)" title="Battle of Valverde (1385)">Battle of Valverde</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cro_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cro-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He continued to watch out for the king of Castile, until his death in 1390. When hostilities ended, he gave the bulk of his wealth to the veterans.<sup id="cite_ref-va_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life">Religious life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg/220px-SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="201" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg/330px-SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg/440px-SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2481" data-file-height="2264" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SantoCondest%C3%A1vel_2008-2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Nuno Álvares Pereira atop the portal of Santo Condestável church, Lisbon</div></div></div>
<p>After the death of his wife, he became a <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> friar (he joined the Order in 1423) at the <a href="/wiki/Carmo_Convent_(Lisbon)" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmo Convent (Lisbon)">Carmo Convent (Lisbon)</a> which he had founded<sup id="cite_ref-cro_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cro-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> in fulfilment of a vow, and took the name of <b>Friar Nuno of Saint Mary</b> (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Frei Nuno de Santa Maria</i>). There he lived until his death on 1 November 1431. He was noted for his prayer, his practise of penance and his filial devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">Mother of God</a>. Nuno suffered from debilitating <a href="/wiki/Arthritis" title="Arthritis">arthritis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the last year of his life, King John I went to visit and embrace him for the last time. He wept for he considered Nuno Álvares Pereira his closest friend, the one who had put him on the throne and saved his country's independence.
</p><p>Nuno Álvares Pereira's tomb was lost in the famous <a href="/wiki/1755_Lisbon_earthquake" title="1755 Lisbon earthquake">1755 Lisbon earthquake</a>. His epitaph read:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"Here lies that famous Nuno, the Constable, founder of the House of Bragança, excellent general, blessed monk, who during his life on earth so ardently desired the Kingdom of Heaven that after his death, he merited the eternal company of the Saints. His worldly honors were countless, but he turned his back on them. He was a great Prince, but he made himself a humble monk. He founded, built and endowed this church in which his body rests."</p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg/220px-Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg/330px-Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg/440px-Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1440" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_Nuno_%C3%81lvares_Pereira.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Nuno Álvares Pereira on horseback in <a href="/wiki/Batalha,_Portugal" title="Batalha, Portugal">Batalha</a></div></div></div>
<p>Álvares Pereira was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 23 January 1918 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cro_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cro-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was celebrated liturgically on 1 April as an obligatory memorial by the Order of <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelites</a> and as an optional memorial by the Order of <a href="/wiki/Discalced_Carmelites" title="Discalced Carmelites">Discalced Carmelites</a>.
</p><p>Álvares Pereira had been on the point of being canonised by decree in 1940 by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_XII" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius XII">Pius XII</a>.  According to a recent statement by the postulator general of the Carmelite Order, his <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">canonisation</a> was postponed for diplomatic reasons (the Portuguese ambassador indicated that the time was not right).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 3 July 2008 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> signed two decrees in Rome, promulgating the heroic virtues of Nuno Álvares Pereira and the authenticity of a miracle that had already been previously confirmed as such by medical and theological commissions.  By this act, the pope formally canonised Friar Nuno de Santa Maria Álvares Pereira.  The public celebration of his <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">canonisation</a> took place on 26 April 2009 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>.  The Carmelites now celebrate St Nuno on 6 November; the date also appointed for his feast in Portugal.
</p><p>The Blessed Nuno Society is a mission society and prayer apostolate officially recognized by the Catholic 
Church as a diocesan Private Association of the Christian Faithful and affiliated with, the Catholic Diocese of Duluth, Minnesota.<sup id="cite_ref-society_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
