<p><b>Saint Engratia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Santa Engrácia</i>, <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Santa Engracia</i>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  Tradition states that she was martyred with eighteen companions in 303 AD.  She should not be confused with the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fructus" title="Saint Fructus">8th-century Spanish martyr of the same name</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engratia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although her martyrdom is traditionally placed around 303 during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>, more recently it is considered probable that she died during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Valerian_(emperor)" title="Valerian (emperor)">Valerian</a> (254-260).<sup id="cite_ref-amado_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-amado-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engratia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Engratia was a native of <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a> who had been promised in marriage to a nobleman of <a href="/wiki/Roussillon" title="Roussillon">Roussillon</a>.  He sent as her escort to Gaul her uncle <b>Lupercius</b> (sometimes identified with the <a href="/wiki/Luperculus" title="Luperculus">Luperculus</a> who was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Eauze" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Eauze">bishop of Eauze</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>) and a suite of sixteen noblemen and a servant named <b>Julie</b> or <b>Julia</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-magnificat.ca_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnificat.ca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon reaching <a href="/wiki/Zaragoza" title="Zaragoza">Zaragoza</a>, they learned of the persecution of Christians there by the governor <a href="/wiki/Dacian_(prefect)" title="Dacian (prefect)">Dacian</a>, who reigned in the time of the emperors <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>.  She attempted to dissuade him from his persecution, but was whipped and imprisoned when it was discovered that she was a Christian.  She died of her wounds.  Her companions were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrs_of_Zaragoza">Martyrs of Zaragoza</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engratia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Martyrs of Zaragoza">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Many others, called the <b>Martyrs of Zaragoza</b>, were martyred at the same time.<sup id="cite_ref-magnificat.ca_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnificat.ca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  They are also called the <i>Countless Martyrs of Zaragoza</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-magnificat.ca_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magnificat.ca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is said that Dacian, to detect and so make an end of all the faithful of Saragossa, ordered that liberty to practice their religion should be promised them on condition that they all went out of the city at a certain fixed time and by certain designated gates. As soon as they had thus gone forth, he ordered them to be put to the sword and their corpses burned. Their ashes were mixed with those of criminals, so that no veneration might be paid them. But a shower of rain fell and washed the ashes apart, forming those of the martyrs into certain white masses. These, known as the "holy masses" (las santas masas) were deposited in the crypt of the church dedicated to St. Engratia, where they are still preserved.<sup id="cite_ref-amadoattribution_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-amadoattribution-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their number includes, besides Engratia, Lupercius and Julia:
</p>
<ul><li>Caius and Crescentius, confessors rather than martyrs: they were imprisoned and tortured, but did not succumb to their treatment.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Successus, Martial, Urban, Quintilian, Publius, Fronto, Felix, Cecilian, Evodius, Primitivus, Apodemius, and</li>
<li>four men all sharing the name Saturninus.<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Engrazia_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Engrazia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> who, according to St. <a href="/wiki/Eugenius_II_of_Toledo" title="Eugenius II of Toledo">Eugenius II of Toledo</a> would be Jenaro, Casiano, Matutino and Fausto.</li>
<li>Januarius<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Engratia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:102px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Engratia.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Saint_Engratia.JPG/100px-Saint_Engratia.JPG" decoding="async" width="100" height="241" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Saint_Engratia.JPG/150px-Saint_Engratia.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Saint_Engratia.JPG/200px-Saint_Engratia.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2000" data-file-height="4823" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Engratia.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Engratia</i>, Bartolomé Bermejo.</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Prudentius" title="Prudentius">Prudentius</a>, a native of Zaragoza, wrote a <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymn</a> in honor of these martyrs, and lists their names, and describes the terrible tortures suffered by <i>Encratis</i> (Engratia).<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Engrazia_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Engrazia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  An important cult arose around these saints. Engratia was certainly the most venerated of the group, and her cult was diffused throughout Spain and the <a href="/wiki/Pyrenees" title="Pyrenees">Pyrenees</a>. Engracia was declared patroness of the city of Saragossa in 1480.
</p><p>During a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> held at Zaragoza in 592, the church dedicated to her there was reconsecrated, an act celebrated on November 3, which sometimes served as an alternate <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Engrazia_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Engrazia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Santa_Engr%C3%A1cia" title="Church of Santa Engrácia">Church of Santa Engrácia</a> in Lisbon is dedicated to her.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Santa_Engracia_de_Zaragoza" title="Church of Santa Engracia de Zaragoza">Church of Santa Engracia de Zaragoza</a> was built on the spot where Engratia and her companions were said to have been martyred. It was destroyed in the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Saragossa_(1809)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Saragossa (1809)">Spanish War of Independence</a>, with only the crypt and the doorway being left.<sup id="cite_ref-amadoattribution_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-amadoattribution-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It was rebuilt in the late 19th or early 20th century, and served as a <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-amado_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-amado-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
