<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Ultan</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 May</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Ultan</b> was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> who later became an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>. He was the brother of Saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fursey" title="Saint Fursey">Fursey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Foillan" title="Foillan">Foillan</a>. He was a member of Fursey's mission from Ireland to <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a> in c. 633, and lived there both as a monastic probationary and later alone as an anchorite. In c. 651 he accompanied his brother Foillan to <a href="/wiki/Nivelles" title="Nivelles">Nivelles</a> in <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> where they continued their monastic life together.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Ultan.2C_brother_of_Saint_Fursa"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Ultan,_brother_of_Saint_Fursa">Ultan, brother of Saint Fursa</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Ultan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Ultan, brother of Saint Fursa">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The seventh century St. Ultan was a brother of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fursey" title="Saint Fursey">Saint Fursey</a> or Fursa, and of <a href="/wiki/Foillan" title="Foillan">Saint Foillan</a>. He was therefore apparently the son of the royal woman Gelges, herself a daughter of King Áed of <a href="/wiki/Kings_of_Connacht" class="mw-redirect" title="Kings of Connacht">Connacht</a> (possibly <a href="/wiki/%C3%81ed_mac_Echach" title="Áed mac Echach">Áed mac Echach</a>).
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Venerable_Bede" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable Bede">Venerable Bede</a>, in his <i><a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_History_of_the_English_People" title="Ecclesiastical History of the English People">Ecclesiastical History of the English People</a></i>, relates that Ultan joined the mission led by Fursa which went from Ireland through British territory to <a href="/wiki/East_Anglia" title="East Anglia">East Anglia</a> in around 633 AD, to the kingdom of King <a href="/wiki/Sigeberht_of_East_Anglia" title="Sigeberht of East Anglia">Sigeberht of East Anglia</a>. The monastery of which he was a member there was established in the precinct of an old Roman stone-built <a href="/wiki/Roman_fort" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman fort">shore-fort</a> near the sea, at a place called <a href="/wiki/Cnobheresburg" title="Cnobheresburg">Cnobheresburg</a>. The King received them and endowed the monastery, and it was later re-endowed by King <a href="/wiki/Anna_of_East_Anglia" title="Anna of East Anglia">Anna of East Anglia</a> and his nobles.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The site is commonly identified with <a href="/wiki/Burgh_Castle" title="Burgh Castle">Burgh Castle</a> (Norfolk) near the mouth of the river <a href="/wiki/Yare" class="mw-redirect" title="Yare">Yare</a>, thought to be the <a href="/wiki/Garianonum" class="mw-redirect" title="Garianonum">Garianonum</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Notitia_Dignitatum" title="Notitia Dignitatum">Notitia Dignitatum</a> and of the geographical description of Britain by <a href="/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy" class="mw-redirect" title="Claudius Ptolemy">Claudius Ptolemy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After several years in which he served a probation in the monastery at Cnobheresburg, Ultan went off to live alone in East Anglia as a hermit. In around 643 Fursey handed his duties to Foillan and went to join Ultan, taking nothing with him, and they lived for a year together by the labour of their hands in a life of contemplation and philosophy. However the kingdom was disturbed by inroads of heathens, and Fursey left East Anglia in around 644, entrusting the monastery in East Anglia and its brethren to his brother Foillan. After being welcomed by <a href="/wiki/Erchinoald" title="Erchinoald">Erchinoald</a> at <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9ronne,_Somme" title="Péronne, Somme">Péronne</a> and by <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> and Queen <a href="/wiki/Balthild" class="mw-redirect" title="Balthild">Balthild</a>, Fursey was granted an estate at <a href="/wiki/Lagny-sur-Marne" title="Lagny-sur-Marne">Lagny</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Marne_(river)" title="Marne (river)">Marne</a>, where he built a monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A record preserved at <a href="/wiki/Nivelles" title="Nivelles">Nivelles</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> shows that Foillan and his brethren (including Ultan) fled the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia" title="Kingdom of East Anglia">Kingdom of East Anglia</a> with the help of King <a href="/wiki/Anna_of_East_Anglia" title="Anna of East Anglia">Anna of East Anglia</a> in 651, when the monastery was under attack from King <a href="/wiki/Penda_of_Mercia" title="Penda of Mercia">Penda of Mercia</a>, and King Anna himself was then exiled from his own kingdom. Foillan and Ultan took away the precious property and books of the monastery, and after unhappy dealings with Erchinoald they were received by Ste <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles" title="Gertrude of Nivelles">Gertrude of Nivelles</a> and her mother <a href="/wiki/Itta" class="mw-redirect" title="Itta">Itta</a>. Foillan went off to found a monastery at Fosse (now <a href="/wiki/Fosses-la-Ville" title="Fosses-la-Ville">Fosses-la-Ville</a>) near <a href="/wiki/Namur_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Namur (city)">Namur</a> with the encouragement and support of Itta, but was murdered with some companions not long afterwards by bandits, during a journey from Nivelles to Fosse.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Musician">Musician</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Ultan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Musician">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to Grattan Flood, "About the year 653, St. Gertrude, of Brabant, (daughter of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Landen" title="Pepin of Landen">Pepin</a>, Mayor of the Palace), abbess of Nivelle, in Brabant, sent for St. Foillan and St. Ultan, brothers of our celebrated St. <a href="/wiki/Fursey" class="mw-redirect" title="Fursey">Fursey</a> (Patron of <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9ronne,_Somme" title="Péronne, Somme">Perrone</a>), to teach <a href="/wiki/Psalmody" class="mw-redirect" title="Psalmody">psalmody</a> to her nuns. These two Irish monks complied with her request, and built an adjoining monastery at Fosse, in the diocese of Liege."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
