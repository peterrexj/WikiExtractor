<p><b>Raymond of Fitero</b> (also known as <i>Ramon Sierra</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Raimundo de Fitero</i>) (*? - †<a href="/wiki/Ciruelos" title="Ciruelos">Ciruelos</a>, 1163) was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, and founder of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Calatrava" title="Order of Calatrava">Order of Calatrava</a>.
</p><p>His birthplace is unknown; <a href="/wiki/Saint-Gaudens,_Haute-Garonne" title="Saint-Gaudens, Haute-Garonne">Saint-Gaudens</a> (<a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>), <a href="/wiki/Tarazona" title="Tarazona">Tarazona</a> (<a href="/wiki/Aragon" title="Aragon">Aragon</a>), and <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> (<a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a>) have all claimed to be Raymond's birthplace.
</p><p>As a young man, Raymond felt a religious vocation, and became a <a href="/wiki/Canon_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon priest">canon</a> of the new cathedral at Tarazona, established after King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_I_of_Aragon" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso I of Aragon">Alfonso I of Aragon</a> <a href="/wiki/Reconquista" title="Reconquista">reconquered</a> the historic city from the <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moors</a> in 1119.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:264px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg/262px-Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg" decoding="async" width="262" height="142" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg/393px-Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg/524px-Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="348" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Calatrava_Vieja_coracha.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Calatrava la Vieja</div></div></div>
<p>Across the <a href="/wiki/Pyrenees" title="Pyrenees">Pyrenees</a> mountains, at <a href="/wiki/Escaladieu_Abbey" title="Escaladieu Abbey">Escaladieu Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>, Raymond became a monk of the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian Order">Cistercian Order</a>, which had been founded relatively recently (in 1098) and which accepted many former <a href="/wiki/Knights" class="mw-redirect" title="Knights">knights</a> as members. When King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VII_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso VII of Castile">Alfonso VII of Castile</a> supported the order's extension into <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, Raymond joined abbot Durando (a.k.a. Durandus, Durand) and other monks and established a new monastery near the Ebro River at <a href="/wiki/Rinc%C3%B3n_de_Soto" title="Rincón de Soto">Nienzabas (Niencebas)</a>, between <a href="/wiki/Calahorra" title="Calahorra">Calahorra</a> (reconquered from the Moors in 1045) and <a href="/wiki/Tudela,_Navarre" title="Tudela, Navarre">Tudela</a> (which Alfonso I had recaptured from the Moors in 1114 and was still subject to raids). At Durando's death, fellow monks elected Raymond (who had been <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a>) his successor. The monks then moved across the Ebro to strategic <a href="/wiki/Castej%C3%B3n,_Navarre" title="Castejón, Navarre">Castejón, Navarre</a>, and finally built their new monastery at a spot named <a href="/wiki/Fitero" title="Fitero">Fitero</a> (Castellón de Fitero), situated slightly up the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Alhama_river&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Alhama river (page does not exist)">Alhama river</a> from Castejón along the frontier between Castile's <a href="/wiki/La_Rioja_(Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="La Rioja (Spain)">La Rioja region</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Navarre" title="Kingdom of Navarre">Kingdom of Navarre</a>. They called their new monastery the <a href="/wiki/Monasterio_de_Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_la_Real_de_Fitero" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasterio de Santa María la Real de Fitero">Monasterio de Santa María la Real de Fitero</a>.
</p><p>When King Alfonso VII died in 1158, Raymond went to <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a> so that the new king, <a href="/wiki/Sancho_III_of_Castile" title="Sancho III of Castile">Sancho III of Castile</a>, could confirm the privileges that his father had granted the new monastery. In Toledo Raymond's companion, former knight Father Diego Velásquez, learned that Christian leaders planned a major offensive south against the Moors. Furthermore, Sancho promised to grant the strategic town of Calatrava (<a href="/wiki/Calatrava_la_Vieja" title="Calatrava la Vieja">Calatrava la Vieja</a>) on the <a href="/wiki/Guadiana_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Guadiana River">Guadiana River</a> to anyone who promised to defend it from the Moors, who might themselves be planning a sally north to test the new Christian king. His father had reconquered Calatrava in 1147, and it was on the road from <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a> (reconquered in 1085) to <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Andalusia" class="mw-redirect" title="Córdoba, Andalusia">Córdoba</a> and Moorish strongholds.
</p><p>Encouraged by Father Diego, Raymond took up the challenge, and Sancho granted them the privilege of defending Calatrava. With the support of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Toledo" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Toledo">Archbishop of Toledo</a>, Raymond organized an army that successfully prevented a Moorish attack on Calatrava that year.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This success prompted Raymond to found the military Order of Calatrava, organized along Cistercian<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> lines.  Raymond then moved some fighting monks south from the relatively safe Fitero in Navarre to Calatrava in what became the <a href="/wiki/Castilla-La_Mancha" class="mw-redirect" title="Castilla-La Mancha">Castilla-La Mancha</a> province. He himself retired to Ciruelos, near <a href="/wiki/Oca%C3%B1a,_Spain" title="Ocaña, Spain">Ocaña</a>, where he died in 1163. On September 26, 1164 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a> recognized the new military order, which played a crucial role in the Reconquest.
</p>
