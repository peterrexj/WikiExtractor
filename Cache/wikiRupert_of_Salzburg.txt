<p><b>Rupert of Salzburg</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Ruprecht</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Robertus, Rupertus</i>; <span title="circa">c.</span> 660<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> &#8211; 710 AD) was <a href="/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Worms" title="Prince-Bishopric of Worms">Bishop of Worms</a> as well as the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Salzburg">Bishop of Salzburg</a> and abbot of <a href="/wiki/St_Peter%27s_Abbey,_Salzburg" title="St Peter&#39;s Abbey, Salzburg">St. Peter's Abbey</a> in Salzburg. He was a contemporary of the <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Childebert_III" title="Childebert III">Childebert III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Rupert is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> Churches.<sup id="cite_ref-ortheng_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ortheng-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Rupert is also <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austrian</a> state of <a href="/wiki/Salzburg_(state)" title="Salzburg (state)">Salzburg</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rupert_of_Salzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Holy tradition states that Rupert was a <a href="/wiki/Lineal_descendant" title="Lineal descendant">scion</a> of the Frankish royal <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian dynasty</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-ortheng_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ortheng-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> he was possibly related to the <a href="/wiki/Robertians" title="Robertians">Robertians</a>, and likely a descendant of Count palatine <a href="/wiki/Chrodbert_(count_palatine_of_Chlothar_III)" title="Chrodbert (count palatine of Chlothar III)">Chrodbert II</a>.  
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Franciscus_de_Neve_(II)_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Franciscus_de_Neve_%28II%29_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg/220px-Franciscus_de_Neve_%28II%29_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="302" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Franciscus_de_Neve_%28II%29_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg/330px-Franciscus_de_Neve_%28II%29_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Franciscus_de_Neve_%28II%29_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg/440px-Franciscus_de_Neve_%28II%29_-_De_H._Rupert_doopt_de_Beijerse_hertog_Theodo.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="474" data-file-height="650" /></a><figcaption>Baptism of Duke Theodo by Bishop Rupert, <a href="/wiki/Franciscus_de_Neve_(II)" title="Franciscus de Neve (II)">Franciscus de Neve (II)</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1670</span>).</figcaption></figure>
<p>In his missionary work in Germany Rupert was accompanied by Saints <a href="/wiki/Chuniald" title="Chuniald">Chuniald</a> and Gislar, but no records of their acts have survived.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873536_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873536-6">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
As bishop at Worms, Rupert was first accepted as a wise and devout dignitary, but the mostly pagan community came to reject him and forced him out of the city by the end of the 7th century. The <a href="/wiki/Agilolfings" title="Agilolfings">Agilolfing</a> duke <a href="/wiki/Theodo_of_Bavaria" title="Theodo of Bavaria">Theodo of Bavaria</a> requested that he come to his residence at <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a> (Ratisbon) to help spread the <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christian</a> faith among the <a href="/wiki/Bavarians" title="Bavarians">Bavarian</a> tribes.
</p><p>Rupert then moved to <a href="/wiki/Alt%C3%B6tting" title="Altötting">Altötting</a>, where he started his missionary work by preaching to the locals. He would sail down the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> river, visiting many towns, villages and forts. Soon he had converted a large population along the Danube, reaching southeastward to the Bavarian border with the <a href="/wiki/Pannonia" title="Pannonia">Pannonian</a> lands, which were under the rule of the <a href="/wiki/Avar_Khaganate" class="mw-redirect" title="Avar Khaganate">Avar Khaganate</a>. Here, he stayed at Lorch, the former <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> city of <i>Lauriacum</i> (today part of <a href="/wiki/Enns_(town)" title="Enns (town)">Enns</a>), where an <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" title="Early Christianity">early Christian</a> church—the present <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Lawrence,_Enns" title="Basilica of St. Lawrence, Enns">Basilica of St. Lawrence</a>—already existed.
</p><p>Warlike conditions in the borderlands made him abandon plans of missionary work in the territories of the <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Avars" title="Pannonian Avars">Pannonian Avars</a>. Instead, he proceeded along the <a href="/wiki/Roman_road" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman road">Roman road</a> via <a href="/wiki/Seekirchen_am_Wallersee" title="Seekirchen am Wallersee">Seekirchen</a> to the ruined city of <i><a href="/wiki/Salzburg#Antiquity_to_Early_Modern_period" title="Salzburg">Juvavum</a></i>, which he made his base and renamed "Salzburg" (Latin: <i>Salisburgum</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDelehaye1911_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelehaye1911-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> As in Lorch, Rupert was able to build on early Christian traditions that were already in place. He re-established the monastic community at St. Peter's Abbey and laid the foundations of <a href="/wiki/Salzburg_Cathedral" title="Salzburg Cathedral">Salzburg Cathedral</a>, which was finished by his successor <a href="/wiki/Vergilius_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Vergilius of Salzburg">Vergilius</a>. He also founded the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> nunnery of <a href="/wiki/Nonnberg_Abbey" title="Nonnberg Abbey">Nonnberg</a> beneath the <a href="/wiki/Festungsberg" title="Festungsberg">Festungsberg</a> fortifications (later <a href="/wiki/Hohensalzburg_Fortress" title="Hohensalzburg Fortress">Hohensalzburg Fortress</a>), where his niece <a href="/wiki/Saint_Erentrude" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Erentrude">Erentrude</a> became the first abbess. 
</p><p>Rupert also introduced higher <a href="/wiki/Education" title="Education">education</a> and other reforms. From Duke Theodo of Bavaria his bishopric received estates around <a href="/wiki/Piding" title="Piding">Piding</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bad_Reichenhall" title="Bad Reichenhall">Reichenhall</a>, where he promoted the development of the local <a href="/wiki/Salt" title="Salt">saltworks</a>. Rupert's mission work also spread into the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Alps" title="Eastern Alps">Alps</a>, where the first <a href="/wiki/Monastic_cell" title="Monastic cell">monastic cell</a> (<i>Cella Maximiliana</i>) was founded at present-day <a href="/wiki/Bischofshofen" title="Bischofshofen">Bischofshofen</a> about 711.
</p><p>Rupert reportedly died on <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter Sunday</a> around 710.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to other sources, he returned to his hometown of Worms, where he died in 717. His mortal remains were transferred to Salzburg Cathedral by Bishop Vergilius on 24 September 774.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rupert_of_Salzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rupert's life and mission work is documented in medieval chronicles such as the <i><a href="/wiki/Conversio_Bagoariorum_et_Carantanorum" title="Conversio Bagoariorum et Carantanorum">Conversio Bagoariorum et Carantanorum</a></i>. In accordance with Christian tradition, St. Rupert's feast day is celebrated by the Eastern Orthodox Church on the anniversary of his death, 27 March<sup id="cite_ref-ortheng_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ortheng-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (28 March according to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Lutheran)" title="Calendar of saints (Lutheran)">Lutheran Calendar of Saints</a>). In <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, it is 24 September,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> commemorating the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translation</a> of his relics to Salzburg Cathedral. <i>Rupertitag</i> or <i><a href="/wiki/Rupertikirtag" title="Rupertikirtag">Rupertikirtag</a></i> is also a public holiday in the <a href="/wiki/Salzburg_(state)" title="Salzburg (state)">state of Salzburg</a>, associated with popular <i><a href="/wiki/Volksfest" title="Volksfest">Volksfest</a></i> events.
</p><p>Rupert is the patron saint of the state of Salzburg, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Salzburg" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Salzburg">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Salzburg</a> (together with his successor Vergilius), and of the adjacent Bavarian <a href="/wiki/Rupertiwinkel" title="Rupertiwinkel">Rupertiwinkel</a> region. He is also known as the "Apostle of the Bavarians" and is patron of several settlements, such as <a href="/wiki/Sankt_Ruprecht_an_der_Raab" title="Sankt Ruprecht an der Raab">Sankt Ruprecht</a> in <a href="/wiki/Styria" title="Styria">Styria</a> and <a href="/wiki/%C5%A0entrupert" title="Šentrupert">Šentrupert</a> in <a href="/wiki/Slovenia" title="Slovenia">Slovenia</a>, and of numerous church buildings.
</p>
