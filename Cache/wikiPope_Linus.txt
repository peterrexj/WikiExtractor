<p><b>Pope Linus</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/aɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;tide&#39;">aɪ</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span>&#32;<span data-nosnippet="" id="ooui-php-1" class="ext-phonos ext-phonos-PhonosButton noexcerpt ext-phonos-PhonosButton-emptylabel oo-ui-widget oo-ui-widget-enabled oo-ui-buttonElement oo-ui-buttonElement-frameless oo-ui-iconElement oo-ui-buttonWidget" data-ooui="{&quot;_&quot;:&quot;mw.Phonos.PhonosButton&quot;,&quot;href&quot;:&quot;\/\/upload.wikimedia.org\/wikipedia\/commons\/transcoded\/5\/5c\/Pope_Linus_Pronunciation.ogg\/Pope_Linus_Pronunciation.ogg.mp3&quot;,&quot;rel&quot;:[&quot;nofollow&quot;],&quot;framed&quot;:false,&quot;icon&quot;:&quot;volumeUp&quot;,&quot;data&quot;:{&quot;ipa&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;text&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;lang&quot;:&quot;en&quot;,&quot;wikibase&quot;:&quot;&quot;,&quot;file&quot;:&quot;Pope Linus Pronunciation.ogg&quot;},&quot;classes&quot;:[&quot;ext-phonos&quot;,&quot;ext-phonos-PhonosButton&quot;,&quot;noexcerpt&quot;,&quot;ext-phonos-PhonosButton-emptylabel&quot;]}"><a role="button" tabindex="0" href="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5c/Pope_Linus_Pronunciation.ogg/Pope_Linus_Pronunciation.ogg.mp3" rel="nofollow" aria-label="Play audio" class="oo-ui-buttonElement-button"><span class="oo-ui-iconElement-icon oo-ui-icon-volumeUp"></span><span class="oo-ui-labelElement-label"></span><span class="oo-ui-indicatorElement-indicator oo-ui-indicatorElement-noIndicator"></span></a></span><sup class="ext-phonos-attribution noexcerpt"><a href="/wiki/File:Pope_Linus_Pronunciation.ogg" title="File:Pope Linus Pronunciation.ogg">i</a></sup></span>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Λῖνος</span>, <i>Linos</i>; As with all the early popes, he was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Irenaeus" title="Irenaeus">Irenaeus</a>, Linus is the same person as the one mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Linus is mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Valediction" title="Valediction">valediction</a> of the <i><a href="/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="Second Epistle to Timothy">Second Epistle to Timothy</a></i> as being with <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> near the end of Paul's life.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Linus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The earliest witness to the episcopate of Linus was <a href="/wiki/Irenaeus" title="Irenaeus">Irenaeus</a>, who in <span title="circa">c.</span> AD 180 wrote that "the blessed apostles, then, having founded and built up the Church, committed into the hands of Linus the office of the episcopate".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the earliest succession lists of bishops of Rome, passed down by Irenaeus and <a href="/wiki/Hegesippus_(chronicler)" title="Hegesippus (chronicler)">Hegesippus</a> and attested by the historian <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius</a>, Linus was entrusted with his office by the apostles <a href="/wiki/Peter_the_Apostle" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter the Apostle">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a> after they had established the Christian church in Rome. By this reckoning he might be considered therefore the first pope, but from the late 2nd or early 3rd century the convention began of regarding Peter as the first pope.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> described Linus as "the first after Peter to be in charge of the Roman Church"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> described him as "the first to receive the episcopate of the church at Rome, after the martyrdom of Paul and Peter".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a> wrote that "this Linus, some say, was second bishop of the Church of Rome after Peter",<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> while the <i><a href="/wiki/Liberian_Catalogue" title="Liberian Catalogue">Liberian Catalogue</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> described Peter as the first bishop of Rome and Linus as his successor in the same office.
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> also enumerated Linus as the second <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> after Peter, and stated that Peter consecrated two bishops, Linus and <a href="/wiki/Anacletus" class="mw-redirect" title="Anacletus">Anacletus</a>, for the priestly service of the community, while devoting himself instead to prayer and preaching, and that it was <a href="/wiki/Clement_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement I">Clement I</a> to whom he entrusted the universal Church and whom he appointed as his successor. <a href="/wiki/Tertullian" title="Tertullian">Tertullian</a> also wrote of Clement as the successor of Peter.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> named Clement as "the fourth bishop of Rome after Peter, if indeed the second was Linus and the third Anacletus, although most of the Latins think that Clement was second after the apostle".<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Constitutions" title="Apostolic Constitutions">Apostolic Constitutions</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> note that Linus, whom Paul the Apostle consecrated, was the first bishop of Rome and that he was succeeded by Clement I, whom Peter the Apostle ordained and consecrated.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Linus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Liberian_Catalogue" title="Liberian Catalogue">Liberian Catalogue</a></i> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i> date the episcopate of Linus as AD 56 to 67, during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>, but <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> dated it as AD 67 to 78, and <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> dated the end of his episcopate in the second year of the reign of <a href="/wiki/Titus" title="Titus">Titus</a>, <i>scire licet</i>, AD 80.
</p><p>Linus is named in the <a href="/wiki/Valediction" title="Valediction">valediction</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="Second Epistle to Timothy">Second Epistle to Timothy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In that epistle, Linus is noted as being with Paul the Apostle in Rome near the end of Paul's life. Irenaeus stated that this is the same Linus who became Bishop of Rome.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_II_-_Patrobulus,_Hermas,_Linus,_Caius,_Philologus_of_70_disciples_(Portrait_of_Linus).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/eb/Menologion_of_Basil_II_-_Patrobulus%2C_Hermas%2C_Linus%2C_Caius%2C_Philologus_of_70_disciples_%28Portrait_of_Linus%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="260" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="160" data-file-height="260" /></a><figcaption>Depiction of Linus in the <a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>According to the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i>, Linus was an Italian born in <a href="/wiki/Volterra" title="Volterra">Volterra</a> in Tuscany. His father's name was recorded as Herculanus. The <i>Apostolic Constitutions</i> denominated his mother Claudia; immediately after the name Linus in 2 <i>Timothy</i> 4:21 a Claudia is named, but the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a> does not explicitly identify Claudia as Linus' mother. According to the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i>, Linus decreed that women should cover their heads in church, created the first 15 bishops, and died a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Loomis_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loomis-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> It dated his death as 23 September, on which date he is still commemorated.<sup id="cite_ref-martyr_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyr-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> His name is included in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Canon" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Canon">Roman Canon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>.
</p><p>With respect to Linus' purported decree prescribing the covering of women's heads, <a href="/wiki/J.P._Kirsch" class="mw-redirect" title="J.P. Kirsch">J.P. Kirsch</a> commented in the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> that "without doubt this decree is apocryphal, and copied by the author of the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i> from Paul's <i><a href="/wiki/First_Epistle_to_the_Corinthians" title="First Epistle to the Corinthians">First Epistle to the Corinthians</a></i> (11:5) and arbitrarily attributed to the first successor of the Apostle in Rome. The statement made in the same source, that Linus suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>, cannot be proved and is improbable. For between <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a> and <a href="/wiki/Domitian" title="Domitian">Domitian</a> there is no mention of any persecution of the Roman Church; and Irenaeus (1. c., III, iv, 3) from among the early Roman bishops designates only <a href="/wiki/Pope_Telesphorus" title="Pope Telesphorus">Telesphorus</a> as a glorious martyr."<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> does not categorize Linus as a martyr as does the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i>; the current entry in the former regarding him states: "At Rome, the commemoration of Saint Linus, Pope, to whom, as Saint Irenaeus narrates, the blessed Apostles entrusted the responsibility of the episcopate of the Church founded in the City, and whom the blessed Paul the Apostle mentions as a companion of his."<sup id="cite_ref-martyr_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyr-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Linus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A tomb that <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Torrigio" title="Francesco Maria Torrigio">Torrigio</a> discovered in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> in 1615 and which was inscribed with the letters LINVS was assumed to be the tomb of Pope Linus. However, a note by Torrigio records that these were merely the final five letters of some unknown longer name, such as "Aquilinus" or "Anullinus". A letter on the martyrdom of Peter and Paul was attributed to Linus, but in fact it was determined to date to the 6th century.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i> asserts that Linus was buried on the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Hill" title="Vatican Hill">Vatican Hill</a> adjacent to Peter the Apostle in what is now known as the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Necropolis" title="Vatican Necropolis">Vatican Necropolis</a> beneath <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> in Vatican City.<sup id="cite_ref-Loomis_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loomis-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The city of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Lin-Laurentides" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Lin-Laurentides">Saint-Lin-Laurentides</a> in Canada is named in his honour.
</p>
