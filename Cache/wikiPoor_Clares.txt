<p>The <b>Poor Clares</b>, officially the <b>Order of Saint Clare</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ordo sanctae Clarae</i>) – originally referred to as the <b>Order of Poor Ladies</b>, and later the <b>Clarisses</b>, the <b>Minoresses</b>, the <b>Franciscan Clarist Order</b>, and the <b>Second Order of Saint Francis</b> – are members of a <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">contemplative Order</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. The Poor Clares were the second <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> branch of the order to be established. Founded by <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> on <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a> in the year 1212, they were organized after the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> (the <i>first Order</i>), and before the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> for the laity. As of 2011, there were over 20,000 Poor Clare nuns in over 75 countries throughout the world. They follow several different observances and are organized into federations.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Poor Clares follow the <i><a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Clare">Rule of St. Clare</a></i>, which was approved by Pope <a href="/wiki/Innocent_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Innocent IV">Innocent IV</a> on the day before Clare's death in 1253. The main branch of the Order (O.S.C.) follows the observance of Pope Urban. Other branches established since that time, who operate under their own unique <a href="/wiki/Constitutions" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitutions">Constitutions</a>, are the <a href="/wiki/Colettine_Poor_Clares" title="Colettine Poor Clares">Colettine Poor Clares</a> (P.C.C.) (founded 1410), the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Poor_Clares" title="Capuchin Poor Clares">Capuchin Poor Clares</a> (O.S.C. Cap) (founded 1538) and the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares_of_Perpetual_Adoration" title="Poor Clares of Perpetual Adoration">Poor Clares of Perpetual Adoration</a> (P.C.P.A.) (founded 1854).
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_and_rule">Foundation and rule</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Foundation and rule">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>The Poor Clares were founded by <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a> in 1212. Little is known of Clare's early life, although popular tradition hints that she came from a fairly well-to-do family in <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>. At the age of 17, inspired by the preaching of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Assisi_Cathedral" title="Assisi Cathedral">cathedral</a>, Clare ran away from home to join her community of friars at the <a href="/wiki/Porziuncola" class="mw-redirect" title="Porziuncola">Portiuncula</a>, some distance outside the town.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Although, according to tradition, her family wanted to take her back by force, Clare's dedication to holiness and poverty inspired the friars to accept her resolution. She was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> of a nun and transferred to <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monasteries, first at Bastia and then at Sant' Angelo di Panzo, for her monastic formation.
</p><p>By 1216, Francis was able to offer Clare and her companions a monastery adjoining the chapel of <a href="/wiki/San_Damiano,_Assisi" title="San Damiano, Assisi">San Damiano</a> where she became <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>. Clare's mother, two of her sisters and some other wealthy women from <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> soon joined her new order. Clare dedicated her order to the strict principles of Francis, setting a rule of extreme poverty far more severe than that of any female order of the time.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer,_David_1997_p._103_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer,_David_1997_p._103-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Clare's determination that her order not be wealthy or own property, and that the nuns live entirely from alms given by local people, was initially protected by the papal bull <i>Privilegium paupertatis</i>, issued by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_Encyclopedia_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic_Encyclopedia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> By this time the order had grown to number three monasteries.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spread_of_the_order">Spread of the order</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Spread of the order">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The movement quickly spread, though in a somewhat disorganized fashion, with several monasteries of women devoted to the Franciscan ideal springing up elsewhere in Northern Italy. At this point Ugolino, Cardinal Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica_(district)" title="Ostia Antica (district)">Ostia</a> (the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a>), was given the task of overseeing all such monasteries and preparing a formal <a href="/wiki/Monastic_Rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic Rule">rule</a>. Although monasteries at <a href="/wiki/Monticello" title="Monticello">Monticello</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, Gattajola and elsewhere adopted the new rule – which allowed for property to be held in trust by the papacy for the various communities – it was not adopted by Clare herself or her monastery at San Damiano.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_Encyclopedia_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic_Encyclopedia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ugolino's Rule, originally based on the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> one, was amended in 1263 by Pope Urban IV to allow for the communal ownership of property, and was adopted by a growing number of monasteries across <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>. Communities adopting this less rigorous rule came to be known as the Order of Saint Clare (O.S.C.) or the Urbanist Poor Clares.<sup id="cite_ref-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2007,_p._603_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2007,_p._603-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Clare herself resisted the Ugolino Rule, since it did not closely enough follow the ideal of complete poverty advocated by Francis. On 9 August 1253, she managed to obtain a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a>, <i>Solet annuere</i>, establishing a rule of her own, more closely following that of the friars, which <a href="/wiki/Corporate_poverty" title="Corporate poverty">forbade the possession of property either individually or as a community</a>. Originally applying only to Clare's community at San Damiano, this rule was also adopted by many monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_Encyclopedia_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic_Encyclopedia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Communities that followed this stricter rule were fewer in number than the followers of the rule formulated by Cardinal Ugolino, and became known simply as "Poor Clares" (P.C.) or Primitives. Many sources before 1263 refer to them as Damianites (after San Damiano).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The situation was further complicated a century later when <a href="/wiki/Colette_of_Corbie" title="Colette of Corbie">Colette of Corbie</a> restored the primitive rule of strict poverty to 17 French monasteries. Her followers came to be called the <a href="/wiki/Colettine_Poor_Clares" title="Colettine Poor Clares">Colettine Poor Clares</a> (P.C.C.). Two further branches, the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Poor_Clares" title="Capuchin Poor Clares">Capuchin Poor Clares</a> (O.S.C. Cap.) and the <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Alcantara" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter of Alcantara">Alcantarines</a>, also followed the strict observance.<sup id="cite_ref-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2007,_p._603_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2007,_p._603-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The later group disappeared as a distinct group when their observance among the friars was ended, with the friars being merged by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> into the wider observant branch of the First Order.
</p><p>The spread of the order began in 1218 when a monastery was founded in <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>; new foundations quickly followed in <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>. <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Assisi" title="Agnes of Assisi">Agnes of Assisi</a>, a sister of Clare, introduced the order to <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> and <a href="/wiki/Burgos" title="Burgos">Burgos</a> hosted major communities. The order then expanded to <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a> and <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, where a monastery was founded at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> in 1229, followed by <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cahors" title="Cahors">Cahors</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bordeaux" title="Bordeaux">Bordeaux</a>, <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on" title="Besançon">Besançon</a>. A monastery at <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a> was founded directly from Assisi in 1254.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_Encyclopedia_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic_Encyclopedia-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Poor Clares monastery founded by <a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Provence" title="Margaret of Provence">Queen Margaret</a> in Paris, St. Marcel, was where she died in 1295.<sup id="cite_ref-Robson328_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robson328-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Philip_IV_of_France" title="Philip IV of France">King Philip IV</a> and <a href="/wiki/Joan_I_of_Navarre" title="Joan I of Navarre">Queen Joan</a> founded a monastery at Moncel in the Beauvais diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-Robson328_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Robson328-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> By A.D. 1300 there were 47 Poor Clare monasteries in Spain alone.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer,_David_1997_p._103_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer,_David_1997_p._103-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Europe">Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="United_Kingdom">United Kingdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: United Kingdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>The first Poor Clare monastery in England was founded in 1286 in <a href="/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne" title="Newcastle upon Tyne">Newcastle upon Tyne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Hereford_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hereford-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In medieval England, where the nuns were known as "minoresses", their principal monastery was located near <a href="/wiki/Aldgate" title="Aldgate">Aldgate</a>, known as the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_the_Order_of_St_Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of the Order of St Clare">Abbey of the Order of St Clare</a>. The order gave its name to the still-extant street known as <a href="/wiki/Minories" title="Minories">Minories</a> on the eastern boundary of the <a href="/wiki/City_of_London" title="City of London">City of London</a>.
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries" title="Dissolution of the monasteries">dissolution of the monasteries</a> under King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII" title="Henry VIII">Henry VIII</a>, several religious communities formed in <a href="/wiki/Continental_Europe" title="Continental Europe">continental Europe</a> for <a href="/wiki/English_people" title="English people">English</a> Catholics. One such was a <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clare_Convent_(Gravelines)" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clare Convent (Gravelines)">Poor Clare monastery</a> founded in 1609 at <a href="/wiki/Gravelines" title="Gravelines">Gravelines</a> by <a href="/wiki/Mary_Ward_(nun)" title="Mary Ward (nun)">Mary Ward</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Hereford_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hereford-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Later expelled from their monastery by the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Army" title="French Revolutionary Army">French Revolutionary Army</a> in 1795, the community eventually relocated to <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>. They settled first in Northumberland, and then in 1857 built a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Darlington" title="Darlington">Darlington</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Hereford_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hereford-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> which was in existence until 2007.
</p><p>Following <a href="/wiki/Catholic_emancipation" title="Catholic emancipation">Catholic emancipation</a> in the first half of the 19th century, other Poor Clares came to the <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> eventually establishing communities in, e.g., <a href="/wiki/Notting_Hill" title="Notting Hill">Notting Hill</a> (1857, which was forced to relocate by the local council in the 1960s, and settled in the village of <a href="/wiki/Arkley" title="Arkley">Arkley</a> in 1969),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Convent_of_Poor_Clares,_Woodchester" title="Convent of Poor Clares, Woodchester">Woodchester</a> (1860–2011), <a href="/wiki/Levenshulme" title="Levenshulme">Levenshulme</a> (1863),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Much_Birch" title="Much Birch">Much Birch</a> (1880), <a href="/wiki/Arundel" title="Arundel">Arundel</a> (1886), <a href="/wiki/Lynton" title="Lynton">Lynton</a> (founded from <a href="/wiki/Rennes" title="Rennes">Rennes</a>, France, 1904–2010s), <a href="/wiki/Woodford_Green" title="Woodford Green">Woodford Green</a> (1920–1969), <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> (1865–2015)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Nottingham" title="Nottingham">Nottingham</a> (1927).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The community in <a href="/wiki/Luton" title="Luton">Luton</a> was founded in 1976 to meet a shortage of teachers for local Catholic schools. It was originally based at 18 London Road in a large Edwardian house. In 1996, the community refocused on a ministry of social work and prayer, and moved to a smaller, modern home at Abigail Close, Wardown Park.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Communities of Colettine Poor Clares were founded in England at <a href="/wiki/Baddesley_Clinton" title="Baddesley Clinton">Baddesley Clinton</a> (1850–2011),<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Ellesmere,_Shropshire" title="Ellesmere, Shropshire">Ellesmere, Shropshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> They have communities in <a href="/wiki/Belfast" title="Belfast">Belfast</a>, <a href="/wiki/Northern_Ireland" title="Northern Ireland">Northern Ireland</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Bothwell" title="Bothwell">Bothwell</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> (1952). In Wales, there was a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Hawarden" title="Hawarden">Hawarden</a>. The one that used to be based in Neath moved to Bothwell.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Ireland">Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> there are seven monasteries of the Colettine Observance. The community with the oldest historical roots is the monastery on Nuns' Island in <a href="/wiki/Galway" title="Galway">Galway</a>, which traces its history back to the monastery in Gravelines. The community has a rare book collection which is the most comprehensive single collection of early-modern Clarissan material in English in the world.  <sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Originally a separate community of <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish women</a> under a common <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mother_superior" class="extiw" title="wikt:mother superior">mother superior</a> with the English nuns, they moved to <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> in 1629, the first monastic community in Ireland for a century. The first Abbess was <a href="/wiki/Cecily_Dillon" title="Cecily Dillon">Cecily Dillon</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Theobald_Dillon,_1st_Viscount_Dillon" title="Theobald Dillon, 1st Viscount Dillon">Theobald Dillon, 1st Viscount Dillon</a>. War forced the community to move back to Galway in 1642. From that point on, persecution under the <a href="/wiki/Penal_Laws" title="Penal Laws">Penal Laws</a> and war led to repeated destruction of their monastery and scattering of the community over two centuries, until 1825, when fifteen nuns were able to re-establish monastic life permanently on the site.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Later monasteries were founded in 1906 in both <a href="/wiki/Carlow" title="Carlow">Carlow</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>. From these, foundations were established in <a href="/wiki/Cork_(city)" title="Cork (city)">Cork</a> (1914) and <a href="/wiki/Ennis" title="Ennis">Ennis</a> (1958). In 1973, an <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">enclosed</a> community of nuns of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis#Third_Order_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Franciscan Third Order Regular</a> in <a href="/wiki/Drumshanbo" title="Drumshanbo">Drumshanbo</a>, founded in England in 1852 and established there in 1864, transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a>, under this Observance.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is Poor Clares monastery in <a href="/wiki/Faughart" title="Faughart">Faughart</a>, <a href="/wiki/Co._Louth" class="mw-redirect" title="Co. Louth">Co. Louth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Continental_Europe">Continental Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Continental Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Klarenaltar1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Klarenaltar1.jpg/250px-Klarenaltar1.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="174" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Klarenaltar1.jpg/375px-Klarenaltar1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Klarenaltar1.jpg/500px-Klarenaltar1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3676" data-file-height="2553" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Klarenaltar1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gothic altar in <a href="/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral" title="Cologne Cathedral">Cologne Cathedral</a> dedicated to Poor Clare saints</div></div></div>
<p>Currently there are communities of Colettine Poor Clares in <a href="/wiki/Bruges" title="Bruges">Bruges</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, as well as in <a href="/wiki/Eindhoven" title="Eindhoven">Eindhoven</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Larvik" title="Larvik">Larvik</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a>. There are several monasteries in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a> and <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> of the Urbanist and Capuchin Observances.
</p><p>There are notable Clarissine churches in <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bratislava" title="Bratislava">Bratislava</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brixen" title="Brixen">Brixen</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Nuremberg" title="Nuremberg">Nuremberg</a>. There also is a small community in <a href="/wiki/M%C3%BCnster" title="Münster">Münster</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, and a Capuchin monastery in <a href="/wiki/Sigolsheim" title="Sigolsheim">Sigolsheim</a>, France.
</p><p>The last six Poor Clare nuns from a convent in Belgium were able to sell their convent and move to the South of France in luxury cars.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Americas">Americas</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Americas">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="United_States">United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>After an abortive attempt to establish the Order in the United States in the early 1800s by three nuns who were refugees of <a href="/wiki/Revolutionary_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Revolutionary France">Revolutionary France</a>, the Poor Clares were not permanently established in the country until the late 1870s.
</p><p>A small group of Colettine nuns arrived from <a href="/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf" title="Düsseldorf">Düsseldorf</a>, Germany, seeking a refuge for the community which had been expelled from their monastery by the government policies of the <i><a href="/wiki/Kulturkampf" title="Kulturkampf">Kulturkampf</a></i>. They found a welcome in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Cleveland" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Cleveland">Diocese of Cleveland</a>, and in 1877 established a monastery in that city. At the urging of <a href="/wiki/Mother_Ignatius_Hayes" title="Mother Ignatius Hayes">Mary Ignatius Hayes</a> in 1875 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> had already authorized the sending of nuns to establish a monastery of Poor Clares of the Primitive Observance from San Damiano in Assisi. After the reluctance on the part of many bishops to accept them, due to their reliance upon donations for their maintenance, a community was finally established in <a href="/wiki/Omaha" class="mw-redirect" title="Omaha">Omaha</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nebraska" title="Nebraska">Nebraska</a>, in 1878.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Currently there are also monasteries in (among other places): <a href="/wiki/Alexandria,_Virginia" title="Alexandria, Virginia">Alexandria, Virginia</a> (P.C.C);<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Andover,_Massachusetts" title="Andover, Massachusetts">Andover, Massachusetts</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Belleville,_Illinois" title="Belleville, Illinois">Belleville, Illinois</a> (P.C.C.);<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Bordentown,_New_Jersey" title="Bordentown, New Jersey">Bordentown, New Jersey</a>; <a href="/wiki/Boston,_Massachusetts" class="mw-redirect" title="Boston, Massachusetts">Boston, Massachusetts</a>; <a href="/wiki/Brenham,_Texas" title="Brenham, Texas">Brenham, Texas</a>; <a href="/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois" class="mw-redirect" title="Chicago, Illinois">Chicago, Illinois</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Cincinnati,_Ohio" class="mw-redirect" title="Cincinnati, Ohio">Cincinnati, Ohio</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleveland, Ohio">Cleveland, Ohio</a> (O.S.C., P.C.C. and P.C.P.A.); <a href="/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana" title="Fort Wayne, Indiana">Fort Wayne, Indiana</a>; <a href="/wiki/Evansville,_Indiana" title="Evansville, Indiana">Evansville, Indiana</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Kokomo,_Indiana" title="Kokomo, Indiana">Kokomo, Indiana</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Los_Altos_Hills,_California" title="Los Altos Hills, California">Los Altos Hills, California</a>; <a href="/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee" title="Memphis, Tennessee">Memphis, Tennessee</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/New_Kent_County,_Virginia" title="New Kent County, Virginia">metropolitan Richmond, Virginia</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/New_Orleans" title="New Orleans">New Orleans</a>; <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="Philadelphia, Pennsylvania">Philadelphia, Pennsylvania</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Phoenix,_Arizona" title="Phoenix, Arizona">Phoenix, Arizona</a>; Rockford, Illinois (P.C.C.);<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Roswell,_New_Mexico" title="Roswell, New Mexico">Roswell, New Mexico</a> (P.C.C.);<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saginaw,_Michigan" title="Saginaw, Michigan">Saginaw, Michigan</a>; <h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Canada">Canada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Canada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>There are three monasteries of the Order in Canada: <a href="/wiki/St._Clare%27s_Monastery_(Duncan)" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Clare&#39;s Monastery (Duncan)">St. Clare's Monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Duncan,_British_Columbia" title="Duncan, British Columbia">Duncan, British Columbia</a>; and at <a href="/wiki/Mission,_British_Columbia" title="Mission, British Columbia">Mission, British Columbia</a>; and a <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French-speaking</a> community in <a href="/wiki/Valleyfield,_Quebec" class="mw-redirect" title="Valleyfield, Quebec">Valleyfield, Quebec</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>

<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Latin_America">Latin America</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Latin America">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente,_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/170px-Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="256" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/255px-Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/340px-Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1269" data-file-height="1909" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Madre_Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Fuente,_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Mother Jeronima of the Assumption, P.C.C., by <a href="/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez" title="Diego Velázquez">Diego Velázquez</a> (1620)</div></div></div>
<p>There have been monasteries of the Order in <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a> since colonial days. The Capuchin nuns alone number some 1,350 living in 73 different monasteries around the country.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A monastery was founded in <a href="/wiki/Huehuetenango" title="Huehuetenango">Huehuetenango</a>, <a href="/wiki/Guatemala" title="Guatemala">Guatemala</a>, by nuns from the community in <a href="/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee" title="Memphis, Tennessee">Memphis, Tennessee</a>, in November 1981, in the early days of a bloody <a href="/wiki/Civil_war" title="Civil war">civil war</a> which ravaged that country; as of 2011, it consisted of seven nuns; five Guatemalans and two <a href="/wiki/El_Salvador#Salvadoran_people" title="El Salvador">Salvadorans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Asia">Asia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Asia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Poor Clares were massacred at <a href="/wiki/Acre,_Israel" title="Acre, Israel">Acre</a> during the reconquest of <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a> after the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a>. They returned to <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares_of_Nazareth" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clares of Nazareth">Nazareth</a> in 1884 and Jerusalem in 1888. <a href="/wiki/Charles_de_Foucauld" title="Charles de Foucauld">St<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>Charles de Foucauld</a> served both communities between 1897 and 1900. These French Clarissians were expelled from the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> at the onset of <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>}I</a>; the communities were subsequently reestablished in 1949 amid the <a href="/wiki/Creation_of_Israel" class="mw-redirect" title="Creation of Israel">creation of Israel</a>.
</p><p>The Poor Clare in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> was led by <a href="/wiki/Jer%C3%B3nima_de_la_Asunci%C3%B3n" title="Jerónima de la Asunción">Jeronima of the Assumption</a> who was authorized by the King of Spain and the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of the Order of Friars Minor to go there to found a monastery. She was from <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo, Spain</a> and left Madrid in April 1620 in her 60s and arrived in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a> on 5 August 1621 with other 14 sisters. They are the first contemplative nuns who arrived in the Philippine archipelago to support the active works of evangelization of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a> working in the country through their life of contemplation, penance, poverty, and enclosure.
</p><p>Together with the Alcantarine Friars who came to the Philippines in 1578 and strive to live the ideals of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> in a very rigorous way, the Poor Clare sisters also professed the Rule and life of <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a>. They heightened their witnessing of the "privilege of poverty" of Clare by not having a permanent income but rather opened their gates of the Divine Providence through alms and the generosity of the people. 
</p><p>Their Monastery in <a href="/wiki/Intramuros" title="Intramuros">Intramuros</a> was severely devastated by an earthquake. However, through the efforts of the people around, it was rebuilt and has a larger space compared to the former monastery. During the war for independence in the year 1945, the monastery was destroyed again and the sisters were forced to evacuate the place. For the meantime, they were sheltered at the Minor Seminary of the Franciscans in San Francisco del Monte, Quezon City for 5 years. The present location of the Monastery is at Aurora Boulevard, C5, Katipunan, Quezon City. Because of the zeal for the contemplative life, the founder's cause is ongoing for beatification.
</p><p>Apart from the said monastery, it also expand its presence from the different parts of the country. The country is blessed with 27 monasteries: <a href="/wiki/Sariaya" title="Sariaya">Sariaya</a> Quezon (1957); <a href="/wiki/Calbayog" title="Calbayog">Calbayog</a>, Samar (1965); Betis and <a href="/wiki/Guagua" title="Guagua">Guagua</a>, Pampanga (1968); <a href="/wiki/Cabuyao" title="Cabuyao">Cabuyao</a>, Laguna and Tayud, <a href="/wiki/Cebu" title="Cebu">Cebu</a> (1975): <a href="/wiki/Maria,_Siquijor" title="Maria, Siquijor">Maria, Siquijor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Isabela,_Basilan" title="Isabela, Basilan">Isabela, Basilan</a> in (1986); Josefina, Zamboanga del Sur, (1989); <a href="/wiki/Kidapawan" title="Kidapawan">Kidapawan</a>, North Cotabato, <a href="/wiki/Balanga,_Bataan" title="Balanga, Bataan">Balanga</a> Bataan, <a href="/wiki/Lopez" class="mw-redirect" title="Lopez">Lopez</a> (Quezon Province), and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cabid-an&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cabid-an (page does not exist)">Cabid-an</a>, Sorsogon (1990); <a href="/w/index.php?title=Guibang,_Isabela&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guibang, Isabela (page does not exist)">Guibang, Isabela</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mondragon,_Northern_Samar" title="Mondragon, Northern Samar">Mondragon</a>, Northern Samar and <a href="/wiki/Naval,_Biliran" title="Naval, Biliran">Naval, Biliran</a> (1991); Iguig, Tuguegarao (1992); <a href="/wiki/Bolinao" title="Bolinao">Bolinao</a>, Pangasinan and <a href="/wiki/Cantilan" title="Cantilan">Cantilan</a>, Surigao del Sur (1993); <a href="/wiki/Boac,_Marinduque" title="Boac, Marinduque">Boac, Marinduque</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Polomoloc&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Polomoloc (page does not exist)">Polomoloc</a>, South Cotabato (1998); <a href="/wiki/Aritao" title="Aritao">Aritao</a>, Nueva Vizcaya (1999); Tabon-tabon, <a href="/wiki/Albay" title="Albay">Albay</a> and <a href="/wiki/San_Jose,_Antique" class="mw-redirect" title="San Jose, Antique">San Jose, Antique</a> (2004); <a href="/wiki/Borongan" title="Borongan">Borongan</a>, Eastern Samar and <a href="/wiki/Malasiqui" title="Malasiqui">Malasiqui</a> Pangasinan (2011) and <a href="/wiki/Tabuk,_Kalinga" title="Tabuk, Kalinga">Tabuk</a>, Kalinga (2017). The Poor Clare Monastery in Palawan province is founded by the Monastery from China.
</p><p>Furthermore, their expansion does not only limit in the Philippine archipelago but also helped the aging communities in <a href="/wiki/Tahiti" title="Tahiti">Tahiti</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, USA. They were able to found new monasteries in abroad such as in <a href="/wiki/Malaysia" title="Malaysia">Malaysia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea" title="Papua New Guinea">Papua New Guinea</a>, <a href="/wiki/Taiwan" title="Taiwan">Taiwan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hongkong" class="mw-redirect" title="Hongkong">Hongkong</a>. 
</p><p>There are also monastery from <a href="/wiki/Kiry%C5%AB,_Gunma" title="Kiryū, Gunma">Kiryū, Gunma</a>, <a href="/wiki/Japan" title="Japan">Japan</a>, which was founded from the monastery in Boston in 1965.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Connections_with_television">Connections with television</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poor_Clares&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Connections with television">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
