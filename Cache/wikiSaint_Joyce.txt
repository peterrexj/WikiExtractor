<p>Saint <b>Judoc</b>, otherwise known as <b>Jodoc</b>,  <b>Joyce</b> or <b>Josse</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Iudocus</i>; traditionally <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 600 – 668 AD)<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a seventh-century <a href="/wiki/Breton_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton people">Breton</a> noble. Though he was never officially <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>, Saint Judoc is considered to be a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Judoc was a son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Judicael" title="Saint Judicael">Juthael</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brittany" title="Duchy of Brittany">King of Brittany</a>. He renounced his wealth and position to become a priest and lived alone for the rest of his lifetime<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in the coastal forest near the mouth of the <a href="/wiki/Canche" title="Canche">River Canche</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Etymology">Etymology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Etymology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The name Judoc, meaning "Lord", is the 14th century Breton version of <i>Iudocus</i> in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <i>Josse</i> in French, <i>Jost,</i> <i>Joost,</i> or <i>Joos</i> in <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>, and <i>Joyce</i> in English. The name Judoc was rarely used after the 14th century except in the Netherlands.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, Judoc was the son of Juthael, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brittany" title="Duchy of Brittany">King of Brittany</a>, and the brother of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Judicael" title="Saint Judicael">Saint Judicael</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winnoc" title="Winnoc">Saint Winnoc</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In approximately 636, Judoc renounced his inheritance and wealth and embarked on a pilgrimage to Rome. He was ordained as a priest during this voyage and subsequently became a hermit in <a href="/wiki/Ponthieu" title="Ponthieu">Ponthieu</a>, Saint-Josse-sur-Mer, where he resided until his death. According to ancient <a href="/wiki/Folklore" title="Folklore">folklore</a>, his body was said to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorruptible</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> leaving his followers with the task of continually cutting his hair after death.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg/220px-Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="262" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg/330px-Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg/440px-Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1564" data-file-height="1860" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ravensburg_St_Christina_Hl_Jodokus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Judoc, as depicted in the Church of St. Christina in <a href="/wiki/Ravensburg" title="Ravensburg">Ravensburg</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Saint Judoc developed a local <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a></i>. Built in the eighth century at the place where Judoc's <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> was kept, the <a href="/wiki/Saint-Josse" title="Saint-Josse">Abbey of Saint-Josse</a> was a small <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> situated on the site of his retreat. In 903, some monks of the abbey fled <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman raiders</a> for England, where they bore Judoc's relics, which were enshrined in the newly built <a href="/wiki/New_Minster,_Winchester" title="New Minster, Winchester">New Minster</a> in <a href="/wiki/Winchester" title="Winchester">Winchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> To honor the event, feasts were held on the 9th of January.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> of Judoc spread from France through the <a href="/wiki/Low_Countries" title="Low Countries">Low Countries</a>, England, Germany, and Scandinavia. In these regions, variations of <i>Josse, Joyce, Joos, <a href="/wiki/Joost_(name)" title="Joost (name)">Joost</a></i>, and the diminutive <i>Jocelyn</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> became popular names for both men and women, and a number of chapels and churches were dedicated to him.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The <i>mal Saint-Josse</i> was the term for an illness resulting from a snakebite, against which the saint's name was invoked by the fifteenth-century French poet <a href="/wiki/Eustache_Deschamps" title="Eustache Deschamps">Eustache Deschamps</a> in an <a href="/wiki/Imprecatory_Psalms" title="Imprecatory Psalms">imprecatory</a> <a href="/wiki/Ballade_(forme_fixe)" title="Ballade (forme fixe)">ballade</a>:<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> "...Du mau saint Leu, de l'esvertin, Du saint Josse et saint Matelin... soit maistre Mahieu confondus!".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> gave the abbey to <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> who turned it into a hostel for those crossing the <a href="/wiki/English_Channel" title="English Channel">English Channel</a>. It later became a site of pilgrimage, especially popular with <a href="/wiki/Flemish" title="Flemish">Flemish</a> and Germans in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries.
</p><p><i>La vie de Saint Josse</i> was written in <a href="/wiki/Old_French" title="Old French">Old French</a> verses by the poet and translator Pierre de Beauvais in the thirteenth century.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Suaire_de_St-Josse" class="mw-redirect" title="Suaire de St-Josse">Suaire de St-Josse</a>, or "Shroud of Saint Judoc," is a rich, silk <a href="/wiki/Samite" title="Samite">samite</a> saddlecloth that was woven in northeastern <a href="/wiki/Iran" title="Iran">Iran</a> prior to 961. When Saint Judoc was reinterred in 1134, the shroud was used to wrap his bones.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Louvre" title="Louvre">Louvre Museum</a> currently houses his shroud.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> was closed in 1772, and subsequently sold and dismantled in 1789, leaving no traces of the buildings. The abbey church then became the parish church of the <a href="/wiki/French_commune" class="mw-redirect" title="French commune">French commune</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Josse" title="Saint-Josse">Saint-Josse</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_depictions">Cultural depictions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cultural depictions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cultural depictions usually portray Judoc holding the pilgrim's staff. He is also shown with a crown at his feet, referring to his renunciation of his lands and fortune. In Austria, there is a depiction of Judoc on the <a href="/wiki/Mausoleum" title="Mausoleum">mausoleum</a> of Maximilian in <a href="/wiki/Innsbruck" title="Innsbruck">Innsbruck</a>. Judoc was most famously mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Chaucer" class="mw-redirect" title="Chaucer">Chaucer</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Wife_of_Bath" class="mw-redirect" title="Wife of Bath">Wife of Bath</a>, who swears "by God and by Seint Joce [Saint Joyce]." This suggests that his name was often <a href="/wiki/Invocation" title="Invocation">invoked</a> in oaths.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
