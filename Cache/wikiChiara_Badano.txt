<p><b>Chiara Badano</b> (October 29, 1971 – October 7, 1990) was a young <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> teenager who is on the path to sainthood in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. At age nine she joined the <a href="/wiki/Focolare_Movement" title="Focolare Movement">Focolare Movement</a> and received the nickname "Luce" (light) by the founder <a href="/wiki/Chiara_Lubich" title="Chiara Lubich">Chiara Lubich</a>. When she was 16, she was diagnosed with <a href="/wiki/Osteogenic_sarcoma" class="mw-redirect" title="Osteogenic sarcoma">osteogenic sarcoma</a>, a painful bone cancer. Chiara succumbed to the cancer on October 7, 1990, after a two-year battle with the disease.<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on September 25, 2010, at the <a href="/wiki/Santuario_della_Madonna_del_Divino_Amore" title="Santuario della Madonna del Divino Amore">Sanctuary of Our Lady of Divine Love</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. Her feast day is celebrated on October 29.<sup id="cite_ref-Benedictin_XVI_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benedictin_XVI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chiara_Badano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chiara Badano was born on October 29, 1971, to Ruggero and Maria Teresa Badano in the small village of <a href="/wiki/Sassello" title="Sassello">Sassello</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. The couple waited and prayed eleven years to have Chiara. They considered her to be their greatest blessing. While Ruggero worked as a truck driver, Maria Teresa stayed at home to raise their daughter.
Chiara grew up with a strong and healthy relationship with her parents, but she did not always obey them and would occasionally have fights with them.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>One day, Chiara took an apple off a tree in a neighbour's orchard; her mother later recounted the event:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>One afternoon, Chiara came home with a beautiful red apple. I asked her where it came from. She replied that she had taken it from our neighbor's orchard without asking her permission. I explained to her that she always had to ask before taking anything and that she had to take it back and apologize to our neighbor. She was reluctant to do this because she was too embarrassed. I told her that it was far more important to own up than to eat an apple. So Chiara took the apple back to our neighbor and explained everything to her. That evening, the woman brought her a whole box of apples, saying that on that day Chiara had learnt something very important.<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Focolare_Movement">Focolare Movement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chiara_Badano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Focolare Movement">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chiara attended her first meeting of the <a href="/wiki/Focolare_Movement" title="Focolare Movement">Focolare Movement</a> in September 1980; she was only 9 years old. This group, especially its founder <a href="/wiki/Chiara_Lubich" title="Chiara Lubich">Chiara Lubich</a>, had a profound impact on Chiara's life. The group focused on the image of the forsaken Christ as a way to make it through difficult times.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Chiara later wrote that, "I discovered that Jesus forsaken is the key to unity with God, and I want to choose him as my only spouse. I want to be ready to welcome him when he comes. To prefer him above all else."<sup id="cite_ref-Ann_Ball_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ann_Ball-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While Chiara was a conscientious student, she struggled in school and even failed her first year of high school.<sup id="cite_ref-Ann_Ball_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ann_Ball-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was often teased in school for her strong beliefs and was given the nickname "Sister." Chiara made several good friends, however, often going out late to have coffee with them. She enjoyed the normal teenage pastimes such as listening to pop music, dancing, and singing. She was also an avid tennis player and enjoyed hiking and swimming.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the summer of 1988, when she was 16 years old, Chiara had a life-changing experience in Rome with the Focolare Movement. She wrote to her parents, "This is a very important moment for me: it is an encounter with Jesus Forsaken. It hasn't been easy to embrace this suffering, but this morning Chiara Lubich explained to the children that they have to be the spouse of Jesus Forsaken."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After this trip she started to correspond regularly with Lubich. She then asked for her new name as this was going to be the start of a new life for her. Lubich gave her the name Chiara Luce. This was a kind of a play on words, since in Italian "Chiara" is a common girl's name, taken for example from the name of <a href="/wiki/Clare_of_Assisi" title="Clare of Assisi">Clare of Assisi</a>, but it is also an everyday word meaning "clear." "Luce" is occasionally found as a girl's name in Italy, though it is mostly secular rather than religious; and it is also an everyday word meaning "light." So "Chiara Luce" means "clear light."<sup id="cite_ref-Ann_Ball_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ann_Ball-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Lubich wrote to Chiara,"Your luminous face shows your love for Jesus," which is why she gave her the name Luce.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Illness">Illness</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chiara_Badano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Illness">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the summer of 1988, Chiara felt a sting of pain in her shoulder while playing tennis. At first she thought nothing of it, but when the pain continued to be present she underwent a series of tests. The doctors then discovered she had a rare and painful form of <a href="/wiki/Bone_cancer" class="mw-redirect" title="Bone cancer">bone cancer</a>, <a href="/wiki/Osteogenic_sarcoma" class="mw-redirect" title="Osteogenic sarcoma">osteogenic sarcoma</a>. In response, Chiara simply declared, "It's for you, Jesus; if you want it, I want it, too."<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Throughout the treatment process, Chira refused to take any morphine so she could stay aware. She felt it was important to know her illness and pain so she could offer up her sufferings. She said, "It reduces my lucidity and there's only one thing I can do now: to offer my suffering to Jesus because I want to share as much as possible in his sufferings on the cross."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During her stays in the hospital, she would take the time to go on walks with another patient who was struggling with depression. These walks were beneficial to the other patient but caused Chiara great pain. Her parents often encouraged her to stay and rest but she would simply reply, "I'll be able to sleep later on."<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of her doctors, Antonio Delogu, said, "Through her smile, and through her eyes full of light, she showed us that death doesn't exist; only life exists."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A friend from the Focolare Movement said, "At first we thought we'd visit her to keep her spirits up, but very soon we understood that, in fact, we were the ones who needed her. Her life was like a magnet drawing us to her."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chiara kept her spirits up, even when the harsh chemotherapy caused her hair to fall out. When a lock of her hair would fall, Chiara would simply offer it to God, saying, "For you, Jesus."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She also donated all her savings to a friend who was performing mission work in Africa. She wrote to him, "I don't need this money any more. I have everything."<sup id="cite_ref-Ann_Ball_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ann_Ball-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To help prepare her parents for life after she died, Chiara made them dinner reservations for Valentine's Day after they refused to leave her bedside and ordered them to not return until after midnight. At Christmas she wrote, "Holy Christmas 1990. Thank you for everything. Happy New Year," on a Christmas card and hid it among some blank ones for her mother to find later.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While undergoing a painful medical procedure, Chiara was visited by a lady. As she described the experience: "When the doctors began to carry out this small, but quite demanding, procedure, a lady with a very beautiful and luminous smile came in. She came up to me and took me by the hand, and her touch filled me with courage. In the same way that she arrived, she disappeared, and I could no longer see her. But my heart was filled with an immense joy and all fear left me. In that moment I understood that if we're always ready for everything, God sends us many signs of his love."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chiara's faith and spirit never dwindled even after the cancer left her unable to walk and a CAT scan showed that any hope of remission was gone. In response, she simply said, "If I had to choose between walking again and going to heaven, I wouldn't hesitate. I would choose heaven."<sup id="cite_ref-Ann_Ball_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ann_Ball-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On July 19, 1989, Chiara almost died of a hemorrhage. Her faith did not falter as she said, "Don't shed any tears for me. I'm going to Jesus. At my funeral, I don't want people crying, but singing with all their hearts."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cardinal Saldarini, Archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a>, Italy, heard about Chiara's illness and visited her at the hospital. He asked her, "The light in your eyes is splendid. Where does it come from?" Chiara simply replied, "I try to love Jesus as much as I can."<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Chiara realized she was not going to get better, she started to plan for her "wedding" (her funeral) with her mother. She chose the music, songs, flowers, and the readings for mass.<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She wanted to be buried in her "wedding dress," a white dress with a pink waist, because her death would allow her to become the bride of Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She told her mother, "When you're getting me ready, Mum, you have to keep saying to yourself, 'Chiara Luce is now seeing Jesus.<span style="padding-right:.15em;">'</span>"<sup id="cite_ref-Chiara_Badano_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Chiara_Badano-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Before she died, she told her mother, "Oh, Mama, young people…young people…they are the future. You see, I can't run anymore, but how I would like to pass on to them the torch, like in the Olympics! Young people have only one life and it's worthwhile to spend it well."<sup id="cite_ref-Ann_Ball_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ann_Ball-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chiara_Badano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During her final hours, Chiara made her final confession and received the Eucharist. She had her family and friends pray with her, "Come, Holy Spirit." She died at 4 am on October 7, 1990, with her parents at her bedside. Her final words were, "Bye, Mum, be happy, because I am."<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Two thousand people attended her funeral; the mayor of Sassello shut down the town so people would be able to attend.<sup id="cite_ref-Colleen_Swain_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Colleen_Swain-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Chiara_Badano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chiara's cause for sainthood was promoted by <a href="/wiki/Livio_Maritano" title="Livio Maritano">Livio Maritano</a>, the former Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Acqui_Terme" title="Acqui Terme">Acqui Terme</a>, Italy, beginning in 1999. It was through this process that she was declared a "<a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>" on July 3, 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-Press_Release_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Press_Release-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In December 2009, <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XVI">Benedict XVI</a> acknowledged the miracle of a young Italian boy whose parents interceded to Chiara to heal him from meningitis that was destroying his organs. His doctors could not medically explain his sudden healing.<sup id="cite_ref-Christine_Kelly_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Christine_Kelly-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chiara Badano was declared a "Blessed" in the Catholic Church on September 25, 2010, at the <a href="/wiki/Santuario_della_Madonna_del_Divino_Amore" title="Santuario della Madonna del Divino Amore">Sanctuary of Our Lady of Divine Love</a> in Rome. Thousands of people came for the event. Archbishop Angelo Amato, head of the Vatican <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a>, said that Chiara was a great example of how the short life of the young could be lived out in great holiness and "today there are virtuous people, who in family, at school, in society, do not fritter away their lives."<sup id="cite_ref-Benedictin_XVI_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benedictin_XVI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is celebrated on October 29.<sup id="cite_ref-Benedictin_XVI_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benedictin_XVI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
