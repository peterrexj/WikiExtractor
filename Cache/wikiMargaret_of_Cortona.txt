<p><b>Margaret of Cortona</b> (1247 – 22 February 1297) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Penitent_order" title="Penitent order">penitent</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a>. She was born in <a href="/wiki/Castiglione_del_Lago" title="Castiglione del Lago">Laviano</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, and died in <a href="/wiki/Cortona" title="Cortona">Cortona</a>. She was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1728.
</p><p>She is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the falsely accused, <a href="/wiki/Hobo" title="Hobo">hoboes</a>, <a href="/wiki/Homeless" class="mw-redirect" title="Homeless">homeless</a>, <a href="/wiki/Insane" class="mw-redirect" title="Insane">insane</a>, <a href="/wiki/Orphaned" class="mw-redirect" title="Orphaned">orphaned</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mentally_ill" class="mw-redirect" title="Mentally ill">mentally ill</a>, <a href="/wiki/Midwives" class="mw-redirect" title="Midwives">midwives</a>, <a href="/wiki/Penitent" class="mw-redirect" title="Penitent">penitents</a>, single mothers, reformed <a href="/wiki/Prostitute" class="mw-redirect" title="Prostitute">prostitutes</a>, stepchildren, and tramps.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Cortona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret was born of farming parents, in Laviano, a little town in the diocese of Chiusi.<sup id="cite_ref-goodier_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goodier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At the age of seven, Margaret's mother died and her father remarried. Neither Margaret nor her stepmother liked each other.<sup id="cite_ref-hess_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hess-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As she grew older, Margaret became more willful and reckless, and her reputation in the town suffered.<sup id="cite_ref-goodier_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goodier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At the age of 17 she met a young man, according to some accounts, the son of Gugliemo di Pecora, lord of Valiano, and she ran away with him. Soon Margaret found herself installed in the <a href="/wiki/Castle" title="Castle">castle</a>, not as her master's <a href="/wiki/Wife" title="Wife">wife</a>, for convention would never allow that, but as his <a href="/wiki/Mistress_(lover)" title="Mistress (lover)">mistress</a>, which was more easily condoned.<sup id="cite_ref-goodier_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goodier-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> For ten years, she lived with him near <a href="/wiki/Montepulciano" title="Montepulciano">Montepulciano</a> and bore him a son.
</p><p>When her lover failed to return home from a journey one day, Margaret became concerned. The unaccompanied return of his favorite hound alarmed Margaret, and the hound led her into the forest to his murdered body. That crime shocked Margaret into a life of prayer and penance.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Margaret returned to his family all the gifts he had given her and left his home. With her child, she returned to her father's house, but her stepmother would not have her. Margaret and her son then went to the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> friars at <a href="/wiki/Cortona" title="Cortona">Cortona</a>, where her son eventually became a <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>. She fasted, avoided meat, and subsisted on bread and vegetables.
</p><p>In 1277, after three years of probation, Margaret joined the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> and chose to live in poverty. Following the example of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, she begged for sustenance and <a href="/wiki/Bread" title="Bread">bread</a>. She pursued a life of prayer and penance at Cortona, and there established a <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">hospital</a> for the sick, homeless and impoverished. To secure nurses for the hospital, she instituted a congregation of <a href="/wiki/Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order">Tertiary Sisters</a>, known as "<i>le poverelle</i>" (Italian for "the little poor ones").
</p><p>While in prayer, Margaret recounted hearing the words, "What is your wish, <i>poverella?</i>" ("little poor one?"), and she replied, "I neither seek nor wish for anything but You, my Lord Jesus."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> She also established an order devoted to <a href="/wiki/Virgin_of_Mercy" title="Virgin of Mercy">Our Lady of Mercy</a> and the members bound themselves to support the hospital and to help the needy.
</p><p>On several occasions, Margaret participated in public affairs. Twice, claiming divine command, she challenged the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Arezzo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Arezzo">Bishop of Arezzo</a>, Guglielmo Ubertini Pazzi, in whose diocese Cortona lay, because he lived and warred like a <a href="/wiki/Prince" title="Prince">prince</a>. She moved to the ruined church of <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Santa_Margherita,_Cortona" title="Santa Margherita, Cortona">Santa Margherita</a>, and spent her remaining years there; she died on 22 February 1297.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Cortona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After her death, the Church of Santa Margherita in Cortona was rebuilt in her honor. Her body, found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a> even after 400 years, is preserved in a silver casket inside the church. Margaret was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> on 16 May 1728.
</p><p>Margaret of Cortona is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">honored</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Feasts_and_Fasts" class="mw-redirect" title="Lesser Feasts and Fasts">Lesser Feast</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">liturgical calendar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church in the United States of America</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> on <a href="/wiki/February_22" title="February 22">22 February</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_of_Cortona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Paintings depicting Margaret have been completed by <a href="/wiki/Ecstasy_of_St_Margaret_of_Cortona_(Giovanni_Lanfranco)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecstasy of St Margaret of Cortona (Giovanni Lanfranco)">Giovanni Lanfranco</a> (1622) and <a href="/wiki/St_Margaret_of_Cortona_(Gaspare_Traversi)" title="St Margaret of Cortona (Gaspare Traversi)">Gaspare Traversi</a> (c. 1758).
</p><p>In 1938, the Italian composer <a href="/wiki/Licinio_Refice" title="Licinio Refice">Licinio Refice</a> wrote his second opera, <i>Margherita da Cortona</i> based on the life of the Margaret, with libretto by <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emidio_Mucci" class="extiw" title="it:Emidio Mucci">Emidio Mucci</a>.
</p><p>A 1950 biographical film, <i><a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Cortona_(film)" title="Margaret of Cortona (film)">Margaret of Cortona</a></i>, by <a href="/wiki/Mario_Bonnard" title="Mario Bonnard">Mario Bonnard</a> featured <a href="/wiki/Maria_Frau" title="Maria Frau">Maria Frau</a> as Margaret.
</p>
