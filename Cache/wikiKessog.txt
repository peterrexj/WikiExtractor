<p><b>Saint Kessog</b> was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> of the mid-sixth century active in the <a href="/wiki/Lennox_(district)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lennox (district)">Lennox</a> area and southern <a href="/wiki/Perthshire" title="Perthshire">Perthshire</a>. Son of the king of <a href="/wiki/Cashel,_County_Tipperary" title="Cashel, County Tipperary">Cashel</a> in Ireland, Kessog is said to have worked <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a>, even as a child. He left Ireland and became a missionary bishop in Scotland. Using <a href="/wiki/Inchtavannach" title="Inchtavannach">Monks' Island</a> in <a href="/wiki/Loch_Lomond" title="Loch Lomond">Loch Lomond</a> as his headquarters, he evangelized the surrounding area until he was martyred, supposedly at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bandry&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bandry (page does not exist)">Bandry</a>, where a heap of stones was known as <i>St Kessog's Cairn</i>. Kessog was killed in 520 AD.
</p><p>The St Kessog's church in <a href="/wiki/Luss" title="Luss">Luss</a> on the banks of Loch Lomond is named after Kessog and the church contains an effigy of the saint. Kessog is claimed to have brought Christianity to the area around Luss in 510 AD and 1500 years of continuous Christian presence in the area was celebrated in 2010.
</p><p>Elsewhere in Scotland, the medieval parish churches of <a href="/wiki/Auchterarder" title="Auchterarder">Auchterarder</a> and <a href="/wiki/Comrie,_Perth_and_Kinross" title="Comrie, Perth and Kinross">Comrie</a>, both in <a href="/wiki/Perthshire" title="Perthshire">Perthshire</a>, were dedicated to Kessog, and may have been founded by him or one of his followers. A 19th century church in <a href="/wiki/Callander" title="Callander">Callander</a> is named St Kessog's and a nearby circular mound by the <a href="/wiki/River_Teith" title="River Teith">River Teith</a> is named in pseudo-Gaelic as "Tom na Chessaig", meaning "the Hill of Kessog".
</p><p>The Kessock area of <a href="/wiki/Inverness" title="Inverness">Inverness</a> is named after the saint, as is the Kessog oil field in the <a href="/wiki/North_Sea_oil" title="North Sea oil">North Sea</a>.
</p><p>Soldiers had a special veneration for him and he's portrayed in military dress with arrows and then bended bow. As late as 1695 his bell, a sacred relic, was listed among the funeral investitures of the Earldom of Perth.
</p>
