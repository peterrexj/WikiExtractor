<p><b>Giuseppe Marello</b> (known as <b>Joseph Marello</b> in English; 26 December 1844 – 30 May 1895) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> prelate who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Acqui" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Acqui">Bishop of Acqui</a> from 1889 until his death and was also the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Oblates_of_Saint_Joseph" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblates of Saint Joseph">Oblates of Saint Joseph</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Marello served as an aide to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Asti" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Asti">Bishop of Asti</a> prior to his episcopal appointment after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> named him to head the Acqui diocese; the pope had known Marello while a cardinal when the pair participated in the <a href="/wiki/First_Vatican_Council" title="First Vatican Council">First Vatican Council</a> more than a decade before.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He became a proponent for the poor and destitute and never stopped rendering his assistance to those who needed it the most; this was something he undertook even in his childhood. Bishop Marello issued several <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_letters" class="mw-redirect" title="Pastoral letters">pastoral letters</a> that dealt with a range of issues such as <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> and organized one big pastoral visitation to visit all parishes in his diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marello's cause for sainthood resulted in his beatification on 26 September 1993 in <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a> and his canonization less than a decade later on 25 November 2001 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Marello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_priesthood">Childhood and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Marello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Giuseppe Marello was born on 26 December 1844  on Bakers' Street in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> to Vincenzo Marello and Anna Maria Viale, and he was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> just hours later in the Corpus Domini church.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had a younger brother named Vittorio. His <a href="/wiki/Godparents" class="mw-redirect" title="Godparents">godparents</a> were Chiaffredo Viale and Teresa Secco. He received his Confirmation on 15 August 1855 from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Asti" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Asti">Bishop of Asti</a> Filippo Artico. He was an <a href="/wiki/Altar_server" title="Altar server">altar server</a> in his childhood, and he often invited the homeless to his home for food.<sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His father was a friend of <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Benedetto_Cottolengo" title="Giuseppe Benedetto Cottolengo">Giuseppe Benedetto Cottolengo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marello's mother died on 5 April 1848, and in 1852 his father decided to relocate with his children to <a href="/wiki/San_Martino_Alfieri" title="San Martino Alfieri">San Martino Alfieri</a>, where his paternal grandparents resided.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Marello began his studies for the priesthood on 31 October 1856, but his father wished him to continue with his education and take up a career in business. But in December 1863 Giuseppe contracted <a href="/wiki/Typhus" title="Typhus">typhus</a> and promised the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary_(Roman_Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary (Roman Catholic)">Virgin Mary</a> that if he survived he would continue his studies to become a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He recovered, and attributed the cure to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Consolation" title="Our Lady of Consolation">Our Lady of Consolation</a>. He continued on with his ecclesial studies in February 1864. At one stage he considered becoming a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> monk, but the new Bishop of Asti, Carlo Savio, dissuaded him, suggesting that God had other plans for him.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Marello underwent the clerical investiture on 9 January 1864, and received both the <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> and all <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a> from Bishop Savio on 21 December 1867. He was made a subdeacon on 28 March 1868, and was elevated to the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> on 6 June 1868. Marello was ordained to the priesthood on 19 September 1868 with Savio again officiating. The new priest celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> on 20 September 1868 in San Martino Alfieri.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his ordination, he became the private assistant to Bishop Savio at <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a> on 21 October 1868. He served in that capacity until 1881. On 2 March 1880 he was named a Canon of the <a href="/wiki/Asti_Cathedral" title="Asti Cathedral">Asti Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was also friends with <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Bosco" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni Bosco">Giovanni Bosco</a> and <a href="/wiki/Leonardo_Murialdo" title="Leonardo Murialdo">Leonardo Murialdo</a>. He attended the <a href="/wiki/First_Vatican_Council" title="First Vatican Council">First Vatican Council</a> with Bishop Savio and it was there that he met Cardinal Gioacchino Pecci – the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> – who praised the priest for his virtues and talents.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He and Savio went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 21 November 1869 and were there until late July 1870; he even had the chance to meet <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>. His father died on 17 May 1873.<sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Later he took over an Asti <a href="/wiki/Retirement_home" title="Retirement home">retirement home</a> to save it from being bankrupt and he soon became the spiritual director and catechist in his local diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 14 March 1878 he founded the <a href="/wiki/Oblates_of_Saint_Joseph" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblates of Saint Joseph">Oblates of Saint Joseph</a> which would be dedicated to caring for the poor and educating children and adolescents as well as rendering assisting bishops in whatever capacities were required.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Marello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pope Leo XIII appointed him <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Acqui" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Acqui">Bishop of Acqui</a> on 11 February 1889. The formal appointment as a bishop came on 11 February 1889 during a consistory for the elevation of new cardinals after having received word of his appointment in November 1888 (in the late morning) since the pope wanted to invest new bishops with the <a href="/wiki/Rochet" title="Rochet">rochet</a> at the consistory. From 5:30 to 6:15pm on 10 February he met with the pope and that evening had dinner with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Luigi_Oreglia_di_Santo_Stefano" title="Luigi Oreglia di Santo Stefano">Luigi Oreglia di Santo Stefano</a>. He received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> on 17 February, from Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Raffaele_Monaco_La_Valletta" title="Raffaele Monaco La Valletta">Raffaele Monaco La Valletta</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Concezione_dei_Cappuccini" title="Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini">Santa Maria della Concezione dei Cappuccini</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The co-consecrators were the Archbishop of Chieti Rocco Cocchia and the Archbishop of Damiata Ignazio Persico.
</p><p>He visited all the parishes in his diocese and wrote six <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_letters" class="mw-redirect" title="Pastoral letters">pastoral letters</a> to his flock.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He issued his first pastoral letter on the subject of peace on 31 May 1889 and issued another on 2 February 1890 regarding a pastoral visitation that he would undertake. From 13 April 1890 to 1895 he visited 143 individual parishes.<sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On 16 December 1890 he received an <a href="/wiki/Honorary_degree" title="Honorary degree">honorary degree</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sacred_theology" title="Sacred theology">sacred theology</a> from the Theological College of Saint Thomas in <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>, and then issued a third pastoral letter regarding <a href="/wiki/Penance,_Sacrament_of" class="mw-redirect" title="Penance, Sacrament of">penance</a> on 13 January 1891. On 26 September 1891 he participated in a pilgrimage to Rome for the third centennial of the death of <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a> and later on 4 February 1892 issued another pastoral letter on the subject of Christian education. Bishop Marello issued another one on 25 January 1893 regarding the profession of faith. From 14 to 28 February 1893 he went to Rome to celebrate the 50th anniversary of Pope Leo XIII's episcopal consecration though on 23 February made a detour to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> to visit the <a href="/wiki/Shrine_of_the_Virgin_of_the_Rosary_of_Pompei" title="Shrine of the Virgin of the Rosary of Pompei">Pompeii shrine</a>. He issued another pastoral letter on catechism on 20 January 1894 and a final one on 8 February 1895 regarding missions and the propagation of the faith.<sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Marello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Marello travelled to <a href="/wiki/Savona" title="Savona">Savona</a>, arriving on 26 May, to participate in a celebration of the third centennial of <a href="/wiki/Philip_Neri" title="Philip Neri">Philip Neri</a>. He died of a <a href="/wiki/Cerebral_hemorrhage" class="mw-redirect" title="Cerebral hemorrhage">cerebral hemorrhage</a> on 30 May 1895.  
</p><p>Marello was due to leave a week after the centennial celebration, but wanted to offer his respects to Bishop Giuseppe Boraggini of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Savona-Noli" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Savona-Noli">diocese of Savona</a> (1879–1897), who was not there, and so Marello decided to stay longer in the town until Boraggini's return; this was on 27 May 1895, after he celebrated what would be his final Mass. Marello first went on a Marian pilgrimage in the town and then called on the bishop that evening; he was exhausted but accepted a dinner invitation.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> But as the bishop showed him the room where <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a> was once confined in he fainted and was rushed to a bed. The illness did not seem at all serious, so his aide telegrammed the Acqui diocese vicar-general to give word the illness would keep the bishop out of his see for a little while. But his acute headaches caused even the slightest noise to torment him.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On 28 May his two doctors thought they saw an improvement that indicated he might soon be able to leave, but the bishop's condition deteriorated on 29 May, and grew worse around 4:30pm on 30 May, when he managed with difficulty to utter a few garbled and incoherent words.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The cerebral hemorrhage claimed his life at 6:00pm on 30 May. His funeral was celebrated on 1 June. Leo XIII said of his death at a general audience in Rome in 1891: "he was a gem among bishops".<sup id="cite_ref-LC_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His remains were exhumed and relocated to his order's motherhouse on 30 June 1923. His order received diocesan approval on 18 March 1901 and papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> on 11 April 1909.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Parish">Parish</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Marello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Parish">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first parish church named after Giuseppe Marello was dedicated in <a href="/wiki/Granite_Bay" class="mw-redirect" title="Granite Bay">Granite Bay</a>, California, in the United States of America on 22 October 2011 at a celebration presided over by Bishop Jaime Soto along with the parish pastor Arnold Ortiz, .
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Marello&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The informative process for beatification opened in the Acqui diocese on 23 November 1924 and it concluded in April 1928 while another informative process was opened in Asti from 22 December 1924 and concluded in April 1928 much like the Acqui process; his spiritual writings and other collections received theological approval on 12 May 1937 and a supplementary process was held in Acqui from 1941 to 1942. The formal introduction to the cause came under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on 28 May 1948 and Marello became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. An apostolic process was later held from 10 October 1948 to 21 April 1951 while the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> validated all previous processes in Rome on 17 March 1954. The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> (C.C.S.) and their consultants met and approved the cause on 25 October 1977. The confirmation of Marello's model life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> to title the late bishop as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 12 May 1978.
</p><p>For Marello to be beatified one miracle needed to be approved – a healing that science could not explain. One such case was investigated in the diocese of its origin and it later received C.C.S. validation prior to a panel of medical experts approving it on 17 December 1992. Theologians concurred with the verdict on 19 February 1993 as did the C.C.S. themselves on 16 March 1993. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved the miracle on 2 April 1993 and presided over Marello's beatification in Asti on 26 September 1993. The miracle in question was the 1944 cure of the <a href="/wiki/Seminarian" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminarian">seminarian</a> Aldo Falconetti who suffered from <a href="/wiki/Tubercular_meningitis" class="mw-redirect" title="Tubercular meningitis">tubercular meningitis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OJ_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OJ-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The second miracle that was investigated took place in <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a> and was the simultaneous healing of the children Alfredo and Isilia Chávez León who were both cured from <a href="/wiki/Bronchopneumonia" title="Bronchopneumonia">bronchopneumonia</a>. The C.C.S. validated the diocesan process on 12 November 1999 and a medical panel of experts approved it on 13 April 2000; theologians also approved it on 3 July 2000 as did the C.C.S. on 20 November 2000. John Paul II issued his final approval to this miracle on 18 December 2000 and in a 13 March 2001 consistory scheduled the date. John Paul II canonized Marello in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 25 November 2001.
</p>
