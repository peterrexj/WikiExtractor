<p><b>Osgyth</b> (or <b>Osyth</b>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 700 AD) was a Mercian noblewoman and prioress, venerated as an English <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> since the 8th century, from soon after her death. She is primarily commemorated in the village of <a href="/wiki/St_Osyth" title="St Osyth">St Osyth</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Essex" title="Essex">Essex</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Colchester" title="Colchester">Colchester</a>. Alternative spellings of her name include <b>Sythe</b>, <b>Othith</b> and <b>Ositha</b>. Born of a noble family, she became a nun and founded a <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> near <a href="/wiki/Chich" class="mw-redirect" title="Chich">Chich</a> which was later named after her.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osgyth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Quarrendon,_Buckinghamshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Quarrendon, Buckinghamshire">Quarrendon, Buckinghamshire</a> (at that time part of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>), she was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Frithuwold_of_Surrey" class="mw-redirect" title="Frithuwold of Surrey">Frithuwald</a>, a sub-king of Mercia in Surrey.<sup id="cite_ref-Wragg_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wragg-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her mother was Wilburh, of the royal house of Mercia.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her parents, with St.&#160;Erconwald, founded <a href="/wiki/Chertsey_Abbey" title="Chertsey Abbey">Chertsey Abbey</a> in 675&#160;AD.
</p><p>Raised in the care of her maternal aunts, <a href="/wiki/St_Edith_of_Aylesbury" class="mw-redirect" title="St Edith of Aylesbury">St Edith of Aylesbury</a> and <a href="/wiki/Edburga_of_Bicester" class="mw-redirect" title="Edburga of Bicester">Edburga of Bicester</a>, her ambition was to become an <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>, but she was too important as a political pawn to be set aside.<sup id="cite_ref-priory_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-priory-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was forced by her father into a <a href="/wiki/Dynastic_marriage" class="mw-redirect" title="Dynastic marriage">dynastic marriage</a> with <a href="/wiki/Sighere_of_Essex" title="Sighere of Essex">Sighere</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Essex" title="Kingdom of Essex">King of Essex</a>. She is likely the mother of <a href="/wiki/Offa_of_Essex" title="Offa of Essex">Offa of Essex</a>, although this is not certain.<sup id="cite_ref-Wragg_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wragg-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While her husband was off on a long hunt to run down a beautiful white stag, Osgyth persuaded two local bishops to accept her vows as a nun. Upon his return some days later, he reluctantly agreed to her decision and granted her some land at <a href="/wiki/Chich" class="mw-redirect" title="Chich">Chich</a> near Colchester where she established a convent,<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and ruled as first abbess. She was beheaded by some raiding pirates, perhaps because she may have resisted being carried off.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osgyth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>One day, St. Edith sent Osgyth to deliver a book to St. Modwenna of Northumbria at her nunnery. To get there, Osgyth had to cross a stream by a bridge. The stream swollen, the wind high, she fell into the water and drowned. Her absence was not noted for two days. Edith thought she was safe with Modwenna who was not expecting her visit. On the third day, Edith, wondering that her pupil had not returned, went to Modwenna. The abbesses were greatly concerned when they discovered Osgyth was apparently lost. They searched for her and found the child lying near the banks of the stream. The abbesses prayed for her restoration, and commanded her to arise from the water and come to them. This she did.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A similar tale is found in Irish hagiography.
</p><p>Her later death was accounted a <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> by some, but <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> makes no mention of Saint Osgyth. The 13th-century chronicler <a href="/wiki/Matthew_Paris" title="Matthew Paris">Matthew Paris</a> repeats some of the legend that had accrued around her name. The site of her martyrdom became transferred to the holy spring at <a href="/wiki/Quarrendon_(village)" class="mw-redirect" title="Quarrendon (village)">Quarrendon</a>. The holy spring at Quarrendon, mentioned in the time of Osgyth's aunts, now became associated with her legend, in which Osgyth stood up after her execution, picking up her head like <a href="/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Denis of Paris">Saint Denis</a> in Paris, and other <a href="/wiki/Cephalophore" title="Cephalophore">cephalophoric martyrs</a> and walking with it in her hands, to the door of a local convent, before collapsing there. Some modern authors link the legends of cephalophores miraculously walking with their heads in their hands<sup id="cite_ref-White_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> to the <a href="/wiki/Celtic_polytheism" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic polytheism">Celtic</a> cult of heads.<sup id="cite_ref-White_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-White-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 123">&#58;&#8202;123&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg/220px-StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg/330px-StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg/440px-StOsyth%27sPrioryGatehouse.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2304" /></a><figcaption>Gatehouse of the former <a href="/wiki/St_Osyth%27s_Priory" title="St Osyth&#39;s Priory">St Osyth's Priory</a> (later abbey), <a href="/wiki/St_Osyth" title="St Osyth">St Osyth</a>, Essex</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osgyth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Her cult was promoted by <a href="/wiki/Maurice_(bishop_of_London)" title="Maurice (bishop of London)">Maurice</a>, bishop of London, where there was a shrine dedicated to her at St. Paul's Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1121, his successor, <a href="/wiki/Richard_de_Belmeis_I" title="Richard de Belmeis I">Richard de Belmeis I</a> founded <a href="/wiki/St_Osyth%27s_Priory" title="St Osyth&#39;s Priory">a priory</a> for <a href="/wiki/Augustinian_Canons" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustinian Canons">canons of Saint Augustine</a>, on the site of a former nunnery at Chich.<sup id="cite_ref-priory_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-priory-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He obtained the relic of an arm for the monastery church. His remains were buried in the chancel of the church in 1127: he bequeathed the church and tithes to the canons, who elected as their first abbot or prior <a href="/wiki/William_de_Corbeil" title="William de Corbeil">William de Corbeil</a>, afterwards <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> (died in 1136). Corbeil acquired the other arm for Canterbury.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>Benefactions, charters, and privileges granted by <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II</a>, made the Canons wealthy: at the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries" title="Dissolution of the monasteries">Dissolution of the monasteries</a> in 1536, priory revenues were valued at £758 5s. 8d. yearly. In 1397 the abbot of St Osgyth was granted the right to wear a <a href="/wiki/Mitre" title="Mitre">mitre</a> and give the solemn benediction, and, more singularly, the right to ordain priests, conferred by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The gatehouse, the so-called 'Abbot's Tower', and some ranges of buildings remain.
</p><p>Osgyth's burial site at <a href="/wiki/St._Mary_the_Virgin,_Aylesbury" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mary the Virgin, Aylesbury">St. Mary the Virgin, Aylesbury</a> became a site of great, though unauthorised,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag needs further explanation. (June 2023)">further explanation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>; following a papal decree in 1500, the bones were removed from the church and buried in secret. Undeterred, according to the curious 17th-century antiquary <a href="/wiki/John_Aubrey" title="John Aubrey">John Aubrey</a> (author of the <i>Brief Lives</i>), "in those days, when they went to bed they did rake up the fire, and make a X on the ashes, and pray to God and Saint Sythe [Saint Osgyth] to deliver them from fire, and from water, and from all misadventure." A house in Aylesbury is still called St Osyth's in her honour.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>Veneration of St Osyth was widespread across the England. Norwich Cathedral and St Albans Abbey had chapels to dedicated to her in the Middle Ages.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 7 October. She is often depicted <a href="/wiki/Cephalophore" title="Cephalophore">carrying her own head</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-drake_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-drake-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
