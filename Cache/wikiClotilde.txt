<p><b>Clotilde</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 474–545), also known as <b>Clothilde</b>, <b>Clotilda</b>, <b>Clotild</b>, <b>Rotilde</b> etc. (Latin: <b>Chrodechildis</b>, <b>Chlodechildis</b> from Frankish <i>*Hrōþihildi</i> or perhaps <i>*Hlōdihildi</i>, both "famous in battle"), was a <a href="/wiki/List_of_Frankish_queens" title="List of Frankish queens">Queen of All</a> <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">the Franks</a>. She was supposedly descended from the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Gothic</a> king <a href="/wiki/Athanaric" title="Athanaric">Athanaric</a> and became the second wife of the Frankish king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis I</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#160;481–509</span>) in 493.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian dynasty</a> to which her husband belonged ruled <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Frankish kingdoms</a> for over 200 years<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (450–758).
</p><p>Venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> as well as by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, she played a role in her husband's famous conversion to Christianity and, in her later years, became known for her <a href="/wiki/Almsgiving" class="mw-redirect" title="Almsgiving">almsgiving</a> and penitential works of mercy.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She is credited with spreading <a href="/wiki/Christianity_in_Europe" title="Christianity in Europe">Christianity</a> within <a href="/wiki/Western_Europe" title="Western Europe">western Europe</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clotilde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:232px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sainte_clothilde.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Sainte_clothilde.jpg/230px-Sainte_clothilde.jpg" decoding="async" width="230" height="231" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Sainte_clothilde.jpg/345px-Sainte_clothilde.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Sainte_clothilde.jpg/460px-Sainte_clothilde.jpg 2x" data-file-width="952" data-file-height="955" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sainte_clothilde.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Clotilde at prayer (illuminated initial)</div></div></div>
<p>Clotilde was born at the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burgundy" title="Kingdom of Burgundy">Burgundian</a> court of <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>, the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Chilperic_II_of_Burgundy" title="Chilperic II of Burgundy">Chilperic II of Burgundy</a>. Upon the death of Chilperic's father King <a href="/wiki/Gondioc" title="Gondioc">Gondioc</a> in 473, Chilperic and his brothers <a href="/wiki/Gundobad" title="Gundobad">Gundobad</a> and <a href="/wiki/Godegisel" title="Godegisel">Godegisel</a> divided their inheritance; Chilperic II apparently reigning at Lyon, Gundobad at <a href="/wiki/Vienne,_Is%C3%A8re" title="Vienne, Isère">Vienne</a>, and Godegesil at <a href="/wiki/Geneva" title="Geneva">Geneva</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kurth_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kurth-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From the sixth century on, the marriage of Clovis and Clotilde was made the theme of epic narratives, in which the original facts were materially altered and the various versions found their way into the works of different Frankish chroniclers.<sup id="cite_ref-Kurth_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kurth-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> (538–594), in 493 Chilperic II was slain by his brother Gundobad  and his wife <a href="/wiki/Caretene" title="Caretene">Caretene</a> was drowned with a stone hung around her neck, while of his two daughters, Chrona took the veil and Clotilde was exiled – it is, however, assumed that this tale is <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-encbrit_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encbrit-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Butler's account follows Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Chilperic, her mother seems to have made her home with Godegisil at Geneva, where her other daughter, Chrona, founded the church of Saint-Victor. Soon after the death of Chilperic in 493, Clovis asked and obtained the hand of Clotilde.<sup id="cite_ref-Kurth_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kurth-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> They were married in 493.
</p><p>The marriage produced the following children:
</p>
<ul><li>Ingomer (born and died 494).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Chlodomer" title="Chlodomer">Chlodomer</a> (495–524), <a href="/wiki/List_of_Frankish_kings" title="List of Frankish kings">King of the Franks</a> at <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a> from 511.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Childebert_I" title="Childebert I">Childebert I</a> (496–558), King of the Franks at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> from 511.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Chlothar_I" title="Chlothar I">Chlothar I</a> (497–561), King of the Franks at <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a> from 511, King of all Franks from 558.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Clotilde_(died_531)" title="Clotilde (died 531)">Clotilde</a> (500–531), married <a href="/wiki/Amalaric" title="Amalaric">Amalaric</a>, King of the <a href="/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom" title="Visigothic Kingdom">Visigoths</a>.</li></ul>
<p>Clotilde was brought up as a Christian and did not rest until her husband had abjured <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">paganism</a> and embraced Christianity.  According to Gregory of Tours' <i>Historia Francorum</i> (History of the Franks), when Clotilde had their first child baptised, he died soon after. Clovis upbraided her; but when Chlodomer was born, she insisted on baptising him also. Although Chlodomer did indeed fall ill, he soon after recovered. More healthy children followed.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Clotilde's victory came in 496, when Clovis converted to Christianity, baptised by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Saint_Remigius" title="Saint Remigius">Remigius of Reims</a> on <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a> Day of that year.  According to tradition, on the eve of the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Tolbiac" title="Battle of Tolbiac">Battle of Tolbiac</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Alamanni" class="mw-redirect" title="Alamanni">Alamanni</a>, Clovis prayed to God, swearing to be baptised if he emerged victorious on the battlefield. When he did indeed triumph, Clovis readily took the faith. With him Clotilde built at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> the Church of the Holy Apostles, afterwards known as the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St_Genevieve" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St Genevieve">Abbey of St Genevieve</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-encbrit_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encbrit-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Significance">Significance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clotilde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Significance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Unusually, Clotilde was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> Christian at a time when <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a>, including the <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundians</a>, were <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> Christians. Therefore Clovis became a Catholic. This ensured that he had the support of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Empire">Eastern Roman Empire</a> in his wars against his rival Arian Gothic tribes, since the <a href="/wiki/Roman_people" title="Roman people">Romans</a> were Catholics. When the Franks eventually gained dominance over Western Europe, it was Catholicism which became the prevalent kind of Christianity, and Arianism died out.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years">Later years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clotilde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg/220px-Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="200" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg/330px-Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg/440px-Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1324" data-file-height="1204" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Clotilde_partageant_le_royaume_entre_ses_fils.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Clotilde and her sons, <i>Grandes Chroniques de Saint-Denis</i></div></div></div>
<p>After Clovis' death in 511, Clotilde retired to the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Martin,_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Martin, Tours">Abbey of St. Martin</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>.
</p><p>In 523 Clotilde's sons went to war against her cousin King <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_of_Burgundy" title="Sigismund of Burgundy">Sigismund of Burgundy</a>, the son of <a href="/wiki/Gundobad" title="Gundobad">Gundobad</a>, which led to Sigismund's deposition and imprisonment. Sigismund was assassinated the following year and his body thrown down a well in symbolic retaliation for the deaths of Clotilde's parents. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> claimed – and many others have followed – that Clotilde incited her sons to war as a means to revenge the supposed murder of her parents by Gundobad while others, such as Godefroid Kurth, find this unconvincing and apocryphal. Subsequently, her eldest son Chlodomer was killed during the following Burgundian campaign under Sigismund's successor King <a href="/wiki/Godomar" class="mw-redirect" title="Godomar">Godomar</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_V%C3%A9zeronce" title="Battle of Vézeronce">Battle of Vézeronce</a>. Her daughter, also named Clotilde, also died about this time.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Clotilde tried in vain to protect the rights of her three grandsons, the children of Chlodomer, against the claims of her surviving sons Childebert and Chlothar. Chlothar had two of them killed, while only <a href="/wiki/Clodoald" title="Clodoald">Clodoald</a> (<i>Cloud</i>) managed to escape and later chose an ecclesiastical career. She was equally unsuccessful in her efforts to prevent the civil discords between her children.<sup id="cite_ref-encbrit_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-encbrit-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After these failures, Clotilde appeared to dedicate herself to a saintly life. She occupied herself with the building of churches and monasteries, preferring to distance herself from the power struggles of the court.<sup id="cite_ref-web_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-web-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Churches associated with her are located at <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a>.
</p><p>Clotilde died in 545 at the tomb of St. Martin of Tours, of natural causes; she was buried at her husband's side, in the Church of the Holy Apostles (now the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St_Genevieve" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St Genevieve">Abbey of St Genevieve</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clotilde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clotilde's veneration made her the patron of queens, widows, brides and those in exile. In <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> especially she was venerated as the patroness of the lame, those who came to a violent death and women who suffered from ill-tempered husbands. In art she is often depicted presiding over the baptism of Clovis, or as a suppliant at the shrine of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Saint Martin</a>. Several fine images of her remain, particularly in the 16th-century stained glass window at <a href="/wiki/Andelys" class="mw-redirect" title="Andelys">Andelys</a>. Her relics survived the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, and are housed in the <a href="/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Leu-Saint-Gilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Église Saint-Leu-Saint-Gilles">Église Saint-Leu-Saint-Gilles</a> in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Clotilde is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Les_Andelys" title="Les Andelys">Les Andelys</a>, Normandy. In 511, the Queen founded a convent for young girls of the nobility there, which was destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a> in 911. In its place was erected Our Lady's Collegiate Church, which contains a statue of Saint Clotilde. Also in Les Andelys is Saint Clotilde's Fountain. The spring is popularly believed to heal skin diseases.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
