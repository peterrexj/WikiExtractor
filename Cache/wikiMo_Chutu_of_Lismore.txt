<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>

<p>Saint <b>Mo Chutu mac Fínaill</b> (died 14 May 639),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> also known as <b>Mochuda</b>, <b>Carthach</b> or <b>Carthach the Younger</b> (a name Latinized as <i>Carthagus</i> and Anglicized as <b>Carthage</b> <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;k&#39; in &#39;kind&#39;">k</span><span title="/ɑːr/: &#39;ar&#39; in &#39;far&#39;">ɑːr</span><span title="/θ/: &#39;th&#39; in &#39;thigh&#39;">θ</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span></span>/</a></span></span>),<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-RM_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RM-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> was abbot of <a href="/wiki/Rahan,_County_Offaly" title="Rahan, County Offaly">Rahan, County Offaly</a> and subsequently, founder and first <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Lismore" title="Abbot of Lismore">abbot of Lismore</a> (Irish <i>Les Mór Mo Chutu</i>), County Waterford.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The saint's <i>Life</i> has come down in several Irish and Latin recensions, which appear to derive from a Latin original written in the 11th or 12th century.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mo_Chutu_of_Lismore&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Through his father, Fínall Fíngein, Mo Chutu belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Ciarraige_Luachra" class="mw-redirect" title="Ciarraige Luachra">Ciarraige Luachra</a>, while his mother, Finmed, was of the <a href="/wiki/Corco_Duibne" class="mw-redirect" title="Corco Duibne">Corco Duibne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Notes added to the <i><a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9lire_%C3%93engusso" class="mw-redirect" title="Félire Óengusso">Félire Óengusso</a></i> (the Martyrology of Óengus) claim that his foster father was Carthach mac Fianáin, that is <a href="/wiki/Carthage_the_Elder" title="Carthage the Elder">Carthach the Elder</a>, whose period of activity can be assigned to the late 6th century.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mo Chutu first became abbot of <a href="/wiki/Rahan,_County_Offaly" title="Rahan, County Offaly">Rahan</a>, a monastery which lay in the territory of the southern <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Uí Néill">Uí Néill</a>. He composed a rule for his monks, an Irish metrical poem of 580 lines, divided into nine separate sections, a notable literary relic of the early Irish Church.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a></i>, he was expelled from the monastery during the Easter season of 637. The incident has been connected with the <a href="/wiki/Easter_controversy" title="Easter controversy">Easter controversy</a>, in which Irish churches were involved during the 7th century. Through his training in Munster, Mo Chutu may have been a supporter of the Roman system of calculation, which would have brought him into conflict with adherents of the 'Celtic' reckoning in <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg/220px-Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg/330px-Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg/440px-Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3504" data-file-height="2336" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Lismore_St_Carthages_Cathedral_2007_08_03.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Carthage Cathedral, Lismore</div></div></div>
<p>Following his expulsion, Mo Chutu journeyed to the <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A9isi" title="Déisi">Déisi</a>, where he founded the great <a href="/wiki/Lismore_Abbey" title="Lismore Abbey">monastery of Lismore</a> (in modern <a href="/wiki/County_Waterford" title="County Waterford">County Waterford</a>). The Latin and Irish <i>Lives</i> make very little of Mo Chutu's earlier misfortune and focus instead on the saint's resistance to the oppressive Uí Néill rulers and his joyous reception among the Déisi.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He has been portrayed in a heroic light in <i>Indarba Mo Chutu a r-Raithin</i> (<i>The expulsion of Mo Chutu from Rahan</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His foundation at Lismore flourished after his lifetime, eclipsing the reputation of the saint's earlier church. It was able to withstand the Viking depredations which plagued the area and benefited from the generosity of Munster kings, notably the <a href="/wiki/Mac_Carthaig" class="mw-redirect" title="Mac Carthaig">Mac Carthaig</a> of Desmond. In the 12th century, St <a href="/wiki/Decl%C3%A1n_of_Ardmore" title="Declán of Ardmore">Déclán</a>'s foundation of Ardmore aspired to the status of episcopal see in the new diocese, but the privilege went instead to Lismore.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Carthage_window.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/St_Carthage_window.jpg/220px-St_Carthage_window.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/St_Carthage_window.jpg/330px-St_Carthage_window.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/St_Carthage_window.jpg/440px-St_Carthage_window.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3672" data-file-height="4896" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Carthage_window.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Carthage depicted in <a href="/wiki/Stained_glass" title="Stained glass">stained glass</a>; he holds a bishop's <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> and a model of <a href="/wiki/Lismore_Abbey" title="Lismore Abbey">Lismore Abbey</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> in the Irish martyrologies is 14 May,<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> as well as in the <i>Great <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">Synaxaristes</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In the present calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Ireland" title="Catholic Church in Ireland">Catholic Church in Ireland</a>, in which 14 May is the feast of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Matthias" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Matthias">Saint Matthias</a>, the memorial of Saint Carthage is celebrated on 15 May.<sup id="cite_ref-RM_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RM-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
