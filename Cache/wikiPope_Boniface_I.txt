<p><b>Pope Boniface I</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Bonifatius I</i>) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> from 28 December 418 to his death on 4 September 422. His <a href="/wiki/Papal_selection_before_1059" title="Papal selection before 1059">election</a> was disputed by the supporters of <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Eulalius" title="Antipope Eulalius">Eulalius</a> until the dispute was settled by <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Honorius" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Honorius">Emperor Honorius</a>. Boniface was active in maintaining church discipline, and he restored certain privileges to the metropolitical sees of <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Narbonne" title="Ancient Diocese of Narbonne">Narbonne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vienne" title="Vienne">Vienne</a>, exempting them from any subjection to the primacy of Arles. He was a contemporary of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, who dedicated to him some of his works.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Boniface_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known of his life antecedent to his election. The "Liber Pontificalis" calls him a Roman and the son of the presbyter Jocundus. He is believed to have been ordained by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a> (366–384) and to have served as representative of Innocent I at Constantinople (c. 405).<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Election">Election</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Boniface_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Election">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On the day of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zosimus" title="Pope Zosimus">Pope Zosimus</a>'s funeral, which was held at <a href="/wiki/San_Lorenzo_fuori_le_Mura" class="mw-redirect" title="San Lorenzo fuori le Mura">San Lorenzo fuori le Mura</a>, partisans of <a href="/wiki/Eulalius" class="mw-redirect" title="Eulalius">Eulalius</a> occupied the <a href="/wiki/Lateran" title="Lateran">Lateran</a>. Later that day, Eulalius arrived, with a crowd consisting of deacons, laity, and a few priests, and was elected bishop. The new pope and his supporters remained at the church until Sunday, 29 December, for the formal ordination customarily took place on a Sunday.
</p><p>Meanwhile, on the Saturday after the funeral, and of Eulalius' election, a majority of the priests of the Church elected Boniface, who had previously been a councilor of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent</a>.  He also was consecrated on 29 December, at the <a href="/wiki/San_Marcello_al_Corso" title="San Marcello al Corso">Church of Saint Marcellus</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Campus_Martius" title="Campus Martius">Campus Martius</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Urban_Prefect" class="mw-redirect" title="Urban Prefect">Urban Prefect</a> <a href="/wiki/Aurelius_Anicius_Symmachus" title="Aurelius Anicius Symmachus">Aurelius Anicius Symmachus</a> warned both parties to keep the peace and wrote to the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Flavius_Augustus_Honorius" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavius Augustus Honorius">Honorius</a> that Eulalius, who had been elected first and in due order, was in the right. The Emperor answered on 3 January 419, recognizing Eulalius as the rightful bishop of Rome. Despite these official acts, violence broke out between the two groups of supporters, and Boniface was seized by the Prefect's police and taken to a lodging outside the walls where he was detained under the surveillance of the Prefect's agents.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, Boniface's partisans did not let the matter rest there and sent a petition to Emperor Honorius alleging irregularities in the election of Eulalius. In response, the Emperor suspended his previous order and summoned both parties to appear for judgment before him and certain Italian bishops on 8 February. This hearing then deferred the decision to a synod scheduled to meet at <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a> on 13 June, but commanded both Boniface and Eulalius to stay out of Rome. Since Easter was approaching, the bishop of Spoleto, an outside party, was asked to celebrate the rites of this important holy day in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Both Empress <a href="/wiki/Galla_Placidia" title="Galla Placidia">Galla Placidia</a> and her husband <a href="/wiki/Constantius_III" title="Constantius III">Constantius III</a> favored Eulalius, who had been elected first. And Stewart Oost observes that papal elections at the time were "still quite indefinite and both parties could thus with right claim proper election and consecration." Although Eulalius appeared to be destined to be confirmed to the post, he defied Honorius' order and entered Rome on 18 March — Easter Sunday that year fell on 30 March — thus losing the support of the authorities.
</p><p>Symmachus sent his police to occupy the Lateran, where Eulalius had established himself, and removed him to a house outside the walls of Rome. Bishop Achilleus of Spoleto celebrated the Mass in the Lateran. The proposed Council of Spoleto was canceled, and on 3 April 419, Emperor Honorius recognized Boniface as the rightful pope.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Boniface_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Boniface ordered the singing of the <i><a href="/wiki/Gloria_in_excelsis_Deo" title="Gloria in excelsis Deo">Gloria in excelsis</a></i> on <a href="/wiki/Maundy_Thursday" title="Maundy Thursday">Maundy Thursday</a>, and regulated several points of Church discipline. He reversed some of his predecessor's policies regarding church administration. He reduced the vicariate authority giving Patroclus, Bishop of Arles, jurisdiction over other Gallic sees and restored the metropolitan powers of the chief bishops of provinces. He supported Hilary, Archbishop of Narbonne, in his choice of a bishop of the vacant See of Lodeve, against Patroclus, who tried to install someone else. He also insisted that Maximus, Bishop of Valence, should be tried for his alleged crimes, not by a primate, but by a synod of the bishops of Gaul, and promised to sustain their decision.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Boniface supported Augustine in combating <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagianism</a>, forwarding to him two Pelagian letters Boniface had received calumniating Augustine. In recognition of this solicitude, Augustine dedicated to Boniface his rejoinder contained in <i>Contra duas Epistolas Pelagianoruin Libri quatuor</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He persuaded Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a> to return <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_prefecture_of_Illyricum" title="Praetorian prefecture of Illyricum">Illyricum</a> to Western jurisdiction and defended the rights of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Boniface I died 4 September 422, in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
