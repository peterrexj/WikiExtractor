<p><b>Agapius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Ἀγάπιος</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> killed at <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima" title="Caesarea Maritima">Caesarea</a> in AD 306.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He is recognized as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> is recorded by <a href="/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Eusebius of Caesarea">Eusebius of Caesarea</a> in his work <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Palestine" title="Martyrs of Palestine">The Martyrs of Palestine</a></i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agapius_(died_306)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agapius was arrested in AD 304. He remained in prison for two years and was tortured on multiple occasions. He was brought out to the arena many times and presented to the judges. There he was threatened and reserved for later matches. The judges, <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> notes, must have been motivated either out of compassion or the hope that he might change his mind and renounce <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. Finally he was brought to the arena and presented to the emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximinus_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Maximinus II">Maximinus</a>. He was offered a pardon on the condition that he disavow his faith. According to <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a>, he not only refused the offer, but he is said to have cheerfully rushed headlong into the bear. The animal inflicted severe injuries, but Agapius survived. Stones were affixed to his feet and he was drowned in the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean" class="mw-redirect" title="Mediterranean">Mediterranean</a> on the following day. 
</p><p>His feast days are observed on November 20 and August 19.
</p>
