<p><b>Cyricus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Κήρυκος</span>, <a href="/wiki/Amharic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Amharic language">Amharic</a>: <span lang="am">ቂርቆስ</span>, <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Aramaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial Aramaic language">Imperial Aramaic</a>: <span lang="arc" dir="rtl">ܡܪܝ ܩܘܪܝܩܘܣ ܣܗܕܐ</span> <i>Mar Quriaqos Sahada</i>; also Cyriacus, Quiriac, Quiricus, Cyr), and his mother, <b>Julitta</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἰουλίττα</span>, <a href="/wiki/Amharic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Amharic language">Amharic</a>: <span lang="am">እየሉጣ</span> <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Aramaic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial Aramaic language">Imperial Aramaic</a>: <span lang="arc" dir="rtl">ܝܘܠܝܛܐ</span>, <i>Yulitha</i>; also Julietta) are venerated as early <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>.  According to tradition, they were put to death at <a href="/wiki/Tarsus_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Tarsus (city)">Tarsus</a> in AD 304.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cyricus">Cyricus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Cyricus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Some evidence exists for an otherwise unknown child-martyr named Cyricus at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is believed that the legends about Cyricus and Julitta refer to him. There are places named after Cyricus in Europe and the Middle East, but without the name Julitta attached. Cyricus is the <a href="/wiki/Saint-Cyr_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint-Cyr (disambiguation)">Saint-Cyr</a> found in many French <a href="/wiki/Toponym" class="mw-redirect" title="Toponym">toponyms</a>, as well as in several named San Quirico in Italy. The cult of these saints was strong in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> after <a href="/wiki/Amator" title="Amator">Amator</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Auxerre" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Auxerre">Bishop of Auxerre</a>, brought relics back from Antioch in the 4th century. It is said that <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a> discovered their relics originally and built a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> near <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, and a church not far off from <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. In the 6th century the <i>Acts</i> of Cyricus and Julitta were rejected in a list of <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a> documents by the <a href="/wiki/Decretum_Gelasianum" class="mw-redirect" title="Decretum Gelasianum">Decretum Gelasianum</a>, called as such since the list was erroneously attributed to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Pope Gelasius I</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:GettyCyricusPalm.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Sculpture of St. Cyricus as a bald toddler standing in a small tub and holding a palm branch" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/6/6b/GettyCyricusPalm.jpg/200px-GettyCyricusPalm.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="256" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/6/6b/GettyCyricusPalm.jpg/300px-GettyCyricusPalm.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/6/6b/GettyCyricusPalm.jpg/400px-GettyCyricusPalm.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="769" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:GettyCyricusPalm.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Francesco Laurana, "St. Cyricus," Getty Center, Los Angeles</div></div></div>
<p>According to one version of their martyrdom, Julitta and her three-year-old son Cyricus had fled to <a href="/wiki/Tarsus,_Mersin#Antiquity" title="Tarsus, Mersin">Tarsus</a> and were identified as <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Julitta was tortured and Cyricus, being held by the governor of Tarsus, scratched the governor's face and was killed by being thrown down some stairs. Julitta did not weep but celebrated the fact that her son had earned the crown of martyrdom. In anger, the governor then decreed that Julitta's sides should be ripped apart with <a href="/wiki/Grappling_hook" title="Grappling hook">hooks</a>, and then she was beheaded. Her body, along with that of Cyricus, was flung outside the city, on the heap of bodies belonging to <a href="/wiki/Criminal" class="mw-redirect" title="Criminal">criminals</a>, but two maids rescued the corpses of the mother and child and buried them in a nearby field. This version is recorded in a letter from <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Mopsuestia" title="Theodore of Mopsuestia">Theodore of Mopsuestia</a> to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zosimus" title="Pope Zosimus">Pope Zosimus</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and in the <i>Acta Graece Sincera</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An alternative version of the story is found in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In this version, Julitta was captured without Cyricus and brought before the governor. She refused to sacrifice to idols and tells him to find a child, so that they can ask him if he thinks it is right to worship one god or many. Cyricus was found and he declared himself to be a Christian. The governor inflicted many tortures on them, all of which they miraculously survive. Satan entered Julitta's heart, causing her to be afraid of death, but Cyricus emboldens her with encouragement and prayers. The mother and child are finally <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cyricus_and_Charlemagne">Cyricus and Charlemagne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cyricus and Charlemagne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A story from <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a> states that one night <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> <a href="/wiki/Dream" title="Dream">dreamed</a> he was saved from being killed by a <a href="/wiki/Wild_boar" title="Wild boar">wild boar</a> during a <a href="/wiki/Hunting" title="Hunting">hunt</a>. He was saved by the appearance of a nude child, who had promised to save the Emperor from <a href="/wiki/Death" title="Death">death</a> if he would give him clothes to cover his nakedness.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Nevers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Nevers">bishop of Nevers</a> interpreted this dream to mean that he wanted the Emperor to repair the roof of the <a href="/wiki/Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Cyr-et-Sainte-Julitte_de_Nevers" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathédrale Saint-Cyr-et-Sainte-Julitte de Nevers">Cathédrale Saint-Cyr-et-Sainte-Julitte de Nevers</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Croatia">Croatia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Croatia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Croatia, in the Town of Visnjan, there is a 17th-century loggia and the church of Saint Cyricis (Kvirik) and Julitta (Julita).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Georgia">Georgia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Georgia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Cyricus (<i>Kvirike</i>) and Julitta (<i>Ivlita</i>) are venerated as patron saints of the Kala community in the highland province of <a href="/wiki/Svaneti" title="Svaneti">Svaneti</a>. While the saints were relatively unknown in the rest of Georgia, the Svan mountaineers held them in high esteem. The 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Lagurka" title="Lagurka">Lagurka</a> church, located at 2200 metres above sea and known for its wall paintings, is the scene of an all-Svan festival and pilgrimage, <i>kvirikoba</i> ("the day of Cyricus"), held annually on July 28.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In the words of the historian <a href="/wiki/Ekvtime_Taqaishvili" class="mw-redirect" title="Ekvtime Taqaishvili">Ekvtime Taqaishvili</a>, for the Svans Lagurka is what for the ancient Greeks was <a href="/wiki/Delphi" title="Delphi">Delphi</a>—the symbol of their unity.<sup id="cite_ref-ET_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ET-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Italy">Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG/220px-SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG/330px-SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG/440px-SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SanQuiricod%27Orcia1.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Collegiata dei Santi Quirico e Giulitta, <a href="/wiki/San_Quirico_d%E2%80%99Orcia" class="mw-redirect" title="San Quirico d’Orcia">San Quirico d’Orcia</a></div></div></div>
<p>In Italy, where they are known as <b>Quirico</b> (or Quilico, or Chirico) and <b>Giulitta</b> (or Giuletta or Giulietta ),<sup id="cite_ref-nomi_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nomi-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> the place most commonly linked with the saints is the village of <a href="/wiki/San_Quirico_d%E2%80%99Orcia" class="mw-redirect" title="San Quirico d’Orcia">San Quirico d’Orcia</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Val_d%E2%80%99Orcia" class="mw-redirect" title="Val d’Orcia">Val d’Orcia</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Siena" title="Province of Siena">Province of Siena</a>, region of <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. There a twelfth- or thirteenth-century church (pictured right), based on an eighth-century baptistery, is dedicated to them.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The cult, however, is common in many parts of country and more than 200 churches, monasteries, localities, etc. with signs of devotion to one or both of the saints have been identified.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Other communes named after them are <a href="/wiki/Corvino_San_Quirico" title="Corvino San Quirico">Corvino San Quirico</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Pavia" title="Province of Pavia">Province of Pavia</a>), <a href="/wiki/San_Chirico_Nuovo" title="San Chirico Nuovo">San Chirico Nuovo</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Potenza" title="Province of Potenza">Province of Potenza</a>), <a href="/wiki/San_Chirico_Raparo" title="San Chirico Raparo">San Chirico Raparo</a> (Province of Potenza), <a href="/wiki/Serra_San_Quirico" title="Serra San Quirico">Serra San Quirico</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Ancona" title="Province of Ancona">Province of Ancona</a>), and <a href="/wiki/Santa_Giuletta" title="Santa Giuletta">Santa Giuletta</a> (Province of Pavia). Communes of whom they are patron saints include <a href="/wiki/Borgo_San_Martino" title="Borgo San Martino">Borgo San Martino</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Alessandria" title="Province of Alessandria">Province of Alessandria</a>), <a href="/wiki/Cavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Cavaria">Cavaria</a> in the municipality of <a href="/wiki/Cavaria_con_Premezzo" title="Cavaria con Premezzo">Cavaria con Premezzo</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Varese" title="Province of Varese">Province of Varese</a>), <a href="/wiki/Cisternino" title="Cisternino">Cisternino</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Brindisi" title="Province of Brindisi">Province of Brindisi</a>), <a href="/wiki/Collesalvetti" title="Collesalvetti">Collesalvetti</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Livorno" title="Province of Livorno">Province of Livorno</a>), and <a href="/wiki/Trofarello" title="Trofarello">Trofarello</a> (<a href="/wiki/Province_of_Turin" title="Province of Turin">Province of Turin</a>. San Quirico Province of Pistoia
</p><p>In parts of <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>, including <a href="/wiki/Centallo" title="Centallo">Centallo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a> and <a href="/wiki/Murisengo" title="Murisengo">Murisengo</a>, an unconnected Saint Quirico is venerated, regarded as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nomi_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nomi-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="British_Isles">British Isles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: British Isles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There are a few churches in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> dedicated to Saints Cyricus and Julitta, including <a href="/wiki/Newton_St._Cyres" class="mw-redirect" title="Newton St. Cyres">Newton St. Cyres</a> in Devon, <a href="/wiki/Tickenham" title="Tickenham">Tickenham</a><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> in Somerset, and <a href="/wiki/Swaffham_Prior" title="Swaffham Prior">Swaffham Prior</a> in Cambridgeshire. In <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, they can be found in the villages of <a href="/wiki/Luxulyan" title="Luxulyan">Luxulyan</a> and <a href="/wiki/St_Veep" title="St Veep">St Veep</a>, and there was also once a chapel at <a href="/wiki/Calstock" title="Calstock">Calstock</a> dedicated to these two saints. In Wales there is a least one church dedicated to the saints, in <a href="/wiki/Llanilid" title="Llanilid">Llanilid</a>, but named as St. Ilid and St. Curig.
</p><p>The cult of "St. Giric" was formerly much more widespread in Celtic Britain, however. His feast day was one of the principal Welsh holidays, as codified by the <a href="/wiki/Laws_of_Hywel_Dda" class="mw-redirect" title="Laws of Hywel Dda">laws of Hywel Dda</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Stcyriac.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Stcyriac.jpg/220px-Stcyriac.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Stcyriac.jpg/330px-Stcyriac.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Stcyriac.jpg/440px-Stcyriac.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1152" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Stcyriac.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Print of St Cyricus in Lacock, Wiltshire.</div></div></div><p>St Cyriac's Church, Lacock, Wiltshire, has a framed print of a similar story depicting St Cyricus boxing a governor's ears because the governor had blasphemed. The embittered governor stabs the child dead and the mother is crucified. This print appears to be based on panels from the <a href="/wiki/Predella" title="Predella">predella</a> of a 15th-century Italian <a href="/wiki/Altarpiece" title="Altarpiece">altarpiece</a> dedicated to Cyricus.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Middle_East">Middle East</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Middle East">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Cyricus in particular is mentioned numerous times in the <a href="/wiki/Daily_Office" class="mw-redirect" title="Daily Office">daily office</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_East" title="Church of the East">Church of the East</a> as attested in the large collection of prayers and services known as the Hudra. The mention of a saint from Tarsus in such <a href="/wiki/East_Syriac_Rite" title="East Syriac Rite">East Syriac</a> traditions suggests that there was considerable early sharing of martyrological traditions despite doctrinal differences between churches.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="India">India</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyricus_and_Julitta&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: India">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Cyriacus is one of the saints venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Christians" title="Saint Thomas Christians">Saint Thomas Christians</a> of <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>. Some of their important churches were dedicated to the saint. A document written in 1301 mentions the church at <a href="/wiki/Kodungallur" title="Kodungallur">Kodungallur</a> which was dedicated the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/St._Thomas_Syro-Malabar_Church,_Palayoor" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Thomas Syro-Malabar Church, Palayoor">Pālūr Church</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Ezharappallikal" class="mw-redirect" title="Ezharappallikal">seven churches</a> associated with the mission of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle" title="Thomas the Apostle">Thomas the Apostle</a>, was originally dedicated to Cyriacus.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> There is a small piece of St. Cyricus / Kuriakose's finger at St. Peter's and St. Paul's Jacobite Syrian Orthodox Church in <a href="/wiki/Puthencruz" title="Puthencruz">Puthencruz</a> (<a href="/wiki/Ernakulam" title="Ernakulam">Ernakulam</a>) They celebrate his Perunnal (feast) on July 27, 28 and the anniversary of the relocation of his bone on Nov 13, 14 of every year. Also, a piece of his other finger can be found in <a href="/wiki/St._George%27s_Monastery,_Malekurish" title="St. George&#39;s Monastery, Malekurish">St. George Dayro in Malecruze</a> in Ernakulam.
