<p><b>Sebastian Newdigate</b>, <a href="/wiki/Carthusians" title="Carthusians">O.Cart</a> (7 September 1500 – 19 June 1535) was the seventh child of John Newdigate, <a href="/wiki/Sergeant-at-law" class="mw-redirect" title="Sergeant-at-law">Sergeant-at-law</a>. He spent his early life at court, and later became a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. He was executed for <a href="/wiki/Treason" title="Treason">treason</a> on 19 June 1535 for his refusal to accept <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>'s assumption of supremacy over the Church in England. His death was considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrdom</a>, and he was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sebastian_Newdigate&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sebastian Newdigate, born 7 September 1500 at <a href="/wiki/Harefield,_Middlesex" class="mw-redirect" title="Harefield, Middlesex">Harefield, Middlesex</a>, was the seventh of the fourteen children<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> of John Newdigate (d. 15 August 1528), esquire, <a href="/wiki/Sergeant-at-law" class="mw-redirect" title="Sergeant-at-law">Sergeant-at-law</a> in 1510 and King's Serjeant in 1520, and Amphyllis Neville (d. 1544), daughter and heiress of John Neville of <a href="/wiki/Rolleston,_Nottinghamshire" title="Rolleston, Nottinghamshire">Rolleston, Nottinghamshire</a>, "a kinsman of the <a href="/wiki/Earls_of_Westmorland" class="mw-redirect" title="Earls of Westmorland">Earls of Westmorland</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The births of Sebastian Newdigate and his brothers and sisters are listed in the Newdigate Cartulary:<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ul><li>John Newdigate, son and heir, born at the Whitefriars in Fleet Street, London, 4 January 1490.</li>
<li>Charles Newdigate, born 10 July 1493.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/William_Newdigate" title="William Newdigate">William Newdigate</a>, born at the Whitefriars 3 February 1495.</li>
<li>Jane Newdigate (d. 7 July 1571), born at Harefield, Middlesex, 18 August 1496. Jane Newdigate married Sir Robert Dormer, and was the grandmother of <a href="/wiki/Jane_Dormer" title="Jane Dormer">Jane Dormer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Mary Newdigate, born at Harefield 21 September 1497.</li>
<li>Barbara Newdigate, born at the White Friars 4 November 1498.</li>
<li>Sebastian Newdigate, born at Harefield 7 September 1500. The entry records that his godparents were William Bynchester, George Osborne and Joan Weddon, and that he 'after became a delicate courtier'.</li>
<li>Anthony Newdigate, born at Harefield 17 November 1502.</li>
<li>Silvester Newdigate, born at Harefield 16 January 1504.</li>
<li>Dorothy Newdigate, born at Harefield 20 June 1505.</li>
<li>George Newdigate, born at Harefield 26 April 1507.</li>
<li>Sybil Newdigate, born at Harefield 3 July 1509.</li>
<li>Dunstan Newdigate/Bonaventure  Newdigate (twins), born at Harefield on Saint Dunstan's Day, 19 May 1510.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sebastian_Newdigate&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Newdigate was educated at court, and may have studied at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Cambridge" title="University of Cambridge">Cambridge</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bainbridge_2004_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bainbridge_2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He became a member of <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Privy_Chamber" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Chamber">Privy Chamber</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Bainbridge_2004_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bainbridge_2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and is said to have enjoyed the King's favour.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Bainbridge, Newdigate married Katherine Hampden, widow of Henry Ferrers, and daughter of Sir John Hampden of <a href="/wiki/Great_Hampden" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Hampden">Great Hampden</a>, by whom he had two daughters, Amphyllis and Elizabeth.<sup id="cite_ref-Bainbridge_2004_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bainbridge_2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Crisp also states that Newdigate married, and by an unnamed wife who died in 1524 had an only daughter, Amphyllis, who married Thomas Breme after 3 September 1545.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However Hendriks and Doreau question whether Newdigate ever married,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Origines_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Origines-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and Richardson states that Newdigate's alleged wife, Katherine Hampden, widow of Henry Ferrers, and daughter and heiress of Sir John Hampden, married a different member of the Newdigate family, Thomas Newdigate, gentleman, of <a href="/wiki/Wivelsfield" title="Wivelsfield">Wivelsfield</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sussex" title="Sussex">Sussex</a>, the son of Walter Newdigate.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is said that Newdigate entered the <a href="/wiki/London_Charterhouse" title="London Charterhouse">London Charterhouse</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> priory, after his wife's death<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> in 1524,<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> However it is unlikely that Newdigate's admission as a postulant could have occurred prior to 24 October 1526, when the King granted him a wardship.<sup id="cite_ref-Origines_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Origines-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Not long after Newdigate became a novice, his sister, Jane, who in 1512 had married Sir Robert Dormer of <a href="/wiki/Wing,_Buckinghamshire" title="Wing, Buckinghamshire">Wing, Buckinghamshire</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> visited the Prior, William Tynbygh, to express her concern about Newdigate's suitability for the strictness of the monastic life after his early years at court.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Despite his sister's misgivings, Newdigate remained at the Charterhouse. He was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> on 3 June 1531, and was ordained to the priesthood before his death.<sup id="cite_ref-Bainbridge_2004_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bainbridge_2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1534 Henry VIII required his subjects to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Succession" class="mw-redirect" title="Oath of Succession">Oath of Succession</a> recognizing <a href="/wiki/Anne_Boleyn" title="Anne Boleyn">Anne Boleyn</a> as his lawful wife. Newdigate signed the oath "in as far as the law of God permits" on 6 June 1534.<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> However the Carthusian community at the Charterhouse refused to accept the King's assumption of supremacy over the English church, and on 4 May 1535 the Prior of the Charterhouse, <a href="/wiki/John_Houghton_(martyr)" title="John Houghton (martyr)">John Houghton</a>, was executed, together with two other Carthusian priors, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(martyr)" title="Robert Lawrence (martyr)">Robert Lawrence</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Webster" title="Augustine Webster">Augustine Webster</a>, priors respectively of <a href="/wiki/Beauvale_Charterhouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Beauvale Charterhouse">Beauvale</a> and <a href="/wiki/Axholme" class="mw-redirect" title="Axholme">Axholme</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Newdigate and two other monks, <a href="/wiki/Humphrey_Middlemore" title="Humphrey Middlemore">Humphrey Middlemore</a> and <a href="/wiki/William_Exmew" title="William Exmew">William Exmew</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> were arrested on 25 May 1535 for denying the King's supremacy, and imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>, where they were kept for fourteen days bound to pillars, standing upright, with iron rings round their necks, hands, and feet.<sup id="cite_ref-Hendriks_1889_172_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hendriks_1889_172-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Newdigate was visited there by the King, who is said to have come in disguise,<sup id="cite_ref-Hendriks_1889_172_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hendriks_1889_172-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and to have offered to load Newdigate with riches and honours if he would conform. He was then brought before the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Council</a>, and sent to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>, where Henry again visited him, but was unable to change his mind.<sup id="cite_ref-Hendriks_1889_172_17-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hendriks_1889_172-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The three monks were condemned to death for treason on 11 June,<sup id="cite_ref-Hendriks_1889_172_17-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hendriks_1889_172-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and on 19 June were dragged to <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> on hurdles, and hanged, drawn and quartered.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Their remains were put on display at various locations in London.<sup id="cite_ref-Bainbridge_2004_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bainbridge_2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-wainewright_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wainewright-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This process of attrition was to claim as its victims no fewer than fifteen<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> of the London Carthusians.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Newdigate's courage is said to have inspired members of his family, as well as others, to remain steadfast in their Catholicism. Along with the other members of his Order who suffered martyrdom at this time, Newdigate was beatified by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a> on 9 December 1886.<sup id="cite_ref-Bainbridge_2004_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bainbridge_2004-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
