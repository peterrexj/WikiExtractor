<p><b>Saint Cosmas of <a href="/wiki/Maiuma" title="Maiuma">Maiuma</a></b>, also called <b>Cosmas Hagiopolites</b> ("of the Holy City"), <b>Cosmas of Jerusalem</b>, <b>Cosmas the Melodist</b>, or <b>Cosmas the Poet</b> (d. 773 or 794),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and an important <a href="/wiki/Hymnographer" class="mw-redirect" title="Hymnographer">hymnographer</a> in the East. He is venerated as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cosmas_of_Maiuma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Cosmas (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Κοσμάς</span>) was born in Jerusalem,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but he was orphaned at a young age. He was adopted by <a href="/wiki/Sarjun_ibn_Mansur" title="Sarjun ibn Mansur">Sergius</a>, the father of <a href="/wiki/St._John_of_Damascus" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John of Damascus">St. John of Damascus</a> (ca.676 - 749), and became John's foster-brother. The teacher of the two boys was an elderly <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabrian</a> monk, also named Cosmas (known as "Cosmas the Monk" to distinguish him), who had been freed from slavery to the <a href="/wiki/Saracens" class="mw-redirect" title="Saracens">Saracens</a> by St. John's father.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> John and Cosmas went from <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, where both became monks in the <a href="/wiki/Lavra" title="Lavra">Lavra</a> (monastery) of St. <a href="/wiki/Sabbas_the_Sanctified" title="Sabbas the Sanctified">Sabbas the Sanctified</a> near that city.<sup id="cite_ref-Wellesz149_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wellesz149-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Together they helped defend the Church against the heresy of <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">iconoclasm</a>.
</p><p>Cosmas left the monastery in 743 when he was appointed Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Maiuma" title="Maiuma">Maiuma</a>, the port of ancient <a href="/wiki/Gaza_City" title="Gaza City">Gaza</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wellesz149_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wellesz149-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He outlived St. John by many years and died in great old age.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cosmas_of_Maiuma&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a learned prose-author, Cosmas wrote commentaries, or <i><a href="/wiki/Scholia" title="Scholia">scholia</a></i>, on the poems of St. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus" title="Gregory of Nazianzus">Gregory of Nazianzus</a>. He is regarded with great admiration as a poet. St. Cosmas and St. John of Damascus are considered to be the best representatives of the later Greek classical <a href="/wiki/Hymnography" class="mw-redirect" title="Hymnography">hymnography</a>, the most characteristic examples of which are the artistic liturgical chants known as "<a href="/wiki/Canon_(hymnography)" title="Canon (hymnography)">canons</a>". They worked together on developing the <a href="/wiki/Octoechos_(liturgy)" title="Octoechos (liturgy)">Octoechos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Cosmas has been called "a vessel of divine grace" and "the glory of the Church."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He composed the solemn canons for <a href="/wiki/Matins" title="Matins">Matins</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_Saturday" title="Lazarus Saturday">Lazarus Saturday</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a>, the Triodes (canons with only three <a href="/wiki/Canticles" class="mw-redirect" title="Canticles">Canticles</a>) which are chanted during <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week" title="Holy Week">Holy Week</a>, the first canon of the <a href="/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus" title="Nativity of Jesus">Nativity</a> (based on a Nativity sermon by St. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Theologian">Gregory the Theologian</a>), and is known for his finest work, "<i>Canon for Christmas Day</i>".<sup id="cite_ref-Wellesz149_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wellesz149-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Altogether, fourteen canons are attributed to him in the <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_book" title="Liturgical book">liturgical books</a> of the Orthodox Church.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His most well-known composition is "More honourable than the cherubim…" (which is included in the <i><a href="/wiki/Axion_Estin" title="Axion Estin">Axion Estin</a></i>), sung regularly at Matins, the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">Divine Liturgy</a> and other services.
</p><p>The hymns of St. Cosmas were originally intended for the <a href="/wiki/Canonical_Hours#Eastern_Orthodox_and_Greek-Catholic_usage" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonical Hours">Divine Services</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Orthodox Church of Jerusalem">Church of Jerusalem</a>, but through the influence of <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Constantinople">Constantinople</a> their use became universal in the Orthodox Church. It is not certain, however, that all the hymns ascribed to Cosmas in the liturgical books were really his compositions, especially as his teacher of the same name was also a hymn writer.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Eastern Orthodox Church observes his feast on October 12 (<a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>, it is October 25 of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>) and in Greek Church on October 14 (julian, it is October 27).
</p>
