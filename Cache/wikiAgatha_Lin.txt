<p><b>Agatha Lin</b>, born in <a href="/wiki/Qinglong_County,_Guizhou" title="Qinglong County, Guizhou">Qinglong</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Guizhou" title="Guizhou">Guizhou</a> of southeast China in 1817, was a Chinese saint and martyr.  She was a headmistress and <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechist</a>, and one of the first to evangelize the <a href="/wiki/Miao_people" title="Miao people">Miao people</a>.  She was beheaded for her faith on January 28, 1858.  Agatha was beatified by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pius X</a> on May 2, 1909, and canonized in 2000.  Her feast day is February 18.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_Lin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agatha Lin, born Lin Zhao, was born in the village of Machang in the Qinglong district of Guizhou in 1817. Her father was a salt merchant, and both parents, who were "fervent Christians",<sup id="cite_ref-:1_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> had been converted to Christianity by <a href="/wiki/Zhang_Dapeng" title="Zhang Dapeng">Zhang Dapeng</a>. Agatha was baptized when she was three days old, though her father was in prison at the time for refusing to renounce his Catholic faith. Her parents taught Agatha to read and write, and her mother trained her to become an expert in needlework.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When she was young, Agatha took "a private vow of virginity".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (Her patron was <a href="/wiki/Agatha_of_Sicily" title="Agatha of Sicily">Agatha of Sicily</a>, who was martyred in the 3rd century.) Her parents, however, had betrothed Agatha to a man while she was a child, according to custom, but they agreed to annul the marriage when she was 18 because she had consecrated her life to God.  A priest, Matthew Liu, advised Agatha's father to send her to <a href="/wiki/Guiyang" title="Guiyang">Guiyang</a>, the capital of Guizhou Province, to study at the school for girls founded and run by the consecrated virgin Annie Yuan of <a href="/wiki/Sichuan" title="Sichuan">Sichuan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Two months later, the school was closed due to persecution, and Agatha went to Longping, near Zunyi, with the school's head teacher and studied there for two years.  She returned home to discover that her father had been re-arrested, and she stayed to care for her aging mother.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_work">Religious work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_Lin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Religious work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agatha made her vows when she was 25. After the death of her father, she and her mother moved to the district of <a href="/wiki/Zhengding_County" title="Zhengding County">Zhenning</a>. There she converted people to Christianity.  She moved to <a href="/wiki/Guiyang" title="Guiyang">Guiyang</a> to become the director of a house of formation for consecrated virgins under Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Etienne_Albrand&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Etienne Albrand (page does not exist)">Etienne Albrand</a>. Her father bequeathed her enough money that she was able to buy a house near Xinggi, which served as a chapel and school.  Under the direction of the priest Paul Perny, she moved to Maokou, a <a href="/wiki/Miao_people" title="Miao people">Miao</a> village in the Langdai district, to teach women.  She was one of the first to evangelize the Miao people.  After two years, her pupils were baptized.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_canonization">Death and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agatha_Lin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1858 the village where Agatha was living was raided. She was arrested alongside another catechist, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jerome_Lu_Tingmei&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jerome Lu Tingmei (page does not exist)">Jerome Lu Tingmei</a>. Both were condemned to death, "after they had been denounced as Christians".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They along with <a href="/w/index.php?title=Laurence_Wang_Bing&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Laurence Wang Bing (page does not exist)">Laurence Wang Bing</a>, a successful farmer, were beheaded on January 28, 1858. She was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> in 1909, and was canonized in 1984.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is February 18.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agatha Lin Zhao is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">honored</a> (with <a href="/wiki/Agnes_Tsao_Kou_Ying" title="Agnes Tsao Kou Ying">Agnes Tsao Kou Ying</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lucy_Yi_Zhenmei" title="Lucy Yi Zhenmei">Lucy Yi Zhenmei</a>) with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Feasts_and_Fasts" class="mw-redirect" title="Lesser Feasts and Fasts">Lesser Feast</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">liturgical calendar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church in the United States of America</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> on February 19.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
