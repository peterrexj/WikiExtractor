<p><b>Marius Aventicensis</b> or, popularly, <b>Marius of Avenches</b> (532 &#8211; 31 December 596) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Avenches" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Avenches">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Aventicum" title="Aventicum">Aventicum</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Avenches" title="Avenches">Avenches</a>) from 574, remembered for his terse <a href="/wiki/Chronicle" title="Chronicle">chronicle</a>. After his death in <a href="/wiki/Lausanne" title="Lausanne">Lausanne</a>, he was venerated in that city as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was celebrated on 9 or 12 February.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marius_Aventicensis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>What is known of him, aside from his chronicle, is from the inscription on his tomb in the church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Thyrsus" title="Saint Thyrsus">Saint Thyrsus</a> in Lausanne<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He came of a distinguished, rich family, probably <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> in their culture. In 574 he was made Bishop of <i>Aventicum</i>, took part in the Second Council of <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A2con" title="Mâcon">Mâcon</a> in 585, and shortly afterwards transferred his episcopal see from Aventicum, which was rapidly declining, to <a href="/wiki/Lausanne" title="Lausanne">Lausanne</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>His metrical tomb inscription of unknown date, published in <i><a href="/wiki/Gallia_Christiana" title="Gallia Christiana">Gallia Christiana</a></i>,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> extols him as an ideal bishop; as a skilled goldsmith who made the sacred liturgical vessels with his own hands; as a protector and benefactor of the poor who ploughed his own land; as a man of prayer, and as a scholar. In 587 he consecrated a <a href="/wiki/Proprietary_church" title="Proprietary church">proprietary church</a> built at his expense on property of his own at Paterniacum (<a href="/wiki/Payerne" title="Payerne">Payerne</a>). The church of Saint Thyrsus was rededicated at an early date to Saint Marius.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Chronicle_writer">Chronicle writer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marius_Aventicensis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Chronicle writer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His brief chronicle is a continuation of the <i>Chronicon Imperiale</i> usually said to be the chronicle of <a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a>. Considering himself a Roman, Marius dated the years by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_consul" title="Roman consul">consuls</a> and the emperors of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Empire">Eastern Roman Empire</a>. It covers the years from 455 to 581, and is a valuable source for <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconian</a> history, especially for the second half of the 6th century, "and serves to correct the bias of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arians</a> of Burgundy"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Marius is the first to use the term <i>variola</i> (<a href="/wiki/Smallpox" title="Smallpox">smallpox</a>) to describe an epidemic that afflicted Gaul and Italy in 570.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The chronicle has been frequently published: first by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre-Fran%C3%A7ois_Chifflet&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pierre-François Chifflet (page does not exist)">Pierre-François Chifflet</a> in <a href="/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Duchesne" title="André Duchesne">André Duchesne</a>'s <i>Historiæ Francorum Scriptores</i>, I (1636), 210–214; again by <a href="/wiki/Migne" class="mw-redirect" title="Migne">Migne</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologia Latina</a></i>, LXXII, 793–802; by <a href="/wiki/Theodor_Mommsen" title="Theodor Mommsen">Theodor Mommsen</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/Monumenta_Germaniae_Historica" title="Monumenta Germaniae Historica">Monumenta Germaniae Historica</a>, Auctores antiqui</i>, XI (1893), 232–9; and by Justin Favrod with a French translation, <i>La chronique de Marius d'Avenches (455–581)</i> (Lausanne 1991).
</p>
