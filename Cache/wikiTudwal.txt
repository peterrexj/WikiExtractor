<p><b>Saint Tudwal</b> (died c. 564), also known as <b>Tual</b>, <b>Tudgual</b>, <b>Tugdual</b>, <b>Tugual</b>, <b>Pabu</b>, <b>Papu</b>, or <b>Tugdualus</b> (Latin), was a <a href="/wiki/Breton_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton people">Breton</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, considered to be one of the <a href="/wiki/Brittany#Religion" title="Brittany">seven founder saints of Brittany</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tudwal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tudwal was said to be the son of <a href="/wiki/Hoel" class="mw-redirect" title="Hoel">Hoel Mawr</a> (Hoel I) and his wife, <a href="/wiki/Pompeia_of_Langoat" title="Pompeia of Langoat">Pompeia</a>, and a brother of Saint Lenorius.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Tudwal travelled to <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> to learn the <a href="/wiki/Scriptures" class="mw-redirect" title="Scriptures">scriptures</a>, and then became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> on <a href="/wiki/Saint_Tudwal%27s_Island_East" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Tudwal&#39;s Island East">Saint Tudwal's Island East</a>, off the coast of <a href="/wiki/North_Wales" title="North Wales">North Wales</a>. Tudwal later immigrated to Brittany, settling in Lan Pabu with 72 followers, where he established a large <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> under the patronage of his cousin, King Deroch of <a href="/wiki/Domnonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Domnonia">Domnonée</a>. He traveled to Paris to obtain confirmation of the land grant from King <a href="/wiki/Childebert_I" title="Childebert I">Childebert I</a>, who insisted be was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tr%C3%A9guier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tréguier">Bishop of Tréguier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tudwal is shown in <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> as a bishop holding a <a href="/wiki/Dragon" title="Dragon">dragon</a>, now the symbol of <a href="/wiki/Tregor" class="mw-redirect" title="Tregor">Tregor</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 30 November or 1 December.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Tro_Breizh" title="Tro Breizh">Tro Breizh</a> (Breton for "Tour of Brittany") is a pilgrimage that links the towns of the seven founding saints of Brittany. These seven saints were Celtic monks from Britain from around the 5th or 6th century who went to Brittany to minister to the Britons who had settled there after the Anglo-Saxon incursions in their homeland.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Among the first bishoprics was <a href="/wiki/Tr%C3%A9guier" title="Tréguier">Tréguier</a>, Saint Tudwal's town.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notable_namesake">Notable namesake</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tudwal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Notable namesake">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Tugdual_Menon" title="Tugdual Menon">Tugdual Menon</a></li></ul>
<p>Joseph Tidwell. 15th century monk of the Franciscan order. Noted for being a shepherd and roving the coastline to help travellers stranded by shifting sands. Despite his vows, it is implied in local burial records that he fathered 9 children from 4 different women including his cousin Mary
</p>
