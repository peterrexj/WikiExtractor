<p><b>Paul Aurelian</b> (known in <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a> as <b>Paol Aorelian</b> or <b>Saint Pol de Léon</b> and in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> as <b>Paulinus Aurelianus</b>) was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welshman</a> who became first bishop of the <a href="/wiki/See_of_L%C3%A9on" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Léon">See of Léon</a> and one of the <a href="/wiki/Seven_founder_saints_of_Brittany" class="mw-redirect" title="Seven founder saints of Brittany">seven founder saints of Brittany</a>. He allegedly died in 575, rumoured to have lived to the age of 140, after having been assisted in his labors by three successive coadjutors. This suggests that several Pauls have been conflated.  <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Hunter_Doble" title="Gilbert Hunter Doble">Gilbert Hunter Doble</a> thought that he might have been Saint Paulinus of Wales.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_Aurelian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographic</a> <i>Life</i>, completed in 884 by a Breton monk named Wrmonoc of <a href="/wiki/Land%C3%A9vennec" title="Landévennec">Landévennec Abbey</a>, Paul was the son of a Welsh chieftain named Perphirius/Porphyrius ("clad in purple"), from <a href="/wiki/Penychen" title="Penychen">Penychen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Glamorgan" title="Glamorgan">Glamorgan</a>. He was later given three saintly sister-martyrs; <a href="/wiki/Juthwara" title="Juthwara">Juthwara</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sativola" class="mw-redirect" title="Sativola">Sidwell</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gulval#Saint_Gulval" title="Gulval">Wulvela</a>.
</p><p>It was also suggested that he may have been related to Ambrosius Aurelianus, both of them possibly active in Brittany at some points of their lives. Occurring at a time of Saxon raids on the British Isles, it supports the idea of an organized migration of the local Brythonic population under the rules of leaders belonging to the clergy and to the local nobility.
</p><p>In the <i>Life of <a href="/wiki/Cadoc" title="Cadoc">Cadoc</a></i> the princely founder of <a href="/wiki/Llancarfan" title="Llancarfan">Llancarfan</a> is reckoned the son of <a href="/wiki/Gwynllyw" title="Gwynllyw">Gwynllyw</a>, eponymous founder of the <a href="/wiki/Cantref" title="Cantref">cantref</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gwynllwg" title="Gwynllwg">Gwynllwg</a> and the son of <a href="/wiki/Glywys" title="Glywys">Glywys</a>. Medieval sources give Gwynllyw a brother, called "Pawl", who is chief of neighbouring Penychen.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_Aurelian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg/200px-Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="279" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg/300px-Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg/400px-Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1350" data-file-height="1883" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Thegonnec_-_St_Pol.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Pol, as represented in the parish church of Saint-Thégonnec, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Paul first was a pupil of Saint <a href="/wiki/Illtud" title="Illtud">Illtud</a> at <a href="/wiki/Llantwit_Major" title="Llantwit Major">Llantwit Major</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Later, he studied on <a href="/wiki/Caldey_Island" title="Caldey Island">Caldey Island</a> with <a href="/wiki/Samson_of_Dol" title="Samson of Dol">Samson of Dol</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gildas" title="Gildas">Gildas</a>.
</p><p>He went to Brittany, establishing <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a> in <a href="/wiki/Finist%C3%A8re" title="Finistère">Finistère</a> on the northwest coast of Brittany, at Lampaul on the island of <a href="/wiki/Ushant" title="Ushant">Ushant</a> (French <i>Ouessant</i>), on the island of <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Ele_de_Batz" title="Île de Batz">Batz</a> and at Ocsimor, now the city of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Pol-de-L%C3%A9on" title="Saint-Pol-de-Léon">Saint-Pol-de-Léon</a>, where he is said to have founded a monastery in an abandoned fort. He was consecrated bishop at Ocsimor under the authority of <a href="/wiki/Childebert_I" title="Childebert I">Childebert</a>, King of the Franks.
</p><p>Paul was a <a href="/wiki/Christian_vegetarianism" title="Christian vegetarianism">vegetarian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> One account says he died on the island of <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Ele_de_Batz" title="Île de Batz">Batz</a>. He was first buried at Saint-Pol-de-Léon, but his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were later transferred to <a href="/wiki/Fleury_Abbey" title="Fleury Abbey">Fleury Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was succeeded as bishop by his companion, Saint <a href="/wiki/Joavan" title="Joavan">Joavan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
Paul's bell is kept at Saint-Pol. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 12 March.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Paulinus_of_Wales">Paulinus of Wales</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_Aurelian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Paulinus of Wales">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/G._H._Doble" class="mw-redirect" title="G. H. Doble">G. H. Doble</a> thought Saint Paul Aurelian might be the same as <b>Saint Paulinus of Wales</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> revered in <a href="/wiki/Carmarthenshire" title="Carmarthenshire">Carmarthenshire</a>, southwest <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and teacher at a place usually identified as <a href="/wiki/Whitland" title="Whitland">Whitland</a>. Hywel David Emanuel considered the identification of Paul Aurelian with the Carmarthenshire Paulinus as doubtful.<sup id="cite_ref-hywel_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hywel-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Rhigyfarch" class="mw-redirect" title="Rhigyfarch">Rhigyfarch</a>'s  <i>Life of S. David</i> (chapter x), <a href="/wiki/Saint_David" title="Saint David">Saint David</a> is stated to have completed his education under S. Paulinus (Paulens), who is described as a "scribe, a disciple of S. Germanus the bishop". When Paulinus  became blind, David is said to have miraculously restored his sight.<sup id="cite_ref-hywel_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hywel-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paulinus of Wales founded churches and chapels around <a href="/wiki/Llandovery" title="Llandovery">Llandovery</a>. He is said to have taught <a href="/wiki/Teilo" class="mw-redirect" title="Teilo">Saint Teilo</a> and to have nominated David to speak at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Brefi" title="Synod of Brefi">Synod of Llanddewi Brefi</a> (in around 545). Claims to having founded the church at <a href="/wiki/Paul,_Cornwall" title="Paul, Cornwall">Paul</a> are dubious.
</p><p>A 6th century inscribed stone found at <a href="/wiki/Caeo" class="mw-redirect" title="Caeo">Caeo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Carmarthenshire" title="Carmarthenshire">Carmarthenshire</a>, now in the Carmarthen Museum, appears to honour him as "preserver of the faith, constant lover of his country, champion of righteousness". His feast day is 23 November.<sup id="cite_ref-hywel_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hywel-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
