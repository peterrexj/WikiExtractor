					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>William of Fenoli</b><sup id="cite_ref-william_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-william-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
(d. <i>circa</i> 1205) was a monk of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> Order,<sup id="cite_ref-carthusian_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-carthusian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who
lived in the late 12th - early 13th Century. He was a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay-brother</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Charterhouse</a> of Casularum in Lombardy, where he managed the monastery's external affairs.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> annals, "He was untutored in theology, in philosophy and in worldly knowledge, but in spiritual life and good works he was most learned. His holiness was made known by very many miracles both during his life and after his death".<sup id="cite_ref-ocart_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocart-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> For this, and accounts of other miracles performed at his intercession, William of Fenoli was <i><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>, who approved his <i>cultus</i> (limited, local veneration) in 1860.<sup id="cite_ref-analecta_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-analecta-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is chiefly known for the following miracle. One day, when he was
returning from his field work, he was set upon by robbers<sup id="cite_ref-miracle_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miracle-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
and defended himself by tearing the leg off his donkey, using this as a cudgel to drive off his attackers. Afterwards, he miraculously healed the donkey, restoring its leg. Blessed William is therefore often depicted in iconography with his donkey, brandishing 
the donkey's leg.<sup id="cite_ref-museum_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-museum-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast day falls on 19 December,<sup id="cite_ref-feast_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-feast-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> according to the Roman calendar, but on 16 December<sup id="cite_ref-william_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-william-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> calendar.
</p>
