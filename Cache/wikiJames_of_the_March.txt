<p><b>Jacob de Marchia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Jacobus de Marchia</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Giacomo della Marca</i>; c. 1391 – 28 November 1476), commonly known<sup id="ref_a_1" class="reference"><a href="#cnote_a">[a]</a></sup> in English as <b>Saint James of the Marches</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Friar Minor">Friar Minor</a>, preacher and writer.<sup id="cite_ref-oliger_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oliger-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a Papal legate and <a href="/wiki/Inquisitor" title="Inquisitor">Inquisitor</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_the_Marches&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <b>Dominic Gangala</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Domenico Gangala</i>) in the early 1390s to a poor family in <a href="/wiki/Monteprandone" title="Monteprandone">Monteprandone</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Ancona" title="March of Ancona">March of Ancona</a> (now in <a href="/wiki/Ascoli_Piceno" title="Ascoli Piceno">Ascoli Piceno</a>) in central Italy along the Adriatic Sea. As a child, he began his studies at <a href="/wiki/Offida" title="Offida">Offida</a> under the guidance of his uncle, a priest, who soon afterwards sent him to school in the nearby city of <a href="/wiki/Ascoli_Piceno" title="Ascoli Piceno">Ascoli Piceno</a>. He later studied at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Perugia" title="University of Perugia">University of Perugia</a> where he took the degree of Doctor in Canon and <a href="/wiki/Civil_law_(common_law)" title="Civil law (common law)">Civil Law</a>. After a short stay at <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> as <a href="/wiki/Tutor" class="mw-redirect" title="Tutor">tutor</a> for a noble family, and as judge of <a href="/wiki/Magician_(paranormal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Magician (paranormal)">sorcerers</a>, he was received into the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Portiuncula" title="Portiuncula">Portiuncula</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>, on 26 July 1416. At that time, he took the monastic name Jacobus (Jacob, Jacopo; rendered James in English). Having finished his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Eremo_delle_Carceri" title="Eremo delle Carceri">Carceri</a>, near Assisi, he studied <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at <a href="/wiki/Fiesole" title="Fiesole">Fiesole</a>, near Florence, with <a href="/wiki/John_of_Capistrano" title="John of Capistrano">John of Capistrano</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-foley_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> under <a href="/wiki/Bernardine_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernardine of Siena">Bernardine of Siena</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oliger_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oliger-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He began a very austere life fasting nine months of the year. Bernardine told him to moderate his penances.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priest_and_inquisitor">Priest and inquisitor</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_the_Marches&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priest and inquisitor">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 13 June 1420, he was ordained a priest and soon began to preach in <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Marches" class="mw-redirect" title="Marches">Marches</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>; for half a century he carried on his spiritual labours, remarkable for the miracles he performed and the numerous conversions he wrought. He helped spread devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Name_of_Jesus" title="Holy Name of Jesus">Holy Name of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> From 1427, James preached penance, combated heretics, and was on <a href="/wiki/Legation" title="Legation">legations</a> in Germany, Austria, Sweden, Denmark, Bohemia, Poland, Hungary, and Bosnia. He was also appointed inquisitor against the <a href="/wiki/Fraticelli" title="Fraticelli">Fraticelli</a>, a heretic sect that dissented from the Franciscans on the vow of poverty, among other things.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was sent by the Papal Council as an <a href="/wiki/Inquisitor" title="Inquisitor">Inquisitor</a><sup id="cite_ref-Runciman1947_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Runciman1947-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> to <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bosnia" title="Kingdom of Bosnia">Bosnia</a> in 1432–33, working in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bosnia" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Bosnia">Bosnian Vicariate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Fine2007_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fine2007-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He returned in 1435 and served as Vicar of Bosnia until 1439.<sup id="cite_ref-Fine2007_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fine2007-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He combated the heresies that he found there, which earned him the hostility of its ruler, <a href="/wiki/Tvrtko_II_of_Bosnia" title="Tvrtko II of Bosnia">King Tvrtko II</a>, and even more of his wife, <a href="/wiki/Dorothy_Garai" title="Dorothy Garai">Queen Dorothea</a>, whom James accused of trying to poison him.<sup id="cite_ref-Fine_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fine-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He left Bosnia citing King <a href="/wiki/Tvrtko_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Tvrtko I">Tvrtko I</a> as the cause of failure of Franciscan mission.<sup id="cite_ref-Runciman1947_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Runciman1947-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Between 1434 and 1439 he worked in Southern Hungary against heretics.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In August, 1439, he imprisoned <a href="/w/index.php?title=B%C3%A1lint_%C3%9Ajlaki&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bálint Újlaki (page does not exist)">Bálint Újlaki</a>, who first translated the Bible into Hungarian (<a href="/wiki/Hussite_Bible" title="Hussite Bible">Hussite Bible</a>).
</p><p>At the time of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Basle" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Basle">Council of Basle</a>, James promoted the reunion of the moderate <a href="/wiki/Hussites" title="Hussites">Hussites</a> with the Catholic Church, and later that of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Ferrara-Florence" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Ferrara-Florence">Council of Ferrara-Florence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oliger_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oliger-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Against the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottomans</a>, he preached several <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusades</a>, and at the death of <a href="/wiki/John_Capistran" class="mw-redirect" title="John Capistran">John Capistran</a>, in 1456, James was sent to <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Hungary</a> as his successor.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1457 he was sent to Danish king <a href="/wiki/Christian_I_of_Denmark" title="Christian I of Denmark">Christian I</a> to discuss the Turkish crusade and also the Bohemian issue.<sup id="cite_ref-Jensen2007_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jensen2007-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He instituted several <a href="/wiki/Montes_pietatis" class="mw-redirect" title="Montes pietatis">montes pietatis</a> (literally, "mountains of piety": nonprofit credit organizations that lent money at very low rates on pawned objects), and preached in all the greater cities.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was offered the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">bishopric</a> of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in 1460, which he declined.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>James belonged to the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Observant</a> branch of the Friars Minor, then rapidly spreading and stirring up much controversy. In this task, he encouraged reforms in the Order of Friars Minor. How much he suffered on this account is shown in a letter written by him to <a href="/wiki/John_Capistran" class="mw-redirect" title="John Capistran">John Capistran</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> King Tvrtko II was a major opponent of James's reforms in Bosnia, and was probably strongly influenced in that regard by Queen Dorothy.<sup id="cite_ref-Fine_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fine-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Under Pope <a href="/wiki/Callistus_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Callistus III">Callistus III</a>, in 1455, he was appointed an arbiter on the questions at issue between the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Conventuals" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Friars Minor Conventuals">Conventuals</a> and Observants. His decision was published 2 February 1456 in a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a>, which pleased neither part. A few years later, on <a href="/wiki/Easter_Monday" title="Easter Monday">Easter Monday</a> 1462, James, preaching at <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>, uttered the opinion of some theologians that the <a href="/wiki/Precious_Blood" class="mw-redirect" title="Precious Blood">Precious Blood</a> shed during the <a href="/wiki/Passion_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion (Christianity)">Passion</a> was not united with the <a href="/wiki/Divinity_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Divinity of Christ">Divinity of Christ</a> during the three days of his burial. The <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> friar, <a href="/wiki/James_of_Brescia" title="James of Brescia">James of Brescia</a>, the local <a href="/wiki/Inquisitor" title="Inquisitor">inquisitor</a>, immediately summoned him to his tribunal. James refused to appear, and after some troubles appealed to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. The question was discussed at Rome during Christmas 1462 (not 1463, as some have it), before Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius II">Pius II</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">cardinals</a>, but no decision was ever given.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> James spent the last three years of his life in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, and died there on 28 November 1476.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_the_Marches&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giacomo_-_Confessione,_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Giacomo_-_Confessione%2C_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg/220px-Giacomo_-_Confessione%2C_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="297" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Giacomo_-_Confessione%2C_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg/330px-Giacomo_-_Confessione%2C_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Giacomo_-_Confessione%2C_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg/440px-Giacomo_-_Confessione%2C_circa_1476_-_513846.jpg 2x" data-file-width="826" data-file-height="1114" /></a><figcaption><i>Confessione</i>, 1476</figcaption></figure>
<p>His writings have not yet been collected. His library and <a href="/wiki/Autograph" title="Autograph">autographs</a> are preserved in part at the Municipio of Monteprandone (see Crivellucci, "I codici della libreria raccolta da S. Giacomo della Marca nel convento di S. Maria delle Grazie presso Monteprandone", Leghorn, 1889).
</p><p>He wrote "Dialogus contra Fraticellos" printed in Baluze-Mansi, "Miscellanea", II, Lucca, 1761, 595-610 (cf. Ehrle in "Archiv für Litt. u. Kirchengeschichte", IV, Freiburg im Br., 1888, 107–10).
</p><p>His numerous sermons are not edited. For some of them, and for his treatise on the "Miracles of the Name of Jesus", see Candido Mariotti, O.F.M., "Nome di Gesù ed i Francescani", Fano, 1909, 125–34.
</p><p>On his notebook, or "Itinerarium", See Luigi Tasso, O.F.M., in "Miscellanea Francescana", I (1886), 125-26: "Regula confitendi peccata" was several times edited in Latin and Italian during the fifteenth century. "De Sanguine Christi effuse" and some other treatises remained in manuscript.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_the_Marches&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James was buried in Naples in the Franciscan church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_La_Nova,_Naples" title="Santa Maria La Nova, Naples">Santa Maria la Nova</a>, where his body remained until 2001. At the instigation of the <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">provincial minister</a> (Franciscan superior) of the Marches region, Ferdinando Campana, James's body was relocated to <a href="/wiki/Monteprandone" title="Monteprandone">Monteprandone</a>, where it remains incorrupt and visible to the public today. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Urban_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Urban VIII">Urban VIII</a> in 1624, and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> in 1726. Naples <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerates</a> him as one of its <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints_of_Naples" title="Patron saints of Naples">patron saints</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Liturgical" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgical">liturgical</a> <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is observed by the Franciscan Order on 28 November. He is generally represented holding in his right hand a <a href="/wiki/Chalice_(cup)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chalice (cup)">chalice</a>, out of which a snake is escaping – an allusion to some endeavours of <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretics</a> to poison him or, less likely, to the controversy about the Precious Blood.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Annotations">Annotations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_the_Marches&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Annotations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div style=""><ol class="references" style="list-style-type:upper-alpha;">
<li id="cnote_a"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#ref_a_1">^</a></b></span> <div style="display:inline;" class="reference-text">In English he is known as <i>James of the Marches</i> or <i>James della Marca</i>. He is also known by his surname and monastic given name as <i>Jacob/Jacopo/James Gangala</i>. Another name is <i>Dominic/Domenico Gangala</i>. "James" is a variant of both Jacobus and Giacomo. He is also known as <i>Jacobus Picenus</i> (after his birth place).</div></li>
</ol></div>
