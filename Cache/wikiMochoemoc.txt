<p>Saint <b>Mochoemoc</b> (or <b>Pulcherius</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup>, c. 550–656) was an early Irish abbot, later considered to have been a saint.
He was a nephew of Saint <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Dte_of_Killeedy" title="Íte of Killeedy">Íte of Killeedy</a>, who raised him. He became a monk in <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">Bangor Abbey</a> under the abbot Saint <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">Comgall</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a>. 
He was the founding abbot of Liath-Mochoemoc (<a href="/wiki/Liathmore_Churches" title="Liathmore Churches">Liathmore</a>) monastery.
His feast day is 13 March.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Mochoemoc_CatholicSaints_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Mochoemoc_CatholicSaints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Saint Mochoemoc or Pulcherius was born about 550&#160;AD.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513-9">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
His parents were a craftsman named Beoanus and Nesse, sister of Saint <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Dte_of_Killeedy" title="Íte of Killeedy">Íte of Killeedy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEColgan1849363_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEColgan1849363-10">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
His father was born in <a href="/wiki/Connemara" title="Connemara">Connemara</a> in Connaught, and settled in Hui Conaill Gabhra in the south of <a href="/wiki/County_Limerick" title="County Limerick">County Limerick</a> near <a href="/wiki/Killeedy" title="Killeedy">Killeedy</a>, where Saint Ita lived.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
It is said that Saint <a href="/wiki/Fachtna_of_Ross_Ailither" class="mw-redirect" title="Fachtna of Ross Ailither">Fachtna of Ross Ailither</a> was cured of an affection of his eyes by bathing them in the milk of Mochaemog's mother.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup>
He was brought up by Saint Ite, then at the age of 20 was sent to  <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">Bangor Abbey</a> where he was further instructed by the abbot Saint <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">Comgall</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513-9">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mochoemoc was sent out by Comgall as a missionary accompanied by the saints <a href="/w/index.php?title=Laichtin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Laichtin (page does not exist)">Laichtin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mo_Lua_of_Killaloe" title="Mo Lua of Killaloe">Molua Mac Ochai</a> (a Findbarr) and <a href="/wiki/Luchtigern" title="Luchtigern">Luchtigern</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189490_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189490-13">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
He built cells for his monks at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Anatrim&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Anatrim (page does not exist)">Anatrim</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMonks_of_Ramsgate1921196_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMonks_of_Ramsgate1921196-14">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
He reached southern <a href="/wiki/%C3%89ile" title="Éile">Éile</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>, where a chieftain granted him a site for a monastery in the forest near Lake Lurgan, since known as Liathmochaemog (<a href="/wiki/Liathmore" class="mw-redirect" title="Liathmore">Liathmore</a>) in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Two-Mile_Borris" title="Two-Mile Borris">Two-Mile Borris</a>, Barony of <a href="/wiki/Eliogarty" title="Eliogarty">Eliogarty</a>.
He quarrelled with <a href="/wiki/Fa%C3%ADlbe_Flann_mac_%C3%81edo_Duib" title="Faílbe Flann mac Áedo Duib">Faílbe Flann mac Áedo Duib</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> (619-634), but all the saints of Ireland appeared to the king in visions and forced him to treat Mochaemog with respect.
Failbhe was succeeded by Ronan, son of Bledin, who was hostile to Mochaemog but renewed his grant.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint <a href="/wiki/Dag%C3%A1n" title="Dagán">Dagán</a> was brought to the school of Machoemoc at Liathmore when he was very young.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873316_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873316-15">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
There is a legend that while Dagán was still a boy, some raiders from <a href="/wiki/Osraige" title="Osraige">Osraige</a> killed him.
His decapitated body and his head were taken to Pulcherius, who had promised to give the boy <a href="/wiki/Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Communion">Holy Communion</a> before he died.
Saint <a href="/wiki/Cainnech_of_Aghaboe" title="Cainnech of Aghaboe">Cainnech of Aghaboe</a>, who was present, placed the head in its position on the body and prayed to Christ, who restored life to Dagán.
Pulcherius gave him Holy Communion, and Dagán lived for many more years as head of a large monastery in Inverdaoile.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873317_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873317-16">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
Dagan of Inverdaoile was known as a violent opponent of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Easter" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Easter">Roman Easter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189490_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189490-13">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mochaemog was a friend of Saint <a href="/wiki/Colm%C3%A1n_of_Dromore" title="Colmán of Dromore">Colmán of Dromore</a>, whose monastery was just 4 miles (6.4&#160;km) away, and of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fursey" title="Saint Fursey">Saint Fursey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
There is a story that the saints Pulcherius, Canice, <a href="/wiki/Mo_Lua_of_Killaloe" title="Mo Lua of Killaloe">Molua</a> and Mofecta or Feachtna<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> visited a monk named Mochumb at the church of Tifeachna<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;f&#93;</a></sup>
The saints stayed there for a while, then before leaving each placed a stone, one above the other, to commemorate the visit.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShearman1879236_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShearman1879236-21">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
<a href="/wiki/John_Francis_Shearman" title="John Francis Shearman">John Francis Shearman</a> (1879) wrote that "Thirty years ago there was at Tifeachna, on the western side of the churchyard, a pyramidical-shaped monument, built of small truncated codes, placed loosely one on top of the other, they are probably the memorials ferrred to in the aforesaid chapter."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShearman1879236_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShearman1879236-21">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pulcherius was said to have lived to a great age, and died in Liathmore on 13 of March 656.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885515_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885515-22">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
There is a church named after Mochaemog in the <a href="/wiki/Ida_(barony)" title="Ida (barony)">Barony of Ida</a>, County Kilkenny.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189490_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189490-13">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg/220px-Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="277" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg/330px-Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg/440px-Ballylooby_Church_of_Our_Lady_and_St._Kieran_North_Transept_East_Window_Detail_Saint_Ita_2012_09_08.jpg 2x" data-file-width="625" data-file-height="788" /></a><figcaption>Saint Ita, window in the Church of Our Lady and St. Kieran in <a href="/wiki/Ballylooby" title="Ballylooby">Ballylooby</a>, County Tipperary</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/John_Colgan" title="John Colgan">John Colgan</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum_Hiberniae" title="Acta Sanctorum Hiberniae">Acta Sanctorum Hiberniae</a></i> (1645) gives an account of the birth of Pulcherius,
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>His father's name was Beoanus&#160;; he was a skilful artificer, and of an honourable family in Connaught&#160;; but being compelled to fly into exile, he came into the neighbourhood of St. Ita. She, hearing of his professional skill, and being anxious to make some addition to the buildings of her convent, requested him to undertake the work. He consented, on the conditions of receiving Nesse, the sister of the saint, as his wife, and also some land on which to settle. St. Ita acquiesced in the proposition, and gave him her sister Ness to wife; and he, with great assiduity, applied himself to erect the buildings in the monastery of the saint. It happened, after a time, that in battle, whither he had followed a certain chieftain, Beoanus was killed; and his head, being cut off, was carried away a great distance. St. Ita was, of course, very much grieved at this occurrence, particularly as she had promised her brother in-law that he would have a son, which promise was unfulfilled, as his wife had been sterile up to this time. St. Its went to the field of battle, and found the mutilated body of Beoanus, but, of course, without the head. She, however, prayed that it might be shown to her, and the bead, through the divine power, flew through the air, and stopped where the body lay before her; and the Lord, at the entreaty of his handmaid, made the head adhere to the body as perfectly as if had never been cut off, except that a slight mark of the wound remained&#160;; and the space of one hour having passed, he rose alive, saluting the servant of the Lord, and returning thanks to God. After the return of Beoanus, his wife conceived, and she brought forth a son, as St. Its had promised. This son was Pulcherius, and he remained with the saint until he reached his twentieth year.—Colgans Acta Sanctoran, p. 68.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEColgan1849363_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEColgan1849363-10">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mochoemoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> in <i> The Lives of the Irish Saints</i> (1823),
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Saint Mochoemoc, in Latin, Pulcherius, Abbot.
Having been educated under Saint Comgal, in the monastery of Benchor, he laid the foundation of the great monastery of Liath-Mochoemoc, around which a large town was raised, which still bears that name. His happy death is placed by the chronologists on the 13th of March in 655. See Usher's antiquity, in Tab .Chron. and Colgan.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler182319_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler182319-23">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mochoemoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2><p>
The Monks of Ramsgate wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921),<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Mochaemhog (Mochcemoc, Vulcanius, Pulcherius) (St.) Abbot. (March 13) (7th cent.) He was born in Munster and brought up by his aunt, Saint Ita. He then became a disciple of Saint Comgall. Later he built cells for his own monks at Anatrim, and finally established himself as head of a community at Liath. Great miracles are recorded of him, especially his having raised the dead to life. The precise year of his death is uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMonks_of_Ramsgate1921196_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMonks_of_Ramsgate1921196-14">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Walsh_and_Conyngham_account">Walsh and Conyngham account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mochoemoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Walsh and Conyngham account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Walsh and David Power Conyngham give a more extensive account in their <i>Ecclesiastical History of Ireland</i> (1885).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513ff_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513ff-24">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mosaic,_Bangor_harbour_(2)_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Mosaic%2C_Bangor_harbour_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg/220px-Mosaic%2C_Bangor_harbour_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Mosaic%2C_Bangor_harbour_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg/330px-Mosaic%2C_Bangor_harbour_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Mosaic%2C_Bangor_harbour_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg/440px-Mosaic%2C_Bangor_harbour_%282%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_344038.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="426" /></a><figcaption>Mosaic from Bangor depicting Saint <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">Comgall</a> and other monks</figcaption></figure><p>Leathmore. St. Pulcherius, or Mochoemoc flourished, in the sixth and seventh centuries. He was the son of Bocan, a native of Coumacne, in Connaught, (Conmacne, of Galway,) who, having left his own country, settled in Hy-Conall-Gaura, in the west of the county Limerick, where Pulcherius was born, about the year 550. His mother's name was Nessa, of the Nandesi sept, and through her he was nephew to the celebrated St. Ita, called the Brigid of Munster, with whom he remained twenty years. Being well prepared for the ecclesiastical and monastic state, he went, with her consent and approbation to place himself under the guidance of St. Comgall, of Bangor. Qualified to preside over others, the holy abbot of Bangor advised him to found a religious establishment for himself, wherever the Almighty would direct.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513-9">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Having, in compliance with the instructions of Comgall, repaired from the austere retreat of Bangor to his own country, and having been introduced to the chieftain of Ely O'Carrol, who received him with attention, and generously offered his own residence, for the purpose of converting it into a monastery&#160;; he declined the offer of the prince, but Pulcherius accepted the grant of a lonesome spot in a thick forest, to which he gave the name of Leathemore.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513–514_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885513–514-25">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Here he spent some years shut out from the intercourse of the world, training up a numerous body of disciples in the duties and observances of a spiritual life. The prince, who was his benefactor, having died, Ronan, his successor, intended to expel the saint from his territory, and went with this resolve towards the monastery, having in his train a party of soldiers to execute his mischievous design. When he arrived near the monastery, Pulehecherins was celebrating the divine mysteries. Ronan, suddenly struck by the vengeance of heaven, could not stir from the spot on which he stood. Repenting his rashness, the prince sent word to Pulcherius, requesting that ho would come and relieve him from his situation. The message was not conveyed to Pulcherius until he had finished not only the sacrifice, but likewise Tierce, a part of the divine office. Pulcherius replied, that he would not go out of the monastery until after finishing another part of the office, called None&#160;; having done so, he visited Ronan, and having imparted his benediction, relieved the prince from the awkward position which his own temerity had procured.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514-26">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thenceforth a great friendship existed between them&#160;; and after Ronan's death, the saint fervently supplicated the father of mercy for the repose of his soul. At a later period, Failbhe Fiend, king of all Munster, being displeased with Pulcherius for preventing some horses of his to graze in the field belonging to his monastery, ordered the chieftain to expel him from that country. Pulcherius having proceeded to Cashel, remonstrated with the king on the injustice of his order&#160;; but the king, who received him in a very insulting manner, was immediately seized with violent pains in one of his eyes, and was deprived of its use. The courtiers having entreated St. Pulcherius to procure him some relief, he blessed water, which, on being applied to the eye, the pain ceased—the blindness still continuing. On the following night the king had a vision during his sleep, in which he thought he saw from his castle on the rock, the plains, north and south of the city, covered with all the saints of Ireland, and was told by a venerable-looking person that they had assembled in defence of Pulcherius, and that he and his posterity would be destroyed in case of not complying with the request of Pulcherius. Accordingly, the king on the next day sent for him, and granted what the saint required&#160;: henceforth Pulcherius was hold by the king in great veneration.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514-26">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Several miracles are attributed to Pulcherius, among others, his having cured of blindness a holy virgin Canners, daughter of Fintan, who is considered to have been the relative of St. Molua, of Clonfert Molua. The celebrated Dagan was, in his younger days, a disciple of St. Pulcherius, as was also Cuanchear, of whose history little is known.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514_26-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514-26">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Besides St Cainnech, Colman of Doire-more, Pulcheritts was intimate with St Molua of Clonfert-molua, St. Lacteau, of Achad-ur, a St. Finbhar, and St. Luchern, who had been his fellow-students at Bangor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514–515_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885514–515-27">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
Through the sanctity and labors of Pulcherius, a desolate and uninhabited forest became the abode of saints and scholars. Our saint died, having lived to a great age, on the 13th of March, A.D. 656.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885515_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalshConyngham1885515-22">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>

<li id="cite_note-12"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-12">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Mochaemog, his mother and his aunt were all credited with the ability to cure blindness.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStephenLee189489-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-18"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-18">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Fachtna of Iverk and Feachna, Sapiens, were either the same person or two contemporary monks in the community of Pulcherius, and were not the same as Bishop <a href="/wiki/Fachtna_of_Rosscarbery" title="Fachtna of Rosscarbery">Fachtnan of Ross</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShearman1879237_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShearman1879237-17">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-20"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-20">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Tifeaghna is a graveyard in <a href="/wiki/County_Kilkenny" title="County Kilkenny">County Kilkenny</a>. It is in the <a href="/wiki/Galmoy_(barony)" title="Galmoy (barony)">Barony of Galmoy</a>, the Parish of Sheffin and the Poor Law Union of <a href="/wiki/Urlingford" title="Urlingford">Urlingford</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECasteleynKirwan2019_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECasteleynKirwan2019-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> </span>
</li>
</ol></div></div>
