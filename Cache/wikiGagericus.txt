<p><b>Saint Gaugericus</b>, in <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> <b>Saint Géry</b> (also known as <b>Gorik, Gau</b>; in <a href="/wiki/Walloon_language" title="Walloon language">Walloon</a>, <b>Djèri</b>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 550 &#8211; August 11, 619) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cambrai">bishop of Cambrai</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaugericus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> parents, Gaudentius and Austadiola, at <i>Eposium</i> (present <a href="/wiki/Carignan,_Ardennes" title="Carignan, Ardennes">Carignan</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-van_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Tradition states that Bishop <a href="/wiki/Magnerich_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Magnerich of Trier">Magnerich</a>, successor of Saint Nicetas as Bishopr of Trier was so impressed with the piety of the young man that he ordained him deacon, but not before Gaugericus had memorized the entire psalter.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Magnerich entrusted Gaugericus with the pastoral care of the city of Cambrai.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop">Bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaugericus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When the see of Cambrai-Arras fell vacant around 585, Gaugericus was elected bishop with the consent of <a href="/wiki/Childebert_II" title="Childebert II">Childebert II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
Gaugericus was consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Egidius_(bishop_of_Reims)" title="Egidius (bishop of Reims)">Egidius, bishop of Reims</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Géry devoted himself to fighting paganism, ransoming captives and visiting rural districts and <i><a href="/wiki/Villa_rustica" title="Villa rustica">villae</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-van_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Gaugericus founded churches and abbeys, including a monastery dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Medardus" title="Medardus">St. Medard</a>, to host relics, which contributed powerfully to giving Cambrai both the appearance and functions of a city.
</p><p>He himself went on pilgrimage to the tomb of Saint Martin in Tours.  Géry also built a church dedicated to Saint Martin, where he had relics of this saint deposited. The bell tower of this church was to become, much later, the city's belfry. His work was crucial to the development of the city, particularly in terms of the number of buildings he left behind and the pilgrimages he attracted.
</p><p>Between 584 and 590, he transferred the episcopal see from Arras to Cambrai. He maintained close relations with King <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a>, the new lord of Cambrai after the death of Childebert, and assisted at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Paris_(614)" title="Council of Paris (614)">Council of Paris</a> in 614.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Traditions">Traditions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaugericus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Traditions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to tradition, around the year 580, Gaugericus built a chapel on the largest island in the <a href="/wiki/Zenne" class="mw-redirect" title="Zenne">Senne</a> near <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>. <a href="/wiki/Saint-G%C3%A9ry_Island" title="Saint-Géry Island">Saint-Géry Island</a> is named after the church.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A legend holds that Géry built a chapel (to Saint Michael, later the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._Michael_and_St._Gudula" title="Cathedral of St. Michael and St. Gudula">Cathedral of St. Michael and St. Gudula</a>), which soon became a church and gave birth to the city of Brussels, from which he had chased a dragon whose lair was located where the Impasse de la Poupéeimpasse du Dragon was later built.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaugericus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG/220px-Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG/330px-Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG/440px-Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG 2x" data-file-width="768" data-file-height="1024" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Eglise_St-G%C3%A9ry_Cambrai.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Géry church at <a href="/wiki/Cambrai" title="Cambrai">Cambrai</a></div></div></div>
<p>After serving as bishop for thirty-nine years, he died August 11, 619 and was buried in the church of Saint Médard, which he had founded at <a href="/wiki/Cambrai" title="Cambrai">Cambrai</a>. Veneration commenced immediately after his death.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>When the church of Saint Medard was demolished by the emperor Charles V for the building of the citadel, the canons were removed, and took with them the relics of the saint, to the old church of <a href="/wiki/Vedast" title="Vedast">Saint Vedast</a>, which from that time has borne the name of Saint Gery. The Church of Saint-Géry is one of the oldest in Cambrai, and a listed historical monument since 1919.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrology</a> of <a href="/wiki/Rabanus_Maurus" title="Rabanus Maurus">Rabanus Maurus</a> for August 11.<sup id="cite_ref-van_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Relics of the saint were given to Carignan, to the church of Saint-Géry in Valenciennes, to the abbey of Saint-Sépulcre in Cambrai, which became a cathedral after the destruction of the old one after the Revolution, to the abbey of Arras, to the abbey of Liessies, to the collegiate church of Saint-Pierre in Douai, to the church of Saint-Donat in Bruges, to the church of Saint-Géry in Bierne, and to the church of Saint-Géry in Brussels8. His reliquary is still on display in the south transept of the church of Saint-Géry in Cambrai.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaugericus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He is the patron saint of many churches in the regions of Cambrai, Bierne, Valenciennes and Arras, as well as in Belgium.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>During his wanderings he freed many prisoners, criminals, children taken into slavery. St-Géry is the patron of Cambrai, and of prisoners.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
