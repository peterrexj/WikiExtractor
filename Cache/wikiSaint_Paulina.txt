<p><b>Pauline of the Agonizing Heart of Jesus</b>, C.I.I.C. (December 16, 1865 – July 9, 1942), was an immigrant from <a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austria-Hungary</a> to Brazil, who became the foundress of the Congregation of the Little Sisters of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>, <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sisters</a> who serve the poor. She became the first female <a href="/wiki/Brazilian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Brazilian people">Brazilian</a> to be proclaimed a saint by the Catholic Church, when she was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 19 May 2002 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. Pauline suffered from <a href="/wiki/Diabetes" title="Diabetes">diabetes</a> for much of her life and is considered an "unofficial" <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of diabetics.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_of_the_Agonizing_Heart_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_of_the_Agonizing_Heart_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>She was born <b>Amabile Lucia Visintainer</b> on December 16, 1865, the second daughter of Antonio Napoleone Visintainer and Anna Pianezzer<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in the town of <a href="/wiki/Vigolo_Vattaro" title="Vigolo Vattaro">Vigolo Vattaro</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Tyrol" title="County of Tyrol">County of Tyrol</a>, part of <a href="/wiki/Austro-Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Austro-Hungary">Austro-Hungary</a>, now in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Her ancestors were Germanic, who had settled in the region of Vigolo Vattaro as early as 1491, their surname being originally spelled <b>Wiesenteiner</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Like many others in the area, the Visintainer family was very poor but practising Catholics.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In September 1875, the family, along with a hundred other people of the town, about a fifth of its population, emigrated to the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catarina_(state)" title="Santa Catarina (state)">State of Santa Catarina</a> in Brazil, where they founded the village of Vigolo, now part of <a href="/wiki/Nova_Trento" title="Nova Trento">Nova Trento</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was known even at a youthful age for her piety and charity. From an early age she spoke of giving her life to God. She had very little intellectual education, but great love for the Catholic faith and for the suffering and poor.<sup id="cite_ref-SPC_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SPC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After receiving her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> at about age 12, she began to participate in the life of the local <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a>, teaching <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to children, visiting the sick and cleaning the local <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life">Religious life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_of_the_Agonizing_Heart_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 12 July 1890, Visintainer and her friend, Virginia Rosa Nicolodi, under the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual direction</a> of a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest, Luigi Rossi, committed their lives to religious service, under dedication to the Immaculate Conception of Our Lady.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They began by caring for a woman suffering from terminal cancer, in a small house which was donated to the small community and the young girls began a schedule of religious living. After the woman's death the following year, they were joined by a third friend, Teresa Anna Maule.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1895, Rossi and Visintainer, seeing the need for a more formal and secure organization of the young women coming to them, decided to establish a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> called the Congregation of the Little Sisters of the Immaculate Conception, which was approved by José de Camargo Barros, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Curitiba" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Curitiba">Bishop of Curitiba</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In December of that same year, the founding trio took <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>. Visintainer took the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> by which she is now known. The congregation, Brazil's first locally founded, grew quickly throughout the state, and in 1903 Pauline was elected their <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> for life. She moved from Nova Trento to <a href="/wiki/Ipiranga_(district_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo)" title="Ipiranga (district of São Paulo)">Ipiranga</a>, <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_(state)" title="São Paulo (state)">São Paulo</a>, where she opened a convent of the congregation in order to take care of orphans, the children of former slaves – <a href="/wiki/Slavery_in_Brazil" title="Slavery in Brazil">slavery</a> having been ended by the <a href="/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil" title="Empire of Brazil">Empire of Brazil</a> only in 1888, and aged slaves who had been left to die because they could no longer work.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1909 Pauline was removed from her position as Superior General by Duarte Leopoldo e Silva, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of São Paulo">Archbishop of São Paulo</a>, following a series of disputes within the congregation. She was sent to work with the sick at the Santa Casa and the elderly of the Hospice of St. <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a> at <a href="/wiki/Bragan%C3%A7a_Paulista" title="Bragança Paulista">Bragança Paulista</a>, without being able to assume an active role in her own congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She spent her spare time praying in support of the congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1918, with the permission of Archbishop Duarte, she was brought back by the Superior General, Vicência Teodora, to live at the General <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">Motherhouse</a> of the congregation at Ipiranga, where she would remain until her death.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Pauline was acknowledged as the "Venerable Mother Foundress", when the <a href="/wiki/Decree_of_Praise" class="mw-redirect" title="Decree of Praise">Decree of Praise</a> was granted by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> on 19 May 1933 to the Congregation of the Little Sisters, establishing it as one of <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_right" title="Pontifical right">pontifical right</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pauline's health began a long, slow decline in 1938, as she fought a losing battle with diabetes.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In two operations, first her middle finger and then her right arm were amputated. She spent the last months of her life totally blind. On 9 July 1942 she died with the last words, "God's will be done".<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_of_the_Agonizing_Heart_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Paulina_1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Santa_Paulina_1.jpg/220px-Santa_Paulina_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Santa_Paulina_1.jpg/330px-Santa_Paulina_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Santa_Paulina_1.jpg/440px-Santa_Paulina_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Saint Pauline in <a href="/wiki/Nova_Trento" title="Nova Trento">Nova Trento</a>, <a href="/wiki/Santa_Catarina_(state)" title="Santa Catarina (state)">Santa Catarina</a>, Brazil.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Pauline was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 18 October 1991, during his visit to <a href="/wiki/Florian%C3%B3polis" title="Florianópolis">Florianópolis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vat_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vat-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> For her beatification, three relics were made from the <a href="/wiki/Phalanges" class="mw-redirect" title="Phalanges">fingerbones</a> of her remaining hand. One was given to Pope John Paul, the second to the convent where she had lived and is currently housed in the <a href="/wiki/Shrine_of_St._Paulina" title="Shrine of St. Paulina">Shrine of St. Paulina</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nova_Trento" title="Nova Trento">Nova Trento</a>, and the other to her relations, Albert Visintainer and his family of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Carmel,_Pennsylvania" title="Mount Carmel, Pennsylvania">Mount Carmel, Pennsylvania</a>, United States. This can be seen at the St. Pauline Visintainer Center in <a href="/wiki/Kulpmont,_Pennsylvania" title="Kulpmont, Pennsylvania">Kulpmont, Pennsylvania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SPC_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SPC-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pope John Paul II later <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> Paulina on 19 May 2002 in a ceremony in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where she became the first Brazilian female saint. Hundreds of Brazilians, including then-President <a href="/wiki/Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso" title="Fernando Henrique Cardoso">Fernando Henrique Cardoso</a>, attended the event.<sup id="cite_ref-BBC_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBC-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her feast day is July 9.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
