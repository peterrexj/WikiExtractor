<p><b>Anthony of Saxony</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Anton</i>; 27 December 1755 – 6 June 1836) was a <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Saxony" title="Kingdom of Saxony">King of Saxony</a> from the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Wettin" title="House of Wettin">House of Wettin</a>. He became known as <i>Anton der Gütige</i><sup id="cite_ref-Quinger_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Quinger-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> ("Anthony the Kind").<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was the fifth but third surviving son of <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Christian,_Elector_of_Saxony" title="Frederick Christian, Elector of Saxony">Frederick Christian, Elector of Saxony</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Maria_Antonia_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Maria Antonia of Bavaria">Duchess Maria Antonia of Bavaria</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>With few chances to take part in the politics of the Electorate of Saxony or receive any land from his older brother Frederick Augustus III, Anton lived under the shadows. No Elector of Saxony after Johann Georg I gave appanages to his younger sons.
</p><p>During the first years of the reign of his older brother as Elector, Anthony was the third in line, preceded only by his older brother Charles. The death of Charles (8 September 1781) made him the next in line to the Electorate as Electoral Prince (de: <i>Kurprinz</i>); this was because all the pregnancies of the Electress Amalie, except for one daughter, ended in a stillbirth.
</p><p>His aunt, the <a href="/wiki/Maria_Josepha_of_Saxony_(1731%E2%80%931767)" class="mw-redirect" title="Maria Josepha of Saxony (1731–1767)">Dauphine of France</a>, had wanted to engage her daughter <a href="/wiki/Princess_Marie_Z%C3%A9phyrine_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess Marie Zéphyrine of France">Marie Zéphyrine of France</a> to Anthony; Marie Zéphyrine died in 1755 abandoning plans. Another French candidate was Marie Zéphyrine's sister <a href="/wiki/Clotilde_de_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotilde de France">Marie Clothilde</a> (later <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_Sardinia" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen of Sardinia">Queen of Sardinia</a>) but again nothing happened.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> on 29 September 1781 (by proxy) and again in <a href="/wiki/Dresden" title="Dresden">Dresden</a> on 24 October 1781 (in person), Anthony married firstly with the <a href="/wiki/Princess_Maria_Carolina_of_Savoy" title="Princess Maria Carolina of Savoy">Princess Maria Carolina of Savoy</a>, daughter of the King <a href="/wiki/Victor_Amadeus_III_of_Sardinia" title="Victor Amadeus III of Sardinia">Victor Amadeus III of Sardinia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maria_Antonietta_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Maria Antonietta of Spain">Maria Antonietta of Spain</a>. Caroline died after only one year of marriage, on 28 December 1782 having succumbed to <a href="/wiki/Smallpox" title="Smallpox">smallpox</a>. They had no children.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> on 8 September 1787 (by proxy) and again in <a href="/wiki/Dresden" title="Dresden">Dresden</a> on 18 October 1787 (in person), Anthony entered his second marriage, to the Archduchess <a href="/wiki/Maria_Theresa_of_Austria_(1767%E2%80%931827)" title="Maria Theresa of Austria (1767–1827)">Maria Theresia of Austria</a> (Maria Theresia Josephe Charlotte Johanna), daughter of the Grand Duke Leopold I of Tuscany, later <a href="/wiki/Leopold_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Leopold II, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Leopold II</a>. <a href="/wiki/Mozart" class="mw-redirect" title="Mozart">Mozart</a>'s opera <i><a href="/wiki/Don_Giovanni" title="Don Giovanni">Don Giovanni</a></i> was originally intended to be performed in honor of his bride for a visit to <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> on 14 October 1787, as she traveled between <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> and Dresden for the in-person ceremony, and <a href="/wiki/Libretto" title="Libretto">librettos</a> were printed with mention of the names of both Anton and the archduchess. The <a href="/wiki/Premiere" title="Premiere">premiere</a> could not be arranged in time, however, so the opera <i><a href="/wiki/The_Marriage_of_Figaro" title="The Marriage of Figaro">The Marriage of Figaro</a></i> was substituted on the express orders of the bride's uncle, the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Joseph II, Holy Roman Emperor">Joseph II</a>. The choice of <i>The Marriage of Figaro</i> was considered improper for a new bride by many observers, and the archduchess left the opera theater early without seeing the entire work performed. Mozart complained bitterly of the intrigues surrounding this incident in a letter to his friend Gottfried von Jacquin that was written in stages between 15 October and 25 October 1787. Anthony was present in Prague in September 1791 for the first performance of Mozart's opera <i><a href="/wiki/La_clemenza_di_Tito" title="La clemenza di Tito">La clemenza di Tito</a></i>, which was written as part of the coronation ceremonies of his father-in-law, the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Leopold_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Leopold II, Holy Roman Emperor">Leopold II</a>, as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Bohemia">King of Bohemia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The couple had four children, but none survived to the age of two:
</p>
<ol><li>Maria Ludovika Auguste Fredericka Therese Franziska Johanna Aloysia Nepomucena Ignatia Anna Josepha Xaveria Franziska de Paula Barbara (b. Dresden, 14 March 1795 – d. Dresden, 25 April 1796)</li>
<li>Frederick Augustus (b. and d. Dresden, 5 April 1796)</li>
<li>Maria Johanna Ludovica Anna Amalia Nepomucena Aloysia Ignatia Xaveria Josepha Franziska de Chantal Eva Apollonia Magdalena Crescentia Vincentia (b. Dresden, 5 April 1798 – d. Dresden, 30 October 1799)</li>
<li>Maria Theresia (b. and d. Dresden, 15 October 1799)</li></ol>
<p>Electress Amalie gave birth for last time in 1799 to another stillborn child. After this, it became apparent that Anthony would succeed to the Electorate of Saxony, which was raised to kingdom in 1806.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="King_of_Saxony">King of Saxony</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: King of Saxony">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Anthony succeeded his brother Frederick August I as King of Saxony upon the latter's death, on 5 May 1827. The 71-year-old new king was completely inexperienced in government, and hence had no intention of initiating profound changes in foreign or domestic policy.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia" title="Kingdom of Prussia">Prussian</a> diplomats discussed granting the <a href="/wiki/Rhine_Province" title="Rhine Province">Prussian Rhineland</a> (predominantly <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a>) to Anthony (a Catholic) in exchange for <a href="/wiki/Lutheranism" title="Lutheranism">Lutheran</a> Saxony in 1827, but nothing came of these talks.<sup id="cite_ref-Freitag_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Freitag-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/July_Revolution" title="July Revolution">July Revolution</a> of 1830 in France, disturbances in Saxony began in autumn. These were directed primarily against the old Constitution. Therefore, on 13 September the cabinet dismissed Count <a href="/w/index.php?title=Detlev_von_Einsiedel&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Detlev von Einsiedel (page does not exist)">Detlev von Einsiedel</a>, followed by <a href="/wiki/Bernhard_von_Lindenau" title="Bernhard von Lindenau">Bernhard von Lindenau</a>. Because the people wished to have a younger regent, Anthony agreed to appoint his nephew Frederick Augustus Prince Co-Regent (de: <i>Prinz-Mitregenten</i>). As another consequence of the disturbances, a new constitution was adopted in 1831 and came into effect on 4 September of that year. With it Saxony became a <a href="/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy" title="Constitutional monarchy">Constitutional monarchy</a> and obtained a bi-cameral legislature and a responsible ministry, which replaced the old feudal estates.  The constitution was more conservative than other constitutions existing at this time in the German Union. Nevertheless, it remained in force in Saxony until 1918.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The king kept his exclusive sovereignty but was bound by the Government Business to cooperate with the Ministers and the decisions of both Chambers of the Estates (de: <i>Kammern der Ständeversammlung</i>) meeting. The entry of Saxony into the <i><a href="/wiki/Zollverein" title="Zollverein">Zollverein</a></i> in 1833 let trade, industry and traffic blossom farther.
</p><p>Without surviving male issue, Anthony was succeeded as king by his nephew, <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Augustus_II_of_Saxony" title="Frederick Augustus II of Saxony">Frederick Augustus II</a>.
</p>
