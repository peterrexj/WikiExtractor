<p><b>Charles Borromeo</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Carlo Borromeo</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Carolus Borromeus</i>; 2 October 1538 – 3 November 1584) was the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> from 1564 to 1584 and a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. He was a leading figure of the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a> combat against the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> together with <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a> and <a href="/wiki/Philip_Neri" title="Philip Neri">Philip Neri</a>. In that role he was responsible for significant reforms in the Catholic Church, including the founding of <a href="/wiki/Seminaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminaries">seminaries</a> for the education of priests. He is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the Catholic Church, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 4 November.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Borromeo was a descendant of nobility; the <a href="/wiki/Borromeo_family" class="mw-redirect" title="Borromeo family">Borromeo family</a> was one of the most ancient and wealthy in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, made famous by several notable men, both in the church and state. The family <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> included the <a href="/wiki/Borromean_rings" title="Borromean rings">Borromean rings</a>, which are sometimes taken to symbolize the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Trinity">Holy Trinity</a>. Borromeo's father Gilbert was Count of <a href="/wiki/Arona,_Piedmont" title="Arona, Piedmont">Arona</a>. His mother Margaret was a member of the Milan branch of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Medici" title="House of Medici">House of Medici</a>. The second son in a family of six children, he was born in the castle of Arona on <a href="/wiki/Lake_Maggiore" title="Lake Maggiore">Lake Maggiore</a> 36 miles from <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> on 2 October 1538.<sup id="cite_ref-Britannica_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Britannica-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Borromeo received the <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> when he was about twelve years old. At this time his paternal uncle Giulio Cesare Borromeo turned over to him the income from the rich <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> abbey of Sts. Gratinian and Felin, one of the ancient perquisites of the family. Borromeo made plain to his father that all revenues from the abbey beyond what was required to prepare him for a career in the church belonged to the poor and could not be applied to secular use. The young man attended the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Pavia" title="University of Pavia">University of Pavia</a>, where he applied himself to the study of civil and canon law. Due to a slight speech impediment, he was regarded as slow but his thoroughness and industry meant that he made rapid progress. In 1554 his father died, and although he had an elder brother, Count Federico, he was requested by the family to take the management of their domestic affairs. After a time, he resumed his studies, and on 6 December 1559, he earned a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_both_laws" title="Doctor of both laws">doctorate in canon and civil law</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Rome_period">Rome period</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Rome period">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 25 December 1559 Borromeo's uncle Cardinal Giovanni Angelo Medici was elected as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IV" title="Pope Pius IV">Pope Pius IV</a>. The newly elected pope required his nephew to come to Rome, and on 13 January 1560 appointed him <a href="/wiki/Protonotary_apostolic" title="Protonotary apostolic">protonotary apostolic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Miranda_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miranda-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Shortly thereafter, on 31 January 1560, the pope created him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a>, and thus Borromeo as <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-nephew" title="Cardinal-nephew">cardinal-nephew</a> was entrusted with both the public and the privy seal of the ecclesiastical state.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was also brought into the government of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> and appointed a supervisor of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscans</a>, <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelites</a> and <a href="/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller" title="Knights Hospitaller">Knights of Malta</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his four years in Rome, Borromeo lived in austerity, obliged the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Curia" title="Roman Curia">Roman Curia</a> to wear black, and established an academy of learned persons, the Academy of the Vatican Knights, publishing their memoirs as the <i>Noctes Vaticanae</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Borromeo organized the third and last session of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>, in 1562–63.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had a large share in the making of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catechism" title="Roman Catechism">Tridentine Catechism</a> (<i>Catechismus Romanus</i>). In 1561, Borromeo founded and endowed a college at <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, today known as <a href="/wiki/Almo_Collegio_Borromeo" class="mw-redirect" title="Almo Collegio Borromeo">Almo Collegio Borromeo</a>, which he dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Justina_of_Padua" title="Justina of Padua">Justina of Padua</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 19 November 1562, his older brother, Federico, suddenly died. His family urged Borromeo to seek permission to return to the lay state (<a href="/wiki/Loss_of_clerical_state_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Loss of clerical state (Catholic Church)">laicization</a>), to marry and have children so that the family name would not become extinct, but he decided not to leave the ecclesiastic state.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His brother's death, along with his contacts with the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Theatines" title="Theatines">Theatines</a> and the example of bishops such as <a href="/wiki/Bartholomew_of_Braga" title="Bartholomew of Braga">Bartholomew of Braga</a>, were the causes of the conversion of Borromeo towards a more strict and operative Christian life, and his aim became to put into practice the dignity and duties of the bishop as drafted by the recent Council of Trent.<sup id="cite_ref-DBI_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DBI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Milan">Archbishop of Milan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Milan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Borromeo was appointed an administrator of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan">Archdiocese of Milan</a> on 7 February 1560. After his decision to put into practice the role of bishop, he decided to be ordained <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a> (4 September 1563) and on 7 December 1563 he was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Sistine_Chapel" title="Sistine Chapel">Sistine Chapel</a> by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Serbelloni" title="Giovanni Antonio Serbelloni">Giovanni Serbelloni</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Borromeo was formally appointed <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Milan">archbishop of Milan</a> on 12 May 1564 after the former archbishop <a href="/wiki/Ippolito_II_d%27Este" title="Ippolito II d&#39;Este">Ippolito II d'Este</a> waived his claims on that archbishopric, but he was only allowed by the pope to leave Rome one year later. Borromeo made his formal entry into Milan as archbishop on 23 September 1565.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Reform_in_Milan">Reform in Milan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Reform in Milan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG/220px-Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG/330px-Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG/440px-Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3616" data-file-height="2175" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Intercession_of_Charles_Borromeo_supported_by_the_Virgin_Mary_-_Detail_Rottmayr_Fresco_-_Karlskirche_-_Vienna.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Intercession of Charles Borromeo supported by the Virgin Mary by <a href="/wiki/Johann_Michael_Rottmayr" title="Johann Michael Rottmayr">Rottmayr</a> (<a href="/wiki/Karlskirche" title="Karlskirche">Karlskirche</a>, Vienna)</div></div></div>
<p>After the death of his uncle, Pius IV (1566), Borromeo sent a galley to fetch Cardinal Ugo Boncompagni, the Nuncio in Spain, but he did not arrive in time to be considered at the conclave. Borromeo then reached an agreement with <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Farnese_(cardinal)" title="Alessandro Farnese (cardinal)">Alessandro Farnese</a>, who held a significant number of votes, to support <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Antonio Ghislieri</a>, who was rumored to have the support of <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a>. Ghislieri was elected and took the name Pius V.<sup id="cite_ref-Giussano_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Giussano-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before Borromeo went to Milan, while he was overseeing reform in Rome, a nobleman remarked that the latter city was no longer a place to enjoy oneself or to make a fortune. "Carlo Borromeo has undertaken to remake the city from top to bottom," he said, predicting that the reformer's enthusiasm "would lead him to correct the rest of the world once he has finished with Rome."<sup id="cite_ref-swetnam_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-swetnam-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Subsequently, he devoted himself to the reformation of his diocese which had deteriorated in practice owing to the 80-year absence of previous archbishops.<sup id="cite_ref-action_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-action-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Milan was the largest archdiocese in Italy at the time, with more than 3,000 clergy and 800,000 people. Both its clergy and laity had drifted from church teaching. The selling of indulgences and ecclesiastical positions was prevalent; monasteries were "full of disorder"; many religious were "lazy, ignorant, and debauched".<sup id="cite_ref-swetnam_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-swetnam-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Borromeo made numerous pastoral visits and restored dignity to divine service. He urged churches to be designed in conformity with the decrees of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>, which stated that sacred art and architecture lacking adequate scriptural foundation was in effect prohibited, as was any inclusion of classical pagan elements in religious art.<sup id="cite_ref-blunt_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blunt-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He divided the nave of the church into two compartments to separate the sexes at worship.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He extended his reforms to the collegiate churches, monasteries and even to the <a href="/wiki/Confraternities_of_Penitents" class="mw-redirect" title="Confraternities of Penitents">Confraternities of Penitents</a>, particularly that of <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">St. John the Baptist</a>. This group was to attend to prisoners and those condemned to death, to give them help and support.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg/220px-Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="338" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg/330px-Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg/440px-Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg 2x" data-file-width="809" data-file-height="1242" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Jordaens_St_Charles_Cares_for_the_Plague_Victims_of_Milan.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Charles Borromeo intercedes during the plague; painting by Jacob Jordaens (1655)</div></div></div>
<p>Borromeo believed that abuses in the church arose from ignorant clergy. Among his most important actions, he established seminaries, colleges, and communities for the education of candidates for holy orders.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His emphasis on Catholic learning greatly increased the preparation of men for the priesthood and benefited their congregations. In addition, he founded the fraternity of <a href="/wiki/Oblates" class="mw-redirect" title="Oblates">Oblates</a> of St. Ambrose, a society of secular men who did not take orders, but devoted themselves to the church and followed a discipline of monastic prayers and study. They provided assistance to parishes when so directed.<sup id="cite_ref-action_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-action-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The new archbishop's efforts for catechesis and the instruction of youth included the initiation of the first "Sunday School" classes and the work of the Confraternity for Christian Doctrine.
</p><p>Borromeo's diocesan reforms faced opposition from several religious orders, particularly that of the <a href="/wiki/Humiliati" title="Humiliati">Humiliati</a> (Brothers of Humility), a penitential order which, although reduced to about 170 members, owned some ninety monasteries. Some members of that society formed a conspiracy against his life, and a shot was fired at him with an <a href="/wiki/Arquebus" title="Arquebus">arquebus</a> in the archepiscopal chapel. His survival was considered miraculous.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1576 there was famine at Milan due to crop failures, and later an outbreak of the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">plague</a>. The city's trade fell off, and along with it the people's source of income. The Governor and many members of the nobility fled the city, but the bishop remained, to organize the care of those affected and to minister to the dying. He called together the superiors of all the religious communities in the diocese and won their cooperation. Borromeo tried to feed 60,000 to 70,000 people daily. He used up his own funds and went into debt to provide food for the hungry. Finally, he wrote to the Governor and successfully persuaded him to return.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Influence_on_English_affairs">Influence on English affairs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Influence on English affairs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Borromeo had also been involved in English affairs when he assisted Pius IV. Many English Catholics had fled to Italy at this time because of the persecutions under <a href="/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Elizabeth I">Queen Elizabeth I</a>. He gave pastoral attention to English Catholics who fled to Italy to escape the new laws against the Catholic faith.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a>, a Jesuit, along with <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Sherwin" title="Ralph Sherwin">Ralph Sherwin</a> visited him at Milan in 1580 on their way to England.  They stayed with him for eight days, talking with him every night after dinner. A Welshman, Griffith Roberts, served as his canon theologian, and an Englishman, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Goldwell" title="Thomas Goldwell">Thomas Goldwell</a>, as vicar-general. The archbishop carried on his person a small picture of <a href="/wiki/John_Fisher" title="John Fisher">John Fisher</a>, who, with <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More" title="Thomas More">Thomas More</a>, had been executed during the reign of Henry VIII, and for whom he held a great veneration.  During the nineteenth-century <a href="/wiki/Universalis_Ecclesiae" title="Universalis Ecclesiae">Catholic restoration</a> in England, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Wiseman" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Wiseman">Cardinal Wiseman</a> was to institute an order of Oblates of St Charles, led by <a href="/wiki/Henry_Edward_Manning" title="Henry Edward Manning">Henry Edward Manning</a>, as a congregation of secular priests directly supporting the Archbishop of Westminster.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Persecution_of_religious_dissidents">Persecution of religious dissidents</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Persecution of religious dissidents">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Borromeo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Borromeo.jpg/220px-Borromeo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="286" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Borromeo.jpg/330px-Borromeo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Borromeo.jpg/440px-Borromeo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1040" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Borromeo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Caccianiga" title="Francesco Caccianiga">Francesco Caccianiga</a> showing an angel tending to Charles Borromeo</div></div></div>
<p>Though the Diet of Ilanz of 1524 and 1526 had proclaimed freedom of worship in the Republic of the Three Leagues, Borromeo repressed Protestantism in the Swiss valleys. The Catholic Encyclopedia relates: "In November [1583] he began a visitation as Apostolic visitor of all the cantons of <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Grisons" title="Grisons">Grisons</a>, leaving the affairs of his diocese in the hands of Monsignor <a href="/wiki/Owen_Lewis_(bishop)" title="Owen Lewis (bishop)">Owen Lewis</a>, his vicar-general. He began in the Mesoleina Valley; here not only was their heresy to be fought, but also witchcraft and sorcery, and at Roveredo it was discovered that "the provost or rector, was the foremost in sorceries."<sup id="cite_ref-keogh_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keogh-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> During his pastoral visit to the region, 150 people were arrested for practicing witchcraft. Eleven women and the provost were condemned by the civil authorities to be burned alive.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Reacting to the pressure of the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a>, Borromeo encouraged <a href="/wiki/Ludwig_Pfyffer" title="Ludwig Pfyffer">Ludwig Pfyffer</a> in his development of the "Golden League", but did not live to see its formation in 1586.  Based in <a href="/wiki/Lucerne" title="Lucerne">Lucerne</a>, the organization (also called the Borromean League) linked activities of several Swiss Catholic cantons of <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, which became the centre of Catholic Counter-Reformation efforts.  This organization was determined to expel heretics. It created severe strains in the civil administration of the confederation, and caused the break-up of <a href="/wiki/Appenzell" title="Appenzell">Appenzell</a> canton along religious lines.
<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Controversy_and_last_days">Controversy and last days</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Controversy and last days">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="A coffin with glass sides allowing us to see the preserved body within, on a table inside an elaborately carved wooden nook, under a silver electric chandelier" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg/220px-Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="352" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg/330px-Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg/440px-Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3360" data-file-height="5376" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Coffin_of_St_Charles_Borromeo_in_Milan_Duomo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Crypt of Charles Borromeo, in the <a href="/wiki/Duomo_di_Milano" class="mw-redirect" title="Duomo di Milano">Duomo di Milano</a></div></div></div>
<p>Charged with implementing the reforms dictated by the Council of Trent, Borromeo's uncompromising stance brought him into conflict with secular leaders, priests, and even the Pope.<sup id="cite_ref-swetnam_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-swetnam-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He met with much opposition to his reforms. The governor of the province and many of the senators addressed complaints to the courts of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1584, during his annual retreat at Monte Varallo, he fell ill with "intermittent fever and ague", and on returning to Milan grew rapidly worse. After receiving the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">Last Rites</a>, he quietly died on 3 November at the age of 46.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following his death, popular devotion to Borromeo arose quickly and continued to grow. The Milanese celebrated his anniversary as though he were already a saint, and supporters in a number of cities collected documentation to support his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>.  In 1602 <a href="/wiki/Clement_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement VIII">Clement VIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> Borromeo. In 1604 his case was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Rites">Congregation of Rites</a>. On 1 November 1610, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a> canonized Borromeo. Three years later, the church added his feast to the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration on 4 November.<sup id="cite_ref-keogh_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keogh-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Along with <a href="/wiki/Guarinus_of_Palestrina" title="Guarinus of Palestrina">Guarinus of Palestrina</a> and perhaps <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Lucca" title="Anselm of Lucca">Anselm of Lucca</a>, he is one of only two or three <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-nephew" title="Cardinal-nephew">cardinal-nephews</a> to have been canonized.
</p><p>Charles Borromeo is the patron saint of bishops, catechists and seminarians.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Borromeo's emblem is the Latin word <i>humilitas</i> (humility), which is a portion of the Borromeo shield. He is usually represented in art in his robes, barefoot, carrying the cross as archbishop, a rope around his neck, one hand raised in blessing, thus recalling his work during the plague.<sup id="cite_ref-keogh_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keogh-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Borromeo' biography was originally written by three of his contemporaries: <a href="/wiki/Agostino_Valerio" class="mw-redirect" title="Agostino Valerio">Agostino Valerio</a> (afterwards cardinal and Bishop of Verona) and Carlo Bascape (General of the <a href="/wiki/Barnabites" title="Barnabites">Barnabites</a>, afterwards Bishop of Novara), who wrote their contributions in Latin, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pietro_Giussanno&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pietro Giussanno (page does not exist)">Pietro Giussanno</a> (a priest), who wrote his in Italian. Giussanno's account was the most detailed of the three.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG/220px-San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="291" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG/330px-San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG/440px-San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1232" data-file-height="1632" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Carlo-Arona_01.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Il <a href="/wiki/Sancarlone" title="Sancarlone">Sancarlone</a></i> (English: <span lang="en">The huge Saint Charles</span>): colossal statue of Carlo Borromeo erected in <a href="/wiki/Arona,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Arona, Italy">Arona, Italy</a> in 1697. The work of <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Crespi" title="Giovanni Battista Crespi">Giovanni Battista Crespi</a>, the statue is 23 m tall and stands on a plinth 12 m in height.</div></div></div>
<p>Borromeo's correspondence shows his influential position in Europe during his lifetime. The popes under whom he served sought his advice. The Catholic sovereigns of Europe – <a href="/wiki/Henry_III_of_France" title="Henry III of France">Henry III of France</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mary,_Queen_of_Scots" title="Mary, Queen of Scots">Mary, Queen of Scots</a> – and others showed how they valued his influence. Cardinal Valerio of Verona said of him that Borromeo was "to the well-born a pattern of virtue, to his brother cardinals an example of true nobility." <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Baronius" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Baronius">Cardinal Baronius</a> styled him "a second <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a>, whose early death, lamented by all good men, inflicted great loss on the Church."
</p><p>Late in the sixteenth or at the beginning of the seventeenth century, Catholics in England  circulated among themselves a "Life of St. Charles".<sup id="cite_ref-Giussano_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Giussano-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monuments">Monuments</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Monuments">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>Contrary to Borromeo's last wishes, the <a href="/wiki/Duomo_di_Milano" class="mw-redirect" title="Duomo di Milano">Duomo di Milano</a> created a memorial crypt to honour him at the church.</li>
<li>His relative <a href="/wiki/Federico_Borromeo" title="Federico Borromeo">Federico Borromeo</a> and admirers commissioned a statue 20 m high that was erected on the hill above Arona, as they regarded him an important leader of the Counter-Reformation.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>Besides the <i>Noctes Vaticanae</i>, to which he appeared to have contributed, Borromeo's written legacy consisted only of some homilies, discourses and sermons, with a collection of letters.  Borromeo's sermons have been translated into many languages.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other">Other</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charles_Borromeo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=19" title="Edit section: Other">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>His nephew, <a href="/wiki/Federico_Borromeo" title="Federico Borromeo">Federico Borromeo</a> (1564&#8211;1631), was archbishop of Milan from 1595 and founded the <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Library" class="mw-redirect" title="Ambrosian Library">Ambrosian Library</a> in that city.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911274-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He donated his collection of art and literature to the library. He appeared as a character in <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Manzoni" title="Alessandro Manzoni">Alessandro Manzoni</a>'s novel <a href="/wiki/The_Betrothed_(Manzoni_novel)" title="The Betrothed (Manzoni novel)">The Betrothed</a> (I promessi sposi).</li>
<li>Sint. Carolus hospital, first Catholic hospital in Indonesia founded by Perkumpulan Perhimpunan St. Carolus Vereeniging (PPSC). It is managed by Kongregasi Suster-Suster Cinta Kasih St. Carolus Borromeus (<a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Mercy_of_St._Borromeo" title="Sisters of Mercy of St. Borromeo">Sisters of Mercy of St. Borromeo </a>) since its foundation in 1913.</li>
<li>Borromeo was crucial in furthering the career of composer <a href="/wiki/Orfeo_Vecchi" title="Orfeo Vecchi">Orfeo Vecchi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Borromeo is one of four people mentioned at the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church" title="Catechism of the Catholic Church">Catechism of the Catholic Church</a>, as responsible for the Council of Trent, which gave way to the modern-day catechism. The others are <a href="/wiki/Peter_Canisius" title="Peter Canisius">Peter Canisius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turibius_of_Mongrovejo" class="mw-redirect" title="Turibius of Mongrovejo">Turibius of Mongrovejo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine" title="Robert Bellarmine">Robert Bellarmine</a>.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Saint_Charles_Preparatory_School" title="Saint Charles Preparatory School">Saint Charles Preparatory School</a>, a former college seminary now a four-year Catholic college preparatory school in <a href="/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio" title="Columbus, Ohio">Columbus, Ohio</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Lewis_University" title="Lewis University">Lewis University</a>, a Catholic and Lasallian University, St. Charles Borromeo North Campus</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Charles,_Missouri" title="St. Charles, Missouri">St. Charles, Missouri</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Charles,_Illinois" title="St. Charles, Illinois">St. Charles, Illinois</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Charles,_Minnesota" title="St. Charles, Minnesota">St. Charles, Minnesota</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Carlos" title="São Carlos">São Carlos</a>, Brazil</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Charles-Borrom%C3%A9e,_Quebec" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Charles-Borromée, Quebec">Saint-Charles-Borromée, Quebec</a>, Canada</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/San_Carlos,_Pangasinan" title="San Carlos, Pangasinan">San Carlos City, Pangasinan</a>, Philippines</li>
<li>The <a href="/wiki/San_Carlos_de_Borromeo_Fortress" title="San Carlos de Borromeo Fortress">San Carlos de Borromeo Fortress</a> on Margarita Island, state of Nueva Esparta, Venezuela, completed in 1684, intended to help protect settlements in the Bay of Pampatar area against the constant threat of piracy</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/University_of_San_Carlos" title="University of San Carlos">University of San Carlos</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cebu_City" title="Cebu City">Cebu City</a>, Philippines<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Universidad_de_San_Carlos_de_Guatemala" title="Universidad de San Carlos de Guatemala">Universidad de San Carlos de Guatemala</a> in <a href="/wiki/Guatemala" title="Guatemala">Guatemala</a>, originally named "Royal and Pontifical University of San Carlos Borromeo"</li></ul>
