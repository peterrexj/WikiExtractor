					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eigil&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Eigil">Eigil</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Eigil</b> (also called Aeigil or Egil) (c. 750–822) was the fourth <a href="/wiki/Fulda_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Fulda monastery">abbot of Fulda</a>. He was the nephew and biographer of the abbey's founder and first abbot <a href="/wiki/St._Sturmius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Sturmius">Saint Sturm</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> We know about Eigil primarily from the Latin <i>Life</i> (<i>Vita Aegili</i>) that the monk and teacher of Fulda, <a href="/wiki/Candidus_of_Fulda" title="Candidus of Fulda">Candidus Bruun</a> composed about him after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-MGH_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MGH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eigil's parents, who were nobles of <a href="/wiki/Noricum" title="Noricum">Norica</a>, sent him to the abbey of Fulda (still Under Sturm's rule) for his education.<sup id="cite_ref-VE-prose1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VE-prose1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Sturm died in 779 and was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Baugulf" title="Baugulf">Baugulf</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ann-Ful_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ann-Ful-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The next abbot, <a href="/wiki/Ratgar" title="Ratgar">Ratgar</a>, with his excessive severity, caused deep divisions in the monastery. In 811, monks from Fulda, possibly including Eigil, petitioned Emperor <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> to remove the abbot. Finally, in 817, Ratgar was denounced by the monks. Charlemagne's son and successor, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a>, banished Ratgar and sent two of his delegates, Aaron and Adalfrid, along with their associates to reform the abbey according to proper monastic discipline .<sup id="cite_ref-MGH_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MGH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VE-prose1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VE-prose1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Eigil was elected in 818,<sup id="cite_ref-ann-Ful3_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ann-Ful3-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and, says Candidus, brought harmony to the monastery once again.<sup id="cite_ref-MGH_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MGH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  In a poem,<sup id="cite_ref-RM-_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RM--7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> his student and successor <a href="/wiki/Hrabanus_Maurus" class="mw-redirect" title="Hrabanus Maurus">Hrabanus Maurus</a> celebrated his clemency and gentleness, supporting Candidus' positive portrait.
</p><p>Under Eigil, several building projects were dedicated at Fulda.  In 819, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> Haistolf came to Fulda to dedicate the Basilica of Saint Boniface (who was considered a co-founder of the abbey, with his disciple Sturm), and Boniface's holy <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> - his bones - were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> (that is, conveyed in a public religious ceremony) to the new Basilica.<sup id="cite_ref-ann-Ful3_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ann-Ful3-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eigil died in 822 and was succeeded by the head teacher of Fulda's school, Hrabanus Maurus,<sup id="cite_ref-ann-Ful3_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ann-Ful3-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> one of his former pupils.  Candidus' <i>Vita</i> treats Eigil as a saint, but other writers, such as Hrabanus do not.
</p>

<p>Eigil wrote a <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.fordham.edu/halsall/basis/sturm.html">Life of Saint Sturm</a>, who was a disciple of Saint Boniface as well as the founder, in 742 or 744, and first abbot of the abbey of Fulda (747-779).
