<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p><p><b>Thomas Ford</b> (died 28 May 1582), a <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devonshire</a> native, was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> executed during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Ford_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He received a Masters of Arts at <a href="/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford" title="Trinity College, Oxford">Trinity College</a>, <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">Oxford</a>, on 24 July 1567, and became a fellow (although one source says president) there. In 1570, he left for the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a>, <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>, and was one of its first three students to be ordained, receiving his orders March 1573 in <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon after receiving his <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Divinity" title="Bachelor of Divinity">Bachelor of Divinity</a> in Douai, on 2 May 1576, he left for England. There he settled in <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a>, becoming the chaplain of <a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Braybrooke&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="James Braybrooke (page does not exist)">James Braybrooke</a> at <a href="/wiki/Sutton_Courtenay" title="Sutton Courtenay">Sutton Courtenay</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-rbhbf_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rbhbf-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and then of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Yate&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Francis Yate (page does not exist)">Francis Yate</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Bridgettine" class="mw-redirect" title="Bridgettine">Bridgettine</a> nuns who were staying with Yate at <a href="/wiki/Lyford,_Oxfordshire" title="Lyford, Oxfordshire">Lyford</a> Grange.<sup id="cite_ref-rbhaec_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rbhaec-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 17 July 1581, he was arrested by the government spy, <a href="/wiki/George_Eliot_(spy)" class="mw-redirect" title="George Eliot (spy)">George Eliot</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-rbhaec_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rbhaec-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 22 July of that same year, he was put in the Tower, where he was tortured.
</p><p>Ford was taken to court along with <a href="/wiki/John_Shert" title="John Shert">John Shert</a> on 16 November with a faked charge of conspiracy. It is said he had conspired in places he had never been (<a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims</a>), on days he had been in England.  Both he and Short were condemned on 21 November and, along with <a href="/wiki/Robert_Johnson_(martyr)" title="Robert Johnson (martyr)">Robert Johnson</a>, beheaded in May 1582.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> All three were beatified in 1886.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
