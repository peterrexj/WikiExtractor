<p><b>Álvaro del Portillo y Diez de Sollano</b> (11 March 1914 – 23 March 1994) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Engineer" title="Engineer">engineer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. He served as the <a href="/wiki/Personal_prelature" title="Personal prelature">prelate</a> of <a href="/wiki/Opus_Dei" title="Opus Dei">Opus Dei</a> between 1982 and 1994 as the successor to <a href="/wiki/Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1" title="Josemaría Escrivá">Josemaría Escrivá</a>.
</p><p>Church leaders <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> and Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Caffarra" title="Carlo Caffarra">Carlo Caffarra</a> have praised Portillo as a faithful servant of <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>. John Paul II referred to him as a "good and faithful servant" while Caffarra dubbed him a "disciple of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Christ</a>".
</p><p>His cause of sainthood commenced on 21 January 2004 after being titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The confirmation of his <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> on 28 June 2012 allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> to name him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>. He was beatified on 27 September 2014 in Madrid in a Mass that Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presided over on the behalf of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%81lvaro_del_Portillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alvaro del Portillo was born in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> on 11 March 1914. He was the third of eight children to the devout Ramón del Portillo Pardo and Clementina Diez de Solano Portillo; the couple had married on 11 January 1908. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 17 March in the parish of Saint Joseph. He studied <a href="/wiki/Civil_engineering" title="Civil engineering">civil engineering</a> and after obtaining his <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorate</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid" title="Complutense University of Madrid">University of Madrid</a> taught at its School of Engineering. He briefly worked with the Bureau of Highways and Bridges in the provinces crossed by the rivers <a href="/wiki/J%C3%BAcar" title="Júcar">Júcar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duero" class="mw-redirect" title="Duero">Duero</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ebro" title="Ebro">Ebro</a>.
</p><p>He received his <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Catholic Church)">Confirmation</a> on 28 December 1916 from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Siguenza" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Siguenza">Bishop of Siguenza</a> Eustaquio Nieto y Martín and went on to receive his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on 12 May 1921.
</p><p>In 1935, he joined Opus Dei and was subsequently ordained to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> on June 25, 1944 by Bishop Leopoldo Eijo y Garay of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Madrid" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Madrid">Madrid</a> as one of the first three men ordained for Opus Dei <sup id="cite_ref-Brief_biography_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brief_biography-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He continued his studies to obtain a doctorate in Philosophy and Letters in history in 1944 from <a href="/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid" title="Complutense University of Madrid">Central University of Madrid</a>, with a dissertation entitled <i>Discoveries and Exploration on the California Coast</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  In 1948 he earned a Doctorate in Canon Law from the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_Saint_Thomas_Aquinas" title="Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas">Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas, <i>Angelicum</i></a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG/185px-Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG" decoding="async" width="185" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG/278px-Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG/370px-Madridbeatificacioalvaro.JPG 2x" data-file-width="5184" data-file-height="3456" /></a><figcaption>The beatification of del Portillo.</figcaption></figure>
<p>He then dedicated himself exclusively to the ministry and government of Opus Dei as its Secretary General. During the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> (1939–1958), he worked in several Dicasteries of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. He met with that pontiff in a private audience on 4 June 1943 and met with Giovanni Battista Montini (the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>) on the following 17 June.
</p><p>In 1963, he was named by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> as a consultant on the Pontifical Commission for the revision of the Code of <a href="/wiki/Canon_Law" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon Law">Canon Law</a>. Pope Paul VI named him consultant on several post-Conciliar commissions. In 1975, he was chosen general president of the Opus Dei and successor of <a href="/wiki/Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1_de_Balaguer" class="mw-redirect" title="Josemaría Escrivá de Balaguer">Josemaría Escrivá de Balaguer</a>, and in 1982, the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> appointed him as the first <a href="/wiki/Personal_prelature" title="Personal prelature">Prelate</a> of Opus Dei. In 1991, he was <a href="/wiki/Consecrated" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated">consecrated</a> a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, with Archbishops <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Re" title="Giovanni Battista Re">Giovanni Battista Re</a> and <a href="/wiki/Justin_Francis_Rigali" class="mw-redirect" title="Justin Francis Rigali">Justin Francis Rigali</a> serving as co-consecrators. That same year, he attended <a href="/wiki/World_Youth_Day" title="World Youth Day">World Youth Day</a> in <a href="/wiki/Czestochowa" class="mw-redirect" title="Czestochowa">Czestochowa</a>.
</p><p>He has written extensively about pastoral and ecclesiological matters that examine among others, the role of the lay faithful in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, the human side of priestly formation, the dynamics and functionality of pastoral structures. As Prelate, he also served as Grand Chancellor of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Piura" title="University of Piura">University of Piura</a> in <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a>.
</p><p>Shortly after his 80th birthday, he returned to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. He died shortly thereafter.  He had celebrated his last Mass at the <a href="/wiki/Cenacle" title="Cenacle">Church of the Cenacle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Brief_biography_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brief_biography-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Later that day, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> came to pray before del Portillo's remains, which now lie in the crypt of the Church of the Prelature, Our Lady of Peace at Bruno Buozzi 75, Rome.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Contribution_to_the_Second_Vatican_Council">Contribution to the Second Vatican Council</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%81lvaro_del_Portillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Contribution to the Second Vatican Council">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The history of del Portillo's involvement is as follows:
</p>
<ul><li>May 2, 1959: named Consultor of the congregation of the Council;</li>
<li>August 10, 1959: named President of the VII internal Commission De laicatu catholico;</li>
<li>Named member of the pre-preparatory Commission on the states of perfection;</li>
<li>August 12 elected member of the III Commission of the Congregation of the Council encharged to study the so-called peculiaria nostrae aetatis apostolatus media.</li>
<li>October 4, 1962: named conciliar "Peritus."</li>
<li>November 4, 1962: named "Peritus" of the Commission for the Discipline of the Clergy and Christian People;</li>
<li>November 8, 1962: named Secretary of this organism</li>
<li>Named Consultor of the Commissions for the Bishops and the regime of the dioceses, the Religious and the Discipline of the Faith.</li>
<li>September 29 – December 1963: during the Second Session of the Council, the Commission for the Discipline of the Clergy and the Christian People, of which D. Alvaro was Secretary, was charged to synthesize into a single conciliar decree (to become "Presbyterorum Ordinis). He coordinated the work of the members of the Commission which became a conciliar text of a single chapter subdivided into 10 parts.</li></ul>
<p>According to Salvador Bernal, his biographer: Del Portillo was the person who argued that there be a specific Decree for priests. Also one of the decisions of the Commission for the Discipline of the Clergy and the Christian People was "defend centuries-old traditions against those who regarded them as mere pietism. It discussed the presence of the priest in the world, and why he needed a good formation in the basic human virtues in order to serve the men and women of his time. But it also warned that priests should not adopt lay lifestyles, much less take on commitments of a partisan political nature. Finally, it asserted the freedom to join associations which in one way or another could help them achieve personal sanctification in the carrying out of their priestly ministry."
</p><p>"Not a week had gone by after the close of the Council when Cardinal Ciriaci, president of the commission of which Don Alvaro had been secretary, sent him a note expressing heartfelt gratitude and congratulations for the happy conclusion of a great achievement." The note said: "You steered to a safe harbor your decree, which is by no means the least important of the decrees and constitutions of the Council." The vote on the document was 2390 to 4, a nearly unanimous approval after thorough debate, on December 7, 1965. Ciriaci said: (History would regard this decree as) "a fresh, and practically unanimous, confirmation by the Second Vatican Council of ecclesiastical celibacy and the exalted mission of the priesthood."
</p><p>Pope Paul VI also commented on his work: "I am well aware of the extent to which this is a result of your prudent, tenacious, and courteous efforts. Without failing to respect the freedom of others to have and to express their own opinions, you never swerved from the track of fidelity to the great principles of priestly spirituality."
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%81lvaro_del_Portillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Process">Process</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%81lvaro_del_Portillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Process">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On his death, John Paul II recalled del Portillo's "zealous priestly and episcopal life, the example he always gave of fortitude and of trust in divine providence and his fidelity to the See of Peter."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The then Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger referred to del Portillo's "modesty and availability in every circumstance," in his work at the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, which del Portillo "enriched in a singular way with his competence and experience."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>On December 6, 2002, <a href="/wiki/Javier_Echevarria_Rodriguez" class="mw-redirect" title="Javier Echevarria Rodriguez">Javier Echevarria Rodriguez</a> ordered an investigation into the cause for <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of del Portillo.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 2004, the Cardinal Vicar of Rome, <a href="/wiki/Camillo_Ruini" title="Camillo Ruini">Camillo Ruini</a>, said "The rather frequent occasions that I had to meet Bishop del Portillo imprinted on my soul the conviction that I found myself in the presence of an exemplary pastor." He added: "In the firmness of his adherence to the doctrine of the Church, in his union with the Pope, in his pastoral charity, in his humility, and in his balance, he exhibited an extraordinary interior richness." According to the Cardinal, "the service that Don Alvaro always provided to the Church of Rome and the prompt and effective way that he supported the Holy Father's pastoral initiatives in this diocese showed the love of the Church that he had learned from St. Josemaría."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Ruini said that there is a desire for a "quick beginning of this cause of canonization" on the part of "so many members of the ecclesiastical hierarchy and of the people of God." Also that there is "an abundant store of testimonies of people who knew him, among them, those of quite a few cardinals and bishops," he said. He then added: "The Bishop's Conference [of Italy] has unanimously expressed its favorable opinion" on beginning the cause.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>For the process, 133 witnesses were interviewed. Among them were 19 cardinals and 12 bishops or archbishops. 62 of the witnesses belong to the Prelature; 71 do not belong to it. The documentation submitted to the Vatican comprised 2,530 pages in three volumes.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Venerable">Venerable</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%81lvaro_del_Portillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Venerable">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On June 28, 2012 — as approved by Pope Benedict XVI — the Vatican announced that his life had been recognized as one of "heroic virtue",<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> a major step towards an eventual beatification. From this time on, del Portillo was styled "Venerable Servant of God".
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%81lvaro_del_Portillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On July 5, 2013 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> published a decree from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_the_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of the Saints">Congregation for the Causes of the Saints</a> declaring the miraculous character of a cure of a Chilean boy attributed to the intercession of the del Portillo.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Del Portillo was beatified on September 27, 2014 in his birth city of Madrid by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of the Saints. May 12 was appointed as his feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The former Prefect of the Congregation for the Causes of Saints, Cardinal Angelo Amato, described his holiness as part of the transformation of the world.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>"Authentic joy is based on this foundation: that we want to live for God and want to serve others because of God. Let us tell the Lord that we want nothing more than to serve him with joy. If we behave in this way we shall find that our inner peace, our joy, our good humour will attract many souls to God. Give witness to Christian joy. Show to those around you that this is our great secret. We are happy because we are children of God, because we deal with him, because we struggle to become better for him. And when we fail, we go right away to the Sacrament of joy where we recover our sense of fraternity with all men and women." Homily, 12 Apr 1984; quoted by Francis Fernandez Carvajal in <i>In Conversation With God</i>, vol.5, p.&#160;155
