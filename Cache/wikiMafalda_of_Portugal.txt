<p><b>Infanta Mafalda of Portugal</b> (also known as <b>Blessed Mafalda, O.Cist.</b> (c. 1195 – 1 May 1256 in <a href="/wiki/Rio_Tinto_(Gondomar)" title="Rio Tinto (Gondomar)">Rio Tinto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gondomar,_Portugal" title="Gondomar, Portugal">Gondomar</a>; <span style="font-size:85%;">Portuguese pronunciation:&#160;</span><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="pt-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Portuguese" title="Help:IPA/Portuguese">[mɐˈfaldɐ]</a></span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portuguese</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Infanta" class="mw-redirect" title="Infanta">infanta</a></i> (princess), later <a href="/wiki/Queen_consort" title="Queen consort">Queen consort</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile" title="Kingdom of Castile">Castile</a> for a brief period. She was the second youngest daughter of <a href="/wiki/List_of_Portuguese_monarchs" title="List of Portuguese monarchs">King</a> <a href="/wiki/Sancho_I_of_Portugal" title="Sancho I of Portugal">Sancho I of Portugal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dulce_of_Aragon" title="Dulce of Aragon">Dulce of Aragon</a>. Married briefly to the ten-year-old <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Castile" title="Henry I of Castile">Henry I of Castile</a>, she held for a time the title Queen of Castile.
</p><p>Upon the dissolution of the marriage, Mafalda returned to her homeland. She chose to become a <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, and became noted for the holiness of her life. She was declared <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> by the Catholic Church five centuries after her death.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mafalda_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mafalda_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Mafalda of Portugal was born around 1195, the daughter of King Sancho I of Portugal and his queen, Dulce of Aragon.
</p><p>On the death of her father, Mafalda, under the provisions of his will, was to receive the Seia Castle and the remaining portion of the municipality as well as all income produced there. Furthermore, she was granted the right to use the title of queen. This created a conflict with her brother <a href="/wiki/Afonso_II_of_Portugal" title="Afonso II of Portugal">Afonso II</a> <i>O Gordo</i>, who, wanting a centralized power, hindered his sister from receiving the titles and the corresponding rights. Afonso feared that something similar could happen with his two sisters, Teresa and Sancha, and their eventual heirs, creating a problem of sovereignty that could come to divide the country. Much of the Portuguese nobles sided with Mafalda and her sisters, but they were defeated.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On the death of Afonso II, his son <a href="/wiki/Sancho_II_of_Portugal" title="Sancho II of Portugal">Sancho II</a> granted some lands and castles to his aunts but he made them renounce the title of princess-queen. The final peace came in 1223.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mafalda_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1215, a political marriage was arranged between Mafalda and her young cousin <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Castile" title="Henry I of Castile">Henry I of Castile</a>. As he was about ten years old, the marriage was never consummated, and it was dissolved the following year on grounds of consanguinity.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She then returned to Portugal.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_life">Monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mafalda_of_Portugal&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1220, Mafalda chose not to marry again and became a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Arouca_Abbey" title="Arouca Abbey">Abbey of Arouca</a>, though she was granted a special dispensation to retain control of her inheritance. She became <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of the community, then one of <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> nuns, and in 1226 had her request to transfer the abbey to the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian Order">Cistercian Order</a> granted by the pope. She had a great devotion to Our Lady of Silva and made substantial donations to the shrine. She also had a hospice for travelers built as well as a number of bridges, churches, monasteries, and hospitals.<sup id="cite_ref-Merton_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Merton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She helped to establish her own abbey as a major religious center in the region for centuries.
</p><p>Returning from a pilgrimage to the shrine of Our Lady of Silva, she fell ill at <a href="/wiki/Rio_Tinto_(Gondomar)" title="Rio Tinto (Gondomar)">Rio Tinto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gondomar,_Portugal" title="Gondomar, Portugal">Gondomar</a>, and died at the monastery of Cistercians monks there on 1 May 1256. In 1616, wanting to return her body to <a href="/wiki/Arouca,_Portugal" title="Arouca, Portugal">Arouca</a> as part of the process of her possible <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>, it was found not to have deteriorated, which generated a strong devotion to the Portuguese princess. She was beatified in 1792 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on June 20, together with her two sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
