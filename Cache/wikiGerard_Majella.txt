<p><b>Gerard Majella</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Gerardo Maiella</i>; 6 April 1726 – 16 October 1755) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Redemptorists" title="Redemptorists">Congregation of the Redeemer</a>, better known as the <a href="/wiki/Redemptorists" title="Redemptorists">Redemptorists</a>, who is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>His intercession is sought for children, unborn children, women in childbirth, mothers, expectant mothers, motherhood, the falsely accused, good confessions, lay brothers and <a href="/wiki/Muro_Lucano" title="Muro Lucano">Muro Lucano</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_Majella&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Majella was born in <a href="/wiki/Muro_Lucano" title="Muro Lucano">Muro Lucano</a> on 6 April 1726, the youngest of five children. He was frail, and his parents had him baptized the day he was born.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the son of Domenico Maiella, a <a href="/wiki/Tailor" title="Tailor">tailor</a> who died when Gerard was twelve, leaving the family in poverty.<sup id="cite_ref-occhiodisalerno.it_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-occhiodisalerno.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His mother, Benedetta Galella, then sent him to her brother so that he could teach Gerard to sew and follow in his father's footsteps.<sup id="cite_ref-occhiodisalerno.it_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-occhiodisalerno.it-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, the foreman was abusive. The boy kept silent, but his uncle soon found out and the man who taught him resigned from the job. After four years of apprenticeship, he took a job as a servant to work for the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lacedonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lacedonia">Bishop of Lacedonia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-liguori_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liguori-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Upon the bishop's death, Gerard returned to his trade, working first as a journeyman and then on his own account. He divided his earnings between his mother and the poor and in offerings for the souls in <a href="/wiki/Purgatory" title="Purgatory">Purgatory</a>.
</p><p>He tried to join the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Capuchin Order">Capuchin Order</a> twice, but his health prevented it.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1749, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Most_Holy_Redeemer" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer">Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer</a>, known as Redemptorists.<sup id="cite_ref-cathency_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathency-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The order was founded in 1732 by <a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Liguori" title="Alphonsus Liguori">Alphonsus Liguori</a> (1696-1787) at <a href="/wiki/Scala,_Campania" title="Scala, Campania">Scala</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>. The essentially- missionary order is dedicated to "preaching the word of God to the poor." Its apostolate is principally in giving of missions and retreats.<sup id="cite_ref-carr_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-carr-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his life, he was very close to the peasants and other outsiders who lived in the Neapolitan countryside. In his work with the Redemptorist community, he was variously a gardener, <a href="/wiki/Sacristan" title="Sacristan">sacristan</a>, tailor, porter, cook, carpenter, and clerk of works on the new buildings at Caposele.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At 27, the Majella was controversially identified by a young pregnant woman as the father of her child.To avoid exposing the man behind the pregnancy, St. Gerard accepted the blame silently. His superior St Alphonse Ligouri questioned him and, due to his silence, banned him from receiving the Holy Communion. After several years the women revealed the truth on her deathbed and testified to St Gerard’s holiness. 
</p><p>Some of Majella's reported miracles include restoring life to a boy who had fallen from a high cliff, blessing the scant supply of wheat belonging to a poor family and making it last until the next harvest, and several times multiplying the bread that he was distributing to the poor.
</p><p>One day, he walked across the water to lead a boatload of fishermen through stormy waves to the safety of the shore. He was reputed to have had <a href="/wiki/Bilocation" title="Bilocation">bilocation</a> and the ability to read souls.<sup id="cite_ref-liguori_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liguori-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His last will was a small note on the door of his cell: "Here the will of God is done, as God wills, and as long as God wills." He died at 29 of <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> on 16 October 1755 in <a href="/wiki/Materdomini,_Caposele" title="Materdomini, Caposele">Materdomini</a>, Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patron_of_mothers">Patron of mothers</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_Majella&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Patron of mothers">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>One miracle in particular explains how Majella became known as the special patron of mothers. A few months before his death, he visited the Pirofalo family and accidentally dropped his handkerchief. One of the Pirofalo girls spotted the handkerchief moments after he had left the house, and she ran after Gerard to return it. "Keep it,"<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Inline_citation#When_you_must_use_inline_citations" title="Wikipedia:Inline citation"><span title="The text near this tag needs a citation. (October 2022)">This quote needs a citation</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> he said to her. "You may need it some day."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Inline_citation#When_you_must_use_inline_citations" title="Wikipedia:Inline citation"><span title="The text near this tag needs a citation. (October 2022)">This quote needs a citation</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Years later when the girl, now a married woman, was on the verge of dying in childbirth, she remembered the words of the saintly lay brother. She asked for the handkerchief to be brought to her. Almost immediately, the pain disappeared and she gave birth to a healthy child. That was no small feat in an era when only one out of three pregnancies resulted in a live birth, and word of the miracle spread quickly.
</p><p>Because of the miracles that God worked through Gerard's prayers with mothers, the mothers of Italy took Gerard to their hearts and made him their patron. At the process of his beatification, one witness testified that he was known as "il santo dei felice parti," the saint of happy childbirths.<sup id="cite_ref-Tobin_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tobin-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His devotion has become very popular in North America, both in the United States and Canada.<sup id="cite_ref-liguori_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liguori-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_Majella&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Gerard_Church_(Lima,_Ohio)_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Saint_Gerard_Church_%28Lima%2C_Ohio%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg/170px-Saint_Gerard_Church_%28Lima%2C_Ohio%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="170" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Saint_Gerard_Church_%28Lima%2C_Ohio%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg/255px-Saint_Gerard_Church_%28Lima%2C_Ohio%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Saint_Gerard_Church_%28Lima%2C_Ohio%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg/340px-Saint_Gerard_Church_%28Lima%2C_Ohio%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3107" data-file-height="3111" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Gerard_Church_(Lima,_Ohio)_-_St._Gerard_Majella_relic.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A <a href="/wiki/Relic#Christianity" title="Relic">relic</a> of Majella on display for <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lima,_Ohio" title="Lima, Ohio">Lima, Ohio</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Majella was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 29 January 1893 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonized</a> less than twelve years later on 11 December 1904 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathency_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathency-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Saint Gerard Majella is October 16.
</p><p>In 1977, St. Gerard's Chapel in <a href="/wiki/St._Lucy%27s_Church_(Newark,_New_Jersey)" title="St. Lucy&#39;s Church (Newark, New Jersey)">St. Lucy's Church</a> (<a href="/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey" title="Newark, New Jersey">Newark, New Jersey</a>) was dedicated as a national shrine.  Each year during the Feast days, which include October 16, there are traditional lights, music, food stands and a street procession. People come from all over to celebrate. Devotees also visit the shrine throughout the year to petition the help of St. Gerard.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/St._Gerard_Majella_Annual_Novena" title="St. Gerard Majella Annual Novena">St. Gerard Majella Annual Novena</a> takes place every year at St. Josephs Church in <a href="/wiki/Dundalk" title="Dundalk">Dundalk</a>, Ireland. This annual nine-day novena is the biggest festival of faith in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. St. Joseph's sponsors the St. Gerard's Family League, an association of Christians united in prayer for their own and other families, to preserve Christian values in their home and family lives.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gerard_Majella&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_(Trinity,_Indiana)_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_%28Trinity%2C_Indiana%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg/220px-Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_%28Trinity%2C_Indiana%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="392" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_%28Trinity%2C_Indiana%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg/330px-Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_%28Trinity%2C_Indiana%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_%28Trinity%2C_Indiana%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg/440px-Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_%28Trinity%2C_Indiana%29_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1985" data-file-height="3539" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_(Trinity,_Indiana)_-_St._Gerard_Majella_statue.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Gerard Majella at the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Catholic_Church_(Trinity,_Indiana)" title="Holy Trinity Catholic Church (Trinity, Indiana)">Holy Trinity Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trinity,_Indiana" title="Trinity, Indiana">Trinity, Indiana</a></div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/St_Gerard%27s_Church_and_Monastery" title="St Gerard&#39;s Church and Monastery">St Gerard's Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wellington" title="Wellington">Wellington</a>, New Zealand, built in 1908, was the first church to be dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-NZHPT226_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NZHPT226-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Other churches dedicated to him are located in: Sapugahawatte, Dodangoda, Sri Lanka; Kirimatiyana, Lunuwila, Sri Lanka; <a href="/wiki/Preston,_Lancashire" title="Preston, Lancashire">Preston, Lancashire</a>, England; <a href="/wiki/Bristol" title="Bristol">Bristol</a>, England; and <a href="/wiki/Westminster,_Western_Australia" title="Westminster, Western Australia">Westminster, Western Australia</a>.
</p><p>Catholic parishes dedicated to him are located in Hollis (in the <a href="/wiki/Borough_of_Queens" class="mw-redirect" title="Borough of Queens">Borough of Queens</a>), <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>; <a href="/wiki/Kirkwood,_Missouri" title="Kirkwood, Missouri">Kirkwood, Missouri</a>; <a href="/wiki/Port_Jefferson_Station" class="mw-redirect" title="Port Jefferson Station">Port Jefferson Station</a>, <a href="/wiki/Long_Island" title="Long Island">Long Island</a>, Brooklyn Park, Minnesota, New York; and <a href="/wiki/Del_Rey,_Los_Angeles" title="Del Rey, Los Angeles">Del Rey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Los_Angeles" title="Los Angeles">Los Angeles</a>, California.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_San_Gerardo_Maiella" title="Sanctuary of San Gerardo Maiella">Sanctuary of San Gerardo Maiella</a> is a basilica in <a href="/wiki/Materdomini_(Caposele)" class="mw-redirect" title="Materdomini (Caposele)">Materdomini</a>, Italy dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Senior <a href="/wiki/Coroner" title="Coroner">Coroner</a> for Liverpool and Wirral sits at the Gerard Majella Courthouse in <a href="/wiki/Liverpool" title="Liverpool">Liverpool</a>.
</p><p>In Scotland, there is a church and primary school dedicated to St Gerard Majella in <a href="/wiki/Bellshill" title="Bellshill">Bellshill</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lanarkshire" title="Lanarkshire">Lanarkshire</a>, opened in 1971 &amp; 1973 respectively. The maternity hospital, now a housing estate, was located close by, hence the choice of name of church and school.
</p><p>Two towns in <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>, are named in his honour: <a href="/wiki/Saint-G%C3%A9rard-Majella,_Quebec" title="Saint-Gérard-Majella, Quebec">one in the Montérégie region</a> and <a href="/wiki/L%27Assomption,_Quebec" title="L&#39;Assomption, Quebec">another in the Lanaudière region</a>. Another town, St-Jean-Sur-Richelieu, has one of its parishes named after him.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> (<a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>) a model school was named after Saint Gerard. This school was exhibited on the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Wereldtentoonstelling_van_1913&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Wereldtentoonstelling van 1913 (page does not exist)">world exhibition of 1913</a> in Ghent as a model for Belgium's future school buildings. In 1914 it was rebuilt after the exhibition with the same stones. Nowadays the Saint Gerard School is used by a charity organisation "Geraarke" (local name) which supports poor people with clothes and food packages.
In Nigeria, there is a shrine dedicated to St Gerard Majella at a place called Oba, in <a href="/wiki/Anambra_State" title="Anambra State">Anambra State</a>. It was given to the Redemptorists of the Vice-Province of Nigeria by the Archbishop of Onitsha, Most Rev. Valerian Okeke. The Redemeptorists also built a school for the poor and most abandoned in the shrine site dedicated to St Gerard Majella.
</p><p>He was featured on an Italian 45-<a href="/wiki/Euro" title="Euro">eurocent</a> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.ibolli.it/php/em-italia-3685-Ritratto%20di%20San%20Gerardo%20Maiella%20e%20santuario.php">postage stamp</a> in May 2005.
</p>
