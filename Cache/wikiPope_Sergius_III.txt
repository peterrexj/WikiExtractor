					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Head of the Catholic Church from 904 to 911</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color: #F7D79C;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;display:inline;">Pope</div><br /><div style="display:inline" class="fn">Sergius III</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-subheader role"><a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Church</th><td class="infobox-data org"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Papacy began</th><td class="infobox-data">29 January 904</td></tr><tr class="note"><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Papacy ended</th><td class="infobox-data">14 April 911</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Predecessor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_V" title="Pope Leo V">Leo V</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Successor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Anastasius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Anastasius III">Anastasius III</a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Personal details</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;860</span><br /><div style="display:inline" class="birthplace"><a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a></div></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><span style="display:none">(<span class="dday deathdate">0911-04-14</span>)</span>14 April 911<br />Rome, Papal States</td></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-below"><a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Pope Sergius (disambiguation)">Other popes  named Sergius</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Pope Sergius III</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;860</span> − 14 April 911) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and nominal ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 29 January 904 to his death. He was <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> during a period of violence and disorder in <a href="/wiki/Central_Italy" title="Central Italy">central Italy</a>, when warring aristocratic factions sought to use the material and military resources of the papacy.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At the behest of <a href="/wiki/Theophylact_I_of_Tusculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Theophylact I of Tusculum">Theophylact I of Tusculum</a>, Sergius seized the papal throne from <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Christopher" title="Antipope Christopher">Antipope Christopher</a>, who in turn had deposed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_V" title="Pope Leo V">Pope Leo V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_113_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_113-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Sergius' reign was subsequently marked by Theophylact's influence. As pope, Sergius continued many ecclesiastical controversies of his predecessors, including conflict over <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Pope Formosus</a>' legacy, annulling all ordinations made by the late pope,<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_122_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_122-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Filioque" title="Filioque">filioque</a></i> controversy with eastern patriarchs.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_130_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_130-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His pontificate was similarly marked by temporal conflicts, with Sergius' refusal to crown <a href="/wiki/Berengar_I_of_Italy" title="Berengar I of Italy">Berengar I of Italy</a> as <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_127_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_127-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and his support of <a href="/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors" title="List of Byzantine emperors">Byzantine Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Leo_VI_the_Wise" title="Leo VI the Wise">Leo VI the Wise</a>'s fourth marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-Treadgold_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Treadgold-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Sergius also saw the restoration of the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Palace" title="Lateran Palace">Lateran Palace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sergius III today is largely seen as an unscrupulous character as contemporary records had included a number of accusations against him; Sergius III had reputedly ordered the murder of his two immediate predecessors, Leo V and Christopher, and allegedly fathered an illegitimate son who later became pope, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XI" title="Pope John XI">John XI</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Pontificate" title="Pontificate">pontificate</a> has been variously described as "dismal and disgraceful",<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and "efficient and ruthless".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_career">Early life and career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sergius was the son of Benedictus,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and traditionally was believed descended from a noble Roman family, although it has been speculated that he was in fact related to the family of <a href="/wiki/Theophylact_I_of_Tusculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Theophylact I of Tusculum">Theophylact I of Tusculum</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">subdeacon</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Marinus_I" title="Pope Marinus I">Pope Marinus I</a>, followed by his being raised to the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_V" title="Pope Stephen V">Pope Stephen V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> During the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Pope Formosus</a> (891–896), he was a member of the party of nobles who supported the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Lambert_II_of_Spoleto" class="mw-redirect" title="Lambert II of Spoleto">Lambert</a>, who was the opponent of Formosus and the pope's preferred imperial candidate, <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Carinthia" title="Arnulf of Carinthia">Arnulf of Carinthia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Formosus consecrated Sergius as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Caere" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Caere">Bishop of Caere</a> (<a href="/wiki/Cerveteri" title="Cerveteri">Cerveteri</a>) in 893, apparently in order to remove him from Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Sergius ceased to act as Bishop of Caere with the death of Formosus in 896, as all of the ordinations conferred by Formosus were declared null and void,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> although Formosus' ordination of Sergius was later reconfirmed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Theodore_II_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Theodore II (Catholic)">Theodore II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He also actively participated in the farcical <a href="/wiki/Cadaver_synod" class="mw-redirect" title="Cadaver synod">Cadaver synod</a> that condemned the pontificate of Formosus.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With the death of Theodore in 898, Sergius, with a small following of Roman nobility led by his father Benedictus, attempted to have himself elected <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, contrary to the wishes of <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Lambert" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Lambert">Emperor Lambert</a>, who was also <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Spoleto" title="Duke of Spoleto">duke of Spoleto</a>. Although Sergius was actually elected, a rival candidate, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_IX" title="Pope John IX">Pope John IX</a> (898–900), was also elected.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> With Lambert's support, John was successfully installed as pope, and one of his first acts was to convene a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> which  <a href="/wiki/Excommunicate" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicate">excommunicated</a> Sergius and his followers.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Sergius was then forcibly exiled by Lambert, fleeing to his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> at Caere, where he placed himself under the protection of Margrave <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_II_of_Tuscany" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalbert II of Tuscany">Adalbert II of Tuscany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_reign">Papal reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papal reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By the time the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Christopher" title="Antipope Christopher">Antipope Christopher</a> (903–904) seized the chair of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> by force, circumstances had changed at Rome, with the rise of the <i><a href="/wiki/Magister_militum" title="Magister militum">magister militum</a></i> Theophylact of Tusculum, who had been stationed at Rome by the retreating Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Blind" title="Louis the Blind">Louis the Blind</a> in 902. Putting himself at the head of a faction of the nobility, Theophylact revolted against Christopher, and asked Sergius to return to Rome to become pope.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_113_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_113-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Sergius accepted, and with the armed backing of Adalbert II, he entered Rome, by which stage Christopher had already been cast into prison by Theophylact. Sergius was then consecrated pope on 29 January 904.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sergius III owed his rise to the power of his new patron Theophylact, and rewarded him with the position of <i><a href="/wiki/Vestararius" title="Vestararius">sacri palatii vestararius</a></i>, the principal official at the top of papal patronage in control of the disbursements, and thus of patronage. All real power now devolved onto Theophylact, and Sergius essentially became his puppet.  Perhaps the first clear sign of this shift in power was the fate of Sergius' two predecessors, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_V" title="Pope Leo V">Pope Leo V</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">Antipope</a> Christopher. According to the pro-Formosan <a href="/wiki/Eugenius_Vulgarius" title="Eugenius Vulgarius">Eugenius Vulgarius</a>, Sergius ordered both men to be strangled in prison sometime in early 904.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> That both men were murdered during Sergius' pontificate appears probable, although other accounts state that Christopher at least was allowed to retire to a monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Given where the real power lay, it seems more likely that either Theophylact gave the orders directly, or that he directed Sergius to give the orders.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> For the remainder of his pontificate, Sergius <a href="/wiki/Nepotism" title="Nepotism">promoted his family</a> and members of his aristocratic party to positions of authority and prominence within the church.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Activity_in_Italy">Activity in Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Activity in Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pope Sergius III convoked a synod which annulled all the <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordinations</a> of Formosus and required all <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishops</a> ordained by Formosus to be reordained. It was alleged that Sergius managed to get the consent of the Roman clergy at the synod by threatening them with exile, violence or through the use of bribery.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_122_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_122-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The decision to require reordination was very unpopular, and those affected at sees distant from Rome not only ignored the synod's instructions, but wrote letters both condemning the revoking of ordinations and justifying validity of the original ordinations.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The ruling was subsequently reversed again after his death.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Confirming his continued support of the anti-Formosus faction, Sergius honoured the murdered <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_VI" title="Pope Stephen VI">Pope Stephen VI</a> (896–897), who had been responsible for the "<a href="/wiki/Cadaver_Synod" title="Cadaver Synod">Cadaver Synod</a>" that had condemned and mutilated the corpse of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Pope Formosus</a>, by writing a laudatory <a href="/wiki/Epitaph" title="Epitaph">epitaph</a> on Stephen VI's tombstone.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> For centuries it was believed that Sergius then had the much-abused corpse of Formosus exhumed once more, tried, found guilty again, and beheaded, thus in effect conducting a second Cadaver Synod.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> However, the source for this was <a href="/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona" title="Liutprand of Cremona">Liutprand of Cremona</a>, who mistakenly placed the cadaver synod in the pontificate of Sergius III, instead of Stephen VI.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although neither Sergius nor Theophylact supported the continued nominal rule of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Blind" title="Louis the Blind">Louis the Blind</a>, they were somewhat unwilling to grant the imperial title to the only other contender, <a href="/wiki/Berengar_I_of_Italy" title="Berengar I of Italy">Berengar I of Italy</a>. On the one occasion that Sergius agreed to crown Berengar in around 906, Berengar was prevented from reaching <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> by the forces of <a href="/wiki/Alberic_I_of_Spoleto" title="Alberic I of Spoleto">Alberic I of Spoleto</a> and Adalbert II of Tuscany, both of whom had been supporters of Sergius, but were unhappy with his decision to support Berengar. Nevertheless, Berengar's unwillingness to control his vassals also contributed to the papal reluctance; when Albuinus, the <a href="/wiki/Margrave_of_Istria" class="mw-redirect" title="Margrave of Istria">margrave of Istria</a>, began taking papal territory off <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_X" title="Pope John X">John</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Ravenna" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Ravenna">archbishop of Ravenna</a>, in 907, Sergius had written to Albuinus asking for him to desist.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> When Sergius was ignored, the pope wrote to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Pola" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Pola">bishop of Pola</a> in 910, making it clear that: "he would never bestow the (imperial) crown on Berenger till he promised to take the (Istrian) March from Albuinus, and give it to some better man."<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_127_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_127-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sergius rebuilt the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Palace" title="Lateran Palace">Lateran Palace</a>, which had been shattered by an earthquake in 896, and then stripped of its treasures by the Antipope Christopher. Sergius refurbished it with objects, images and crucifixes, and decorated its newly built walls with frescos.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> In 905 he provided funds to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Suburbicarian_Diocese_of_Porto-Santa_Rufina" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Suburbicarian Diocese of Porto-Santa Rufina">Church of Silva Candida</a>, which had been devastated by a <a href="/wiki/Saracen" title="Saracen">Saracen</a> raid.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> He also helped with the rebuilding of <a href="/wiki/Nonantola_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Nonantola Abbey">Nonantola Abbey</a>, which had suffered attacks from the <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Hungarian people">Magyars</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> and finally he granted privileges to some monasteries and churches in <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West</a> and <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Francia</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_Constantinople">Relations with Constantinople</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Relations with Constantinople">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Sergius, like his predecessors, continued to defend the <a href="/wiki/Filioque" title="Filioque">Filioque</a> interpolation into the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a>, which was at odds with the position of the eastern church. Papal legates who attended the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Synod_of_Trosle&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Synod of Trosle (page does not exist)">Synod of Trosle</a> in June 909 attacked the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Byzantine</a> position, which the synod then condemned in the fourteenth <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon</a>:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>As the Holy Apostolic See has made known to us that the blasphemous errors of a certain <a href="/wiki/Photius" class="mw-redirect" title="Photius">Photius</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Ghost" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Ghost">Holy Ghost</a> are still vigorous in the East, errors which teach that the Holy Spirit proceeds not from the Son but from the Father only we exhort you venerable brethren, together with us, in accordance with the admonition of the ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_See" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman See">Roman See</a>, after a careful study of the works of the Fathers, to draw from the quiver of Holy Writ arrows sharp enough to slay the monster which is again springing into life.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann_130_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann_130-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Almost a century later, this decision led to the removal of Sergius's name from the <a href="/wiki/Diptych" title="Diptych">Diptychs</a> by <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Sergius_II_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Sergius II of Constantinople">Patriarch Sergius II of Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, the major issue with <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> that presented itself during Sergius' pontificate was the question over the fourth marriage of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Byzantine Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Leo_VI_the_Wise" title="Leo VI the Wise">Leo VI the Wise</a>. Both the emperor, who wanted to marry <a href="/wiki/Zoe_Karbonopsina" title="Zoe Karbonopsina">Zoe Karbonopsina</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">patriarch of Constantinople</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Mystikos" title="Nicholas Mystikos">Nicholas Mystikos</a>, appealed to Sergius; the pope sent <a href="/wiki/Papal_legates" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal legates">legates</a> to Constantinople, who confirmed the pope's ruling in favour of the emperor, on the grounds that fourth marriages had not been condemned by the Church as a whole.<sup id="cite_ref-Treadgold_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Treadgold-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Nicholas' refusal to accept this ruling saw him deposed by Leo VI, upon which he too appealed to Sergius, claiming his deposition was unjustified.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Alleged_affair_with_Marozia">Alleged affair with Marozia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Alleged affair with Marozia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Sergius' ties with the family of Theophylact were made even closer, at least according to rumour, by Sergius' supposed affair with Theophylact's daughter, <a href="/wiki/Marozia" title="Marozia">Marozia</a>. This relationship was promoted by Marozia's mother, <a href="/wiki/Theodora_(senatrix)" title="Theodora (senatrix)">Theodora</a>, and the result of this affair was a male child who in time became <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XI" title="Pope John XI">Pope John XI</a> (931–935).<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The only source of this alleged affair is the chronicler <a href="/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona" title="Liutprand of Cremona">Liutprand of Cremona</a>, writing some 50 years after the events of Sergius' pontificate. Neither <a href="/wiki/Auxilius_of_Naples" title="Auxilius of Naples">Auxilius of Naples</a> nor <a href="/wiki/Eugenius_Vulgarius" title="Eugenius Vulgarius">Eugenius Vulgarius</a>, both of whom were exact contemporaries of Sergius, and both of whom were hostile towards Sergius for his attacks on Formosus, mention this allegation at all.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> The highly reliable chronicler <a href="/wiki/Flodoard" title="Flodoard">Flodoard</a>, who also is a contemporary does not mention it either. It should also be noted that Liutprand, as firm supporter of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Otto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I">Otto I</a> was often heavily biased against the papacy.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The affair, while not an impossibility, would certainly not have persisted beyond Marozia's marriage to <a href="/wiki/Alberic_I_of_Spoleto" title="Alberic I of Spoleto">Alberic I of Spoleto</a> in 909. The question of whether Theophylact and Theodora needed to tie Sergius to them by such means, particularly when Sergius was already deeply indebted to them for his elevation to the papacy, as well as wasting Marozia in a relationship when, as the daughter of an important house, she would have been a valuable tool to link via marriage to another noble house, is open to debate. The birth of the future John XI in 910, after her marriage to Alberic, would seem to indicate that Sergius was not the father.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Sergius III died on 14 April 911, and was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Anastasius_III" title="Pope Anastasius III">Pope Anastasius III</a>. He was buried in the Church of St. Peter, between the Silver gate and the gate of Ravenna.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sergius_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Much of Sergius' pontificate has been maligned throughout history, principally through the reporting of his character and the state of Rome at the time by <a href="/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona" title="Liutprand of Cremona">Liutprand of Cremona</a>. His recounting of the period has led sixteenth century cardinal and  historian <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Annales_Ecclesiastici" title="Annales Ecclesiastici">Annales Ecclesiastici</a></i> to call it the <i><a href="/wiki/Saeculum_obscurum" title="Saeculum obscurum">Saeculum obscurum</a></i>, or dark century.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> Similarly 19th century protestant historians saw this period as a "pornocracy", or "rule of the harlots",<sup id="cite_ref-Squatriti_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Squatriti-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> a reversal of the natural order as they saw it, according to <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_pontificalis" class="mw-redirect" title="Liber pontificalis">Liber pontificalis</a></i> and a later chronicler who was also biased against Sergius III. This "pornocracy" was an age with women in power: <a href="/wiki/Theodora_(senatrix)" title="Theodora (senatrix)">Theodora</a>, whom Liutprand characterized as a "shameless whore... [who] exercised power on the Roman citizenry like a man"<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Inline_citation#When_you_must_use_inline_citations" title="Wikipedia:Inline citation"><span title="The text near this tag needs a citation. (January 2021)">This quote needs a citation</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and her daughter <a href="/wiki/Marozia" title="Marozia">Marozia</a>, the mother of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XI" title="Pope John XI">Pope John XI</a> and reputed to be the mistress of Sergius III, largely upon a remark by Liutprand.<sup id="cite_ref-Marozia_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marozia-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a>, writing in the 16th century, and basing himself on Liutprand, was particularly scathing, describing Sergius as: "a wretch, worthy of the rope and of fire... flames could not have caused this execrable monster to suffer the punishments which he merited. It is impossible to believe that such a pope was a lawful one."<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> The reality is that when Sergius was forcibly exiled by <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Spoleto" class="mw-redirect" title="Lambert of Spoleto">Lambert of Spoleto</a>, all the official records were destroyed; consequently, most of the surviving documentation about Sergius comes from his pro-Formosan opponents who had fled to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> Horace Mann, writing in the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> states the following concerning the alleged illicit relationship of Pope Sergius III with Marozia: "that he put his two predecessors to death, and by illicit relations with Marozia had a son, who was afterwards John XI, must be regarded as highly doubtful. These assertions are only made by bitter or ill-informed adversaries, and are inconsistent with what is said of him by respectable contemporaries."<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nevertheless, most modern opinions about Sergius' pontificate remain poor. According to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Horace_K._Mann&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Horace K. Mann (page does not exist)">Horace K. Mann</a>, "Sergius was, unfortunately, a pronounced party-man, and anxious for the supremacy of his party."<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> The best that <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_Gregorovius" title="Ferdinand Gregorovius">Ferdinand Gregorovius</a> could say of him was: "That Sergius, who remained Pope throughout the storms of seven years, was at least a man of energy must be admitted, although apostolic virtues are scarcely to be looked for in a character such as his."<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> James S. Packer described him as malignant and ferocious, slaughtering his enemies with a private army,<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> while Walter Ullmann described Sergius as a typical representative of the House of Theophylact, concerned with power and sexual liaisons.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
