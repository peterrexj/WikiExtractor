<p><b>Agapitus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Agapito</i>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, who died on August 18, perhaps in 274,<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> a date that the latest editions of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> say is uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to his legend, 16-year-old Agapitus, who may have been a member of the noble Anicia family of <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was condemned to death, under the prefect Antiochus and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Aurelian" title="Aurelian">Aurelian</a>, for being a <a href="/wiki/Early_Christians" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christians">Christian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After being captured and tortured during the persecution of Aurelian, he was taken to the local arena in <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Damnatio_ad_bestias" title="Damnatio ad bestias">thrown to the wild beasts</a>. However, the animals refused to touch him and he was thus beheaded.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agapitus_of_Palestrina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agapitus is mentioned in the ancient martyrologies, including the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Fulda_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Fulda monastery">Fulda</a> Martyrology. Based on doubts regarding the details of his martyrdom, some of which were related in earlier editions of the Roman Martyrology,<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> editions from the end of the 20th century onward give only: "In Palestrina, Lazio, Saint Agapitus, martyr."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Around the 5th century, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Felix_III" title="Pope Felix III">Pope Felix III</a> built a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> in his honour on the supposed site of his martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His relics were kept in the basilica, and a cemetery grew around it.<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At some uncertain date, his relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">transferred</a> to the present cathedral of Palestrina.<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTIEBEATI_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTIEBEATI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some of them were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on" title="Besançon">Besançon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SAINTS_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SAINTS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Agapitus is honoured in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> by a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">commemoration</a> added to the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> and <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">canonical hours</a> in the liturgy of the day within the <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">Octave</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_Pope_Pius_XII#Octaves" title="General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII">abolished</a> all octaves apart from those of Christmas, Easter, and Pentecost, including that of the Assumption. Accordingly, in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a> the celebration of Saint Agapitus appears as a commemoration in the ordinary weekday Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
