<p><b>Proclus</b> (died 24 July 446) was an <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodoxy">Oriental Orthodoxy</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Proclus_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Proclus became <a href="/wiki/Secretary" title="Secretary">secretary</a> to <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Atticus_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Atticus of Constantinople">Archbishop Atticus of Constantinople</a> (406–425). Saint Proclus was the friend and disciple of Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a> who <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> him <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>. Atticus' successor, <a href="/wiki/Sisinnius_I_of_Constantinople" title="Sisinnius I of Constantinople">Sisinnius I</a> (426–427), <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> him <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cyzicus" title="Cyzicus">Cyzicus</a>, but the Nestorians there refused to receive him, and he remained at Constantinople. On the death of Sisinnius, the famous <a href="/wiki/Nestorius" title="Nestorius">Nestorius</a> succeeded as Archbishop of Constantinople (428–431), and early in 429, on a festival of the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a> (<a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>), Proclus preached his celebrated <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermon</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Incarnation" title="Incarnation">Incarnation</a>, which was later inserted in the beginning of the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/acta" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:acta">Acts</a> of the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Ephesus" class="mw-redirect" title="First Council of Ephesus">Council of Ephesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Maximianus_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Maximianus of Constantinople">Maximianus</a> (431–434) died on <a href="/wiki/Great_and_Holy_Thursday" class="mw-redirect" title="Great and Holy Thursday">Great and Holy Thursday</a>, Proclus was immediately enthroned by the permission of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a> and the bishops gathered at Constantinople. His first care was the funeral of his predecessor, and he then sent to both Patriarchs <a href="/wiki/Cyril_of_Alexandria" title="Cyril of Alexandria">Cyril of Alexandria</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_I_of_Antioch" title="John I of Antioch">John of Antioch</a> the usual <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synodical</a> letters announcing his appointment, both of whom approved of it.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 436 the bishops of <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a> consulted Proclus upon certain <a href="/wiki/Doctrine" title="Doctrine">doctrines</a> prevalent in their country and attributed to <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Mopsuestia" title="Theodore of Mopsuestia">Theodore of Mopsuestia</a>, asking for their <a href="/wiki/Anathema" title="Anathema">condemnation</a>. Proclus replied the next year in the celebrated letter known as the <i>Tome to the Armenians</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESchwartz,_Eduard._1914.Acta_conciliorum_oecumenicorum.Concilium_universale_Constantinopolitanum_sub_Iustiniano_habitum,4_Volumen_alterum.187–195_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESchwartz,_Eduard._1914.Acta_conciliorum_oecumenicorum.Concilium_universale_Constantinopolitanum_sub_Iustiniano_habitum,4_Volumen_alterum.187–195-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which he sent to the Eastern bishops, asking them to sign it and to join in condemning the doctrines arraigned by the Armenians. They approved of the letters, but from admiration of Theodore hesitated to condemn the doctrines attributed to him. Proclus replied that while he desired the extracts subjoined to his Tome to be condemned, he had not attributed them to Theodore or any individual, not desiring the condemnation of any person.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Rescript" title="Rescript">rescript</a> from Theodosius procured by Proclus, declaring his wish that all should live in peace and that no imputation should be made against anyone who died in communion with the church, appeased the storm. The whole affair showed conspicuously the moderation and tact of Proclus. In 438, he transferred the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of his old master, Saint John Chrysostom, from <a href="/wiki/Comana_Pontica" title="Comana Pontica">Comana</a> back to Constantinople,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> where he interred them with great honour in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Twelve_apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of the Twelve apostles">Church of the Twelve apostles</a>. This action reconciled to the church those of Saint John's adherents who had separated themselves in consequence of the <a href="/wiki/Deposition_(politics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Deposition (politics)">deposition</a> as Archbishop<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> which they regarded as having been unjust.
</p><p>In 439, at the request of a <a href="/wiki/Diplomatic_mission" title="Diplomatic mission">deputation</a> from <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Mazaca" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea Mazaca">Caesarea</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>, Proclus selected as their new bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Thalassius_(bishop)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Thalassius (bishop) (page does not exist)">Thalassius</a>, who was about to be appointed <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_prefect" title="Praetorian prefect">praetorian prefect</a> of the East.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the time of Proclus the <a href="/wiki/Trisagion" title="Trisagion">Trisagion</a> came into use. The occasion is said to have been a time when violent earthquakes lasted for four months at Constantinople, so that the people were obliged to leave the city and encamp in the fields.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Proclus died most probably in July, 446. He appears to have been wise, moderate, and conciliatory, desirous, while strictly adhering to <a href="/wiki/Orthodoxy" title="Orthodoxy">Orthodoxy</a> himself, to win over those who differed from him by persuasion rather than force.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Proclus_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The works of Proclus consist of 20 <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a> (some of doubtful authenticity).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Five were published by <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Mai" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Mai">Cardinal Mai</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> of which 3 are preserved only in a <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> version, the <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> being lost; 7 letters, along with several addressed to him by other persons; and a few fragments of other letters and sermons.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Proclus was cited by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/John_Henry_Newman" title="John Henry Newman">John Henry Newman</a> for his work on <a href="/wiki/Mariology" title="Mariology">Mariology</a> and his strong support of the conciliar dogma on the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDaniel1911-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Proclus_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Eastern Orthodox Church celebrates his feast day on November 20.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic Church lists him on October 24.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
