<p><b>Birinus</b> (also <i>Berin</i>, <i>Birin</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;600</span>&#160;– 3 December 649 or 650) was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Dorchester</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPowickeFryde1961219_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPowickeFryde1961219-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was known as the "<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/apostle" class="extiw" title="wikt:apostle">Apostle</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">West Saxons</a>" for his conversion of the Kingdom of Wessex to Christianity. He is venerated as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican</a> churches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_ministry">Life and ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Birinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Canterbury" title="Augustine of Canterbury">Augustine of Canterbury</a> performed the <a href="/wiki/Christianisation_of_Anglo-Saxon_England" title="Christianisation of Anglo-Saxon England">initial conversions</a> in England, Birinus, a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frank</a>, came to the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> in 634,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalsh2007102_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalsh2007102-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> landing at the port of <i><a href="/wiki/Hamwic" class="mw-redirect" title="Hamwic">Hamwic</a></i>, now in the <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s,_Southampton" title="St Mary&#39;s, Southampton">St Mary's</a> area of <a href="/wiki/Southampton" title="Southampton">Southampton</a>. During Birinus's brief time at Hamwic, <a href="/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Church,_Southampton" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mary&#39;s Church, Southampton">St Mary's Church</a> was founded.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEColes19816_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEColes19816-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk, Birinus had been made bishop by Asterius in <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBede19693.7Thompson1886_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBede19693.7Thompson1886-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_I" title="Pope Honorius I">Pope Honorius I</a> created the commission to convert the West Saxons.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirby200038_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirby200038-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 635, he persuaded the West Saxon king <a href="/wiki/Cynegils" title="Cynegils">Cynegils</a> to allow him to preach. Cynegils was trying to create an alliance with <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria" title="Oswald of Northumbria">Oswald of Northumbria</a>, with whom he intended to fight the <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercians</a>. At the final talks between kings, the sticking point was that Oswald, a Christian, would not ally himself with a <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">pagan</a>. Cynegils then converted and was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptised</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThompson1886_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThompson1886-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He gave Birinus <a href="/wiki/Dorchester-on-Thames" class="mw-redirect" title="Dorchester-on-Thames">Dorchester-on-Thames</a> for his episcopal see.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirby200038_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirby200038-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Birinus's original commission entailed preaching to parts of Britain where no missionary efforts had reached and may have included instructions to reach the Mercians. But he ultimately remained in Wessex.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirby200051_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirby200051-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Birinus is said to have been active in establishing churches in Wessex.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBede19693.7_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBede19693.7-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Birinus supposedly laid the foundations for <a href="/wiki/Reading_Minster" title="Reading Minster">St Mary's</a> in <a href="/wiki/Reading,_Berkshire" title="Reading, Berkshire">Reading</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and other churches such as the church of <a href="/wiki/St_Peter_and_St_Paul,_Checkendon" title="St Peter and St Paul, Checkendon">St Peter and St Paul, Checkendon</a>, near Reading.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Tradition has it that Birinus built the first church at <a href="/wiki/Ipsden" title="Ipsden">Ipsden</a>, as a small chapel on Berins Hill, about two miles east of the present church.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Birinus baptised Cynegils's son <a href="/wiki/Cwichelm_of_Wessex" title="Cwichelm of Wessex">Cwichelm</a> (died 636) in 636<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBately1986an.&amp;nbsp;636Davis191257_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBately1986an.&amp;nbsp;636Davis191257-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and grandson Cuthred (died 661) in 639, to whom he stood as godfather.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBately1986an.&amp;nbsp;639Davis191257_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBately1986an.&amp;nbsp;639Davis191257-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Birinus died in Dorchester on 3 December in 649 or 650.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELivingstone201368_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELivingstone201368-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Birinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Birinus' <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 3 December in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKommodatos1985_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKommodatos1985-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> but some churches celebrate his feast on 5 December.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELivingstone201368_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELivingstone201368-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His feast was added to the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> in the late 16th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFarmer201153_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarmer201153-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>, his <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">feast day</a> falls on 4 September and has the status of a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemoration</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChurch_of_England_Liturgical_Commission200013_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChurch_of_England_Liturgical_Commission200013-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> His relics were eventually translated to Winchester after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirby200049_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirby200049-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A small number of <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England_parish_church" title="Church of England parish church">Church of England parish churches</a> are dedicated to Birinus, including those at <a href="/wiki/Berinsfield" title="Berinsfield">Berinsfield</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Redlynch,_Wiltshire" title="Redlynch, Wiltshire">Redlynch</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>. The Catholic church in Dorchester, one of the first built after the <a href="/wiki/Universalis_Ecclesiae" title="Universalis Ecclesiae">restoration of the hierarchy</a><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>, is also dedicated to Birinus.
</p>
