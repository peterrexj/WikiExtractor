<p><b>Francis Michael Forde</b> (18 July 1890&#160;&#8211;&#32;28 January 1983) was an Australian politician who served as the 15th <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime minister of Australia">prime minister of Australia</a> from 6 to 13 July 1945, holding office as the deputy leader of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party" title="Australian Labor Party">Australian Labor Party</a> (ALP). He was prime minister in a <a href="/wiki/Acting_(law)" title="Acting (law)">caretaker</a> capacity directly following the death of prime minister <a href="/wiki/John_Curtin" title="John Curtin">John Curtin</a>, and is the <a href="/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Australia_by_time_in_office" title="List of prime ministers of Australia by time in office">shortest-serving</a> prime minister in Australia's history.
</p><p>Forde was born in <a href="/wiki/Mitchell,_Queensland" title="Mitchell, Queensland">Mitchell, Queensland</a>, to Irish immigrant parents. He eventually settled in <a href="/wiki/Rockhampton" title="Rockhampton">Rockhampton</a>, and was a schoolteacher and telegraphist before entering politics. Having joined the ALP at a young age, Forde was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Queensland_Legislative_Assembly" class="mw-redirect" title="Queensland Legislative Assembly">Queensland Legislative Assembly</a> in 1917, aged 26. He transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Australian_House_of_Representatives" title="Australian House of Representatives">House of Representatives</a> at the <a href="/wiki/1922_Australian_federal_election" title="1922 Australian federal election">1922 federal election</a>, winning the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Capricornia" title="Division of Capricornia">Division of Capricornia</a>. Forde was an assistant minister and minister in the <a href="/wiki/James_Scullin" title="James Scullin">Scullin government</a> from 1929 to 1932, and was largely responsible for the government's <a href="/wiki/Tariff" title="Tariff">tariff</a> policy. He entered the cabinet in 1931 as <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Trade_and_Customs_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Trade and Customs (Australia)">Minister for Trade and Customs</a>.
</p><p>After Labor's landslide defeat at the <a href="/wiki/1931_Australian_federal_election" title="1931 Australian federal election">1931 election</a>, Forde was elected deputy leader in place of <a href="/wiki/Ted_Theodore" title="Ted Theodore">Ted Theodore</a>. He was expected to become party leader after Scullin's retirement in 1935 but lost to John Curtin <a href="/wiki/1935_Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_election" title="1935 Australian Labor Party leadership election">by one vote</a>. He returned to cabinet in 1941 as <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Defence_(Australia)" title="Minister for Defence (Australia)">Minister for the Army</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Curtin_government" title="Curtin government">Curtin government</a>, and as the <i>de facto</i> <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Deputy Prime Minister of Australia">deputy prime minister</a> was one of the government's most prominent figures. When John Curtin died in office in 1945, Forde was appointed prime minister to serve while the Labor Party elected a new leader. He contested <a href="/wiki/1945_Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_election" title="1945 Australian Labor Party leadership election">the leadership ballot</a> against <a href="/wiki/Ben_Chifley" title="Ben Chifley">Ben Chifley</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norman_Makin" title="Norman Makin">Norman Makin</a>, but Chifley emerged victorious.
</p><p>Forde continued on as deputy leader and army minister in the <a href="/wiki/Chifley_government" title="Chifley government">Chifley government</a>, but lost his seat at the <a href="/wiki/1946_Australian_federal_election" title="1946 Australian federal election">1946 election</a>. He then was <a href="/wiki/High_Commissioner_of_Australia_to_Canada" class="mw-redirect" title="High Commissioner of Australia to Canada">High Commissioner to Canada</a> from 1947 to 1953. Forde attempted to re-enter federal parliament <a href="/wiki/1954_Australian_federal_election" title="1954 Australian federal election">in 1954</a>, but was unsuccessful. He won a state by-election in Queensland the following year – the only former prime minister to enter state parliament – but served only a single term before again being defeated. Forde died at the age of 92, and was accorded a state funeral; <a href="/wiki/Gough_Whitlam" title="Gough Whitlam">Gough Whitlam</a> is the only prime minister to have lived to a greater age.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Young_Frank_Forde.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Young_Frank_Forde.jpg/170px-Young_Frank_Forde.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="219" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Young_Frank_Forde.jpg/255px-Young_Frank_Forde.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Young_Frank_Forde.jpg/340px-Young_Frank_Forde.jpg 2x" data-file-width="839" data-file-height="1080" /></a><figcaption>Forde as a young man</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Forde_(surname)" title="Forde (surname)">Forde</a> was born in <a href="/wiki/Mitchell,_Queensland" title="Mitchell, Queensland">Mitchell, Queensland</a>, on 18 July 1890. He was the second of six children born to Ellen (née Quirk) and John Forde. His parents were both Irish immigrants – his father was born in <a href="/wiki/Ballinaglera" title="Ballinaglera">Ballinaglera</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Leitrim" title="County Leitrim">County Leitrim</a>, while his mother was from <a href="/wiki/County_Tipperary" title="County Tipperary">County Tipperary</a>. His father was working as a grazier at the time of his birth, and later worked as a railway supervisor. Forde began his education at the local state school and later boarded at <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_College,_Toowoomba" title="St Mary&#39;s College, Toowoomba">St Mary's College, Toowoomba</a>. He qualified as a schoolteacher via the <a href="/wiki/Monitorial_system" class="mw-redirect" title="Monitorial system">monitorial system</a>, but at the age of 20 joined <a href="/wiki/Queensland_Rail" title="Queensland Rail">Queensland Railways</a> as a clerk in the telegraphy department. He later moved to <a href="/wiki/Brisbane" title="Brisbane">Brisbane</a> to work as a telegraphist for the <a href="/wiki/Postmaster-General%27s_Department" title="Postmaster-General&#39;s Department">Postmaster-General's Department</a>, at the same time studying electrical engineering. In 1914, Forde was transferred to <a href="/wiki/Rockhampton" title="Rockhampton">Rockhampton</a>. He was involved with the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Natives%27_Association" title="Australian Natives&#39; Association">Australian Natives' Association</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Workers%27_Union" title="Australian Workers&#39; Union">Australian Workers' Union</a>, and the Rockhampton Workers' Political Organisation, and helped campaign for the "No" vote in the <a href="/wiki/Conscription_in_Australia" title="Conscription in Australia">conscription referendums</a> of 1916 and 1917.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_involvement">Early political involvement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early political involvement">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Frank_Forde.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Frank_Forde.jpg/170px-Frank_Forde.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="245" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Frank_Forde.jpg/255px-Frank_Forde.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Frank_Forde.jpg/340px-Frank_Forde.jpg 2x" data-file-width="605" data-file-height="871" /></a><figcaption>Forde early in his political career</figcaption></figure>
<p>Forde joined the Labor Party in 1915, at the urging of state MP <a href="/wiki/James_Larcombe" title="James Larcombe">James Larcombe</a>, who became his mentor. In 1917, aged 26, he won a by-election to the <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Rockhampton" title="Electoral district of Rockhampton">seat of Rockhampton</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Queensland_Legislative_Assembly" class="mw-redirect" title="Queensland Legislative Assembly">Queensland Legislative Assembly</a>. It had been vacated by <a href="/wiki/John_Adamson_(Queensland_politician)" title="John Adamson (Queensland politician)">John Adamson</a>, who had resigned from the Labor Party in the wake of the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_split_of_1916" title="Australian Labor Party split of 1916">1916 party split</a> and unsuccessfully sought <a href="/wiki/Nationalist_Party_(Australia)" title="Nationalist Party (Australia)">Nationalist Party</a> preselection for the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Senate" title="Australian Senate">Senate</a>. Forde was re-elected to Rockhampton at the <a href="/wiki/1918_Queensland_state_election" title="1918 Queensland state election">1918</a> and <a href="/wiki/1920_Queensland_state_election" title="1920 Queensland state election">1920 state elections</a>. He was a supporter of the Central Queensland Separation Movement, one of the many <a href="/wiki/List_of_proposed_states_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="List of proposed states of Australia">new state movements</a> active around that time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000241_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000241-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1921, the state Labor government of <a href="/wiki/Ted_Theodore" title="Ted Theodore">Ted Theodore</a> passed controversial legislation that allowed state MPs to run for federal parliament and automatically return to their seat in state parliament if they lost, without having to face a by-election. It was widely reported that Forde was intended to be the primary beneficiary of the new legislation. However, the federal Nationalist government responded by amending the <i><a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_Electoral_Act_1918" title="Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918">Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918</a></i> to overrule the state law. In October 1922, Forde resigned from state parliament to run in the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Capricornia" title="Division of Capricornia">Division of Capricornia</a> at the <a href="/wiki/1922_Australian_federal_election" title="1922 Australian federal election">1922 federal election</a>. He was successful, defeating <a href="/wiki/Nationalist_Party_of_Australia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nationalist Party of Australia">Nationalist</a> incumbent and Labor defector <a href="/wiki/William_Higgs_(politician)" title="William Higgs (politician)">William Higgs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000241_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000241-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Forde's successful foray into federal politics triggered the <a href="/wiki/1923_Rockhampton_state_by-election" title="1923 Rockhampton state by-election">1923 Rockhampton by-election</a>. The bitterly fought by-election was successfully contested by Labor's <a href="/wiki/George_Farrell_(politician)" title="George Farrell (politician)">George Farrell</a>, who had worked on Forde's federal campaign.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Forde took his seat in the House of Representatives at the age of 32, becoming one of the youngest members of the new parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000241_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000241-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He soon became known as a champion of the sugar and cotton industries. Despite the party's dominance in state politics, he was the only Labor MP in Queensland to be re-elected at the <a href="/wiki/1925_Australian_federal_election" title="1925 Australian federal election">1925 federal election</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He remained the only Queenslander in the ALP caucus until August 1928, when <a href="/wiki/John_MacDonald_(Australian_politician)" title="John MacDonald (Australian politician)">John MacDonald</a> was appointed to a casual vacancy in the Senate. In 1927, Forde was appointed as his Labor Party's representative to the Royal Commission on the Moving Picture Industry. He and the other commissioners travelled around Australia interviewing 250 witnesses.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000242_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000242-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Scullin_government">Scullin government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Scullin government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg/170px-Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="208" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg/255px-Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg/340px-Frank_Forde_and_James_Scullin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="883" data-file-height="1080" /></a><figcaption>Forde and James Scullin</figcaption></figure>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"><table class="infobox" style="float: right; clear: right; margin:0 0 1.5em 1.5em"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above" style="font-size:115%">External video</th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-full-data" style="text-align: left"><span typeof="mw:File"><span><img alt="video icon" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg/16px-Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg.png" decoding="async" width="16" height="16" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg/24px-Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg/32px-Nuvola_apps_kaboodle.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="128" data-file-height="128" /></span></span> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PeMDtHmPD_Q">Newsreel footage of Forde as Minister for Trade and Customs</a> promoting <a href="/wiki/Efftee_Studios" title="Efftee Studios">Efftee Studios</a> in 1931.</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The Labor Party won the <a href="/wiki/1929_Australian_federal_election" title="1929 Australian federal election">1929 election</a>, with <a href="/wiki/James_Scullin" title="James Scullin">James Scullin</a> becoming prime minister. Forde was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Scullin_Ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="Scullin Ministry">Scullin Ministry</a> as an <a href="/wiki/Assistant_minister" class="mw-redirect" title="Assistant minister">assistant minister</a>, and was also appointed to the Committee of Public Accounts. His superior minister was <a href="/wiki/James_Fenton_(Australian_politician)" class="mw-redirect" title="James Fenton (Australian politician)">James Fenton</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Trade_and_Customs_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for Trade and Customs (Australia)">Minister for Trade and Customs</a>. Fenton was absent from the country or otherwise occupied for most of 1930, including as Acting Prime Minister for five months while Scullin attended the <a href="/wiki/1930_Imperial_Conference" title="1930 Imperial Conference">1930 Imperial Conference</a> in London. Forde oversaw the <a href="/wiki/Department_of_Trade_and_Customs_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Department of Trade and Customs (Australia)">Department of Trade and Customs</a> in Fenton's absence, and also deputised for <a href="/wiki/Parker_Moloney" title="Parker Moloney">Parker Moloney</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Department_of_Markets_(1930%E2%80%9332)" class="mw-redirect" title="Department of Markets (1930–32)">Minister for Markets</a> and <a href="/wiki/Department_of_Transport_(1930%E2%80%9332)" class="mw-redirect" title="Department of Transport (1930–32)">Transport</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At the 1930 <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_National_Conference" title="Australian Labor Party National Conference">ALP Federal Conference</a>, he defended the department's decision to ban <a href="/wiki/Norman_Lindsay" title="Norman Lindsay">Norman Lindsay</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Redheap" title="Redheap">Redheap</a></i>, which the first Australian novel to be banned in Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Forde was a supporter of the emerging Australian motion picture industry. Despite his reputation as a protectionist, he agreed to reduce the tariff on imported sound equipment from 60 percent to just 10 percent, after vigorous lobbying from <a href="/wiki/F._W._Thring" title="F. W. Thring">F. W. Thring</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fitzpatrick_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fitzpatrick-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In June 1931, he was invited to officially open <a href="/wiki/Efftee_Studios" title="Efftee Studios">Efftee Studios</a>, Thring's production studio in Melbourne.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFitzpatrick2012551_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFitzpatrick2012551-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was "shamelessly cultivated as a good friend of Efftee, with an open invitation to look in on shooting and mingle with the stars".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFitzpatrick2012132_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFitzpatrick2012132-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> One of Thring's investors was Tom Holt, the father of another future Australian prime minister <a href="/wiki/Harold_Holt" title="Harold Holt">Harold Holt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fitzpatrick_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fitzpatrick-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Forde was the "principal architect" of the Scullin government's policy of high <a href="/wiki/Tariff" title="Tariff">tariffs</a>, which aimed to reduce the effect of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Depression_in_Australia" title="Great Depression in Australia">Great Depression</a> on secondary industries.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He introduced what <i><a href="/wiki/The_Canberra_Times" title="The Canberra Times">The Canberra Times</a></i> called a "tariff extravaganza", and was known as a staunch protectionist.<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> However, the government's measures had little effect on the economy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000242_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000242-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Forde remained loyal to the Scullin government during the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_split_of_1931" title="Australian Labor Party split of 1931">ALP split of 1931</a>, supporting the <a href="/wiki/Premiers%27_Plan" title="Premiers&#39; Plan">Premiers' Plan</a>. When Fenton and <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Lyons" title="Joseph Lyons">Joseph Lyons</a> resigned from cabinet in February 1931, Forde was elected to one of the vacancies and appointed Minister for Trade and Customs.<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At the <a href="/wiki/1931_Australian_federal_election" title="1931 Australian federal election">1931 election</a>, the ALP suffered a landslide and returned only 14 MPs, the lowest total in its history. However, in Forde's state of Queensland the party actually increased its representation, winning an additional two House seats and all three seats in the Senate.<sup id="cite_ref-adb_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Opposition">Opposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Opposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg/170px-Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="233" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg/255px-Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg/340px-Frank_Forde_and_John_Curtin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1217" data-file-height="1671" /></a><figcaption>Forde and John Curtin at a meeting of the Advisory War Council in 1940</figcaption></figure>
<p>One of the MPs defeated at the 1931 election was Treasurer <a href="/wiki/Ted_Theodore" title="Ted Theodore">Ted Theodore</a>, the ALP's deputy leader. A ballot for the party's leadership positions was held on 16 February 1932, at which Scullin was re-elected unopposed as leader and Forde was elected as his deputy. He thus became <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Australia)" title="Leader of the Opposition (Australia)">Deputy Leader of the Opposition</a>. With Scullin suffering frequent bouts of ill health, Forde was Acting Leader of the Opposition on a number occasions. Notably, after the <a href="/wiki/Second_Italo-Ethiopian_War" title="Second Italo-Ethiopian War">Italian invasion of Abyssinia</a> in 1935 he announced that the ALP would pursue a policy of non-participation, as "the control of Abyssinia is not worth the loss of a single Australian life".<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Scullin retired in 1935, Forde contested the leadership ballot but <a href="/wiki/1935_Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_election" title="1935 Australian Labor Party leadership election">was defeated by one vote</a> by <a href="/wiki/John_Curtin" title="John Curtin">John Curtin</a>, eleven votes to ten. <i><a href="/wiki/The_Age" title="The Age">The Age</a></i> called it "one of the greatest surprises in federal political circles during recent years". It has been suggested that some MPs viewed Forde as too closely linked with the activities of the Scullin government.<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1940, Forde was one of three Labor MPs elected to the bipartisan <a href="/wiki/Advisory_War_Council" title="Advisory War Council">Advisory War Council</a>, along with Curtin and <a href="/wiki/Norman_Makin" title="Norman Makin">Norman Makin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000242_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000242-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Curtin_government">Curtin government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Curtin government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg/170px-Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="224" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg/255px-Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg/340px-Frank_Forde_signing_the_UN_Charter.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1936" data-file-height="2556" /></a><figcaption>Forde signing the <a href="/wiki/United_Nations_Charter" class="mw-redirect" title="United Nations Charter">United Nations Charter</a> as Australia's lead delegate to the 1945 <a href="/wiki/San_Francisco_Conference" class="mw-redirect" title="San Francisco Conference">San Francisco Conference</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Forde was a loyal deputy, and in 1941 when Labor returned to power he became <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_the_Army_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister for the Army (Australia)">Minister for the Army</a>, a vital role in wartime.<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was the <i>de facto</i> <a href="/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Australia" title="Deputy Prime Minister of Australia">Deputy Prime Minister</a>, but was disappointed not to be appointed <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_Australia" title="Treasurer of Australia">Treasurer</a> in the new government; that position was instead awarded to <a href="/wiki/Ben_Chifley" title="Ben Chifley">Ben Chifley</a>. Forde chaired the <a href="/wiki/Australian_War_Cabinet" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian War Cabinet">War Cabinet</a> and Advisory War Council when Curtin was ill or absent, and was Acting Prime Minister on a number of occasions.<sup id="cite_ref-naa-before_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-naa-before-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He strongly opposed the <a href="/wiki/Brisbane_Line" title="Brisbane Line">Brisbane Line</a> proposal, which would have seen a strategic withdrawal of troops from northern Australia in the event of a <a href="/wiki/Proposed_Japanese_invasion_of_Australia_during_World_War_II" title="Proposed Japanese invasion of Australia during World War II">Japanese invasion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000243_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000243-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_election,_1945" class="mw-redirect" title="Australian Labor Party leadership election, 1945">Australian Labor Party leadership election, 1945</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Evatt,_Forde,_and_Bruce.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Evatt%2C_Forde%2C_and_Bruce.jpg/220px-Evatt%2C_Forde%2C_and_Bruce.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="156" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Evatt%2C_Forde%2C_and_Bruce.jpg/330px-Evatt%2C_Forde%2C_and_Bruce.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Evatt%2C_Forde%2C_and_Bruce.jpg/440px-Evatt%2C_Forde%2C_and_Bruce.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2370" data-file-height="1680" /></a><figcaption>Forde in April 1945, a few months before he became prime minister, conversing with <a href="/wiki/H._V._Evatt" title="H. V. Evatt">H. V. Evatt</a> (left) and <a href="/wiki/Stanley_Bruce" title="Stanley Bruce">Stanley Bruce</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In April 1945, Forde left Australia to attend the <a href="/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_International_Organization" title="United Nations Conference on International Organization">United Nations Conference on International Organization</a> in San Francisco. Late that month, Curtin was admitted to hospital; in the absence of Forde and <a href="/wiki/H._V._Evatt" title="H. V. Evatt">H. V. Evatt</a> (the <a href="/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_(Australia)" title="Minister for Foreign Affairs (Australia)">Minister for External Affairs</a>), he designated <a href="/wiki/Ben_Chifley" title="Ben Chifley">Ben Chifley</a> as <a href="/wiki/Acting_prime_minister#Australia" title="Acting prime minister">acting prime minister</a>. Forde did not return to Australia until 2 July, but then immediately took over from Chifley. The prime minister's health had severely declined in the meantime, although he had been able to go home to <a href="/wiki/The_Lodge_(Australia)" class="mw-redirect" title="The Lodge (Australia)">The Lodge</a>. Curtin died in his sleep in the early hours of 5 July, at the age of 60.<sup id="cite_ref-mcmullin_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mcmullin-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the day that Curtin died, Forde issued a brief statement announcing the death, and then in the afternoon moved a condolence motion at a brief sitting of parliament. On the morning of the following day, 6 July, he led a procession of MPs past Curtin's coffin at <a href="/wiki/Old_Parliament_House,_Canberra" title="Old Parliament House, Canberra">Parliament House</a>, where his body was <a href="/wiki/Lying_in_state" title="Lying in state">lying in state</a>. In the afternoon, Forde attended a memorial service, and then went to <a href="/wiki/Government_House,_Canberra" title="Government House, Canberra">Government House</a>, where he was formally sworn in as prime minister by <a href="/wiki/Prince_Henry,_Duke_of_Gloucester" title="Prince Henry, Duke of Gloucester">Prince Henry, Duke of Gloucester</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia" title="Governor-General of Australia">Governor-General</a>. He was appointed as prime minister with the understanding that he would resign if the Labor Party elected someone other than him as leader at its next <a href="/wiki/Caucus" title="Caucus">caucus</a> meeting. Forde was the Labor Party's sixth prime minister. He is the only Australian prime minister to have never led a political party. There was little precedent for his appointment, as only one previous prime minister (<a href="/wiki/Joseph_Lyons" title="Joseph Lyons">Joseph Lyons</a>) had died in office, and Lyons had been succeeded by the leader of the smaller party in his governing <a href="/wiki/Coalition_(Australia)" title="Coalition (Australia)">coalition</a> (<a href="/wiki/Earle_Page" title="Earle Page">Earle Page</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-mcmullin_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mcmullin-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 8 July, Forde accompanied <a href="/wiki/Elsie_Curtin" title="Elsie Curtin">Elsie Curtin</a> to <a href="/wiki/Perth" title="Perth">Perth</a> to attend her husband's funeral. Two days later, Ben Chifley told him that he would be contesting the leadership; that evening, they both issued statements announcing their candidacies. <a href="/wiki/Norman_Makin" title="Norman Makin">Norman Makin</a> announced his intention to stand the following day. On the morning of 12 July, <a href="/wiki/Les_Haylen" title="Les Haylen">Les Haylen</a> informed Forde that he did not have the numbers to win. In response, Forde said "I must say a little prayer for Ben. It's not an easy job". In <a href="/wiki/1945_Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_election" title="1945 Australian Labor Party leadership election">the leadership ballot</a>, Chifley received 45 votes to Forde's 16, Makin's seven, and Evatt's two. Several MPs were absent (including Evatt), and <a href="/wiki/Rowley_James" title="Rowley James">Rowley James</a> unsuccessfully proposed that the ballot be postponed. Forde resigned as prime minister on 13 July, after one week in office. He is Australia's shortest-serving prime minister.<sup id="cite_ref-mcmullin_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mcmullin-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Chifley_government">Chifley government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Chifley government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After losing the leadership contest to Chifley, Forde was re-elected unopposed as deputy leader. He remained Minister for the Army and Minister for Defence, having assumed the latter portfolio after Curtin's death. As the Pacific War <a href="/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day" title="Victory over Japan Day">ended on 15 August</a>, Forde's primary responsibility was to oversee the <a href="/wiki/Demobilisation_of_the_Australian_military_after_World_War_II" title="Demobilisation of the Australian military after World War II">demobilisation of the Australian military</a>. At this time service men and women were spread around the country and across the Pacific. He handled the issue in a way that was unpopular with the general public, refusing appeals to release servicemen early but also making public statements about the rate of demobilisation that turned out to be inaccurate.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000243_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000243-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> At the <a href="/wiki/1946_Australian_federal_election" title="1946 Australian federal election">1946 election</a>, Forde unexpectedly lost his seat to the <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Australia" title="Liberal Party of Australia">Liberal</a> candidate <a href="/wiki/Charles_Davidson_(politician)" title="Charles Davidson (politician)">Charles Davidson</a>, a returned soldier. He suffered a 10-point swing against him, compared with a nationwide 0.2-point swing against the Labor Party. As well as the concerns over demobilisation, he had been criticised for moving his family to Sydney during the war and his electorate was experiencing a severe drought.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000244_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000244-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Forde was the only former Labor prime minister who had been a minister under a sitting Labor prime minister until fellow Queenslander Kevin Rudd.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="High_Commissioner_to_Canada">High Commissioner to Canada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: High Commissioner to Canada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In November 1946, it was announced that Forde would become the new <a href="/wiki/List_of_High_Commissioners_of_Australia_to_Canada" class="mw-redirect" title="List of High Commissioners of Australia to Canada">High Commissioner to Canada</a>, cutting short the term of <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Stirling" title="Alfred Stirling">Alfred Stirling</a>. His appointment was the "first major executive act of the new Federal Cabinet" after the election.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Forde and his family left Australia on 7 December,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and arrived in <a href="/wiki/Ottawa" title="Ottawa">Ottawa</a> on 18 January 1947.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In October 1951, the <a href="/wiki/Menzies_Government_(1949%E2%80%9366)" class="mw-redirect" title="Menzies Government (1949–66)">Menzies Government</a> announced a nine-month extension to his initial five-year term.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> From May 1952, he was the <i>doyen</i> (longest-serving head of mission) of the <a href="/wiki/Diplomatic_corps" title="Diplomatic corps">diplomatic corps</a> in Canada.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> He returned to Australia in July 1953.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After returning to Australia, Forde was given a civic reception at <a href="/wiki/Brisbane_City_Hall" title="Brisbane City Hall">Brisbane City Hall</a> by Lord Mayor <a href="/wiki/Frank_Roberts_(Australian_politician)" title="Frank Roberts (Australian politician)">Frank Roberts</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> He was soon invited to re-enter federal politics by <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Calwell" title="Arthur Calwell">Arthur Calwell</a>, on behalf of the party's federal leader <a href="/wiki/H._V._Evatt" title="H. V. Evatt">H. V. Evatt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> In August 1953 he accepted an appointment as paid organiser for the ALP in Queensland.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In February 1954, Forde nominated for ALP <a href="/wiki/Preselection" title="Preselection">preselection</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Wide_Bay" title="Division of Wide Bay">Division of Wide Bay</a>, following the withdrawal of the previous endorsed candidate.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> He was defeated at the <a href="/wiki/1954_Australian_federal_election" title="1954 Australian federal election">1954 federal election</a>, in a seat which the Country Party had held since 1928. In 1955, at a by-election, he returned to the Queensland Parliament as MP for <a href="/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Flinders_(Queensland)" title="Electoral district of Flinders (Queensland)">Flinders</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-APMao_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APMao-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> He is the only Prime Minister who later served in a State Parliament.
</p><p>At the <a href="/wiki/1957_Queensland_state_election" title="1957 Queensland state election">1957 Queensland state election</a> the <a href="/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_split_of_1955" title="Australian Labor Party split of 1955">Labor Party split</a> resulted not only in Labor falling from power, but also in Forde being defeated in his own seat by <a href="/wiki/Bill_Longeran" title="Bill Longeran">Bill Longeran</a> of the <a href="/wiki/National_Party_of_Australia_%E2%80%93_Queensland" class="mw-redirect" title="National Party of Australia – Queensland">Country Party</a> by one vote.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Forde disputed the result and the election was declared void on 4 March 1958. However, at the by-election held on 17 May 1958, Longeran defeated Forde by over 400 votes.<sup id="cite_ref-APMao_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APMao-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Had Forde been elected, he would probably have become Labor leader in Queensland, given that Premier <a href="/wiki/Vince_Gair" title="Vince Gair">Vince Gair</a> and most of Gair's followers had been expelled from the party.
</p><p>
In 1962, Forde was nominated for Labor preselection for the Senate vacancy caused by the death of <a href="/wiki/Max_Poulter" title="Max Poulter">Max Poulter</a>. He received three out of 66 votes in the ballot,<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> with his age probably a factor in his low tally.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup></p><figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg/220px-Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="170" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg/330px-Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg/440px-Toowong_Cemetery_Forde_headstone_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2776" data-file-height="2140" /></a><figcaption>Frank Forde's headstone at Brisbane's <a href="/wiki/Toowong_Cemetery" title="Toowong Cemetery">Toowong Cemetery</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Forde retired to <a href="/wiki/Brisbane" title="Brisbane">Brisbane</a> where he devoted himself to Catholic charity work. In his living room hung a large portrait of wartime US General <a href="/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur" title="Douglas MacArthur">Douglas MacArthur</a>. On 11 April 1964, at the request of Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Robert_Menzies" title="Robert Menzies">Robert Menzies</a>, Forde represented Australia at MacArthur's funeral in <a href="/wiki/Norfolk,_Virginia" title="Norfolk, Virginia">Norfolk, Virginia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-APMao_26-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APMao-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Forde.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/Forde.jpg" decoding="async" width="169" height="222" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="169" data-file-height="222" /></a><figcaption>Bust of fifteenth Prime Minister of Australia Frank Forde located in the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister%27s_Avenue" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime Minister&#39;s Avenue">Prime Minister's Avenue</a> in the Ballarat Botanical Gardens</figcaption></figure>
<p>Forde died in 1983. He was accorded a <a href="/wiki/State_funeral" title="State funeral">state funeral</a> which, on 3 February, proceeded from St Thomas Aquinas Catholic Church in <a href="/wiki/St_Lucia,_Queensland" title="St Lucia, Queensland">St Lucia</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Toowong_Cemetery" title="Toowong Cemetery">Toowong Cemetery</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-funeralnotice_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-funeralnotice-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> the same day that <a href="/wiki/Bob_Hawke" title="Bob Hawke">Bob Hawke</a> was elected ALP leader. Indeed, it was at Forde's funeral that Senator <a href="/wiki/John_Button_(Australian_politician)" title="John Button (Australian politician)">John Button</a> told then Labor leader <a href="/wiki/Bill_Hayden" title="Bill Hayden">Bill Hayden</a> that he must step aside in favor of Hawke, which he did. Forde was the last surviving member of the Scullin, Curtin and his own Cabinet. Furthermore, he was the last surviving MP from when <a href="/wiki/Stanley_Bruce" title="Stanley Bruce">Stanley Bruce</a> was Prime Minister.
</p><p>Forde was the only deputy Labor leader who served under three leaders (Scullin, Curtin and Chifley) until <a href="/wiki/Jenny_Macklin" title="Jenny Macklin">Jenny Macklin</a> (<a href="/wiki/Simon_Crean" title="Simon Crean">Crean</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mark_Latham" title="Mark Latham">Latham</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kim_Beazley" title="Kim Beazley">Beazley</a>, 2001–2006). The electoral <a href="/wiki/Division_of_Forde" title="Division of Forde">Division of Forde</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Canberra" title="Canberra">Canberra</a> suburb of <a href="/wiki/Forde,_Australian_Capital_Territory" title="Forde, Australian Capital Territory">Forde</a> are named after him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Personal_life">Personal life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frank_Forde&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Personal life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Forde married Veronica (Vera) Catherine O'Reilly in 1925 and they had four children:<sup id="cite_ref-APMvf_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-APMvf-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ul><li>Mary Therese (1927–2016)</li>
<li>Mercia (b. 1930)</li>
<li>Clare (1932–2010)</li>
<li>Francis Gerard Forde (1935–1966); his widow, <a href="/wiki/Leneen_Forde" title="Leneen Forde">Leneen Forde</a>, became <a href="/wiki/Governor_of_Queensland" title="Governor of Queensland">Governor of Queensland</a>.</li></ul>
<p>Forde was widowed in November 1967.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> During the years that he spent in Ottawa as High Commissioner to Canada two of his daughters (Mary and Mercia) became married to Canadians. Mary (m. <a href="/wiki/William_Robert_Thompson" title="William Robert Thompson">William Robert Thompson</a>) eventually settled with her husband in Kingston, Ontario, Canada while Mercia (m. Ian Ferrier) returned to Australia and settled with her husband in St. Lucia, Brisbane. Francis Gerald Forde (m. Leneen Forde) also settled in St. Lucia, while Clare (m. John Attridge) settled in Canberra. Between their four children, Frank and Vera Forde had 15 grandchildren.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
