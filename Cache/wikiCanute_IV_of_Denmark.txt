<p><b>Canute IV</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1042</span>&#160;– 10 July 1086), later known as <b>Canute the Holy</b> (<a href="/wiki/Danish_language" title="Danish language">Danish</a>: <i lang="da">Knud IV den Hellige</i>) or <b>Saint Canute</b> (<i>Sankt Knud</i>), was <a href="/wiki/King_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Denmark">King of Denmark</a> from 1080 until 1086. Canute was an ambitious king who sought to strengthen the <a href="/wiki/Danish_monarchy" class="mw-redirect" title="Danish monarchy">Danish monarchy</a>, devotedly supported the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, and had designs on the <a href="/wiki/English_throne" class="mw-redirect" title="English throne">English throne</a>. Slain by rebels in 1086, he was the first Danish king to be <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>. He was recognized by the Roman Catholic Church as <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Denmark in 1101.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Canute was born <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1042</span>, one of the many sons of <a href="/wiki/Sweyn_II_of_Denmark" title="Sweyn II of Denmark">Sweyn II Estridsson</a><sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> by an unknown mistress. He is first noted as a member of Sweyn's <a href="/wiki/Danish_attacks_on_Norman_England" title="Danish attacks on Norman England">1069 raid on England</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i> reports that Canute was one of the leaders of another raid against England in 1075. When returning from England in 1075, the Danish fleet stopped in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Flanders" title="County of Flanders">County of Flanders</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-gyldendal_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gyldendal-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Because of its hostility towards <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of England">William I of England</a>, Flanders was a natural ally for the Danes. He also led successful campaigns to Sember and Ester, according to <a href="/wiki/Skald" title="Skald">skald</a> Kálfr Mánason.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Sweyn died, Canute's brother <a href="/wiki/Harald_III_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="Harald III of Denmark">Harald III</a> was elected king, and as Canute went into exile in <a href="/wiki/Sweden" title="Sweden">Sweden</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he was possibly involved in the active opposition to Harald.<sup id="cite_ref-gyldendal_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gyldendal-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 17 April 1080, Harald died,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and Canute succeeded him to the throne of Denmark. On his accession, he married <a href="/wiki/Adela_of_Flanders" title="Adela of Flanders">Adela</a>, daughter of Count <a href="/wiki/Robert_I,_Count_of_Flanders" title="Robert I, Count of Flanders">Robert I of Flanders</a>. They had one son, <a href="/wiki/Charles_I,_Count_of_Flanders" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles I, Count of Flanders">Charles</a>, who was born in 1084, and twin daughters <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A6cilia_Knudsdatter" class="mw-redirect" title="Cæcilia Knudsdatter">Cæcilia</a> (who later married Erik Jarl) and <a href="/wiki/Ingerid_Knudsdatter" class="mw-redirect" title="Ingerid Knudsdatter">Ingerid</a> (who later married <a href="/wiki/Folke_the_Fat" title="Folke the Fat">Folke the Fat</a>), who were born shortly before his death (ca. 1085/86).<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Ingerid's descendants, the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Bjelbo" title="House of Bjelbo">House of Bjelbo</a>, would ascend to the throne of Sweden and Norway and Canute IV's blood returned to the Danish throne in the person of <a href="/wiki/Olaf_II_of_Denmark" title="Olaf II of Denmark">Olaf II of Denmark</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="King_of_Denmark">King of Denmark</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: King of Denmark">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Canute quickly proved himself to be a highly ambitious king as well as a devout one. He enhanced the authority of the church, and demanded austere observation of church holidays.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He gave large gifts to the churches in <a href="/wiki/Dalby,_Lund" class="mw-redirect" title="Dalby, Lund">Dalby</a>, <a href="/wiki/Odense" title="Odense">Odense</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roskilde" title="Roskilde">Roskilde</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Viborg,_Denmark" title="Viborg, Denmark">Viborg</a>, and especially to <a href="/wiki/Lund" title="Lund">Lund</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Ever a champion of the Church, he sought to enforce the collection of <a href="/wiki/Tithes" class="mw-redirect" title="Tithes">tithes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His aggrandizement of the church served to create a powerful ally, who in turn supported Canute's power position.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In May 1085, Canute wrote a letter of donation to <a href="/wiki/Lund_Cathedral" title="Lund Cathedral">Lund Cathedral</a>, which was under construction, granting it large tracts of land in <a href="/wiki/Scania" title="Scania">Scania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zealand_(Denmark)" class="mw-redirect" title="Zealand (Denmark)">Zealand</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Amager" title="Amager">Amager</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-donation_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donation-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He founded <a href="/wiki/Lund_Cathedral_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Lund Cathedral School">Lund Cathedral School</a> at the same time.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Canute had gathered the land largely as pay for the pardon of <a href="/wiki/Outlaw" title="Outlaw">outlawed</a> subjects. The clerics at Lund got extended <a href="/wiki/Prerogative" title="Prerogative">prerogatives</a> of the land, being able to tax and fine the <a href="/wiki/Peasant" title="Peasant">peasantry</a> there. However, Canute kept his universal <a href="/wiki/Royal_rights" class="mw-redirect" title="Royal rights">royal rights</a> to <a href="/wiki/Pardon" title="Pardon">pardon</a> the outlaws, impose fines on subjects who failed to answer his <i><a href="/wiki/Leding" class="mw-redirect" title="Leding">leding</a></i> call to war, and demand transportation for his <a href="/wiki/Retinue" title="Retinue">retinue</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-donation_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donation-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His reign was marked by vigorous attempts to increase royal power in Denmark, by stifling the <a href="/wiki/Danish_nobility" title="Danish nobility">nobles</a> and keeping them to the word of the law.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Canute issued edicts <a href="/w/index.php?title=Arrogation&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Arrogation (page does not exist)">arrogating</a> to himself the ownership of <a href="/wiki/Common_land" title="Common land">common land</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Law_of_salvage" title="Law of salvage">right to the goods from shipwrecks</a>, and the right to inherit the possessions of foreigners and <a href="/wiki/Kinlessness" title="Kinlessness">kinless</a> folk. He also issued laws to protect freed <a href="/wiki/Thrall" title="Thrall">thralls</a> as well as foreign <a href="/wiki/Clergy" title="Clergy">clerics</a> and merchants.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> These policies led to discontent among his subjects, who were unaccustomed to a king claiming such powers and interfering in their daily lives.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Aborted_attempt_on_England">Aborted attempt on England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Aborted attempt on England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>However, Canute's ambitions were not purely domestic. As the grandnephew of <a href="/wiki/Canute_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Canute the Great">Canute the Great</a>, who had ruled <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>, <a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Denmark</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a> until 1035, Canute considered the crown of England to be rightfully his and regarded <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of England">William I of England</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Usurper" title="Usurper">usurper</a>. In 1085, with the support of his father-in-law, Count Robert, and <a href="/wiki/Olaf_III_of_Norway" title="Olaf III of Norway">Olaf III of Norway</a>, Canute planned an invasion of England and called his fleet in <i>leding</i> at the <a href="/wiki/Limfjord" title="Limfjord">Limfjord</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The fleet never set sail, as Canute was preoccupied in <a href="/wiki/Schleswig" class="mw-redirect" title="Schleswig">Schleswig</a> by the potential threat of <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor</a> with whom both Denmark and Flanders were on unfriendly terms. Canute feared an invasion by Henry, whose enemy <a href="/wiki/Rudolf_of_Rheinfelden" title="Rudolf of Rheinfelden">Rudolf of Rheinfelden</a> had sought refuge in Denmark.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The warriors of the fleet, mostly made up of peasants who needed to be home for the harvest season, got weary of waiting and elected Canute's brother Olaf (the later <a href="/wiki/Olaf_I_of_Denmark" title="Olaf I of Denmark">Olaf I of Denmark</a>) to argue their case. That raised the suspicion of Canute, who had Olaf arrested and sent to Flanders. The <i>leding</i> was eventually dispersed, and the peasants tended to their harvests,<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but Canute intended to reassemble within a year.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Before the fleet could reassemble, a <a href="/wiki/Peasant_revolt" class="mw-redirect" title="Peasant revolt">peasant revolt</a> broke out in <a href="/wiki/Vendsyssel" title="Vendsyssel">Vendsyssel</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> where Canute was staying, in early 1086. Canute first fled to Schleswig and eventually to <a href="/wiki/Odense" title="Odense">Odense</a>. On 10 July 1086, Canute and his men took refuge inside the wooden <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Alban%27s_Priory,_Odense&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Alban&#39;s Priory, Odense (page does not exist)">St. Alban's Priory</a>, in Odense. The rebels stormed into the church and slew Canute, along with his brother Benedict and seventeen of their followers, before the altar.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to the chronicler <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lnoth_of_Canterbury" title="Ælnoth of Canterbury">Ælnoth of Canterbury</a>, Canute died following a <a href="/wiki/Lance" title="Lance">lance</a> thrust in the <a href="/wiki/Flank_(anatomy)" title="Flank (anatomy)">flank</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ct_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ct-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by Olaf as <a href="/wiki/Olaf_I_of_Denmark" title="Olaf I of Denmark">Olaf I of Denmark</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG/220px-Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG/330px-Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG/440px-Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="4000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Skt._Knud_den_Hellige.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Saint Canute at <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Church,_N%C3%A6stved" title="St. Peter&#39;s Church, Næstved">St. Peter's Church, Næstved</a></div></div></div>
<p>Because of his <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> and advocacy of the Church, Canute quickly began to be considered a saint. Under the reign of Olaf, Denmark suffered from crop failure, which was seen as <a href="/wiki/Divine_retribution" title="Divine retribution">divine retribution</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Sacrilege" title="Sacrilege">sacrilegious</a> killing of Canute. <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">Miracles</a> were soon reported as taking place at his grave,<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and his canonization was already being sought during the reign of Olaf.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 19 April 1101, persuaded by the envoys from <a href="/wiki/Eric_I_of_Denmark" title="Eric I of Denmark">Eric I of Denmark</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a> confirmed the "cult of Canute" that had arisen, and King Canute IV was canonized.<sup id="cite_ref-donation_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donation-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was the first Dane to be canonized.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 10 July is recognised by the Catholic Church as his feast day. In Sweden and <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a> he is historically, however, partially associated with <a href="/wiki/St._Knut%27s_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Knut&#39;s Day">St. Knut's Day</a>, which in reality was celebrated in the memory of the death of his <a href="/wiki/Nephew" class="mw-redirect" title="Nephew">nephew</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canute_Lavard" title="Canute Lavard">Canute Lavard</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-tjugo_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tjugo-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-murder_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-murder-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1300, his remains and those of his brother Benedict were interred in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Canute%27s_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Canute&#39;s Cathedral">Saint Canute's Cathedral</a>, built in his honour, where his remains are on display.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Christian-albrecht-von-benzon,_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Christian-albrecht-von-benzon%2C_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg/220px-Christian-albrecht-von-benzon%2C_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="185" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Christian-albrecht-von-benzon%2C_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg/330px-Christian-albrecht-von-benzon%2C_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f2/Christian-albrecht-von-benzon%2C_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg/440px-Christian-albrecht-von-benzon%2C_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2334" data-file-height="1965" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Christian-albrecht-von-benzon,_the_death_of_Canute_the_Holy.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Murder of Canute the Holy</i> by <a href="/wiki/Christian_Albrecht_von_Benzon" title="Christian Albrecht von Benzon">Christian Albrecht von Benzon</a>, 1843</div></div></div>
<p>The reign of Canute has been interpreted differently through the times; from a violent king who tyrannized his subjects, to a strict but fair ruler who devotedly supported the Roman Catholic Church and fought for justice without regard to his own person.<sup id="cite_ref-gyldendal_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gyldendal-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was never a thoroughly popular saint in Denmark, but his sainthood granted the Danish monarchy an aura of <a href="/wiki/Divine_right_of_kings" title="Divine right of kings">divine legitimacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-pajung_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pajung-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The cause of the rebellion which killed Canute is unknown, but has been speculated as originating in fines issued to the peasants breaking the <i>leding</i> of 1085 as specified in the <a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Roskildense" title="Chronicon Roskildense">Chronicon Roskildense</a>, or as a result of his vigorous tithe policy.<sup id="cite_ref-gyldendal_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gyldendal-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The document of his donation to Lund Cathedral was the oldest comprehensive text from Denmark, and provided broad insights into Danish post-<a href="/wiki/Viking_Age" title="Viking Age">Viking Age</a> society.<sup id="cite_ref-donation_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-donation-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The donation might have had the aim of establishing the Danish <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Lund" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Lund">Archdiocese of Lund</a> according to Sweyn II Estridsson's wishes,<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which was finally achieved in 1104. Canute's son Carl became Count of Flanders from 1119 to 1127, ruling as <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Good" title="Charles the Good">Charles the Good</a>. Like his father, Charles was slain in a church by rebels (in <a href="/wiki/Bruges" title="Bruges">Bruges</a>, 1127), and later <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the Catholic church.<sup id="cite_ref-bricka_2-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bricka-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to Niels Lund, Lecturer in Medieval History at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Copenhagen" title="University of Copenhagen">University of Copenhagen</a>, Canute's abortive invasion of England "marked the end of the Viking Age."<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 2008, an <a href="/wiki/X-ray_computed_tomography" class="mw-redirect" title="X-ray computed tomography">X-ray computed tomography</a> was taken of Canute, which showed that he was right-handed and of a slender build. It also specified his cause of death as a thrust to the <a href="/wiki/Sacrum" title="Sacrum">sacrum</a> through the <a href="/wiki/Abdomen" title="Abdomen">abdomen</a>, negating Ælnoth's account. He had no injuries indicating he fought against multiple enemies, which can be seen as supporting an account saying he faced his death without a struggle.<sup id="cite_ref-ct_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ct-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Popular_culture">Popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Canute_IV_of_Denmark&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In parts of Spain, Canute's feast day has reportedly become a <a href="/wiki/Tongue-in-cheek" title="Tongue-in-cheek">tongue-in-cheek</a> "holiday" for the <a href="/wiki/Marijuana" class="mw-redirect" title="Marijuana">marijuana</a> legalization movement, appropriating the Spanish version of his name, <i>Canuto</i>, which coincidentally is also the word for a marijuana cigarette.<sup id="cite_ref-canuto_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canuto-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
