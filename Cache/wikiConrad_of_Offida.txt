<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output <p><b>Conrad of Offida</b> was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Friar_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Friar Minor">Friar Minor</a> preacher and founder of the <a href="/wiki/Celestines" title="Celestines">Celestines</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conrad_of_Offida&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Conrad was born at <a href="/wiki/Offida" title="Offida">Offida</a>, a little town in the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Ancona" title="March of Ancona">March of Ancona</a>, c. 1241. When barely fourteen years old he entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> at <a href="/wiki/Ascoli_Piceno" title="Ascoli Piceno">Ascoli</a>, and was making rapid progress in the study of sacred sciences, when an internal voice called him to humbler offices of the religious life. He therefore abandoned his studies with the consent of his superiors, and for many years was employed as cook and questor.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His superiors subsequently had him ordained and sent him forth to preach. Conrad proved himself an excellent preacher in various convents, among them Rivotorto, Sirolo, Forano, and La Verna.<sup id="cite_ref-Cremona_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cremona-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He modelled his life after that of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>. He was especially zealous for the observance of poverty. During his long religious life he always wore the same <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> and always walked without sandals.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The early legend declares that Conrad's guardian angel was the same that had formerly fulfilled this office for Francis of Assisi, and that <a href="/wiki/Giles_of_Assisi" title="Giles of Assisi">Giles of Assisi</a> came back to earth to teach him the mysteries of contemplation. He was a close friend of <a href="/wiki/Leo_of_Assisi" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo of Assisi">Leo of Assisi</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Cremona_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cremona-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the companion and confessor of Francis of Assisi, who when dying, entrusted Conrad with his writings. Conrad was allied with <a href="/wiki/Angelo_da_Clareno" title="Angelo da Clareno">Angelo da Clareno</a> and with John of La Penna, <a href="/wiki/John_of_Parma" title="John of Parma">John of Parma</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_John_Olivi" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter of John Olivi">Peter of John Olivi</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Monticello&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter of Monticello (page does not exist)">Peter of Monticello</a> and others of the "Spirituals".<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1294 he obtained permission from pope <a href="/wiki/Celestine_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Celestine V">Celestine V</a> to separate from the main body of the order and found the <a href="/wiki/Celestines" title="Celestines">Celestines</a> by whom the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Francis">rule of St. Francis</a> was observed in all its purity. When this congregation was suppressed by <a href="/wiki/Boniface_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface VIII">Boniface VIII</a>, Conrad immediately returned under the authority of the superiors of the order. The letter written in 1295 by Peter of John Olivi to Conrad in which the legitimacy of Boniface VIII's election is defended, has been edited by Ignatius Jeiler (Historisches Jahrbuch, III, 649).
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms,_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms%2C_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg/220px-How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms%2C_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="137" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms%2C_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg/330px-How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms%2C_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms%2C_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg/440px-How_the_Virgin_Came_to_Brother_Conrad_in_Offia_and_Laid_her_Son_in_his_Arms%2C_by_Marie_Spartali_Stillman.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2100" data-file-height="1306" /></a><figcaption><i>How the Virgin Came to Brother Conrad in Offia and Laid her Son in his Arms</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Marie_Spartali_Stillman" title="Marie Spartali Stillman">Marie Spartali Stillman</a> (1892)</figcaption></figure>
<p>During a course of missions he was giving at <a href="/wiki/Bastia" title="Bastia">Bastia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, he died on 12 December 1306 at the age of about sixty-five years and was buried in that place.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conrad_of_Offida&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fifty-six years later his remains were carried off by the <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugians</a> and buried at San Francesco in the Oratory of San Bernardino,<sup id="cite_ref-Cremona_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cremona-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> reposing beside those of Giles of Assisi in the choir of the cathedral at Perugia.
</p><p>Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_VII" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius VII">Pius VII</a> in 1817 ratified the <a href="/wiki/Cult" title="Cult">cult</a> of <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> Conrad.
</p><p>His liturgical feast is kept in the Order of Friars Minor on 19 December.
</p>
