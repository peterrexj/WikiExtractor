<p><b>Ferdinand III</b> (Spanish: <i lang="es">Fernando</i>; 1199/1201&#160;&#8211;&#32;30 May 1252), called <b>the Saint</b>  (<i>el Santo</i>), was <a href="/wiki/King_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Castile">King of Castile</a> from 1217 and <a href="/wiki/King_of_Le%C3%B3n" class="mw-redirect" title="King of León">King of León</a> from 1230 as well as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Galicia" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Galicia">King of Galicia</a> from 1231.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBianchini20121_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBianchini20121-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the son of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_IX_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Alfonso IX of León">Alfonso IX of León</a> and <a href="/wiki/Berenguela_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Berenguela of Castile">Berenguela of Castile</a>. Through his second marriage he was also <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Aumale" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Aumale">Count of Aumale</a>. Ferdinand III was one of the most successful kings of Castile, securing not only the permanent union of the crowns of Castile and León, but also masterminding the most expansive southward territorial expansion campaign yet in the <a href="/wiki/Guadalquivir_Valley" class="mw-redirect" title="Guadalquivir Valley">Guadalquivir Valley</a>, in which Islamic rule was in disarray in the wake of the decline of the <a href="/wiki/Almohad" class="mw-redirect" title="Almohad">Almohad</a> presence in the Iberian Peninsula.
</p><p>By military and diplomatic efforts, Ferdinand greatly expanded the dominions of Castile by annexing the <a href="/wiki/Guadalquivir" title="Guadalquivir">crown of Guadalquivir</a> river valley in the south of the Iberian Peninsula, establishing the boundaries of the Castilian state for the next two centuries. New territories included important cities such as <a href="/wiki/Baeza,_Spain" title="Baeza, Spain">Baeza</a>, <a href="/wiki/%C3%9Abeda" title="Úbeda">Úbeda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ja%C3%A9n,_Spain" title="Jaén, Spain">Jaén</a>, <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a> and <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, that were subject of Repartimiento, given a new general charter and repopulated in the following years.
</p><p>Ferdinand was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1671 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a>. Places such as the cities of <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga" title="San Fernando, Pampanga">San Fernando, Pampanga</a> and <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando,_La_Union" title="San Fernando, La Union">San Fernando, La Union</a>; the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Ilagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Ilagan">Diocese of Ilagan</a> and the <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando_de_Dilao_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="San Fernando de Dilao Church">San Fernando de Dilao Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Paco,_Manila" title="Paco, Manila">Paco, Manila</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>; and in the United States, in <a href="/wiki/California" title="California">California</a> the <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando,_California" title="San Fernando, California">City of San Fernando</a>, the <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley" title="San Fernando Valley">San Fernando Valley</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Texas" title="Texas">Texas</a> the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_San_Fernando_(San_Antonio)" title="Cathedral of San Fernando (San Antonio)">Cathedral of San Fernando</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Antonio" title="San Antonio">San Antonio</a> were named in his honor.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The exact date of Ferdinand's birth is unclear. It has been proposed to have been as early as 1199 or even 1198, although more recent researchers commonly date Ferdinand's birth in the summer of 1201.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAnsón199839_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnsón199839-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEmmerson2013215_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEmmerson2013215-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEzquerra2001284_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEzquerra2001284-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ferdinand was born at the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a> of Valparaíso (Peleas de Arriba, in what is now the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Zamora" title="Province of Zamora">Province of Zamora</a>).
</p><p>As the son of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_IX_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Alfonso IX of León">Alfonso IX of León</a> and his second wife <a href="/wiki/Berengaria_of_Castile" title="Berengaria of Castile">Berengaria of Castile</a>, Ferdinand descended from <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VII_of_Le%C3%B3n_and_Castile" title="Alfonso VII of León and Castile">Alfonso VII of León and Castile</a> on both sides; his paternal grandfather <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Ferdinand II of León">Ferdinand II of León</a> and maternal great grandfather <a href="/wiki/Sancho_III_of_Castile" title="Sancho III of Castile">Sancho III of Castile</a> were the sons of Alfonso VII between whom his kingdom was divided. Ferdinand had other royal ancestors from his paternal grandmother <a href="/wiki/Urraca_of_Portugal" title="Urraca of Portugal">Urraca of Portugal</a> and his maternal grandmother <a href="/wiki/Eleanor_of_England,_Queen_of_Castile" title="Eleanor of England, Queen of Castile">Eleanor of England</a> a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II of England</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eleanor_of_Aquitaine" title="Eleanor of Aquitaine">Eleanor of Aquitaine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShadis2010xix_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShadis2010xix-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The marriage of Ferdinand's parents was annulled by order of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> in 1204, due to <a href="/wiki/Consanguinity" title="Consanguinity">consanguinity</a>, but the legitimacy of the children was recognized.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Berengaria then took their children, including Ferdinand, to the court of her father, King <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VIII_of_Castile" title="Alfonso VIII of Castile">Alfonso VIII of Castile</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShadis201070_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShadis201070-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1217, her younger brother, <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Castile" title="Henry I of Castile">Henry I</a>, died and she succeeded him on the Castilian throne with Ferdinand as her heir, but she quickly surrendered it to her son.
</p><p>Alfonso of León considered himself tricked, and the young king had to begin his reign by a war against his father and a faction of the Castilian nobles. His and his mother's abilities proved too much for the king of Leon and his Castilian allies. Berengaria continued to be a key influence on Ferdinand, following her advice in prosecuting wars and even in the choice of a wife, <a href="/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Swabia" title="Elisabeth of Swabia">Elisabeth of Swabia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Unification_of_Castile_and_Le.C3.B3n"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Unification_of_Castile_and_León">Unification of Castile and León</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Unification of Castile and León">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When Ferdinand's father died in 1230, his will delivered the kingdom to his older daughters <a href="/wiki/Sancha,_heiress_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Sancha, heiress of León">Sancha</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dulce_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Dulce of León">Dulce</a>, from his first marriage to <a href="/wiki/Theresa_of_Portugal,_Queen_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Theresa of Portugal, Queen of León">Teresa of Portugal</a>.  But Ferdinand contested the will, and claimed the inheritance for himself.  At length, an agreement was reached, negotiated primarily between their mothers, Berengaria and Teresa. The resulting <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Benavente" title="Treaty of Benavente">treaty of Benavente</a> was signed on 11 December 1230, by which Ferdinand received the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Kingdom of León">Kingdom of León</a>, in return for a substantial compensation in cash and lands for his half-sisters, Sancha and Dulce.  Ferdinand thus became the first sovereign of both kingdoms since the death of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VII" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso VII">Alfonso VII</a> in 1157.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShadis1999348_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShadis1999348-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Early in his reign, Ferdinand had to deal with a rebellion of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lara" title="House of Lara">House of Lara</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reconquest_of_al-Andalus">Reconquest of al-Andalus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Reconquest of al-Andalus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Since the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Las_Navas_de_Tolosa" title="Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa">Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa</a> in 1212 halted the advance of the <a href="/wiki/Almohads" class="mw-redirect" title="Almohads">Almohads</a> in Spain, a series of truces had kept Castile and the Almohad dominions of <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">al-Andalus</a> more-or-less at peace.  However, a crisis of succession in the Almohad Caliphate after the death of <a href="/wiki/Yusuf_II,_Almohad_caliph" title="Yusuf II, Almohad caliph">Yusuf II</a> in 1224 opened to Ferdinand III an opportunity for intervention.  The Andalusian-based claimant, <a href="/wiki/Abdallah_al-Adil" title="Abdallah al-Adil">Abdallah al-Adil</a>, began to ship the bulk of Almohad arms and men across the <a href="/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar" title="Strait of Gibraltar">straits</a> to <a href="/wiki/Morocco" title="Morocco">Morocco</a> to contest the succession with his rival there, leaving al-Andalus relatively undefended.  Al-Adil's rebellious cousin, Abdullah al-Bayyasi (the <a href="/wiki/Baeza,_Spain" title="Baeza, Spain">Baezan</a>), appealed to Ferdinand III for military assistance against the usurper. In 1225, a Castilian army accompanied al-Bayyari in a campaign, <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Ja%C3%A9n_(1225)" title="Siege of Jaén (1225)">ravaging the regions</a> of <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Ja%C3%A9n_(Spain)" title="Province of Jaén (Spain)">Jaén</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vega_de_Granada" title="Vega de Granada">vega de Granada</a> and, before the end of the year, had successfully installed al-Bayyasi in <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a>.  In payment, al-Bayyasi gave Ferdinand the strategic frontier strongholds of <a href="/wiki/Ba%C3%B1os_de_la_Encina" title="Baños de la Encina">Baños de la Encina</a>, Salvatierra (the old <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Calatrava" title="Order of Calatrava">Order of Calatrava</a> fortress near <a href="/wiki/Ciudad_Real" title="Ciudad Real">Ciudad Real</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Capilla,_Badajoz" title="Capilla, Badajoz">Capilla</a> (the last of which had to be taken by siege).  When al-Bayyari was rejected and killed by a popular uprising in Córdoba shortly after, the Castilians remained in occupation of al-Bayyasi's holdings in <a href="/wiki/And%C3%BAjar" title="Andújar">Andújar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Baeza,_Spain" title="Baeza, Spain">Baeza</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martos" title="Martos">Martos</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n,_1237.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n%2C_1237.png/160px-Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n%2C_1237.png" decoding="async" width="160" height="325" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n%2C_1237.png/240px-Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n%2C_1237.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n%2C_1237.png/320px-Seal_of_Fernando_III_of_Castile_and_Le%C3%B3n%2C_1237.png 2x" data-file-width="334" data-file-height="678" /></a><figcaption>Equestrian seal (1237) of Ferdinand III, <a href="/wiki/Heraldry_of_Castile#Quartering_with_the_arms_of_León" title="Heraldry of Castile">Quartering the arms of Castile and León</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The crisis in the Almohad Caliphate, however, remained unresolved. In 1228, a new Almohad pretender, <a href="/wiki/Idris_al-Ma%27mun" title="Idris al-Ma&#39;mun">Idris al-Ma'mun</a>, decided to abandon Spain, and left with the last remnant of the Almohad forces for Morocco. Al-Andalus was left fragmented in the hands of local strongmen, only loosely led by <a href="/wiki/Ibn_Hud" title="Ibn Hud">Muhammad ibn Yusuf ibn Hud al-Judhami</a>. Seeing the opportunity, the Christian kings of the north – Ferdinand III of Castile, <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_IX_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Alfonso IX of León">Alfonso IX of León</a>, <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon" title="James I of Aragon">James I of Aragon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sancho_II_of_Portugal" title="Sancho II of Portugal">Sancho II of Portugal</a> – immediately launched a series of raids on al-Andalus, renewed almost every year. There were no great battle encounters – Ibn Hud's makeshift Andalusian army was destroyed early on, while attempting to stop the Leonese at <a href="/wiki/Alange" title="Alange">Alange</a> in 1230. The Christian armies romped through the south virtually unopposed in the field. Individual Andalusian cities were left to resist or negotiate their capitulation by themselves, with little or no prospect of rescue from Morocco or anywhere else.
</p><p>The twenty years from 1228 to 1248 saw the most massive advance in the <a href="/wiki/Reconquista" title="Reconquista">reconquista</a> yet. In this great sweep, most of the great old citadels of al-Andalus fell one by one.  Ferdinand III took the lion's share of the spoils – <a href="/wiki/Badajoz" title="Badajoz">Badajoz</a> and <a href="/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain" title="Mérida, Spain">Mérida</a> (which had fallen to the Leonese), were promptly inherited by Ferdinand in 1230; then by his own effort, <a href="/wiki/Cazorla" title="Cazorla">Cazorla</a> in 1231, <a href="/wiki/%C3%9Abeda" title="Úbeda">Úbeda</a> in 1233,  the old <a href="/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Caliphate of Córdoba">Umayyad capital</a> of <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a> in 1236, <a href="/wiki/Niebla,_Spain" title="Niebla, Spain">Niebla</a> and <a href="/wiki/Huelva" title="Huelva">Huelva</a> in 1238, <a href="/wiki/%C3%89cija" title="Écija">Écija</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lucena,_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Lucena, Córdoba">Lucena</a> in 1240, <a href="/wiki/Orihuela" title="Orihuela">Orihuela</a> and <a href="/wiki/Murcia" title="Murcia">Murcia</a> in 1243 (by the famous 'pact of Alcaraz'), <a href="/wiki/Arjona,_Spain" title="Arjona, Spain">Arjona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mula,_Spain" title="Mula, Spain">Mula</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lorca,_Spain" title="Lorca, Spain">Lorca</a> in 1244, <a href="/wiki/Cartagena,_Spain" title="Cartagena, Spain">Cartagena</a> in 1245, <a href="/wiki/Ja%C3%A9n,_Spain" title="Jaén, Spain">Jaén</a> <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Ja%C3%A9n_(1245-46)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Jaén (1245-46)">in 1246</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alicante" title="Alicante">Alicante</a> in 1248 and finally, on 22 December 1248, Ferdinand III entered as a conqueror in <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, the greatest of Andalusian cities.  At the end of this twenty-year onslaught, only a rump Andalusian state, the <a href="/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada" title="Emirate of Granada">Emirate of Granada</a>, remained unconquered (and even so, Ferdinand III managed to extract a tributary arrangement from Granada in 1238).
</p><p>Ferdinand annexed some of his conquests directly into the <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Castile" title="Crown of Castile">Crown of Castile</a>, and others were initially received and organized as vassal states under Muslim governors (e.g. Alicante, Niebla, Murcia), although they too were eventually permanently occupied and absorbed into Castile before the end of the century (Niebla in 1262, Murcia in 1264, Alicante in 1266). Outside of these vassal states, Christian rule could be heavy-handed on the new Muslim subjects. The range of Castilian conquests also sometimes transgressed into the spheres of interest of other conquerors. Thus, along the way, Ferdinand III took care to carefully negotiate with the other Christian kings to avoid conflict, e.g. the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Almizra" title="Treaty of Almizra">treaty of Almizra</a> (26 March 1244) which delineated the Murcian boundary with <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon" title="James I of Aragon">James I of Aragon</a>.
</p><p>Ferdinand divided the conquered territories between the <a href="/wiki/Knights" class="mw-redirect" title="Knights">Knights</a>, the Church, and the nobility, whom he endowed with great <a href="/wiki/Latifundia" class="mw-redirect" title="Latifundia">latifundia</a>. When he took Córdoba, he ordered the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Iudiciorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Liber Iudiciorum">Liber Iudiciorum</a></i> to be adopted and observed by its citizens, and caused it to be rendered, albeit inaccurately, into <a href="/wiki/Castilian_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Castilian language">Castilian</a>.
</p><p>The capture of Córdoba was the result of a well-planned and executed process whereby parts of the city (the Ajarquía) first fell to the independent <a href="/wiki/Almogavars" title="Almogavars">almogavars</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Sierra_Morena" title="Sierra Morena">Sierra Morena</a> to the north, which Ferdinand had not at the time subjugated.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEdwards20016_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdwards20016-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  Only in 1236 did Ferdinand arrive with a royal army to take the Medina, the religious and administrative centre of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEdwards20016_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdwards20016-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Ferdinand set up a council of <i>partidores</i> to divide the conquests and between 1237 and 1244 a great deal of land was parcelled out to private individuals and members of the royal family as well as to the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEdwards20017_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdwards20017-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> On 10 March 1241, Ferdinand established seven outposts to define the boundary of the province of Córdoba.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Domestic_policy">Domestic policy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Domestic policy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On the domestic front, Ferdinand strengthened the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Salamanca" title="University of Salamanca">University of Salamanca</a> and erected the current <a href="/wiki/Burgos_Cathedral" title="Burgos Cathedral">Cathedral of Burgos</a>. He was a patron of the newest movement in the Church, that of the <a href="/wiki/Mendicant_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Mendicant Orders">mendicant Orders</a>. Whereas the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a>, and then the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercians</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cluniac" class="mw-redirect" title="Cluniac">Cluniacs</a>, had taken a major part in the <i>Reconquista</i> up until then, Ferdinand founded houses for <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friars</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a>, <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Trinitarian</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Mercedarian" class="mw-redirect" title="Mercedarian">Mercedarian</a> Orders throughout Andalusia, thus determining the future religious character of that region. Ferdinand has also been credited with sustaining the <i><a href="/wiki/Convivencia" title="Convivencia">convivencia</a></i> in Andalusia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEdwards2001182_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdwards2001182-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He himself joined the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>, and is honored in that Order.
</p><p>He took care not to overburden his subjects with taxation, fearing, as he said, the curse of one poor woman more than a whole army of Saracens.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHeckmann1909_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeckmann1909-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stferdinandjf.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Stferdinandjf.JPG/175px-Stferdinandjf.JPG" decoding="async" width="175" height="233" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Stferdinandjf.JPG/263px-Stferdinandjf.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Stferdinandjf.JPG/350px-Stferdinandjf.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="4608" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Ferdinand III (Patio of <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Cathedral_of_San_Fernando_(Pampanga)" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan Cathedral of San Fernando (Pampanga)">Metropolitan Cathedral of San Fernando</a> in the Philippines)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ferdinand III had started out as a contested king of Castile.  By the time of his death he had delivered to his son and heir, <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso X">Alfonso X</a>, a massively expanded kingdom. The boundaries of the new Castilian state established by Ferdinand III remained nearly unchanged until the late 15th century. His biographer, Sister María del Carmen Fernández de Castro Cabeza, <a href="/wiki/Handmaids_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" title="Handmaids of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">A.C.J.</a>, asserts that, on his deathbed, Ferdinand said to his son "you will be rich in land and in many good vassals, more than any other king in Christendom."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFernández_de_Castro_Cabeza1988277_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFernández_de_Castro_Cabeza1988277-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ferdinand's death was attributed to a <a href="/wiki/Dropsy" class="mw-redirect" title="Dropsy">dropsy</a> he contracted in the winter of 1251. His death took place on 30 May 1252, and he was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Seville" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Seville">Cathedral of Seville</a> by his son, Alfonso X. The funeral took place on 1 June 1252 and was officiated by Remondo, Bishop of Segovia, in the cathedral. In the city there were royal vassals, bishops, abbots and wealthy men of the kingdom, who had come to show their lament. His tomb was inscribed in four languages: <a href="/wiki/Arabic" title="Arabic">Arabic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>, <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, and an early version of <a href="/wiki/Castilian_Spanish" title="Castilian Spanish">Castilian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMenocal200947_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenocal200947-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> as Saint Ferdinand by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a> in 1671.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEReilly1993133_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEReilly1993133-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Today, the <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt body</a> of Saint Ferdinand can still be seen in the Cathedral of Seville, for he rests enclosed in a gold and crystal casket worthy of the king.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> His golden crown still encircles his head as he reclines beneath the statue of the Virgin of the Kings.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFitzhenry20096_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFitzhenry20096-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Several places named <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="San Fernando (disambiguation)">San Fernando</a> were founded across the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Empire" title="Spanish Empire">Spanish Empire</a> in his honor. His supposed likeliness, <a href="/wiki/Enthronement" title="Enthronement">enthronement</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lobera_(sword)" title="Lobera (sword)">sword</a> and orb are depicted on the crest of <a href="/wiki/Sevilla_FC" title="Sevilla FC">Sevilla Fútbol Club</a>.
</p><p>The symbol of his power as a king was his sword <a href="/wiki/Lobera_(sword)" title="Lobera (sword)">Lobera</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Ferdinand is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Seville, <a href="/wiki/Aranjuez" title="Aranjuez">Aranjuez</a>, <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando_de_Henares" title="San Fernando de Henares">San Fernando de Henares</a>, <a href="/wiki/Maspalomas" title="Maspalomas">Maspalomas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pivijay" title="Pivijay">Pivijay</a>, and of several other localities. He is also the patron of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Army" title="Spanish Army">Spanish Army</a>'s Corps of Engineers,<sup id="cite_ref-engineers_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-engineers-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and engineers generally.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Since the establishment in 1819 of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of San Cristóbal de La Laguna">Diocese of San Cristóbal de La Laguna</a>, also called "Diocese of Tenerife" (<a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>), Saint Ferdinand is the co-patron of the diocese and of its <a href="/wiki/La_Laguna_Cathedral" title="La Laguna Cathedral">Cathedral</a> pursuant to the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> issued by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> This is because La Laguna is a <a href="/wiki/Suffragan_diocese" title="Suffragan diocese">suffragan diocese</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Seville" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Seville">Archdiocese of Seville</a> whose capital city has Saint Ferdinand as one of its co-patrons, together with the <i>Virgen de los Reyes</i>.  Saint Ferdinand is also the patron of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_La_Laguna" title="University of La Laguna">University of La Laguna</a>, since this institution was founded under the name of <i>Universidad Literaria de San Fernando</i> (Literary University of Saint Ferdinand).<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="First_marriage">First marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: First marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg/220px-Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg/330px-Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg/440px-Ferdinand_III_%26_Beatriz_of_Swabia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3024" data-file-height="4032" /></a><figcaption>King Ferdinand and his wife, Beatrice, depicted in the Burgos Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1219, Ferdinand married <a href="/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Swabia" title="Elisabeth of Swabia">Elisabeth of Swabia</a> (1203–1235).<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBianchini201966_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBianchini201966-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> She was the fourth daughter of <a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Swabia" title="Philip of Swabia">Philip</a>, Duke of Swabia, and <a href="/wiki/Irene_Angelina" title="Irene Angelina">Irene Angelina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKinkade20198_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKinkade20198-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Their children were:
</p>
<ol><li><a href="/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile" title="Alfonso X of Castile">Alfonso X</a> (1221–1284), his successor</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Frederick_of_Castile" title="Frederick of Castile">Frederick</a> (1223–1277)</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Infante_Ferdinand_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Infante Ferdinand of Castile (page does not exist)">Ferdinand</a> (1225–1243/1248)</li>
<li>Eleanor (born 1227), died young</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Berengaria_of_Castile_(1228%E2%80%931289)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Berengaria of Castile (1228–1289) (page does not exist)">Berengaria</a> (1228–1288/89), a nun at <a href="/wiki/Las_Huelgas" class="mw-redirect" title="Las Huelgas">Las Huelgas</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Henry_of_Castile_the_Senator" title="Henry of Castile the Senator">Henry</a> (1230–1303)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Castile" title="Philip of Castile">Philip</a> (1231–1274). He was promised to the Church, but was so taken by the beauty of <a href="/wiki/Christina_of_Norway,_Infanta_of_Castile" title="Christina of Norway, Infanta of Castile">Christina of Norway</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Haakon_IV_of_Norway" class="mw-redirect" title="Haakon IV of Norway">Haakon IV of Norway</a>, who had been intended as a bride for one of his brothers, that he abandoned his holy vows and married her. She died in 1262, childless.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Sancho_of_Castile_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sancho of Castile (archbishop)">Sancho</a>, Archbishop of Toledo and Seville (1233–1261)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Manuel_of_Castile" title="Manuel of Castile">Manuel of Castile</a> (1234–1283)</li>
<li>Maria, died an infant in November 1235</li></ol>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_marriage">Second marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ferdinand_III_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Second marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After he was widowed, he married <a href="/wiki/Joan,_Countess_of_Ponthieu" title="Joan, Countess of Ponthieu">Joan, Countess of Ponthieu</a>, before August 1237.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJohnstone1914436_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnstone1914436-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> They had four sons and one daughter:
</p>
<ol><li><a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_of_Castile_(born_1238)" title="Ferdinand of Castile (born 1238)">Ferdinand</a> (1238–1264/1269), <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Aumale" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Aumale">Count of Aumale</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Eleanor_of_Castile" title="Eleanor of Castile">Eleanor</a> (c. 1241–1290), married <a href="/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England">Edward I of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPowicke1991235_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPowicke1991235-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_of_Castile&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Louis of Castile (page does not exist)">Louis</a> (1243–1269)</li>
<li>Simon (1244), died young and buried in a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a></li>
<li>John (1245), died young and buried at the cathedral in <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a></li></ol>
