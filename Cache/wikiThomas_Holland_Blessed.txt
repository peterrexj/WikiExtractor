<p><b>Thomas Holland</b> (1600 at <a href="/wiki/Sutton,_St_Helens" title="Sutton, St Helens">Sutton, Lancashire</a> – executed 12 December 1642 at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a>) was an English <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Holland_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Holland was born in Lancashire, possibly son of Richard Holland, gentleman. He attended the English College at <a href="/wiki/St._Omer%27s_College" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Omer&#39;s College">St. Omer's</a> and subsequently in August, 1621, went to the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Valladolid" title="English College, Valladolid">English College, Valladolid</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Jesuits_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jesuits-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When the abortive negotiations for the "<a href="/wiki/Spanish_Match" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish Match">Spanish Match</a>" were taking place in 1623, Holland was sent to <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> to assure <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_England" title="Charles II of England">Prince Charles</a> of the loyalty of the seminarists of Valladolid, which he did in a Latin oration.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1624 he entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> at <a href="/wiki/Watten,_Nord" title="Watten, Nord">Watten</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Southern_Netherlands" title="Southern Netherlands">Southern Netherlands</a>, and not long after was ordained to <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">priesthood</a> at <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Singapore_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Singapore-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He took the missionary oath 29 December 1633 and served as minister at <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> and prefect at <a href="/wiki/St._Omer%27s_College" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Omer&#39;s College">St. Omer's</a>, where he acquired the nickname, <i>bibliotheca pietatis</i> ("Library of Piety") because of his vast knowledge of the ascetical life.<sup id="cite_ref-Singapore_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Singapore-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He made his solemn <a href="/wiki/Religious_profession" title="Religious profession">religious profession</a> as spiritual coadjutor at <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> (28 May 1634) and was sent on the English mission the following year, in hopes that the change might improve his health.<sup id="cite_ref-Jesuits_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jesuits-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Holland worked in London, sometimes assuming the aliases of Saunderson and Hammond. He was an adept in disguising himself, and could speak perfect French, Spanish, and Flemish. He had to stay indoors during the day and only travel at night because of the priest-hunters. His health did not improve.<sup id="cite_ref-fnd_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fnd-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was eventually arrested on suspicion in a London street returning from a sick call, 4 October 1642, and committed to the <a href="/wiki/New_Prison" title="New Prison">New Prison</a>. He was afterwards transferred to <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate</a>, and arraigned at the <a href="/wiki/Old_Bailey" title="Old Bailey">Old Bailey</a>, 7 December, for being a priest. There was no conclusive evidence as to this; but as he refused to swear he was not, the jury found him guilty,<sup id="cite_ref-Jesuits_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jesuits-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to the indignation of the Lord Mayor, <a href="/wiki/Isaac_Penington_(Lord_Mayor)" title="Isaac Penington (Lord Mayor)">Isaac Penington</a>, and another member of the bench named Garroway. On Saturday, 10 December, Sergeant Peter Phesant, presumably acting for the recorder, passed sentence on him. On his return to prison Holland heard many <a href="/wiki/Sacrament_of_reconciliation" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacrament of reconciliation">confessions</a>.
</p><p>Some Capuchin friends smuggled in supplies so he could celebrate Mass one last time. Soon after his last Mass he was taken off to <a href="/wiki/Execution_by_hanging" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution by hanging">execution</a>. There he was allowed to make a speech and to say many prayers, and when the cart was turned away, he was left to hang till he was dead.<sup id="cite_ref-fnd_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fnd-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
