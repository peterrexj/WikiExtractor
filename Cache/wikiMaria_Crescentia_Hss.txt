<p><b>Maria Crescentia Höss (Höß)</b>, <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_Regular_of_Saint_Francis_of_Penance" title="Third Order Regular of Saint Francis of Penance">TOR</a> (1682–1744) was a <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sister</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_Regular_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order Regular of St. Francis">Third Order Regular of St. Francis</a>. In 1900, she was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>, and she was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 2001 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Crescentia_H%C3%B6ss&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anna Höss was born on 20 October 1682 in <a href="/wiki/Kaufbeuren" title="Kaufbeuren">Kaufbeuren</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to Matthias Höss and his wife, Lucia Hoermann, as the sixth of their eight children. Only three of the children survived into adulthood.
</p><p>Anna became a <a href="/wiki/Weaver_(occupation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Weaver (occupation)">weaver</a>, but her greatest ambition was to enter the local convent of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order">Tertiary Franciscans</a> in Kaufbeuren, which occupied the old <a href="/wiki/Meierhof" title="Meierhof">Meierhof</a> of the town, in whose <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> she often prayed. As a poor weaver, however, her father did not have enough money to pay the customary <a href="/wiki/Dowry" title="Dowry">dowry</a> expected of a candidate, so she was not admitted.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monasteries_of_the_Third_Order_Regular">Monasteries of the Third Order Regular</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Crescentia_H%C3%B6ss&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monasteries of the Third Order Regular">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Unlike monasteries of the nuns of the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a>, known as the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a>, the sisters of the Third Order were completely local, living under the authority of the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. The history of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a> had a range of organizational models, in that many communities of <a href="/wiki/Religious_sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sisters">religious sisters</a> did not embrace the <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">enclosure</a>, but considered active works of charity, tending to the poor and sick, as part of their Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Charism" class="mw-redirect" title="Charism">charism</a>. Monasteries like that of Kaufbeuren were established to pursue the purely contemplative life, usually in an urban setting.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>, however, refused to accept spiritual supervision or responsibility for those monasteries which did not accept the strictest form of <a href="/wiki/Enclosure" title="Enclosure">enclosure</a>, such as the Poor Clares had. Thus the monastic communities of the Third Order like that of Kaufbeuren, who did not have the same connection with the public as did the active Sisters, were usually entirely dependent on the local <a href="/wiki/Clergy" title="Clergy">clergy</a> for spiritual direction and on local patrons for their survival. They were often marked by their precarious financial situations.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_in_the_monastery">Life in the monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Crescentia_H%C3%B6ss&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Life in the monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1703 the Mayor of Kaufbeuren, a <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a>, performed a major service to the monastery by purchasing a tavern adjacent to it which was often the source of disturbance to the quiet of the cloister, and donating the building to the sisters. He refused compensation but asked simply that, in return, Anna be accepted as a candidate.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> As a result of this intervention, the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mother_superior" class="extiw" title="wikt:mother superior">mother superior</a> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Oberin</i>) of the monastery felt obligated to receive her, and Anna was admitted in June of that year. The superior, however, resented this and referred to Anna as a "parasite", since she was felt not to be contributing to the community. Nevertheless, Anna received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> and took the name <b>Maria Crescentia</b>.
</p><p>The siters were not kind to her at first, due to the manner of her admission. Once clothed as a member of the Order, Crescentia was subjected to a prolonged persecution by the unfriendly superior and some of the older sisters. They treated her as a servant, giving her the most menial tasks to perform. Although Crescentia was at first given a <a href="/wiki/Room" title="Room">cell</a> of her own, it was later taken from her and given to a new <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> who had brought with her the customary dowry.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>   Thereafter she had to beg the other sisters for a corner of their cells in which she might sleep. When she was finally given a place of her own again, it was a dark and damp cubbyhole. Nevertheless, Crescentia was allowed to <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">profess</a> <a href="/wiki/Vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Vows">vows</a> and become a full member of the monastic community. She was assigned to serve in the kitchen and did the weaving for the monastery.
</p><p>Eventually, in 1707, a new superior was elected who was more sympathetic to Crescentia, and she was entrusted first with the important office of <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">portress</a>, and in 1717 she was appointed <a href="/wiki/Novice_master" title="Novice master">mistress of novices</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At this stage of her monastic life, Crescentia was a prolific letter writer, who left many letters to people in various social positions, in which she gave them advice and comfort in their worries.
</p><p>Though by then she had begun to suffer from poor health, even paralysis, in 1741 she was elected as the monastery's mother superior, serving in that office until her death on 5 April, <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> Sunday, 1744.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During her short tenure in this position of leadership of the community, <a href="/wiki/Mother_(religious_title)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother (religious title)">Mother</a> Crescentia led a renewal of their way of life. She counseled unlimited trust in <a href="/wiki/Divine_Providence" class="mw-redirect" title="Divine Providence">Divine Providence</a>, readiness to serve in community life, a love of silence, devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">Crucified</a> Jesus, to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a> and to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Mother" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Mother">Blessed Mother</a>. She encouraged the sisters to turn to the <a href="/wiki/Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospels">Gospels</a> to develop their inner <a href="/wiki/Spirituality#Religion" title="Spirituality">spiritual life</a>, and was noted for the selectivity of her choices regarding candidates to the community. She justified this by saying: "God wants the monastery rich in virtue, not in temporal goods".
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Crescentia_H%C3%B6ss&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The process of her <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> was begun in 1775. The <a href="/wiki/Secularization" title="Secularization">secularization</a> of monasteries which occurred in the Revolutionary upheavals of the late 18th century and the <a href="/wiki/Anti-Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-Catholic">anti-Catholic</a> policies of the German government during the <i><a href="/wiki/Kulturkampf" title="Kulturkampf">Kulturkampf</a></i> of the 19th century prevented the monastic community from proceeding with the process.
</p><p>Finally, in 1900, Mother Crescentia was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>. She was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 25 November 2001 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, along with three others.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her monastery was then renamed St. Crescentia Monastery (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Crescentiakloster</i>) in her honor.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
