<p><b>Virgil</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;700</span>– 27 November 784), also spelled <b>Vergil</b>, <b>Vergilius</b>, <b>Virgilius</b>, <b>Feirgil</b> or <b>Fearghal</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and early <a href="/wiki/Astronomer" title="Astronomer">astronomer</a>. He left Ireland around 745, intending to visit the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>; but, like many of his countrymen, he settled in <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a>. Virgil served as <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Aghaboe" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Aghaboe">abbot of Aghaboe</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Ossory" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Ossory">bishop of Ossory</a> and later <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Salzburg">bishop of Salzburg</a>. He was called "the Apostle of <a href="/wiki/Carinthia" title="Carinthia">Carinthia</a>"<sup id="cite_ref-Warren1881_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Warren1881-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and "the <a href="/wiki/Geometer" class="mw-redirect" title="Geometer">geometer</a>".
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Virgil_of_Salzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He originated from a noble family of Ireland, where his name was <a href="/wiki/Fearghal" title="Fearghal">Feirgil or Fearghal</a>, and is said to have been a descendant of <a href="/wiki/Niall_of_the_Nine_Hostages" title="Niall of the Nine Hostages">Niall of the Nine Hostages</a>. Feirgil was probably educated at the <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Iona</a> monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_the_Four_Masters" title="Annals of the Four Masters">Annals of the Four Masters</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a></i>, he is referenced as the Abbot of <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Aghaboe" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Aghaboe">Aghaboe</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-turner_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-turner-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/County_Laois" title="County Laois">County Laois</a>, where he was known as "the Geometer" because of his knowledge of geography.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 745, he left Ireland, intending to visit the Holy Land; but, like many of his countrymen, who seemed to have adopted this practice as a work of piety, he settled down in France, where he was received with great favour by <a href="/wiki/Pippin_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin the Younger">Pippin the Younger</a>, who was then <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Mayor of the Palace">Mayor of the Palace</a> under <a href="/wiki/Childeric_III" title="Childeric III">Childeric III</a> of <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconia</a>. He was an adviser to Pippin. He probably used a copy of the <i><a href="/wiki/Collectio_canonum_Hibernensis" title="Collectio canonum Hibernensis">Collectio canonum Hibernensis</a></i> (an Irish collection of canon law) to advise him to receive royal unction in 751, to assist his recognition as king Pippin III after the deposition of Childeric.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After spending two years at <a href="/wiki/Cressy,_Seine-Maritime" title="Cressy, Seine-Maritime">Cressy</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Compi%C3%A8gne" title="Compiègne">Compiègne</a>, he went to <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, at the invitation of <a href="/wiki/Odilo_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Odilo of Bavaria">Duke Odilo</a>, where he founded the <a href="/wiki/Herrenchiemsee#Old_Palace_(Herrenchiemsee_Abbey)" title="Herrenchiemsee">monastery of Chiemsee</a>, and within a year or two, was made Abbot of <a href="/wiki/St_Peter%27s_Abbey,_Salzburg" title="St Peter&#39;s Abbey, Salzburg">St Peter's Abbey</a> at <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>. Among his notable accomplishments was the conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Slavic_settlement_of_the_Eastern_Alps" title="Slavic settlement of the Eastern Alps">Alpine Slavs</a> to Christianity; he also sent missionaries to Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Abbot of St Peter's, he clashed with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a>. A priest having, through ignorance, conferred the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament" title="Sacrament">Sacrament</a> of <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">Baptism</a> using, in place of the correct formula, the words "<i>Baptizo te in nomine patria et filia et spiritu sancta</i>" (instead of "<i>Baptizo te in nomine patris et filii et spiritus sancti</i>"), Vergilius held that the sacrament had been validly conferred, but Boniface complained to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zachary" title="Pope Zachary">Pope Zachary</a>. The latter, however, decided in favour of Virgil. Later on, Boniface accused Virgil of spreading discord between himself and Duke Odilo of Bavaria and of teaching a doctrine in regard to men descended not from Adam, which was "contrary to the Scriptures". Pope Zachary's decision in this case was that "if it shall be clearly established that he professes belief in another world and other people existing beneath the earth, or in [another] sun and moon there, thou art to hold a council, and deprive him of his sacerdotal rank, and expel him from the church."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The treatise in which Virgil expounded his doctrine no longer exists. Two things, however, are certain: first, that there was involved the problem of <a href="/wiki/Original_sin" title="Original sin">original sin</a> and the universality of redemption; secondly, that Virgil succeeded in freeing himself from the charge of teaching a doctrine contrary to Scripture. It is likely that Boniface, already biased against Virgil because of the preceding case, misunderstood him, taking it for granted, perhaps, that if there are <a href="/wiki/Antipodes" title="Antipodes">antipodes</a>, the "other race of men" are not descendants of <a href="/wiki/Adam_(Bible)" class="mw-redirect" title="Adam (Bible)">Adam</a> and were not redeemed by Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-turner_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-turner-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the martyrdom of Boniface, Virgil was made <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Salzburg">Bishop of Salzburg</a> in 766 or 767 and laboured successfully for the upbuilding of his diocese as well as for the <a href="/wiki/Germanic_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Germanic Christianity">spread of Christianity</a> in neighbouring <a href="/wiki/Germanic_paganism" title="Germanic paganism">heathen</a> countries, especially in <a href="/wiki/Carantania" title="Carantania">Carinthia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-turner_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-turner-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Virgil_of_Salzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg/220px-St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="329" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg/330px-St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg/440px-St.Adolari_-_Empore_6_Virgil.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1712" data-file-height="2560" /></a><figcaption></figcaption></figure>
<p>He is venerated as a saint in both the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. In 1233, he was formally canonized by Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His doctrine that the earth is a sphere was derived from the teaching of ancient geographers, and his belief in the existence of the antipodes was probably influenced by the accounts which the ancient Irish voyagers gave of their journeys. This, at least, is the opinion of Rettberg (<i>Kirchengesch. Deutschlands</i>, II, 236).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Dedications">Dedications</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Virgil_of_Salzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Dedications">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Aside from being personally associated with <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Aghaboe" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Aghaboe">Aghaboe Abbey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Salzburg_Cathedral" title="Salzburg Cathedral">Salzburg Cathedral</a>, a number of parishes around the world are dedicated to him, mostly being founded by small populations of far-flung <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Catholic">Irish Catholics</a>, like himself.
</p><p>There is a church dedicated to him in <a href="/wiki/Broad_Channel,_Queens" title="Broad Channel, Queens">Broad Channel, Queens</a>, New York, which merged with another parish in 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A parish in <a href="/wiki/Morris_Plains" class="mw-redirect" title="Morris Plains">Morris Plains</a>, New Jersey, is also dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Fittingly for this Irish geometer who predicted antipodes, several churches in the <a href="/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere" title="Southern Hemisphere">Southern Hemisphere</a> are dedicated to him. <a href="/wiki/St_Virgil%27s_College" title="St Virgil&#39;s College">St Virgil's College</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hobart" title="Hobart">Hobart</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tasmania" title="Tasmania">Tasmania</a> is named for him.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> There was a St Virgilius Church in <a href="/wiki/Balclutha,_New_Zealand" title="Balclutha, New Zealand">Balclutha, New Zealand</a> designed by <a href="/wiki/List_of_buildings_by_Francis_Petre" title="List of buildings by Francis Petre">Francis Petre</a> which moved and was re-dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Mary_MacKillop" title="Mary MacKillop">Mary MacKillop</a>, however the new chapel remains dedicated to Virgilius.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dhiraja.srichinmoycentre.org_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dhiraja.srichinmoycentre.org-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Art_and_culture">Art and culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Virgil_of_Salzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Art and culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A large marble statue of him stands outside the doors of Salzburg Cathedral. A <a href="/wiki/Stained-glass" class="mw-redirect" title="Stained-glass">stained-glass</a> window of St Virgil contemplating the antipodes sits in <a href="/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_University_of_Sydney" title="St John&#39;s College, University of Sydney">St John's College, University of Sydney</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Sebastian Stief painted him amidst Salzburg's construction,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Alec Szolomiak painted him,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> as did <a href="/wiki/Jan_Toorop" title="Jan Toorop">Jan Toorop</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and Dhiraja McBryde.<sup id="cite_ref-dhiraja.srichinmoycentre.org_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dhiraja.srichinmoycentre.org-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Yalumba" title="Yalumba">Yalumba</a>, Australia's oldest family-owned winery, produces a <a href="/wiki/Viognier" title="Viognier">Viognier</a> named <i>Virgilius</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
