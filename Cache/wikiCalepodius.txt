<p><b>Saint Calepodius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Calepodio</i>; died 232 AD) was a priest who was killed during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> by the Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Severus" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexander Severus">Alexander Severus</a>.  One of the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome" title="Catacombs of Rome">catacombs of Rome</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Cemetery_of_Calepodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Cemetery of Calepodius">cemetery of Calepodius</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Aurelian_Way" class="mw-redirect" title="Aurelian Way">Aurelian Way</a>, was named after him.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_of_Calepodius,_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Martyrdom_of_Calepodius%2C_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg/220px-Martyrdom_of_Calepodius%2C_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="296" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Martyrdom_of_Calepodius%2C_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg/330px-Martyrdom_of_Calepodius%2C_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Martyrdom_of_Calepodius%2C_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg/440px-Martyrdom_of_Calepodius%2C_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2279" data-file-height="3066" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_of_Calepodius,_a_Christian_minister._Engraving._Wellcome_V0031764.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">Martyrdom</a> of Calepodius</div></div></div><p>His <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Hagiography</a> recalls that he was tortured and then thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Tiber_river" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiber river">Tiber river</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Mill_stone" class="mw-redirect" title="Mill stone">mill stone</a> tied round his neck.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Calepodius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, along with those of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Callistus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Callistus">Saint Callistus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cornelius_the_Centurion" title="Cornelius the Centurion">Saint Cornelius</a>, were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> in the 10th century to the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Maria in Trastevere">Santa Maria in Trastevere</a> (St. Mary beyond the Tiber) and deposited under the high altar.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>   Some relics of the three saints were translated to <a href="/wiki/Fulda" title="Fulda">Fulda</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>, and some of Callistus’ relics were also translated to <a href="/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Notre-Dame de Reims">Notre-Dame de Reims</a>.  However, some of Callistus’ relics are still kept with those of Calepodius in Santa Maria in Trastevere.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Callistus is venerated on May 10 with saints Palmatius, Simplicius, Felix, Blanda, and companions. St. Palmatius was of consular rank, and he died with his wife, children, and household. St. Simplicius was a senator who suffered death with sixty-five members of his family and household. Sts. Felix and Blanda were husband and wife.
</p>
