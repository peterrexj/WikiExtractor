<p><b>Saints Victoria, Anatolia, and Audax</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sante Vittoria, Anatolia, e Audace</i>) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. Victoria and Anatolia are mentioned (without Audax) in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> under the date of 10 July.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Anatolia was first mentioned in the <i>De Laude Sanctorum</i> composed in 396 by <a href="/wiki/Victricius" title="Victricius">Victrice (Victricius), bishop of Rouen</a> (330-409).
</p><p>Anatolia and Victoria are mentioned together in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> under 10 July: <i>VI idus iulii in Savinis Anatholiae Victoriae</i>; Victoria is also mentioned alone under 19 December: <i>In Savinis civitate Tribulana Victoriae</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Vittoria_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Vittoria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The two saints appear in the mosaics of <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Apollinare_Nuovo" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Apollinare Nuovo">Basilica of Sant'Apollinare Nuovo</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, between Saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paulina" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Paulina">Paulina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Christina_of_Bolsena" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Christina of Bolsena">Christina</a>. A <i>Passio SS. Anatoliae et Audacis et S. Victoriae</i> of the 6th or seventh century, which added the name of Audax, was mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Aldhelm" title="Aldhelm">Aldhelm</a> (died 709) and <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> (died  735), who list the saints in their martyrologies. <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a> lists Anatolia and Audax under 9 July and Victoria under 23 December.<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Vittoria_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Vittoria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Their legend recounts that, in the time of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius_(emperor)" class="mw-redirect" title="Decius (emperor)">Decius</a>, Anatolia and Victoria were sisters whose <a href="/wiki/Arranged_marriage" title="Arranged marriage">marriage was arranged</a> to two noble, non-Christian <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> men. They resisted <a href="/wiki/Matrimony" class="mw-redirect" title="Matrimony">matrimony</a> and their prospective grooms denounced them as Christians. They received permission to imprison the women on their estates and convince them to renounce their faith. Anatolia's suitor, Titus Aurelius, gave up, and handed her back to the authorities. Victoria's suitor, Eugenius, was more persistent, but also ended up returning her to the authorities.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Deaths">Deaths</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victoria,_Anatolia,_and_Audax&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Deaths">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"/><table class="box-Unreferenced_section plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-Unreferenced" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><a href="/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/50px-Question_book-new.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="39" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/75px-Question_book-new.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/100px-Question_book-new.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="399" /></a></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>does not <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources">cite</a> any <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">sources</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Please help <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Victoria,_Anatolia,_and_Audax&amp;action=edit">improve this section</a> by <a href="/wiki/Help:Referencing_for_beginners" title="Help:Referencing for beginners">adding citations to reliable sources</a>. <p>Victoria's legend states that she was stabbed through the heart in 250 AD at Trebula Mutuesca (today <a href="/wiki/Monteleone_Sabino" title="Monteleone Sabino">Monteleone Sabino</a>) after chasing away a dragon terrorizing the residents in exchange for their conversion.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> An elaboration on her legend states that her murderer was immediately struck with <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leprosy</a>, and died six days later. Anatolia was killed, also in 250 AD, at "Thora" (identified with present-day <a href="/w/index.php?title=Santa_Anatolia_(Borgorose)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Santa Anatolia (Borgorose) (page does not exist)">Sant'Anatolia di Borgorose</a>). Her legend states that she was at first locked up with a poisonous <a href="/wiki/Snake" title="Snake">snake</a>. The snake refused to bite her, and a soldier named Audax was sent into her cell to kill her. The snake attacked him instead, but Anatolia saved him from the snake. Impressed by her example, he converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and was martyred by the sword with her.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spread_of_cult">Spread of cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victoria,_Anatolia,_and_Audax&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Spread of cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Due to the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation of their relics</a>, their cult spread across Italy.  Some relics of Saint Victoria were transferred in 827 by Abbot Peter of <a href="/wiki/Farfa_Abbey" title="Farfa Abbey">Farfa</a> from the Abbey to Mount Matenano in the Picene area (roughly the south of Le Marche) because the Abbey was besieged by "<a href="/wiki/Saracens" class="mw-redirect" title="Saracens">Saracens</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Vittoria_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Vittoria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The town of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Vittoria_in_Matenano" title="Santa Vittoria in Matenano">Santa Vittoria in Matenano</a> is named after her. Ratfredus, a later Abbot of Farfa, brought the body from Farfa to Santa Vittoria in Matenano on 20 June 931.
</p><p>The bodies of Anatolia and Audax were transferred by Abbot Leo to <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Subiaco, Italy">Subiaco</a> around 950. At an unknown date, a scapula of Anatolia was translated to the present-day <a href="/w/index.php?title=Santa_Anatolia_(Borgorose)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Santa Anatolia (Borgorose) (page does not exist)">Sant'Anatolia di Borgorose</a> and an arm of the saint was translated to the present-day <a href="/wiki/Esanatoglia" title="Esanatoglia">Esanatoglia</a>. The bodies of Anatolia and Audax still rest at Subiaco in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> of Santa Scholastica, under the <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">sacrament</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santa_Vittoria_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santa_Vittoria-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day was actually 9 July. A simulacrum and other relics of Saint Victoria are currently on display at the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Vittoria,_Rome" title="Santa Maria della Vittoria, Rome">Santa Maria della Vittoria</a> church in Rome. 
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Cathedral,_Kilkenny" title="St Mary&#39;s Cathedral, Kilkenny">St Mary's Cathedral, Kilkenny</a>, Ireland also claims to hold St Victoria's body, preserved in wax, along with a <a href="/wiki/Chalice" title="Chalice">chalice</a> containing some of her blood. These were supposedly sent to Kilkenny in 1845 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
