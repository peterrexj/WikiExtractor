<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sulpitius_the_Pious&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to his <i>Vita</i>, Sulpitius was born at <a href="/wiki/Vatan,_Indre" title="Vatan, Indre">Vatan</a> (<a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Bourges" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Bourges">Diocese of Bourges</a>), of noble parents, before the end of the sixth century. From his youth he devoted himself to good works and to the study of <a href="/wiki/Scripture" class="mw-redirect" title="Scripture">Scripture</a>, and donated his large patrimony to the Church and the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Austregisilus" title="Austregisilus">Austregisilus</a>, Bishop of Bourges, ordained him <a href="/wiki/Cleric" class="mw-redirect" title="Cleric">cleric</a> of his church, then <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, and finally made him director of his episcopal school.  <a href="/wiki/Clotaire_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotaire II">Clotaire II</a> (King of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a> from 613 to 629), who had heard of his merits, summoned him and made him almoner and chaplain of his armies.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Upon the death of Bishop Austregisilus (c. 624) Sulpicious was recalled to <a href="/wiki/Bourges" title="Bourges">Bourges</a> to succeed him. Thenceforth he labored with much zeal and success to re-establish ecclesiastical discipline, for the relief of the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-degert_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-degert-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 626 Sulpitius attended the Council of Clichy and held several others with the bishops of his province. 
St. <a href="/wiki/Didier_of_Cahors" title="Didier of Cahors">Desiderius of Cahors</a>, treasurer to King Clothar II and later Bishop of Cahors,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> was his personal friend; three letters survive which he addressed to him.<sup id="cite_ref-degert_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-degert-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In the settings of <i>Vita Sulpicii Episcopi Biturgi</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Sulpicius' miracles show him receiving "Theudogisilus", a noble from the <i><a href="/wiki/Palatium" class="mw-redirect" title="Palatium">palatium</a></i> of the king with entertainments and a "great heaped fire" (in a fireplace in the centre of the <a href="/wiki/Great_hall" title="Great hall">great hall</a>, the smoke issuing through a vent in the roof). Sulpitius allegedly extinguished this fire, when it threatened to get out of control, with an outstretched hand. The <i>vita</i> asserts with approval that "he, the holy man gave leave for no-one, neither heretic, gentile or Jew, to live in the city of Bourges without the grace of baptism" - with many consequent conversions from the Jews of Bourges.
</p><p>The <i>Vita</i> tells that <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> sent his representative the merciless general Lollo (<i>Lollonius</i>) to reside at Bourges and to bring the city more closely under the king's command.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Sulpitius intervened with King Dagobert on behalf of his flock, of whom a too heavy tax was exacted.<sup id="cite_ref-degert_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-degert-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When the people came complaining of their treatment to Sulpicius, he decreed a three-day fast for clergy and laity, but also sent one of his clergy, Ebargisilus by name, to the king.
</p><p>Towards the end of his life Sulpitius took a <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor" title="Coadjutor">coadjutor</a>, Vulfolendus,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and retired to a monastery which he had founded near Bourges. There he died on 17 January 646, which day several manuscripts of the <a href="/wiki/Hieronymian_Martyrology" class="mw-redirect" title="Hieronymian Martyrology">Hieronymian Martyrology</a> indicate as his feast. The reports of miracles at his tomb in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> he had ordered built began soon after his death and the place became a place of pilgrimage.
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>That place, the basilica, where the memorable man of God is buried, is called Navis, because the port of ships is seen to be there. It is a most lovely place between two rivers with pastures and woods and vineyards in great number, with fields and rivers flowing between huge plains so that there, the inhabitants may be seen to possess the image of paradise.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sulpitius_the_Pious&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In his honour the church of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Sulpice_(Paris)" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Sulpice (Paris)">Saint-Sulpice</a> was built in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, from which the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Saint-Sulpice" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Saint-Sulpice">Society of Saint-Sulpice</a> derives its name.
</p><p>The <i>vita</i> of Sulpitius also contains a <i>vita</i> of his saintly contemporary, <a href="/wiki/Eustadiola" title="Eustadiola">Eustadiola</a>.
</p>
