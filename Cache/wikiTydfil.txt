<p><b>Saint Tydfil</b> (<a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: Tudful; <a href="/wiki/Martyred" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyred">martyred</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;480</span>) is the legendary dedicatee of <a href="/wiki/Merthyr_Tydfil" title="Merthyr Tydfil">Merthyr Tydfil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OXDIC_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OXDIC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The old parish church of St Tydfil, Merthyr Tydfil is dedicated to her and is reputed to be the site of her death.
</p><p>According to legend, Tydfil was the twenty-third daughter of <a href="/wiki/Brychan" title="Brychan">Brychan</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Brycheiniog" title="Brycheiniog">Brycheiniog</a>, by his fourth wife.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was murdered with her brother Rhun in Merthyr Tydfil, by either <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> or <a href="/wiki/Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Saxon">Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Celtic_polytheism" class="mw-redirect" title="Celtic polytheism">pagans</a>, and buried in the town.<sup id="cite_ref-OXDIC_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OXDIC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> No trace remains of her holy well Ffynnon Dydfil, which is thought to have been near the southern end of Well Street in Merthyr Tydfil.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The daughter church of St Tydfil's Well is in the area of Merthyr Tydfil known as 'The Quar' (quarry). 
</p><p>Llysworney Church in the Vale of Glamorgan is dedicated to this female saint, as was a chapel in Llantwit Major until it was given to the Abbey of Tewkesbury.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
