					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">6th-century cleric and bishop of Tours</div>
<p><b>Eufronius</b> or <b>Euphronius</b> was the eighth <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tours">Bishop of Tours</a>; he served from 555 to 573, and was a near relative of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>.
</p><p>When upon the death of Bishop Gunthar, <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_I" title="Chlothar I">King Chlothar's</a> nominee declined appointment to the See, it remained vacant for ten months until the people and clergy elected Eufronius. He was a priest at Tours, from a family of senatorial rank.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> a grandson of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Langres" title="Gregory of Langres">Gregory of Langres</a>, and a friend of <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>. When Clothar learned of the election, he confirmed their choice.<sup id="cite_ref-Bennett_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bennett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 552, the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey_(Poitiers)" title="Holy Cross Abbey (Poitiers)"><i>Abbaye de Sainte-Marie</i></a> was founded near Poitiers by <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> Queen, <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Radegund</a>. It was the first monastery for women in the <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Frankish Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Radegund subsequently retired to the monastery, where she helped to care for the infirm. Upon her request, <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Byzantine Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Justin_II" title="Justin II">Justin II</a> sent the abbey a relic of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a>. When Bishop <a href="/wiki/Maroveus_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Maroveus of Poitiers">Maroveus of Poitiers</a> refused to preside over its installation in the abbey, at Radegund's request, king <a href="/wiki/Sigebert" title="Sigebert">Sigebert</a> sent Eufronius to Poitiers to perform the ceremony.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Sigebert also returned church lands that had been seized by <a href="/wiki/Charibert_I" title="Charibert I">King Charibert</a>.
</p><p>In 561 much of Tours burned down during the warfare that raged at that time. Eufronius rebuilt two of the churches at his own expense. According to Gregory, Eufronius predicted the death of Charibert. 
</p><p>Eufronius took part in the Council of Paris in 557, and presided over the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Tours#Council_of_Tours_567" title="Council of Tours">Council of Tours</a> in 567. The bishops of Brittany declined to attend, as Eufronius claimed authority over the Breton church.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The council dealt mostly with church discipline. The bishops noted that some Gallo-Roman customs of ancestor worship were still being observed. Canon XXII decreed that anyone known to be participating in these practices was barred from receiving communion and not allowed to enter a church.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The bishops of the Kingdom of Paris were particularly concerned about the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> practice of seizing ecclesiastical properties in outlying areas in order to fund their internecine wars.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eufronius was Bishop of Tours for eighteen years, and died at the age of seventy. He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory of Tours</a>. Eufronius was either a cousin of Gregory's mother's or her brother.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  He is mentioned in the Roman Martyrology on August 4.<sup id="cite_ref-Bennett_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bennett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
