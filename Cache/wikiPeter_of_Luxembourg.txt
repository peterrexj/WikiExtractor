<p><b>Pierre de Luxembourg</b> (20 July 1369 – 2 July 1387) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Metz">Bishop of Metz</a>, and as a cardinal of the Avignon Obedience from 1384 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pierre was descended from nobles who secured his entrance into the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> when he started to serve in several places as a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> before he was named as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Metz">Bishop of Metz</a> and a pseudocardinal under an <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was noted for his austerities and successes in diocesan reform as well as for his dedication to the faithful but he tried to end the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> that pitted pope against antipope and rulers against rulers.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His efforts were in vain and he was soon driven from Metz but moved to southern <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> where he died from <a href="/wiki/Anorexia" class="mw-redirect" title="Anorexia">anorexia</a> as a result of his harsh self-imposed penances.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>But both sides in the conflict recognized his deep holiness and his dedication to the people in Metz and elsewhere.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There were continual lobbies for him to be beatified and this later materialized when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a> beatified him on 9 April 1527 in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre_de_Luxembourg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg/180px-Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="255" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg/270px-Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg/360px-Nicolas_Mignard_002.jpg 2x" data-file-width="423" data-file-height="600" /></a><figcaption>Sforza places Avignon under the protection of Pierre during the plague outbreak.</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Pierre de Luxembourg</b> was born in mid-1369 in <a href="/wiki/Meuse_(department)" title="Meuse (department)">Meuse</a> as the second of six children to <a href="/wiki/Guy_of_Luxembourg,_Count_of_Ligny" class="mw-redirect" title="Guy of Luxembourg, Count of Ligny">Guido de Luxembourg</a> (1340-1371) and Mahaut de Châtillon (1335-1378); the couple married circa 1354. His name originates from the fact that was a 5th generation descendant of <a href="/wiki/Henry_V,_Count_of_Luxembourg" title="Henry V, Count of Luxembourg">Henry V, Count of Luxembourg</a>, and thus belonged to the French branch of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Luxembourg" title="House of Luxembourg">House of Luxembourg</a>. His parents died in his childhood (father when he was two and mother when he was four) which prompted his aunt Jeanne - the countess of Orgières - to raise him in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His siblings were:
</p>
<ul><li>Valeran (1355-12 April 1415)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/John_of_Luxembourg,_Lord_of_Beauvoir" class="mw-redirect" title="John of Luxembourg, Lord of Beauvoir">Jean</a> (c. 1370–1397)</li>
<li>André (1374-1396; later <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cambrai">Bishop of Cambrai</a>)</li>
<li>Marie (d. 1391)</li>
<li>Jeanne</li></ul>
<p>Pierre was the uncle to <a href="/wiki/Lewis_of_Luxembourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Lewis of Luxembourg">Louis de Luxembourg</a> and the quasi-cardinal Thibault de Luxembourg; he was the great-granduncle of <a href="/wiki/Philippe_de_Luxembourg" title="Philippe de Luxembourg">Philippe de Luxembourg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1381 he travelled to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> to offer himself as a hostage to the English to secure his brother's release back to France. The English were so perplexed but enthralled with this offer that his brother was released back to France. This even reached the ears of <a href="/wiki/Richard_II_of_England" title="Richard II of England">Richard II</a> who invited him to remain at his court though he decided to go back to Paris to follow <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> in his vocation to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1377 he was sent for his education to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">Parisian college</a> where an instructor was the theologian and <a href="/wiki/Astrologer" class="mw-redirect" title="Astrologer">astrologer</a> <a href="/wiki/Pierre_d%27Ailly" title="Pierre d&#39;Ailly">Pierre d'Ailly</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1379 he was selected to be the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> for the cathedral chapter of <a href="/wiki/Notre_Dame_de_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Notre Dame de Paris">Notre Dame de Paris</a>. In 1381 he became a canon for the cathedral chapter of <a href="/wiki/Chartres_Cathedral" title="Chartres Cathedral">Notre Dame de Chartres</a> and was elevated to the position of <a href="/wiki/Dreux" title="Dreux">Archdeacon of Dreux</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Chartres" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Chartres">Chartres diocese</a>. In 1382 he was selected to be the Archdeacon for Cambrai.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1384 the episcopal see of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Metz" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Metz">Metz</a> became vacant. The selection of a new bishop was complicated due to the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> in which the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">Kingdom of France</a> supported <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_VII" title="Antipope Clement VII">Antipope Clement VII</a> while the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> supported <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> named Pierre as the new Bishop of Metz in 1384 and he was enthroned in his new see that September entering barefoot on a <a href="/wiki/Donkey" title="Donkey">mule</a>. He began his diocesan reforms in which he divided revenue into three: the first two were for the Church and the poor while the third would be for his household.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was able to take Metz with armed troops for a brief period of time but was later forced to withdraw sometime in 1385. This was about the same time that Pope Urban VI selected Tilman Vuss de Bettenburg as the legitimate Bishop of Metz.
</p><p>Pierre was later made a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">pseudocardinal</a> after <a href="/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France" title="Charles VI of France">King Charles VI</a> and <a href="/wiki/John,_Duke_of_Berry" title="John, Duke of Berry">Duke John</a> requested that the antipope elevate him as such. This occurred on 15 April 1384 and he received the diaconal title of <a href="/wiki/San_Giorgio_in_Velabro" title="San Giorgio in Velabro">San Giorgio in Velabro</a>. During his time as a pseudocardinal he made attempts to end the Western Schism which were all unsuccessful.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The antipope invited Pierre on 23 September 1386 to join him at his court in <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> where he would remain until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pierre died in mid-1387 from <a href="/wiki/Anorexia" class="mw-redirect" title="Anorexia">anorexia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fever" title="Fever">fever</a> due to the austerities he had imposed upon himself; he had fallen ill in March. He died at a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> in <a href="/wiki/Villeneuve-l%C3%A8s-Avignon" title="Villeneuve-lès-Avignon">Villeneuve-lès-Avignon</a> in Avignon.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His wish was to be buried in a common grave like that of the <a href="/wiki/Paupers" class="mw-redirect" title="Paupers">paupers</a>. In 1387, the mystic <a href="/wiki/Marie_Robine" title="Marie Robine">Marie Robine</a> was reportedly healed at his tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-Warner24_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Warner24-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On 16 March 1395, his brother Jean ordered the construction of a church dedicated to the sainted <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_V" title="Pope Celestine V">Pope Celestine V</a> to which his remains were transferred.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre_de_Luxembourg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The subject for his canonization was raised at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Basel" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Basel">Council of Basel</a> but without a solid conclusion. In 1432 he was named as the patron saint for Avignon. The vice-legate Sforza placed the town under his protection during a 1640 plague outbreak. His cult following included Metz and Paris in addition to Verdun and Luxembourg. In 1597 his relics were taken to Paris but were damaged during the French Revolution; some relics remain in Saint Didier in Avignon. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> (in 1629) allowed the Carthusians to celebrate a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a> in his name.
</p><p>His beatification had been requested on numerous occasions and <a href="/wiki/Marie_of_Blois,_Duchess_of_Anjou" title="Marie of Blois, Duchess of Anjou">Queen Maria of Naples</a> made one such request on 1 February 1388 as did several other nobles and princes. The process had opened on numerous occasions but faced frequent interruptions (1389 and 1390 and later 1433 and 1435) causing its frequent suspension. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a> beatified Pierre on 9 April 1527 (some sources suggest 24 March).<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
