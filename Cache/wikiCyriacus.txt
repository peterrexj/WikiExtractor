<p><b>Cyriacus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἅγιος Κυριακός</span>, <a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl.</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 303 AD), sometimes <a href="/wiki/Anglicization" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglicization">Anglicized</a> as <b>Cyriac</b>, according to Christian tradition, is a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian martyr</a> who was killed in the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>. He is one of twenty-seven saints, most of them martyrs, who bear this name,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> of whom only seven are honoured by a specific mention of their names in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyriacus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Of the Saint Cyriacus who, together with <b>Saints Largus and Smaragdus</b> and others (of whom Crescentianus, Memmia and Juliana are mentioned in the Roman Martyrology), is venerated on 8 August, all that is known with certainty, apart from their names and the fact of their martyrdom, is that they were buried at the seventh milestone of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ostiensis" title="Via Ostiensis">Via Ostiensis</a> on that date.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, legend has it that Cyriacus was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Nobleman" class="mw-redirect" title="Nobleman">nobleman</a> who converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> as an adult and, renouncing his material wealth, gave it away to the poor. He spent the rest of his life ministering to the slaves who worked in the <a href="/wiki/Baths_of_Diocletian" title="Baths of Diocletian">Baths of Diocletian</a>. Under the reign of <a href="/wiki/Western_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Roman Emperor">Western Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>, co-emperor with <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, Cyriacus was tortured and put to death, <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> in 303 on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Salaria" title="Via Salaria">Via Salaria</a>, where he was subsequently buried. With him were martyred his companions Largus and Smaragdus, and twenty others, including Crescentianus, Sergius, Secundus, Alban, Victorianus, Faustinus, Felix, Sylvanus, and four women: Memmia, Juliana, Cyriacides, and Donata.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Cyriacus is credited with <a href="/wiki/Exorcism" title="Exorcism">exorcizing</a> demons from two girls. The first was Artemisia (or Artemia), the daughter of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, which resulted in both Artemisia and her mother Saint Serena converting to Christianity.  The second was Jobias, the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Shapur_I" title="Shapur I">Shapur I</a> of <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a> (reigned 241–272), which led to the conversion of the King's entire household. However, this is impossible, because Shapur died thirty years before the reputed exorcism and because he had only one daughter named Adur-Anahid.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
It is possible that the girl reportedly exorcised was a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Narseh" title="Narseh">Narseh</a> (293–303) or of <a href="/wiki/Hormizd_II" title="Hormizd II">Hormizd II</a> (303–309). It is more likely that many Bishops throughout the world (to this day) have been named in honor of Saint Cyriacus at the Baths (of Diocletian), including maybe the first so honored, Judas the Jew from Jerusalem, who converted and upon being named Bishop of Ancona took the name Cyriacus as his own in the latter part of the 4th century.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyriacus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> included the feast day of Cyriacus, Largus and Smaragdus on 8 August as a Semidouble. In 1955 this rank was lowered to that of Simple.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The 1962 Calendar, issued together with <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a>, the licit private and, under certain conditions, public use of which was authorized by the 2007 motu proprio <i><a href="/wiki/Summorum_Pontificum" title="Summorum Pontificum">Summorum Pontificum</a></i>, reduced their celebration to a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">Commemoration</a>. They are not included in the 1970 reformed Calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> liturgy, which was issued under the authority of Pope Paul VI, but, being included, all three, together with Memmia, Juliana and Smaragdus, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> they are recognized saints of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg/200px-Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="201" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg/300px-Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg/400px-Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2440" data-file-height="2448" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Pfarrkirche_Weitnau_Nothelferfenster_Pantaleon_Cyriacus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Stained-glass image of St Cyriacus (right). <a href="/wiki/Saint_Pantaleon" title="Saint Pantaleon">St Pantaleon</a> is on the left.  <a href="/wiki/Weitnau" title="Weitnau">Weitnau</a></div></div></div>
<p>Saint Cyriacus is venerated as one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>.
</p><p>It is claimed his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were moved to <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Via_Lata" title="Santa Maria in Via Lata">Santa Maria in Via Lata</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and the abbey of St Cyriaque in <a href="/wiki/Altorf" title="Altorf">Altorf</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>.
</p><p>The Church of "Saint Cyriacus in the Baths of Diocletian" (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">"Sanctus Ciriacus in Thermis Diocletiani"</i>), was dedicated to this martyr, a former <i><a href="/wiki/Titular_church" title="Titular church">titulus</a></i> church. The "tituli" were commonly named after their patron, often a lay patron in the early centuries: <i>"Cyriac"</i> in Greek signifies simply <i>"patron."</i> This <i>"titulus,"</i> to which a cardinal was assigned, whatever its claimed second- or third-century origins, existed certainly in the fifth century, when Marcianus was cardinal priest of the title of S. Ciriaco alle Terme di Diocleziano in 494, at the time of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Pope Gelasius I</a>. The titulus was suppressed in 1477 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a> in favor of <a href="/wiki/Quiricus_and_Julietta" class="mw-redirect" title="Quiricus and Julietta">Saints Ciro and Giulitta</a>. In 1493, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Pope Alexander VI</a> restored the name of S. Ciriaco. The title was definitively suppressed in 1587 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V" title="Pope Sixtus V">Pope Sixtus V</a>, who assigned a titulus of <a href="/wiki/Quiricus_and_Julietta" class="mw-redirect" title="Quiricus and Julietta">Sts Quirico e Giulitta</a> to <a href="/wiki/Ferdinando_I_de%27_Medici,_Grand_Duke_of_Tuscany" title="Ferdinando I de&#39; Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany">Cardinal Ferdinando de' Medici</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
St Cyriacus is the patron saint of the Italian city of <a href="/wiki/Ciri%C3%A9" title="Cirié">Cirié</a>.
</p><p>There were monasteries dedicated to St Cyriacus in the now destroyed <a href="/wiki/Arab" class="mw-redirect" title="Arab">Arab</a> village of <a href="/wiki/Majdal_Yaba" title="Majdal Yaba">Majdal Yaba</a> in <a href="/wiki/Israel" title="Israel">Israel</a> and the existing village of <a href="/wiki/Al-Fasayil" class="mw-redirect" title="Al-Fasayil">Al-Fasayil</a> near <a href="/wiki/Jericho" title="Jericho">Jericho</a>. The residents of both these villages venerated him during the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine">Byzantine</a> era.
</p><p>On St Cyriacus' feast day, 8 August 1899, a <a href="/wiki/Saffir-Simpson_Hurricane_Scale" class="mw-redirect" title="Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Scale">category four</a> <a href="/wiki/Hurricane" class="mw-redirect" title="Hurricane">hurricane</a> made landfall on the island of <a href="/wiki/Puerto_Rico" title="Puerto Rico">Puerto Rico</a> and was named after him. It was known as the <a href="/wiki/1899_Hurricane_San_Ciriaco" class="mw-redirect" title="1899 Hurricane San Ciriaco">Hurricane San Ciriaco</a>.
</p>
