<p><b>Marcellinus of Carthage</b> was a Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> who died in 413. He was <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_state" title="Secretary of state">secretary of state</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire" title="Western Roman Empire">Western Roman Empire</a> under <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Honorius_(emperor)" title="Honorius (emperor)">Honorius</a> and a close friend of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, as well as a correspondent of <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Saint Jerome</a>. Saint Augustine dedicated the first books of his landmark <i><a href="/wiki/City_of_God_(book)" class="mw-redirect" title="City of God (book)">The City of God</a></i> to Marcellinus in 413.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_of_Carthage&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Flavius Marcellinus was born in <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, and had a brother, Apringius. He came to hold the rank of <i>tribunus et notarius</i> under Emperor Honorius.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was described as "a cultured, generous aristocrat, interested in theology".<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His interest in religious questions, brought about close and friendly relations between him and St. Augustine, who wrote him several letters, and dedicated various books to him ("<i>De peccatorum meritis et remissione</i>", "<i>De baptismo parvulorum</i>", and the first three books of "<i><a href="/wiki/The_City_of_God" title="The City of God">De Civitate Dei</a></i>").<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Jerome">Saint Jerome</a> also wrote him.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was married to <a href="/wiki/Anapsychia" title="Anapsychia">Anapsychia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Donatist_controversy">Donatist controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_of_Carthage&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Donatist controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Africa, the dispute between the <a href="/wiki/Donatism" title="Donatism">Donatists</a> and orthodox Christians was not only doctrinal but also involved regional and social tensions: <a href="/wiki/Numidia" title="Numidia">Numidia</a> against proconsular Africa, and proletarians against Roman landowners.<sup id="cite_ref-adotta_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adotta-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 405 an imperial decree declared the Donatists heretics and prescribed confiscation of their property. The fall of <a href="/wiki/Stilicho" title="Stilicho">Stilicho</a> in 408, led them to hope for a change in imperial policy; however, in 410 Marcellinus was given instructions to "abolish the new superstition".<sup id="cite_ref-Noonan_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Noonan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The orthodox bishops hoped the Donatists would be more susceptible to reasoned debate and petitioned the emperor to call a conference.
</p><p>Marcellinus was sent to Africa in 411, charged with presiding over the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Carthage_(411)" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Carthage (411)">Council of Carthage</a> in early June of that year. In his summons to the parties, Marcellinus made no secret of the emperor's position. Bishops representing each side of the controversy debated the issue. Marcellinus, as the emperor's representative, ruled that the Donatists were heretics and that they had to give up their churches and return to churches under the control of orthodox bishops and priests. According to John T. Noonan, the Donatists alleged that Marcellinus had been bribed as that was a stronger argument than that he was merely enforcing imperial policy.<sup id="cite_ref-Noonan_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Noonan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The judgment was enforced by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a> with great severity.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> So bloody was the persecution of the Donatists that Augustine, who had been one of the leaders in condemning Donatism as a heresy, protested at their treatment.
</p><p>Subsequently, some of the Donatists engaged in sporadic violence against orthodox priests. Marcellinus presided at the trials of those arrested. Augustine appealed for clemency both to Marcellinus and Marcellinus's brother Apringius, who was proconsul for Africa. Noonan views the fact that Augustine and Marcellinus remained close friends as suggesting that the bishop's petition was granted.<sup id="cite_ref-Noonan_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Noonan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_of_Carthage&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 413, the Donatists accused Marcellinus and Apringius of involvement in the rebellion of <a href="/wiki/Heraclianus" title="Heraclianus">Heraclianus</a>. General Marinus, who had quashed the rebellion and who had Donatist sympathies, arrested the brothers and put them in <a href="/wiki/Prison" title="Prison">prison</a>. Even with Saint Augustine intervening on their behalf with Caecilian, the presiding judge of their case, the two brothers were executed,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> on September 13. The following year, Marcellinus was exonerated by Emperor Honorius.<sup id="cite_ref-adotta_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adotta-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_of_Carthage&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Marcellinus was subsequently listed in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Marcellinus has a statue adorning the spire atop the south <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">transept</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Milan_Cathedral" title="Milan Cathedral">Duomo di Milano</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-adotta_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-adotta-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and a statue on the southern half of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square#Colonnades" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">Bernini's colonnade</a> in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
