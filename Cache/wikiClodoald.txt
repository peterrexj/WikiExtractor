<p><b>Clodoald</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">C(h)lodoaldus, Cloudus</i>; reconstructed <a href="/wiki/Old_Frankish" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Frankish">Frankish</a>: <i>*Hlōdōwald</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 522 – <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;560 AD</span>), better known as <b>Saint Cloud</b> (<small>French:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[klu]</a></span>), was a <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> prince, grandson of Clovis I and son of <a href="/wiki/Chlodomer" title="Chlodomer">Chlodomer</a>, who preferred to renounce royalty and became a hermit and monk. Clodoald found a hill along the Seine, two leagues below Paris, in a place called Novigentum (the present commune of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Cloud" title="Saint-Cloud">Saint-Cloud</a>). Here, among the fishermen and farmers, he led a life of solitude and prayer, and built a church, which he dedicated in honor of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a>.
</p><p>He is venerated as a saint in both the Catholic and Orthodox Churches.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clodoald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon the death of <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis</a>, his sons, Chlodomer, Childebert, Clothaire, and their half-brother Thierry shared the kingdom. In 523–524, at Clotilde's instigation, her sons joined in an expedition against King <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_of_Burgundy" title="Sigismund of Burgundy">Sigismund</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Burgundians" title="Kingdom of the Burgundians">Burgundians</a>. After the arrest of Sigismund and his family, Chlodomer returned to <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>. Sigismund's brother, Godomar III, supported by his ally and relative, the Ostrogoth king <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great" title="Theodoric the Great">Theodoric the Great</a>, slaughtered the garrison the Franks had left in Burgundy. In retaliation, Chlodomer then had Sigismund and his sons, Gisald and Gondebaud, murdered. On May 1, 524, Chlodomer set out on a second expedition against the Burgundians and was killed at the Battle of Vézeronce on June 25 of the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clodoald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clodoald was the son of <a href="/wiki/Chlodomer" title="Chlodomer">King Chlodomer of Orléans</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Guntheuc" title="Guntheuc">Guntheuc</a>. He was one of three brothers, raised in Paris by their grandmother, the <a href="/wiki/Clotilde" title="Clotilde">Queen dowager Clotilde</a>. Clodoald's brothers, Theodoald and Gunther, were killed by Clotaire when they were ten and nine respectively, but Clodoald survived by escaping to <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Salic law required the division of the kingdom among the sons of Chlodomer. However, the boys' uncles, Childebert I, king of Paris, and his brother Clotaire I, king of Soissons, coveted the kingdom of Orléans and determined to murder the nephews.
</p><p>By one account, in 525, Childebert and Clotaire asked their mother Clotilde to send them the children so that they might be proclaimed their father's successors. She clothed the brothers in their best clothes and sent them with confidence, unaware of her sons' plans. The two uncles then had the children of Clodomir killed.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Some claim that they killed the two older boys, Thibault and Gonthaire, aged ten and seven, with their own hands, to the great despair of Saint Clotilde, who saw her grandchildren killed by her own sons. Only the youngest, Clodoald, was saved by the dedication of a few of the faithful. He found sanctuary with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Remigius" title="Saint Remigius">Remigius</a>, the Bishop of Rheims, and thus escaped his uncles' searches.
</p><p>Another account states that Childebert and Clotaire considered cutting children's hair, because long hair was a sign of nobility in Frankish culture. But as the hair would inevitably grow back, they asked Clotilde what they had to do. She replied that she would rather see them dead than sheared. They first killed Gonthaire, before Thibault threw himself at their feet to beg them to leave him alive. So Childebert hesitated, and his brother reminded him that it was his idea. Thus ended the short life of Clodomir's descendants, at least two of the three since Clodoald had been able to escape.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Adulthood">Adulthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clodoald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Adulthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clodoald renounced all claims to the throne and lived as a studious hermit and disciple of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Severin_of_Paris&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Severin of Paris (page does not exist)">Séverin of Paris</a>, who led a solitary and contemplative life in a hermitage at the gates of Paris (on the site of the present Saint-Séverin Church in the 5th arrondissement). The young prince became his disciple and received from his hands the religious habit. Clodoald preferred a humble and quiet life of solitude, to a bright, but perilous life in a royal palace.<sup id="cite_ref-dsc_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dsc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> For some time he remained in his company, to be trained in all the monastic virtues. At the age of twenty, Clodoald left his hermitage and appeared before the Bishop of Paris surrounded by religious and civic leaders and members of the royal family. The bishop cut Cloud's long hair, which was a symbol of his royalty. Childebert and Clotaire, who saw him as no threat, left him undisturbed and even gave him some inheritances to live more comfortably in the place of his retirement.
</p><p>After Séverin's death, Clodoald left the surroundings of Paris and secretly retired to Provence. The inhabitants of the surrounding area came to him because they learned that Cloud had the gift of healing.<sup id="cite_ref-dsc_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dsc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Clodoald remained there eleven years, and then went back to his first hermitage, where the people greeted his return with joy.
</p><p>At the people's request, he was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a priest by Bishop Eusebius of Paris in 551 and served the Church for some time.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His humility and his charity were praised. Clodoald could not endure these honours for long, and to avoid them, retired to a hill along the Seine, two leagues below Paris, in a place called Novigentum (the present commune of Saint-Cloud). Here among the fishermen and farmers, he led a life of solitude and prayer, and built a church, which he dedicated in honor of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a>.
</p><p>As soon as the place of his retreat was known, disciples came to place themselves under his direction. Some cells were first built, soon a monastery became necessary. According to tradition, Clodoald had a monastery with a chapel built and endowed with the goods that the kings, his uncles, gave him. He lived seven years in his monastery, among his brothers, giving them an example of all the virtues. He died there on September 7, 560, at the age of thirty-eight.<sup id="cite_ref-dsc_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dsc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clodoald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Clodoald predicted his death in advance, which was followed by several miracles, which occurred near his tomb. Clodoald was then canonized and the hamlet quickly transformed into a place of pilgrimage, where huge crowds flocked. Novigentum then changed its name to "Sanctus Clodoaldus" (Saint-Cloud) in his honour. The abbey is now a collegiate church of <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canons regular</a> called <a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%89glise_Saint-Clodoald&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Église Saint-Clodoald (page does not exist)">Église Saint-Clodoald</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89glise_Saint-Clodoald_de_Saint-Cloud" class="extiw" title="fr:Église Saint-Clodoald de Saint-Cloud">fr</a>&#93;</span> wherein his relics are kept.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/St._Cloud,_Wisconsin" title="St. Cloud, Wisconsin">St. Cloud, Wisconsin</a>, St. Cloud, Florida, and <a href="/wiki/St._Cloud,_Minnesota" title="St. Cloud, Minnesota">St. Cloud, Minnesota</a>, are in turn named after the French town.
</p><p>Clodoald's feast day is September 7.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Clodoald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>In his <i>History of the Franks</i>, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> recounts the struggles for power between the children of Clovis, and after having mentioned the murder of his two brothers, dedicates a few lines to Clodoald: "They could not take the third, Clodoald, who was saved by the help of brave warriors; despising an earthly kingdom, he consecrated himself to God, and, having cut his hair with his own hand, he was made a cleric. He persisted in good works, and died a priest."</li>
<li>A painting by Sébastien-Melchior Cornu (1804–1870) depicting Saint-Clodoald decorates the chapel of the Elysée Palace.</li></ul>
