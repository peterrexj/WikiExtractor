<p><b>Saint Rumbold</b> (or <i>Rumold</i>, <i>Romuold</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Rumoldus</i>, <a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>: <i lang="nl">Rombout</i>, <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Rombaut</i>) was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> or <a href="/wiki/Scottish_people" title="Scottish people">Scottish</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Christianism">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a>, although his true nationality is not known for certain.<sup id="cite_ref-tradition_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tradition-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2004date1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2004date1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2004date2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2004date2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
He was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> near <a href="/wiki/Mechelen" title="Mechelen">Mechelen</a> by two men, whom he had denounced for their evil ways.<sup id="cite_ref-CathOL_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathOL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Rumbold's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Western_Rite_Orthodoxy" title="Western Rite Orthodoxy">Western Rite Orthodox</a> Churches, on 24 June;<sup id="cite_ref-CathOL_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathOL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-OrthEU_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OrthEU-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
and it is celebrated in Ireland on 3 July.<sup id="cite_ref-DvdA-Romb_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DvdA-Romb-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-spdc_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spdc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Mechelen,<sup id="cite_ref-CathOL_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathOL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> where <a href="/wiki/St._Rumbold%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Rumbold&#39;s Cathedral">St. Rumbold's Cathedral</a> possesses an elaborate golden shrine on its high altar, containing <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> attributed to the saint. It is rumoured that his remains are buried inside the cathedral. Twenty-five paintings in the choir illustrate his life.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_legend">Life and legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rumbold_of_Mechlin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rumbold is assumed to have been consecrated a regionary <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. <a href="/wiki/Aodh_Buidhe_Mac_an_Bhaird" title="Aodh Buidhe Mac an Bhaird">Aodh Buidhe Mac an Bhaird</a> (c. 1590–1635) argued that Rumbold had been born in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. He is also said to have been a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Dublin" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Dublin">Bishop of Dublin</a>, the son of a <a href="/wiki/List_of_Scottish_monarchs" title="List of Scottish monarchs">Scottish king</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-CathOL_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathOL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and the brother of <a href="/wiki/Himelin" title="Himelin">Saint Himelin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He is assumed to have worked under <a href="/wiki/Saint_Willibrord" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Willibrord">St. Willibrord</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a> and <a href="/wiki/Flemish_Brabant" title="Flemish Brabant">Brabant</a>, and also to have been a close companion of the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> <a href="/wiki/Gummarus" title="Gummarus">St. Gummarus</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-tradition_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tradition-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CathOL_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathOL-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and of the preacher monk <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fredegand_van_Deurne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fredegand van Deurne (page does not exist)">Fredegand van Deurne</a>, who, according to one tradition, maintained contact with <a href="/wiki/St._Foillan" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Foillan">St. Foillan</a> (who was murdered in the <a href="/wiki/Sonian_Forest" title="Sonian Forest">Sonian Forest</a> around 665).<sup id="cite_ref-DvdA-Fred_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DvdA-Fred-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Rumbold's biography, written around 1100 AD by Theodoricus, prior of <a href="/wiki/Sint-Truiden" title="Sint-Truiden">Sint-Truiden</a> Abbey, caused 775 to be the traditional year of the saint's death. The surrounding areas of Mechelen however, had been Christianized much earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-tradition_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tradition-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
In 2004 a state-of-the-art examination of the relics assumed to be St. Rumbold's showed a death date between 580 and 655.<sup id="cite_ref-2004date1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2004date1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2004date2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2004date2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
This would make Saint Rumbold a <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Scottish_mission" title="Hiberno-Scottish mission">Hiberno-Scottish</a> rather than an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_mission" title="Anglo-Saxon mission">Anglo-Saxon missionary</a>, and not a contemporary of either St. Willibrord, St. Himelin, or St. Gummarus.<sup id="cite_ref-2004date1_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2004date1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Rumwold_of_Buckingham">St. Rumwold of Buckingham</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rumbold_of_Mechlin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: St. Rumwold of Buckingham">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There has been some historical confusion between Rumbold of Mechelen and the infant Saint <a href="/wiki/Rumwold_of_Buckingham" title="Rumwold of Buckingham">Rumwold of Buckingham</a>, who died in 662 AD at the age of 3&#160;days. The latter has become referred to as Romwold, Rumwald, Runwald, Rumbald, or Rumbold. A compilation of three saints' lives as translated by Rosalind Love mentioned that on 15th-century records in <a href="/wiki/Salisbury" title="Salisbury">Salisbury</a>, an unknown author 'corrected' the attribution as "martyr" (possibly the Rumbold murdered in Mechelen) by annotating "confessor" (fitting in the miraculous infant Rumwold who was not a martyr). Also, the original dedication of churches to a St. Rumbold in Northern England appears uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-TECALSL-excerpts_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TECALSL-excerpts-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
