<p>The <b>Diocese of Como</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Dioecesis Comensis</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a> ecclesiastical jurisdiction or diocese of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. It was established in the Fourth Century. It is a <a href="/wiki/Suffragan_diocese" title="Suffragan diocese">suffragan diocese</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">ecclesiastical province</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Metropolis_(religious_jurisdiction)" title="Metropolis (religious jurisdiction)">metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Milan">Archdiocese of Milan</a>. The Bishop of Como's <a href="/wiki/Cathedra" title="Cathedra">cathedra</a> is in the <a href="/wiki/Como_Cathedral" title="Como Cathedral">Como Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Local legend credits the conversion of <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a> to the apostolate of <a href="/wiki/Hermagoras_of_Aquileia" title="Hermagoras of Aquileia">Hermagoras of Aquileia</a> (died c. 70).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The diocese of Como was originally suffragan of Milan, as the consecration of its first bishop by Ambrose of Milan demonstrates.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> By the mid 6th century the diocese was subject to Aquileia.<sup id="cite_ref-Orsini,_p._4_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orsini,_p._4-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_V" title="Pope Stephen V">Pope Stephen V</a> (885-891) twice ordered Patriarch Walpert of Aquileia to consecrate Liutard, the Bishop-elect of Como.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Until 1751 Como was, indeed, a <a href="/wiki/Suffragan_of_the_patriarchate_of_Aquileia" class="mw-redirect" title="Suffragan of the patriarchate of Aquileia">suffragan of the patriarchate of Aquileia</a> and followed the <a href="/wiki/Aquileian_Rite" title="Aquileian Rite">Aquileian Rite</a>; the Patriarchate was suppressed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a>, who, on 18 April 1752, created the metropolitanate of Gorizia, and made Como subject to Goriza.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1789 <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a> was placed under the jurisdiction of the Archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>From the 6th century to the 15th, the rite of Aquileia, commonly called the patriarchal rite, was used in the Church of Como.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It was only in 1598 that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> substituted the Roman rite.<sup id="cite_ref-Orsini,_p._4_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orsini,_p._4-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the tenth century the Bishops of Como were also its temporal lords. From the election of Bishop Raimundus in 1061, the episcopal elections were carried out by the Canons of the Cathedral Chapter and the Abbots of S. Carpoforo, S. Abondio, and S. Giuliano.<sup id="cite_ref-Orsini,_p._4_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orsini,_p._4-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Como lost a relevant part of its territory to the newly formed <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Lugano" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Lugano">Diocese of Lugano</a> in 1884.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cathedral_and_Chapter">Cathedral and Chapter</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Como&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cathedral and Chapter">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Como_Cathedral" title="Como Cathedral">The Cathedral of S. Maria Assunta</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> was begun in 1396, and was completed only in 1595; later the <a href="/wiki/Cupola" title="Cupola">cupola</a> and some small chapels were added (1730–44). The Cathedral was granted the honorary title and privileges of a minor basilica by Pope Pius XII, at the request of Bishop Felix Bonomini, in a decree of 18 January 1951.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1695, the Chapter of the cathedral was composed of three dignities (the Archdeacon, the Archpriest, and the Provost) and twenty Canons.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1764, there were three dignities and seventeen Canons.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 1846 there were twenty members of the Chapter, headed by the Archpriest, the Theologos, and the Penitentiary.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The Chapter of the Cathedral currently (2018) consists of ten Canons, headed by the Archpriest.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the city of Como there was also the Collegiate Church of S. Fedele, which was presided over by a Provost and seven Canons.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>–===Synods===
A diocesan synod was an irregular but important meeting of the bishop of a diocese and his clergy. Its purpose was (1) to proclaim generally the various decrees already issued by the bishop; (2) to discuss and ratify measures on which the bishop chose to consult with his clergy; (3) to publish statutes and decrees of the diocesan synod, of the provincial synod, and of the Holy See.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bishop Gianantonio Volpi (1559–1588) held a diocesan synod on 16 March 1565. This was the first synod since the closing of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>, which had mandated regular and frequent diocesan synods. He held a second synod on 3–5 September 1579.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> A diocesan synod was held by Bishop Filippo Archinti (1595–1621) in 1598; and another synod on 16–18 May 1618.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Fifth Diocesan Synod was held by Bishop Lazzaro Carafino (1626–1665) on 18–20 September 1633.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The Sixth Diocesan Synod was held by Bishop Ambrogio Torriano (1680–1694) on 13–15 September 1672. It issued particularly strong regulations against the use of snuff by the clergy, following the decree of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_X" title="Pope Innocent X">Pope Innocent X</a>, and prescribed a fine of two aurei for any priest who used snuff before the Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The Seventh Diocesan Synod was held on 10–12 September 1682 by Cardinal Carlo Cicero, Bishop of Como.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bishop Teodoro de Valfrè held a diocesan synod on 13–15 September 1904 in the Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The Ninth Diocesan Synod was held by Bishop Alessandro Macchi (1930–1947) on 8–10 September 1942.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> The Tenth Diocesan Synod was held by Bishop Felice Bonomini (1948–1974) in 1953. Bishop Oscar Cantoni announced the Eleventh Diocesan Synod, that was to take place in 2020.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> It concluded in May 2022.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Parishes">Parishes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Como&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Parishes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The 338 parishes of the diocese are spread across four provinces of the <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> region:
the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Como" title="Province of Como">Province of Como</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Lecco" title="Province of Lecco">Province of Lecco</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Sondrio" title="Province of Sondrio">Province of Sondrio</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Varese" title="Province of Varese">Province of Varese</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-109" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-109">&#91;109&#93;</a></sup> There is one priest for every 1,156 Catholics (2021).
</p>

<li id="cite_note-56"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-56">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Raimundus was the son of Martinus <a href="/wiki/Della_Torre" title="Della Torre">Della Torre</a> of the noble Lombard family which held the lordship of Milan until expelled by the Visconti. His father happened to be <a href="/wiki/Podest%C3%A0" title="Podestà">Podestà</a> of Como at the time that his son was elected bishop. He was expelled from his seat, but managed to return in July 1264. He was transferred to the diocese of Aquileia on 21 December 1273 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_X" title="Pope Gregory X">Pope Gregory X</a>, who issued a mandate on 18 February 1274 to elect another bishop for Como. Ughelli, pp. 300-301. Eubel, I, pp. 99, 217, with note 3.</span>

<li id="cite_note-57"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-57">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Leo died on 10 July 1325. Eubel, I, p. 217.</span>

<li id="cite_note-58"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-58">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Benedetto, who held the degree of master of theology, was appointed bishop of Como by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXII" title="Pope John XXII">Pope John XXII</a> on 16 December 1328. He fled from the persecution of Franchino and Valeriano Rusca, Lords of Como, and took up residence first in Volturena and then in Cremona. Valeriano had been chosen by the Canons of the Cathedral as bishop, but the election was quashed by Pope John, who would have nothing to do with a supporter of Louis of Bavaria as emperor. The Rusca were declared to be heretics and schismatics, and Bishop Benedetto administered the diocese as best he could through Fra Luca de Castello, O.Min. This situation continued for seven years, climaxing in the placing of the entire territory of Como under the papal interdict. The Bishop, who finally obtained his see in 1335, died in October 1339. Ughelli, pp. 307-308 (who places his appointment as bishop on 1 January 1328). Eubel, I, p. 217.</span>
</li>
<li id="cite_note-59"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-59">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Bishop Benedetto was succeeded by Bishop Beltramino Paravicini on 24 November 1339. The Canons of the Cathedral Chapter were intent on electing the Vicar General, Canon Bartolommeo de Monte Cucco, but pressure applied by Azzone Visconti led the Pope to provide Paravicini instead. Beltramino had been serving as Bishop of Chieti since 1336, and was Papal Legate to King Peter of Aragon. He was transferred to Como on 24 November 1339. He was transferred to the diocese of Bologna on 6 November 1340. He died in Avignon on 7 August 1351 (Tatti, p. 98). Tatti, III, pp. 78-82. Ughelli, p. 308. Eubel, I, pp. 141, 217, 481.</span>

<li id="cite_note-60"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-60">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">Boniface, Doctor of Canon Law (or <i>Doctor in utroque iure</i>, according to Ughelli, p. 308), who had been serving as Bishop of Modena (1336–1340), was transferred to the diocese of Como by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XII" title="Pope Benedict XII">Pope Benedict XII</a> on 6 November 1340. He held a diocesan synod, and restored the episcopal palace. He died in 1351. Tatti, III, pp. 82-99. Eubel, I, p. 217.</span>
