<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other people named Mamercus or Mamertus, see <a href="/wiki/Mamercus_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mamercus (disambiguation)">Mamercus</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Mamertus</b> (died c. 475) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Vienne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Vienne">bishop of Vienne</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, venerated as a saint. His primary contribution to ecclesiastical practice was the introduction of <a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">litanies</a> prior to <a href="/wiki/Ascension_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascension Day">Ascension Day</a> as an intercession against earthquakes and other disasters, leading to "<a href="/wiki/Rogation_Days" class="mw-redirect" title="Rogation Days">Rogation Days</a>." His feast day is the first of the <a href="/wiki/Ice_Saints" title="Ice Saints">Ice Saints</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mamertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Prior to his elevation to the see of Vienne, little has been recorded about Mamertus' life. The fact that his brother, <a href="/wiki/Claudianus_Mamertus" title="Claudianus Mamertus">Claudianus Mamertus</a>, the theological writer, received in his youth a sound training in rhetoric, and enjoyed the personal acquaintance of Bishop <a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Eucherius of Lyons">Eucherius of Lyons</a> (434-50), suggests that the brothers belonged to a wealthy Gallic family from the neighbourhood of Lyons. Like his brother, St Mamertus was distinguished for his secular learning as well as theology, and, before his elevation to the episcopate, appears to have been married. <sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His election and consecration took place shortly before 462. As bishop he enlisted the services of his brother, who had withdrawn to a cloister, and ordained him priest of Vienne. The activity of the brothers is described in one letter of <a href="/wiki/Sidonius_Apollinaris" title="Sidonius Apollinaris">Sidonius Apollinaris</a>, (Sidonius, <i>Epist</i>., IV, xi)  while another is addressed to Bishop Mamertus.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_(ao%C3%BBt_2020).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_%28ao%C3%BBt_2020%29.jpg/220px-Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_%28ao%C3%BBt_2020%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="294" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_%28ao%C3%BBt_2020%29.jpg/330px-Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_%28ao%C3%BBt_2020%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_%28ao%C3%BBt_2020%29.jpg/440px-Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_%28ao%C3%BBt_2020%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3016" data-file-height="4032" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Maurice_de_Vienne_-_Statue_Saint_Mamert_de_Vienne_(ao%C3%BBt_2020).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Saint Mamertus, Vienne Cathedral</div></div></div> 
<p>In 463, Mamertus was engaged in a dispute with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Hilarius" title="Pope Hilarius">Pope Hilarius</a> on the question of the privileges of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Arles">Bishop of Arles</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_I" title="Pope Leo I">Pope Leo I</a> had regulated the boundaries of the ecclesiastical provinces of Arles and Vienne: under the latter he left the Dioceses of <a href="/wiki/Valence,_Dr%C3%B4me" title="Valence, Drôme">Valence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mo%C3%BBtiers" title="Moûtiers">Tarentaise</a>, <a href="/wiki/Geneva" title="Geneva">Geneva</a> and <a href="/wiki/Grenoble" title="Grenoble">Grenoble</a>, but all the other dioceses in this district were made subordinate to Arles. Regardless of this decision and infringing on the rights of his colleague of Arles, Mamertus consecrated in 463 a bishop for the city of <a href="/wiki/Die,_Dr%C3%B4me" title="Die, Drôme">Die</a> (Dea). King <a href="/wiki/Gundioc" class="mw-redirect" title="Gundioc">Gundioc</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundians</a> complained to Pope Hilary of this action, whereupon the latter wrote to Bishop Leontius of Arles on 10 October 463, bidding him summon a synod of bishops from the different provinces to enquire into the matter. In a subsequent letter to the bishops of the Roman provinces of Lyons, Vienne, Narbonnensis I and II and Alpina, he also refers to the matter, and directs them to obey Leontius's summons to a regularly constituted synod.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The synod decided against Mamertus, as we learn from another letter of the pope dated 25 February 464.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In this letter, St Hilary declares that Mamertus and the bishop unlawfully consecrated by him should really be deposed; desiring, however that clemency be used, he commissioned Bishop Veranus to inform Mamertus that, if he did not recognize and submit to the regulations of Pope Leo, he would be deprived also of the four suffragan dioceses, still subject to Vienne. The bishop invalidly installed by Mamertus was to be confirmed in his office by Leontius, after which he might retain the bishopric. Mamertus evidently submitted, since we find no subsequent reference to the incident.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>During his episcopate, the remains of Ferreolus were discovered, and were translated by Mamertus to a church in Vienne, built in honour of that martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Sidonius_Apollinaris" title="Sidonius Apollinaris">Sidonius Apollinaris</a>, (Sidonius, <i>Epist</i>., V, xiv; VII, i) and Mamertus' second successor, <a href="/wiki/Avitus_of_Vienne" title="Avitus of Vienne">Avitus</a>, ("Homilia de Rogat." in P. L., LIX, 289-94) Bishop Mamertus was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Rogation_days" title="Rogation days">Minor Rogations Processions</a>, held on Monday to Wednesday preceding <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Ascension" title="Feast of the Ascension">Ascension Thursday</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Plague_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Plague-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The Minor Rogations were traditionally observed with processional litanies and fasting as a petition for good weather for the crops and deliverance from pestilence and famine.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In connexion with these intercessory processions, Mamertus summoned a synod at Vienne between 471 and 475. About 475 he attended a synod at <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a>, which dealt with the predestination teaching of Lucidus, a Gallic priest. As this is the latest information we possess concerning him, we may assume that he died shortly afterwards.
</p><p>William of Auxerre (d. 1231) says that Mamertus instituted the litanies after "a plague of wolves. For just as is told in the Gospel, that demons, by God's permission and on account of the sins of men, entered pigs, similarly they entered wolves in order to hurt and kill people better, not only in villages but even in cities."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mamertus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his death he was venerated as a saint. Saint Mamertus' name stands in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> and in the <i>Martyrologium</i> of <a href="/wiki/Florus_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Florus of Lyons">Florus of Lyons</a> under 11 May, on which day his liturgical <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is still celebrated (Henri Quentin, <i>Les martyrologes historiques</i>, 348) in the Roman Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
