<p><b>Thérèse Couderc</b> (1 February 1805 &#8211; 26 September 1885) - born <b>Marie-Victoire Couderc</b> - was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the co-founder of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Cenacle" title="Sisters of the Cenacle">Sisters of the Cenacle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Couderc underwent humiliations during her time as a nun for she was forced to resign from positions and was ridiculed and mocked due to false accusations made against her though this softened towards the end of her life. She was a spiritual writer having written on sacrifice and service to <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>. After her death, she left a series of spiritual writings.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> beatified the late religious in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> on 4 November 1951 and in 1970 she was canonized as a saint by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Couderc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Marie-Victoire Couderc was born in 1805 in <a href="/wiki/Le_Mas" title="Le Mas">Le Mas</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> as the fourth of twelve children to farmers Claude Michel Corderc (1780-???) and Anne Méry; her parents married in 1801. One sibling was Jean and two others died in their childhood. The surviving children were eight males and two females that included herself (she was the eldest of the girls). In her childhood she attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> twice a week.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She made her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a> on 15 May 1815.
</p><p>In 1822 her parents sent her to a boarding school at Vans and she remained there until 1825 in <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> when her father wanted her to attend a school in their local area. She entered the novitiate after she had met Father Jean-Pierre Etienne Terme in late March 1825 and confided in him her desire to become a religious.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Couderc underwent her period of the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> in 1825 with the Sisters of Saint Regis, a teaching order in <a href="/wiki/Lalouvesc" title="Lalouvesc">Lalouvesc</a>; she made her perpetual vows on 6 January 1837 with one other. Couderc assumed a religious name when she became a novice.
</p><p>Couderc and two other sisters were sent to manage a mountain hostel for women pilgrims at the shrine of St. <a href="/wiki/John_Francis_Regis" title="John Francis Regis">John Francis Regis</a> in Lalouvesc. It became a successful retreat house under her guidance. Couderc co-founded the Sisters of the Cenacle with Father Terme in 1826 and became its superior in 1828. Desirous to provide women a place for recollection in solitude, prayer, and meditation, they resolved to open houses where women might follow the exercises of a retreat.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the motherhouse was established, Couderc became <a href="/wiki/Superior_general" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior general">superior general</a>. In 1828 Terme began to hold Ignatian retreats for the sisters. He continued to do so until his death in December 1834. After Terme's death the order split into the Sisters of Saint Regis who retained their teaching ministry, and <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Cenacle" title="Sisters of the Cenacle">the Congregation of Our Lady of the Cenacle</a>, which continued its retreat ministry. The <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a> then led the retreats.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The regular school teaching of the hostel was separated from the retreats, and this resulted in financial hardship for the sisters. Although she was not at fault, Couderc accepted responsibility. This led, in October 1838, to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Viviers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Viviers">Bishop of Viviers</a> Abbon-Pierre-François Bonnel de la Brageresse to remove her from her office and replace her with a new novice as the "Foundress Superior"; Couderc resigned in full on 27 October 1838.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The novice led for a few months but did so bad a job the bishop removed her. The Jesuit advisers began replacing her with a succession of wealthy women.<sup id="cite_ref-cns_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cns-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1842 she was sent for almost eighteen months alone with one other sister to a small house in <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>; in 1852 she went to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>. In November 1856 she was appointed as the superior of the <a href="/wiki/Tournon-sur-Rh%C3%B4ne" title="Tournon-sur-Rhône">Tournon</a> house until it was to be sold off and so she returned to Lyon.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 20 October 1859 a Jesuit gave a retreat on the topic of Christian sacrifice that had a profound impact on her. At the end of August 1860 she was sent to the house at <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a> but its closure in 1867 saw her return to Lyon once more.
</p><p>In the beginning of 1885 she fainted and was unconscious for several hours in an occurrence that left her bedridden until her death.<sup id="cite_ref-CL_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CL-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Couderc died on 26 September 1885 and was buried in Lalouvesc.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Couderc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification cause commenced in an informative process that opened in France in 1920 and concluded its work in 1921 which then led to the approval of all of her spiritual writings from theologians on 23 July 1924; the informative process was validated by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Rites">Congregation of Rites</a> on 13 July 1927. The formal introduction to the cause came on 18 July 1927 in which she was titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> - the first official stage in the process.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> proclaimed Couderc to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 12 May 1935 after he confirmed that the late nun lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. Pope Pius XII beatified her on 4 November 1951 after approving two miracles attributed to her intercession while the cause was resumed in a decree issued on 26 July 1953. Pope Paul VI canonized Couderc as a saint on 10 May 1970 after approving two more miracles attributed to her intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-cns_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cns-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spirituality">Spirituality</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Couderc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Spirituality">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="To_Surrender_Oneself">To Surrender Oneself</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Couderc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: To Surrender Oneself">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1864 Couderc wrote:
</p>
<dl><dd>I understand the full extent of the expression to surrender oneself, but I cannot explain it. I only know that it is very vast, that it embraces both the present and the future.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>To surrender oneself is more than to devote oneself, more than to give oneself, it is even something more than to abandon oneself to God. In a word, to surrender oneself is to die to everything and to self, to be no longer concerned with self except to keep it continually turned toward God.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>To surrender oneself is, moreover, no longer to seek oneself in anything, either for the spiritual or the physical, that is to say, no longer to seek one's own satisfaction, but solely the divine good pleasure.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>It should be added that to surrender oneself is also to follow that spirit of detachment which clings to nothing, neither to persons nor to things, neither to time nor to place. It means to adhere to everything, to accept everything, to submit to everything.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>But perhaps you will think that this is very difficult to do. Do not let yourself be deceived. There is nothing so easy to do, nothing so sweet to put into practice. The whole thing consists in making a generous act once and for all, saying with all the sincerity of your soul: "My God, I wish to be entirely thine; deign to accept my offering." And all is said. But from then on, you must take care to keep yourself in this disposition of soul and not to shrink from any of the little sacrifices which can help you advance in virtue. You must always remember that you have surrendered yourself.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>I pray to our Lord to give an understanding of this word to all souls desirous of pleasing him and to inspire them to take advantage of so easy a means of sanctification. Oh! If people could just understand ahead of time the sweetness and peace that are savored when nothing is held back from the good God! How he communicates himself to the one who seeks him sincerely and has known how to surrender herself. Let them experience it and they will see that here is found the true happiness they are vainly seeking elsewhere.</dd></dl>
<p>The surrendered soul has found paradise on earth.<sup id="cite_ref-cns_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cns-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Goodness">Goodness</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_Couderc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Goodness">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1866, Couderc reported having a vision of goodness which was a defining moment for her life and spirituality, and which she describes in a letter to Mother de Larochenégly:
</p>
<dl><dd>A few days ago, I saw something that consoled me very much. It was during my thanksgiving, when I was making a few reflections on the goodness of God — and how would it be possible not to think of this in such moments: of this infinite goodness, uncreated goodness, source of all goodness! And without which there would be no goodness, neither in people nor in other creatures.</dd></dl>
<dl><dd>I was extremely touched by these reflections, when I saw written as in letters of gold this word Goodness, which I repeated for a long while with an indescribable sweetness. I saw it, I say, written on all creatures, animate and inanimate, rational or not — all bore this name of goodness. I saw it even on the chair which I was using for a kneeler. I understood then that all that these creatures have of good and all the services and help that we receive from each of them are a blessing that we owe to the goodness of our God, who has communicated to them something of his infinite goodness, so that we may meet it in everything and everywhere.</dd></dl>
