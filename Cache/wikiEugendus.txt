					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Eugendus</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 449<br />Izernore, France</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">January 1, 510</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">4 January (formerly January 2) 1 January in Eastern Orthodox Church</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saint Eugendus</b> (also <b>Augendus</b>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Oyand, Oyan</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 449 – January 1, 510) was the fourth abbot of <a href="/wiki/Condat_Abbey" title="Condat Abbey">Condat Abbey</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Claude,_Jura" title="Saint-Claude, Jura">Saint-Claude, Jura</a>.  He was born at <a href="/wiki/Izernore" title="Izernore">Izernore</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eugendus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was instructed in reading and writing by his father, who had become a priest, and at the age of seven was given to <a href="/wiki/Romanus_of_Condat" title="Romanus of Condat">Saint Romanus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lupicinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Lupicinus">Saint Lupicinus</a> to be educated at Condat Abbey. Thenceforth he never left the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>. Eugendus acquired much learning, read the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greece" title="Ancient Greece">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> authors, and was well versed in the <a href="/wiki/Scriptures" class="mw-redirect" title="Scriptures">Scriptures</a>. He led a life of great austerity, including being said to have never laughed, supposedly in respect to a passage in the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St Benedict">Rule of St Benedict</a> not to take pleasure in unrestrained or raucous laughter (despite the fact that <a href="/wiki/St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Benedict">St. Benedict</a> was born thirty years after him and would scarcely have been known at the time of Eugendus' death). He also refused ever to be ordained a priest. 
</p><p>Abbot Minausius made him his <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor" title="Coadjutor">coadjutor</a>, and after the former's death (about 496) Eugendus became his successor.  After the monastery, which <a href="/wiki/Romanus_of_Condat" title="Romanus of Condat">Saint Romanus</a> had built of wood, was destroyed by fire, Eugendus erected another of stone, and improved the community life; thus far the brethren had lived in separate cells after the fashion of the Eastern ascetics. He built an abbey church in honour of the holy Apostles <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a>, <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrew">Andrew</a>, and enriched it with precious <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>; the church was the predecessor of the rebuilt abbey church that is now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Claude_Cathedral" title="Saint-Claude Cathedral">Saint-Claude Cathedral</a>
</p><p>The order, which had been founded on the rules of the Oriental monasteries, now took on more of the active character of the Western brethren; the rule of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarnate&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Tarnate (page does not exist)">Tarnate</a> is thought to have served as a model. Condat began to flourish as a place of refuge for all those who suffered from the misfortunes and afflictions of those eventful times, a school of virtue and knowledge amid the surrounding darkness, an oasis in the desert. When Eugendus felt his end approaching he had his breast anointed by a priest, took leave of his brethren, and died quietly after five days, at the age of sixty-one.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eugendus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A few years after his death, his successor, Saint <a href="/wiki/Viventiolus" title="Viventiolus">Viventiolus</a>, erected a shrine over his tomb in the abbey church, to which numerous pilgrims travelled. The village that grew round Condat Abbey came to be called, after the saint, <i>Saint-Oyand de Joux</i>, a name it retained as late as the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, while the abbey's former name of Condat passed into oblivion; the abbey was secularised in 1742.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eugendus has a biography in the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_patrum_Jurensium" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita patrum Jurensium">Vita patrum Jurensium</a></i>.
</p><p>The feast of Saint Eugendus was at first transferred to 2 January; in the dioceses of Besançon and Saint-Claude it is now celebrated on 4 January.
</p>
