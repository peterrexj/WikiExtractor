<p><b>Saint Lucian of Beauvais (</b><a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: Lucien, <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: Lucianus, died c. 290 AD) is a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, called the "Apostle of <a href="/wiki/Beauvais" title="Beauvais">Beauvais</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He was killed in the 3rd century during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecution, although later traditions make him a martyr of the 1st century instead. This was because the church of Beauvais attempted to claim <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_succession" title="Apostolic succession">apostolic</a> origins for itself. <a href="/wiki/Odo_I_of_Beauvais" title="Odo I of Beauvais">Odo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Beauvais" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Beauvais">bishop of Beauvais</a> during the 9th century, was actually the first writer to designate Lucien as the first bishop of Beauvais.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nevertheless, the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Beauvais-Noyons-Senlis" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Beauvais-Noyons-Senlis">diocese of Beauvais</a> is traditionally attributed to him.  His <i>Passio</i> assigns him two disciples, Maximian (Maxien, Maximien) and Julian (Julien), who were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a> with him on the hill of Montmille.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucien_of_Beauvais&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The details of his life are largely unknown; the date of his death was moved backwards in time in order to lend his see more antiquity, a common practice during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>.  As <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> writes, "To have lived amongst the Saviour's immediate following was...honorable...and accordingly old patrons of churches were identified with certain persons in the <a href="/wiki/Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Gospels">gospels</a> or who were supposed to have had some part of <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ's</a> life on earth."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tradition holds he came from a noble family of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was named "Lucius" like his father, but when he was converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> himself, he took the name of Lucian.  As a young man, he preached in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> and then he was ordained <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Clement I">Pope Clement I</a> (who actually lived in a different century), who sent him to <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Denis_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Denis of Paris">Saint Denis</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rieul_of_Reims" title="Rieul of Reims">Rieul of Reims</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> among others (Lucian is also called an associate of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quentin" title="Saint Quentin">Saint Quentin</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>), to preach there.  He was imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a>, but was freed by Christians there.  He converted people in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> before arriving in Arles, where he once again met up with Saint Rieul.  Denis and Lucian continued towards <a href="/wiki/Lutetia" title="Lutetia">Lutetia</a>.  Marcellinus and those accompanying him continued on to Spain. Denis remained in Lutetia while Lucian continued onto Beauvais, at the time known as <i>Caesaromagus</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Beauvais, he acquired fame for his <a href="/wiki/Mortifications" class="mw-redirect" title="Mortifications">mortifications</a> and <a href="/wiki/Penances" class="mw-redirect" title="Penances">penances</a>.  He preached against the <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a>.  He lived in a house that is considered to be the place now occupied by the <a href="/wiki/Collegiate_church" title="Collegiate church">collegiate church</a> of Saint-Nicolas.  Denis and Rieul visited him here.  According to Rolandus, the author of the <i>Acta Sancti Luciani</i>, he retired to a mountain near the city, living as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> on grass and water.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  According to one account, he converted 30,000 people to Christianity, and was assisted in this task by his 2 disciples.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Assassination" title="Assassination">assassins</a> Latinus, Jarius and Antor were sent by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> (his legendary account gives the contradictory name of Diocletian, though this emperor lived during the 3rd century) to kill him.  They killed his disciples first and then beat Lucian with rods, finally slicing his head off.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  His legend states that after Lucian was decapitated, he picked up his own head and walked towards the town of Beauvais.  Having crossed the river <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9rain" title="Thérain">Thérain</a> at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Miauroy&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Miauroy (page does not exist)">Miauroy</a> (Beauvais lies at the foot of wooded hills on the left bank of the Thérain at its confluence with the Avelon), Lucian stopped within a quarter mile of Beauvais, and died there, thus indicating to his followers that he wanted to be buried on that very spot.  This part of his legend thus makes Lucian one of the legendary <a href="/wiki/Cephalophores" class="mw-redirect" title="Cephalophores">cephalophores</a>, whose number also include his alleged companion, Denis.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the legend, the <a href="/wiki/Angels" class="mw-redirect" title="Angels">angels</a> themselves attended the <a href="/wiki/Funeral" title="Funeral">funeral</a> of the saint, and according to local tradition, <a href="/wiki/Vermilion" title="Vermilion">vermilion</a>-colored <a href="/wiki/Rose" title="Rose">rosebushes</a> blossomed on the spot where Lucian's blood had run.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucien_of_Beauvais&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lucian's body was buried in the cemetery of Thil.  His name occurred in the calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Common_Prayer" title="Book of Common Prayer">Book of Common Prayer</a> from an early date.
</p><p>At the end of the Christian persecutions, a church was built over his tomb; it was called the Church of Saints Peter and Lucian.  It was destroyed in the 5th century.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Around 583, at the request of Dodo, bishop of Beauvais, and Saint <a href="/w/index.php?title=Evrou&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Evrou (page does not exist)">Evrou</a> (Evrost), <a href="/wiki/Chilperic_I" title="Chilperic I">Chilperic I</a> ordered to be built a new <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> and monastery on the same site.  Dodo consecrated the church, dedicating it once again to Saints Peter and Lucian.  Saint Evrou served as abbot of the monastery.  The abbey was destroyed in 845 during the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> invasions, but a new one was built in the 12th century, serving also as a burial place for the cathedral <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a>.  During the Middle Ages, a <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> was also built on the alleged site of their death, at Montmille, which became a place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> during the Middle Ages.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1261, the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of Lucian, Maximian, and Julian were placed in a new <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> by William of Grès (Guillaume de Grès), bishop of Beauvais.  The <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation</a> took place in the presence of <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">St. Louis IX</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="King of France">king of France</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Theobald_II_of_Navarre" title="Theobald II of Navarre">Theobald II</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Navarre" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Navarre">king of Navarre</a>, and much of the French nobility.  The memory of this translation was formerly celebrated in the abbey of Beauvais as the <i>fête des Corps Saints</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On January 5, 1791, the abbey was put on sale and was bought by a rich Parisian, Vicente Alterio.  The <a href="/wiki/Liturgical" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgical">liturgical</a> objects were transported to the church of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Notre-Dame_du-Thil&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Notre-Dame du-Thil (page does not exist)">Notre-Dame du-Thil</a>.  The basilica and the monastery were demolished between 1795 and 1819.  Of the monastery, only the round tower and part of the wall remain.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On November 20, 1793, Lucian's relics were tossed into a fire by Protestant extremists.<sup id="cite_ref-catho60.cef.fr_1-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catho60.cef.fr-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the Orthodox Church, Lucian is commemorated on June 3 and January 8 (See Wikipedia's Eastern Orthodox Liturgics).<sup id="cite_ref-oca_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
