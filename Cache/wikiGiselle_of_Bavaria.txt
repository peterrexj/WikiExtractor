<p><b>Gisela of Hungary</b> (or <b>Gisele</b>, <b>Gizella</b> and <b>of Bavaria</b>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 985 – 7 May 1065) was the first <a href="/wiki/Queen_consort" title="Queen consort">queen consort</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a> by marriage to <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a>, and the sister of <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>. She has been <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gisela_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gisela was a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Duke_of_Bavaria" title="Henry II, Duke of Bavaria">Henry II, Duke of Bavaria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gisela_of_Burgundy" title="Gisela of Burgundy">Gisela of Burgundy</a>. Gisela was raised very devout, most likely with bishop Wolfgang of Regensburg as her mentor and governor. She married King <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a> in 996<sup id="cite_ref-zenty_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zenty-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> as a part of Hungary's policy of opening up to the West. The couple had a son, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary" title="Saint Emeric of Hungary">Saint Emeric</a>, who died on 2 September 1031, while hunting boar.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The wedding of Stephen and Gisela marked a turning point in Hungary's history.
</p><p>Queen Gisela played a fundamental role in spreading the Christian faith and <a href="/wiki/Western_culture" title="Western culture">Western culture</a> in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-zenty_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zenty-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Ironside" title="Edmund Ironside">Edmund Ironside</a> of England died, he was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Cnut" title="Cnut">Cnut</a>. Edmund's infant sons were sent abroad and ended up under the protection of King Stephen of Hungary. One of the twins died young, but the other, <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Exile" title="Edward the Exile">Edward Atheling</a>, was brought up as a protégé of Queen Gisela, and regarded in that foreign court as the heir to the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon">Anglo-Saxon</a> throne.<sup id="cite_ref-selden_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-selden-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>King Stephen died in 1038. In 1046 Gisela, her attendants, and a number of many Bavarian settlers left Hungary to return to Bavaria, where she joined Niedernburg convent in Passau and became the abbess.<sup id="cite_ref-zenty_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zenty-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She lived in the nunnery of Niedernburg in <a href="/wiki/Passau" title="Passau">Passau</a>, where she died.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gisela_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gisela's <a href="/wiki/Canonisation" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonisation">canonisation</a> was attempted in the 18th century but failed. She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1975. Her memorial days are 7 May and 1 February.
</p><p>Gisela and her husband were not buried together. On 4 May 1996, the remains of King Stephen's right hand was preserved, and it was brought back together with a bone taken from the arm of Gisela. Both are now safely protected in glass and gold cases, and are displayed in the basilica in the western Hungarian town of <a href="/wiki/Veszpr%C3%A9m" title="Veszprém">Veszprém</a>, where Gisela once lived.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her grave is well known, and regarded as a holy place. The cross was commissioned by Queen Gisela for the tomb of her mother, who died in 1006 and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Niederm%C3%BCnster,_Regensburg" title="Niedermünster, Regensburg">Niedermünster</a> in <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a>.
</p><p>Blessed Gisela is depicted on a white limestone panel by Hungarian artist Sandor Kiss on the wall of the Chapel of Our Lady – Queen of Hungary in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>, Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
