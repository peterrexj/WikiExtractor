<p><b>Saint Ghislain</b> (died 9 October 680) was a <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a> in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. He died at the town named after him, <a href="/wiki/Saint-Ghislain" title="Saint-Ghislain">Saint-Ghislain</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Ghislain&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was probably of <a href="/wiki/Germanic_peoples" title="Germanic peoples">Germanic</a> origin. Ghislain lived in the province of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Hainaut" title="County of Hainaut">Hainaut</a>  in the time of <a href="/wiki/Amandus" title="Amandus">Amandus</a> (d. 679) and saints <a href="/wiki/Waltrude" title="Waltrude">Waltrude</a>, <a href="/wiki/Aldegonde" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldegonde">Aldegonde</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Amalberga_of_Maubeuge" title="Amalberga of Maubeuge">Amalberga of Maubeuge</a>. With two disciples, Lambert and Berler, he made a clearing in the vicinity of <i>Castrilocus</i> (now <a href="/wiki/Mons" title="Mons">Mons</a>, in Hainault), taking up later his abode at a place called <i>Ursidongus</i>, where he built an <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> or <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-van_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Aubert_of_Avranches" title="Aubert of Avranches">Aubert of Avranches</a> summoned him to the episcopal presence in order to sound the intentions of this almost unknown hermit, but he afterwards accorded him efficient protection. During his visit to <a href="/wiki/Cambrai" title="Cambrai">Cambrai</a>, Ghislain spent some time in the villa of <a href="/wiki/Honnelles" title="Honnelles">Roisin</a> and received as a gift the estates of Celles and Hornu. He soon entered into relations with Waltrude, who was induced by him to build a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Mons" title="Mons">Castrilocus</a>, his former place of refuge.<sup id="cite_ref-van_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is probable that Ghislain influenced the religious vocation of <a href="/wiki/Aldegonde" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldegonde">Aldegonde</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbess_of_Maubeuge" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbess of Maubeuge">Abbess of Maubeuge</a>, also of Amalberga and <a href="/wiki/Aldetrude" title="Aldetrude">Aldetrude</a>, of whom the first was the sister and the last two the daughters of Waltrude. One day Aldegonde in her monastery of Maubeuge, had a vision in which, according to her biographer, the death of Amandus, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tongeren" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tongeren">Bishop of Tongeren</a>, was revealed to her. Ghislain visited the saint in her villa of Mairieu, near <a href="/wiki/Maubeuge" title="Maubeuge">Maubeuge</a>, and explained to her that the vision was an announcement of her own approaching death. The intercourse between Ghislain and Aldegonde brought about a perfect understanding between Maubeuge and the monastery founded at Ursidongus under Ghislain's direction. St. Waudru rewarded her counsellor with a portion of the villa of Frameries and of the oratory of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Quentin,_Aisne" title="Saint-Quentin, Aisne">Saint-Quentin</a>, comprised within the boundaries of the villa of <a href="/wiki/Quaregnon" title="Quaregnon">Quaregnon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-van_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Ghislain&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-Ghislain,_Tour_St_Martin.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Saint-Ghislain%2C_Tour_St_Martin.JPG/220px-Saint-Ghislain%2C_Tour_St_Martin.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Saint-Ghislain%2C_Tour_St_Martin.JPG/330px-Saint-Ghislain%2C_Tour_St_Martin.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Saint-Ghislain%2C_Tour_St_Martin.JPG/440px-Saint-Ghislain%2C_Tour_St_Martin.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1704" data-file-height="2272" /></a><figcaption>Tour St Martin, Saint-Ghislain</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ghislain died at Ursidongus, and <a href="/wiki/Saint-Ghislain_Abbey" title="Saint-Ghislain Abbey">the monastery which he had founded</a> took his name. The <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of the saint were first disinterred c. 929. They were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Grandlieu" class="mw-redirect" title="Grandlieu">Grandlieu</a>, near Quaregnon, about the end of the tenth century or the beginning of the eleventh, and in 1025 <a href="/wiki/Gerard_of_Florennes" title="Gerard of Florennes">Gerard of Florennes, Bishop of Cambrai</a>, removed them to <a href="/wiki/Le_Cateau-Cambr%C3%A9sis" title="Le Cateau-Cambrésis">Le Cateau-Cambrésis</a>. They were visited several times in the course of the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> by the bishops of Cambrai.
</p><p>In 1647 they were removed to St-Ghislain of which place he is patron. His feast is celebrated on 9 October and his intercession is sought to protect children from <a href="/wiki/Convulsions" class="mw-redirect" title="Convulsions">convulsions</a> and <a href="/wiki/Epilepsy" title="Epilepsy">epilepsy</a>. In the old church of St. Martin in Saint-Ghislain there used to be an altar dedicated to St. Ghislain to which children suffering from epilepsy would be brought in hopes of a cure.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> he is frequently represented with a <a href="/wiki/Bear" title="Bear">bear</a> or bear's cub beside him. This is an allusion to the popular legend which relates that a bear, pursued in the chase by King <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>, sought refuge with Ghislain and later showed him the place where he should establish a monastery. Moreover, the site of the saint's <a href="/wiki/Cella" title="Cella">cella</a> was called Ursidongus, "bear's den".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is sometimes portrayed holding a church.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>There is a Rue Saint-Ghislain/Sint-Gissleinsstraat in Brussels.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
