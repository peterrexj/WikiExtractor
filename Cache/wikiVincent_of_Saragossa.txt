<p><b>Vincent of Saragossa</b> (also known as <b>Vincent Martyr</b>, <b>Vincent of Huesca</b> or <b>Vincent the Deacon</b>), the Protomartyr of <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, was a deacon of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Zaragoza" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Zaragoza">Church of Saragossa</a>. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Valencia_(city_in_Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="Valencia (city in Spain)">Valencia</a>. His feast day is 22 January in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>, with an additional commemoration on 11 November in the Orthodox Church. He was born at <a href="/wiki/Huesca" title="Huesca">Huesca</a> and martyred under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> around the year 304.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_Saragossa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The earliest account of Vincent's martyrdom is in a <i>carmen</i> (lyric <a href="/wiki/Poem" class="mw-redirect" title="Poem">poem</a>) written by the poet <a href="/wiki/Prudentius" title="Prudentius">Prudentius</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> who wrote a series of lyric poems, <i>Peristephanon</i> ("Crowns of Martyrdom"), on <a href="/wiki/Hispania" title="Hispania">Hispanic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> martyrs.
</p><p>He was born at <a href="/wiki/Huesca" title="Huesca">Huesca</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Saragossa" class="mw-redirect" title="Saragossa">Saragossa</a>, Spain sometime during the latter part of the 3rd century; it is believed his father was Eutricius (Euthicius), and his mother was Enola, a native of Osca (Huesca).<sup id="cite_ref-texas_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-texas-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vincent spent most of his life in the city of Saragossa, where he was educated and ordained to the diaconate by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Valerius_of_Saragossa" title="Valerius of Saragossa">Valerius of Saragossa</a>, who commissioned Vincent to preach throughout the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-texas_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-texas-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Because Valerius suffered from a speech impediment, Vincent acted as his spokesman.
</p><p>When the Roman Emperor Diocletian began persecuting Christians in Spain, both were brought before the Roman governor, <a href="/wiki/Dacian_(prefect)" title="Dacian (prefect)">Dacian</a>, in Valencia. Vincent and his bishop Valerius were confined to the prison of Valencia. Though he was finally offered release if he would consign Scripture <a href="/wiki/Book_burning" title="Book burning">to the fire</a>, Vincent refused. Speaking on behalf of his bishop, he informed the judge that they were ready to suffer everything for their faith, and that they could pay no heed either to threats or promises.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His outspoken manner so angered the governor that he had every sort of torture inflicted on Vincent. He was stretched on the rack and his flesh torn with iron hooks. Then his wounds were rubbed with salt and he was burned alive upon a red-hot gridiron. Finally, he was cast into prison and laid on a floor scattered with broken pottery, where he died. During his martyrdom he preserved such peace and tranquillity that it astonished his jailer, who repented from his sins and was converted. Vincent's dead body was thrown into the sea in a sack, but was later recovered by the Christians and his veneration immediately spread throughout the church.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The aged bishop Valerius was exiled.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Vincent_of_Saragossa_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Vincent_of_Saragossa_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/220px-Vincent_of_Saragossa_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="145" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Vincent_of_Saragossa_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/330px-Vincent_of_Saragossa_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Vincent_of_Saragossa_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/440px-Vincent_of_Saragossa_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1949" data-file-height="1287" /></a><figcaption>St. Vincent of Saragossa (<i><a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a></i>, 10th century).</figcaption></figure>

<p>The story that Vincent was tortured on a gridiron is perhaps adapted from the <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> of another son of Huesca, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lawrence" title="Saint Lawrence">Lawrence</a>&#8212; Vincent, like many early martyrs in the early <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographic</a> literature, succeeded in converting his jailer.
</p><p>According to legend, after being martyred, <a href="/wiki/Raven" title="Raven">ravens</a> protected Vincent's body from being devoured by vultures, until his followers could recover the body. His body was taken to what is now known as <a href="/wiki/Cape_St._Vincent" title="Cape St. Vincent">Cape St. Vincent</a>; a shrine was erected over his grave, which continued to be guarded by flocks of ravens.  In the time of <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">Muslim rule in the Iberian Peninsula</a>, the Arab <a href="/wiki/Geographer" title="Geographer">geographer</a> <a href="/wiki/Al-Idrisi" class="mw-redirect" title="Al-Idrisi">Al-Idrisi</a> noted this constant guard by ravens, for which the place was named by him كنيسة الغراب "Kanīsah al-Ghurāb" (Church of the Raven). King <a href="/wiki/Afonso_I_of_Portugal" title="Afonso I of Portugal">Afonso I of Portugal</a> (1139–1185) had Vincent's body exhumed in 1173 and brought it by ship to the <a href="/wiki/Lisbon_Cathedral" title="Lisbon Cathedral">Lisbon Cathedral</a>. This transfer of the relics is depicted on the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Lisbon" title="Coat of arms of Lisbon">coat of arms of Lisbon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_and_veneration">Legacy and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_Saragossa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg/250px-San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg/375px-San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg/500px-San_Vicente_M%C3%A1rtir_arrojado_al_muladar.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>San Vicente Mártir thrown into the dung-hill.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Three elaborated hagiographies, all based ultimately on a lost 5th-century <i>Passion</i>, circulated in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. His "Acts" have been "rather freely colored by the imagination of their compiler".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Though Vincent's tomb in Valencia became the earliest center of his <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cult (religion)">cult</a>, he was also honoured at his birthplace and his reputation spread from Saragossa. The city of <a href="/wiki/Oviedo" title="Oviedo">Oviedo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Asturias" title="Asturias">Asturias</a> grew about the church dedicated to Vincent. Beyond the <a href="/wiki/Pyrenees" title="Pyrenees">Pyrenees</a>, he was venerated first in the vicinity of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9ziers" title="Béziers">Béziers</a>, and at <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbonne</a>. <a href="/wiki/Castres" title="Castres">Castres</a> became an important stop on the international pilgrimage routes to <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a> when the relics of Vincent were transferred to its new abbey-church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Benedict">Saint Benedict</a> from Saragossa in 863, under the patronage of Salomon, count of <a href="/wiki/Cerdanya" title="Cerdanya">Cerdanya</a>.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg/220px-Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="279" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg/330px-Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg/440px-Transladacao_para_S%C3%A9_de_Braga.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2848" data-file-height="3617" /></a><figcaption>Tiled mosaic in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Braga" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Braga">Cathedral of Braga</a> depicting the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translation</a> of St. Vincent's arm.</figcaption></figure>
<p>A church was built in honour of Vincent, by the Catholic bishops of Visigothic Iberia, when they succeeded in converting King <a href="/wiki/Reccared_I" title="Reccared I">Reccared</a> and his nobles to Trinitarian Christianity. When the <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moors</a> came in 711, the church was razed, and its materials incorporated in the <i><a href="/wiki/Mezquita" class="mw-redirect" title="Mezquita">Mezquita</a></i>, the "Great <a href="/wiki/Mosque" title="Mosque">Mosque</a>" of Cordova.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Cape_Verde" title="Cape Verde">Cape Verde</a> island of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente,_Cape_Verde" title="São Vicente, Cape Verde">São Vicente</a>, a former <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_Empire" title="Portuguese Empire">Portuguese colony</a>, was named in his honour because it was discovered on 22 January, Saint Vincent's feast day, in 1462.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The island of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_(island)" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Vincent (island)">St. Vincent</a> in the Caribbean, now a part of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines" title="Saint Vincent and the Grenadines">Saint Vincent and the Grenadines</a>, was named by <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Columbus" title="Christopher Columbus">Christopher Columbus</a> after Vincent of Saragossa, as the island was discovered by Europeans on 22 January, Saint Vincent's feast day.
</p><p>The 15th century Portuguese artist <a href="/wiki/Nuno_Gon%C3%A7alves" title="Nuno Gonçalves">Nuno Gonçalves</a> depicted him in his <a href="/wiki/Saint_Vincent_Panels" title="Saint Vincent Panels">Saint Vincent Panels</a>. A small fresco cycle of stories of Vincent is in the apse of the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Vincenzo" title="Basilica di San Vincenzo">Basilica di San Vincenzo</a> near <a href="/wiki/Cant%C3%B9" title="Cantù">Cantù</a>, in northern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Vicente_de_Zaragoza_(School_of_Francisco_Ribalta)_XVII_century.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Vicente_de_Zaragoza_%28School_of_Francisco_Ribalta%29_XVII_century.jpeg/250px-Vicente_de_Zaragoza_%28School_of_Francisco_Ribalta%29_XVII_century.jpeg" decoding="async" width="250" height="376" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Vicente_de_Zaragoza_%28School_of_Francisco_Ribalta%29_XVII_century.jpeg/375px-Vicente_de_Zaragoza_%28School_of_Francisco_Ribalta%29_XVII_century.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Vicente_de_Zaragoza_%28School_of_Francisco_Ribalta%29_XVII_century.jpeg/500px-Vicente_de_Zaragoza_%28School_of_Francisco_Ribalta%29_XVII_century.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="672" data-file-height="1012" /></a><figcaption>Painting of Saints Vincent Martyr, <a href="/wiki/Vincent_Ferrer" title="Vincent Ferrer">Vincent Ferrer</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Raymond_of_Penyafort" title="Raymond of Penyafort">Raymond of Penyafort</a>. Oil on canvas. Anonymous author, school of <a href="/wiki/Francesc_Ribalta" title="Francesc Ribalta">Francisco Ribalta</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Vincent's left arm is on display as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> in <a href="/wiki/Valencia_Cathedral" title="Valencia Cathedral">Valencia Cathedral</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> located near the extensive Carrer de Sant Vicent Mártir (Saint Vincent the Martyr Street).
</p><p>There is also the small town of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente_(Madeira)" class="mw-redirect" title="São Vicente (Madeira)">São Vicente</a> on the Portuguese island of <a href="/wiki/Madeira" title="Madeira">Madeira</a>, and the city of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente,_S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Vicente, São Paulo">São Vicente, São Paulo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> named after this saint.
</p><p>Vincent is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/January_22" title="January 22">22 January</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church_in_North_America" title="Anglican Church in North America">Anglican</a> <a href="/wiki/St._Vincent%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Vincent&#39;s Cathedral">St. Vincent's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bedford,_Texas" title="Bedford, Texas">Bedford, Texas</a>, is dedicated in his honor.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_Saragossa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Vincent is the patron of the Order of the Deacons of the Catholic Diocese of Bergamo (Italy). He is honoured as patron in Valencia, Saragossa, Portugal, etc., and is invoked by <a href="/wiki/Winemaker" title="Winemaker">vignerons</a> (wine-makers), <a href="/wiki/Winemaker" title="Winemaker">vintners</a> (wine-merchants), <a href="/wiki/Vinegar" title="Vinegar">vinegar</a>-makers, brickmakers, and sailors.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vincent_of_Saragossa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Vincent of Saragossa is portrayed as a deacon; in the <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western church</a>, wearing the <a href="/wiki/Dalmatic" title="Dalmatic">dalmatic</a> of a deacon.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
