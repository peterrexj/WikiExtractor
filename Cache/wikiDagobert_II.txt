<p><b>Dagobert II</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Dagober(c)tus</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPertz187242_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPertz187242-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Dægberht</i></span>;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShippey2005392_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShippey2005392-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> died 679) was a <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="King of the Franks">king of the Franks</a>, ruling in <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> from 675 or 676 until his death. He is one of the more obscure Merovingians. He has been considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> since at least the ninth century.
</p><p>None of the narrative histories of the Merovingian period give an account of Dagobert's reign, which must be reconstructed from several different sources. Upon the death of his father in 656, he was deprived of the succession and exiled to <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> to live as a monk. His return to Austrasia was arranged by <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of York">bishop of York</a>. He ascended the throne following the assassination of his cousin in 675. During his brief reign he made war on the neighbouring Frankish kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>, signed a peace treaty with the <a href="/wiki/Lombard_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Lombard Kingdom">Lombard Kingdom</a> in Italy and reintroduced gold coinage.
</p><p>The only near-contemporary assessment of Dagobert's character portrays him as a tyrant. He antagonized the bishops and imposed new taxes. He was assassinated by a conspiracy of the highest nobility. He was succeeded by his cousin, <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III" title="Theuderic III">Theuderic III</a>, king of Neustria, against whom he had previously warred.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Exile">Exile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dagobert_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Exile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Dagobert was the son of <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_III" title="Sigebert III">Sigebert III</a> (ruled 632–51/6) and an unknown woman. It is unlikely that he was a son of Sigebert's only known wife, Chimnechild, who survived him. He was thus the half-brother of <a href="/wiki/Bilichild" title="Bilichild">Bilichild</a>, Chimnechild's daughter by Sigebert. He was named for his grandfather, <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> (623–39).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994222–24_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994222–24-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_historiae_Francorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Liber historiae Francorum">Book of the History of the Franks</a></i>, which dates to 727, after Sigebert's death, <a href="/wiki/Grimoald_the_Elder" title="Grimoald the Elder">Grimoald</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_palace" title="Mayor of the palace">mayor of the palace</a> and the most powerful official under the king, arranged for Dagobert to be <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> and placed in the custody of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Dido_(bishop_of_Poitiers)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Dido (bishop of Poitiers) (page does not exist)">Dido</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Poitiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Poitiers">bishop of Poitiers</a>. Tonsuring rendered Dagobert unfit for the throne, since Merovingian kings always wore their hair long. Dido then sent Dagobert to Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The dating of these events is uncertain. They are usually placed in 656, but a date as early as 651 has also been proposed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994222–24_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994222–24-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>No contemporary source describes Dagobert's time in Ireland. The eighteenth-century antiquary <a href="/wiki/Mervyn_Archdall_(Irish_antiquary)" title="Mervyn Archdall (Irish antiquary)">Mervyn Archdall</a> was the first to record the association of Dagobert with a specific place in Ireland. He wrote that a local oral tradition current at that time put Dagobert in the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Slane" title="Slane">Slane</a>, a conclusion accepted by some modern scholars.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPicard199143–46_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicard199143–46-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Grimoald placed his own son, <a href="/wiki/Childebert_the_Adopted" title="Childebert the Adopted">Childebert</a>, on the Austrasian throne, but the Neustrians under <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> (639–657) had Grimoald arrested and brought to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, where he was executed "because he had acted against his lord", that is, the rightful Merovingian claimant.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Clovis's second son, <a href="/wiki/Childeric_II" title="Childeric II">Childeric II</a>, who was still a minor, was placed on the Austrasian throne in 662. He was married to Bilichild, Dagobert's half-sister, and placed under the regency of Chimnechild.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994222–24_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994222–24-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was assassinated, along with his queen and his son, in 675.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The murder of Childeric provided the occasion for Dagobert's return, but its immediate result was civil war. The former mayor of the palace, <a href="/wiki/Ebroin" title="Ebroin">Ebroin</a>, declared a certain <a href="/wiki/Clovis_III" title="Clovis III">Clovis III</a>, son of the Neustrian king <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_III" title="Chlothar III">Chlothar III</a> (658–673), as king in Austrasia, while Clovis II's third son, <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III" title="Theuderic III">Theuderic III</a>, was placed on the Neustrian throne by <a href="/wiki/Leodegar" title="Leodegar">Leodegar</a>. After Leodegar's capture, Ebroin abandoned Clovis for Theuderic, and in so doing lost his Austrasian allies. In this situation, Dagobert was recalled from his Irish exile.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_from_exile">Return from exile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dagobert_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Return from exile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>While the <i>Book of the History of the Franks</i> is the only source to describe the circumstances of Dagobert's exile, the <i><a href="/wiki/Life_of_Wilfrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Life of Wilfrid">Life of Wilfrid</a></i> is the only one to describe his return. This biography of the English bishop <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a> was composed in the first decades of the eighth century by <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_Ripon" title="Stephen of Ripon">Stephen of Ripon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> According to Stephen, Dagobert was exiled to Ireland "in his youth" and when his friends and relatives later learned that he was still living they asked Wilfrid to bring him to England and from there send him on to Austrasia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Life of Wilfrid</i> does not specify who was responsible for recalling Dagobert, only that it was "friends" (<i>amici</i>) and "relatives" (<i>proximi</i>). The relatives may have been on his mother's side. There is little consensus on who the friends could have been, possibly <a href="/wiki/Wulfoald" title="Wulfoald">Wulfoald</a>, the former mayor of the palace; <a href="/wiki/Pippin_of_Herstal" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin of Herstal">Pippin of Herstal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Laon" title="Martin of Laon">Martin of Laon</a>, who came to power, according to the <i>Book of the History of the Franks</i>, after "the deaths of kings", perhaps in 675; or even <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ultan" title="Saint Ultan">Ultan</a>, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maur-des-Foss%C3%A9s" title="Saint-Maur-des-Fossés">Saint-Maur-des-Fossés</a>, who was Irish himself and had connections in Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The exact date of Dagobert's accession is not known. Childeric II was killed in 675 and Dagobert was on the throne by July 676.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981275_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981275-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reign">Reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dagobert_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg/220px-Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="110" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg/330px-Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg/440px-Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="620" data-file-height="309" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Tiers_de_sou_de_Dagobert_II.jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A gold tremissis of Dagobert II, showing a <a href="/wiki/Diadem" title="Diadem">diademed</a> bust of the king on the obverse and a <a href="/wiki/Globus_cruciger" title="Globus cruciger">cross surmounting a globe</a> on the reverse.</div></div></div>
<p>Dagobert's reign is sparsely recorded. The <i>Book of the History of the Franks</i>, which has a Neustrian perspective, does not mention him again after describing his exile. The historian <a href="/wiki/Richard_Gerberding" title="Richard Gerberding">Richard Gerberding</a> says of the <i>History</i>'s author, "Either he did not believe that Dagobert had returned to become king or he did not want us to know of it".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGerberding198779_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGerberding198779-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Paul Fouracre suggests that he did not regard Dagobert as a legitimate king, either because he had not been chosen with the support of the Neustrians; because he was considered a foreigner after his long exile; or because he was not considered throneworthy on account of the circumstances of his birth, his mother having been a concubine.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981275_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981275-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Only one seventh-century work from within the Merovingian kingdoms, the <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Sadalbergae_abbatissae_Laudunensis" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita Sadalbergae abbatissae Laudunensis">Life of Abbess Sadalberga of Laon</a></i>, mentions the reign of Dagobert, and then only in passing. It records that <a href="/wiki/Sadalberga" title="Sadalberga">Sadalberga</a> moved her convent from the suburbs of <a href="/wiki/Langres" title="Langres">Langres</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Burgundy" title="Burgundy">Burgundy</a> to the city of <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a> because of forebodings, later proven true by "recent fighting between Kings Dagobert and Theuderic". This is the only mention of a war between Dagobert and his first cousin, Theuderic III of Neustria.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcNamaraHalborgWhatley1992186_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcNamaraHalborgWhatley1992186-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> It is an indication of the continuing animosity between Ebroin and the Austrasians,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> although it may have begun as a border dispute in the <a href="/wiki/Champagne_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Champagne (region)">Champagne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981265–266_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981265–266-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That the war was waged deep in Burgundy, which was under Neustrian rule, suggests that Dagobert for a time had the initiative.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It may be during this war that Pippin of Herstal and Martin of Laon launched the attack on Theuderic III that was defeated by Ebroin at the famous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Lucofao" title="Battle of Lucofao">Battle of Lucofao</a>, resulting in Martin's death. While this battle may have taken place after Dagobert's death, a more likely date is September 679, when Dagobert was still alive and able to issue orders for the raising of levies.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGerberding198779–84_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGerberding198779–84-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracreGerberding199623_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracreGerberding199623-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The war mentioned in the <i>Life</i>, however, cannot be dated precisely. It may have ended as early as September 677.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981265–266_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981265–266-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 676, Dagobert signed a "most firm pact of peace"<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> with the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a>. This event can be dated precisely because the only source for it, the <i><a href="/wiki/History_of_the_Lombards" title="History of the Lombards">History of the Lombards</a></i> written by <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Deacon" title="Paul the Deacon">Paul the Deacon</a> towards the end of the eighth century, reports the appearance of a comet in August the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This comet was widely reported across the world from Ireland to Japan.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKronk1999124–25_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKronk1999124–25-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Paul, however, mistakenly places the pact in the reign of the Lombard king <a href="/wiki/Grimoald,_King_of_the_Lombards" title="Grimoald, King of the Lombards">Grimoald</a>, who died in 671, before Dagobert had even returned from Ireland. The Lombard king at the time of the comet was actually <a href="/wiki/Perctarit" title="Perctarit">Perctarit</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The only surviving authentic charter issued by Dagobert confirms the possessions of the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Stavelot-Malmedy" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Stavelot-Malmedy">monastery of Stavelot-Malmedy</a>. In the charter Dagobert refers to the donations made by his father, but does not mention that the monastery was founded by Grimoald, the man who had exiled him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dagobert reintroduced the minting of gold, which had apparently been suspended by Childeric II around 670. His gold <i><a href="/wiki/Tremissis" title="Tremissis">tremissis</a></i> broke with the old Frankish style and copied the <a href="/wiki/Cross_potent" title="Cross potent">cross potent on three steps</a> of contemporary <a href="/wiki/Solidus_(coin)" title="Solidus (coin)">Byzantine <i>solidi</i></a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGriersonBlackburn2007130–31_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGriersonBlackburn2007130–31-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Dagobert was also the last king in whose name coins were struck in <a href="/wiki/Marseille" title="Marseille">Marseille</a>. The important royal coinage of Marseille, lasting from 613 until 679, was always struck with the name of a king, which was unusual since Frankish coins typically contain only the names of the moneyer and the mint.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGriersonBlackburn2007130–31_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGriersonBlackburn2007130–31-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 679, while on his way to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to attend a church council, Wilfrid stayed at the court of Dagobert, who was grateful to the bishop for having facilitated his return from Ireland. Dagobert offered to appoint Wilfrid to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Strasbourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Strasbourg">diocese of Strasbourg</a>, which the <i>Life of Wilfrid</i> calls the "chief bishopric of his realm", but Wilfrid declined. Dagobert provided him with arms and companions for the rest of his journey to Rome. In Italy, Wilfrid stayed for a time at the court of Dagobert's new ally, Perctarit.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In late 679, shortly after Wilfrid's visit, Dagobert was assassinated. The <i>Life of Wilfrid</i> claims that this was engineered by "treacherous dukes" with the consent of the bishops.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to a late tradition, he was killed by his own godson, John, while hunting in the <a href="/wiki/Wo%C3%ABvre" title="Woëvre">Woëvre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPeters1991101_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeters1991101-20">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The traditional date of his death, 23 December, is likewise based on late sources but widely accepted.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGerberding1983150_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGerberding1983150-21">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following Dagobert's death, Ebroin managed to extend Theuderic III's authority over Austrasia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> On Wilfrid's return trip through Austrasia in 680, he was arrested by Ebroin's men, who blamed him for having arranged Dagobert's return.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994231–34-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> According to the report in the <i>Life</i>, Dagobert was a "destroyer of cities, despising the counsels of the magnates, reducing the people with taxation ... being contemptuous of God's churches and their bishops."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood1994235_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood1994235-22">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> It is possible that the reference to the destruction of cities refers to Dagobert's failure to protect them when Ebroin devastated the land after his victory at Lucofao.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981279_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre1981279-23">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyr_cult">Martyr cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dagobert_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyr cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:402px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg/400px-St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg" decoding="async" width="400" height="297" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg/600px-St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg/800px-St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1608" data-file-height="1192" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St%C3%A8le_du_martyr_de_saint_Dagobert.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Carving depicting the murder of Dagobert from the crypt of the Basilica of Saint Dagobert in Stenay</div></div></div>
<p>The <i>Life of Wilfrid</i> is the only source to record Dagobert's assassination, but some corroboration comes from the fact that he was being revered as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ardennes" title="Ardennes">Ardennes</a> region before the end of the ninth century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are conflicting claims about where Dagobert was buried. According to the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vita_Audoini_episcopi_Rotomagensis&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Vita Audoini episcopi Rotomagensis (page does not exist)">Life</a></i> of Bishop <a href="/wiki/Audoin_(bishop)" title="Audoin (bishop)">Audoin of Rouen</a>, written in the early eighth century, Dagobert was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Ouen,_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Ouen, Rouen">church of Saint Peter</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> alongside his predecessor, Childeric II, and Childeric's queen, <a href="/wiki/Bilichild" title="Bilichild">Bilichild</a>, and infant son, also named Dagobert. This source refers to the "Dagobert whom Grimoald tonsured" without mentioning his exile, return or reign.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Vita_Dagoberti" title="Vita Dagoberti">Life of Dagobert</a></i>, on the other hand, says that Dagobert was buried at <a href="/wiki/Stenay" title="Stenay">Stenay</a> in the church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Remigius" title="Saint Remigius">Saint Remigius</a>. This is not implausible, since Stenay was in the centre of Austrasia. The source, however, dates to the 890s and confuses Dagobert II and <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_III" title="Dagobert III">Dagobert III</a> (711–715), who died of illness.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 872, the cult of Dagobert was brought to life (or revived) by <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West Francia</a> (840–877), who had his relics translated to a specially built basilica in Stenay staffed with its own <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a>. The timing seems to indicate that Charles was trying to establish himself in that part of <a href="/wiki/Lotharingia" title="Lotharingia">Lotharingia</a> that he had only acquired in 870 by the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Meerssen" title="Treaty of Meerssen">Treaty of Meerssen</a>. The translation of the relics is mentioned in the <i>Life of Dagobert</i> and in a charter preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Cartulary" title="Cartulary">cartulary</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Gorze" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Gorze">Abbey of Gorze</a>, which provides the date.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The endowment of the Basilica of Saint Dagobert is known from a charter of 1124, also in the cartulary of Gorze. The endowment had passed at some point to <a href="/wiki/Beatrice_of_Lorraine" title="Beatrice of Lorraine">Beatrice</a>, wife of <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_III,_Duke_of_Lower_Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Godfrey III, Duke of Lower Lorraine">Godfrey III, Duke of Lower Lorraine</a>, who in 1069 left it to the abbey of Gorze. The canons having grown lax, the monks turned it into a <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictine</a> priory.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dagobert's feast day was 23 December. This day is given in the <i>Life of Dagobert</i>; in the now lost <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">calendar of saints</a> made for <a href="/wiki/Emma_of_Italy" title="Emma of Italy">Emma</a>, wife of King <a href="/wiki/Lothair_of_France" title="Lothair of France">Lothair of France</a> (954–986); and in the <i>auctaria</i> (local additions) to the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Usuard" title="Martyrology of Usuard">Martyrology of Usuard</a> from the area of modern Belgium. One <i>auctarium</i>, however, places his feast on 11 September.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79_5-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200870–79-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a> manuscript of the martyrology of <a href="/wiki/Ado_of_Vienne" title="Ado of Vienne">Ado of Vienne</a> also places Dagobert's death on 23 December.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGerberding1983150_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGerberding1983150-21">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dagobert's festival was never widespread outside of Stenay. Generally, it became associated with places associated with Dagobert I. It was still being celebrated at <a href="/wiki/Verdun" title="Verdun">Verdun</a> as late as the sixteenth century. The prior of Stenay was suppressed in 1580 and in 1591 the buildings were sacked by <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Protestants</a>. Dagobert's relics were dispersed, with some ending up in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Ghislain" title="Saint-Ghislain">Saint-Ghislain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFouracre200881_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFouracre200881-24">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
