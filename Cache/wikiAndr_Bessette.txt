<p><b>André Bessette</b>, <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross" title="Congregation of Holy Cross">C.S.C.</a> (9 August 1845 – 6 January 1937), more commonly known as <b>Brother André</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Frère André</i>), and since his canonization as <b>Saint André of Montreal</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross" title="Congregation of Holy Cross">Congregation of Holy Cross</a> and a significant figure of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> among <a href="/wiki/French-Canadian" class="mw-redirect" title="French-Canadian">French-Canadians</a>, credited with thousands of reported <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miraculous oil</a> <a href="/wiki/Faith_healing" title="Faith healing">healings</a> associated with his pious devotion to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bessette was declared venerable in 1978 and was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1982.<sup id="cite_ref-saint_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saint-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> approved the decree of sainthood for Bessette on 19 February 2010, with the formal <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> taking place on 17 October 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-saint_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saint-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He is the first Canadian living after <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Confederation" title="Canadian Confederation">Confederation</a> to be canonized.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Bessette&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <b>Alfred Bessette</b> in <a href="/wiki/Mont-Saint-Gr%C3%A9goire,_Quebec" title="Mont-Saint-Grégoire, Quebec">Mont-Saint-Grégoire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canada_East" title="Canada East">Canada East</a> (Québec), a small town situated 40 kilometres (25&#160;mi) southeast of <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His father, Isaac Bessette, was a carpenter and lumberman, while his mother, Clothilde Foisy Bessette, saw to the education of the children. Bessette was the eighth of 12 children, four of whom died in infancy. At birth, Bessette was so frail that the <a href="/wiki/Cur%C3%A9" class="mw-redirect" title="Curé">curé</a> baptized him "conditionally" in an emergency ritual the following day.  In 1849, with employment scarce and his family living in poverty, Bessette's father moved to Farnham, Quebec to work as a lumberman, but was shortly thereafter killed by a falling tree. Bessette was nine years old, and his mother, at 40, remained with ten children in her care. Clothilde died of <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> within three years, and Bessette became orphaned at the age of twelve.<sup id="cite_ref-DCB_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Laurent Boucher, author of the book <i>Brother André: the Miracle Man of Mount Royal,</i> Bessette was placed under the care of Timothée and Rosalie Nadeau of <a href="/wiki/Saint-C%C3%A9saire,_Quebec" title="Saint-Césaire, Quebec">Saint-Césaire, Quebec</a> following his mother's death. While with the Nadeau family, Bessette attended catechetical lessons taught by his parish's pastor, André Provençal. It was during these lessons that Bessette developed his two lifelong devotions: <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Passion_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion of Christ">Passion of Christ</a>. In June 1858, at age 12, Bessette was confirmed by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Jean-Charles_Prince" title="Jean-Charles Prince">Jean-Charles Prince</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Saint-Hyacinthe" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Saint-Hyacinthe">Diocese of Saint-Hyacinthe</a>. When Bessette was 14, the Nadeau's began to send him to school. However, Bessette was soon removed from school, having only learned to read and sign his name, both with difficulty. Timothée Nadeau intended to train Bessette as a labourer, seeing no need for an orphan to be educated. Bessette soon left the Nadeaus and was brought in by Louis Ouimet, the mayor of Saint-Césaire. While living with the Ouimet family, Bessette had a series of short-lived occupations, working as a farmer, tinsmith, blacksmith, wheelwright, cobbler, and baker, all of which Bessette was too physically weak to sustain.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Searching for work, Bessette moved to Moosup, Connecticut at the age of 18, where he joined several of his relatives in work at textile mills across Connecticut and Rhode Island.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Bessette returned to Canada in 1867 following the Canadian Confederation.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Call_to_devotion">Call to devotion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Bessette&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Call to devotion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg/170px-Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="210" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg/255px-Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg/340px-Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg 2x" data-file-width="469" data-file-height="580" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fr%C3%A8re_Andr%C3%A9_1920.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Brother André (ca. 1920)</div></div></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a> of his parish, André Provençal, noticed the devotion and generosity of the young man. He decided to present Bessette to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross" title="Congregation of Holy Cross">Congregation of Holy Cross</a> in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, writing a note to the superior, "I'm sending you a saint."<sup id="cite_ref-DCB_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Although he was initially rejected by the order because of frail health, Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Ignace_Bourget" title="Ignace Bourget">Ignace Bourget</a> of Montreal intervened on his behalf, and in 1872, Bessette was accepted and entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">congregation</a>, receiving the religious name of Brother André, by which he was known for the rest of his life.  He made his final vows on February 2, 1874, at the age of 28.
</p><p>"<i>André was given the task of <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">porter</a> at <a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_Notre-Dame_du_Sacr%C3%A9-C%C5%93ur" title="Collège Notre-Dame du Sacré-Cœur">Collège Notre-Dame</a> in <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B4te-des-Neiges" title="Côte-des-Neiges">Côte-des-Neiges</a>, Quebec, with additional duties as sacristan, laundry worker and messenger. "When I joined this community, the superiors showed me the door, and I remained 40 years," he said.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"<i>His great confidence in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a> inspired him to recommend the saint's devotion to all those who were afflicted. On his many visits to the sick in their homes, he would rub the sick person lightly with oil taken from a lamp burning in the college chapel and recommend them in prayer to Saint Joseph.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>People claimed that they had been cured through the prayers of Bessette and Saint Joseph, and they were grateful their prayers had been heard. Bessette steadfastly refused to take any credit for these cures. Bessette's desire to see Saint Joseph honoured led him in 1904 to launch a campaign to build a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> for that purpose.<sup id="cite_ref-DCB_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"<i>When an epidemic broke out at a nearby college, André volunteered to nurse. Not one person died. The trickle of sick people to his door became a flood. His superiors were uneasy; diocesan authorities were suspicious; doctors called him a quack. "I do not cure," he said again and again. "Saint Joseph cures." In the end he needed four secretaries to handle the 80,000 letters he received each year.</i>"<sup id="cite_ref-foley_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg/170px-Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg/255px-Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg/340px-Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Bessette.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Stained Glass window with Andre Bessette at Stinson Remick Chapel at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame" title="University of Notre Dame">University of Notre Dame</a></div></div></div>
<p>As tensions increased at the college with so many of the sick coming to see the porter, the school officials decided that Bessette could no longer continue with his ministry. He was permitted to receive the sick in the nearby tramway station rather than the college. As his reputation spread, Bessette became quite a controversial figure. There were many religious in the Congregation of Holy Cross, teachers and parents of students at the College who supported him but many others opposed him and even considered him dangerous to the well-being of the school's reputation because they regarded him as a charlatan. Others were concerned for the good health of the children, fearing the possibility of contagion in the school spread from diseases carried by the sick who frequented Bessette.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1924 construction of a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> named <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph%27s_Oratory" title="Saint Joseph&#39;s Oratory">Saint Joseph's Oratory</a> began on the side of the mountain, near Bessette's <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pound2005_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pound2005-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_path_to_canonization">Death and path to canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Bessette&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and path to canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Brother-andre.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Brother-andre.jpg/170px-Brother-andre.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="371" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Brother-andre.jpg/255px-Brother-andre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Brother-andre.jpg/340px-Brother-andre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="522" data-file-height="1140" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Brother-andre.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Statue_of_Andr%C3%A9_Bessette" title="Statue of André Bessette">Statue of André Bessette</a> by <a href="/wiki/Joseph-%C3%89mile_Brunet" title="Joseph-Émile Brunet">Joseph-Émile Brunet</a> on the grounds of Saint Joseph's Oratory in Montreal, QC, Canada</div></div></div>
<p>Bessette died in 1937, at the age of 91. One million people filed past his coffin.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The remains of Bessette lie in the church he helped build. His body lies in a tomb built below the Oratory's Main Chapel, except for his heart, which is preserved in a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in the same Oratory. The heart was stolen in March 1973, but was recovered in December 1974 with the help of criminal lawyer <a href="/wiki/Frank_Shoofey" title="Frank Shoofey">Frank Shoofey</a> .<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bessette was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on May 23, 1982.<sup id="cite_ref-DCB_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DCB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The miracle cited in support of his beatification was the healing in 1958 of Giuseppe Carlo Audino, who suffered from <a href="/wiki/Cancer" title="Cancer">cancer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> 
Saint Andre is commemorated in most of the world by an optional memorial on January 6.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> His memorial is celebrated in Canada on January 7.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On December 19, 2009, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> promulgated a decree recognizing a second miracle at Bessette's intercession,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and on October 17, 2010, formally declared sainthood for him. Bessette was the first saint of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross" title="Congregation of Holy Cross">Congregation of Holy Cross</a>, the same religious order that founded the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame" title="University of Notre Dame">University of Notre Dame</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_Bessette&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Andre Hall houses the School of Humanities at <a href="/wiki/St._Edward%27s_University" title="St. Edward&#39;s University">St. Edward's University</a>, a Holy Cross school, in <a href="/wiki/Austin,_Texas" title="Austin, Texas">Austin, Texas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> is named in his honour.
</p><p>There is a high school built on Fanshawe Park Rd in northwest <a href="/wiki/London,_Ontario" title="London, Ontario">London, Ontario</a> Canada called Saint Andre Bessette Secondary School.
</p><p>A program to support student learning at Notre Dame College Prep in Niles, Illinois, is named after Saint Andre. Students in the program are known as St. Andre Scholars.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is an elementary school, Brother André Catholic School, in Beacon Heights (a suburb of Ottawa), Ontario, Canada. Another elementary school in <a href="/wiki/Brampton,_Ontario" class="mw-redirect" title="Brampton, Ontario">Brampton, Ontario</a> is called St. Andre Bessette.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The French Catholic mission in York Region - North of Toronto - is named "Mission Catholique St-Frère-André". Services are held at the region's only French Catholic High School: ÉSC Renaissance.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A Roman Catholic church named St. André Bessette will be opening soon in Vaughan, Ontario. It is now under construction.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. André Bessette Parish is located in <a href="/wiki/Laconia,_New_Hampshire" title="Laconia, New Hampshire">Laconia, New Hampshire</a>; a small New England city with strong French Canadian Heritage.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. André Bessette Catholic Church is a bilingual church (Spanish, English) located in <a href="/wiki/Ecorse,_Michigan" title="Ecorse, Michigan">Ecorse, Michigan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St André Bessette Catholic Church is located in Downtown <a href="/wiki/Portland,_Oregon" title="Portland, Oregon">Portland, Oregon</a>. Its former name was Downtown Chapel.
</p><p>St André Bessette Catholic Church is a church located in Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania <a href="/wiki/Wilkes-Barre,_Pennsylvania" title="Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania">Wilkes-Barre, Pennsylvania</a>.
</p><p>There is a Catholic high school called St. André Bessette Catholic High School in Fort Saskatchewan, Alberta, serving students in grades 9-12.
</p><p>St. André French Immersion Catholic Elementary School in <a href="/wiki/Tecumseh,_Ontario" title="Tecumseh, Ontario">Tecumseh, Ontario</a> was opened in 2015 by the <a href="/wiki/Windsor-Essex_Catholic_District_School_Board" title="Windsor-Essex Catholic District School Board">Windsor-Essex Catholic District School Board</a>.
</p><p>A mission station in Cadiz City, Philippines was named in his honor.
</p><p>André House is a ministry to the homeless and poor populations of the Phoenix area.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brother André's Café, a coffee shop housed at Epiphany Roman Catholic Church in Pittsburgh, PA, employs workers with intellectual and developmental disabilities.
</p>
