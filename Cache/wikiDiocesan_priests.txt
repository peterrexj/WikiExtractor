					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Diocesan_priests&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocesan priests">Diocesan priests</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Deacons and priests who are not members of religious orders</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">


</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, the term <b>secular clergy</b> refers to <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacons</a> and <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priests</a> who are not <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monastics</a> or otherwise members of <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">religious life</a>. <b>Secular priests</b> (sometimes known as <b>diocesan priests</b>) are <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priests</a> who commit themselves to a certain geographical area and are <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> into the service of the residents of a <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or equivalent church administrative region. That includes serving the everyday needs of the people in <a href="/wiki/Parish_(Catholic_Church)" title="Parish (Catholic Church)">parishes</a>, but their activities are not limited to that of their parish.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Etymology_and_terminology">Etymology and terminology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Secular_clergy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Etymology and terminology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin word</a> <i lang="la"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/saeculum#Latin" class="extiw" title="wikt:saeculum">saeculum</a></i> referred to a period of time roughly equivalent to 100 years. It forms the basis of the word for <i><a href="/wiki/Century" title="Century">century</a></i> in <a href="/wiki/Romance_languages" title="Romance languages">Romance languages</a> (e.g., <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> <i lang="fr"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/si%C3%A8cle#French" class="extiw" title="wikt:siècle">siècle</a></i>, or <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a> <i lang="it"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/secolo#Italian" class="extiw" title="wikt:secolo">secolo</a></i>). <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Christianity</a> adopted the term in <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_Latin" title="Ecclesiastical Latin">Ecclesiastical Latin</a> to refer to matters of an earthly and temporal, as opposed to a <a href="/wiki/Heaven_in_Christianity" title="Heaven in Christianity">heavenly</a> and eternal, nature.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In the 12th century, the term came to apply to priests obligated with parochial and ministerial duties rather than the "regular" duties of monastic clergy who were bound to the rule of a <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">religious order</a>, often called "<a href="/wiki/Religious_(Western_Christianity)" title="Religious (Western Christianity)">religious clergy</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While the term "diocesan priest" is often used to describe secular priests, not all secular priests are diocesan. In the Latin Church, other territorial and non-territorial ecclesial jurisdictions such as the <a href="/wiki/Personal_prelature" title="Personal prelature">personal prelature</a> of <a href="/wiki/Opus_Dei" title="Opus Dei">Opus Dei</a>, <a href="/wiki/Military_ordinariate" title="Military ordinariate">military</a> and <a href="/wiki/Personal_ordinariate" title="Personal ordinariate">personal ordinariates</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_vicariate" title="Apostolic vicariate">apostolic vicariates</a> all include secular clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglicanism</a>, and other contexts, the term "secular priest" does not delineate between celibate and non-celibate priests.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Latin Church <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon law</a> makes specific demands on clergy, whether regular or secular, quite apart from the obligations consequent to religious vows. Thus in the Latin Church, among other regulations, clerics other than permanent deacons "are obliged to observe perfect and perpetual continence for the sake of the kingdom of heaven and therefore are bound to <a href="/wiki/Celibacy" title="Celibacy">celibacy</a>"<sup id="cite_ref-auto_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and to carry out the <a href="/wiki/Canonical_hours" title="Canonical hours">canonical hours</a>, typically according to the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Liturgy of the Hours</a>, daily.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> They are forbidden to "assume public offices which entail a participation in the exercise of civil power."<sup id="cite_ref-auto_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> All clerics, once ordained, are forbidden from marrying or remarrying.
</p><p>The teachings of the Catholic Church and some scholars hold that a tradition of clerical <i>continence</i> existed in early Christianity, whereby married men who became priests were expected to abstain from sexual relations with their wives.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Boniveto_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boniveto-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>   The <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Elvira" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Elvira">Council of Elvira</a>, held before <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Constantine I">Constantine</a> legalized Christianity, made it an explicit law that bishops and other clergy should not have sexual relations with their wives. Despite consistently upholding the doctrine of clerical celibacy, over the following centuries the Church experienced many difficulties in enforcing it, particularly in rural areas of Europe.  Finally, in the 12th century the Western Church declared that <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> were not merely a prohibitive but a <a href="/wiki/Canonical_impediment" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonical impediment">diriment canonical impediment</a> to marriage, making marriage by priests invalid and not merely forbidden.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The secular clergy, in which the hierarchy essentially resides, takes precedence over the regular clergy of equal rank. The episcopal office was the primary source of authority in the Church, and the secular clergy arose to assist the bishop. Only bishops can ordain Catholic clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One root of the 1896 <a href="/wiki/Philippine_Revolution" title="Philippine Revolution">Philippine Revolution</a> was the agitation of native secular priests for parish assignments. Priests of powerful religious orders were given preferential treatment in these assignments and were usually Spaniards who trained in European <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">chapters</a>. The agitation led to the execution of the "<a href="/wiki/Gomburza" title="Gomburza">Gomburza</a> <i>filibusteros</i>", under charges of involvement in the 1872 <a href="/wiki/Cavite_Mutiny" class="mw-redirect" title="Cavite Mutiny">Cavite Mutiny</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Thomas_Becket" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Thomas Becket">St. Thomas Becket</a> is a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of secular clergy. <a href="/wiki/St._John_Vianney" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John Vianney">St. John Vianney</a> is patron saint of parish priests. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen" title="Saint Stephen">St. Stephen</a> is patron saint of deacons.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Preparation">Preparation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Secular_clergy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Preparation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Preparation for Catholic priesthood generally requires eight years of study beyond high school, usually including a college degree followed by four or more years of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> study at a <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the time of their ordination as <a href="/wiki/Deacon#Latin_Catholicism" title="Deacon">deacons</a> (usually about a year before their ordination as priests) they promise respect and <a href="/wiki/Vow_of_obedience" title="Vow of obedience">obedience</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Diocesan_bishop" title="Diocesan bishop">diocesan bishop</a> and his successors. They also promise to live in <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_counsels" title="Evangelical counsels">chastity</a>, and according to the status of clergy (which includes a comparatively simple life). Diocesan priests do make vows, and must remain celibate and adhere to Canon law, but they do not promise <a href="/wiki/Poverty" title="Poverty">poverty</a>, so they may own their own property, such as cars, and handle their own financial affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Liturgical_responsibilities">Liturgical responsibilities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Secular_clergy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Liturgical responsibilities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In his <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_letter" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic letter">apostolic letter</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Dies_Domini" title="Dies Domini">Dies Domini</a></i>, Pope <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> wrote: "Among the many activities of a parish, none is as vital or as community-forming as the Sunday celebration of the Lord's Day and his Eucharist".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A diocesan priest spends much of his time preparing for and celebrating the <a href="/wiki/Sacrament" title="Sacrament">Sacraments</a> (<a href="/wiki/Eucharist_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Eucharist in the Catholic Church">Eucharist</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sacrament_of_Penance_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacrament of Penance (Catholic Church)">Reconciliation</a>, <a href="/wiki/Baptism#Catholicism" title="Baptism">Baptism</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marriage_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Marriage (Catholic Church)">Marriage</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anointing_of_the_Sick_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Anointing of the Sick (Catholic Church)">Anointing of the Sick</a>, <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Catholic Church)">Confirmation)</a>. In the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Dogmatic_Constitution&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Dogmatic Constitution (page does not exist)">Dogmatic Constitution</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Lumen_gentium" title="Lumen gentium">Lumen gentium</a></i>, the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a> teaches that the priest acting <i>in persona Christi</i> celebrates the Sacrifice of the Mass and administers the Sacraments. "Christ is also present through preaching and the guidance of the faithful, tasks to which the priest is personally called."<sup id="cite_ref-va_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are many parishioners whom he visits, those who are ill, those who are dying, and those who are unable to travel outside their homes.  Sometimes, he is directly involved in the <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechetical</a> work of the parish and teaches catechism classes. He works with parish and finance councils that assist him in overseeing the welfare of the parish.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Diocesan priests may serve in myriad different capacities, these services include, but are not limited to, campus ministry, teaching, and chaplain work for hospitals or prisons.
</p>

<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, the term "secular clergy" refers to married priests and deacons, as opposed to <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monastic</a> clergy (<a href="/wiki/Hieromonk" title="Hieromonk">hieromonks</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hierodeacon" title="Hierodeacon">hierodeacons</a>). The secular clergy are sometimes referred to as "white clergy", black being the customary colour worn by monks.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Traditionally, <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> priests are expected to be secular clergy rather than monastics, as the support of a wife is considered necessary for a priest living "in the world".
</p><p>Since there are no orders like Catholic ones, all clergy in Eastern Orthodoxy, secular and monastic, are diocesan.
</p>
