<p><b>Pelagia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Πελαγία</span></span>), distinguished as <b>Pelagia of Antioch</b>, <b>Pelagia the Penitent</b>, and <b>Pelagia the Harlot</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">Christian saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in the 4th or 5th century. Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was celebrated on 8 October, originally in common with Saints <a href="/wiki/Pelagia_the_Virgin" title="Pelagia the Virgin">Pelagia the Virgin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pelagia_of_Tarsus" title="Pelagia of Tarsus">Pelagia of Tarsus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;n 1&#93;</a></sup> Pelagia died as a result of extreme <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>, which had emaciated her to the point she could no longer be recognized. According to Orthodox tradition, she was buried in her cell. Upon the discovery that the renowned monk had been a woman, the holy fathers tried to keep it a secret, but the gossip spread and her relics drew pilgrims from as far off as <a href="/wiki/Jericho" title="Jericho">Jericho</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Jordan_River" title="Jordan River">Jordan valley</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pelagia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pelagia's story is attributed to James<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoon1997&#91;httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidw_1-t_K2ZuMCpgPA78_78&#93;_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoon1997[httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidw_1-t_K2ZuMCpgPA78_78]-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> or Jacob<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECameron201685_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECameron201685-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Jacobus</i>), <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> of the church of <a href="/wiki/Heliopolis_(Syria)" class="mw-redirect" title="Heliopolis (Syria)">Heliopolis</a> (modern <a href="/wiki/Baalbek" title="Baalbek">Baalbek</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBunson_&amp;_al.2003-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He states that <b>Margarita</b> was the "foremost actress" and a prominent <a href="/wiki/Prostitution_in_ancient_Rome" title="Prostitution in ancient Rome">harlot</a> in <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> During one of the <a href="/wiki/Synods_of_Antioch" title="Synods of Antioch">city's church councils</a>, she passed by on a donkey surrounded by her entourage and a "worldly crowd".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Perfumed and "immodestly bareheaded", the outlines of her body were "clearly visible" beneath her <a href="/wiki/Gold_cloth" class="mw-redirect" title="Gold cloth">gold cloth</a>, pearls, and precious stones, which ran from her bare shoulders to her feet. Most of the fathers were shamed into looking away, but the bishop <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nonnus" title="Saint Nonnus">Nonnus</a> stared openly and confessed himself "delighted".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Mocking his fellows, he condemned both them and himself for taking less care of their <a href="/wiki/Soul" title="Soul">souls</a> than she had of her body.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She appeared at his next Sunday <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermon</a> and Nonnus's sermon on hell and the goodness of paradise prompted her to repent. She had two of her slaves trail Nonnus to his residence and then wrote him on wax tablets, calling herself "sinful" and a "servant of the devil" but seeking mercy from God, who "came down to earth not for the sake of the righteous but to save sinners".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Nonnus replied to the anonymous request that God knew her and her past and that he would receive her, but only in the presence of the other bishops.
</p><p>She went to the <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> of <a href="/wiki/Julian_of_Antioch" title="Julian of Antioch">Saint Julian</a> to see them; when Nonnus demanded surety that she would not return to her former life, she fell to the ground and threatened that if she were refused admission to the Church, all her future sins would be held against him at his judgment. The archbishop was informed and sent the <a href="/wiki/Deaconess" title="Deaconess">deaconess</a> Romana to clothe her in the <a href="/wiki/Baptismal_gown" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptismal gown">baptismal gown</a>. Nonnus took her confession and baptized "Margarita" under her birth name Pelagia, with Romana serving as her <a href="/wiki/Godparent" title="Godparent">godmother</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The devil shortly afterward arrived to complain, but was driven off when Pelagia made the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Sign of the Cross">Sign of the Cross</a> and breathed upon him. He returned the next night to renew his complaints and was driven off the same way. On the third day, Pelagia directed her <a href="/wiki/Steward_(office)" title="Steward (office)">steward</a> to inventory her possessions. She then turned them over to Nonnus, who disbursed them to the widows, orphans, and poor of the city. She <a href="/wiki/Manumission" title="Manumission">freed her slaves</a>, male and female, "taking their golden <a href="/wiki/Torc" title="Torc">torcs</a> off with her own hands".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She then began living with Romana.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The night before it came time to remove her baptismal gown, she stole out in the dark wearing one of Nonnus's <a href="/wiki/Chiton_(costume)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chiton (costume)">chitons</a>. She headed for <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, where she built a <a href="/wiki/Cell_(monk)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cell (monk)">cell</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Mount_of_Olives" title="Mount of Olives">Mount of Olives</a>. She lived there for three or four years, disguising herself as a male <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">recluse</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eunuch" title="Eunuch">eunuch</a> under the name <b>Pelagi<em>us</em></b>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She then died, apparently as a result of extreme <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>, which had emaciated her to the point she could no longer be recognized. According to Orthodox tradition, Pelagia was buried in her cell.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Upon the discovery that the renowned monk had been a woman, the "holy fathers" tried to keep it a secret, but the gossip spread and her relics drew pilgrims from as far off as <a href="/wiki/Jericho" title="Jericho">Jericho</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Jordan_River" title="Jordan River">Jordan valley</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon_6-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames_the_Deacon-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The story appeared in the <i><a href="/wiki/Menaea_Graeca" title="Menaea Graeca">Greek Menaea</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1866_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1866-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It significantly omits dates and (on eight occasions) the name of the archbishop under whom Nonnus served.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECameron201687_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECameron201687-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;n 2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pelagia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Pelagia_the_Virgin" title="Pelagia the Virgin">historical St&#160;Pelagia</a>, mentioned by <a href="/wiki/St_Ambrose" class="mw-redirect" title="St Ambrose">St&#160;Ambrose</a><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> and in two sermons by <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-chrys_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chrys-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> was an <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antiochene</a> <a href="/wiki/Holy_virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy virgin">virgin</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyred</a> because of her refusal to offer <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Roman_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Roman religion">pagan</a> <a href="/wiki/Sacrifice" title="Sacrifice">sacrifice</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>. Chrysostom's <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;390</span> sermon also mentions an anonymous (but apparently famous) actress and prostitute "from a wicked city in <a href="/wiki/Phoenice" title="Phoenice">Phoenice</a>" (possibly <a href="/wiki/Baalbek#History" title="Baalbek">Heliopolis</a>) who seduced "the empress's brother" but converted "in our own day".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECameron201686–87_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECameron201686–87-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a>'s wife <a href="/wiki/Eusebia_(empress)" title="Eusebia (empress)">Eusebia</a> had two brothers, <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hypatius_(consul_359)" title="Hypatius (consul 359)">Hypatius</a>, joint <a href="/wiki/Roman_consul" title="Roman consul">consuls</a> in 359, who both lived for many years in Antioch.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECameron201687–88_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECameron201687–88-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In his account, attempts were made to lure her back to her former life by the Roman prefect and some of his soldiers,<sup id="cite_ref-chrys_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chrys-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> a role played by Satan in the hagiography.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECameron201686_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECameron201686-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Similar_other_accounts">Similar other accounts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pelagia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Similar other accounts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Marina_the_Monk" title="Marina the Monk">Saint Marina</a>, the Latin equivalent of "Pelagia", was another bride who disguised herself as a monk, in her case to escape an unwanted marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECameron201687_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECameron201687-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;n 3&#93;</a></sup> Aspects of their stories were apparently combined with <a href="/wiki/New_Testament_apocrypha" title="New Testament apocrypha">apocryphal</a> accounts of <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoon1997&#91;httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidw_1-t_K2ZuMCpgPA77_77–78&#93;_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoon1997[httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidw_1-t_K2ZuMCpgPA77_77–78]-22">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Biblical</a> accounts of <a href="/wiki/Solomon" title="Solomon">Solomon</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_Sheba" title="Queen of Sheba">Queen of Sheba</a> and of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a> and various women in the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoon1997&#91;httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidw_1-t_K2ZuMCpgPA80_80–82&#93;_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoon1997[httpsbooksgooglecojpbooksidw_1-t_K2ZuMCpgPA80_80–82]-23">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
