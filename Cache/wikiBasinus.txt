					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Basinus&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Basinus">Basinus</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">5th-century Thuringian king</div>
<p><b>Bisinus</b> (sometimes shortened to <b>Bisin</b>) was the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Thuringia" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Thuringia">king of Thuringia</a> in the 5th century AD or around 500. He is the earliest historically attested ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Thuringians" class="mw-redirect" title="Thuringians">Thuringians</a>. Almost nothing more about him can be said with certainty, including whether all the variations on his name in the sources refer to one or two different persons. His name is given as Bysinus, Bessinus or Bissinus in <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Frankish</a> sources, and as Pissa, Pisen, Fisud or Fisut in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards" title="Kingdom of the Lombards">Lombard</a> ones.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWeddige198910_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeddige198910-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bisinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bisinus was the first husband of <a href="/wiki/Menia" title="Menia">Menia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009281_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009281-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> a fact attested only in the  9th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Langobardorum_codicis_Gothani" title="Historia Langobardorum codicis Gothani">Historia Langobardorum codicis Gothani</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGrierson194119n_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrierson194119n-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had a daughter, <a href="/wiki/Raicunda" title="Raicunda">Raicunda</a>, who became the first wife of the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombard</a> king <a href="/wiki/Wacho" title="Wacho">Wacho</a> (c.&#8201;510–540),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009279_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009279-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> a fact attested in all three of the main Lombard chronicles (two of which specify that he was king of the Thuringians).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Menia later married a man (unnamed in the sources) of the <a href="/wiki/Gausian_dynasty" title="Gausian dynasty">Gausus family</a> and became the mother of <a href="/wiki/Audoin" title="Audoin">Audoin</a>, who in 540 became the regent of Wacho's son by his third wife, <a href="/wiki/Walthari" title="Walthari">Walthari</a>, and then succeeded him to the throne in 546.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009281_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009281-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bisinus was also the father of the three brothers who ruled Thuringia in the 520s and 530s: <a href="/wiki/Hermanafrid" title="Hermanafrid">Hermanafrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bertachar" title="Bertachar">Bertachar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Baderich" class="mw-redirect" title="Baderich">Baderich</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009288,_contains_a_family_tree._9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009288,_contains_a_family_tree.-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Bertachar had a daughter, <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Radegund</a>, who founded <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey_(Poitiers)" title="Holy Cross Abbey (Poitiers)">Holy Cross Abbey</a> in Poitiers and was recognised as a saint. She died in 587. Two hagiographies of her were produced by her friends <a href="/wiki/Baudovinia" title="Baudovinia">Baudovinia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalsall2001125_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalsall2001125-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009283–84_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009283–84-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Fortunatus specifies that she was "from the Thuringian region", a daughter of King Bertachar and granddaughter of King Bisinus.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While most scholars accept that the Thuringian kings called Bisinus in the Frankish sources and Pissa in the Lombard ones are one and the same, Martina Hartmann rejects the identification and points out that the <i><a href="/wiki/Prosopography_of_the_Later_Roman_Empire" title="Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire">Prosopography of the Later Roman Empire</a></i> makes no such identification either.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHartmann200962,_yet_see_p._13,_where_she_rejects_Bisinus&#39;_marriage_to_Basina_on_the_grounds_that_he_was_the_husband_of_Menia_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHartmann200962,_yet_see_p._13,_where_she_rejects_Bisinus&#39;_marriage_to_Basina_on_the_grounds_that_he_was_the_husband_of_Menia-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Gregory_of_Tours">Gregory of Tours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bisinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Gregory of Tours">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:MajorianEmpire.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/MajorianEmpire.png/220px-MajorianEmpire.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="250" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/MajorianEmpire.png/330px-MajorianEmpire.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/MajorianEmpire.png/440px-MajorianEmpire.png 2x" data-file-width="1220" data-file-height="1385" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:MajorianEmpire.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Roman Empire under Majorian, with Frankish Kingdom and Thuringians.</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, writing in the last quarter of the 6th century, says that when the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a> rebelled against their king, <a href="/wiki/Childeric_I" title="Childeric I">Childeric I</a>, who was accused of seducing their daughters, he went into exile at the court of Bisinus in Thuringia for eight years. In his absence, the Franks elected the Roman commander <a href="/wiki/Aegidius" title="Aegidius">Aegidius</a> as their king.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames201480_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames201480-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Childeric's exile corresponds to the period between Aegidius' appointment as <a href="/wiki/Magister_militum_per_Gallias" class="mw-redirect" title="Magister militum per Gallias"><i>magister militum</i> for Gaul</a> in 456 or 457 and his death in 465.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames201480_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames201480-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartindale1980244,_dates_the_exile_to_c.&amp;thinsp;456–c.&amp;thinsp;464_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartindale1980244,_dates_the_exile_to_c.&amp;thinsp;456–c.&amp;thinsp;464-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> When Childeric returned from exile, Bisinus' wife <a href="/wiki/Basina,_Queen_of_Thuringia" class="mw-redirect" title="Basina, Queen of Thuringia">Basina</a> abandoned her husband to go with him. She became his wife and the mother of <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames201480_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames201480-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Gregory does not describe Bisinus as king of the Thuringians or even as a Thuringian himself, but as king "in Thuringia".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENeumeister201490:_&#39;&#39;in_Thuringiam_..._regem_Bysinum&#39;&#39;_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENeumeister201490:_&#39;&#39;in_Thuringiam_..._regem_Bysinum&#39;&#39;-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Gregory's account was used by the authors of the 7th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Fredegar" title="Chronicle of Fredegar">Chronicle of Fredegar</a></i><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and the 8th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Historiae_Francorum" title="Liber Historiae Francorum">Book of the History of the Franks</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Scholars have been skeptical of Gregory's account. It has been called a "folk tale", albeit one that corresponds well chronologically to the dates of Aegidius' magistracy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJames201480_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJames201480-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> It has been suggested that Gregory in fact confused the <a href="/wiki/Civitas_Tungrorum" title="Civitas Tungrorum">civitas Tungrorum</a> (today <a href="/wiki/Tongeren" title="Tongeren">Tongeren</a>) in northern Gaul with Thuringia and that Childeric was exiled to Tongeren. It has even been suggested that Gregory, knowing only that the name of Clovis' mother was Basina and that an early king of Thuringia was named Bisinus, invented the relationship between Basina and Bisinus based on the similarity of their names (having already, possibly erroneously, presumed the location of exile to be Thuringia).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalsall2001125_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalsall2001125-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Although most scholars accept Childeric's exile as historical, Berthold Schmidt rejected Gregory's entire account of it as a fiction.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalsall2010196_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalsall2010196-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is highly unlikely (but not impossible) that the Bisinus of Gregory and the Bisinus of the Lombard chronicles are one and the same.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009281_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009281-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In that case Bisinus would have been married to Basina almost eighty years before his youngest son's death in battle and had a living grandchild in 587.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalsall2010196_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalsall2010196-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMladjov2014564,_presents_a_genealogical_reconstruction_in_which_Bisinus_married_first_Basina_and_had_his_sons_by_her_before_marrying_Menia,_by_whom_he_had_Radegund,_who_is_presented_as_his_daughter_rather_than_granddaughter_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMladjov2014564,_presents_a_genealogical_reconstruction_in_which_Bisinus_married_first_Basina_and_had_his_sons_by_her_before_marrying_Menia,_by_whom_he_had_Radegund,_who_is_presented_as_his_daughter_rather_than_granddaughter-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Taking Gregory's account as historical, it has been suggested therefore that there were in fact two kings named Bisinus. Bisinus (II), husband of Menia, may then have been the nephew of Bisinus (I), husband of Basina.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009288,_contains_a_family_tree._9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJarnut2009288,_contains_a_family_tree.-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The alternative is that Gregory misused the name of the historical Bisinus, husband of Menia and grandfather of Radegund, in reconstructing the events of the 460s.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalsall2001125_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalsall2001125-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Location_of_kingdom">Location of kingdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bisinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Location of kingdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The location of Bisinus' kingdom is a matter of some debate. Usually it is located in the place of present-day <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a>, well to the east of the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>. An artefact that may be associated with Basina was found in the vicinity of <a href="/wiki/Weimar" title="Weimar">Weimar</a>: a silver ladle engraved with the name Basena that may date to the 5th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood199437–40_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood199437–40-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The heartland of 5th-century Thuringia, however, may have initially been west of the Rhine, with the kingdom only expanding eastward in the decades after Bisinus' reign. The 12th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_de_compositione_castri_Ambaziae_et_ipsius_dominorum_gesta" title="Liber de compositione castri Ambaziae et ipsius dominorum gesta">Liber de compositione castri Ambaziae et ipsius dominorum gesta</a></i> records that Bisinus' territory lay on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Saône">Saône</a> between <a href="/wiki/Toul" title="Toul">Toul</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>. It also refers to Bisinus as a <i><a href="/wiki/Dux" title="Dux">dux</a></i> (duke) only and not as a king.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENeumeister201488:_&#39;&#39;Bissinus_iste_terram_suam_super_Sunnam_fluvium,_qui_alio_nomine_Arar_dictur,_a_Tullo_usque_Lugdunum_possidebat&#39;&#39;_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENeumeister201488:_&#39;&#39;Bissinus_iste_terram_suam_super_Sunnam_fluvium,_qui_alio_nomine_Arar_dictur,_a_Tullo_usque_Lugdunum_possidebat&#39;&#39;-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
