<p><b>Zosimas of Palestine</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ζωσιμᾶς</span>) (<a href="/wiki/Palestinian_Arabic" title="Palestinian Arabic">Palestinian Arabic</a>:  زوسيموس الفلسطيني), is commemorated as a Palestinian saint. His feast day is on the 4 of April.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Zosimas_of_Palestine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Zosimas was born in the second half of the fifth century, during the reign of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodosius the Younger">Theodosius II</a>. He became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> in a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a> at a very young age, gaining a reputation as a great <a href="/wiki/Starets" title="Starets">elder</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a>. At the age of fifty-three, now a <a href="/wiki/Hieromonk" title="Hieromonk">hieromonk</a>, he moved to a very strict monastery located in the wilderness close to the <a href="/wiki/Jordan_River" title="Jordan River">Jordan River</a>, where he spent the remainder of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is best known for his encounter with <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Egypt" title="Mary of Egypt">Mary of Egypt</a> (commemorated on April 1). It was the custom of that monastery for all of the brethren to go out into the desert for the forty days of <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> spending the time in fasting and prayer, and not returning until <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a>. While wandering in the desert he met Mary, who told him her life story and asked him to meet her the next year on <a href="/wiki/Holy_Thursday" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Thursday">Holy Thursday</a> on the banks of the Jordan, in order to bring her <a href="/wiki/Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Communion">Holy Communion</a>. He did so, and the third year came to her again in the desert, but he found that she had died and he buried her. Zosimas is reputed to have lived to be almost one hundred years of age.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>All that we know of Zosimas' life comes from the <i>Vita of St. Mary of Egypt</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> recorded by <a href="/wiki/Sophronius_of_Jerusalem" title="Sophronius of Jerusalem">Sophronius</a>, who was the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Orthodox Patriarch of Jerusalem">Patriarch of Jerusalem</a> from 634 to 638. Sophronius based his work on <a href="/wiki/Oral_tradition" title="Oral tradition">oral tradition</a> he had heard from monks in Palestine. This <i>Vita</i> is traditionally read as a part of the <a href="/wiki/Matins" title="Matins">Matins</a> of the Great <a href="/wiki/Canon_(hymnography)" title="Canon (hymnography)">Canon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Andrew_of_Crete" title="Andrew of Crete">Andrew of Crete</a>, on the fifth Thursday of Great Lent.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The story shares many similarities with one recorded in the <a href="/wiki/Western_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Western church">Western church</a> as a story of <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>, with Zosimas renamed as <a href="/wiki/Maximinus_of_Aix" title="Maximinus of Aix">Maximin</a>, as recounted in the <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i> and elsewhere.  The <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a> illustrated by <a href="/wiki/Giotto" title="Giotto">Giotto</a> and his workshop in <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>, shows this version.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
