<p><b>Maximus the Greek</b>, also known as <b>Maximos the Greek</b> or <b>Maksim Grek</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Μάξιμος ὁ Γραικός</span>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Максим Грек</span>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1475</span>–1556), was a Greek <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, publicist, writer, scholar, and translator active in Russia.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-McGuckin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGuckin-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He is also called <b>Maximos the Hagiorite</b> (<span title="Greek-language text"><span lang="el">Μάξιμος ὁ Ἁγιορίτης</span></span>),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> as well as <b>Maximus the Philosopher</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-Arans_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arans-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His signature was <i>Maximus Grecus Lakedaimon</i> (l<small>it.</small> Maximus the Greek of, and originating from, <a href="/wiki/Lakedaimonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Lakedaimonia">Lakedaimonia</a>) and his family origins were probably from <a href="/wiki/Mystras" title="Mystras">Mystras</a>, a location in <a href="/wiki/Laconia" title="Laconia">Laconia</a>, which was the geographical site of <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Sparta" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Sparta">Ancient Sparta</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Peloponnese" title="Peloponnese">Peloponnese</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_the_Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Maximus was born <b>Michael Trivolis</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Μιχαήλ Τριβώλης</span>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Михаил Триволис</span>) <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1475</span> in <a href="/wiki/Arta,_Greece" title="Arta, Greece">Arta</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>, the scion of a noble Greek family with ties to the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors" title="List of Byzantine emperors">Byzantine emperor</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-McGuckin_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McGuckin-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Arans_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arans-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Medlin_and_Patrinelis_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Medlin_and_Patrinelis-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and originating from <a href="/wiki/Sparta" title="Sparta">Sparta</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Both Maximus's parents were Christian <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greeks</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> his mother was Irene and his father, Manuel, was a <a href="/wiki/Voivode" title="Voivode">voivode</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Irene and Manuel left Constantinople together for Arta and the latter may have been a Byzantine military governor of Arta before the city fell to the Ottomans in 1449.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Demetrios Trivolis, Manuel's brother and Maximus's uncle, was a Greek <a href="/wiki/Scribe" title="Scribe">scribe</a> who self-identified as "a Peloponnesian from Sparta" (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πελοποννήσιος εκ Σπάρτης</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-Pagani_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pagani-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Kapodistrias_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kapodistrias-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ammonius_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ammonius-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> In 1461–1462, Demetrios reproduced a manuscript of Plato's <i><a href="/wiki/Timaeus_(dialogue)" title="Timaeus (dialogue)">Timaeus</a></i> while in <a href="/wiki/Corfu" title="Corfu">Corfu</a> and in 1465, he copied <a href="/wiki/Plotinus" title="Plotinus">Plotinus</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Enneads" title="Enneads">Enneads</a></i> while in <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-Pagani_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pagani-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> after his completion of Plato's work, Demetrius wrote the following note: "<span title="Greek-language text"><span lang="el">Η βίβλος ἥδε ἐγράφη δι᾿οἰκείας χειρὸς ἐμοῦ Δημητρίου Τριβώλη Πελοποννησίου ἐκ Σπάρτης τὰς διατριβὰς ποιοῦντος ἐν Κερκυραίων νήσῳ · μετὰ τὴν τῆς ἡμετέρας πατρίδος ἅλωσιν</span></span>" which translates as: "The present book was written by my own hand of Demetrios Trivolis Peloponnesian from Sparta who made these works in the island of the <a href="/wiki/Corcyraeans" class="mw-redirect" title="Corcyraeans">Kerkyreans</a> after the fall of our fatherland" (referencing the fall of his native <a href="/wiki/Laconia" title="Laconia">Laconia</a> to the Ottomans).<sup id="cite_ref-Kapodistrias_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kapodistrias-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In another note, Demetrios writes: "<span title="Greek-language text"><span lang="el">κάλλιστον βιβλίον εμόν εστι κτήμα Δημητρίου Τριβώλη Πελοποννησίου εκ Σπαρτης. επριαμην δε και τουτο μετα την εμης πατριδος αλωσιν της Λακεδαιμονος της ποτ' ευδαιμονος.</span></span>" which translates as: "This most beautiful book of mine is property of Demetrios Trivolis Peloponnesian from Sparta. I bought it after the fall of our fatherland Lacedaemonia, which was once fortunate."<sup id="cite_ref-Ammonius_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ammonius-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The Trivolis family was later associated with the <a href="/wiki/Palaiologos_dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Palaiologos dynasty">Palaiologos dynasty</a> in Mystras.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maximus studied on the island of <a href="/wiki/Corfu" title="Corfu">Corfu</a> under the supervision of John Moschos and <a href="/wiki/Janus_Lascaris" title="Janus Lascaris">John Lascaris</a> and later went with Lascaris to Florence (in 1492<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas-Chesworth_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas-Chesworth-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> or 1493<sup id="cite_ref-Medlin_and_Patrinelis_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Medlin_and_Patrinelis-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>) and continued his studies in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ferrara" title="Ferrara">Ferrara</a>, Milan, <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Speake_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Speake-37">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Vakalopoulos_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vakalopoulos-38">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> While in Italy, he studied ancient languages, as well as ecclesiastic and philosophic works (especially <a href="/wiki/Neoplatonism" title="Neoplatonism">Neoplatonism</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Vakalopoulos_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vakalopoulos-38">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> He knew prominent figures of the <a href="/wiki/Renaissance" title="Renaissance">Renaissance</a> era such as the <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venetian</a> <a href="/wiki/Printer_(publisher)" class="mw-redirect" title="Printer (publisher)">printer</a> <a href="/wiki/Aldus_Manutius" title="Aldus Manutius">Aldus Manutius</a> and made the acquaintance of scholars <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Poliziano" class="mw-redirect" title="Angelo Poliziano">Angelo Poliziano</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marsilio_Ficino" title="Marsilio Ficino">Marsilio Ficino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pico_della_Mirandola" class="mw-redirect" title="Pico della Mirandola">Pico della Mirandola</a>, Scipio Callerges, and Fonteguerri.<sup id="cite_ref-Medlin_and_Patrinelis_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Medlin_and_Patrinelis-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Speake_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Speake-37">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Maximus was also greatly influenced by the preachings of the fiery <a href="/wiki/Dominican_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominican order">Dominican</a> priest and reformer <a href="/wiki/Girolamo_Savonarola" title="Girolamo Savonarola">Girolamo Savonarola</a> whose ashes he gathered in 1498.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_the_Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1504 (according to other accounts, 1505 or early 1506<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>), Maximus left the Dominican monastery of St. Mark and went to <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> where he took monastic vows at the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Vatopedi" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Vatopedi">Monastery of Vatopedi</a> in 1507.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> In 1515, <a href="/wiki/Grand_Prince" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Prince">Grand Prince</a> <a href="/wiki/Vasili_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Vasili III">Vasili III</a> asked the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the monastery to send him a certain <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> by the name of Savva to translate a number of religious texts.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> Savva was so old that the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> decided to send the energetic Maximus instead, though he had no knowledge of the <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Church Slavonic language">Church Slavonic language</a>. Nevertheless, the monks vouched for him, and he went to Moscow, where he was met with great honor.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> Upon arriving in Moscow in 1518, Maximus headed the movement of religious reform.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> In 1518, Maximus met Prince Kurbskii, who later wrote about Maximus and his time in Moscow in <i>Skazanie o Maksime Filosofe</i> (The Tale of Maxim the Philosopher).<sup id="cite_ref-Arans_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arans-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In this work, Kurbskii describes a meeting between Maximus and Vasili III whereupon Maximus was astonished at the countless multitudes of Greek books displayed at Vasili III's court.<sup id="cite_ref-Arans_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arans-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Maximus assured the Prince that he had never seen so many Greek works in Greece itself.<sup id="cite_ref-Arans_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arans-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This testimony is the earliest known reference of a collection of ancient manuscripts belonging to the Russian Tsars which has never been found, also referred to as <i><a href="/wiki/The_Lost_Library_of_the_Moscow_Tsars" class="mw-redirect" title="The Lost Library of the Moscow Tsars">The Lost Library of the Moscow Tsars</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Arans_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Arans-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This lost library later became a favorite research topic of early twentieth century Russian archaeologist <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_Stelletskii" class="mw-redirect" title="Ignatius Stelletskii">Ignatius Stelletskii</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Assignment_to_Moscow">Assignment to Moscow</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_the_Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Assignment to Moscow">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Maximus's first major work in Russia was a translation of the <a href="/wiki/Psalter" title="Psalter">Psalter</a> together with the Russian translators (including the scholar <a href="/wiki/Dmitry_Gerasimov" title="Dmitry Gerasimov">Dmitry Gerasimov</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Scrivener" title="Scrivener">scriveners</a>, which would be solemnly approved by the Russian clergy and the grand prince himself. After <a href="/wiki/Vasili_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Vasili III">Vasili III</a> rejected his request to go home, Maximus continued to work on translations and correcting the books for <a href="/wiki/Divine_Service_(Eastern_Orthodoxy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Divine Service (Eastern Orthodoxy)">divine service</a>. Observing the "defects" and injustices of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow" title="Grand Duchy of Moscow">Muscovite</a> life, which seemed to him in direct opposition to his Christian ideals, Maximus began to expose them and criticize the authorities, attracting different people with similar views, such as <a href="/wiki/Ivan_Bersen-Beklemishev" title="Ivan Bersen-Beklemishev">Ivan Bersen-Beklemishev</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vassian_Patrikeyev" title="Vassian Patrikeyev">Vassian Patrikeyev</a>, and others. With regards to the question of monastic estates, which had already divided the Russian clergy into two <a href="/wiki/Antagonist" title="Antagonist">antagonistic</a> camps (the Possessors and the Non-Possessors), Maximus took sides with <a href="/wiki/Nil_Sorsky" class="mw-redirect" title="Nil Sorsky">Nilus of Sors</a> and his <i><a href="/wiki/Startsy" class="mw-redirect" title="Startsy">startsy</a></i>, who headed the Non-Possessors camp.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> This would make him one of the worst enemies of the <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Volotsky" title="Joseph Volotsky">Josephites</a>, who stood for the right of the monasteries to own land. Maximus and his followers discussed freely the shortcomings of Russia's internal and foreign policies, criticized the lifestyle of the Russian clergy, exploitation of <a href="/wiki/Peasant" title="Peasant">peasants</a>, and the system of supporting local authorities by "milking" the peasants (the so-called <i>кормление</i>, or <i>kormleniye</i>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment">Imprisonment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_the_Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Imprisonment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Maximgrek.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Maximgrek.jpg/200px-Maximgrek.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="284" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Maximgrek.jpg/300px-Maximgrek.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c5/Maximgrek.jpg 2x" data-file-width="342" data-file-height="485" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Maximgrek.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Maximus the Greek has been held in the greatest repute by <a href="/wiki/Old_Believer" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Believer">Old Believers</a>, and his images are normally featured in every Old Believer church.</div></div></div>
<p>Maximus's relations with <a href="/wiki/Vassian_Patrikeyev" title="Vassian Patrikeyev">Vassian Patrikeyev</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ivan_Bersen-Beklemishev" title="Ivan Bersen-Beklemishev">Ivan Bersen-Beklemishev</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Turkish</a> ambassador Skinder, <a href="/wiki/Daniel,_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" title="Daniel, Metropolitan of Moscow">Metropolitan Daniel</a>'s hostility towards him, and Greek's own negative attitude towards Vasili III's intention to divorce <a href="/wiki/Solomonia_Saburova" title="Solomonia Saburova">Solomonia Saburova</a> decided his fate. A <a href="/wiki/Synod#Orthodox_usage" title="Synod">sobor</a> in 1525 accused Maximus of <a href="/wiki/Nonconformist_(Protestantism)" title="Nonconformist (Protestantism)">nonconformism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a> based on his views and translations of ecclesiastic books, disregarding his incomplete knowledge of Russian and obvious mistakes on the part of the Russian scriveners (his improper use of the imperfect tense was used to imply that he no longer believed the Holy Spirit was the Third Person of the Trinity but only had been temporarily). He was then exiled to the <a href="/wiki/Joseph-Volokolamsk_Monastery" title="Joseph-Volokolamsk Monastery">Joseph-Volokolamsk Monastery</a> and placed in a <a href="/wiki/Dungeon" title="Dungeon">dungeon</a> without the right to take communion or correspond. Maximus's "irritating" behavior at the monastery, newly discovered mistakes in his translations, and old suspicions of his unscrupulous relations with the now dead Turkish ambassador were all used against him once again at a new sobor in 1531. Worn out by the harsh conditions of his imprisonment, Maximus acknowledged some minor mistakes in his translations and excessive wine drinking. Finally, the sobor banned him from receiving <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">communion</a> and exiled him to the Otroch Monastery in <a href="/wiki/Tver" title="Tver">Tver</a>, where he would spend his next twenty years. The <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">Patriarch of Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Jerusalem">Patriarch of Jerusalem</a> all attempted to negotiate Maximus's release with the Russian authorities, but to no avail. He himself pleaded with <a href="/wiki/Ivan_the_Terrible" title="Ivan the Terrible">Ivan the Terrible</a> (r. 1547–1584) and <a href="/wiki/Macarius,_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" title="Macarius, Metropolitan of Moscow">Metropolitan Macarius</a> for his freedom. Moscow was afraid of Maximus's ability to expose wrongdoings and criticize the powers that be and, therefore, was reluctant to let him go. In 1551 (or 1553 according to other accounts), Maximus was transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Troitse-Sergiyeva_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Troitse-Sergiyeva Lavra">Troitse-Sergiyeva Lavra</a> at the solicitation of some <a href="/wiki/Boyar" title="Boyar">boyars</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">Hegumen</a> Artemiy of the <a href="/wiki/Lavra" title="Lavra">lavra</a>. That same year, according to some accounts, the <a href="/wiki/Tsar" title="Tsar">tsar</a> is said to have visited Maximus during his <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Kirillo-Belozersky_Monastery" title="Kirillo-Belozersky Monastery">Kirillo-Belozersky Monastery</a>; Maximus is described as having advised the ruler to take care of the families of soldiers who died in the conquest of <a href="/wiki/Kazan" title="Kazan">Kazan</a> instead of merely praying for the dead. In 1554, Maximus was invited to join a sobor, which would deal with the heresy of <a href="/wiki/Matvei_Bashkin" title="Matvei Bashkin">Matvei Bashkin</a>, but he refused, being wary of getting entangled in this case.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_the_Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Maximus died in 1556 in <a href="/wiki/Troitse-Sergiyeva_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Troitse-Sergiyeva Lavra">Troitse-Sergiyeva Lavra</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sergiyev_Posad" title="Sergiyev Posad">Sergiyev Posad</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas-Chesworth_35-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas-Chesworth-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> He is buried in the Refectory Church in the Lavra.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_the_Greek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg/200px-Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="273" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg/300px-Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg/400px-Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg 2x" data-file-width="830" data-file-height="1131" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Maximus_the_Greek.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Hagiographical</a> <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a> of Saint Maximus the Greek (<i>Graikos</i>) in the Greek Orthodox Church tradition.</div></div></div>
<p>Maximus left a voluminous body of original writings and translations into Russian Church Slavonic. He spent a great deal of energy and ink in his efforts to prove his innocence and Orthodoxy. He interpreted and explained for his Muscovite readers a large number of points of ancient and Biblical history, Orthodox Church practice and teachings, and features of the contemporary world outside of Muscovy. For example, he was the first to bring the discovery of the <a href="/wiki/New_World" title="New World">New World</a> to the attention of Muscovite readers. He is considered a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, which commemorates him on 21 January.
</p>
