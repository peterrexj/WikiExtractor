<p><b>Siméon-François Berneux</b> (14 May 1814 &#8211; 8 March 1866) was a French Catholic missionary to Asia, and a member of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a> who was canonized as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  Berneux was executed in the anti-Christian purges at <a href="/wiki/Saenamteo" title="Saenamteo">Saenamteo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seoul" title="Seoul">Seoul</a>, Korea, in 1866. His death provoked the <a href="/wiki/French_campaign_against_Korea_(1866)" class="mw-redirect" title="French campaign against Korea (1866)">French campaign against Korea</a> the same year.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sim%C3%A9on-Fran%C3%A7ois_Berneux&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Siméon-François Berneux was born in <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau-du-Loir" title="Château-du-Loir">Château-du-Loir</a> on 14 May 1814 and entered the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Seminary of Foreign Missions</a> in 1831 at the age of seventeen.  In 1843 he entered the Seminary of <a href="/wiki/Le_Mans" title="Le Mans">Le Mans</a> to complete his studies. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> on 20 May 1837 and appointed as Professor of Theology at the Foreign Missions Seminary in October 1838.
</p><p>Berneux departed from <a href="/wiki/Le_Havre" title="Le Havre">Le Havre</a> on 12 February 1840 and arrived at <a href="/wiki/Anyer" title="Anyer">Anyer</a> in Java on 31 May.  He spent the summer in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a> before sailing to <a href="/wiki/Macau" title="Macau">Macau</a>.  In January 1841 he sailed to <a href="/wiki/Tonkin" title="Tonkin">Tonkin</a>.  In Vietnam he was imprisoned and taken to <a href="/wiki/Hu%E1%BA%BF" title="Huế">Huế</a> for trial, where he arrived on 28 May. In 1842 he was convicted of proselytism and sentenced to death, a sentence which was indefinitely postponed.  He was released on 12 March 1843 and set sail to return to France.  However, at the Isle of Bourbon (now <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9union" title="Réunion">Réunion</a>), he received permission to return to Macao.  He was appointed to begin missions in Manchuria, and arrived in the <a href="/wiki/Liaodong_Peninsula" title="Liaodong Peninsula">Liaodong Peninsula</a> on 15 March 1844.
</p><p>Berneux was consecrated as bishop on 27 December 1854, appointed <a href="/wiki/Titular_bishop" title="Titular bishop">titular bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gafsa" title="Gafsa">Capsus</a><sup id="cite_ref-pratt_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pratt-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_vicariate" title="Apostolic vicariate">Apostolic Vicar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Korea" title="Korea">Corea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The previous Apostolic Vicar of Korea, Jean-Joseph-Jean-Baptiste Ferréol, had been consecrated in 1843 and ministered in Korea from 1845 until his death in 1853.  Berneux sailed from Shanghai to Korea in January 1856 on a Chinese ship, arriving in Korean waters on 15 March. They met a vessel owned by Korean Christians on <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> and were landed near Seoul.  Entering the city on foot, Berneux and his company were taken to the house of <a href="/wiki/Marie-Nicolas-Antoine_Daveluy" title="Marie-Nicolas-Antoine Daveluy">Marie-Nicolas-Antoine Daveluy</a>, a French priest who had come to Korea along with Bishop Ferréol.  Berneux set up a residence in <a href="/wiki/Seoul" title="Seoul">Seoul</a>, living and working secretly. 
</p><p>In 1866 Berneux was arrested and put on trial.  In February 1866 he was repeatedly interrogated under torture, and sentenced to death.  He was executed by decapitation on 8 March 1866 at <a href="/wiki/Saenamteo" title="Saenamteo">Saenamteo</a> outside Seoul.  Daveluy served as Apostolic Vicar for a short time until he was also executed on 30 March. The deaths of Berneux and other Catholic missionaries in Korea was followed by a <a href="/wiki/French_campaign_against_Korea_(1866)" class="mw-redirect" title="French campaign against Korea (1866)">French punitive expedition</a> which reinforced the Korean policy of isolationism.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sim%C3%A9on-Fran%C3%A7ois_Berneux&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Berneux was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 6 October 1968 and canonized on 6 May 1984 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicsaints_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicsaints-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  His feast day is 7 March,<sup id="cite_ref-martyrology_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrology-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and he is also venerated along with the rest of the 103 <a href="/wiki/Korean_Martyrs" title="Korean Martyrs">Korean Martyrs</a> on 20 September.
</p><p>His relics were brought to Berlin in 2001 and are held in the Institut St. Philipp Neri in Berlin, Germany.
</p>
