<p>Saint <b>Sergius of Valaam</b> (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Сергий Валаамский</span></span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greek</a> monk and <a href="/wiki/Wonderworker" class="mw-redirect" title="Wonderworker">wonderworker</a> credited with bringing <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Christianity</a> to <a href="/wiki/Karelians" title="Karelians">Karelian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Finns" title="Finns">Finnish</a> people. Conflicting church traditions place him possibly as early as the 10th century or as late as the 14th. His feast day is celebrated on <a href="/wiki/June_28_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="June 28 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">June 28</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Church legends about St. Sergius of Valaam were not committed to writing until the 18th century. In these writings, Sergius is described as an <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Athonite</a> monk sent by the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Byzantine Emperor</a> to enlighten the heathen tribes of Karelia with the light of the Christian faith. He traveled along the <a href="/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks" class="mw-redirect" title="Trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks">trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks</a>, passing the <a href="/wiki/Rus%27_(people)" class="mw-redirect" title="Rus&#39; (people)">Rus'</a> towns of <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a>, <a href="/wiki/Novgorod" class="mw-redirect" title="Novgorod">Novgorod</a> and <a href="/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga" title="Staraya Ladoga">Staraya Ladoga</a>. He arrived on the northern shores of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Ladoga" title="Lake Ladoga">Lake Ladoga</a>, and soon moved to <a href="/wiki/Valaam" title="Valaam">Valaam</a> island, where he would spend the rest of his life preaching.
</p><p>A tradition placing his arrival on Valaam as early as 992 would make him a contemporary of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Basil_II" title="Basil II">Basil II</a>. Another pious legend describes Sergius as a disciple of <a href="/wiki/Apostle_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostle Andrew">Apostle Andrew</a> who reportedly visited <a href="/wiki/Crimea" title="Crimea">Crimea</a> in the 1st century AD. However, the earliest record of this saint's activity claims that he arrived in Valaam in 1329.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His work was carried on by, among others, <a href="/wiki/Herman_of_Valaam" title="Herman of Valaam">Herman of Valaam</a>, who may or may not have a contemporary of Sergius. Saints Sergius and Herman are considered the founders of the <a href="/wiki/Valaam_Monastery" title="Valaam Monastery">Valaam Monastery</a>. Yet another church tradition dates his death to 1353, but there are no medieval documents to validate this claim either.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Archaeological_record" title="Archaeological record">archaeological record</a> places the arrival of <a href="/wiki/Christianity_in_Finland" class="mw-redirect" title="Christianity in Finland">Christianity in Finland</a> at the 10th or 11th centuries.
</p><p>The date of the monastery's foundation is not clear, as all records of its early history have been destroyed, most of them in the <a href="/wiki/Russo-Swedish_Wars" class="mw-redirect" title="Russo-Swedish Wars">Russo-Swedish Wars</a>. According to an 18th-century church chronicle, the <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of Sergius and Herman were moved to safety in <a href="/wiki/Novgorod" class="mw-redirect" title="Novgorod">Novgorod</a> in 1162 (or 1163), possibly before a major Swedish offensive against <a href="/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga" title="Staraya Ladoga">Staraya Ladoga</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> roughly a decade after the legendary <a href="/wiki/First_Swedish_Crusade" title="First Swedish Crusade">First Swedish Crusade</a>, to be returned in 1182 (or 1180). The date of the translation, September 11, continues to be commemorated by the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Finland" title="Orthodox Church of Finland">Orthodox Church of Finland</a>.
</p>
