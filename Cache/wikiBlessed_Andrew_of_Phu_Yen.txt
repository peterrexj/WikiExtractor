<p><b>Andrew of Phu Yen</b> (1624 – 26 July 1644)<sup id="cite_ref-Blessed_Andrew_of_Phu_Yen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blessed_Andrew_of_Phu_Yen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> is known as the "<a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">Protomartyr</a> of <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a>." Baptized in 1641, he was a dedicated assistant to <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionaries</a> and was thus arrested in the purge of Christians launched in 1644. After refusing to abjure the <a href="/wiki/Faith" title="Faith">faith</a>, he was put to death in Kẻ Chàm. Andrew was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on March 5, 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is 26 July.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_of_Ph%C3%BA_Y%C3%AAn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Andrew came from the province of RanRan (<a href="/wiki/Ph%C3%BA_Y%C3%AAn_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Phú Yên Province">Phú Yên</a>), today in <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a>. On the insistence of his mother, Jeanne, <a href="/wiki/Alexandre_de_Rhodes" title="Alexandre de Rhodes">Alexandre de Rhodes</a>, a French <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a>, agreed to include him among his students. Andrew soon surpassed his fellow pupils. Together with his mother, he received <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">Baptism</a> in 1641. He would have been about 15 years of age, having been born in 1625 or 1626. At the time of his death in 1644, he was 19 or 20. In 1642 Andrew become one of <a href="/wiki/Alexander_de_Rhodes" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexander de Rhodes">Alexander de Rhodes</a>' closest co-workers and, after a year of further formation, he joined the <i>Maison Dieu</i> ("House of God") <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechist</a> association which de Rhodes had instituted. Its members made a public promise to spend their entire lives serving the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> by helping <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">priests</a> and spreading the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>.
</p><p>Before the end of July 1644, <a href="/wiki/Mandarin_(bureaucrat)" title="Mandarin (bureaucrat)">Mandarin</a> Ong Nghe Bo returned to the province which he governed and where Andrew was living. He had orders from the King of Annam to prevent the expansion of Christianity in his kingdom. De Rhodes, unaware of the Mandarin's intentions, paid him a courtesy visit, but was quickly informed that the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Kingdom_of_Annam&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Kingdom of Annam (page does not exist)">King of Annam</a> was angered at the great number of Cochin-Chinese who were following the Christian faith. De Rhodes must therefore leave the country and no longer teach Christian doctrine to the Cochin-Chinese; since the latter were the subjects of the king, they would incur the most severe penalties.
</p><p>De Rhodes left the palace and went directly to the prison where an elderly catechist was already incarcerated. Meanwhile, the Mandarin had sent soldiers to Fr. de Rhodes' house in search of another catechist, but he had left on an apostolic mission. They found young Andrew instead. In order not to return empty-handed to Ong Nghe Bo, they beat Andrew, bound him and transferred him to the Governor's palace.
</p><p>On 25 July 1644 Andrew was taken to the Mandarin, who tried in various ways to make Andrew "desist from that foolish opinion of his, and give up the faith". But he replied that he was a Christian and most ready to undergo any suffering rather than abandon the law that he professed. Indignant at Andrew's inflexibility, the Mandarin ordered that he be taken to prison. The young Andrew was so serene and joyous at being able to suffer for Christ that people who came to see him recommended themselves to his prayers. He would not hear of this, but asked them to pray that God might give him the grace to be faithful to the end and to "respond with fullness of love to the infinite love of his Lord, who gave his life for men, by giving his own life".
</p><p>The next day, 26 July, Andrew was taken to the Governor's public audience, where he was sentenced to <a href="/wiki/Death" title="Death">death</a>. In the afternoon, a captain led Andrew down the streets of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ke_Cham&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ke Cham (page does not exist)">Ke Cham</a> to the place of <a href="/wiki/Execution" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution">execution</a>, a field outside the city. De Rhodes, many Portuguese and Vietnamese Christians, and even pagans followed the procession and witnessed the killing. Andrew exhorted the Christians to remain firm in their faith, not to be saddened by his death, and to help him with their prayers to be faithful to the end. He was executed with some blows of a lance and, finally, when he was about to be beheaded with a scimitar, he cried out the name of Jesus in a loud voice. Andrew accepted the sacrifice of his life for the faith and love of Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_of_Ph%C3%BA_Y%C3%AAn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Andrew was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 5 March 2000. In Andrew’s beatification <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homily</a>, Pope John Paul II preached "The words he repeated as he advanced on the path of <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> are the expression of what motivated his whole life: ‘Let us return love for love to our God, let us return life for life.’"
</p><p>26 July, the date of his martyrdom, is Andrew of Phú Yên's feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-Blessed_Andrew_of_Phu_Yen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blessed_Andrew_of_Phu_Yen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
