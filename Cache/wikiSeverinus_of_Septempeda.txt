<p>Saints <b>Severinus of Sanseverino</b> (or <i>of Septempeda</i>) (d. 550 AD) and <b>Victorinus of Camerino</b> (d. 543 AD) were brothers who were both <a href="/wiki/Bishops" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishops">bishops</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hermits" class="mw-redirect" title="Hermits">hermits</a> of the 6th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severinus_of_Sanseverino_and_Victorinus_of_Camerino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to an unreliable legend, the brothers were noblemen who had given away their great wealth to the poor and had become hermits at <a href="/wiki/San_Severino_Marche" title="San Severino Marche">Monte Nero</a> near <a href="/wiki/Septempeda" title="Septempeda">Septempeda</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>Victorinus then withdrew to a cave near Pioraco.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Victorinus was prone to strong temptations, and he inflicted upon himself a difficult and painful <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a>: he had himself tied to a tree, with his hands clasped between two branches.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Vittorino_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Vittorino-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Victorinus’ particular method of self-mortification was depicted on a small panel in the church of San Venanzio, in Camerino, by the artist <a href="/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_da_Foligno" class="mw-redirect" title="Niccolò da Foligno">Niccolò da Foligno</a> (called l'Alunno), who created the piece between 1478–80.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, in 540 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">Pope Vigilius</a> appointed them each as bishops of two separate sees: Severinus became bishop of what was then called <i>Septempeda</i>, later called <a href="/wiki/San_Severino_Marche" title="San Severino Marche">San Severino Marche</a> after him, in the <a href="/wiki/Marches_of_Ancona" class="mw-redirect" title="Marches of Ancona">Marches of Ancona</a>; Victorinus became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Camerino" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Camerino">bishop of Camerino</a>.
</p><p>Severinus died in 545. His remains were hidden in the Cathedral of St. Maria in Septempeda to protect them from looting; found in 590 during the restoration of the cathedral, they were brought to Monte Nero. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severinus_of_Sanseverino_and_Victorinus_of_Camerino&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is a church dedicated to San Vittorino in Pioraco.
Their joint <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is June 8.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
