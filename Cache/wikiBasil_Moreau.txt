<p><b>Basil  Moreau</b>, <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross" title="Congregation of Holy Cross">C.S.C.</a> (February 11, 1799 &#8211; January 20, 1873) was the <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> who founded the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross" title="Congregation of Holy Cross">Congregation of Holy Cross</a> from which three additional congregations were founded, namely the <a href="/wiki/Marianites_of_Holy_Cross" title="Marianites of Holy Cross">Marianites of Holy Cross</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Holy_Cross" title="Sisters of the Holy Cross">Sisters of the Holy Cross</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Holy_Cross" title="Sisters of Holy Cross">Sisters of Holy Cross</a>. Father Moreau was beatified on September 15, 2007 in Le Mans, France.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Basile-Antoine Moreau was born on February 11, 1799, in <a href="/wiki/Laign%C3%A9-en-Belin" title="Laigné-en-Belin">Laigné-en-Belin</a>, a small village near <a href="/wiki/Le_Mans" title="Le Mans">Le Mans</a>, the ninth of fourteen children of Louis and Louise Pioger Moreau.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father was a farmer and a wine merchant.<sup id="cite_ref-marianites_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marianites-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He grew up in the midst of the turmoil of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bros_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bros-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  As his parents were devout Catholics involved in the underground Church, the aspect of the Revolution which most affected him was the suppression of the Church.
</p><p>He was able to receive a good education primary by the generosity of his parish priest Abbé Julian Le Provost who tutored him. In 1814 priest then made arrangements for him to enter the Minor seminary at Chateau Gontier (today the  <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Victor-Hugo_(Ch%C3%A2teau-Gontier)" class="extiw" title="fr:Lycée Victor-Hugo (Château-Gontier)">Lycée Victor-Hugo</a>  ).<sup id="cite_ref-marianites_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marianites-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1816 Moreau then entered the diocesan <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in Le Mans (located St. Vincent's Abbey, today the <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Bellevue_(Le_Mans)" class="extiw" title="fr:Lycée Bellevue (Le Mans)">Lycée Bellevue</a>), when the hostilities of the Revolution toward the Church had subsided. The seminary was run by the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Saint-Sulpice" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Saint-Sulpice">Society of Saint-Sulpice</a> and schooled him in the <a href="/wiki/French_school_of_spirituality" title="French school of spirituality">French school of spirituality</a> which remained an inspiration in his preaching and writings all his life.  At the age of 22, in 1821, Basil Moreau was ordained a priest<sup id="cite_ref-sisters_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sisters-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Le_Mans" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Le Mans">Diocese of Le Mans</a> at the Old Visitation Convent Chapel of the Sacred Heart, while the <a href="/wiki/Le_Mans_Cathedral" title="Le Mans Cathedral">Cathedral of St. Julien</a> in Le Mans was under restoration. After ordination Fr. Moreau spent two more years with the Sulpicians in Paris, first at the <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_s%C3%A9minaire_Saint-Sulpice" class="extiw" title="fr:Ancien séminaire Saint-Sulpice">Seminary of St. Sulpice</a> and then at the <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9minaire_Saint-Sulpice" class="extiw" title="fr:Séminaire Saint-Sulpice">Suplician Solitude at Issy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-marianites_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marianites-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry">Ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg/220px-Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg/330px-Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg/440px-Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Stinson_Remick_Chapel_-_Moreau.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Basil Moreau in a stained glass window in Stinson-Remick hall at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame" title="University of Notre Dame">University of Notre Dame</a></div></div></div>
<p>In 1823, he became professor of philosophy at the minor seminary of Tessé (today the Museum of Tessé in Le Mans). Then in 1825 he taught at St Vincent Seminary and later became the vice-rector and spiritual director.<sup id="cite_ref-va_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As most of the pastors and teachers in France before the Revolution were priests and religious forced into exile, by the 1820s most of the nation was ill-catechized, illiterate, and without benefit of the sacraments. Restoration of the Church was the principal theme and work of Fr. Moreau's life.<sup id="cite_ref-va_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As a young priest and throughout his life, Basil was an effective preacher who preached parish missions and offered the sacraments on an itinerant basis to rekindle the neglected faith in towns and villages throughout the region.
</p><p>In 1835, he was assigned to be the assistant <a href="/wiki/Superior_(hierarchy)" title="Superior (hierarchy)">superior</a> of the seminary at Le Mans, where he was a popular and inspiring professor of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>.  He founded a group of priests within the Diocese of Le Mans to assist diocesan clergy in his various endeavors to re-invigorate the Church, especially preaching parish missions.  He called them the Society of Auxiliary Priests.  In the same year, an older priest of the same diocese, Fr. <a href="/wiki/Jacques-Francois_Dujari%C3%A9" class="mw-redirect" title="Jacques-Francois Dujarié">Jacques-Francois Dujarié</a>, who fifteen years before, in 1820, had founded a band of young men to re-establish and teach in the schools throughout the region, handed responsibility for them over to Fr. Moreau on account of his failing health.<sup id="cite_ref-Portland_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Portland-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  While not technically religious because they had not made a novitiate or taken public vows, these young men were known as the Brothers of St. Joseph.<sup id="cite_ref-va_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_Holy_Cross">Foundation of Holy Cross</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundation of Holy Cross">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following the developments of 1835 which placed in Fr. Moreau's hands all the pieces of a nascent religious community, he began to lay the groundwork for just that. In 1837, under the leadership of Fr. Moreau, the Brothers of St. Joseph and the Society of Auxiliary Priests joined by signing together the "Fundamental Pact of Union", becoming two equal societies in one community, the Congregation of Holy Cross.<sup id="cite_ref-sisters_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sisters-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The congregation took its name from the neighborhood of Sainte-Croix in Le Mans, where the 12th-century church, <a href="/wiki/Notre_Dame_de_Sainte_Croix,_Le_Mans" title="Notre Dame de Sainte Croix, Le Mans">Notre-Dame de Sainte-Croix</a>, was to become the mother church of the new foundation.<sup id="cite_ref-Portland_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Portland-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Holy Cross, following the example of its founder, would be <a href="/wiki/Ultramontanism" title="Ultramontanism">ultramontane</a> in its outlook, even adopting at the behest of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> the <a href="/wiki/Clerical_collar" title="Clerical collar">Roman collar</a> and the black cape for the priest (which is identical to the pope's, but in black).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundations_of_sisters">Foundations of sisters</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Foundations of sisters">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is evidence that Fr. Dujarié's dream was to found a religious community of three societies, priests, brothers, and sisters under one rule and one superior general.  Fr. Moreau made good on that vision by founding in 1841 a third society within the Congregation, that of the sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-sisters_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sisters-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Today, there are three distinct congregations of women religious: the Marianites of Holy Cross (France), Sisters of the Holy Cross (United States), and Sisters of Holy Cross (Canada).<sup id="cite_ref-Portland_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Portland-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Taking his inspiration from Fr. Dujarié, Moreau named the societies the Salvatorists, the Josephites and the Marianites, after the three persons of the Holy Family.  It was Fr. Moreau's vision for the three societies to be united in a single religious institute, but Church norms did not allow for men and women to be so joined in a canonically recognised congregation. To this day, though in separate congregations, the priests, brothers and sisters of Holy Cross refer to  themselves collectively as the Holy Cross Family.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the late 1850s, several influential members at a general chapter had him ousted as superior general over differences in what direction the congregation should take. Forced to live apart from the community, he moved in with his sisters but continued to preach retreats in the country parishes around Le Mans. Moreau died on January 20, 1873.<sup id="cite_ref-Pitre_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pitre-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Important_dates_in_the_life_of_Fr._Moreau">Important dates in the life of Fr. Moreau</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Important dates in the life of Fr. Moreau">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>1835 Taught and served as assistant superior in Le Mans; named leader of the Brothers of St. Joseph founded by Fr. Jacques Dujarié. Founded the society of Auxiliary Priests.</li>
<li>1837 United the brothers and priests into the Association of Holy Cross.</li>
<li>1844 Received the vows of <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9ocadie_Gascoin" title="Léocadie Gascoin">Léocadie Gascoin</a> and three Marianites.</li>
<li>1857 Received papal approval of the constitutions for the Association, which became the Congregation of Holy Cross.</li>
<li>1866 Resigned as superior general, but continued an active preaching and retreat ministry.</li>
<li>1873 Died on January 20 at age 73.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1957, Basil Moreau was declared a Servant of God. On April 12, 2003, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaimed him <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
Basil Moreau was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in Le Mans by Pope Benedict XVI on September 15, 2007, the feast day of Our Lady of Sorrows, and feast day of the Congregation of Holy Cross.<sup id="cite_ref-bros_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bros-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Blessed Basil Moreau is commemorated on January 20.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Moreau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Education is Moreau's lasting legacy.<sup id="cite_ref-Pitre_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pitre-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Moreau Seminary, located on the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame" title="University of Notre Dame">University of Notre Dame</a> campus, is the main seminary for the American congregation of the Holy Cross Fathers. In 1841, Moreau sent Fr. Edward Sorin and six brothers to the Diocese of Vincennes, Indiana to assist the bishop is setting up a school for boys. A year later, the bishop gave them land at the northern end of the diocese for a new foundation. Thus, Moreau is credited with playing a key role in the foundation of the University of Notre Dame as well as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Mary%27s_College_(Indiana)" title="Saint Mary&#39;s College (Indiana)">Saint Mary's College</a> in Notre Dame, Indiana. Dozens of other schools, colleges, and universities around the world have been erected under the auspices of the four congregations of Holy Cross who continue to follow in the footsteps of their founder.  
</p><p>In 1966, the Josephites (or Brothers of Holy Cross) founded the third institution of higher education in Notre Dame, Indiana to base its educational philosophy on the teachings of Basil Moreau. <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_College_(Indiana)" title="Holy Cross College (Indiana)">Holy Cross College</a> began as a community college with the mission of helping students with the intent to transfer into one of the other two institutions. However, in 2003 Holy Cross College became a residential baccalaureate liberal arts college with its own reputation for superior teaching. Basil Hall, a men's residential hall at Holy Cross College, is named in honor of Basil Moreau. Holy Cross College's mascot, the Saints, is represented by a <a href="/wiki/St._Bernard_(dog)" title="St. Bernard (dog)">St. Bernard (dog)</a> named Basil, in honor of Basil Moreau.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Edward%27s_University" title="St. Edward&#39;s University">St. Edward's University</a> in Austin, Texas is a Holy Cross school and has a co-ed residential hall called Basil Moreau Hall.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/University_of_Portland" title="University of Portland">University of Portland</a> in Portland, Oregon is run by the Congregation of Holy Cross. It features the Moreau Center for Service and Leadership, which connects students to service opportunities throughout Portland and beyond.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Moreau_Catholic_High_School" title="Moreau Catholic High School">Moreau Catholic High School</a> is located in <a href="/wiki/Hayward,_California" title="Hayward, California">Hayward, California</a>, about 25 miles (40&#160;km) southeast of <a href="/wiki/San_Francisco" title="San Francisco">San Francisco</a>.  It is among nine Catholic high schools in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Oakland" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Oakland">Diocese of Oakland</a>, and it is sponsored by the South-West Province of the Congregation of Holy Cross. Moreau Catholic is affiliated with many other Holy Cross educational institutions worldwide, but they are the only one named after the founder of Holy Cross, Blessed Father Basil Anthony Moreau.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_High_School,_New_Orleans" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Cross High School, New Orleans">Holy Cross High School, New Orleans</a> in New Orleans, Louisiana was founded by Fr. Basil Moreau. It was once called Holy Cross College, but is now known as Holy Cross School. It is located on Paris Avenue.
</p>
