<p><b>Maurus Scott</b> (c. 1579 – 30 May 1612), born <b>William Scott</b>, was an English lawyer who became a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk and priest, serving as a <a href="/wiki/Mission_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (Christian)">missionary</a> in <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> during the period of <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusancy</a>. He was executed at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a>, and is a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_conversion">Early life and conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maurus_Scott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Scott was born in <a href="/wiki/Chigwell" title="Chigwell">Chigwell</a>, <a href="/wiki/Essex" title="Essex">Essex</a> around 1579, and was baptised in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>. He was sent to school in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, and entered the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Cambridge" title="University of Cambridge">University of Cambridge</a> during <a href="/wiki/Lent_term" title="Lent term">Lent term</a> 1593-94.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At first he joined <a href="/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge" title="Trinity College, Cambridge">Trinity College</a>, however, after a year he decided to migrate to <a href="/wiki/Trinity_Hall,_Cambridge" title="Trinity Hall, Cambridge">Trinity Hall</a>, which at the time was particularly noted for its excellence in the field of <a href="/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system)" title="Civil law (legal system)">civil law</a>, which Scott described as "a branch of study which was more comformable with my disposition and interested me more". He received the degree of <a href="/wiki/LL.B." class="mw-redirect" title="LL.B.">LL.B.</a> in 1600, and proceeded to London and the <a href="/wiki/Inner_Temple" title="Inner Temple">Inner Temple</a> to train as a <a href="/wiki/Barrister" title="Barrister">barrister</a>.
</p><p>It was at this time that he began to seriously consider his religious faith. He had been firmly of the position that <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholicism</a> and its claims were both false and treasonable, however, while visiting a Catholic friend, he began casually flicking through a book of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and was struck by the force of an argument he read there. This caused him to enter into a period of intensive study and prayer, and it was only after two years of intellectual and spiritual struggle that he finally decided to be received into the Catholic Church.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Entry_into_Benedictine_life">Entry into Benedictine life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maurus_Scott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Entry into Benedictine life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A notable catalyst in the process of Scott's conversion was the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_Roberts" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John Roberts">John Roberts</a>, who had come to London as a <a href="/wiki/Mission_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (Christian)">missionary</a>. It was Roberts who received Scott into the Catholic Church, and also Roberts who accepted him as a Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulant</a>. Scott was to be taken to <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> for his monastic formation in the <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> of 1604, but on attempting to leave England, he was arrested along with Roberts and a group of other postulants on suspicion of being Catholic. However, this arrest proved only a temporary delay, as immediately after his release he was taken to Valladolid by <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Bradshaw" title="Augustine Bradshaw">Augustine Bradshaw</a>, where it was agreed that he should serve his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at the Abbey of San Facundo, <a href="/wiki/Sahag%C3%BAn" title="Sahagún">Sahagún</a>.
</p><p>As a Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> he took the name <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Maurus" title="Saint Maurus">Maurus</a></i>, and, in 1610, was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> priest. He begged to be sent on the mission to England, and his request was granted. He was sent to St Gregory's monastery, <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> (which still survives as <a href="/wiki/Downside_Abbey" title="Downside Abbey">Downside Abbey</a>, having moved to England in the 19th century), where he was to make his preparations, and arrived in England in December 1610.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Witness_to_the_martyrdom_of_John_Roberts">Witness to the martyrdom of John Roberts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maurus_Scott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Witness to the martyrdom of John Roberts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Immediately after arriving in England, he was to discover that John Roberts, who had had such a profound influence upon him, had been arrested for serving as a priest. He was able to join Roberts for his last meal the night before he was put to death. The next day Scott watched as John Roberts was <a href="/wiki/Hanging,_drawing_and_quartering" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanging, drawing and quartering">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a>.
</p><p>After the execution, wishing to preserve Roberts' body as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a>, Scott watched to see where it was thrown. The limbs had been thrown into a large trench, and upon them were heaped the bodies of 16 criminals executed at the same time. Nonetheless, two days later Scott was part of a group which successfully recovered the body parts from the trench, although one quarter was lost when the party was disturbed by watchmen, and Scott was subsequently arrested. Following this, he was imprisoned for a year, until in December 1611 the <a href="/wiki/Ambassador" title="Ambassador">ambassador</a> for <a href="/wiki/Savoy" title="Savoy">Savoy</a> negotiated the release of imprisoned Catholic priests. Scott was <a href="/wiki/Exile" title="Exile">banished</a> from the country and went back to Douai. Nonetheless, he was extremely keen to return to England. Around the Easter week of 1612 he was arrested on a boat on the <a href="/wiki/River_Thames" title="River Thames">River Thames</a> before he was even able to set foot in London.
</p>
<h2><span id="Arrest.2C_trial_and_martyrdom"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest,_trial_and_martyrdom">Arrest, trial and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maurus_Scott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Arrest, trial and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his arrest, he was examined by <a href="/wiki/George_Abbot_(Archbishop_of_Canterbury)" class="mw-redirect" title="George Abbot (Archbishop of Canterbury)">George Abbot</a>, who had been <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_London" title="Bishop of London">Bishop of London</a> at the time of the execution of John Roberts and Scott's arrest in 1610, and was now <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. Abbot offered Scott an oath of loyalty to <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="James I of England">King James I</a>. However, seeing that the oath was constructed in such a way that it implied disloyalty to the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> and the Catholic Church, Scott instead suggested his own formula, declaring "that I am a loyal servant to His Majesty... also for the past and for the future, that I have been and to the last moment of my life will ever be loyal to my King; which is more than you exact by your statute". However, this was not accepted and Scott was placed in close confinement (although not before drawing on his legal expertise to insist that Abbot did not have jurisdiction to hand down such a punishment).
</p><p>Scott was tried on the 28 May 1612 at the <a href="/wiki/Old_Bailey" title="Old Bailey">Old Bailey</a>. Again, his legal training was in evidence, as he skillfully avoided the questioning of those accusing him of being a priest, and insisted that it was the business of the <a href="/wiki/Prosecutor" title="Prosecutor">prosecutor</a> to bring evidence against him, not for him to convict himself. He entered a plea of not guilty. Only after the <a href="/wiki/Jury" title="Jury">jury</a> had found him <a href="/wiki/Guilt_(law)" title="Guilt (law)">guilty</a> did he fall to his knees, crying out Deo Gratias, and admitted to his being a priest. He then addressed the people in the <a href="/wiki/Court" title="Court">court</a>, explaining that the only reason that he had not yet confessed himself as a priest was to see if the law would take its course, or whether he would be condemned on presumption of guilt without any proof.
</p><p>On the morning of May 30 he was to be executed with Richard Newport, another Catholic priest. He appeared wearing his Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> and declared himself once again a loyal subject of the King, before being tied to a horse and dragged through the streets to the gallows at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a>. Before being executed, he made a declaration of his life, his faith and his conversion to the Catholic Church, and gave the small number of gold coins he had in his purse to the executioner, saying, "Take these, friend, for love of me. I give them to you with good will and gladly do I forgive you my death". He was then hanged, drawn and quartered.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maurus_Scott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Scott was one of a number of English martyrs <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_XI" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius XI">Pius XI</a> in 1929.
</p>
