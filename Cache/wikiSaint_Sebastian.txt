<p><b>Saint Sebastian</b> (in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>: <i>Sebastianus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Narbonne" title="Narbonne">Narbo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gallia_Narbonensis" title="Gallia Narbonensis">Gallia Narbonensis</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;AD 255</span> – <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Italy" title="Roman Italy">Italia</a>, Roman Empire c. AD 288) was an early <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. According to traditional belief, he was killed during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> of Christians. He was initially tied to a post or tree and shot with arrows, though this did not kill him. He was, according to tradition, rescued and healed by Saint <a href="/wiki/Irene_of_Rome" title="Irene of Rome">Irene of Rome</a>, which became a popular subject in 17th-century painting. In all versions of the story, shortly after his recovery he went to <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> to warn him about his sins, and as a result was clubbed to death.<sup id="cite_ref-independent_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-independent-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>.
</p><p>The oldest record of the details of Sebastian's martyrdom is found in the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronograph_of_354" title="Chronograph of 354">Chronograph of 354</a></i>, which mentions him as a martyr, venerated on January 20. He is also mentioned in a sermon on <a href="/wiki/Psalm_118" title="Psalm 118">Psalm 118</a> by 4th-century bishop <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose of Milan (Saint Ambrose)</a>: in his sermon, Ambrose stated that Sebastian came from <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and that he was already venerated there at that time. The full account of his martyrdom comes from the <i>Passio Sancti Sebastiani</i>, a 5th-century text written by an anonymous author, possibly <a href="/wiki/Arnobius_the_Younger" title="Arnobius the Younger">Arnobius the Younger</a>.
</p><p>Saint Sebastian is a popular male saint, especially today among athletes.<sup id="cite_ref-accsport.asn.au_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-accsport.asn.au-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In medieval times, he was regarded as a saint with a special ability to intercede to protect from <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a>, and devotion to him greatly increased when plague was active.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Nuremberg_chronicles_f_125r_1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Nuremberg_chronicles_f_125r_1.jpg/220px-Nuremberg_chronicles_f_125r_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/13/Nuremberg_chronicles_f_125r_1.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="230" data-file-height="324" /></a><figcaption>St Sebastian (Sebianus) in the <i><a href="/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle" title="Nuremberg Chronicle">Nuremberg Chronicle</a></i></figcaption></figure>
<p>The ancient source mentioning Sebastian is found in the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronograph_of_354" title="Chronograph of 354">Chronograph of 354</a></i>, a compilation of chronological and calendrical texts produced in 354 AD by the calligrapher and illustrator <a href="/wiki/Furius_Dionysius_Filocalus" title="Furius Dionysius Filocalus">Furius Dionysius Filocalus</a>, which mentions him as a martyr who was venerated on January 20. His cult is also mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose of Milan</a> in his <i>Expositio in Psalmum CXVIII</i>, a theological and exegetical commentary of <a href="/wiki/Psalm_118" title="Psalm 118">Psalm 118</a> dated to 386-390 AD; Ambrose states that Sebastian came from <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and that he was venerated as a Saint there.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The first surviving account of Sebastian's life and death is the <i>Passio Sancti Sebastiani</i>, long thought to have been written by Ambrose in the 4th century, but now regarded as a 5th-century account by an unknown author (possibly <a href="/wiki/Arnobius_the_Younger" title="Arnobius the Younger">Arnobius the Younger</a>). This includes the "two martyrdoms", and the care by Irene in between, and other details that remained part of the story.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Sebastian's 18th-century entry in <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> still attributed to Ambrose by the 17th-century <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographer</a> <a href="/wiki/Jean_Bolland" title="Jean Bolland">Jean Bolland</a>, and the briefer account in the 14th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Legenda Aurea</a></i>, he was a man of <a href="/wiki/Gallia_Narbonensis" title="Gallia Narbonensis">Gallia Narbonensis</a> who was taught in <a href="/wiki/Mediolanum" title="Mediolanum">Mediolanum</a> (<a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>). In 283, Sebastian entered the army in Rome under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Carinus" title="Carinus">Carinus</a> to assist the <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyrs</a>. Because of his courage he became one of the captains of the <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Guard" title="Praetorian Guard">Praetorian Guards</a> under Diocletian and <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>, who were unaware that he was a Christian.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to tradition, Marcus and Marcellianus were twin brothers from a distinguished family and were <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacons</a>. Both brothers married, and they resided in Rome with their wives and children. The brothers refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods and were arrested. They were visited by their parents Tranquillinus and Martia in prison, who attempted to persuade them to renounce Christianity. Sebastian succeeded in converting Tranquillinus and Martia, as well as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Tiburtius" title="Saint Tiburtius">Saint Tiburtius</a>, the son of Chromatius, the local prefect. Another official, Nicostratus, and his wife Zoe were also converted. It has been said that Zoe had been a mute for six years; however, she made known to Sebastian her desire to be converted to Christianity. As soon as she had, her speech returned to her. Nicostratus then brought the rest of the prisoners; these 16 persons were converted by Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Chromatius and Tiburtius converted; Chromatius set all of his prisoners free from jail, resigned his position, and retired to the country in <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a>. Marcus and Marcellianus, after being concealed by a Christian named <a href="/wiki/Castulus" title="Castulus">Castulus</a>, were later martyred, as were Nicostratus, Zoe, and Tiburtius.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

Dorigny.</figcaption></figure> 
<p>Sebastian had prudently concealed his faith, but in 286 it was detected. Diocletian reproached him for his supposed betrayal, and he commanded him to be led to a field and there to be bound to a stake so that the chosen archers from <a href="/wiki/Mauretania" title="Mauretania">Mauretania</a> would shoot arrows at him. "And the archers shot at him till he was as full of arrows as an <a href="/wiki/Hedgehog" title="Hedgehog">urchin</a><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;Note 1&#93;</a></sup> is full of pricks, and thus left him there for dead."<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Miraculously, the arrows did not kill him. The widow of Castulus, <a href="/wiki/Irene_of_Rome" title="Irene of Rome">Irene of Rome</a>, went to retrieve his body to bury it, and discovered he was still alive. She brought him back to her house and nursed him back to health.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sebastian later stood by a staircase where the emperor was to pass and harangued Diocletian for his cruelties against Christians. This freedom of speech, and from a person whom he supposed to have been dead, greatly astonished the emperor; but recovering from his surprise, he gave orders for Sebastian to be seized and beaten to death with <a href="/wiki/Cudgels" class="mw-redirect" title="Cudgels">cudgels</a>, and his body thrown into the common sewer. A holy lady, named Lucina, admonished by the martyr in a vision, privately removed the body and buried it in the catacombs at the entrance of the cemetery of Calixtus,<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> where now stands the <a href="/wiki/San_Sebastiano_fuori_le_mura" title="San Sebastiano fuori le mura">Basilica of St. Sebastian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Location_of_remains">Location of remains</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Location of remains">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sebastia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Sebastia.jpg/215px-Sebastia.jpg" decoding="async" width="215" height="292" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Sebastia.jpg/323px-Sebastia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1a/Sebastia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="354" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption><i>St. Sebastian</i> (detail), <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Mantegna" title="Andrea Mantegna">Andrea Mantegna</a>, 1480, <a href="/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre" class="mw-redirect" title="Musée du Louvre">Musée du Louvre</a>, Paris</figcaption></figure>
<p>Remains reputed to be those of Sebastian are housed in Rome in the <i>Basilica Apostolorum</i>, built by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a> in 367 on the site of the provisional tomb of Saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a>. The church, today called <a href="/wiki/San_Sebastiano_fuori_le_mura" title="San Sebastiano fuori le mura">San Sebastiano fuori le mura</a>, was rebuilt in the 1610s under the patronage of <a href="/wiki/Scipione_Borghese" title="Scipione Borghese">Scipione Borghese</a>.
</p><p>St. Ado, Eginard, Sigebert, and other contemporary authors relate that, in the reign of Louis Debonnair, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugenius_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Eugenius II">Pope Eugenius II</a> gave the body of St. Sebastian to Hilduin, Abbot of St. Denys, who brought it into <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">France</a>, and it was deposited at <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Medard,_Soissons" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Medard, Soissons">Saint Medard Abbey</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>, on 8 December, in 826.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sebastian's <a href="/wiki/Cranium" class="mw-redirect" title="Cranium">cranium</a> was brought to the town of <a href="/wiki/Ebersberg" title="Ebersberg">Ebersberg</a> (<a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Germany</a>) in 934. A Benedictine abbey was founded there and became one of the most important pilgrimage sites in southern Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> It is said the silver-encased cranium was used as a cup in which to present the consecrated wine of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a> to the faithful during the feast of Saint Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="As_protector_against_plague">As protector against plague</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: As protector against plague">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg/220px-Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="328" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg/330px-Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg/440px-Josse_Lieferinxe_-_Saint_Sebastian_Interceding_for_the_Plague_Stricken_-_Walters_371995.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1206" data-file-height="1799" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Sebastian Interceding for the Plague Stricken</i> (at top),<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Josse_Lieferinxe" title="Josse Lieferinxe">Josse Lieferinxe</a>, 1497–1499, <a href="/wiki/The_Walters_Art_Museum" class="mw-redirect" title="The Walters Art Museum">The Walters Art Museum</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The belief that Saint Sebastian was a defense against <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">the plague</a> was a medieval addition to his reputation, which largely accounts for the enormous increase in his importance in the <a href="/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages" title="Late Middle Ages">Late Middle Ages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The connection of the martyr shot with arrows with the plague is not an intuitive one, however. In Greco-Roman myth, <a href="/wiki/Apollo" title="Apollo">Apollo</a>, the archer god, at times destroys his enemies by shooting plague-arrows from the heavens, but is also the deliverer from <a href="/wiki/Infectious_disease" class="mw-redirect" title="Infectious disease">pestilence</a>; the figure of Sebastian <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianizes</a> this <a href="/wiki/Folkloric" class="mw-redirect" title="Folkloric">folkloric</a> association.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research" title="Wikipedia:No original research"><span title="The material near this tag possibly contains original research. (January 2022)">original research?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The hopeful example of Sebastian being able to recover from his "first <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>" (or "sagittation", as it is sometimes called) was also relevant, and the arrow-wounds can resemble the <a href="/wiki/Buboe" class="mw-redirect" title="Buboe">buboes</a> that were symptoms of bubonic plague. Visually, "the arrow wounds call to God for mercy to us, as the symptoms of the infirm call for pity from the passerby", as <a href="/wiki/Molanus" class="mw-redirect" title="Molanus">Molanus</a> put it.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Chronicler" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicler">chronicler</a> <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Deacon" title="Paul the Deacon">Paul the Deacon</a> relates that, in 680, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> was freed from a raging pestilence by him. The <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i> transmits the episode of a great plague that afflicted the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a> in the time of King Gumburt, which was stopped by the erection of an altar in honor of Sebastian in the Church of Saint Peter in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Pavia" title="Province of Pavia">Province of Pavia</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art_and_literature">In art and literature</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In art and literature">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>The earliest known representation of Sebastian is a mosaic in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Apollinare_Nuovo" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Apollinare Nuovo">Basilica of Sant'Apollinare Nuovo</a> (Ravenna, Italy) dated between 527 and 565.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The right lateral wall of the basilica contains large mosaics representing a procession of 26 martyrs, led by Saint Martin and including Sebastian. The martyrs are represented in Byzantine style, lacking any individuality, and all have identical expressions.
</p><p>Another early representation is in a mosaic in the Church of <a href="/wiki/San_Pietro_in_Vincoli" title="San Pietro in Vincoli">San Pietro in Vincoli</a> in Rome, probably made in the year 682. It shows a grown, bearded man in court dress but contains no trace of an arrow.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The archers and arrows begin to appear by 1000, and ever since have been far more commonly shown than the actual moment of his death by clubbing, so that there is a popular misperception that this is how he died.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As protector of potential plague victims (a connection popularized by the <i>Golden Legend</i><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>) and soldiers, Sebastian occupied an important place in the popular medieval mind. He was among the most frequently depicted of all saints by Late Gothic and Renaissance artists, in the period after the <a href="/wiki/Black_Death" title="Black Death">Black Death</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The opportunity to show a semi-nude young male, often in a contorted pose, also made Sebastian a favorite subject.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> His shooting with arrows was the subject of the largest <a href="/wiki/Engraving" title="Engraving">engraving</a> by the <i><a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Playing_Cards" title="Master of the Playing Cards">Master of the Playing Cards</a></i> in the 1430s, when there were few other current subjects with male nudes other than Christ. Sebastian appears in many other <a href="/wiki/Old_master_print" title="Old master print">prints</a> and paintings, although this was due to his popularity with the faithful. Among many others, <a href="/wiki/Botticelli" class="mw-redirect" title="Botticelli">Botticelli</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perugino" class="mw-redirect" title="Perugino">Perugino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Titian" title="Titian">Titian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antonio_del_Pollaiuolo" title="Antonio del Pollaiuolo">Pollaiuolo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Bellini" title="Giovanni Bellini">Giovanni Bellini</a>, <a href="/wiki/Guido_Reni" title="Guido Reni">Guido Reni</a> (who painted the subject seven times), <a href="/wiki/Andrea_Mantegna" title="Andrea Mantegna">Mantegna</a> (three times), <a href="/wiki/Hans_Memling" title="Hans Memling">Hans Memling</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gerrit_van_Honthorst" class="mw-redirect" title="Gerrit van Honthorst">Gerrit van Honthorst</a>, <a href="/wiki/Luca_Signorelli" title="Luca Signorelli">Luca Signorelli</a>, <a href="/wiki/El_Greco" title="El Greco">El Greco</a>, <a href="/wiki/Honor%C3%A9_Daumier" title="Honoré Daumier">Honoré Daumier</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Singer_Sargent" title="John Singer Sargent">John Singer Sargent</a> and <a href="/wiki/Louise_Bourgeois" title="Louise Bourgeois">Louise Bourgeois</a> all painted Saint Sebastians. An early work by the sculptor <a href="/wiki/Gianlorenzo_Bernini" class="mw-redirect" title="Gianlorenzo Bernini">Gianlorenzo Bernini</a> is of <a href="/wiki/St._Sebastian_(Bernini)" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Sebastian (Bernini)">Saint Sebastian</a>.
</p><p>The saint is ordinarily depicted as a handsome youth pierced by arrows. <a href="/wiki/Predella" title="Predella">Predella</a> scenes when required often depicted his arrest, confrontation with the Emperor, and final beheading. The illustration in the infobox is the <i>Saint Sebastian</i> of <a href="/wiki/Il_Sodoma" title="Il Sodoma">Il Sodoma</a>, at the <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Pitti" title="Palazzo Pitti">Pitti Palace</a>, Florence.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg/240px-Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="308" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg/360px-Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg/480px-Georges_de_La_Tour_003.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2024" data-file-height="2601" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian_Tended_by_Saint_Irene_(Georges_de_La_Tour,_Louvre)" title="Saint Sebastian Tended by Saint Irene (Georges de La Tour, Louvre)">Saint Sebastian Tended by Saint Irene (Georges de La Tour, Louvre)</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1645</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Hans_Holbein_the_Elder" title="Hans Holbein the Elder">Hans Holbein the Elder</a> created a statuette of Saint Sebastian "in silver and parcel-gilt," now in the <a href="/wiki/Victoria_and_Albert_Museum" title="Victoria and Albert Museum">Victoria and Albert Museum</a> in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A mainly 17th-century subject, though found in <a href="/wiki/Predella" title="Predella">predella</a> scenes as early as the 15th century,<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> was <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian_Tended_by_Saint_Irene" title="Saint Sebastian Tended by Saint Irene">Saint Sebastian Tended by Saint Irene</a></i>, painted by <a href="/wiki/Georges_de_La_Tour" title="Georges de La Tour">Georges de La Tour</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trophime_Bigot" title="Trophime Bigot">Trophime Bigot</a> (four times), <a href="/wiki/Jusepe_de_Ribera" title="Jusepe de Ribera">Jusepe de Ribera</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hendrick_ter_Brugghen" title="Hendrick ter Brugghen">Hendrick ter Brugghen</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian_Tended_by_Irene_(Ter_Brugghen)" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Sebastian Tended by Irene (Ter Brugghen)">perhaps his masterpiece</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> and others. This may have been a deliberate attempt by the Church to get away from the single nude subject, which is already recorded in <a href="/wiki/Vasari" class="mw-redirect" title="Vasari">Vasari</a> as sometimes arousing inappropriate thoughts among female and male churchgoers.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The Baroque artists usually treated it as a nocturnal <a href="/wiki/Chiaroscuro" title="Chiaroscuro">chiaroscuro</a> scene, illuminated by a single candle, torch or lantern, in the style fashionable in the first half of the 17th century. There exist several cycles depicting the life of Saint Sebastian. Among them are the frescos in the basilica church of <a href="/wiki/San_Sebastiano,_Acireale" class="mw-redirect" title="San Sebastiano, Acireale">San Sebastiano, Acireale</a> in Sicily painted by <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Paolo_Vasta" title="Pietro Paolo Vasta">Pietro Paolo Vasta</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Egon_Schiele" title="Egon Schiele">Egon Schiele</a>, an Austrian Expressionist artist, painted a self-portrait as Saint Sebastian in 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Literature.2C_fiction.2C_and_music"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Literature,_fiction,_and_music">Literature, fiction, and music</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Literature, fiction, and music">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg/220px-Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="305" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg/330px-Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg/440px-Woodblock_of_St_Sebastian.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1641" data-file-height="2274" /></a><figcaption>Woodblock of St Sebastian from South Germany, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1470–1475</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1911, the Italian playwright <a href="/wiki/Gabriele_d%27Annunzio" class="mw-redirect" title="Gabriele d&#39;Annunzio">Gabriele d'Annunzio</a> in conjunction with <a href="/wiki/Claude_Debussy" title="Claude Debussy">Claude Debussy</a> produced <i><a href="/wiki/Le_Martyre_de_saint_S%C3%A9bastien" title="Le Martyre de saint Sébastien">Le Martyre de saint Sébastien</a></i>. The American composer <a href="/wiki/Gian_Carlo_Menotti" title="Gian Carlo Menotti">Gian Carlo Menotti</a> composed a ballet score for a <a href="/wiki/Ballets_Russes" title="Ballets Russes">Ballets Russes</a> production which was first given in 1944. In his novella <i><a href="/wiki/Death_in_Venice" title="Death in Venice">Death in Venice</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Mann" title="Thomas Mann">Thomas Mann</a> hails the "Sebastian-Figure" as the supreme emblem of <a href="/wiki/Apollonian_and_Dionysian" title="Apollonian and Dionysian">Apollonian</a> beauty, that is, the artistry of differentiated forms; beauty as measured by discipline, proportion, and luminous distinctions. This allusion to Saint Sebastian's suffering, associated with the writerly professionalism of the novella's protagonist, Gustav Aschenbach, provides a model for the "heroism born of weakness", which characterizes poise amidst agonizing torment and plain acceptance of one's fate as, beyond mere patience and passivity, a stylized achievement and artistic triumph.<sup id="cite_ref-artintuscany_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artintuscany-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sebastian's death was depicted in the 1949 film <i><a href="/wiki/Fabiola_(1949_film)" title="Fabiola (1949 film)">Fabiola</a></i>, in which he was played by <a href="/wiki/Massimo_Girotti" title="Massimo Girotti">Massimo Girotti</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> In 1976, the British director <a href="/wiki/Derek_Jarman" title="Derek Jarman">Derek Jarman</a> made a film, <i><a href="/wiki/Sebastiane" title="Sebastiane">Sebastiane</a></i>, which caused controversy in its treatment of the martyr as a "<a href="/wiki/Homosexual_icon" class="mw-redirect" title="Homosexual icon">homosexual icon</a>", according to a number of critics reflecting a subtext perceptible in the imagery since the Renaissance.<sup id="cite_ref-independent_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-independent-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Also in 1976, in the American horror film <i><a href="/wiki/Carrie_(1976_film)" title="Carrie (1976 film)">Carrie</a></i>, a figure of Saint Sebastian (commonly misconstrued as a figure of the crucified Christ) appears in Carrie's prayer closet.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Boxer <a href="/wiki/Mohammad_Ali" class="mw-redirect" title="Mohammad Ali">Mohammad Ali</a> was pictured with the <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> of a bound Saint Sebastian pierced by arrows, in the  April 1968 issue of <i><a href="/wiki/Esquire_Magazine" class="mw-redirect" title="Esquire Magazine">Esquire Magazine</a>.</i> The image was created by photographer <a href="/wiki/George_Lois" title="George Lois">George Lois</a> and art director Carl Fischer.
</p><p>A depiction of Saint Sebastian in a fresco restoration in an isolated Italian village is the central motif and cryptic mystery of the 1976 <a href="/wiki/Giallo" title="Giallo">giallo</a> horror film <i><a href="/wiki/The_House_with_Laughing_Windows" title="The House with Laughing Windows">The House with Laughing Windows</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In her 1965 story "<a href="/wiki/Everything_That_Rises_Must_Converge" title="Everything That Rises Must Converge">Everything That Rises Must Converge</a>", <a href="/wiki/Flannery_O%27Connor" title="Flannery O&#39;Connor">Flannery O'Connor</a>'s character Julian feels as if he were the martyr while taking his mother to "reducing" classes at the Y.
</p><p>In 1997, the <a href="/wiki/The_Hand_of_St._Sebastian" title="The Hand of St. Sebastian">eighth episode</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Millennium_(season_2)" title="Millennium (season 2)">second season</a> of the television series <i><a href="/wiki/Millennium_(TV_series)" title="Millennium (TV series)">Millennium</a></i>, the protagonists search for the hand of Saint Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2007, artist <a href="/wiki/Damien_Hirst" title="Damien Hirst">Damien Hirst</a> presented Saint Sebastian, Exquisite Pain from his Natural History series. The piece depicts a cow in formaldehyde, bound in metal cable and shot with arrows.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>British pop band <a href="/wiki/Alt-J" title="Alt-J">Alt-J</a>'s video for "<a href="/wiki/Hunger_of_the_Pine" title="Hunger of the Pine">Hunger of the Pine</a>" contains references to the story of Saint Sebastian's death, adapted to fit the lyrics of the song. <a href="/wiki/Tarsem_Singh" title="Tarsem Singh">Tarsem Singh</a>'s video for the <a href="/wiki/R.E.M." title="R.E.M.">R.E.M.</a> song "<a href="/wiki/Losing_My_Religion" title="Losing My Religion">Losing My Religion</a>" makes use of imagery of St. Sebastian, drawing particular inspiration from paintings by Guido Reni<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Cecco_del_Caravaggio" title="Cecco del Caravaggio">Caravaggio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> The indie folk band <a href="/wiki/The_Mountain_Goats" title="The Mountain Goats">the Mountain Goats</a> have a song called "Hail, St. Sebastian" that makes reference to his life.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> Scottish musician <a href="/wiki/Momus_(musician)" title="Momus (musician)">Momus</a> has a song "Lucky like St Sebastian", featuring on his 1986 debut album <i><a href="/wiki/Circus_Maximus_(Momus_album)" title="Circus Maximus (Momus album)">Circus Maximus</a>.</i>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Madonna" title="Madonna">Madonna</a>'s song "I'm a Sinner" from her 2012 album <i><a href="/wiki/MDNA_(album)" title="MDNA (album)">MDNA</a></i> has a segment resembling a <a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">litany</a>, with one line saying, "St. Sebastian, don't you cry; let those poisoned arrows fly."
</p><p>The 2013–2018 Canadian drama series <i><a href="/wiki/Forgive_Me_(TV_series)" title="Forgive Me (TV series)">Forgive Me</a></i> centres on a priest haunted by recurring visions of Saint Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The look of the character Gemino in the popular action-platform videogame <i><a href="/wiki/Blasphemous_(video_game)" title="Blasphemous (video game)">Blasphemous</a></i> is clearly inspired by Saint Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The family del Valle in <a href="/wiki/Isabel_Allende" title="Isabel Allende">Isabel Allende</a>'s novel "<a href="/wiki/The_House_of_the_Spirits" title="The House of the Spirits">House of the Spirits</a>" attends Sunday mass in the Church of Saint Sebastian.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_(1).JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_%281%29.JPG/220px-0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_%281%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="279" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_%281%29.JPG/330px-0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_%281%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_%281%29.JPG/440px-0_Saint_S%C3%A9bastien_secouru_par_les_anges_-_Pierre_Paul_Rubens_%281%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2535" data-file-height="3214" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Sebastian</i> by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens" title="Peter Paul Rubens">Peter Paul Rubens</a> (1604), oil on canvas, 120 x 100 cm, <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg/220px-Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="276" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg/330px-Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg/440px-Glyn_Philpot_St_Sebastian_Painting_1932.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="804" /></a><figcaption>St Sebastian by <a href="/wiki/Glyn_Philpot" title="Glyn Philpot">Glyn Philpot</a> (1932), oil on canvas, 36 x 28½</figcaption></figure>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, Sebastian is commemorated by an optional memorial on 20 January. In the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Greece" title="Church of Greece">Church of Greece</a>, Sebastian's feast day is on 18 December.
</p><p>As a protector from the <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a>, Sebastian was formerly one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>. In Catholicism, Sebastian is the patron saint of archers, pin-makers, athletes (a modern association) and of a holy death.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg/200px-San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="260" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg/300px-San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg/400px-San_Sebastian_El_Greco.jpg 2x" data-file-width="900" data-file-height="1171" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Sebastian</i> by <a href="/wiki/El_Greco" title="El Greco">El Greco</a> (1578) in <a href="/wiki/Palencia_Cathedral" title="Palencia Cathedral">Cathedral of San Antolín</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palencia" title="Palencia">Palencia</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Sebastian is one of the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of the city of <a href="/wiki/Qormi" title="Qormi">Qormi</a> in <a href="/wiki/Malta" title="Malta">Malta</a><sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Sebastian is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Acireale, <a href="/wiki/Caserta" title="Caserta">Caserta</a> and <a href="/wiki/Petilia_Policastro" title="Petilia Policastro">Petilia Policastro</a> in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Melilli" title="Melilli">Melilli</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, and <a href="/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_(Spain)" class="mw-redirect" title="San Sebastián (Spain)">San Sebastián</a> as well as <a href="/wiki/Palma,_Majorca" class="mw-redirect" title="Palma, Majorca">Palma de Mallorca</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lubr%C3%ADn" title="Lubrín">Lubrín</a> and <a href="/wiki/Huelva" title="Huelva">Huelva</a> in Spain. He is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Negombo" title="Negombo">Negombo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sri_Lanka" title="Sri Lanka">Sri Lanka</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro" title="Rio de Janeiro">Rio de Janeiro</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>. Informally, in the tradition of the <a href="/wiki/Afro-Brazilian" class="mw-redirect" title="Afro-Brazilian">Afro-Brazilian</a> <a href="/wiki/Syncretic_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Syncretic religion">syncretic religion</a> <a href="/wiki/Umbanda" title="Umbanda">Umbanda</a>, Sebastian is often associated with <a href="/wiki/Oxossi" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxossi">Oxossi</a>, especially in the state of Rio de Janeiro itself.<sup id="cite_ref-artintuscany_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-artintuscany-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
In <a href="/wiki/Lubr%C3%ADn" title="Lubrín">Lubrín</a>, every year on the 20th of January, there is a festival in honor of Saint Sebastian. A statue of Saint Sebastian leads a procession around the village, and people hurl bread rolls from their balconies to the crowds following the saint in the streets below. The rolls have a hole in the middle and some people string them on a rope around their body. The festival is thought to have originated in the 14th century, after a plague of <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> hit the area. At this time, the wealthy were said to have thrown bread and money to the poor on the streets below, so as to avoid catching the disease.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> The San Sebastian 'bread festival' is so unusual that it has been declared a Fiesta of National Tourist Interest in <a href="/wiki/Andalusia" title="Andalusia">Andalusia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>King <a href="/wiki/Sebastian_I_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="Sebastian I of Portugal">Sebastian I of Portugal</a>, the only King to ever have this name, was so named for having been born on this saint's feast day.
</p><p>The Feast of St. Sebastian is celebrated among <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> communities of <a href="/wiki/Kerala" title="Kerala">Kerala</a> in India. Churches are illuminated and decorated, with fireworks being a main event in Catholic homes to commemorate the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicsetcultures_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicsetcultures-48">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Every parish has its own date of celebration, especially in the districts of <a href="/wiki/Thrissur" title="Thrissur">Thrissur</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ernakulam" title="Ernakulam">Ernakulam</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._Andrew%27s_Basilica,_Arthunkal" title="St. Andrew&#39;s Basilica, Arthunkal">St. Andrew's Basilica, Arthunkal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kottayam" title="Kottayam">Kottayam</a>. In <a href="/wiki/St._Thomas_Syro-Malabar_Church,_Malayattoor" title="St. Thomas Syro-Malabar Church, Malayattoor">Kanjoor Syro Malabar Church</a> the feast is celebrated with the largest procession of Golden Crosses and Decorated umbrellas in Asia.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicsetcultures_48-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicsetcultures-48">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Besides this, many pilgrim centres, churches, shrines and many educational institutions too, throughout Kerala, bear the name of the saint.
</p><p>He is the patron of San Sebastian College – Recoletos in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, which is adjacent to the Minor Basilica of San Sebastian, the all-steel church in the Philippines and in Asia administered by the Order of Augustinian Recollect (OAR). At the Catholic Newman Community at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Rochester" title="University of Rochester">University of Rochester</a>, the St. Sebastian Society is an organization of campus-wide Christian athletes that works to serve the greater <a href="/wiki/Rochester,_New_York" title="Rochester, New York">Rochester, New York</a> area through methods of <a href="/wiki/Restorative_justice" title="Restorative justice">restorative justice</a>, special needs <a href="/wiki/Fundraising" title="Fundraising">fundraising</a> and <a href="/wiki/Community_service" title="Community service">community service</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sebastian is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Bacolod" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Bacolod">Roman Catholic Diocese of Bacolod</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Negros_Occidental" title="Negros Occidental">Negros Occidental</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lipa_City" class="mw-redirect" title="Lipa City">Lipa City</a> in <a href="/wiki/Batangas" title="Batangas">Batangas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>. Also, Saint Sebastian is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Guanajuato" title="León, Guanajuato">Leon</a> City <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>. A representation of the Saint in his martyrdom is present in the upper left corner of the city coat of arms.
</p><p>Saint Sebastian is the patron of <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_Columbus" title="Knights of Columbus">Knights of Columbus</a> Council #4926 in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_San_Jose_in_California" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of San Jose in California">Roman Catholic Diocese of San Jose in California</a>, serving the cities of <a href="/wiki/Mountain_View,_California" title="Mountain View, California">Mountain View</a> and <a href="/wiki/Los_Altos,_California" title="Los Altos, California">Los Altos</a>. Saint Sebastian is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_War_Veterans" title="Catholic War Veterans">Catholic War Veterans</a> of the United States of America. The highest award given by the CWV is the Honor Legion of the Order of St. Sebastian.
</p><p>In his 1906 <i>Reminiscences</i>, <a href="/wiki/Carl_Schurz" title="Carl Schurz">Carl Schurz</a> recalls the annual "bird shoot" pageant of the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhenish</a> town of <a href="/wiki/Liblar" class="mw-redirect" title="Liblar">Liblar</a> (<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liblar" class="extiw" title="de:Liblar">de</a>), sponsored by the Saint Sebastian Society, a club of <a href="/wiki/Sharpshooter" title="Sharpshooter">sharpshooters</a> and their sponsors to which nearly every adult member of the town belonged.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/St._Sebastian_River" title="St. Sebastian River">St. Sebastian River</a> in the American state of <a href="/wiki/Florida" title="Florida">Florida</a> is named after him. The river is a tributary of the <a href="/wiki/Indian_River_Lagoon" title="Indian River Lagoon">Indian River Lagoon</a> and comprises part of the boundary between <a href="/wiki/Indian_River_County,_Florida" title="Indian River County, Florida">Indian River County</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brevard_County,_Florida" title="Brevard County, Florida">Brevard County</a>. The adjacent city of <a href="/wiki/Sebastian,_Florida" title="Sebastian, Florida">Sebastian, Florida</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Sebastian_River_Preserve_State_Park" title="St. Sebastian River Preserve State Park">St. Sebastian River Preserve State Park</a> are also named for Saint Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> Within the Diocese of Central Florida, the nearby Episcopal Church on Melbourne Beaches is named St Sebastian-by-the-Sea.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="LGBT_association">LGBT association</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Sebastian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: LGBT association">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Christianity_and_sexual_orientation#Saints" title="Christianity and sexual orientation">Christianity and sexual orientation §&#160;Saints</a>, and <a href="/wiki/LGBT_themes_in_mythology" title="LGBT themes in mythology">LGBT themes in mythology</a></div><p>American author Richard A. Kaye wrote in 1996 that<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p> "Contemporary gay men have seen in Sebastian at once a stunning advertisement for homosexual desire (indeed, a <a href="/wiki/Homoeroticism" title="Homoeroticism">homoerotic ideal</a>), and a prototypical portrait of tortured <a href="/wiki/The_closet" class="mw-redirect" title="The closet">closet case</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-QUEER2_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-QUEER2-52">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Some religious images depicting Saint Sebastian have been adopted by the LGBT community.<sup id="cite_ref-QUEER_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-QUEER-54">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> A combination of his strong, shirtless physique, the symbolism of the arrows penetrating his body, and the countenance of rapturous pain have intrigued artists (gay or otherwise) for centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-QUEER_54-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-QUEER-54">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> Sebastian's resilience in the face of persecution juxtaposed with the supposed "erotic" elements of his suffering resonates particularly with the <a href="/wiki/Queer" title="Queer">queer</a> community.<sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Yukio_Mishima" title="Yukio Mishima">Yukio Mishima</a>'s novel <i><a href="/wiki/Confessions_of_a_Mask" title="Confessions of a Mask">Confessions of a Mask</a></i>, the protagonist Kochan has his first gay sexual experience while looking at a reproduction of <a href="/wiki/Guido_Reni" title="Guido Reni">Guido Reni</a>'s <i>Saint Sebastian</i>. Kochan remarks: "It is an interesting coincidence that Hirschfeld should place 'pictures of St. Sebastian' in the first rank of those kinds of art works in which the invert takes special delight," referencing <a href="/wiki/Magnus_Hirschfeld" title="Magnus Hirschfeld">Magnus Hirschfeld</a>'s belief that gay men have an inclination towards certain artistic subjects including Saint Sebastian.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
