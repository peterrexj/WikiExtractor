<p><b>Alberto Hurtado</b> (<small>American Spanish:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="es-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish" title="Help:IPA/Spanish">[alˈβeɾto wɾˈtaðo]</a></span>; born <b>Luis Alberto Hurtado Cruchaga</b> on January 22, 1901 in <a href="/wiki/Vi%C3%B1a_del_Mar" title="Viña del Mar">Viña del Mar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chile</a> – August 18, 1952 in <a href="/wiki/Santiago,_Chile" class="mw-redirect" title="Santiago, Chile">Santiago</a>, Chile), popularly known in <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chile</a> as <b>Padre Hurtado</b> (Spanish for "Father Hurtado"), was a <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chilean</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>, lawyer, social worker, and writer, of <a href="/wiki/Basque_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Basque people">Basque</a> ancestry. He founded the <a href="/wiki/Hogar_de_Cristo" title="Hogar de Cristo">Hogar de Cristo</a> foundation in 1944. He was canonized on October 23, 2005, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>, becoming his country's second saint.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alberto Hurtado was born in Viña del Mar, Chile, on January 22, 1901, to an aristocratic family. After the death of his father when Alberto was four years old, his mother, with just two small sons, decided to sell their large estate. Unfortunately the buyer defrauded her. The family, now impoverished, was forced to live with a succession of relatives.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From an early age, Hurtado experienced what it meant to be poor and without a home. Thanks to a scholarship, he was able to attend the prestigious, all-boys, Jesuit school of St. Ignacio, <a href="/wiki/Santiago,_Chile" class="mw-redirect" title="Santiago, Chile">Santiago, Chile</a> (1909–17).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During this time, he volunteered at the <a href="/wiki/Parroquia_Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_Andacollo,_Santiago" title="Parroquia Nuestra Señora de Andacollo, Santiago">Parroquia Nuestra Señora de Andacollo, Santiago</a>, a Catholic parish and school in a poor neighborhood of Santiago,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> where he assisted in the office and was librarian. From 1918 to 1923, he attended the <a href="/wiki/Pontificia_Universidad_Cat%C3%B3lica_de_Chile" class="mw-redirect" title="Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile">Pontificia Universidad Católica de Chile</a>, studying in its law school and writing his thesis on <a href="/wiki/Labour_law" title="Labour law">labour law</a>. After interrupting his studies for obligatory military service, he earned his degree in August 1923.
</p><p>Hurtado entered the Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> in 1923. In 1925 he went to <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Argentina" title="Córdoba, Argentina">Córdoba, Argentina</a>, where he studied <a href="/wiki/Humanities" title="Humanities">humanities</a>. In 1927 he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>, Spain, to study philosophy and theology. When the Jesuits were suppressed in Spain in 1931, he continued his studies in theology at Louvain, Belgium. He was ordained a priest there on August 24, 1933, and in 1935 he obtained a doctorate in <a href="/wiki/Pedagogy" title="Pedagogy">pedagogy</a> and psychology.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Educator">Educator</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Educator">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Before returning to <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chile</a>, Hurtado visited social and educational centers in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>. He returned to Chile in January 1936 and took up his post as professor of religion at Colegio San Ignacio and of Pedagogy at the Catholic University of Santiago. He was entrusted with the Sodality of Our Lady for the students, and he involved them in teaching catechism to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Conservative Catholics in Chile had difficulty accepting the church's social teachings. As late as 1931, the official organ of the party aligned with the church hierarchy refused to publish the papal encyclical <i><a href="/wiki/Quadragesimo_anno" title="Quadragesimo anno">Quadragesimo anno</a></i> and considered it "an orientation directed to other parts of the world but not Chile."
</p><p>In 1936, Hurtado authored an article entitled <i>The Priesthood Crisis In Chile</i>, which addressed the problem of the shortage of priests in Chile; his analysis was criticized as "exaggerated".<sup id="cite_ref-Gertrude_M._Yeager_1932._pp._207-242_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gertrude_M._Yeager_1932._pp._207-242-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He criticized the quality of catechism instruction offered in Chile and wrote that young men often signed up as catechists but lacked the necessary certificate.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Social_apostolate">Social apostolate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Social apostolate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG/220px-Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG/330px-Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG/440px-Padre_Hurtado_02.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>Padre Hurtado</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1940, Hurtado was appointed diocesan director of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Action" title="Catholic Action">Catholic Action</a> youth movement and he served as its national director from 1941 to 1944. Also in 1941, Hurtado authored <i>Is Chile a Catholic Country?</i> The book published statistics demonstrating a lack of priests assigned to the working class and rural populations, and it reported on parishes that had one priest assigned to 10,000 laypeople spread across huge geographic areas. He advocated an increase in the number of priests and better education for them. Almost half of Chile's clergy were foreigners, including missionaries from the United States and Canada, who rode circuits of towns to administer the sacraments. Most Chileans regarded devotion to the Virgin and the saints as more important than attending Mass or receiving the Eucharist, which they could not do regularly.<sup id="cite_ref-Gertrude_M._Yeager_1932._pp._207-242_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gertrude_M._Yeager_1932._pp._207-242-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the book, Hurtado published the results of a 1939 survey of Chilean religious practices and reported that only 9% of Chilean women and 3.5% of Chilean men regularly attended Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-Gertrude_M._Yeager_1932._pp._207-242_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gertrude_M._Yeager_1932._pp._207-242-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The book was heavily criticised by more conservative Catholics, who accused Hurtado of being a Communist.<sup id="cite_ref-livingspace_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-livingspace-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Keeping in mind his own origins, and ever grateful for the help he and his family had received when they were in great difficulties, Hurtado was led to active social involvement. His strong faith was transformed into action with his founding of an organization similar to <a href="/wiki/Boys_Town_(organization)" title="Boys Town (organization)">Boys Town</a> in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>. His shelters, called <i>Hogar de Cristo</i> (Home of Christ), took in all children in need of food and shelter, abandoned or not. He also purchased a 1946 green pickup truck and monitored the streets at night to help those in need that he could reach. His own charisma brought him many collaborators and benefactors. The movement was a huge success. The shelters multiplied throughout Chile and it is estimated that between 1945 and 1951 more than 850,000 children received some help from the movement.<sup id="cite_ref-livingspace_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-livingspace-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Labor_movement_and_social_doctrine_of_the_church">Labor movement and social doctrine of the church</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Labor movement and social doctrine of the church">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1947, Hurtado entered the <a href="/wiki/Labor_movement" class="mw-redirect" title="Labor movement">labor movement</a>. Inspired by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_social_teaching" title="Catholic social teaching">social teaching of the church</a> he founded the Chilean Trade Union Association, meant to train <a href="/wiki/Leaders" class="mw-redirect" title="Leaders">leaders</a> and instill <a href="/wiki/Christian_values" title="Christian values">Christian values</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Labor_union" class="mw-redirect" title="Labor union">labor unions</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He wrote three books: <i>Social Humanism</i> (1947), <i>The Christian Social Order</i> (1947) and <i>Trade Unions</i> (1950). He served as a confessor to the <a href="/wiki/Falange_Nacional" class="mw-redirect" title="Falange Nacional">Falange Nacional</a>, the precursor to the modern Christian Democratic Party. To disseminate the social teaching of the church and help Christians reflect and act on the serious social problems faced by Chile, he founded in 1951 the periodical <i>Mensaje</i> ("Message"). He published numerous articles and books on labor issues in relation to the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic faith</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-livingspace_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-livingspace-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Deeply spiritual, Hurtado was untiring in his work for the workers and the youth, combining intellectual reflection and practical actions. Ever optimistic and joyful, he had also an attractive personality that brought many people to Christ and the Catholic Church, young and old, intellectuals and manual workers.<sup id="cite_ref-livingspace_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-livingspace-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1952, Hurtado was stricken with intense pain and rushed to the hospital. He was diagnosed with <a href="/wiki/Pancreatic_cancer" title="Pancreatic cancer">pancreatic cancer</a>. Day after day the media kept the country informed of Hurtado's state of health. Before his death he had become a national hero. After a brief battle with the illness, he died in <a href="/wiki/Santiago,_Chile" class="mw-redirect" title="Santiago, Chile">Santiago</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hurtado was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on October 16, 1994, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> on October 23, 2005.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Hurtado was one of the first people to be elevated to sainthood during the papacy of Pope Benedict XVI; he was also the second Chilean saint, after <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_the_Andes" title="Teresa of the Andes">Teresa of Los Andes</a>.
</p><p>Hurtado is one of the most popular and cherished saints in his country, <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chile</a>. An indication of his lasting popularity was the presence in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>, on the day of Hurtado's canonization, of a very large contingent of Chilean people, led by the highest authorities of the country, starting with President <a href="/wiki/Ricardo_Lagos" title="Ricardo Lagos">Ricardo Lagos</a> and some high-ranking Chilean politicians who actually had been Hurtado's students during his school teacher time, like Senator Gabriel Valdés.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I hold that every poor man, every vagrant, every beggar is Christ carrying his cross. And as Christ, we must love and help him. We must treat him as a brother, a human being like ourselves. If we were to start a campaign of love for the poor and homeless, we would, in a short time, do away with depressing scenes of begging, children sleeping in doorways and women with babies in their arms fainting in our streets.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite>St. Alberto Hurtado, SJ<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></cite></div></blockquote>
<ul><li>The "Hogar de Cristo" he founded still exists, and through its fight for social justice, it has become one of the biggest charity groups in Chile.</li>
<li>There is also an avenue and the <a href="/wiki/San_Alberto_Hurtado_metro_station" title="San Alberto Hurtado metro station">San Alberto Hurtado metro station</a> in <a href="/wiki/Santiago_Metro" title="Santiago Metro">Santiago</a> (the closest to his main shrine, which also houses the Hogar's headquarters) named after him.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Alberto_Hurtado_University" title="Alberto Hurtado University">Alberto Hurtado University</a>, located in Santiago and run by the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, preserves his name and strives to bring his legacy into contemporary education and social affairs, facilitating activities through its <a href="/wiki/Center_for_Reflection_and_Social_Action_(CREAS)" class="mw-redirect" title="Center for Reflection and Social Action (CREAS)">Center for Reflection and Social Action (CREAS)</a>.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/St._Joseph%27s_Preparatory_School" title="St. Joseph&#39;s Preparatory School">St. Joseph's Preparatory School</a> in Philadelphia, PA, established <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.sjprep.org/page.cfm?p=1046">The Hurtado Food Pantry</a>, where a team of high school students organizes monthly food collections and deliveries, sending out thousands of pounds of food to the community.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Xavier_High_School_(New_York_City)" title="Xavier High School (New York City)">Xavier High School</a> in New York, New York, renamed a hall (in which a soup kitchen feeds over 900 meals every Sunday) and <a href="/wiki/Seattle_University" title="Seattle University">Seattle University</a> has a Residential Learning Community named after him.</li>
<li>Jesuit High School in Portland, Oregon, opens its empty classrooms in the evenings to an ESL program called The Hurtado Center.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Belen_Jesuit_High_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Belen Jesuit High School">Belen Jesuit High School</a> has started the Hurtado Experience for its ninth graders, taking them on retreats to help out the needy in Miami.</li>
<li>The famous Jesuit boarding school <a href="/wiki/Clongowes_Wood_College" title="Clongowes Wood College">Clongowes Wood College</a>, Co. Kildare. Ireland, named their Bursary Programme after him in 2007. Ten percent (10%) of the student population are educated free in the interest of the school being socially just. This is not a scholarship but a bursary for students who would benefit most from a Clongowes education in the Jesuit tradition. Currently six years in Clongowes would cost €100800.00 ( 2011/2012 figures €16800 per annum ).</li>
<li>Chilean historian Marciano Barrios Valdes considered the Catholic Action movement in Chile to be what sustained the Catholic Church's continued existence in Chile into the 1960s.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The Jesuit run <a href="/wiki/Rockhurst_High_School" title="Rockhurst High School">Rockhurst High School</a> in Kansas City, Missouri, runs an accelerated learning program for inner-city, Catholic, middle school boys named "The Hurtado Scholars Program".</li>
<li>Since 2006 the Press Club of Chile has presented the annual Alberto Hurtado Cruchaga Award (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Premio Alberto Hurtado Cruchaga</i>) to people and institutions for service to the community in the field of journalism.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Jesuit_High_School_(Tampa)" title="Jesuit High School (Tampa)">Jesuit High School</a> of Tampa Bay has a scholarship named after him for those students who serve in their community.</li>
<li>He is patron of the Novitiate of the USA Midwest province of the Society of Jesus.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Criticism">Criticism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Criticism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Members of the <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_(Chile)" title="Conservative Party (Chile)">Conservative Party</a> denounced what they saw as Hurtado's endorsement of the <a href="/wiki/National_Falange" title="National Falange">National Falange</a>, a party founded after young social Catholics split from the conservative party. There were also attacks from the left. An anonymous article published in <i>Policarpo</i> in 1982 called Hurtado "the last prophet of the bourgeoisie" while it contrasts him unfavorably with the figure of Enrique Alvear who is hailed as the "first Pastor of the Church of the poor in Chile". <a href="/wiki/Clotario_Blest" title="Clotario Blest">Clotario Blest</a>, who like Hurtado was also intellectually indebted to <a href="/wiki/Fernando_Vives" title="Fernando Vives">Fernando Vives</a>, is reported to have distanced himself from Hurtado.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Media">Media</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Media">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the 1990s there was a short TV series dedicated to him, named <i>Crónica de un Hombre Santo</i> (English: <i>Chronicles of a Holy Man</i>). Four actors portrayed Hurtado, from his childhood to his last years; popular <a href="/wiki/Telenovela" title="Telenovela">telenovela</a> actor Cristián Campos played the adult Hurtado during his ministry.
</p><p>Hurtado remains very popular in Chile to this day. His Facebook fan page has more than 50,000 followers.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Main_works">Main works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alberto_Hurtado&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Main works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><i>¿Es Chile un pais católico?</i> (English: <i>Is Chile a Catholic country?</i>), Santiago (Chile), 1941.</li>
