<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Thalelæus</div><br /><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Hermit</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a>, Asia Minor</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;460</span><br />Syria</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">27 February</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Thalelæus</b> (or <b>Thalilaeus Epiklautos</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el"><b>Θαλλελαίου</b></span>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;460</span>) was a 5th-century Syrian hermit known for continuous weeping. His feast day is 27 February.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thalel%C3%A6us&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Thalelæus was born in Cilicia in Asia Minor.
He was ordained as a presbyter in the monastery of Saint <a href="/wiki/Sabbas_the_Sanctified" title="Sabbas the Sanctified">Sabbas the Sanctified</a>.
He later moved to a graveyard beside a run-down pagan temple in Syria near Habala, where he lived in a tent.
Travellers passing by were afraid of the unclean spirits that haunted the place.
The monk lived alone, praying day and night, and eventually drove away the demons.
He built himself a tiny cramped hut where he lived for another ten years.
Through his example and miracles he made many converts, who built a church in place of the pagan temple.
He died about 460.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVenerable_Thalelaeus_..._Orthodox_Church_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVenerable_Thalelaeus_..._Orthodox_Church-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thalel%C3%A6us&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a> wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921), 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Thalilæus (St.) (Feb. 27)<br />(5th cent.) A hermit in <a href="/wiki/Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a> (Asia Minor), who passed sixty years in the practice of the most severe penance.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921252_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921252-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Hone.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hone's_account">Hone's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thalel%C3%A6us&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Hone&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/William_Hone" title="William Hone">William Hone</a> in his <i>Every Day Book, Or, A Guide to the Year</i> wrote, under February 27,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St. Thalilæus.<br />This saint was a weeper in Syria. He hermitized on a mountain during sixty years, wept almost without intermission for his sins, and lived for ten years in a wooden cage.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHone1826155_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHone1826155-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thalel%C3%A6us&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> (1710–1773) wrote in his <i>Lives of the Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i> under February 27, 
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St. Thalilæus, a Cilician, lived a recluse on a mountain in Syria. He shut himself up ten years in an open cage of wood. <a href="/wiki/Theodoret" title="Theodoret">Theodoret</a> asked him why he had chosen so singular a practice. The penitent answered: "I punish my criminal body, that God seeing my affliction for my sins, may be moved to pardon them, and to deliver me from, or at least to mitigate, the excessive torments of the world to come, which I have deserved." See Theodoret, Phil. c. 28. <a href="/wiki/John_Mosch" class="mw-redirect" title="John Mosch">John Mosch</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Meadow" title="Spiritual Meadow">Spiritual Meadow</a>, c. 59. p. 872, relates that Thalilæus, the Cilician, spent sixty years in an ascetic life, weeping almost without intermission; and that he used to say to those who came to him: "Time is allowed us by the divine mercy for repentance and satisfaction, and woe to us if we neglect it."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1798271_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1798271-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Baring-Gould.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Baring-Gould's_account">Baring-Gould's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thalel%C3%A6us&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Baring-Gould&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a> (1834–1924) in his <i>The Lives Of The Saints</i> wrote under February 27,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>S. THALELÆUS, H. (about A.D. 460.)<br />[Greek Menaea. No commemoration in Western Church. Authority: —Theodoret, Philothaus, c. xxviii.]
</p><p>NOT only have I heard of this man from others," says Theodoret, " but I saw him myself." Thalelæus erected for his habitation a small hut against an idol shrine, near Gabala, to which many people resorted, and where they offered sacrifice to devils. The evil spirits, enraged at his thus assaulting them in their sanctuary, endeavoured by hideous clamours and frightful apparitions, to scare the Christian hermit away&#160;; but every effort of demons and idolaters to drive him from this shrine proved ineffectual. Thalelæus succeeded in converting many who came as votaries to the temple, and persuaded them to bend their necks to the sweet yoke of Christ's law.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897444_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897444-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> With many of these converts Theodoret conversed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897444–445_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897444–445-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
After that Thalelæus had lived thus a while, he devised for himself a strange and horrible penance. He made two wheels, and then joined them by pieces of wood into a species of barrel, but open between the bars. He enclosed himself within this case, which was so low that his chin rested on his knees, and remained therein for many years. He had been ten years in it when Theodoret saw him. This frightful self-immolation is by no means to be regarded as deserving of imitation. But it was called forth by peculiar circumstances, and for a special purpose. The rude people of Syria could be impressed no other way. To win these souls from heathenism this phase of the ascetic life was evoked, it served its purpose, and passed away.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897445_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaring-Gould1897445-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
