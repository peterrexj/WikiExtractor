					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">German Augustinian canon regular and the first Bishop of Livonia</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" <p><b>Saint Meinhard</b> (b. 1134 or 1136 - died August 14 or October 11, 1196) was a German <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a> and the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Livonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Livonia">Bishop of Livonia</a>. His life was described in the <a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Henry_of_Livonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicle of Henry of Livonia">Chronicle of Henry of Livonia</a>. His body rests in the now-Lutheran <a href="/wiki/Riga_Cathedral" title="Riga Cathedral">Riga Cathedral</a>, as his remains were moved to <a href="/wiki/Riga" title="Riga">Riga</a> in 1226. He is venerated as the apostle of the Church in <a href="/wiki/Latvia" title="Latvia">Latvia</a> (Livonia in the Middle Ages).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a canon at the <a href="/wiki/Segeberg" title="Segeberg">Segeberg</a> Abbey in <a href="/wiki/Holstein" title="Holstein">Holstein</a>, Meinhard was possibly inspired by <a href="/wiki/Vicelinus" title="Vicelinus">Vicelinus</a> missionary work among the <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fonne_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fonne-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Meinhard traveled with <a href="/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck" title="Lübeck">Lübeck</a> merchants, probably trading costly furs, to <a href="/wiki/Livonia" title="Livonia">Livonia</a> on a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_mission" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic mission">Catholic mission</a> in the 1170s or early 1180s to convert pagan <a href="/wiki/Semigallians" title="Semigallians">Semigallians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Latgalians" title="Latgalians">Latgalians</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Livonians" title="Livonians">Livonians</a> into Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He settled on the <a href="/wiki/Daugava_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Daugava River">Daugava River</a> at <a href="/wiki/Ik%C5%A1%C4%B7ile" title="Ikšķile">Ikšķile</a> (German: <i>Üxküll</i>) southeast of <a href="/wiki/Riga" title="Riga">Riga</a>. In 1185–1186, he built a stone church, dedicated to Our Lady. Following an attack by the <a href="/wiki/Lithuanians" title="Lithuanians">Lithuanians</a>, Meinhard brought stonemasons from <a href="/wiki/Gotland" title="Gotland">Gotland</a> to build a fortress to defend against future attacks from raiders from Lithuania looking to carry off slaves.<sup id="cite_ref-turnbull_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-turnbull-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> These were the first known stone buildings among the <a href="/wiki/Baltic_tribes" class="mw-redirect" title="Baltic tribes">Baltic tribes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-turnbull_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-turnbull-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-jovai_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jovai-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Remains of the church survive to this day. Another stone castle was built in <a href="/wiki/Salaspils" title="Salaspils">Salaspils</a> (German: <i>Holm</i>) as a gift to newly converted pagans. But the inhabitants rebelled and attacked Meinhard attempting to drive him out of Livonia.<sup id="cite_ref-turnbull_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-turnbull-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When he briefly returned to Germany in 1186, Meinhard was consecrated as Bishop of Üxküll (present-day <a href="/wiki/Ik%C5%A1%C4%B7ile" title="Ikšķile">Ikšķile</a>, <a href="/wiki/Latvia" title="Latvia">Latvia</a>) by <a href="/wiki/Hartwig_of_Uthlede" title="Hartwig of Uthlede">Hartwig of Uthlede</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Bremen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Bremen">Archbishop of Bremen</a>. The new bishopric was confirmed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_III" title="Pope Clement III">Pope Clement III</a> in September 1188.<sup id="cite_ref-fonne_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fonne-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1190, Clement III allowed any monk to join Meinhard's mission. New <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_III" title="Pope Celestine III">Pope Celestine III</a> showed more enthusiastic support for the mission in his letter in April 1193, authorizing active missionary recruitment, making exceptions to rules governing monks' food and clothing, and granting <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgences</a> to those who joined the mission.<sup id="cite_ref-fonne_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fonne-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Among the recruits was <a href="/wiki/Theoderich_von_Treyden" title="Theoderich von Treyden">Theodorich</a> from <a href="/wiki/Loccum_Abbey" title="Loccum Abbey">Loccum Abbey</a>, who started a mission in <a href="/wiki/Turaida" title="Turaida">Turaida</a> (German: <i>Treyden</i>). Meinhard initially converted the pagans by peaceful means, but faced with resistance and <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostasy</a>, he turned to the idea of a <a href="/wiki/Crusade" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusade">crusade</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fonne_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fonne-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Meinhard was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Berthold_of_Hanover" title="Berthold of Hanover">Berthold of Hanover</a> and <a href="/wiki/Albert_of_Riga" title="Albert of Riga">Albert of Riga</a>, who began the <a href="/wiki/Livonian_Crusade" title="Livonian Crusade">Livonian Crusade</a> and established the <a href="/wiki/Livonian_Brothers_of_the_Sword" title="Livonian Brothers of the Sword">Livonian Brothers of the Sword</a>, a crusading <a href="/wiki/Military_order_(society)" class="mw-redirect" title="Military order (society)">military order</a>, in Riga.
</p><p>In September 1993, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> paid a visit to the <a href="/wiki/Baltic_states" title="Baltic states">Baltic states</a> and solemnly proclaimed on 8 September of that year that he formally restores the veneration of Saint Meinhard on 14 August each year, which is considered as an <a href="/wiki/Equipollent" class="mw-redirect" title="Equipollent">equipollent</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>.
</p>
