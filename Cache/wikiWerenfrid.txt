<p>Saint <b>Werenfried</b> (or <b>Werenfrid, Werenfridus</b>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;780</span>) was an English <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk, <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Priesthood in the Catholic Church">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christian_mission" title="Christian mission">missionary</a> among the <a href="/wiki/Frisians" title="Frisians">Frisians</a>.
His <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercession</a> is thought to relieve the pain of <a href="/wiki/Arthritis" title="Arthritis">arthritis</a> and to help gardeners.
His feast day is 14 August.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Werenfried_of_Elst&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg/220px-Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="362" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg/330px-Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg/440px-Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="237" data-file-height="390" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Coat_of_arms_of_Overbetuwe.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Coat of arms of Overbetuwe showing the saint's coffin floating downstream</div></div></div>
<p>Werenfried was a Benedictine monk.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Werenfridus_CatholicSaints.info_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Werenfridus_CatholicSaints.info-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
He was probably born in <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> and spent time in Ireland before becoming a missionary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873204_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873204-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
He worked with Saint <a href="/wiki/Willibrord" title="Willibrord">Willibrord</a> of <a href="/wiki/Echternach" title="Echternach">Echternach</a> to convert the Frisians to Christianity.
He died around 780 at <a href="/wiki/Arnhem" title="Arnhem">Arnhem</a>, what is now the Netherlands.
His coffin was placed in a boat that was washed down the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a> and came to rest in <a href="/wiki/Elst,_Gelderland" title="Elst, Gelderland">Elst</a>.
The <a href="/wiki/Overbetuwe" title="Overbetuwe">Overbetuwe</a> municipal coat of arms depicts this event.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESaint_Werenfridus_CatholicSaints.info_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESaint_Werenfridus_CatholicSaints.info-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The tomb of St. Werenfried in the 8th century church in what is now Elst attracted pilgrims who wanted release from arthritic pain, or who wanted to become better gardeners.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKoutrafouriSanders2013132_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKoutrafouriSanders2013132-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
The church where Werenfridus was originally buried has been called Werenfriduskerk since 1483. 
In 1944 the original Werenfriduskerk was destroyed, but after the Second World War the church was rebuilt. 
Today a small museum has been set up under the church , because the church appears to have been built on two large <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> temples.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDerks2002_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDerks2002-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHeitink200745_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeitink200745-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Werenfried_of_Elst&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a>, wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921),
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Werenfrid (St.) (Aug. 14)<br />(8th cent.) An Englishman, a fellow-labourer with Saint Willibrord among the Frisians. He died at Arnheim (A.D. 780), miracles testifying to his sanctity.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921270_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921270-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Werenfried_of_Elst&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> ( 1710–1773) wrote in his <i>Lives of the Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i>, under November 7,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St Werenfrid, Priest and Confessor.<br />He was an English monk, and, according to <a href="/wiki/Jean_Mabillon" title="Jean Mabillon">Mabillon</a>, accompanied, or, as the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandists</a> rather think, followed Saint Willibrord into Friseland, and assisted him in preaching the gospel. Saint Werenfrid planted the faith in the isle or territory of Betawe, or Batavia, in Holland, lying between the Rhine, the Leck, the Maes, and Merve, especially at Elste, a town in that territory, where he was buried. His tomb was famous for pilgrimages, and the miraculous cures of sick persons, especially those afflicted with the gout. <a href="/wiki/Balderic_of_Utrecht" title="Balderic of Utrecht">Baldericus</a>, the fifteenth bishop of <a href="/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Utrecht" title="Prince-Bishopric of Utrecht">Utrecht</a>, founded there a collegiate church in his honour, with eight canonries. Saint Werenfrid is honoured in Holland on the 14th of August. See his life in <a href="/wiki/Laurentius_Surius" title="Laurentius Surius">Surius</a>, and much more correctly in the Bollandists, on the 28th of August. Also John a Leidis, l.2 c.42. Wilhelmus Heda, p.30. <i>Batavia Sacra</i>, p.42.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1799162_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1799162-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="O.27Hanlon.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="O'Hanlon's_account">O'Hanlon's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Werenfried_of_Elst&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: O&#039;Hanlon&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest,_Elst,_Overbetuwe,_the_Netherlands.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest%2C_Elst%2C_Overbetuwe%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg/220px-Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest%2C_Elst%2C_Overbetuwe%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest%2C_Elst%2C_Overbetuwe%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg/330px-Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest%2C_Elst%2C_Overbetuwe%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest%2C_Elst%2C_Overbetuwe%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg/440px-Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest%2C_Elst%2C_Overbetuwe%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2816" data-file-height="2112" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sculpture_%22Werenfridus%22_by_Alphons_ter_Avest,_Elst,_Overbetuwe,_the_Netherlands.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sculpture "Werenfridus" by Alphons ter Avest in Elst, Overbetuwe</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/John_O%27Hanlon_(writer)" title="John O&#39;Hanlon (writer)">John O'Hanlon</a> (1821–1905) in his <i>Lives of the Irish saints</i> wrote,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St. Werenfrid, Priest and Confessor, Missionary in Frisia. [<i>Eighth Century.</i>] 
</p><p>
... He was an Englishman by birth, and probably he was born in the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>. For greater improvement in the science of the saints, he forsook country and friends, to dedicate himself wholly to the service of his fellow creatures. He thereupon passed over into Ireland, where he served God in solitude, and recollection. He is said to have been one of those twelve apostolic men belonging to the English nation, who were destined for a missionary career. With their leader St. Willebrord, these were sent out of Ireland by <a href="/wiki/St._Egbert" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Egbert">St. Egbert</a>. These were destined to carry the word of life to the Frisons, Saxons, and other pagans in Germany. The exact time of St. Werenfrid's arrival there, is not so well known. He was one of those Gospel preachers, however, to whom the Netherlanders were indebted for their Christian teaching. He particularly planted the faith and church of Christ in the Isle of Batavia or Betuwe. He likewise converted the inhabitants of Medemblick, Durostadt, Elst, and Westerwort. His admirable virtues were very remarkable.
</p><p>The writer of his Acts assures us, that it was impossible to express how rich he was in all good work, and how careful he had been in administering comfort to the afflicted. He was incomparable for his humanity, while he was an exemplar of charity towards the poor. He was assiduous in his watching, and rigorous in his fasting. He was diligent in prayer, and he excelled in chastity. In fine, he was conspicuous for all good qualities. Great success attended his labours in gaining souls to Christ. In a good old age, he received the reward of his labour. He departed some time in the eighth century. His body was interred at Elst. There formerly stood a collegiate church dedicated to God in his name. This was much frequented, because miracles were often wrought within it. St. Werenfrid's feast is kept as a Double in the diocese of Utrecht, on the 27th day of August. The 14th was the day of his decease, however, according to the best accounts. He is likewise entered in the anonymous Calendar of national saints, at the 14th of August, as published in O'Sullevan Beare's work.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873204–205_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEO&#39;Hanlon1873204–205-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
