<p><b>Józef Cebula</b> (23 March 1902 – 9 May 1941) was a Polish priest of the <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Oblates_of_Mary_Immaculate" title="Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate">Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate</a> (OMI).
</p><p>Born on 23 March 1902 into a modest family in Malnia, Poland, Joseph Cebula was the eldest of three children. He suffered <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> as a youth and was declared incurable at first; but after his recovery, he went to an Oblate shrine and shared his story with Father Jan Pawolik.  Pawolik suggested he study at the minor seminary at Krotoszyn. Cebula completed his secondary studies there. In 1921 he began his novitiate at Markowice. He studied philosophy in Liege, Belgium, and finished his theological studies at Lubliniec.<sup id="cite_ref-China_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-China-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was ordained as a priest on 5 June 1927 while still in a seminary.  Father Cebula became a superior at the Oblate seminaries in 1931, and became novice master at <a href="/wiki/Markowice,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship" title="Markowice, Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship">Markowice</a> in 1937.<sup id="cite_ref-Assumption_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Assumption-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two years later, when <a href="/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland" title="Invasion of Poland">the Germans occupied Poland</a>, they declared loyalty to the Church illegal. In October 1939 the 100 member community at Markowice was placed under house arrest, and set to work as farm laborers. On 4 May 1940, the Oblate novices at Markowice were sent to the concentration camp at <a href="/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp" title="Dachau concentration camp">Dachau</a> in <a href="/wiki/Upper_Bavaria" title="Upper Bavaria">Upper Bavaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.  However, Father Cebula continued to minister as a priest in secret despite the ban on it. After being denounced for administering the sacraments to the sick he was arrested by the SS on April 2, 1941 and sent to the camp at Inowroclaw.<sup id="cite_ref-China_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-China-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On April 7, he was taken to a <a href="/wiki/Mauthausen-Gusen_concentration_camp" class="mw-redirect" title="Mauthausen-Gusen concentration camp">concentration camp</a> at <a href="/wiki/Mauthausen,_Upper_Austria" title="Mauthausen, Upper Austria">Mauthausen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> and was harassed and forced to hard labor. On 9 May, Father Cebula suddenly summoned up his strength and said, "It is not you who are in charge. God will judge you." The Nazis ordered him to run with a rock on his back, towards the camp's barbed wire fence, where a guard shot him to death with a submachine gun and declared that Father Cebula "was shot while trying to escape".  His body was taken to a <a href="/wiki/Crematorium" title="Crematorium">crematorium</a> and burned to ashes.<sup id="cite_ref-Assumption_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Assumption-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 13 June 1999 as one of the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Martyrs of World War II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
