					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Saint and abbess from Anglo-Saxon England</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the 9th-century queen of King Ethelred, see <a href="/wiki/Wulfthryth_of_Wessex" title="Wulfthryth of Wessex">Wulfthryth of Wessex</a>.</div>
<p>
<b>Wulfthryth</b>, also known as <b>Wilfrida</b> (died c. 1000), was the second known consort of <a href="/wiki/Edgar,_King_of_England" title="Edgar, King of England">Edgar, King of England</a> in the early 960s. Historians disagree whether she was his wife or mistress. Their daughter <a href="/wiki/Edith_of_Wilton" title="Edith of Wilton">Edith</a> was widely regarded in the eleventh century as a saint. In 964 Edgar married <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfthryth_(wife_of_Edgar)" title="Ælfthryth (wife of Edgar)">Ælfthryth</a>, and then or earlier Wulfthryth returned to <a href="/wiki/Wilton_Abbey" title="Wilton Abbey">Wilton Abbey</a>, where she had been brought up, with her daughter. Wulfthryth remained there for the rest of her life as abbess and died on 21 September in an unknown year around 1000. She was regarded as a saint at Wilton, but her cult did not spread more widely.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Primary_sources">Primary sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfthryth_of_Wilton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Primary sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wulfthryth is known to <a href="/wiki/History" title="History">history</a> through several sources, including the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Secgan" class="mw-redirect" title="Secgan">Secgan</a> Manuscript,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/John_of_Worcester" title="John of Worcester">John of Worcester</a>’s Chronicle, <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Osburn" class="mw-redirect" title="Osburn">Osburn</a>’s <i>life of <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a></i>, the 
Life of St Wulfthryth found in <i>The Wilton Chronicle</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Royal_Charter" class="mw-redirect" title="Royal Charter">Royal Charter</a> of King <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceful" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceful">Edgar</a> to Wulfthryth,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and the <i>Vita <a href="/wiki/Saint_Edith_of_Wilton" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Edith of Wilton">Edithae</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Goscelin" title="Goscelin">Goscelin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  The <a href="/wiki/Medieval" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval">medieval</a> source record her as living an exemplary life of sanctity and virtue and her virtues were often contrasted to the machinations of Edgar's second (third?) wife, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfthryth_(wife_of_Edgar)" title="Ælfthryth (wife of Edgar)">Ælfthryth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfthryth_of_Wilton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Edith_of_Wilton.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Edith_of_Wilton.jpg/150px-Edith_of_Wilton.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Edith_of_Wilton.jpg/225px-Edith_of_Wilton.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Edith_of_Wilton.jpg/300px-Edith_of_Wilton.jpg 2x" data-file-width="418" data-file-height="418" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Edith_of_Wilton.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Near contemporary drawing of Edith.</div></div></div> 
<p>Wulfthryth was an English <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">noblewoman</a>, a cousin of <a href="/wiki/Wulfhilda_of_Barking" title="Wulfhilda of Barking">Wulfhild</a>, born about 937, whom <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceful" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceful">King Edgar of England</a> carried off from the <a href="/wiki/Nunnery" class="mw-redirect" title="Nunnery">nunnery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Wilton_Abbey" title="Wilton Abbey">Wilton Abbey</a> and took to his residence at <a href="/wiki/Kemsing" title="Kemsing">Kemsing</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Sevenoaks" title="Sevenoaks">Sevenoaks</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> While in Kent, Wulfthryth gave birth to a daughter, <a href="/wiki/Edith_of_Wilton" title="Edith of Wilton">Edith</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After at least a year, Wulfthryth returned to <a href="/wiki/Wilton_Abbey" title="Wilton Abbey">Wilton Abbey</a>, taking Edith with her.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> She later became head of the abbey and outlived her daughter.
</p><p>According to early monastic texts, under <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a> <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a>'s direction Edgar did <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> for this crime by not wearing his crown for seven years.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  As part of his penance, Edgar gave Wilfrida six estates in <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a> which she passed on to Wilton Abbey in 965<a href="/wiki/Anno_Domini" title="Anno Domini">AD</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  Some form of bride abduction, often more simulated force than actual, by this time, may have existed as a vestige of earlier Anglo-Saxon tradition, and historians have alternatively referred to Wilfrida as Edgar's concubine or his second wife, although never as a captive. Given the religious customs of the time, his penance was probably related to his violation of the sanctity of her religious vocation, rather than to any personal affront to Wilfrida. It is clear that the two may have had a continuing friendship long after her return to Wilton. In any event, Edgar seems to have acknowledged Edith as his daughter; the relationship may have been considered a marriage, despite the formal church sanction, as was the custom of the time, and if so Edith was a legitimate daughter.
</p><p>Wulfthryth continued to have considerable influence upon Edgar after her return to Wilton. She was able to stop <a href="/wiki/Bailiff" title="Bailiff">bailiffs</a> from arresting a <a href="/wiki/Thief" class="mw-redirect" title="Thief">thief</a> who had taken <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary" title="Sanctuary">sanctuary</a> in the Abbey<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and was able to secure the release of two Wilton <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priests</a> who had been imprisoned by the <a href="/wiki/Reeve_(England)" title="Reeve (England)">reeve</a> of <a href="/wiki/Wilton,_Wiltshire" title="Wilton, Wiltshire">Wilton</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
As Abbess of Wilton, she built a stone wall around the <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">Abbey</a> and also used her wealth to build up the abbey's collection of <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Goscelin calls her the "hidden treasure and light" of the Abbey, and she was held in high esteem during her life.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and she is credited with <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> during her lifetime,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Alms" title="Alms">Alms</a> giving.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfthryth_of_Wilton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Mary,_Wilton.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/St_Mary%2C_Wilton.jpg/150px-St_Mary%2C_Wilton.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="113" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/St_Mary%2C_Wilton.jpg/225px-St_Mary%2C_Wilton.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/St_Mary%2C_Wilton.jpg/300px-St_Mary%2C_Wilton.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Mary,_Wilton.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Mary's church, Wilton</div></div></div>
<p>Both Wulfthryth and her daughter <a href="/wiki/Edith_of_Wilton" title="Edith of Wilton">Edith</a> were regarded as <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a> after their lifetimes.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Wulfthryth died at Wilton on 21 September, probably in the year 1000, and was buried before the main altar of the <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Church,_Wilton" title="St Mary&#39;s Church, Wilton">Wilton Abbey church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her Feast Day is 13 September.
</p>
