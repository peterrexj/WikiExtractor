<p><b>Osmund</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died&#160;3 December 1099), Count of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9es" title="Sées">Sées</a>, was a <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> <a href="/wiki/Nobleman" class="mw-redirect" title="Nobleman">noble</a> and <a href="/wiki/Clergyman" class="mw-redirect" title="Clergyman">clergyman</a>. Following the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman conquest of England</a>, he served as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor" title="Lord Chancellor">Lord Chancellor</a> (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1070</span>–1078) and as the second <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salisbury" title="Bishop of Salisbury">bishop of Salisbury</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Old_Sarum" title="Old Sarum">Old Sarum</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Osmund&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Osmund, a native of <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, accompanied <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William, Duke of Normandy</a> to England, and was made Chancellor of the realm about 1070.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook83_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook83-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was employed in many civil transactions and was engaged as one of the Chief Commissioners for drawing up the <a href="/wiki/Domesday_Book" title="Domesday Book">Domesday Book</a>.
</p><p>Osmund became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salisbury" title="Bishop of Salisbury">bishop of Salisbury</a> by authority of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Parker_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parker-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and was consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a> around 3 June 1078.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook270_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook270-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His diocese comprised the <a href="/wiki/County" title="County">counties</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dorset" title="Dorset">Dorset</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a>, having absorbed the former bishoprics of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sherborne" title="Bishop of Sherborne">Sherborne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ramsbury" title="Bishop of Ramsbury">Ramsbury</a> under its incumbent <a href="/wiki/Herman_(bishop_of_Salisbury)" title="Herman (bishop of Salisbury)">Herman</a> at the <a href="/wiki/1075_Council_of_London" class="mw-redirect" title="1075 Council of London">1075 Council of London</a>. In his <i><a href="/wiki/Gesta_pontificum_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Gesta pontificum Anglorum">Acts of the English Bishops</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> describes medieval Salisbury as a fortress rather than a city, placed on a high hill, surrounded by a massive wall. <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Blois" title="Peter of Blois">Peter of Blois</a> later referred to the castle and church as <i>"the <a href="/wiki/Ark_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Ark of God">ark of God</a> shut up in the temple of <a href="/wiki/Ba%27al" class="mw-redirect" title="Ba&#39;al">Baal</a>."</i>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry I</a>'s biographer <a href="/wiki/C._Warren_Hollister" title="C. Warren Hollister">C. Warren Hollister</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> suggests the possibility that Osmund was in part responsible for Henry's education; Henry was consistently in the bishop's company during his formative years, around 1080 to 1086.
</p><p>In 1086 Osmund was present at the <a href="/wiki/Great_Gemot" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Gemot">Great Gemot</a> held at <a href="/wiki/Old_Sarum" title="Old Sarum">Old Sarum</a> when the <a href="/wiki/Domesday_Book" title="Domesday Book">Domesday Book</a> was accepted and the great landowners swore fealty to the sovereign.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Osmund died on the night of 3 December 1099,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook270_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook270-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and was succeeded, after the see had been vacant for eight years, by <a href="/wiki/Roger_of_Salisbury" title="Roger of Salisbury">Roger of Salisbury</a>, a statesman and counsellor of <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry I</a>. His remains were buried at Old Sarum, translated to <a href="/wiki/Salisbury,_Wiltshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Salisbury, Wiltshire">New Salisbury</a> on 23 July 1457, and deposited in the <a href="/wiki/Lady_Chapel" class="mw-redirect" title="Lady Chapel">Lady Chapel</a>, where his sumptuous shrine was destroyed under <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>. A flat slab with the simple inscription <a href="/wiki/Roman_numerals" title="Roman numerals"><span class="smallcaps"><span style="font-variant: small-caps; text-transform: lowercase;">MXCIX</span></span></a> has lain in various parts of the cathedral. In 1644 it was in the middle of the Lady Chapel. It is now under the easternmost arch on the south side.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Osmund&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG/220px-Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG/330px-Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG/440px-Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="4000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Vitrail_S%C3%A9es_Saint_Osmondus.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Osmond</div></div></div>
<p>Osmund's work was threefold&#160;— first, the building of the <a href="/wiki/Old_Sarum_Cathedral" title="Old Sarum Cathedral">cathedral at Old Sarum</a>, which was consecrated on 5 April 1092.<sup id="cite_ref-Parker_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parker-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Second was the constitution of a <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral body</a>. This was framed on the usual <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> model, with <a href="/wiki/Dean_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dean (religion)">dean</a>, <a href="/wiki/Precentor" title="Precentor">precentor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chancellor" title="Chancellor">chancellor</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Treasurer" title="Treasurer">treasurer</a>, whose duties were exactly defined, some thirty-two <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Subdean" class="mw-redirect" title="Subdean">subdean</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Succentor" title="Succentor">succentor</a>. All save the last two were bound to a residence. These canons were <i>"secular",</i> each living in his own house. Their duties were to be special companions and advisers of the bishop, to carry out with fitting solemnity the full round of liturgical services and to do missionary work in the surrounding districts. There was formed a school for clergy of which the chancellor was the head. The cathedral was thoroughly constituted <i>"the Mother Church"</i> of the diocese. Osmund's canons were renowned for their musical talent and their zeal for learning, and had great influence on the foundation of other cathedral bodies.<sup id="cite_ref-Parker_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parker-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Third was the formation of the "<a href="/wiki/Sarum_Use" class="mw-redirect" title="Sarum Use">Sarum Use</a>". Osmund made selections of the practices he saw around him and arranged the offices and services. He initiated some revisions to the extant Celtic-Anglo-Saxon rite and the local adaptations of the Roman rite, drawing on both Norman and <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> traditions. Given the similarities between the liturgy in Rouen and that of Sarum, it appears that the liturgical practices of <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> likely inspired the Sarum <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_books" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgical books">liturgical books</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Intended primarily for his own diocese, the Ordinal of Osmund, regulating the Divine Office, Mass, and Calendar, was used, within a hundred years, almost throughout England, Wales, and Ireland, and was introduced into Scotland about 1250. 
</p><p>The <i>"Register of St. Osmund"</i> is a collection of documents without any chronological arrangement, gathered together after his time, divided roughly into two parts: the <i>"Consuetudinary"</i> (<a href="/wiki/Rolls_Series" title="Rolls Series">Rolls Series</a>, 1–185, and in Rock, vol. III, 1–110), styled <i>"De Officiis Ecclesiasticis"</i>, and a series of documents and charters, all more or less bearing on the construction of the cathedral at Old Sarum, the foundation of the cathedral body, the treasures belonging to it, and the history of dependent churches. The existing <a href="/wiki/Consuetudinary_(book)" class="mw-redirect" title="Consuetudinary (book)"><i>"Consuetudinary"</i></a> was taken from an older copy, re-arranged with additions and modifications and ready probably when Richard Poore consecrated the cathedral at New Salisbury in 1225. A copy, almost verbatim the same as this, was taken from the older book for the use of St. Patrick's, Dublin, which was erected into a cathedral and modelled on the church at Sarum by <a href="/wiki/Henry_de_Loundres" title="Henry de Loundres">Henry de Loundres</a> who was bishop from 1213 to 1228.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> in summing up Osmund's character says he was "so eminent for chastity that common fame would itself blush to speak otherwise than truthfully concerning his virtue. Stern he might appear to penitents, but not more severe to them than to himself. Free from ambition, he neither imprudently wasted his own substance, nor sought the wealth of others".
</p><p>Osmund gathered together a good library for his canons. A late-medieval source notes, somewhat disdainfully, that even as a bishop he would scribe, illuminate and bind books himself; by that period this was eccentric behaviour, but it was not so in 11th-century England.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  At one time Osmund thought <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Anselm" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Anselm">Archbishop Anselm</a> too unyielding and needlessly scrupulous in the <a href="/wiki/Dispute_concerning_investitures" class="mw-redirect" title="Dispute concerning investitures">dispute concerning investitures</a> and in 1095 at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Rockingham" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Rockingham">Council of Rockingham</a> favoured the king. But after the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Lateran Council">Lateran Council</a> in 1099, he boldly sided with the archbishop and the beautiful anecdote is related, showing his simple sincerity, how when Anselm was on his way to <a href="/wiki/Windsor,_Berkshire" title="Windsor, Berkshire">Windsor</a>, Osmund knelt before him and received his forgiveness. He had a great reverence for <a href="/wiki/Aldhelm" title="Aldhelm">St. Aldhelm</a> who 300 years before as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sherborne_(historic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sherborne (historic)">Bishop of Sherborne</a> had been Osmund's predecessor. He officiated at the saint's translation to a more fitting shrine at <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury,_Wiltshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Malmesbury, Wiltshire">Malmesbury</a> and helped Lanfranc to obtain his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>. Abbot Warin gave him a bone of the left arm of St. Aldhelm which he kept at Sarum where <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> were wrought. In 1228 the Bishop of Sarum and the canons applied to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a> for Osmund's canonization but not until some 200 years afterwards on 1 January 1457, was the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">bull</a> issued by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Callixtus_III" title="Pope Callixtus III">Callistus III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOSalis_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOSalis-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1472 a special <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgence</a> was granted by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Sixtus IV</a> for a visit to his cathedral on his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">festival</a> and a convocation held in <a href="/wiki/St._Paul%27s_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Paul&#39;s Cathedral">St. Paul's</a> in 1481 fixed 4 December as the day to commemorate him. A house at <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Wordsworth%27s_School" title="Bishop Wordsworth&#39;s School">Bishop Wordsworth's School</a> is named for him. He is commemorated by a statue in <a href="/wiki/Table_of_the_Statuary_of_the_West_Front_of_Salisbury_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Table of the Statuary of the West Front of Salisbury Cathedral">niche 178</a> on the west front of <a href="/wiki/Salisbury_Cathedral" title="Salisbury Cathedral">Salisbury Cathedral</a>.
</p><p>Osmund's canonization took almost 230 years,<sup id="cite_ref-Swanson315_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swanson315-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> with papal proceedings that started in 1228 not concluding until 1457.<sup id="cite_ref-Swanson148_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Swanson148-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Osmund is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/July_16" title="July 16">16 July</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
