<p><b>Hyacinth</b> (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Święty Jacek</i> or <i>Jacek Odrowąż</i>; ca. 1185 &#8211; 15 August 1257) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> who worked to reform women's <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> in his native <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>. He was a Doctor of Sacred Studies, educated in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>.

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hyacinth_of_Poland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Called the "Apostle of the North", Hyacinth was the son of Eustachius Koński of the noble <a href="/wiki/House_of_Odrow%C4%85%C5%BC" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Odrowąż">family of Odrowąż</a>. He was born in 1185 at the castle of Lanka, at <a href="/wiki/Kamie%C5%84_%C5%9Al%C4%85ski" title="Kamień Śląski">Kamień</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Silesia" title="Silesia">Silesia</a>, Poland. A near relative of <a href="/wiki/Ceslaus" title="Ceslaus">Ceslaus</a>, he made his studies in notable cities: <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, and at the latter place merited the title of Doctor of Law and Divinity. On his return to <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> he was given a prebend at <a href="/wiki/Sandomierz" title="Sandomierz">Sandomierz</a>, a medieval centre of administration in the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Sandomierz" title="Duchy of Sandomierz">south-eastern part of the country</a>. He subsequently accompanied his uncle <a href="/wiki/Iwo_Odrow%C4%85%C5%BC" title="Iwo Odrowąż">Ivo Konski</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Krak%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Kraków">Bishop of Kraków</a>, to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, he witnessed a miracle performed by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Dominic of Osma</a>, and became a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Ceslaus" title="Ceslaus">Ceslaus</a> and two attendants of the Bishop of Kraków, Herman and Henry. In 1219 <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/Honorius_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Honorius III">Honorius III</a> invited Dominic and his followers to take up residence at the ancient Roman <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Santa Sabina</a>, which they did by early 1220. Before that time, the friars had only a temporary residence in Rome at the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of <a href="/wiki/San_Sisto_Vecchio" title="San Sisto Vecchio">San Sisto Vecchio</a> which Honorius III had given to Dominic in about 1218, intending it to be used for a reformation of Roman <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> under Dominic's guidance.  Hyacinth and his companions were among the first to enter the convent. They were also the first <a href="/wiki/List_of_people_associated_with_the_Pontifical_University_of_St._Thomas_Aquinas" title="List of people associated with the Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas">alumni of the <i>studium</i></a> of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Santa Sabina</a> out of which would grow the 16th century College of Saint Thomas at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_sopra_Minerva" title="Santa Maria sopra Minerva">Santa Maria sopra Minerva</a>, which became the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_Saint_Thomas_Aquinas" title="Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas">Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas</a> in the 20th century.  After an abbreviated <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a>, Hyacinth and his companions received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of the Order from Dominic himself in 1220.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The young friars were then sent back to their homeland to establish the Dominican Order in <a href="/wiki/History_of_Poland_during_the_Piast_dynasty" title="History of Poland during the Piast dynasty">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Kyiv" class="mw-redirect" title="Principality of Kyiv">Kyiv</a>. As Hyacinth and his three companions traveled back to Kraków, he set up new monasteries with his companions as superiors, until finally he was the only one left to continue on to Kraków. Hyacinth went throughout <a href="/wiki/Northern_Europe" title="Northern Europe">Northern Europe</a> <a href="/wiki/Proselytism" title="Proselytism">spreading the faith</a>. He died in the year 1257.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Tradition holds that he also <a href="/wiki/Evangelism" title="Evangelism">evangelized</a> throughout <a href="/wiki/Sweden" title="Sweden">Sweden</a>, <a href="/wiki/Norway" title="Norway">Norway</a>, <a href="/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark">Denmark</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prussia" title="Prussia">Prussia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a>. However, these travels are heavily disputed and are not supported by the earliest <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> of Hyacinth.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hyacinth_of_Poland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Hyacinth-01.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Saint_Hyacinth-01.jpg/220px-Saint_Hyacinth-01.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="185" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Saint_Hyacinth-01.jpg/330px-Saint_Hyacinth-01.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Saint_Hyacinth-01.jpg 2x" data-file-width="357" data-file-height="300" /></a><figcaption>Hyacinth with a <a href="/wiki/Monstrance" title="Monstrance">monstrance</a> and a statue of Mary, a detail from <i>Three Dominican Saints</i> by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Piazzetta" title="Giovanni Battista Piazzetta">Giovanni Battista Piazzetta</a> (1738), in the <a href="/wiki/Gesuati" title="Gesuati">Gesuati</a> Church, <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, Italy</figcaption></figure>
<p>One of the major miracles attributed to Hyacinth came about during a <a href="/wiki/Mongol" class="mw-redirect" title="Mongol">Mongol</a> <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Kyiv_(1240)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Kyiv (1240)">attack on Kyiv</a>. As the friars prepared to flee the invading forces, Hyacinth went to save the <a href="/wiki/Ciborium_(container)" title="Ciborium (container)">ciborium</a> containing the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Church_tabernacle" title="Church tabernacle">tabernacle</a> in the monastery <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a>, when he heard the voice of <a href="/wiki/Mary,_the_mother_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary, the mother of Jesus">Mary, the mother of Jesus</a>, asking him to take her, too.
</p><p>Hyacinth lifted the large, stone statue of Mary, as well as the ciborium. He was easily able to carry both, despite the fact that the statue weighed far more than he could normally lift. Thus, he saved them both. For this reason, he is usually shown holding a <a href="/wiki/Monstrance" title="Monstrance">monstrance</a> (though they did not come into use until several centuries later)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and a statue of Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a> exclamation <i>Święty Jacku z pierogami!</i> ("St. Hyacinth and his <a href="/wiki/Pierogi" title="Pierogi">pierogi</a>!") is an old-time saying, a call for help in some hopeless circumstance.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It has derived from two legends. One of them is about his visit on July 13, 1238, to <a href="/wiki/Ko%C5%9Bcielec,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship" title="Kościelec, Lesser Poland Voivodeship">Kościelec</a>. During his visit, a <a href="/wiki/Hailstorm" class="mw-redirect" title="Hailstorm">hailstorm</a> broke out, destroying crops and leaving people with the terrible prospect of poverty and <a href="/wiki/Famine" title="Famine">famine</a>. Hyacinth told them to pray. The next day, the crops were miraculously restored. The people then treated Hyacinth to pierogi made from those crops as a token of gratitude. The second legend mentions Hyacinth feeding people with pierogi during a famine caused by the <a href="/wiki/First_Mongol_invasion_of_Poland" title="First Mongol invasion of Poland">Mongol invasion of 1241</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hyacinth_of_Poland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The tomb of Hyacinth is in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Holy_Trinity,_Krak%C3%B3w" title="Basilica of Holy Trinity, Kraków">Basilica of Holy Trinity</a> in <a href="/wiki/Krakow" class="mw-redirect" title="Krakow">Kraków</a>, Poland, in a chapel that bears his name.<sup id="cite_ref-DiPippo_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DiPippo-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hyacinth was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 17 April 1594 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and his memorial day is celebrated on 17 August. In 1686 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a> named him a patron of <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of those in danger of <a href="/wiki/Drowning" title="Drowning">drowning</a> and <a href="/wiki/Weight_lifting" class="mw-redirect" title="Weight lifting">weight lifting</a>.
</p><p>Among notable churches in <a href="/wiki/North_America" title="North America">North America</a> dedicated to Hyacinth of Poland is <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Hyacinth" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Hyacinth">St. Hyacinth's Basilica</a> in <a href="/wiki/Chicago" title="Chicago">Chicago</a>, <a href="/wiki/Illinois" title="Illinois">Illinois</a>.
</p>
