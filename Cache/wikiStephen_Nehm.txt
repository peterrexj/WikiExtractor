<p><b>Esțfān Nehmé</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">يوسف نعمة</span>; 8 March 1889 – 30 August 1938) - born <b>Yūsuf Nehmé</b> - was a <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_people" title="Lebanese people">Lebanese</a> <a href="/wiki/Maronite_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Maronite Catholic">Maronite</a> <a href="/wiki/Brother_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Brother (Christian)">professed religious</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Lebanese Maronite Order</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ZN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ZN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Nehmé worked to alleviate people's pain during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> through the distribution of food to those suffering from famine and was known for an intense devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Mother of God</a>. He worked in the fields of his convent and other monasteries and also worked in construction for a brief period - he became well known and other monasteries sought him to live with them for his spiritual insight and work ethic.<sup id="cite_ref-FG_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nehmé was beatified on 27 June 2010 in Lebanon.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_Nehm%C3%A9&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Yūsuf Nehmé was born on 8 March 1889 in <a href="/wiki/Lehfed" title="Lehfed">Lehfed</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a> as the last of seven children to Estephanos Bou Haykal <a href="/wiki/Nehme_family" class="mw-redirect" title="Nehme family">Nehmé</a> and Christina Badawi Hanna Khaled. He had three male siblings and two female siblings. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 15 March 1889 at the church of Our Lady in Lehfed; it was Father Gerges Fadel that baptized him.<sup id="cite_ref-ZN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ZN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father died in 1903.<sup id="cite_ref-FG_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nehmé studied under the <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_Maronite_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Lebanese Maronite Order">Lebanese Maronite Order</a> at the Our Lady of Grace school in Sakii Rishmaya. A tale from his childhood reports that he once observed a <a href="/wiki/Badger" title="Badger">badger</a> enter a cave; Nehmé dug at that spot and unearthed a <a href="/wiki/Spring_(hydrosphere)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spring (hydrosphere)">spring</a> which is known at present as the "Badger's Fountain". In 1905 he entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the Lebanese Maronite Order at the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of Saints Cyprian and Justina in Kfifan. Nehmé made his monastic vows on 23 August 1907 in the name of Esțfan. He made his solemn vows as a professed religious  on 13 April 1924. At various monasteries he did manual labor in the fields and gardens as well as working in construction and as a carpenter. Nehmé's contemporaries made special note of his constant repetition of the mantra: "God can see me".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> he distributed food to those suffering from famine.<sup id="cite_ref-AN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nehmé died of a severe fever that led to a <a href="/wiki/Apoplexy" title="Apoplexy">stroke</a> at 7:00&#160;p.m. on 30 August 1938 at the convent in Kfifan. Moments before his death Brother Charbel Nehmeh asked him if it would be fine to fetch water and the monk replied Nehmeh could do so if he wanted. The monk died mere moments after as his fellow friar searched for water. His remains reside at the convent of Saints Cyprian and Justina has been said to have been found <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>. Monks investigating the tomb on 10 March 1951 found that his remains had not decomposed. The remains were moved to a new tomb where visitors seek his intercession and ask for his healing.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_Nehm%C3%A9&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process started in a diocesan process that spanned for several weeks from 27 November 2001 until 17 December 2001 while the formal introduction came under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 16 January 2002 after the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> issued the official "<a href="/wiki/Nihil_obstat" title="Nihil obstat">nihil obstat</a>" to the cause and titled him as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The C.C.S. later validated the diocesan process in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 26 April 2002 and received the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> in 2005 for assessment.
</p><p>Theologians met and approved the cause on 2 March 2007 as did the C.C.S. members on 16 October 2007 which in turn allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> to name him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 17 December 2007 upon the confirmation of his <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. The process for a miracle took place where it originated in and later received C.C.S. validation on 14 February 2003 before receiving the approval of the medical board in Rome on 1 October 2009. Theologians later assented to this on 16 December 2009 as did the C.C.S. on 16 March 2010 before <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> approved the miracle and beatification on 27 March 2010.
</p><p>Nehmé's beatification took place in Lebanon on 27 June 2010 and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presided over the celebration on the pope's behalf. The miracle in question was the cure of Sister Marina Nehmeh from <a href="/wiki/Osteosarcoma" title="Osteosarcoma">osteosarcoma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FG_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FG-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The President and the Prime Minister of the nation were both in attendance as were 50 thousand other people.<sup id="cite_ref-AN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> assigned to this cause is the Rev. Boulos Azzi.
</p>
