<p><b>Severus of Barcelona</b> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Sant Sever</i>, <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Severo</i>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches.  His legend states that he was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Barcelona">bishop of Barcelona</a> and was martyred during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> by <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> in <a href="/wiki/Anno_Domini" title="Anno Domini">AD</a> 304. <sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
Details concerning his life and death are uncertain and of questionable historicity.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Severo_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Severo-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severus_of_Barcelona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The only documented Severus of Barcelona was known as Severus II of Barcelona and lived in the 7th century. He was represented by a priest at the Fourth Council of Toledo in 633, but died shortly thereafter.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This Severus is not known to have suffered a violent death.
</p><p>The 3rd-4th-century Severus is, in any case, a traditional or legendary figure in Catalonia and one of the "lesser patron saints" of the Cathedral of Barcelona.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> For centuries his saint day was a festivity in Catalonia.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severus_of_Barcelona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg/200px-Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg/300px-Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg/400px-Ermita_de_Sant_Medir_5.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1240" data-file-height="1654" /></a><figcaption>Severus and Emeterius (Medir).  The miracle of the fields.  Hermitage of Sant Medir, <a href="/wiki/Sant_Cugat_del_Vall%C3%A8s" title="Sant Cugat del Vallès">Sant Cugat</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>According to his legend, he was born in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> to a noble family and received a good education.  In a <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymn</a> associated with his <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">office</a>, it is stated explicitly that he was a citizen of Barcelona.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Severo_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Severo-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  A variant of the legend status that he was a humble weaver upon whose head a dove landed.  The people of the city saw this as a sign and elected him bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was chosen bishop of Barcelona around 290.  During the persecution of Diocletian, Severus fled to <i>Castrum Octavianum</i> (<a href="/wiki/Sant_Cugat_del_Vall%C3%A8s" title="Sant Cugat del Vallès">Sant Cugat del Vallès</a>).  As he crossed the <a href="/wiki/Serra_de_Collserola" title="Serra de Collserola">Collserola Mountains</a>, he encountered a fellow Christian, named <b>Emeterius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Sant Medir, Medí</i>, <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Medir, Medín</i>), sowing broad beans<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> in the field.  Severus instructed the man that if the soldiers sent to kill him asked the farmer where he had gone, to tell them that he had passed this way.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> made the beans he was cultivating sprout immediately after Severus left.  When the soldiers came across Emeterius and asked him if he had seen the bishop, Emeterius replied that he had and it was when he was sowing the fields.  Angry at this lie and believing that Emeterius was mocking them, the soldiers arrested the man<sup id="cite_ref-Whissel_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Whissel-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and took him to <i>Castrum Octavianum</i>.
</p><p>Severus meanwhile appeared to the soldiers, along with four other priests from Barcelona who had fled with him. Severus and the four priests were beheaded; Emeterius also suffered this fate.<sup id="cite_ref-Whissel_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Whissel-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Another tradition says that Severus was martyred by later Arian Visigoths who drove a nail into his head.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severus_of_Barcelona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg/200px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="249" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg/300px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg/400px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Chapel_of_Severus_of_Barcelona_by_Francesc_Santacruz.jpg 2x" data-file-width="7143" data-file-height="8879" /></a><figcaption><i>Altar of Saint Severus</i>.  <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Barcelona">Cathedral of Barcelona</a>.  <a href="/wiki/Francesc_Santacruz" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesc Santacruz">Francesc Santacruz</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Some sources state that Severus' <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were buried at Sant Cugat, where a church dedicated to him was built.  A <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, the one that stands there today, was built alongside the church.  When that church was destroyed, Severus’ relics were translated to the monastery.  In the fifteenth century, some of the relics were taken to Barcelona, to which were attributed various miracles, including curing <a href="/wiki/Martin_I_of_Aragon" class="mw-redirect" title="Martin I of Aragon">King Martin I</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gangrene" title="Gangrene">gangrene</a> in his leg.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Severo_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Severo-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Baroque" title="Baroque">Baroque</a> church of Sant Sever, near the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Barcelona">Cathedral of Barcelona</a>, was dedicated to him.
</p><p>The Church of Sant Pere Nolasc, in Barcelona was originally dedicated to Saints Severus and <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a> but renamed when the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary_of_Mercy" title="Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy">Mercedarians</a> took it over. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Saint_Medir">Saint Medir</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Severus_of_Barcelona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Saint Medir">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hermitage of Sant Medir, near Sant Cugat, is dedicated to Emeterius. It dates back to the tenth century. It is a small Romanesque church with a bell tower above the entrance. Every year on March 3  <i>la festa més dolça</i> —the sweetest festival- is celebrated in various neighborhoods of Barcelona in honor of Saint Medir. The tradition started in 1828 with a pilgrimage to l’ermita de Sant Medir. A statue of Sant Medir is brought from the church in Sant Cugat and paraded to the Barcelona neighborhood of Gràcia. As they return, the pilgrims toss candy in lieu of broad beans to the crowds that line the streets.<sup id="cite_ref-Whissel_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Whissel-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This is not the legionary <a href="/wiki/Emeterius_and_Celedonius" title="Emeterius and Celedonius">Saint Emeterius</a>, who was martyred with Celedonius in <a href="/wiki/Calahorra" title="Calahorra">Calahorra</a>. 
</p>
