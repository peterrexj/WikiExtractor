<p><b>Angelus of Jerusalem</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sant'Angelo</i>; 1185 – 5 May 1220) was a  <a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity" title="Conversion to Christianity">Catholic convert</a> from <a href="/wiki/Judaism" title="Judaism">Judaism</a> and a professed <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelites</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He and his twin brother were converted to the faith once their mother did so while both became <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> priests and Carmelite <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friars</a>. Unlike his brother, he retreated into the desert to a hermitage after his ordination, but he emerged once he was instructed to go to the Italian mainland to evangelize as well as to meet with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> to have him approve a new rule for the Carmelites.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was slain whilst preaching and was believed a saint after his death. The Carmelites venerated him as such until during his <a href="/wiki/Pontificate" title="Pontificate">pontificate</a> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_II" title="Pope Pius II">Pope Pius II</a> beatified the slain priest circa 1459.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angelus_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_%C3%81ngelo,_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_(Museo_del_Prado).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/San_%C3%81ngelo%2C_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_%28Museo_del_Prado%29.jpg/220px-San_%C3%81ngelo%2C_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_%28Museo_del_Prado%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="335" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/San_%C3%81ngelo%2C_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_%28Museo_del_Prado%29.jpg/330px-San_%C3%81ngelo%2C_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_%28Museo_del_Prado%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/San_%C3%81ngelo%2C_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_%28Museo_del_Prado%29.jpg/440px-San_%C3%81ngelo%2C_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_%28Museo_del_Prado%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1913" data-file-height="2913" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_%C3%81ngelo,_de_Antonio_de_Pereda_y_Salgado_(Museo_del_Prado).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1667 - <a href="/wiki/Antonio_de_Pereda" title="Antonio de Pereda">Antonio de Pereda</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> in 1185 to the Jewish parents.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His mother later converted to Christianity, and both he and his twin brother John were <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptised</a> into the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> when she converted. His parents died while Angelus and his brother were still children, and the Patriarch Nicodemus oversaw their education until the twins turned eighteen. At that age, he and his brother John entered the <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Order of the Brothers of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mount Carmel</a> at the Saint Anne <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> near the <a href="/wiki/Golden_Gate_(Jerusalem)" title="Golden Gate (Jerusalem)">Golden Gate</a> to commence their <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a>. The two could speak <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> as well as both <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hebrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Hebrew">Hebrew</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1210, Angelus was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> and he travelled in <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>. Miraculous cures were attributed to him around this time and his "acta" stated that he sought to avoid fame and withdrew to a hermitage in the desert when he was becoming popular for his miracles. He became a hermit on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Carmel" title="Mount Carmel">Mount Carmel</a> when he was instructed in 1218 to leave for the Italian peninsula in order to preach against the <a href="/wiki/Pataria" title="Pataria">patarini</a> as well as the <a href="/wiki/Albigensians" class="mw-redirect" title="Albigensians">Albigensians</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Bogomilism" title="Bogomilism">Bulgars</a>. He had likewise been instructed to go to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to obtain from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> confirmation of the new and definitive rule for the order (later granted in 1226).<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angelus was one of the first friars to come to Sicily from Mount Carmel.<sup id="cite_ref-British_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-British-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He set off on a Genoese ship on 1 April 1219 and stopped first in <a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a> before heading off to <a href="/wiki/Civitavecchia" title="Civitavecchia">Civitavecchia</a> before he ended up in Rome to meet with the pope. The friar preached in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Saint John Lateran">Basilica of Saint John Lateran</a> while in Rome where he met both <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dominic_of_Osma" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominic of Osma">Dominic of Osma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It is said that he foretold that Francis would receive the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a> while Francis foretold his premature death. From there, he was a guest of the <a href="/wiki/Basilian_monks" title="Basilian monks">Basilians</a> in <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> where he was for over a month before preaching in <a href="/wiki/Agrigento" title="Agrigento">Agrigento</a> for over a month before settling in <a href="/wiki/Licata" title="Licata">Licata</a>. He had healed seven <a href="/wiki/Lepers" class="mw-redirect" title="Lepers">lepers</a> and the ailing <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Palermo" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Palermo">Archbishop of Palermo</a> Bernardo de Castanea while in Palermo. He settled on the Sicilian island though his fame as a wonderworker caused crowds to flock to him. He also had success in converting some Jews though most Jews in Palermo came to despise him for this since he himself was once Jewish.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He wanted to convert a <a href="/wiki/Cathar" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathar">Cathar</a> <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a> named Berenger (known also in sources as Berengarius).<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Tradition states that Berenger was living in <a href="/wiki/Incest" title="Incest">incest</a> and that the friar convinced the knight's companion to leave Berenger. Berenger became enraged and had him attacked in front of the church of Santi Filippo e Giacomo in <a href="/wiki/Licata" title="Licata">Licata</a><sup id="cite_ref-British_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-British-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> on 1 May 1220. He died of his wounds within the week of 5 May and according to tradition asked for his assassin to be pardoned while urging the faithful not to avenge his death. He was buried at Santi Filippo e Giacomo.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angelus_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His sepulcher at Licata became a site of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. The Carmelites venerated him as a saint since at least 1456 and the cult received papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_II" title="Pope Pius II">Pope Pius II</a> at some stage during the latter's pontificate. In 1486, his remains were moved from a wooden casket to a silver urn before being moved to a more precious urn on 5 May 1623.  In 1632 his relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to the Carmelite Church,<sup id="cite_ref-British_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-British-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and are now housed at the <a href="/wiki/Santuario_della_Madonna_del_Carmine,_Catania" title="Santuario della Madonna del Carmine, Catania">Santuario della Madonna del Carmine</a> in Catania; the ending of a <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">plague</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a> in 1656, was attributed to his intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Since 4 May 1626, he has been known as the patron saint for <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Daniel_Papebroch" title="Daniel Papebroch">Daniel Papebroch</a> sets no great value on any of the three different acts or relations of his martyrdom, but gives long accounts of miracles attributed to his intercession since his death, and of the great veneration which is paid to him in Sicily, especially at Licata and Palermo.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As he and <a href="/wiki/Albert_of_Trapani" title="Albert of Trapani">Albert of Trapani</a> were the first two saints in the Order to have a cult, they are frequently found in medieval Carmelite iconography alongside the Virgin Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In the Carmelite church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Traspontina" title="Santa Maria in Traspontina">Santa Maria del Carmelo in Traspontina</a> in Rome, there is a chapel dedicated to St. Angelus with an altarpiece by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Ricci" title="Giovanni Battista Ricci">Giovanni Battista Ricci</a>. 
</p>
