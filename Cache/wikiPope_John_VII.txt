<p><b>Pope John VII</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ioannes VII</i>; c. 650 – 18 October 707) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> from 1 March 705 to his death. He was an ethnic Greek, one of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_popes" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine popes">Byzantine popes</a>, but had better relations with the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Lombards" title="Kingdom of the Lombards">Lombards</a>, who ruled much of <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, than with Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a>, who ruled the rest.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was an ethnic <a href="/wiki/Greeks_in_Italy" title="Greeks in Italy">Greek</a>, a native of <a href="/wiki/Rossano" title="Rossano">Rossano</a> in <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Murzaku,_Ines_Angeli_2009_47_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Murzaku,_Ines_Angeli_2009_47-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Hare,_Augustus_J._C._2007_344-345_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hare,_Augustus_J._C._2007_344-345-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father, Plato (c. 620 – 686), was the curator of the <a href="/wiki/Palatine_Hill" title="Palatine Hill">Palatine Hill</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a>. This makes John the first pope to be the son of a Byzantine official.<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His mother was called Blatta (c. 627 – 687).<sup id="cite_ref-Kelly_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kelly-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John VII was <a href="/wiki/Papal_selection_before_1059" title="Papal selection before 1059">selected</a> to succeed <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_VI" title="Pope John VI">John VI</a>, another Greek, on 1 March 705.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had good relations with the Lombards, who then ruled much of Italy. However, his relations with Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a> were far from smooth. Papal relations with Byzantium had soured over the <a href="/wiki/Quinisext_Council" title="Quinisext Council">Quinisext Council</a> of 692.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Scholarly debate contests John VII's stance on the canons.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He did not ratify them, and they were deeply unpopular in Italy. Nonetheless, he was criticized, most unusually, by the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_pontificalis" class="mw-redirect" title="Liber pontificalis">Liber pontificalis</a></i> for not signing them:
</p>
<blockquote><p>He [Emperor Justinian II] despatched two metropolitan bishops, also sending with them a mandate in which he requested and urged the pontiff [John VII] to gather a council of the apostolic church, and to confirm such of them as he approved, and quash and reject those which were adverse. But he, terrified in his human weakness, sent them back to the prince by the same metropolitans without any emendations at all.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p></blockquote>
<p>John VII died 18 October, 707 and was buried in the Chapel of the Blessed Virgin Mary which had been added on to St. Peter's.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sisinnius" title="Pope Sisinnius">Sisinnius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Several monuments in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> are connected with John. The most notable is the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Antiqua" title="Santa Maria Antiqua">Santa Maria Antiqua</a> church at the foot of the <a href="/wiki/Palatine_Hill" title="Palatine Hill">Palatine Hill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Traces of an episcopal palace <a href="/wiki/Episcopium" title="Episcopium">Episcopium</a> associated with John have been discovered upon the Palatine.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> John VII also constructed the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Oratory_of_John_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Oratory of John VII (page does not exist)">Oratory of John VII</a> in <a href="/wiki/Old_St_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Old St Peter&#39;s Basilica">Old St Peter's Basilica</a>, dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a>. Fragments of the mosaic decoration can be found in the Vatican grottoes. Furthermore, a sizeable icon, known as the <i><a href="/wiki/Madonna_della_Clemenza" title="Madonna della Clemenza">Madonna della Clemenza</a></i> and housed in <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Trastevere" title="Santa Maria in Trastevere">Santa Maria in Trastevere</a>, is believed to have been commissioned under the patronage of John.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He also restored the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Subiaco,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Subiaco, Italy">Subiaco</a>, destroyed by the Lombards in 601.
</p>
