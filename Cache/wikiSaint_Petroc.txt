<p><b>Saint Petroc</b> or <b>Petrock</b> (<a href="/wiki/Medieval_Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval Latin language">Medieval Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Petrocus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Pedrog</i>; French: <i lang="fr">Perreux</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;468</span>&#160;– c.<span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;564</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Britons_(historic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (historic)">British</a> prince and <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Christian</a> saint.
</p><p>Probably born in South <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, he primarily ministered to the <a href="/wiki/Brython" class="mw-redirect" title="Brython">Britons</a> of <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> (Dewnans) and <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a> (Kernow) then forming the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a> where he is associated with a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Padstow" title="Padstow">Padstow</a>, which is named after him (Pedroc-stowe, or 'Petrock's Place').<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Padstow appears to have been his earliest major cult centre, but <a href="/wiki/Bodmin" title="Bodmin">Bodmin</a> became the major centre for his veneration when his relics were moved to the monastery there in the later ninth century.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Bodmin monastery became one of the wealthiest Cornish foundations by the eleventh century.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> There is a second ancient dedication to him nearby at <a href="/wiki/Little_Petherick" title="Little Petherick">Little Petherick</a> or "Saint Petroc Minor".
</p><p>In Devon ancient dedications total a probable seventeen (plus <a href="/wiki/Timberscombe" title="Timberscombe">Timberscombe</a> just over the border in Somerset), mostly coastal and including one within the old Roman walls of <a href="/wiki/Exeter" title="Exeter">Exeter</a> as well as the villages of <a href="/wiki/Petrockstowe" title="Petrockstowe">Petrockstowe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Newton_St_Petroc" class="mw-redirect" title="Newton St Petroc">Newton St Petroc</a>. In Wales his name is commemorated at St Petrox near <a href="/wiki/Pembroke,_Pembrokeshire" title="Pembroke, Pembrokeshire">Pembroke</a>, Ferwig near <a href="/wiki/Cardigan,_Ceredigion" title="Cardigan, Ceredigion">Cardigan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Llanbedrog" title="Llanbedrog">Llanbedrog</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Ll%C5%B7n_Peninsula" title="Llŷn Peninsula">Llŷn Peninsula</a>. He also became a popular saint in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> by the end of the tenth century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petroc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:127px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Petroc-vit.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Petroc-vit.jpg/125px-Petroc-vit.jpg" decoding="async" width="125" height="240" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Petroc-vit.jpg/188px-Petroc-vit.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/Petroc-vit.jpg/250px-Petroc-vit.jpg 2x" data-file-width="390" data-file-height="749" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Petroc-vit.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Detail of stained glass window at Bodmin showing St Petroc</div></div></div>
<p>The earliest <i>Life</i> of Petroc states that he was a younger son of an unnamed Welsh chieftain:<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> the twelfth century version known as the <i>Gotha Life</i>, written at Bodmin, identifies that king as <a href="/wiki/Glywys" title="Glywys">Glywys</a> of <a href="/wiki/Glywysing" title="Glywysing">Glywysing</a> (Orme 2000, p.&#160;215) and Petroc as a brother of <a href="/wiki/Gwynllyw" title="Gwynllyw">Gwynllyw</a> and uncle of <a href="/wiki/Cadoc" title="Cadoc">Cadoc</a>. 
</p><p>He studied in Ireland,<sup id="cite_ref-padstow_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padstow-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> where later he is said to have been the teacher of <a href="/wiki/Kevin_of_Glendalough" title="Kevin of Glendalough">Kevin of Glendalough</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapa_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapa-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He made a pilgrimage to Rome, and returning to Cornwall, the wind and tide brought him to <a href="/wiki/Trebetherick" title="Trebetherick">Trebetherick</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapa_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapa-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He founded a monastery and school at Lanwethinoc (the church of Wethinoc, an earlier holy man), at the mouth of the river Camel on the North Cornish Coast. It came to be called Petrocs-Stow (Petroc's Place), now Padstow. 
</p><p>St Guron founded a hermitage at Bodmin, but left for <a href="/wiki/St_Goran" title="St Goran">the coast</a> upon the arrival of Petroc. St Guron's Well is located a at the western entrance to the churchyard of <a href="/wiki/St_Petroc%27s_Church,_Bodmin" title="St Petroc&#39;s Church, Bodmin">St Petroc's Church, Bodmin</a>. All accounts indicate that Petroc retired from Padstow to Bodmin.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Petroc ministered throughout <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a>, which included Kernow (Cornwall), and parts of Dewnans (Devon), Somerset and Dorset. He also served in Brittany.
</p><p>Petroc founded churches in Little Petherick and in many parts of Britain, Wales and Brittany. He is said to have converted <a href="/wiki/Constantine_of_Cornwall" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine of Cornwall">Constantine of Cornwall</a> to Christianity by saving a deer Constantine was hunting.<sup id="cite_ref-padstow_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padstow-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Constantine later established a hermitage at what is now called <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Bay" title="Constantine Bay">Constantine Bay</a>. After thirty years, legend says that Petroc went on the pilgrimage to Rome by way of <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>. The place of his death was reputedly at a house belonging to a family named Rovel, thought to be a farm now called Treravel near <a href="/wiki/Little_Petherick" title="Little Petherick">Little Petherick</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petroc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bodmin,_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_(1170)_(geograph_5100462).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Bodmin%2C_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_%281170%29_%28geograph_5100462%29.jpg/220px-Bodmin%2C_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_%281170%29_%28geograph_5100462%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="140" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Bodmin%2C_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_%281170%29_%28geograph_5100462%29.jpg/330px-Bodmin%2C_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_%281170%29_%28geograph_5100462%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Bodmin%2C_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_%281170%29_%28geograph_5100462%29.jpg/440px-Bodmin%2C_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_%281170%29_%28geograph_5100462%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3238" data-file-height="2059" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bodmin,_St._Petrocs_Church-_St._Petrocs_ivory_and_gold_casket_(1170)_(geograph_5100462).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Bodmin casket which once contained the saint's relics</div></div></div> 
<p>With <a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran" title="Saint Piran">Saint Piran</a> and <a href="/wiki/Michael_(archangel)" title="Michael (archangel)">Saint Michael</a>, he is one of the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saints</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was described by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Fuller" title="Thomas Fuller">Thomas Fuller</a> as "the captain of Cornish saints".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Humility" title="Humility">Humility</a> is a virtue strongly associated with Petroc.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> One of the pervasive stories about this describes his return from the pilgrimage to Rome and Jerusalem. As he set foot home in Britain under a heavy rain, he confidently told his companions that it would clear up by morning. Instead, the downpour lasted for many days and Petroc, ashamed of his presumptuousness, is said to have left on another pilgrimage, this one of <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Legendarily he travelled as far as <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> where he is said to have tamed a wolf.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a>, he is frequently depicted with this unlikely pet.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Also, like several other British saints, Petroc is often shown with a stag.
</p><p>In 936, King <a href="/wiki/Athelstan" class="mw-redirect" title="Athelstan">Athelstan</a> of England annexed Cornwall and granted privilege of sanctuary to Padstow, there only being two other churches in Cornwall with this privilege.<sup id="cite_ref-padstow_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padstow-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 4 June.<sup id="cite_ref-Magni_12-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Magni-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> was always at <a href="/wiki/Bodmin_Parish_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Bodmin Parish Church">St Petroc's Church</a>, Bodmin. In 1177, a <a href="/wiki/Breton_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton people">Breton</a> stole his relics from Bodmin and gave them to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St_Meen" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St Meen">Abbey of St Meen</a>. However, <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II</a> restored them and, though the relics were thrown out during the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>, their <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">ivory casket</a> is still on public display at St. Petroc's in Bodmin.<sup id="cite_ref-padstow_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-padstow-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His remains were reputed to have ended up in the bay of Hailemouth near Padstow.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> A list in the Leofric missal and another manuscript list in Exeter Cathedral record that the cathedral possessed relics of the saint. It is likely that they had been presented by King Athelstan.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Petroc is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on 4 June.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petroc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Flag_of_Devon.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Flag_of_Devon.svg/150px-Flag_of_Devon.svg.png" decoding="async" width="150" height="87" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Flag_of_Devon.svg/225px-Flag_of_Devon.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Flag_of_Devon.svg/300px-Flag_of_Devon.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="516" data-file-height="300" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Flag_of_Devon.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The flag of Devon is dedicated to Saint Petroc</div></div></div>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Perreux" title="Saint-Perreux">Saint-Perreux</a> in Brittany is named for him.</li>
<li>The <a href="/wiki/Flag_of_Devon" title="Flag of Devon">Flag of Devon</a> is dedicated to Saint Petroc. This flag, designed by Ryan Sealey, was the winner of a competition organised by the BBC in 2003, and represents the traditional county. It has been used by residents, and since October 2006 by Devon County Council.</li>
<li>St Petrock's (Exeter) Ltd is a charity working with people who are homeless and vulnerably housed in Devon. <sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The St. Petroc's Society is a charity working to address homelessness in Cornwall.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>St Petroc's <a href="/wiki/Western_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Orthodoxy">Orthodox</a> Monastery in <a href="/wiki/Tasmania" title="Tasmania">Tasmania</a>, which used the <a href="/wiki/Sarum_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Sarum Rite">Sarum Rite</a>, was named after him from 1992 until its closure in 2012.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The 2008 merger of <a href="/wiki/North_Devon_College" title="North Devon College">North</a> and <a href="/wiki/East_Devon_College" title="East Devon College">East</a> Devon Colleges led to them being re-branded under the name <a href="/wiki/Petroc_College" class="mw-redirect" title="Petroc College">Petroc</a> in 2009.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
