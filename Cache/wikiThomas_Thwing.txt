</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>Thomas Thwing</b> (1635–1680) was an English Roman Catholic priest and martyr, executed for his supposed part in the Barnbow Plot, an offshoot of the fabricated <a href="/wiki/Popish_Plot" title="Popish Plot">Popish Plot</a> invented by Titus Oates. His feast day is 23 October.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Thwing&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His father was George Thwing, Esq. of Kilton Castle, <a href="/wiki/Brotton" title="Brotton">Brotton</a>, and Heworth Hall. His mother was Anne, daughter of Sir John Gascoigne and his wife Anne Ingleby, and sister of <a href="/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Gascoigne,_2nd_Baronet" title="Sir Thomas Gascoigne, 2nd Baronet">Sir Thomas Gascoigne, 2nd Baronet</a>, of Barnbow Hall, <a href="/wiki/Barwick_in_Elmet" class="mw-redirect" title="Barwick in Elmet">Barwick in Elmet</a>. Both parents were <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire" title="Yorkshire">Yorkshire</a> recusants. The martyr <a href="/wiki/Edward_Thwing" title="Edward Thwing">Edward Thwing</a> was his great-uncle.
</p><p>Thomas was born at <a href="/wiki/Heworth,_York" title="Heworth, York">Heworth Hall, Heworth, York</a>, and educated at <a href="/wiki/College_of_St._Omer" class="mw-redirect" title="College of St. Omer">St Omer</a> and at the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> (<a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>), ordained a priest and sent to minister at the English Mission in 1665, which he did for roughly 14 years.<sup id="cite_ref-Challoner_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Challoner-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Until April 1668, he was chaplain at <a href="/wiki/Carlton_Towers" title="Carlton Towers">Carlton Hall</a>, the seat of his cousins, the Stapleton family. He opened a school at Quosque, the Stapletons' dower-house. He lived on Hepworth Lane, in <a href="/wiki/Carlton,_Selby" title="Carlton, Selby">Carlton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Selby" title="Selby">Selby</a>.
</p><p>In 1677 <a href="/wiki/Mary_Ward_(nun)" title="Mary Ward (nun)">Mary Ward</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Institute_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Institute of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Institute of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a> (I.B.V.M.) began its foundation at Heworth Manor (also called Heworth Hall), which had been purchased in 1678 by Thomas' maternal uncle, <a href="/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Gascoigne,_2nd_Baronet" title="Sir Thomas Gascoigne, 2nd Baronet">Sir Thomas Gascoigne</a>, from Sir George "for my niece Ellen," Thomas' sister, after which it was given to the order. Thwing's sisters, Anne, Catherine, Ellen (Helen) and cousin Jane, were instrumental to the founding of the order, Dolebank Convent and the <a href="/wiki/Bar_Convent" title="Bar Convent">Bar Convent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It was at the Manor House that Thwing would become chaplain and where he was arrested in early 1679.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id=".22Barnbow_plot.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Barnbow_plot&quot;">"Barnbow plot"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Thwing&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: &quot;Barnbow plot&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the time of the <a href="/wiki/Titus_Oates" title="Titus Oates">Titus Oates</a> scare, or "Popish Plot", two servants, Bolron and Mowbray, who had been discharged from Sir Thomas Gascoigne's service for dishonesty, sought vengeance and reward by revealing a supposed plot by Gascoigne and others to murder King <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_England" title="Charles II of England">Charles II</a>. At first, the informers made no mention of Thwing. Nevertheless, Gascoigne, his daughter Lady Tempest, Thwing, and others were arrested on the night of 7 July 1679, and removed to London for trial at Newgate.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gascoigne sensibly demanded to be tried by a Yorkshire jury, whom the judges admitted were better equipped to decide on the credibility of witnesses, most of whom they knew personally, than were the judges themselves. The trial was postponed to the summer <a href="/wiki/Assizes" title="Assizes">assizes</a>. Thwing was brought to the bar on 29 July, and Gascoigne's former servant, Robert Bolron, testified against him. All of the accused were acquitted except Thwing, who was brought back to York, where he was arraigned at York on 17 March 1680, along with, among others, a kinsman, Sir Miles Stapleton. The prosecution played upon a list of Catholics which had been found on the night of the arrest. In reality, they were not conspirators but supporters of the new <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> at Dolebank which Gascoigne's daughter Lady Tempest had recently founded. At her father's trial, the Court had heard much evidence about the convent, but the judges apparently did not regard her actions as treasonable, since at her own trial she was acquitted. Sir Miles Stapleton was also acquitted, as was another alleged conspirator, Mary Pressicks: the judges, showing far more impartiality than in earlier <a href="/wiki/Popish_Plot" title="Popish Plot">Popish Plot</a> trials, ruled that her statement that  "we shall never be at peace till we are all of the Roman Catholic faith" was not treasonable, but a simple expression of opinion.
</p><p>Despite the acquittal of Stapleton and Mrs. Pressicks, Thwing was promptly found guilty on the very same evidence upon which his relatives had been acquitted. Upon hearing the sentence, he humbly bowed his head, saying in Latin, "Innocens ego sum" (I am innocent).<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The King at first reprieved him, but owing to a remonstrance of the Commons the death warrant was issued on the day after the meeting of Parliament. Thwing was hung, drawn, and quartered at the Tyburn in York on October 23, 1680. His friends interred his quartered body.<sup id="cite_ref-Challoner_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Challoner-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to the antiquary <a href="/wiki/Francis_Drake_(antiquary)" title="Francis Drake (antiquary)">Francis Drake</a> (1736) he was buried in the churchyard of St Mary Castlegate, York (nearest to York Castle) in a coffin with this inscription on a brass plate: <i>R D Thomas Thweng of Heworth collegii Anglo-Duaceni sacerdos, post 15 annos in Anglicana missione transactos Eboraci condemnatus, martyrio affectus est Oct die 23 anno Dom. 1680.  Duobus falsis testibus ob crimen conspirationis tunc temporis catholicis maliciose (?) impositum</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/John_Philipps_Kenyon" title="John Philipps Kenyon">John Philipps Kenyon</a> observed that Thwing was executed for conspiracy, despite the logical difficulty of a conspiracy without any other conspirators.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Posthumous">Posthumous</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Thwing&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Posthumous">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Thwing was declared Venerable by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on 9 December 1886<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and beatified by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_XI" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius XI">Pius XI</a> on 15 December 1929 and henceforth known as the "Blessed Thomas Thwing".
</p>
