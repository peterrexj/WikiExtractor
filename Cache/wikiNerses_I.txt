<p><b>Nerses I the Great</b> (<a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Ներսէս Ա Մեծ</span>) was an <a href="/wiki/Catholicos_of_All_Armenians" title="Catholicos of All Armenians">Armenian Catholicos</a> (or <a href="/wiki/Patriarch" title="Patriarch">Patriarch</a>) who lived in the fourth century. He was the son of At'anagenes and the <a href="/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia" title="Arsacid dynasty of Armenia">Arsacid</a> princess Bambish, a sister of King <a href="/wiki/Tiran_of_Armenia" title="Tiran of Armenia">Tiran</a> and a daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Khosrov_III_the_Small" title="Khosrov III the Small">Khosrov III</a>. His paternal grandfather was <a href="/wiki/St._Husik_I" title="St. Husik I">St. Husik I</a> whose paternal grandfather was Saint <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator" title="Gregory the Illuminator">Gregory the Illuminator</a>. 
</p><p>Nerses spent his youth in <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Caesarea</a>, where he received a Hellenistic education and married a <a href="/wiki/Mamikonian" title="Mamikonian">Mamikonian</a> princess called Sandukht. Sandukht bore Nerses a son called <a href="/wiki/Isaac_of_Armenia" title="Isaac of Armenia">Sahak</a> (Isaac), who would later become Catholicos. After the death of his wife, he was appointed sword-bearer to Arsacid king <a href="/wiki/Arshak_II" title="Arshak II">Arshak II</a> (Arsaces II).  A few years later, having entered the ecclesiastical state, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Catholicos" title="Catholicos">Catholicos</a> in 353 and confirmed in the office in Caesarea in accordance with tradition. 
</p><p>His patriarchate marks a new era in Armenian history. Until that point, the Church had been more or less identified with the royal family and the nobles; Nerses brought it into closer connection with the people. At the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Ashtishat" title="Council of Ashtishat">Council of Ashtishat</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;356</span>) he promulgated numerous laws on marriage, fast days, and divine worship. Among other things, the council forbade people to marry their first cousin and forbade mutilation and other extreme actions in mourning. Nerses built schools, hospitals, leprosaria and poor houses and sent monks throughout the land to preach the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199788_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199788-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Nerses's relations with Arshak II, however, soon deteriorated. Some of the Catholicos's reforms drew upon him the king's displeasure. Nerses also clashed with Arshak over the latter's extermination of certain Armenian noble houses.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In approximately 358 (possibly earlier), Nerses was sent to Constantinople to escort Arshak's bride <a href="/wiki/Olympias_of_Armenia" title="Olympias of Armenia">Olympias</a> to Armenia. Arshak, like his father, pursued a pro-<a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> policy, which led to a falling out with Catholicos Nerses.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerian200518_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerian200518-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> According to the 5th-century historian <a href="/wiki/Faustus_of_Byzantium" title="Faustus of Byzantium">Faustus of Byzantium</a>, Nerses never again appeared at Arshak's court after the king ordered the murder of his own nephew, <a href="/wiki/Gnel_of_Armenia" title="Gnel of Armenia">Gnel</a>, in defiance of the Catholicos's exhortations.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Nerses was exiled for some nine years along with other anti-Arian bishops,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> supposedly to <a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a>. It was probably at some point during the latter part of Arshak's reign that Nerses went to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> to ensure the emperor's support of Armenia against the Persians. According to Faustus of Byzantium, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Valens" title="Valens">Valens</a> became outraged at Nerses condemning his following of the teachings of <a href="/wiki/Arius" title="Arius">Arius</a> and sent Nerses into exile.
</p><p>Upon the accession of pro-<a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Pap_of_Armenia" title="Pap of Armenia">Pap (Papas)</a> in 369/370, Nerses returned to his see. Nerses undertook the reconstruction of Armenian churches and monasteries that had been destroyed during the Persian occupation of Armenia and strove toward the elimination of Zoroastrian influence in the country.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The classical Armenian historians write that Papas proved a dissolute and unworthy ruler and Nerses forbade him entrance to the church. Other historians believe that Nerses tried to bring the young king under his control using his considerable influence and with the help of some Armenian princes, prompting Pap to dissolve the Patriarch's benevolent institutions and confiscate holdings belonging to the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to Faustus of Byzantium and <a href="/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi" title="Movses Khorenatsi">Moses of Choren</a>, Papas invited Nerses to his table under the pretence of seeking reconciliation and reportedly poisoned him in 373.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to another theory, Nerses died of an illness of the lungs that he had contracted early in his life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Pap appointed Nerses's successor without the approval of Caesarea, which refused to recognize the bishop's authority.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
