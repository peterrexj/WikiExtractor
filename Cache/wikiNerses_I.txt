<dl><dd><dl><dd><i>There was also a Caucasian Albanian <b><a href="/wiki/List_of_Caucasian_Albanian_Catholicoi" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Caucasian Albanian Catholicoi">Catholicos Nerses I</a></b>, who ruled in 689–706, and a <b><a href="/wiki/List_of_Armenian_Patriarchs_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Armenian Patriarchs of Constantinople">Patriarch Nerses I of Constantinople</a></b>, who ruled in 1704.</i></dd></dl></dd></dl>

<p><b>Nerses I the Great</b> (<a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Ներսէս Ա Մեծ</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Armenian" title="Romanization of Armenian">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Armenian-language romanization"><i lang="hy-Latn">Nersēs I Mets</i></span>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;373</span>), also known as <b>Nerses the Parthian</b> (<span title="Armenian-language text"><span lang="hy">Ներսէս Պարթև</span></span>, <span title="Armenian-language romanization"><i lang="hy-Latn">Nersēs Part'ev</i></span>), was an <a href="/wiki/Catholicos_of_All_Armenians" title="Catholicos of All Armenians">Armenian Catholicos</a> (or <a href="/wiki/Patriarch" title="Patriarch">Patriarch</a>) who lived in the fourth century. He was the son of At'anagines and the <a href="/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia" title="Arsacid dynasty of Armenia">Arsacid</a> princess Bambishn, a sister of King <a href="/wiki/Tiran_of_Armenia" title="Tiran of Armenia">Tiran</a> and a daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Khosrov_III_the_Small" title="Khosrov III the Small">Khosrov III</a>. His paternal grandfather was Catholicos <a href="/wiki/St._Husik_I" title="St. Husik I">Husik</a>, whose paternal grandfather was Saint <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator" title="Gregory the Illuminator">Gregory the Illuminator</a>, the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenian Church</a>. 
</p><p>Nerses spent his youth in <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Caesarea</a>, where he received a Hellenistic education and married a <a href="/wiki/Mamikonian" title="Mamikonian">Mamikonian</a> princess called Sandukht. Sandukht bore Nerses a son called <a href="/wiki/Isaac_of_Armenia" title="Isaac of Armenia">Sahak</a> (Isaac), who would later become Catholicos. After the death of his wife, he was appointed sword-bearer (<span title="Old Armenian-language romanization"><i lang="xcl-Latn">senekapet</i></span>) to Arsacid king <a href="/wiki/Arshak_II" title="Arshak II">Arshak II</a>. A few years later, having entered the ecclesiastical state, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Catholicos" title="Catholicos">Catholicos</a> probably in 353 and confirmed in the office in Caesarea in accordance with tradition. 
</p><p>His patriarchate marks a new era in Armenian history. Until that point, the Church had been more or less identified with the royal family and the nobles; Nerses brought it into closer connection with the people. At the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Ashtishat" title="Council of Ashtishat">Council of Ashtishat</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;356</span>) he promulgated numerous laws on marriage, fast days, and divine worship. Among other things, the council forbade people to marry their first cousin and forbade mutilation and other extreme actions in mourning. Nerses built schools, hospitals, leprosaria and poor houses and sent monks throughout the land to preach the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199788_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199788-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Nerses's relations with Arshak II, however, soon deteriorated. Some of the Catholicos's reforms drew upon him the king's displeasure. Nerses also clashed with Arshak over the latter's extermination of certain Armenian noble houses.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In approximately 358 (possibly earlier), Nerses was sent to Constantinople to escort Arshak's bride <a href="/wiki/Olympias_of_Armenia" title="Olympias of Armenia">Olympias</a> to Armenia. Arshak, like his father, pursued a pro-<a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> policy, which led to a falling out with Catholicos Nerses.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETerian200518_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETerian200518-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> According to the 5th-century historian <a href="/wiki/Faustus_of_Byzantium" title="Faustus of Byzantium">Faustus of Byzantium</a>, Nerses never again appeared at Arshak's court after the king ordered the murder of his own nephew, <a href="/wiki/Gnel_of_Armenia" title="Gnel of Armenia">Gnel</a>, in defiance of the Catholicos's exhortations.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Nerses was exiled for some nine years along with other anti-Arian bishops,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199789-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> supposedly to <a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a>. It was probably at some point during the latter part of Arshak's reign that Nerses went to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> to ensure the emperor's support of Armenia against the Persians. According to Faustus of Byzantium, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Valens" title="Valens">Valens</a> became outraged at Nerses condemning his following of the teachings of <a href="/wiki/Arius" title="Arius">Arius</a> and sent Nerses into exile.
</p><p>Upon the accession of pro-<a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Pap_of_Armenia" title="Pap of Armenia">Pap (Papas)</a> in 369/370, Nerses returned to his see. Nerses undertook the reconstruction of Armenian churches and monasteries that had been destroyed during the Persian occupation of Armenia and strove toward the elimination of Zoroastrian influence in the country.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The classical Armenian historians write that Papas proved a dissolute and unworthy ruler and Nerses forbade him entrance to the church. Other historians believe that Nerses tried to bring the young king under his control using his considerable influence and with the help of some Armenian princes, prompting Pap to dissolve the Patriarch's benevolent institutions and confiscate holdings belonging to the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to Faustus of Byzantium and <a href="/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi" title="Movses Khorenatsi">Movses Khorenatsi</a>, Papas invited Nerses to his table under the pretence of seeking reconciliation and reportedly poisoned him in 373.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> According to another theory, Nerses died of an illness of the lungs that he had contracted early in his life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarutʽyunyan1982-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Pap appointed Nerses's successor without the approval of Caesarea, which refused to recognize the bishop's authority.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGarsoïan199791-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Nerses.27s_vision"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Nerses's_vision">Nerses's vision</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nerses_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Nerses&#039;s vision">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In medieval Armenia, a legend about a prophetic vision supposedly seen by Nerses in his dying moments gained widespread popularity and underwent several transformations over the centuries. Nerses's legendary vision is not mentioned by the main classical sources on Nerses's life, <a href="/wiki/Faustus_of_Byzantium" title="Faustus of Byzantium">Faustus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ghazar_Parpetsi" title="Ghazar Parpetsi">Ghazar Parpetsi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi" title="Movses Khorenatsi">Movses Khorenatsi</a>, although Faustus and Parpetsi do write that Nerses's cursed the Arsacids, causing the fall of their kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHovhannisyan195717–18_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHovhannisyan195717–18-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The legend first appears in a 10th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> of Nerses attributed to Mesrop Yerets ("the Priest"), although the main version that has reached us was redacted sometime between 1099 and 1131, that is, soon after the first <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1099)" title="Siege of Jerusalem (1099)">Crusader conquest of Jerusalem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPogossian2014466–468_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPogossian2014466–468-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> According to this version of the legend, Nerses predicted the fall of the Arsacid and Gregorid houses, the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_conquest_of_Jerusalem" title="Sasanian conquest of Jerusalem">conquest of Jerusalem by the Persians</a>, the capture of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a>, and its recapture by the Byzantines; the Armenian princes will be subjugated by the Byzantines, but the latter will soon be defeated by the "nation of the archers" (later associated with the <a href="/wiki/Seljuk_Empire" title="Seljuk Empire">Seljuks</a>), leading to the total ruination of Armenia and its church; these calamities will be followed by the coming of the "Franks" or "Romans" (the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusaders</a>), who will defeat the infidels and subject them to Roman authority, leading to the creation of a heavenly kingdom on Earth where peace, prosperity and justice will reign until the coming of the <a href="/wiki/Antichrist" title="Antichrist">Antichrist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHovhannisyan195719_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHovhannisyan195719-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_arts">In the arts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nerses_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: In the arts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Nerses is a character in the tragedy <i>Nerses the Great, Patron of Armenia</i> written in 1857 by the Western Armenian playwright, actor &amp; editor, Sargis Vanadetsi, also known as Sargis Mirzayan.</li></ul>
