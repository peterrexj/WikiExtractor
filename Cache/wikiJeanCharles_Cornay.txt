<p><b>Jean-Charles Cornay</b>, (27 February 1809 – 20 September 1837) was a French missionary of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a>. He was executed in <a href="/wiki/Ha_Tay" class="mw-redirect" title="Ha Tay">Ha Tay</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tonkin" title="Tonkin">Tonkin</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Vietnam" title="Vietnam">Vietnam</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_p.295_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_p.295-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> during the persecutions of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Minh_M%E1%BA%A1ng" title="Minh Mạng">Minh Mạng</a>.
</p>

<p>A town in France still carries his name.
</p><p>Cornay was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 27 May 1900, and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 19 June 1988.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_p.295_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_p.295-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Charles_Cornay&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Loudun" title="Loudun">Loudun</a>, Vienne, France, the third child of Jean-Baptiste Cornay (born 1777) and Françoise Mayaud (1780-1857); he had one brother, Eugène (1817-1893), and three sisters: Élisabeth (1804-1871), Olympe (1806-1888), and Louise (1821-1890). Their parents brought them up as good Catholics. Jean-Charles was baptized on 3 March 1809 in the church of Saint-Pierre-du-Marché in Loudun. His godparents were Henri Mayaud and Thérèse Cornay, his aunt.
</p><p>Cornay studied first at the school Saint-Louis in Saumur, then in the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> in Montmorillon, after which he studied at the <a href="/wiki/Major_seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Major seminary">major seminary</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>. He was known as a normal student, humble and with a gentle disposition.
</p><p>At Poitiers, Cornay received the tonsure on 1 June 1828 and <a href="/wiki/Minor_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Minor Orders">Minor Orders</a> on 14 June 1829. He was installed as a sub-deacon on 6 June 1830 in the Cathedral of Sts. Peter and Paul of Poitiers.
</p><p>After he completed his theological studies, Corday left Poitiers to enter the seminary of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a> on 13 October 1830. His missionary vocation was met with reluctance and misunderstanding on the part of his parents. This his first struggle to be faithful to God's call put him in opposition to the wishes of his parents, while maintaining his filial love. To his mother he said: "Just let me go to Paris, I will have at least three years there with every opportunity to examine my vocation, and all the means of preparing for it if it is authentic". But his stay at the Foreign Missions Seminary in Paris was brief, due to periods of insecurity following the <a href="/wiki/July_Revolution" title="July Revolution">July Revolution</a>. The seminary was targeted; Cornay wrote in his diary: "Yesterday our seminary was invaded and seven or eight posters were put up, proclaiming ‘Death to the Jesuits of the Rue du Bac', and a dagger as signature".
</p><p>Corday was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a deacon in 1831 and left France to go to the <a href="/wiki/Sichuan" title="Sichuan">Sichuan</a> province of China. His departure was hurried because of the need to replace another missionary. His intended destination was <a href="/wiki/Szechwan" class="mw-redirect" title="Szechwan">Szechwan</a> in China, some 1,250 miles from the coast. After six months of travel he landed in <a href="/wiki/Macau" title="Macau">Macau</a>, in Tonkin, in the midst of the violent persecution of 1831. Two guides had been sent to accompany him up the Yang-Tse-Kiang to reach Sichuan, but they never arrived. Cornay was stuck in Tonkin.
</p><p>Cornay was secretly ordained to the priesthood three years later on 26 April 1834 by <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholicism)">Bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Joseph-Marie-P%C3%A9lagie_Havard" title="Joseph-Marie-Pélagie Havard">Joseph-Marie-Pélagie Havard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_bishop" title="Coadjutor bishop">Coadjutor</a> <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicar" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicar">Apostolic Vicar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Hanoi" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Hanoi">Western Tonkin</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> after traveling along the <a href="/wiki/Red_River_(Asia)" title="Red River (Asia)">Red River</a> disguised as a Chinese. With no hope of reaching China from Tonkin, he chose to stay there. In his grueling ministry he was always calm, even joyous. Even his deteriorating health did nothing to reduce his faith.
</p><p>Cornay was arrested in 1837, accused of being the leader of an evil sect and of fomenting a rebellion. He was subjected to the <a href="/wiki/Cangue" title="Cangue">cangue</a> (a wooden torture collar) and then the cage. His response to torture was to sing. "After fifty blows I was untied. Arriving at the prison, I sang the <i><a href="/wiki/Salve_Regina" title="Salve Regina">Salve Regina</a></i>, the hymn to the Virgin".
</p><p>On 20 September 1837, as decreed by the Emperor Minh Mang, he was dismembered and beheaded near the Son-Tay citadel, not far from Hanoi. His head was displayed for three days and then thrown into the river.
</p><p>In the midst of all kinds of difficulties and even when facing death, Cornay proclaimed his faith: "The Lord is faithful: he expects from us a total trust in his promises". He wrote to his parents: "When you receive this letter, my dear father, my dear mother, do not grieve for my death; by consenting to my departure, you have already made most of the sacrifice". His remains are in the church at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Chieu-Ung&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Chieu-Ung (page does not exist)">Chieu-Ung</a>.
</p><p>Cornay's example occasioned the vocation of <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9ophane_V%C3%A9nard" title="Théophane Vénard">Théophane Vénard</a> (1829-1861), who was among those canonized the same day as he.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cornay was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 19 June 1840 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a>. He was included in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Martyrology</a> on 2 July 1899 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> after he had been beatified by that pope on 27 May 1900. He was canonized by Pope John Paul II on 19 June 1988 as one of the 117 <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Vietnam" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrs of Vietnam">Martyrs of Vietnam</a>, whose <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 24 November.
</p>
