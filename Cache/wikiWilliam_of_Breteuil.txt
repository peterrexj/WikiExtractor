<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Not to be confused with his father <a href="/wiki/William_FitzOsbern" class="mw-redirect" title="William FitzOsbern">William FitzOsbern</a>, sometimes also known as 'William of Breteuil' or 'William de Breteuil'.</div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">St</a> <b>William of Breteuil</b> or <b>William de Breteuil</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Guillaume de Breteuil</i>; <abbr title="died">d</abbr>. 12 January 1103) was a <a href="/wiki/Norman_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman people">Norman</a> abbot and magnate who held extensive lands in central <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy" title="Duchy of Normandy">Normandy</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Seigneur" title="Seigneur">lord</a> of <a href="/wiki/Breteuil,_Eure" title="Breteuil, Eure">Breteuil</a> at the end of the reign of <a href="/wiki/King_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="King of England">King</a> <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of England">William I</a> and during the chaotic period afterwards when William's eldest son <a href="/wiki/Robert_Curthose" title="Robert Curthose">Robert Curthose</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Normandy" title="Duke of Normandy">duke of Normandy</a>, contested with his younger brother <a href="/wiki/William_Rufus" class="mw-redirect" title="William Rufus">William Rufus</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">England</a>. Upon the death of William Rufus while hunting in the <a href="/wiki/New_Forest" title="New Forest">New Forest</a>, Lord William attempted—but failed—to block <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Prince Henry</a> seizing the <a href="/wiki/Crown_Jewels_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Crown Jewels of England">crown jewels</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Winchester" title="Winchester">Winchester</a> <a href="/wiki/Treasury" title="Treasury">treasury</a> and declaring himself king in preference to the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">crusading</a> Robert. Lord William was later abducted and tortured by a French noble who wanted to marry his illegitimate daughter Isabel. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_Breteuil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William was the first-born son of <a href="/wiki/William_FitzOsbern,_1st_Earl_of_Hereford" title="William FitzOsbern, 1st Earl of Hereford">William FitzOsbern</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Companions_of_William_the_Conqueror" title="Companions of William the Conqueror">companion</a> of <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of England">Duke William II</a> of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy" title="Duchy of Normandy">Normandy</a> during his <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">conquest</a> of <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon England">England</a> in 1066. Following the conquest, the father became the 1st <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Hereford" title="Earl of Hereford">earl</a> of <a href="/wiki/Earldom_of_Hereford" class="mw-redirect" title="Earldom of Hereford">Hereford</a>. The son William served as <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> at <a href="/wiki/Breteuil,_Oise" title="Breteuil, Oise">Breteuil</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oise" title="Oise">Oise</a>, helping rebuild the monastery there after it had been nearly destroyed during the local struggles of the era<sup id="cite_ref-yerasaintbilly_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-yerasaintbilly-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> until his father's death in 1071, whereupon he inherited extensive estates in central Normandy including the <a href="/wiki/Lordship" title="Lordship">lordship</a> of <a href="/wiki/Breteuil,_Eure" title="Breteuil, Eure">Breteuil</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pacy-sur-Eure" title="Pacy-sur-Eure">Pacy</a> in <a href="/wiki/Eure" title="Eure">Eure</a>. 
</p><p>During the turbulent period following the death of the new English king in 1087, his sons <a href="/wiki/Robert_Curthose" title="Robert Curthose">Robert Curthose</a> and <a href="/wiki/William_Rufus" class="mw-redirect" title="William Rufus">William Rufus</a> contested control over Normandy and England and their <a href="/wiki/Vassal" title="Vassal">vassals</a> waged numerous local wars against one another. In the conflict between <a href="/wiki/William,_Count_of_Evreux" class="mw-redirect" title="William, Count of Evreux">William</a>, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Evreux" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Evreux">count</a> of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Evreux" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Evreux">Evreux</a>, and his half-brother <a href="/wiki/Raoul_II_of_Tosny" title="Raoul II of Tosny">Raoul II</a>, lord of <a href="/wiki/Conches-en-Ouche" title="Conches-en-Ouche">Conches</a>, over their wives' insults towards one another, William of Breteuil and <a href="/wiki/Richard_of_Montfort" class="mw-redirect" title="Richard of Montfort">Richard of Montfort</a> allied with Count William and joined his invasion of Raoul's lands in November 1091.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore201791_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore201791-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Lord William's capture in 1092 doomed Count William's cause.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarlow1983286_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarlow1983286-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Ransom" title="Ransom">ransom</a> amounted to 3000 <a href="/wiki/Livre_parisis" title="Livre parisis">livres</a> (about 300 kilograms or 660 pounds of <a href="/wiki/Fine_silver" class="mw-redirect" title="Fine silver">fine silver</a>) and the recognition of Raoul's son Roger as heir to both Lord William and Count William.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMoore201791_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoore201791-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Roger, however, died young and the issue became moot.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMason1979125_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMason1979125-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lord William was present at the hunt in the <a href="/wiki/New_Forest" title="New Forest">New Forest</a> (probably near <a href="/wiki/Brockenhurst" title="Brockenhurst">Brockenhurst</a>) where <a href="/wiki/William_Rufus" class="mw-redirect" title="William Rufus">William Rufus</a> was killed by an arrow through his lung. He attempted to defend the  <a href="/wiki/Crown_Jewels_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Crown Jewels of England">crown jewels</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Winchester" title="Winchester">Winchester</a> <a href="/wiki/Treasury" title="Treasury">treasury</a> against <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Prince Henry</a> in deference to the claims of his duke Robert but was forced to yield.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDoyle1864125_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDoyle1864125-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Henry, supported by other nobles against his "foreign" brother, was crowned king shortly afterwards at London.
</p><p>William of Breteuil was married to Adeline of <a href="/wiki/Montfort-l%27Amaury" title="Montfort-l&#39;Amaury">Montfort</a><sup id="cite_ref-air_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-air-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> but is only known to have had two illegitimate children. His son Eustace of Breteuil (<span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Eustace de Breteuil</i></span>) succeeded him as lord of Breteuil and married <a href="/wiki/Juliane_de_Fontevrault" title="Juliane de Fontevrault">Juliane de Fontevrault</a>, the illegitimate daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">King Henry I</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">England</a>. His daughter Isabel of Breteuil (<span title="French-language text"><i lang="fr">Isabel de Breteuil</i></span>) was sought by Ascelin Gouel, Lord of <a href="/wiki/Ivry-la-Bataille" title="Ivry-la-Bataille">Ivry</a>, who captured William and tortured him until he finally permitted Isabel's marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>William died at <a href="/wiki/Bec_Abbey" title="Bec Abbey">Bec Abbey</a> in Normandy on 12 January 1103.<sup id="cite_ref-air_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-air-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by his son Eustace in preference to his legal heirs William of Gael and Reginald of Grancey as his Norman subjects "chose to be ruled by a fellow countryman who was a bastard rather than by a legitimate <a href="/wiki/Bretons" title="Bretons">Breton</a> or <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy" title="Duchy of Burgundy">Burgundian</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-air_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-air-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> William of Breteuil was later <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, which <a href="/wiki/Veneration_of_the_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Veneration of the saints">venerates</a> him as a <a href="/wiki/Saint_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint (Catholicism)">saint</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 14 July.<sup id="cite_ref-yerasaintbilly_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-yerasaintbilly-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
