<p><b>John Almond</b> (alias <i>Lathom</i> or <i>Molyneux</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <i>c</i>. 1577 &#8211; 5 December 1612) was an English Catholic priest. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> in 1598 suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> in 1612. Canonised in 1970, John Almond is one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Almond_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Almond was born around 1577 in <a href="/wiki/Allerton,_Liverpool" title="Allerton, Liverpool">Allerton, Liverpool</a>, and spent his childhood there and at <a href="/wiki/Woolton" title="Woolton">Much Woolton, Lancashire</a> (now Woolton, Liverpool) until at the age of eight, he was taken to Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-acts_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-acts-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He went to the <a href="/wiki/English_College_in_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="English College in Rome">English College in Rome</a>, at the age of twenty, where he was ordained as a priest, returning to England as a missionary in 1602.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He concluded his term there by giving the 'Grand Act' -- a public defence of theses which cover the whole course of <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>—and was warmly congratulated by Cardinals <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Tarugi" title="Francesco Maria Tarugi">Francesco Maria Tarugi</a>, who presided.<sup id="cite_ref-ryan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ryan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_England_and_martyrdom">Return to England and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Almond_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Return to England and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He continued to minister to the faithful and labored in his mission for 10 years, during that time he would eagerly speak out against the anti-Catholic powers of the state.
</p><p>He returned to the dangers of <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> in 1602 as a secular <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and administered to the faithful there. He was arrested in 1608, and then again in 1612. In November 1612, seven priests escaped from prison. It was alleged that, whilst in prison, Almond had said that "whosoever had <a href="/wiki/Regicide" title="Regicide">killed a king</a>, and were afterward penitent ... and did confesse the same to a priest, might have absolution".<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before his death, he prayed, distributed alms to the poor, and gave his final oration, concluding with these words: "To use this life well is the pathway through death to everlasting life".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> During his final speech on the scaffold he condemned regicide, discussed the nature of grace and true repentance, and said that he conceded as much authority to James as to any Christian prince.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John Almond was <a href="/wiki/Hung,_drawn_and_quartered" class="mw-redirect" title="Hung, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a> on 5 December 1612 at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a>, <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Almond_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> canonized John Almond on 25 October 1970. A bone <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of Almond survives, which is in the Martyrs' crypt in Tyburn Convent, London.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He is the patron saint of Liverpool.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>A book entitled <i>Saint John Almond and the Society of His Time</i> was written by Kenneth Alfred Almond, and the book cover has a detail drawing of the said martyr. This broad historical study explores the key events of John Almond's life and looks at them in an historical context alongside key events of the 16th and early 17th centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Almond_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A Catholic comprehensive in <a href="/wiki/Garston,_Liverpool" title="Garston, Liverpool">Garston, Liverpool</a> took the name of the martyr, initially as Blessed John Almond School and then St John Almond Catholic High School.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The school is currently <a href="/wiki/The_Academy_of_St_Nicholas" title="The Academy of St Nicholas">The Academy of St Nicholas</a>. 
</p>
