<p><b>Peter of Sebaste</b> (ca. 340 – 391) was a bishop, taking his usual name from the city of his bishopric, <a href="/wiki/Sivas" title="Sivas">Sebaste</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Armenia" title="Lesser Armenia">Lesser Armenia</a>. He was the younger brother of <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa" title="Gregory of Nyssa">Gregory of Nyssa</a>, the famous Christian jurist <a href="/wiki/Naucratius" title="Naucratius">Naucratius</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Younger" title="Macrina the Younger">Macrina the Younger</a>. 
He is also known as Peter of Sebasteia.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Sebaste&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His parents were <a href="/wiki/Basil_the_Elder" title="Basil the Elder">Basil</a> and <a href="/wiki/Emmelia_of_Caesarea" title="Emmelia of Caesarea">Emmelia</a> of <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Mazaca" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea Mazaca">Caesarea-in-Cappadocia</a>, who were banished for their faith in the reign of the Emperor Galerius Maximian, and fled into the deserts of Pontus. His grandmother was <a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Elder" title="Macrina the Elder">Macrina the Elder</a>, who was instructed by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Thaumaturgus" title="Gregory Thaumaturgus">Gregory Thaumaturgus</a>. The youngest of ten, he was brother to St.&#160;<a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Younger" title="Macrina the Younger">Macrina the Younger</a> and the two Cappadocian doctors, St.&#160;Basil of Caesarea and St.&#160;Gregory of Nyssa.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Macrina, his eldest sister, exercised a great influence over his religious training, acting as his instructor and directing him toward the spiritual and ascetic life.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Renouncing the study of the profane sciences, he devoted himself to meditation on Holy Writ and the cultivation of the religious life. Shortly after his brother's elevation to the episcopal See of Caesarea in 370, Peter received from him priestly ordination. Basil appears to have employed his brother as his confidential agent in some matters.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Subsequently, he withdrew from active affairs, and resumed the life of a solitary <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a>. He assisted his sister and his mother in establishing their monastic community after his father's death. Peter became involved in the gradual transformation of his mother's household from a community of virgins to a cenobitic community of both women and men. He headed the male monastery while Macrina was responsible for the female community.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When the provinces of Pontus and Cappadocia were visited by a severe famine, he gave a remarkable proof of his charity, liberally disposing of all that belonged to his monastery, and whatever he could raise, to supply with necessaries the numerous crowds that daily resorted to him, in that time of distress.
</p><p>About 380 he was elevated to the See of Sebaste in Armenia and, without displaying any literary activity, took his stand beside his brothers Basil and Gregory in their fight against the Arian heresy. In his life and episcopal administration he displayed the same characteristics as Basil. Linked together in the closest manner with his brothers, he followed their writings with the greatest interest. At his advice Gregory of Nyssa wrote his great work, <i>Against Eunomius</i>, in defense of Basil's similarly named book answering the polemical work of Eunomius. It was also at his desire that Gregory wrote the <i>Treatise on the Work of the Six Days</i>, to defend Basil's similar treatise against false interpretations and to complete it. Another work of Gregory's, <i>On the Endowment of Man</i>, was also written at Peter's suggestion and sent to the latter with an appropriate preface as an Easter gift in 397. No detailed information is available concerning his activity as a bishop, except that he was present at the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Constantinople" title="First Council of Constantinople">Ecumenical Council of Constantinople</a> in 381.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After his death in 391 he was venerated as a saint. His feast is kept by Roman Catholics on 9 January.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
