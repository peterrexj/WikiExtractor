<p><b>Adelaide of Italy</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Adelheid</i>; 931 – 16 December 999 AD), also called <b>Adelaide of Burgundy</b>, was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empress" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Empress">Holy Roman Empress</a> by marriage to Emperor <a href="/wiki/Otto_the_Great" title="Otto the Great">Otto the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECampbell1907_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECampbell1907-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was crowned with him by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XII" title="Pope John XII">Pope John XII</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 2 February 962. She was the first empress designated <i>consors regni</i>, denoting a "co-bearer of royalty" who shared power with her husband. She was essential as a model for future consorts regarding both status and political influence.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilson2016PR17_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilson2016PR17-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was regent of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> as the guardian of her grandson in 991–995.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECampbell1907_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECampbell1907-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Adelaide was born in <a href="/wiki/Orbe_Castle" title="Orbe Castle">Orbe Castle</a>, <a href="/wiki/Orbe" title="Orbe">Orbe</a>, Kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Upper_Burgundy" title="Upper Burgundy">Upper Burgundy</a> (now in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>), to <a href="/wiki/Rudolf_II_of_Burgundy" class="mw-redirect" title="Rudolf II of Burgundy">Rudolf II of Burgundy</a>, a member of the <a href="/wiki/Elder_House_of_Welf" title="Elder House of Welf">Elder House of Welf</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bertha_of_Swabia" title="Bertha of Swabia">Bertha of Swabia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEReuterMcKitterick1999699_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEReuterMcKitterick1999699-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adelaide was involved from the beginning of the complicated fight to control not only Burgundy but also Lombardy. The battle between her father Rudolf II and <a href="/wiki/Berengar_I_of_Italy" title="Berengar I of Italy">Berengar I</a> to control northern Italy ended with Berengar's death, and Rudolf could claim the throne.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJestice201849-51_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJestice201849-51-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, the inhabitants of Lombardy weren't happy with this outcome and called for help of another ally, <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Provence" class="mw-redirect" title="Hugh of Provence">Hugh of Provence</a>, who had long considered Rudolf an enemy. Although Hugh challenged Rudolf for the Burgundian throne, he only succeeded when Adelaide's father died in 937.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJestice201849-51_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJestice201849-51-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In order to be able to control Upper Burgundy Hugh decided to marry his son <a href="/wiki/Lothair_II_of_Italy" title="Lothair II of Italy">Lothair II</a>, the nominal <a href="/wiki/King_of_Italy" title="King of Italy">King of Italy</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGallick2009_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGallick2009-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> to the 15-year-old Adelaide (in 947, before 27 June).
</p><p>The marriage produced a daughter, <a href="/wiki/Emma_of_Italy" title="Emma of Italy">Emma of Italy</a>, born about 948. Emma became Queen of West Francia by marrying King <a href="/wiki/Lothair_of_France" title="Lothair of France">Lothair of France</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_and_alliance_with_Otto_I">Marriage and alliance with Otto I</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Marriage and alliance with Otto I">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg/220px-Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="141" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg/330px-Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg/440px-Meissner-dom-stifter.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1340" data-file-height="856" /></a><figcaption>Adelaide and her second spouse Otto&#160;I; <a href="/wiki/Meissen_Cathedral" title="Meissen Cathedral">Meissen Cathedral</a>, Germany</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">calendar of saints</a> states that Lothair was poisoned on 22 November 950 in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> by the holder of real power, his successor, <a href="/wiki/Berengar_II_of_Italy" title="Berengar II of Italy">Berengar II of Italy</a>.
</p><p>There were some suspicions amongst the people of Lombardy that Adelaide wanted to rule the kingdom by herself.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJestice2018?_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJestice2018?-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Berengar attempted to thwart this and cement his political power by forcing her to marry his son <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Italy" title="Adalbert of Italy">Adalbert</a>. Adelaide refused and fled, taking refuge in the castle of <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a>. Nevertheless, she was quickly tracked down and was imprisoned for four months at <a href="/wiki/Garda,_Veneto" title="Garda, Veneto">Garda</a>.
</p><p>According to Adelaide's contemporary biographer, <a href="/wiki/Odilo_of_Cluny" title="Odilo of Cluny">Odilo of Cluny</a>, she managed to escape from captivity. After a time spent in the marshes nearby, she was rescued by a priest and taken to a "certain impregnable fortress," likely the fortified town of <a href="/wiki/Canossa_Castle" title="Canossa Castle">Canossa Castle</a> near <a href="/wiki/Reggio_Emilia" title="Reggio Emilia">Reggio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOdilo_of_Cluny2004131_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOdilo_of_Cluny2004131-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> She was able to send an emissary to the <a href="/wiki/East_Frankish" class="mw-redirect" title="East Frankish">East Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto I</a> asking for his protection. Adelaide met Otto at the old <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombard</a> capital of <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> and they married on 23 September 951.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBouchard1995342_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBouchard1995342-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Early in their marriage, Adelaide and Otto had two children, Henry and Bruno, both of whom died before reaching adulthood.
</p><p>A few years later, in 953, <a href="/wiki/Liudolf,_Duke_of_Swabia" title="Liudolf, Duke of Swabia">Liudolf, Duke of Swabia</a>, Otto's son by his first marriage, instigated a big revolt against his father that was quelled by the latter. On account of this episode, Otto decided to dispossess Liudolf of his ducal title. This decision favoured the position of Adelaide and her descendants at court. Adelaide also managed to retain her entire territorial dowry.
</p><p>After returning to Germany with his new wife, Otto cemented the existence of the Holy Roman Empire by defeating the Hungarian invaders at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Lechfeld" title="Battle of Lechfeld">Battle of Lechfeld</a> on 10 August 955. In addition, he extended the boundaries of <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Francia</a> beyond the <a href="/wiki/Elbe_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Elbe River">Elbe River</a>, defeating the <a href="/wiki/Obotrites" title="Obotrites">Obotrites</a> and other Slavs of the Elbe at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_on_the_Raxa" title="Battle on the Raxa">battle of Recknitz</a> on 16 October 955. That same year, Adelaide gave birth to <a href="/wiki/Otto_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto II, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto II</a>. In 955 or 956, she gave birth to <a href="/wiki/Matilda,_Abbess_of_Quedlinburg" title="Matilda, Abbess of Quedlinburg">Matilda, Abbess of Quedlinburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Holy_Roman_Empress">Holy Roman Empress</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Holy Roman Empress">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Adelaide accompanied her husband on his second expedition to Italy  to subdue the revolt of Berengar II and to protect <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XII" title="Pope John XII">Pope John XII</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, Otto the Great was crowned <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> on 2 February 962 by Pope John XII. Breaking tradition, Pope John XII also crowned Adelaide as <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empress" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Empress">Holy Roman Empress</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller-Mertens1995251_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller-Mertens1995251-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 960, a new <i>ordo</i> was created for her coronation and anointing, including prayers to biblical female figures, especially <a href="/wiki/Esther" title="Esther">Esther</a>. The <i>ordo</i> presents a theological and political concept that legitimizes the empress's status as a divinely ordained component of the earthly rule.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201612_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201612-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 966, Adelaide and the eleven-year-old Otto II, traveled again with Otto on his third expedition to Italy, where the Emperor restored the newly elected <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XIII" title="Pope John XIII">Pope John XIII</a> to his throne (and executed some of the Roman rioters who had deposed him). The support of Adelaide (the legitimate heir to the Italian throne, which according to late Carolingian traditions would also denote legitimate claim to the imperial throne) and her extensive network of relations were crucial in ensuring Otto's legitimacy in his conquest of Italy and in bringing the imperial crown to the couple.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilson2016PR17_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilson2016PR17-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuchinger20165–8_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchinger20165–8-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adelaide remained in Rome for six years while Otto ruled his kingdom from Italy. Otto II was crowned co-emperor in 967, then married the Byzantine princess <a href="/wiki/Theophanu" title="Theophanu">Theophanu</a> in April 972, resolving the conflict between the two empires in southern Italy and ensuring the imperial succession. Adelaide and her husband returned to Germany, where Otto died in May 973, at the same <a href="/wiki/Memleben" title="Memleben">Memleben</a> palace where his father had died 37 years earlier.
</p><p>After her coronation, which increased her power as she was now <i>consors regni</i> and able to receive people from the entire Empire, Adelaide's interventions in political decisions increased. According to Buchinger, "Between 962 and 972 Adelheid appears as intervenient in seventy-five charters. Additionally Adelheid and Otto I are named together in Papal bulls". She often protected the ecclesiastic institutions, seemingly to gain a sphere of influence separate from that of her husband. Between 991 and 993, the brothers of Feuchtwang wrote to her and requested to be "protected by the shadow of your rule from now on, we may be safe from the tumults of secular attacks". They promised they would pray for her so that her reign would be long and stable.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201610,_13_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201610,_13-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adelaide wielded a great amount of power during her husband's reign, as evidenced by the requests her made of her.  A letter, written in the 980s by her daughter Emma demanded that Adelaide intervene against Emma's enemies and mobilize forces in the Ottonian Empire. She also asked  that Adelaide capture <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Capet" title="Hugh Capet">Hugh Capet</a>, who was already elected king of <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West Frankia</a> in 987.
Another enemy of Emma's was <a href="/wiki/Charles,_Duke_of_Lower_Lorraine" title="Charles, Duke of Lower Lorraine">Charles</a>, the brother of Emma's deceased consort Lothar, who had accused his sister-in-law of adultery. Another pleader was <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Gerbert of Aurillac</a>, at that time archbishop of Reims (the later Pope Sylvester II), who wrote to Adelaide to ask for protection against his enemies. Buchinger remarks that, "These examples are remarkable, because they imply that Adelheid had the possibilities to help in both cases or at least Emma and Gerbert do believe that she could have intervened and succeeded. Both are themselves important political figures in their realm and still they rely on Adelheid. Adelheid’s power and importance must have been extremely stable and reliable to do as wished by the pleaders."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201614_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201614-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Otto_II.27s_era"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Otto_II's_era">Otto II's era</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Otto II&#039;s era">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the years following Otto's death, Adelaide exerted a powerful influence at court. However, Adelaide was in conflict with her daughter-in-law, the Byzantine princess <a href="/wiki/Theophanu" title="Theophanu">Theophanu</a>, as only one woman could be queen and hold the associated functions and powers at court. Adelaide was able to maintain the title <i>imperatrix augusta</i> even though Theophanu now also used it. Moreover, Theophanu opposed Adelaide in the use of her dowry lands, which Adelaide wanted to continue to use and donate to ecclesiastical institutions, ensuring her power base. Adelaide had the right to make transactions of her Italian lands as she pleased, but she needed the permission of the emperor to use her Ottonian lands.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Adelaide also sided with her extended kin against Otto II. Wilson compares this action with those of other royal women: "Royal women possessed agency and did not always do the bidding of male relatives. <a href="/wiki/Engelberga" title="Engelberga">Engelberge</a> greatly influenced her husband, <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Louis_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Louis II">Emperor Louis II</a>, in his attempts to extend imperial control to southern Italy in the 870s. Matilda’s favouritism for her younger son Heinrich caused Otto I considerable trouble, while Adelaide sided with her extended kin against her own son, Otto II, until he temporarily exiled her to Burgundy in 978. Agency was clearest during regencies, because these lacked formal rules, offering scope for forceful personalities to assert themselves."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilson2016PR18_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilson2016PR18-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> After being expelled from court by Otto II in 978, she divided her time between living in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> in the royal palace of <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a> with her brother <a href="/wiki/Conrad_I_of_Burgundy" title="Conrad I of Burgundy">Conrad I</a>, King of Burgundy, through whom she was finally reconciled with her son. In 983 (shortly before his death) Otto II appointed her his viceroy in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Kleinhenz2004_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kleinhenz2004-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Regency">Regency</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Regency">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 983, her son Otto II died and was succeeded by Adelaide's grandson <a href="/wiki/Otto_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto III</a> under the regency of his Theophanu while Adelaide remained in Italy. For some time, Adelaide and Theophanu were able to put aside their separate interests and work together to ensure Otto III's succession. This is seen through their joint appearance in the charters. According to the <i>Annales Quedlinburgenses</i>, after Otto II's death, Henry, duke of Bavaria kidnapped Otto III. The narrative claims that Adelaide returned from Lombardy to join with  Theophanu, Matilda, and other leaders of Europe and reclaim the child.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>When Theophanu died in 990, Adelaide assumed regency on behalf for Otto III until he reached legal majority four years later. 
Adelaide's role in establishing Otto's position can be seen in a letter Otto III wrote to his grandmother in 996: "According to your [Adelheid’s] wishes and desires, the divinity has conferred the rights of an empire on us [Otto III] with a happy outcome".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201611,_12_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201611,_12-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Troubles in the East continued under Adelaide, as <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" title="Boleslaus I, Duke of Bohemia">Boleslaus of Bohemia</a> wavered in his loyalty. In 992, there was war between Bohemia and Poland , and again like in Theophanu's time, the Ottonian regime sided with Poland. Jestice comments that, "Christianity was not re-established in the land of the Liutizi during their lifetimes. But there were territorial gains, and by 987 it was possible to begin rebuilding destroyed fortresses along the Elbe". A Saxon army, with Otto III's presence, took Brandenburg in 991. The <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hildesheim_annal&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hildesheim annal (page does not exist)">Hildesheim annal</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildesheimer_Annalen" class="extiw" title="de:Hildesheimer Annalen">de</a>&#93;</span> reports that there was another expedition in 992.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEJestice2018253_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJestice2018253-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thietmar of Merseburg reports that Otto III dismissed his grandmother after his mother's death, but Althoff doubts this story. Even after Otto attained majority, Adelaide often accompanied him in his travels and influenced him, along with other women.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201053–54_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201053–54-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Burgundy, Adelaide's homeland, the counts and castellans behaved increasingly independently from their king <a href="/wiki/Rudolph_III_of_Burgundy" title="Rudolph III of Burgundy">Rudolph III</a>. Just before her death in 999, she had to intervene in Burgundy to restore peace.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years">Later years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Later years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Adelaide resigned as regent when Otto III was declared to be of the legal majority in 995. From then on, she devoted herself exclusively to her works of charity, in particular to the foundation and restoration of religious houses, i.e. monasteries, churches and abbeys.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawler2018_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawler2018-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rose1857_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rose1857-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG/220px-Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG/330px-Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG/440px-Seltz_StEtienne_63.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3648" data-file-height="5472" /></a><figcaption>Chapel of St. Adelaide, Église Saint-Étienne de Seltz</figcaption></figure>
<p>Adelaide had long entertained close relations with <a href="/wiki/Cluny_Abbey" title="Cluny Abbey">Cluny</a>, then the center of the movement for ecclesiastical reform, and in particular with its abbots <a href="/wiki/Majolus" class="mw-redirect" title="Majolus">Majolus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Odilo_of_Cluny" title="Odilo of Cluny">Odilo</a>. She retired to a nunnery she had founded in c.&#160;991 at <a href="/wiki/Selz_Abbey" title="Selz Abbey">Selz</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On her way to <a href="/wiki/Burgundy" title="Burgundy">Burgundy</a> to support her nephew <a href="/wiki/Rudolph_III_of_Burgundy" title="Rudolph III of Burgundy">Rudolf III</a> against a rebellion, she died at Selz Abbey on 16 December 999, days short of the millennium she thought would bring the <a href="/wiki/Second_Coming_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Coming of Christ">Second Coming of Christ</a>. She was buried in the Abbey and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a> canonized her in 1097.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilson2016374_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilson2016374-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> After serious flooding, which almost completely destroyed it in 1307, Adelaide's relics were moved elsewhere. A goblet reputed to have belonged to Saint Adelaide has long been preserved in Seltz.; it was used to give potions to people with fever and the healings were said to have been numerous.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg/220px-Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="123" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg/330px-Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg/440px-Relic_of_St._Adelaide.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3400" data-file-height="1904" /></a><figcaption>Relic attributed to St. Adelaide.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Adelaide constantly devoted herself to the service of the church and peace, and to the empire as guardian of both; she also interested herself in the conversion of the <a href="/wiki/Early_Slavs" title="Early Slavs">Slavs</a>. She was thus a principal agent — almost an embodiment — of the work of the pre-<a href="/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism" title="East–West Schism">schism</a> Church at the end of the <a href="/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages" title="Early Middle Ages">Early Middle Ages</a> in the construction of the religious culture of Central Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
Some of her relics are preserved in a shrine in <a href="/wiki/Hanover" title="Hanover">Hanover</a>. Her feast day, 16 December, is still kept in many German dioceses.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler1956_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler1956-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Issue">Issue</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Issue">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 947, Adelaide was married to King <a href="/wiki/Lothair_II_of_Italy" title="Lothair II of Italy">Lothair II of Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002126_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002126-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> The union produced one child:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Emma_of_Italy" title="Emma of Italy">Emma of Italy</a> (948 – after 987), queen of France and wife of <a href="/wiki/Lothair_of_France" title="Lothair of France">Lothair of France</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002126_32-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002126-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>In 951, Adelaide was married to King <a href="/wiki/Otto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I">Otto I</a>, the future Holy Roman Emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> The union produced four children:
</p>
<ul><li>Henry (952 – 7 April 954)<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127_33-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Bruno (953 – 8 September 957)<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127_33-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Matilda,_Abbess_of_Quedlinburg" title="Matilda, Abbess of Quedlinburg">Matilda</a> (early 955 – † 6 February 999), the first <a href="/wiki/Princess-Abbess_of_Quedlinburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Princess-Abbess of Quedlinburg">Princess-Abbess of Quedlinburg</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127_33-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Otto_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto II">Otto II</a> (end 955 – 7 December 983), later <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127_33-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHolböck2002127-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historiography_and_cultural_depictions">Historiography and cultural depictions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Historiography and cultural depictions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_Adelaide_of_Italy" title="Cultural depictions of Adelaide of Italy">Cultural depictions of Adelaide of Italy</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Historiography">Historiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Historiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_Adelaide_of_Italy#Historiography" title="Cultural depictions of Adelaide of Italy">Historiography of Adelaide of Italy</a></div>
<p>Adelaide was one of the most important and powerful medieval female rulers.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Historically, as empress and saint, she has been described as powerful, with both male attributes (like strength, justness and prudence) and female attributes (piety, self denying).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201638_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuchinger201638-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Modern German historiography tends to focus on her contributions to the Ottonian dynasty and the development of the Holy Roman Empire.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Depictions_in_art">Depictions in art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Depictions in art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Adelaide is usually represented in the garb of an empress, with sceptre and crown. Since the 14th century, she is also given as an attribute a model church or a ship (by which she is said to have escaped from captivity).
</p><p>The most famous representation of Adelaide in German art belongs to a group of sandstone figures in the choir of <a href="/wiki/Meissen_Cathedral" title="Meissen Cathedral">Meissen Cathedral</a>, which was created around 1260. She is shown here with her husband, who was not canonized, since he founded the diocese of Meissen with her.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Operas">Operas</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adelaide_of_Italy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Operas">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<ul><li>Adelaide of Burgundy is the main character of the opera <a href="/wiki/Adelaide_(Sartorio)" title="Adelaide (Sartorio)"><i>l'Adelaide</i></a> (1672) by <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Sartorio" title="Antonio Sartorio">Antonio Sartorio</a>.</li>
<li>Adelaide is the subject of a 1723 opera by <a href="/wiki/Nicola_Porpora" title="Nicola Porpora">Nicola Porpora</a>, where she was played by the great castrato <a href="/wiki/Farinelli" title="Farinelli">Farinelli</a> en travesti.</li>
<li><i><a href="/wiki/Lotario" title="Lotario">Lotario</a></i> is a 1729 opera seria in three acts by <a href="/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel" title="George Frideric Handel">George Frideric Handel</a>. It is a fictionalisation of some events in the life of Adeläide.</li>
<li>Adelaide is the heroine of <i><a href="/wiki/Adelaide_di_Borgogna" title="Adelaide di Borgogna">Adelaide di Borgogna</a></i>, an opera with two acts (1817) by <a href="/wiki/Gioachino_Rossini" title="Gioachino Rossini">Gioachino Rossini</a> (music) and <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Schmidt" title="Giovanni Schmidt">Giovanni Schmidt</a> (libretto).</li>
<li>Adelaide is the heroine of <a href="/wiki/William_Bernard_McCabe" title="William Bernard McCabe">William Bernard McCabe</a>'s 1856 novel <i>Adelaide, Queen of Italy, or The Iron Crown</i>.</li></ul>
