<p>Saint <b>Adalbert of Egmond</b> (also called <b>Æthelberht of Egmond</b>) (died c. 710 in <a href="/wiki/Egmond_(municipality)" title="Egmond (municipality)">Egmond</a>) was a <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> missionary. He was one of Saint <a href="/wiki/Willibrord" title="Willibrord">Willibrord</a>'s companions in preaching the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">gospel</a> in <a href="/wiki/Holland" title="Holland">Holland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Egmond&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adalbert (or Æthelberht) is said to have been born in <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>, a member of the Northumbrian royal family.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to some sources, Adalbert studied at the <a href="/wiki/Rath_Melsigi" title="Rath Melsigi">Rath Melsigi</a>) in Ireland with <a href="/wiki/Ecgberht_of_Ripon" title="Ecgberht of Ripon">Egbert</a>. Around 690 he went to assist <a href="/wiki/Willibrord" title="Willibrord">Willibrord</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (who had also been at Rath Melsigi) in the mission field of <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a>. According to <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a>'s late eighth-century <i>Vita Willibrordi archiepiscopi Traiectensis</i>, they landed near <a href="/wiki/Domburg" title="Domburg">Domburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Faber_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Faber-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adelbert became associated particularly with Egmond. He was made Archdeacon of the recently founded See of Utrecht and died at Egmund about A.D. 740.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  He was buried there, and miracles were reported at his tomb, over which a church was built. Adalbert is said to have shielded the settlement of Egmond from pirates by causing a fog to arise along the coast.<sup id="cite_ref-Faber_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Faber-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalbert_of_Egmond&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His continued remembrance rests largely on the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> monastery, <a href="/wiki/Egmond_Abbey" title="Egmond Abbey">Egmond Abbey</a>, the first in the country, some two hundred years later by <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Holland" title="Count of Holland">Count Dirk I of West Frisia</a> (or Holland), of which Adalbert was made the patron. The <i>Vita</i> was not commissioned until the 990s, which presumably accounts for its lack of facts. Adalbert's relics were translated to the newly built abbey (initially a nunnery) for veneration. After the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a> and the destruction of the abbey, they were preserved in <a href="/wiki/Haarlem" title="Haarlem">Haarlem</a>. The cult was reinstated when the abbey was re-founded in 1935, and the relics were returned there in 1984. The saint's skull, painstakingly restored, is also preserved beneath the high altar. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 25 June.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
