<p><b>Anna Maria Rubatto</b> (14 February 1844 – 6 August 1904) was an Italian Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> who assumed the name of <b>Maria Francesca</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Sisters_of_Mother_Rubatto" title="Capuchin Sisters of Mother Rubatto">Capuchin Sisters of Mother Rubatto</a>. Most of her work was done in <a href="/wiki/Uruguay" title="Uruguay">Uruguay</a> where she died at the age of 59 in 1904.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified her on 10 October 1993 and she is considered the first Uruguayan person to be beatified.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> confirmed her canonization after approving a miracle attributed to her in early 2020; her canonization was celebrated on 15 May 2022.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anna_Maria_Rubatto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Anna Maria Rubatto was born in 1844 to Giovanni Rubatto and Catarina Pavesio as one of eight children; she lost her father at the age of four.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She received a marriage offer while a teenager but turned the offer down in favor of her religious vocation. Her mother died when she was nineteen and she moved to <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> where she became a friend of noblewoman Marianna Scoffone.
</p><p>Rubatto helped Scoffone with teaching <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to children and visiting the sick and poor. Scoffone died in 1882. One morning after mass in <a href="/wiki/Loano" title="Loano">Loano</a> a stone fell from a construction site and struck a worker. Rubatto helped the worker and the sisters of the convent close to the incident noticed what had happened. The sisters took it as a sign that Rubatto was the person the sisters needed. She took the name of "Maria Francesca of Jesus" when she became a nun in 1885. On the orders of Bishop Filippo Allegro she became the superior of the group. This became the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Sisters_of_Mother_Rubatto" title="Capuchin Sisters of Mother Rubatto">Capuchin Sisters of Mother Rubatto</a>.
</p><p>In 1892 she travelled to <a href="/wiki/Montevideo" title="Montevideo">Montevideo</a> and spread their apostolate there and in <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a>.
</p><p>She contracted <a href="/wiki/Cancer" title="Cancer">cancer</a> while in Montevideo and she died in 1904 at the age of 60. She is buried in Montevideo as per her requests.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anna_Maria_Rubatto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cause for beatification commenced under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> on 13 April 1965 which conferred upon her the title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. This introduction took place despite the fact that the cause had opened processes in <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a> and Montevideo that spanned from 1941 to 1970. The processes were ratified on 12 December 1975. The <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> - documentation on her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> - was submitted to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in 1984.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> recognized her heroic virtue and proclaimed her to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 1 September 1988.
</p><p>A miracle attributed to her intercession was investigated in Genoa in 1951 and was ratified on 12 November 1991. John Paul II approved it on 2 April 1993 and beatified her on 10 October 1993.
</p><p>A second miracle needed for her canonization was discovered and an investigation was held. That process was ratified in 2013. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> approved this miracle on 21 February 2020 and Rubatto was canonized on 15 May 2022.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
