<p><b>Colette of Corbie</b>, <a href="/wiki/Colettine_Poor_Clares" title="Colettine Poor Clares">PCC</a> (13 January 1381 – 6 March 1447) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> and the foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Colettine_Poor_Clares" title="Colettine Poor Clares">Colettine Poor Clares</a>, a reform branch of the Order of Saint Clare, better known as the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a>. She is honored as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. Due to a number of miraculous events claimed during her life, she is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of women seeking to <a href="/wiki/Human_fertilization" title="Human fertilization">conceive</a>, expectant mothers, and sick children.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>She was born <b>Nicole Boellet</b> (or <b>Boylet</b>) in the village of <a href="/wiki/Corbie" title="Corbie">Corbie</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a> region of France, on 13 January 1381<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_Dictionary_of_Saints_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford_Dictionary_of_Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to Robert Boellet, a poor carpenter at the noted Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Corbie_Abbey" title="Corbie Abbey">Abbey of Corbie</a>, and to his wife, Marguerite Moyon.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERoest2013169_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERoest2013169-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her contemporary biographers say that her parents had grown old without having children, before praying to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Nicholas" title="Saint Nicholas">Saint Nicholas</a> for help in having a child. Their prayers were answered when, at the age of 60, Marguerite gave birth to a daughter. Out of gratitude, they named the baby after the saint to whom they credited the miracle of her birth.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was affectionately called Nicolette by her parents, which soon came to be shorted to Colette, by which name she is known.
</p><p>After her parents died in 1399, Colette joined the <a href="/wiki/Beguines" class="mw-redirect" title="Beguines">Beguines</a> but found their manner of life unchallenging.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERoest2013169_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERoest2013169-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Joining a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> order as a lay sister, most likely to avoid an arranged marriage,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERoest2013169_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERoest2013169-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> she again became dissatisfied. In September 1402, Colette received the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFarmer199796_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarmer199796-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> under the direction of the Abbot of Corbie, living near the abbey church.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERoest2013170_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERoest2013170-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After four years of following this <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> way of life (1402–1406), through several dreams and visions, she came to believe that she was being called to reform the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a> and to return it to its original Franciscan ideals of absolute poverty and austerity.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundress">Foundress</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundress">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In October 1406, she turned to the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Benedict_XIII" title="Antipope Benedict XIII">Antipope Benedict XIII</a> of Avignon who was recognized in France as the rightful pope. Benedict received her in <a href="/wiki/Nice" title="Nice">Nice</a>, in southern France, and allowed her to transfer to the Order of Poor Clares. Additionally, he empowered her through several <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bulls</a>, issued between 1406 and 1412, to found new <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a> and to complete the reform of the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFarmer199796_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarmer199796-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With the approval of the <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Geneva" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Geneva">Countess of Geneva</a> and the aid of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> itinerant preacher, <a href="/wiki/Henry_de_Beaume" title="Henry de Beaume">Henry de Beaume</a> (her <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a>), Colette began her work at <a href="/wiki/Beaune" title="Beaune">Beaune</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Lausanne,_Geneva_and_Fribourg#Geneva" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Lausanne, Geneva and Fribourg">Diocese of Geneva</a>. She remained there only a short time. In 1410, she opened her first monastery at <a href="/wiki/Besan%C3%A7on" title="Besançon">Besançon</a>, in an almost-abandoned house of Urbanist Poor Clares. From there, her reform spread to <a href="/wiki/Auxonne" title="Auxonne">Auxonne</a> (1412), to <a href="/wiki/Poligny,_Jura" title="Poligny, Jura">Poligny</a> (1415), to <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> (1412), to <a href="/wiki/Heidelberg" title="Heidelberg">Heidelberg</a> (1444), to <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Pont-%C3%A0-Mousson" title="Pont-à-Mousson">Pont-à-Mousson</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lorraine_(duchy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorraine (duchy)">Lorraine</a>, and to other communities of Poor Clares. During her lifetime 18 monasteries of her reform were founded. For the monasteries which followed her reform, she prescribed extreme poverty, going barefoot, and the observance of perpetual fasting and <a href="/wiki/Abstinence" title="Abstinence">abstinence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bihl_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bihl-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In addition to the strict rules of the Poor Clares, the Colettines follow their special <a href="/wiki/Constitutions" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitutions">Constitutions</a>, approved in 1434 by the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of the friars, William of Casale, and approved in 1448 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V" title="Pope Nicholas V">Pope Nicholas V</a>, again in 1458 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_II" title="Pope Pius II">Pope Pius II</a>, and in 1482 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a>.
</p><p>Colette died at <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a> in March 1447.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_Dictionary_of_Saints_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford_Dictionary_of_Saints-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Colette was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> 23 January 1740 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 24 May 1807 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> She is invoked by childless couples desiring to become parents and is also the patroness of expectant mothers and sick infants.<sup id="cite_ref-pcb_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pcb-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Colettine_nuns">Colettine nuns</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Colettine nuns">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Colettine <a href="/wiki/Nuns" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuns">nuns</a> are found in France, Belgium, Germany, Ireland, Japan, Norway, the Philippines, Spain, the United Kingdom, and the United States.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Colettine_friars">Colettine friars</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Colettine friars">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Together with friar Henry of Beaume, Colette also inaugurated a reform among the Franciscan friars (who were known as the <i>Coletans</i>), not to be confounded with the Observants. These friars formed a unique branch of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> under Henry's authority but remained obedient to the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Provincial_Superior" class="mw-redirect" title="Provincial Superior">Minister Provincial</a> of the Observant Franciscan friars in France and never attained much importance, even there. In 1448, they had only thirteen friaries, all attached to monasteries of the Colettine nuns. Together with other small branches of the Friars Minor, they were merged into the wider Observant branch in 1517 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_X" title="Pope Leo X">Pope Leo X</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to biographers, Colette performed numerous miracles, including multiplication of food or wine and effecting cures, partly after her death.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Helping_a_mother_in_childbirth">Helping a mother in childbirth</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Helping a mother in childbirth">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>While traveling to Nice to meet Pope Benedict, Colette stayed at the home of a friend. His wife was in labor at that time with their third child, and was having major difficulties in the childbirth, leaving her in danger of death. Colette immediately went to the local church to pray for her.
</p><p>The mother gave birth successfully and survived the ordeal. She credited Colette's prayers for this. The child born, a girl named Petronilla, later entered a monastery founded by Colette. She would become Colette's secretary and biographer.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Saving_a_sick_child">Saving a sick child</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Colette_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Saving a sick child">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the pope had authorized Colette to establish a regimen of strict poverty in the Poor Clare monasteries of France, she started with that of Besançon. The local populace was suspicious of her reform, with its total reliance on them for the sustenance of the monastery. One incident helped turn this around.
</p><p>According to some sources<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>, a local peasant woman gave birth to a <a href="/wiki/Stillbirth" title="Stillbirth">stillborn</a> child. In desperation, out of fear for the child's soul, the father took the baby to the local parish priest for <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>. Seeing that the child was already dead, the priest refused to baptize the body. When the man became insistent, out of frustration, the priest told him to go to the nuns, which he did immediately. When he arrived at the monastery, Mother Colette was made aware of his situation by the <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">portress</a>. Her response was to take off the veil given to her by the Pope, when he gave her the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> of the Second Order, and told the portress to have the father wrap the child's body in it and for him to return to the priest. By the time he arrived at the parish church with his small bundle, the child was conscious and crying. The priest immediately baptized the baby.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
