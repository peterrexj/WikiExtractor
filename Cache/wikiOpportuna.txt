<p><b>Opportuna of Montreuil</b> (died 770) was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>. A <i>Vita et miracula Sanctae Opportunae</i> was written within a century of her death (<i>ca</i> 885-88) by <a href="/wiki/Adalhelm_of_S%C3%A9ez" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalhelm of Séez">Adalhelm</a> (later rendered Adelin), <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_S%C3%A9ez" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Séez">bishop of Séez</a>, who believed he owed his life and his see to Opportuna.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Opportuna_of_Montreuil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When she was still young, Opportuna became a Benedictine nun at the convent called the Monasteriolum<sup class="reference" id="ref_Anone"><a href="#endnote_Anone">[a]</a></sup> near <a href="/w/index.php?title=Almen%C3%A8ches&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Almenèches (page does not exist)">Almenèches</a>, where her cousin <a href="/w/index.php?title=Lantildis&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Lantildis (page does not exist)">Lantildis</a> was abbess.  Opportuna took the veil from her brother, Chrodegang  (later rendered Godegrand), the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_S%C3%A9ez" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Séez">Séez</a>.<sup class="reference" id="ref_Bnone"><a href="#endnote_Bnone">[b]</a></sup> Although Montreuil was only three miles away from Séez, Chrodegang was murdered on the way to visit his sister at the abbey.<sup class="reference" id="ref_Cnone"><a href="#endnote_Cnone">[c]</a></sup> Later Opportuna succeeded her cousin as abbess. In this position, she was conventionally described as "a true mother to all her nuns",<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-patrick_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> correcting their faults, significantly enough to record, with words, not blows.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some sources say that Opportuna died<sup class="reference" id="ref_Enone"><a href="#endnote_Enone">[e]</a></sup> from a brief illness which was compounded by <a href="/wiki/Grief" title="Grief">grief</a> from the death of her brother, as Chrodegang had died on 3 September 769. His murder had been planned by <a href="/wiki/Chrodobert" title="Chrodobert">Chrodobert</a>, a powerful relation to whom he had entrusted the administration of his <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> during his seven years' absence at Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Though she foresaw her brother's death in a prophetic vision, Opportuna was powerless to intervene;<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> afterwards she buried Chrodegang in her own convent. Whatever the cause, Opportuna died on 22 April 770.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lives_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Opportuna_of_Montreuil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Like all Carolingian saints, Opportuna's sanctity was not expressed in charismatic actions during her lifetime: she effected no miracles during her lifetime. Though the cult of the saintly dead and <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> flourished, "the Carolingian era forms an interlude in the history of sainthood, for no charismatic ascetics, healers, prophets or visionaries made their mark on a church whose bishops were implacably hostile to any such forms of expression," Julia Smith has observed, in analysing Carolingian attitudes towards appropriate representations of female sainthood through the lens of the <i> Vita et miracula Sanctae Opportunae</i>.
</p><p>The account of miracles worked at the site of Opportuna's tomb reminded readers and hearers that the abbess remained present in her former precincts, extending her protection to her flock forward in time. Julia Smith has detected that the comparatively small <i>corpus</i> of hagiographies of female saints<sup class="reference" id="ref_Dnone"><a href="#endnote_Dnone">[d]</a></sup> are restricted in the area in which they were produced, north of the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a> and east of the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a> for the greatest part.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Opportuna's <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> records that once a <a href="/wiki/Peasant" title="Peasant">peasant</a> stole a <a href="/wiki/Donkey" title="Donkey">donkey</a> from the convent and refused to acknowledge his crime. Opportuna turned the matter over to God, and the next day the farmer's field was <a href="/wiki/Salting_the_earth" title="Salting the earth">sown with salt</a>. The repentant peasant both returned the donkey and gave the nuns the field.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Opportuna_of_Montreuil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Though she was not recorded in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a></i> of Saint Opportuna has always flourished locally, as a patroness of Almanèches and among the patrons of Paris; she was praised in the <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">acta</a></i> of her kinsman, Chrodegang/Godegrand. During the reign of <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a>, according to the sources,<sup class="reference" id="ref_Fnone"><a href="#endnote_Fnone">[f]</a></sup> Vikings invaded; both the convent at Montreuil and the abbey at Almenèches were destroyed, and her <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were translated to the <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> of <a href="/wiki/Moussy,_Val-d%27Oise" title="Moussy, Val-d&#39;Oise">Moussy</a>. Later they were moved to <a href="/wiki/Senlis,_Oise" class="mw-redirect" title="Senlis, Oise">Senlis</a>. In 1374, her right arm and a rib were enshrined in a small church dedicated to her in Paris, near a <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> called <i>Notre Dame des Bois Paris</i>. As the city grew, so did the church. Most of Opportuna's head still rests at Moussy, while her left arm and part of her skull are still at Almenèches; additionally, a jaw bone can be found in the priory of Saint <a href="/wiki/Chrodegang" title="Chrodegang">Chrodegang</a> at <a href="/w/index.php?title=%C3%8Ele-Adam&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Île-Adam (page does not exist)">Île-Adam</a>. Her shrine in Paris is carried in processions along with the relics of <a href="/wiki/Honoratus" title="Honoratus">Saints Honoré</a> and <a href="/wiki/Genevi%C3%A8ve" class="mw-redirect" title="Geneviève">Geneviève</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Opportuna_of_Montreuil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In art, Saint Opportuna is often depicted carrying an abbess's <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">casket of relics</a>. She may also be shown with the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Virgin</a> appearing at her deathbed or as a <a href="/wiki/Princess" title="Princess">princess</a> with a basket of <a href="/wiki/Cherry" title="Cherry">cherries</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Fleur-de-lys" class="mw-redirect" title="Fleur-de-lys">fleur-de-lys</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notes">Notes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Opportuna_of_Montreuil&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Notes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1054258005">.mw-parser-output .refbegin{font-size:90%;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .refbegin-hanging-indents>ul{margin-left:0}.mw-parser-output .refbegin-hanging-indents>ul>li{margin-left:0;padding-left:3.2em;text-indent:-3.2em}.mw-parser-output .refbegin-hanging-indents ul,.mw-parser-output .refbegin-hanging-indents ul li{list-style:none}@media(max-width:720px){.mw-parser-output .refbegin-hanging-indents>ul>li{padding-left:1.6em;text-indent:-1.6em}}.mw-parser-output .refbegin-columns{margin-top:0.3em}.mw-parser-output .refbegin-columns ul{margin-top:0}.mw-parser-output .refbegin-columns li{page-break-inside:avoid;break-inside:avoid-column}</style><div class="refbegin" style="">
<ol type="a">
<li><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1041539562">.mw-parser-output .citation{word-wrap:break-word}.mw-parser-output .citation:target{background-color:rgba(0,127,255,0.133)}</style><span class="citation wikicite" id="endnote_Anone"><a href="#ref_Anone"><b><sup>^</sup></b></a></span> Meaning "little monastery", it later gave its name to <a href="/wiki/Montreuil-la-Cambe" title="Montreuil-la-Cambe">Montreuil-la-Cambe</a>.</li>
<li><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1041539562"/><span class="citation wikicite" id="endnote_Bnone"><a href="#ref_Bnone"><b><sup>^</sup></b></a></span> He should probably not be conflated with Chrodegang of Metz.</li>
<li><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1041539562"/><span class="citation wikicite" id="endnote_Cnone"><a href="#ref_Cnone"><b><sup>^</sup></b></a></span> Opportuna's brother is identified with <a href="/wiki/Chrodegang_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Chrodegang of Metz">Saint Chrodegang of Metz</a> by at least one source.<sup id="cite_ref-patron2_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> However, Opportuna's story, which places him as Bishop of Séez and being murdered by a rival while on the road to Montreuil, is not mentioned in any resources relating to his life, which state instead that he died on 6 March 776 at <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>.</li>
<li><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1041539562"/><span class="citation wikicite" id="endnote_Dnone"><a href="#ref_Dnone"><b><sup>^</sup></b></a></span> Lacking opportunities for public careers, their lives offered few events. Three Merovingian hagiographies of female saints circulated widely: <a href="/wiki/Genevieve" title="Genevieve">Geneviève of Paris</a> (d. 502), <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a>' <i>vita</i> of <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Radegund</a> (d. 587) and that of <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles" title="Gertrude of Nivelles">Gertrude of Nivelles</a>. To these can be added the Hiberno-Latin <i>vita</i> of <a href="/wiki/Brigid" title="Brigid">Brigid</a> (Smith 1995:13).
</li><li><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1041539562"/><span class="citation wikicite" id="endnote_Enone"><a href="#ref_Enone"><b><sup>^</sup></b></a></span> Here, Adalhelm envisions her being summoned to Heaven by <a href="/wiki/Cecilia_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cecilia (saint)">Cecilia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lucy" title="Saint Lucy">Lucy</a>.</li>
<li><link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1041539562"/><span class="citation wikicite" id="endnote_Fnone"><a href="#ref_Fnone"><b><sup>^</sup></b></a></span> The <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Paris_(885-886)" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Paris (885-886)">Siege of Paris (885-886)</a> took place during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Fat" title="Charles the Fat">Charles the Fat</a>, who may have been intended.</li>
