<p><b>Mateo Correa Magallanes</b> (also known as Mateo Correa, Fr. Correa; July 23, 1866 – February 6, 1927) was a <a href="/wiki/Knight_of_Columbus" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of Columbus">Knight of Columbus</a>, of Council 2140.<sup id="cite_ref-knights_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-knights-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Correa was born at <a href="/wiki/Tepechitl%C3%A1n" title="Tepechitlán">Tepechitlán</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zacatecas" title="Zacatecas">Zacatecas</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mexico" title="Mexico">Mexico</a>.  He attended the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Zacatecas" title="Zacatecas">Zacatecas</a> on a scholarship, in 1881. He was ordained as priest in 1893 at the age of 27. As a young priest, he gave first communion to <a href="/wiki/Miguel_Pro" title="Miguel Pro">Miguel Pro</a> who also became a priest and was later martyred. Correa was assigned as a parish priest to <a href="/wiki/Concepci%C3%B3n_del_Oro" title="Concepción del Oro">Concepción del Oro</a> in 1898, and then to <a href="/wiki/Colotl%C3%A1n" title="Colotlán">Colotlán</a> in 1908. Following the government's repression of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> Church in 1910, he went into hiding. He was assigned to <a href="/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso,_Zacatecas" title="Valparaíso, Zacatecas">Valparaíso</a> in 1926.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mateo_Correa_Magallanes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1927, during the government's continuing persecution of the church, Correa was arrested by soldiers as he was bringing <a href="/wiki/Viaticum" title="Viaticum">Viaticum</a> to a woman who was an invalid. Accused of being part of the armed <a href="/wiki/Cristero" class="mw-redirect" title="Cristero">Cristero</a> defense, he was jailed in Zacatecas, and then in Durango. On February 5, 1927, Correa was asked by General Eulogio Ortiz to hear the confessions of some imprisoned members of the <a href="/wiki/Cristeros" class="mw-redirect" title="Cristeros">Cristeros</a>, an uprising of Catholic men who decided to fight back against the persecution of the church led by Mexico's president Plutarco Elias Calles. Correa agreed to administer the Sacrament of Confession to these prisoners, but afterward Ortiz demanded to know what the condemned prisoners had confessed. Correa refused. Ortiz then pointed a gun at Correa's head and threatened him with immediate death. Correa continued to refuse, and at dawn on February 6, 1927, he was taken to the cemetery on the outskirts of Durango and shot through the head.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mateo_Correa_Magallanes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> on November 22, 1992, and then <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on May 21, 2000 during the Jubilee of Mexico.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
