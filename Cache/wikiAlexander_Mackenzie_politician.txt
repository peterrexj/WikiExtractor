<p><b>Alexander Mackenzie</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/King%27s_Privy_Council_for_Canada" title="King&#39;s Privy Council for Canada">PC</a></span> (January 28, 1822&#160;– April 17, 1892) was a Canadian politician who served as the second <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Canada" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime minister of Canada">prime minister of Canada</a>, in office from 1873 to 1878.
</p><p>Mackenzie was born in <a href="/wiki/Logierait" title="Logierait">Logierait</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perthshire" title="Perthshire">Perthshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>. He left school at the age of 13, following his father's death, to help his widowed mother, and trained as a <a href="/wiki/Stonemason" class="mw-redirect" title="Stonemason">stonemason</a>. Mackenzie immigrated to the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Canada" title="Province of Canada">Province of Canada</a> when he was 19, settling in what became <a href="/wiki/Ontario" title="Ontario">Ontario</a>. His masonry business prospered, allowing him to pursue other interests – such as the editorship of a pro-<a href="/wiki/Reform_movement_(pre-Confederation_Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Reform movement (pre-Confederation Canada)">Reformist</a> newspaper called the<i> Lambton Shield</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mackenzie was elected to the <a href="/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_the_Province_of_Canada" title="Legislative Assembly of the Province of Canada">Legislative Assembly of the Province of Canada</a> in 1862, as a supporter of <a href="/wiki/George_Brown_(Canadian_politician)" title="George Brown (Canadian politician)">George Brown</a>.
</p><p>In 1867, Mackenzie was elected to the new <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada" title="House of Commons of Canada">House of Commons of Canada</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Canada" title="Liberal Party of Canada">Liberal Party</a>. He became leader of the party (thus <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Leader of the Opposition (Canada)">Leader of the Opposition</a>) in mid-1873, and a few months later succeeded <a href="/wiki/John_A._Macdonald" title="John A. Macdonald">John A. Macdonald</a> as prime minister, following Macdonald's resignation in the aftermath of the <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Scandal" title="Pacific Scandal">Pacific Scandal</a>. Mackenzie and the Liberals won a clear majority at the <a href="/wiki/1874_Canadian_federal_election" title="1874 Canadian federal election">1874 election</a>. He was popular among the general public for his humble background and consistent democratic principles.
</p><p>As prime minister, Mackenzie continued the nation-building programme that had been begun by his predecessor. His government established the <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Canada" title="Supreme Court of Canada">Supreme Court of Canada</a> and <a href="/wiki/Royal_Military_College_of_Canada" title="Royal Military College of Canada">Royal Military College of Canada</a>, and created the <a href="/wiki/District_of_Keewatin" title="District of Keewatin">District of Keewatin</a> to better administer Canada's newly acquired western territories. However, it made little progress on the <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway" title="Canadian Pacific Railway">transcontinental railway</a>, and struggled to deal with the aftermath of the <a href="/wiki/Panic_of_1873" title="Panic of 1873">Panic of 1873</a>. At the <a href="/wiki/1878_Canadian_federal_election" title="1878 Canadian federal election">1878 election</a>, Mackenzie's government suffered a landslide defeat. He remained leader of the Liberal Party for another two years, and continued on as a <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(Canada)" title="Member of Parliament (Canada)">Member of Parliament</a> (MP) until his death, due to a stroke.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mackenzie was born on January 28, 1822, in Logierait, Perthshire, Scotland, the son of Mary Stewart (Fleming) and Alexander Mackenzie Sr. (born 1784) who were married in 1817.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The site of his birthplace is known as Clais-'n-deoir (the Hollow of the Weeping), where families said their goodbyes as the convicted were led to nearby Gallows Hill. The house in which he was born was built by his father. He was the third of 10 boys, seven of whom survived infancy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Alexander Mackenzie Sr. was a carpenter and ship's joiner who had to move around frequently for work after the end of the Napoleonic Wars in 1815. Mackenzie's father died on March 7, 1836, and at the age of 13, Alexander Mackenzie Jr. was thus forced to end his formal education to help support his family. He apprenticed as a stonemason and met his future wife, Helen Neil, in Irvine, where her father was also a stonemason.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Neils were Baptist and shortly thereafter, Mackenzie converted from <a href="/wiki/Presbyterianism" title="Presbyterianism">Presbyterianism</a> to <a href="/wiki/Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptist">Baptist</a> beliefs.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Together with the Neils, he immigrated to Canada in 1842 to seek a better life. Mackenzie's faith was to link him to the increasingly influential <a href="/wiki/Temperance_cause" class="mw-redirect" title="Temperance cause">temperance cause</a>, particularly strong in <a href="/wiki/Canada_West" class="mw-redirect" title="Canada West">Canada West</a> where he lived, a constituency of which he was to represent in the <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada" title="Parliament of Canada">Parliament of Canada</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Neils and Mackenzie settled in Kingston, Ontario. The limestone in the area proved too hard for his stonemason tools, and not having money to buy new tools, Mackenzie took a job as a labourer constructing a building on Princess Street.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The contractor on the job claimed financial difficulty, so Mackenzie accepted a promissory note for summer wages. The note later proved to be worthless. Subsequently, Mackenzie won a contract building a bomb-proof arch at <a href="/wiki/Fort_Henry,_Ontario" class="mw-redirect" title="Fort Henry, Ontario">Fort Henry</a>. He later became a foreman on the construction of Kingston's four <a href="/wiki/Martello_tower" title="Martello tower">Martello Towers</a> – <a href="/wiki/Murney_Tower" title="Murney Tower">Murney Tower</a>, <a href="/wiki/Fort_Frederick_(Kingston,_Ontario)" title="Fort Frederick (Kingston, Ontario)">Fort Frederick</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cathcart_Tower" title="Cathcart Tower">Cathcart Tower</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Shoal_Tower" title="Shoal Tower">Shoal Tower</a>. He was also a foreman on the construction of the <a href="/wiki/Welland_Canal" title="Welland Canal">Welland Canal</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Lachine_Canal" title="Lachine Canal">Lachine Canal</a>. While working on the <a href="/wiki/Beauharnois_Canal" title="Beauharnois Canal">Beauharnois Canal</a>, a one-ton stone fell and crushed one of his legs. He recovered, but never regained the strength in that leg. While in Kingston, Mackenzie became a vocal opponent of religious and political entitlement and corruption in government.
</p><p>Mackenzie married Helen Neil (1826–52) in 1845 and with her had three children, with only one girl, Mary, surviving infancy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarris1893136_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarris1893136-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Helen and he moved to Sarnia, Ontario (known as Canada West) in 1847 and Mary was born in 1848.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They were soon joined from Scotland by the rest of Mackenzie's brothers and his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He began working as a general contractor, earning a reputation for being a hard-working, honest man, as well as having a working man's view on fiscal policy. Mackenzie helped construct many courthouses and jails across southern Ontario. A number of these still stand today, including the Sandwich Courthouse and Jail now known as the <a href="/wiki/Windsor,_Ontario" title="Windsor, Ontario">Mackenzie Hall Cultural Centre</a> in Windsor, Ontario, and the <a href="/wiki/Chatham_Jail" title="Chatham Jail">Kent County Courthouse and Jail</a> in Chatham, Ontario. He even bid, unsuccessfully, on the construction of the Parliament buildings in Ottawa in 1859. Helen died in 1852, finally succumbing to the effects of excessive doses of mercury-based calomel used to treat a fever while in Kingston. In 1853, he married <a href="/wiki/Jane_Mackenzie" title="Jane Mackenzie">Jane Sym</a> (1825–93).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHarris1893136_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarris1893136-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mason_overseer.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Mason_overseer.jpg/150px-Mason_overseer.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="212" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Mason_overseer.jpg/225px-Mason_overseer.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Mason_overseer.jpg/300px-Mason_overseer.jpg 2x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="637" /></a><figcaption>An 1875 <i><a href="/wiki/Canadian_Illustrated_News" title="Canadian Illustrated News">Canadian Illustrated News</a></i> cartoon shows Mackenzie the Mason and Governor General <a href="/wiki/Lord_Dufferin" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Dufferin">Lord Dufferin</a> the Overseer.</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_involvement">Early political involvement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early political involvement"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mackenzie involved himself in politics almost from the moment he arrived in Canada. He fought passionately for equality and the elimination of all forms of class distinction. In 1851, he became the secretary for the <a href="/wiki/Reform_movement_(pre-Confederation_Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Reform movement (pre-Confederation Canada)">Reform Party</a> for Lambton. After convincing him to run in Kent/Lambton, Mackenzie campaigned relentlessly for <a href="/wiki/George_Brown_(Canadian_politician)" title="George Brown (Canadian politician)">George Brown</a>, owner of the <a href="/wiki/Reform_movement_(pre-Confederation_Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Reform movement (pre-Confederation Canada)">Reformist</a> paper <i><a href="/wiki/The_Globe_(Toronto_newspaper)" title="The Globe (Toronto newspaper)">The Globe</a></i> in the 1851 election, helping Brown to win his first seat in the Legislative Assembly. Mackenzie and Brown remained the closest of friends and colleagues for the rest of their lives. In 1852, Mackenzie became editor of another reformist paper, the <i>Lambton Shield</i>. As an editor, Mackenzie was perhaps a little too vocal, leading the paper to a lawsuit for libel against the local conservative candidate. Because a key witness claimed Cabinet Confidence and would not testify, the paper lost the suit and was forced to fold due to financial hardship. After his brother, Hope Mackenzie, declined to run, Alexander was petitioned to run and won his first seat in the Legislative Assembly as a supporter of George Brown in 1861. When Brown resigned from the <a href="/wiki/Great_Coalition" title="Great Coalition">Great Coalition</a> in 1865 over reciprocity negotiations with the United States, Mackenzie was invited to replace him as president of the council. Wary of <a href="/wiki/John_A._Macdonald" title="John A. Macdonald">Macdonald's</a> motivations and true to his principles, Mackenzie declined.
</p><p>He entered the House of Commons of Canada in 1867, representing the <a href="/wiki/Lambton_(federal_electoral_district)" title="Lambton (federal electoral district)">Lambton</a>, Ontario, riding. No cohesive national <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Canada" title="Liberal Party of Canada">Liberal Party of Canada</a> existed at the time and with Brown not winning his seat, no official leader emerged. Mackenzie did not believe he was the best qualified for the position, and although he resisted offers of the position, he nevertheless sat as the <i>de facto</i> leader of the Official Opposition.
</p><p>Mackenzie served as a Major in the 27th Lambton Battalion of Infantry from 1866 to 1874,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> serving on active duty during the <a href="/wiki/Fenian_Raids" class="mw-redirect" title="Fenian Raids">Fenian Raids</a> in 1870.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Prime_Minister_.281873.E2.80.931878.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister_(1873–1878)">Prime Minister (1873–1878)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Prime Minister (1873–1878)"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Electoral_history_of_Alexander_Mackenzie" title="Electoral history of Alexander Mackenzie">Electoral history of Alexander Mackenzie</a></div>
<p>When the <a href="/wiki/John_A._Macdonald" title="John A. Macdonald">Macdonald</a> government fell due to the <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Scandal" title="Pacific Scandal">Pacific Scandal</a> in 1873, the <a href="/wiki/Governor_General_of_Canada" title="Governor General of Canada">Governor General</a>, <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Hamilton-Temple-Blackwood,_1st_Marquess_of_Dufferin_and_Ava" title="Frederick Hamilton-Temple-Blackwood, 1st Marquess of Dufferin and Ava">Lord Dufferin</a>, called upon Mackenzie, who had been chosen as leader of the <a href="/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Canada" title="Liberal Party of Canada">Liberal Party</a> a few months earlier, to form a new government. Mackenzie formed a government and asked the Governor General to call an <a href="/wiki/1874_Canadian_federal_election" title="1874 Canadian federal election">election for January 1874</a>. The Liberals won a majority of the seats in the House of Commons having garnered 40% of the popular vote.
</p><p>Mackenzie remained prime minister until the <a href="/wiki/1878_Canadian_federal_election" title="1878 Canadian federal election">1878 election</a> when Macdonald's <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada_(historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Conservative Party of Canada (historical)">Conservatives</a> returned to power with a <a href="/wiki/Majority_government" title="Majority government">majority government</a>.
</p><p>For a man of Mackenzie's humble origins to attain such a position was unusual in an age which generally offered such opportunity only to the privileged. Lord Dufferin expressed early misgivings about a stonemason taking over government, but on meeting Mackenzie, Dufferin revised his opinions:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1211633275">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 32px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>However narrow and inexperienced Mackenzie may be, I imagine he is a thoroughly upright, well-principled, and well-meaning man.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite>Lord Dufferin</cite></div></blockquote>
<p>Mackenzie served concurrently as Minister of Public Works and oversaw the completion of the Parliament buildings. While drawing up the plans for the <a href="/wiki/West_Block" title="West Block">West Block</a>, he included a circular staircase leading directly from his office to the outside of the building, which allowed him to escape the patronage-seekers waiting for him in his ante-chamber. Proving Dufferin's reflections on his character to be true, Mackenzie disliked intensely the patronage inherent in politics. Nevertheless, he found it a necessary evil to maintain party unity and ensure the loyalty of his fellow Liberals.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg/220px-Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="335" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg/330px-Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg/440px-Alexander_Mackenzie_Statue.jpg 2x" data-file-width="536" data-file-height="816" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Alexander Mackenzie on Parliament Hill, Ottawa by <a href="/wiki/Hamilton_MacCarthy" title="Hamilton MacCarthy">Hamilton MacCarthy</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In keeping with his democratic ideals, Mackenzie refused the offer of a knighthood three times,<sup id="cite_ref-grave_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grave-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and was thus the only one of Canada's first eight Prime Ministers not to be knighted. He also declined appointment to the UK Privy Council and hence does not bear the title "Right Honourable". His pride in his working class origins never left him. Once, while touring <a href="/wiki/Fort_Henry,_Ontario" class="mw-redirect" title="Fort Henry, Ontario">Fort Henry</a> as prime minister, he asked the soldier accompanying him if he knew the thickness of the wall beside them. The embarrassed escort confessed that he didn't and Mackenzie replied, "I do. It is five feet, ten inches. I know, because I built it myself!"<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Prime Minister, Alexander Mackenzie strove to reform and simplify the machinery of government, achieving a remarkable record of reform legislation. He introduced the <a href="/wiki/Secret_ballot" title="Secret ballot">secret ballot</a>; advised the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Canada" title="Supreme Court of Canada">Supreme Court of Canada</a>; the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Military_College_of_Canada" title="Royal Military College of Canada">Royal Military College of Canada</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario" title="Kingston, Ontario">Kingston</a> in 1874 and the creation of the Office of the <a href="/wiki/Auditor_General_of_Canada" title="Auditor General of Canada">Auditor General</a> in 1878. He completed the <a href="/wiki/Intercolonial_Railway" title="Intercolonial Railway">Intercolonial Railway</a>, but struggled to progress on the <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway" title="Canadian Pacific Railway">national railway</a> due to a worldwide economic depression, almost coming to blows with Governor General Lord Dufferin over imperial interference. Mackenzie stood up for the rights of Canada as a nation and fought for the supremacy of Parliament and honesty in government. Above all else, he was known and loved for his honesty and integrity.
</p><p>However, his term was marked by <a href="/wiki/Economic_depression" title="Economic depression">economic depression</a> that had grown out of the <a href="/wiki/Panic_of_1873" title="Panic of 1873">Panic of 1873</a>, which Mackenzie's government was unable to alleviate. In 1874, Mackenzie negotiated a new <a href="/wiki/Free_trade" title="Free trade">free trade</a> agreement with the United States, eliminating the high protective tariffs on Canadian goods in US markets. However, this action did not bolster the economy, and construction of the CPR slowed drastically due to lack of funding. In 1876, the Conservative opposition announced a <a href="/wiki/National_Policy" title="National Policy">National Policy</a> of protective tariffs, which resonated with voters. When an <a href="/wiki/1878_Canadian_federal_election" title="1878 Canadian federal election">election</a> was held at the conclusion of Mackenzie's five-year term, the Conservatives were swept back into office in a landslide victory.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Later life"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After his government's defeat, Mackenzie remained <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Leader of the Opposition (Canada)">Leader of the Opposition</a> for another two years, until 1880. In 1881, he became the first president of <a href="/wiki/North_American_Life" title="North American Life">The North American Life Assurance Company</a>. He was soon struck with a mysterious ailment that sapped his strength and all but took his voice. Sitting in silence, he nevertheless remained an undefeated MP until his death in 1892 from a stroke that resulted from hitting his head during a fall. He died in <a href="/wiki/Toronto" title="Toronto">Toronto</a> and was buried in Lakeview Cemetery in <a href="/wiki/Sarnia" title="Sarnia">Sarnia</a>, Ontario.<sup id="cite_ref-grave_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grave-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Character">Character</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Character"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mackenzie's first biography in 1892 referred to him as Canada's Stainless Statesman.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892dedication_introduction_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892dedication_introduction-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  He was a devout Baptist and teetotaller who found refuge in, and drew strength from, his family, friends, and faith.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss189255,_93,_100_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss189255,_93,_100-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEForster1990_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEForster1990-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was also a loyal friend and an incorrigible prankster (stuffed chimney on young in-laws; rolled boulder down Thunder Cape towards friend A. McKellar; burned Tory campaign placards in hotel woodstove early in morning).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson196018,_87_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson196018,_87-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Unpretentious and down to earth,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss189299,_633,_660_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss189299,_633,_660-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> his public official austerity was in striking contrast to private compassion and giving nature.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss189299–100_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss189299–100-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He was the soul of honour and integrity,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarquis1903401_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarquis1903401-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> a proud man who sought no recognition or personal enrichment and accepted gifts reluctantly.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1960354,_367_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1960354,_367-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> He preferred to follow than to lead (unreferenced – many times he refused leadership offers) and often found that duty outweighed the heavy burden of office.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He was uncompromising on his principles, perhaps too much so.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> An historian at the time said, "He was, and ever will remain, the Sir Galahad of Canadian politics."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMarquis1903418_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarquis1903418-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Very proud of his Scottish heritage, he was forever a Scot: "<i>Nemo me impune lacessit</i>" (no one attacks me with impunity).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERoss191356_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERoss191356-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The Upper Canada rebellion leader <a href="/wiki/William_Lyon_Mackenzie" title="William Lyon Mackenzie">W.L. Mackenzie</a> said of him, "He is every whit a self-made, self-educated man. Has large mental capacity and indomitable energy." <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892120_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892120-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Canada's Governor General, Lord Dufferin, said he was "as pure as crystal, and as true as steel, with lots of common sense."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1960211_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1960211-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> A close friend, <a href="/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Canada" title="Chief Justice of Canada">Chief Justice</a> <a href="/wiki/Louis_Henry_Davies" title="Louis Henry Davies">Sir Louis Davies</a>, said he was "the best debater the House of Commons has ever known."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> A friend and colleague in Cabinet who went on to become prime minister of Canada, Sir <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid_Laurier" title="Wilfrid Laurier">Wilfrid Laurier</a>, said he was "one of the truest and strongest characters to be met within Canadian history. He was endowed with a warm heart and a copious and rich fancy, though veiled by a somewhat reticent exterior, and he was of friends the most tender and true."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892633_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892633-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Another friend and colleague, who went on to become premier of Ontario, Sir <a href="/wiki/George_William_Ross" title="George William Ross">George Ross</a>, said, "Mackenzie was <i>sui generis</i> a debater. His humorous sallies blistered like a blast from a flaming smelter. His sterling honesty is a great heritage, and will keep his memory green to all future generations."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERoss191331_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERoss191331-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> At his eulogy, Rev. Dr. Thomas compared him to the <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a>, who "stood four square, to all the winds that blow."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892643,_quoting_Tennyson’s_&quot;Ode_to_the_Death_of_the_Duke_of_Wellington&quot;_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892643,_quoting_Tennyson’s_&quot;Ode_to_the_Death_of_the_Duke_of_Wellington&quot;-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Newspapers around the world and in Canada had this to say about him. <i>The London Times</i> – the untiring energy, the business-like accuracy, the keen perception and reliable judgment, and above all the inflexible integrity, which marked his private life, he carried without abatement of one jot into his public career.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892663_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892663-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> <i>The Westminster Review</i> – a man, who although, through failing health and failing voice, he had virtually passed out of public life, yet retained to the last the affectionate veneration of the Canadian people as no other man of the time can be said to have done.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892651_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892651-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>  The <i>Charlottetown Patriot</i> – in all that constitutes the real man, the honest statesman, the true patriot, the warm friend, and sincere Christian, he had few equals. Possessed of a clear intellect, a retentive memory, and a ready command of appropriate words, he was one of the most logical and powerful speakers we have ever heard.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892662_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892662-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The <i>St. John Telegraph</i> – he was loved by the people and his political opponents were compelled to respect him even above their own chosen leader. As a statesman, he has had few equals.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892660_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892660-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Montreal Star</i> – it is one of the very foremost architects of the Canadian nationality that we mourn. In the dark days of ’73, Canadians were in a state of panic, distrusting the stability of their newly-built Dominion; no one can tell what would have happened had not the stalwart form of Alexander Mackenzie lifted itself above the screaming, vociferating and denying mass of politicians, and all Canada felt at once, there was a man who could be trusted.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892661_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892661-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Toronto Globe</i> – he was a man who loved the people and fought for their rights against privilege and monopoly in every form.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892661_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892661-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Philadelphia Record</i> – Like Caesar, who twice refused a knightly crown, Alexander Mackenzie refused knighthood three times. Unlike Caesar, he owed his political overthrow to his incorruptible honesty and unswerving integrity.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892660_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuckinghamRoss1892660-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In their 1999 study of the Prime Ministers of Canada, which included the results of a survey of Canadian historians, <a href="/wiki/J._L._Granatstein" title="J. L. Granatstein">J. L. Granatstein</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norman_Hillmer" title="Norman Hillmer">Norman Hillmer</a> found that Mackenzie was in 11th place just after <a href="/wiki/John_Sparrow_David_Thompson" title="John Sparrow David Thompson">John Sparrow David Thompson</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Namesakes">Namesakes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Namesakes"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:RMC_1880.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/RMC_1880.jpg/220px-RMC_1880.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="133" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/RMC_1880.jpg/330px-RMC_1880.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/RMC_1880.jpg/440px-RMC_1880.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="386" /></a><figcaption>Mackenzie Building at the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Military_College_of_Canada" title="Royal Military College of Canada">Royal Military College of Canada</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The following are named in honour of Alexander Mackenzie:
</p>
<ul><li>The <a href="/wiki/Mackenzie_Mountains" title="Mackenzie Mountains">Mackenzie Mountain Range</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Yukon" title="Yukon">Yukon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Northwest_Territories" title="Northwest Territories">Northwest Territories</a></li>
<li>Mount Mackenzie, in the Selkirk Mountains of British Columbia</li>
<li>The Mackenzie Building, and the use of the <a href="/wiki/Clan_Mackenzie" title="Clan Mackenzie">Mackenzie</a> tartan by the bands at the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Military_College_of_Canada" title="Royal Military College of Canada">Royal Military College of Canada</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario" title="Kingston, Ontario">Kingston</a>, Ontario, "Alexander Mackenzie", the Royal Military College of Canada March for bagpipes, was composed in his honour by Pipe Major Don M. Carrigan, who was the College Pipe Major 1973 to 1985.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Mackenzie Hall in <a href="/wiki/Windsor,_Ontario" title="Windsor, Ontario">Windsor</a>, Ontario</li>
<li>Alexander Mackenzie Scholarships in Economics and Political Science at <a href="/wiki/McGill_University" title="McGill University">McGill University</a> and the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Toronto" title="University of Toronto">University of Toronto</a></li>
<li>Alexander MacKenzie Park in <a href="/wiki/Sarnia" title="Sarnia">Sarnia</a>, Ontario<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Alexander Mackenzie High School in Sarnia</li>
<li>Alexander Mackenzie Housing Co-Operative Inc. in Sarnia</li>
<li>Mackenzie Avenue, Ottawa, Ontario</li>
<li>Mackenzie Tower, West Block, Parliament Hill, Ottawa, Ontario</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_honours">Other honours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Other honours"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>A monument is dedicated to his tomb in <a href="/wiki/Lakeview_Cemetery,_Sarnia" class="mw-redirect" title="Lakeview Cemetery, Sarnia">Lakeview Cemetery, Sarnia</a>, Ontario</li>
<li>"Honourable Alexander Mackenzie" (1964) by <a href="/wiki/Lawren_Harris" title="Lawren Harris">Lawren Harris</a>, head of the Department of Fine Arts, <a href="/wiki/Mount_Allison_University" title="Mount Allison University">Mount Allison University</a>, now hangs in the Mackenzie Building, Royal Military College of Canada. The unveiling ceremony was performed by the Right Honourable <a href="/wiki/Louis_St._Laurent" title="Louis St. Laurent">Louis St. Laurent</a>, a Canadian former Prime Minister, and the gift was accepted by the Commandant, Air Commodore L.J. Birchall. The painting was commissioned in memory of No. 244, Lieut.-Col, F.B. Wilson, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire" title="Order of the British Empire">O.B.E.</a>, her deceased husband, by Mrs, F.W. Dashwood. Also taking part in the ceremony was the Honourable <a href="/wiki/Paul_Hellyer" title="Paul Hellyer">Paul Hellyer</a>, <a href="/wiki/Minister_of_National_Defence_(Canada)" title="Minister of National Defence (Canada)">Minister of National Defence</a>, President and Chancellor of the college.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> In attendance was Mrs. Burton R. Morgan of Ottawa, great-granddaughter of Alexander Mackenzie.</li>
