<p><b>Adalard of Corbie</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Adalhardus Corbeiensis</i>; c. 751, <a href="/wiki/Huise" title="Huise">Huise</a> – 2 January 827)<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was son of <a href="/wiki/Bernard,_son_of_Charles_Martel" title="Bernard, son of Charles Martel">Bernard</a> the son of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a> and half-brother of <a href="/wiki/Pippin_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin the Younger">Pepin</a>; <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> was his <a href="/wiki/Cousin" title="Cousin">cousin</a>. He ia recognised as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> within the Catholic and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalard_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adalard received a good education in the Palatine School at the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty" title="Carolingian dynasty">Court of Charlemagne</a> in <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Aachen" title="Palace of Aachen">Aachen</a>, and while still very young was made Count of the Palace. At the age of twenty he entered the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Corbie_Abbey" title="Corbie Abbey">Corbie</a> in <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>, a monastery that had been founded by queen <a href="/wiki/Bathild" class="mw-redirect" title="Bathild">Bathild</a>, in 662.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  In order to be more secluded, he went to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>, but was ordered by Charlemagne to return to Corbie, where he was elected abbot. At the same time Charlemagne made him <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister" title="Prime minister">prime minister</a> to his son <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Italy" title="Pepin of Italy">Pepin</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Italy" title="King of Italy">King of Italy</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As a high court administrator, he attended some meetings that discussed military planning. His <i>De ordine palatinii</i> discusses in some detail a well-developed intelligence system by the end of Pepin's reign.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At his death in Milan in 810, Pepin appointed Adalard tutor to his son <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Italy" title="Bernard of Italy">Bernard of Italy</a>, then but twelve years of age.
</p><p>When, in 817, Bernard, son of Pepin, aspired to the imperial crown, emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> suspected Adalard of being in sympathy with Bernard and banished him to Hermoutier, the modern <a href="/wiki/Noirmoutier" title="Noirmoutier">Noirmoutier</a>, on the island of the same name. Adalard's brother <a href="/wiki/Wala_of_Corbie" title="Wala of Corbie">Wala</a> was obliged to become a monk at Corbie.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After seven years Louis saw his mistake and made Adalard one of his chief advisers.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Several hospitals were erected by him. In 822 Adalard and his brother <a href="/wiki/Wala_of_Corbie" title="Wala of Corbie">Wala</a> founded <a href="/wiki/Corvey_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Corvey Abbey">Corvey Abbey</a> ("New Corbie") in <a href="/wiki/Westphalia" title="Westphalia">Westphalia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Corvey was an imperial abbey; its territory extending from the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Paderborn" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Paderborn">bishopric of Paderborn</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brunswick" title="Duchy of Brunswick">duchy of Brunswick</a>. Its abbot was one of the eleven abbots, who sat with twenty-one bishops in the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Diet_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" title="Imperial Diet (Holy Roman Empire)">imperial diet</a> at <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adalard was returning from Corvey to old Corbie, when he fell sick three days before Christmas: he died about three in the afternoon, on January 1 in the year 827,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> at the age of seventy-three.
</p><p>Shortly after his death the <i>Vitae Adalhardi</i> was written by <a href="/wiki/Paschasius_Radbertus" title="Paschasius Radbertus">Paschasius Radbertus</a>, who admired Adalhard greatly. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalard_of_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adalard is honoured as <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of many churches and towns in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and along the lower <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
