<p><b>Lucia of Syracuse</b> (283–304), also called <b>Saint Lucia</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Sancta Lucia</i>) better known as <b>Saint Lucy</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_people" title="Roman people">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a> who died during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>. She is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lutheranism" title="Lutheranism">Lutheran</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches. She is one of eight women (including <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">the Virgin Mary</a>) explicitly commemorated by Catholics in the <a href="/wiki/Canon_of_the_Mass" title="Canon of the Mass">Canon of the Mass</a>. Her traditional feast day, known in Europe as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lucy%27s_Day" title="Saint Lucy&#39;s Day">Saint Lucy's Day</a>, is observed by <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western Christians</a> on 13 December. Lucia of Syracuse was honored in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> and remained a well-known saint in early modern <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She is one of the best known <a href="/wiki/Virgin_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin martyrs">virgin martyrs</a>, along with <a href="/wiki/Agatha_of_Sicily" title="Agatha of Sicily">Agatha of Sicily</a>, <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Rome" title="Agnes of Rome">Agnes of Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cecilia" title="Saint Cecilia">Cecilia of Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria" title="Catherine of Alexandria">Catherine of Alexandria</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The oldest record of her story comes from the fifth-century <i>Acts of the Martyrs</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The single fact upon which various accounts agree is that a disappointed suitor accused Lucy of being a Christian, and she was executed in Syracuse, Sicily, in the year 304 during the Diocletianic Persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her veneration spread to Rome, and by the sixth century to the whole Church. The oldest archaeological evidence comes from the Greek inscriptions from the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Catacombs_of_St._John&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Catacombs of St. John (page does not exist)">Catacombs of St. John</a> in Syracuse. <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Legenda Aurea</a></i> was the most widely read version of the Lucy legend in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. In medieval accounts, Saint Lucy's eyes were gouged out prior to her execution.
The most ancient archaeological traces attributable to the cult of Saint Lucia have been brought back to Sicily, particularly in Syracuse and are preserved in the archaeological museums of the city.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>All the details of her life are the conventional ones associated with female martyrs of the early fourth century. John Henry Blunt views her story as a Christian <a href="/wiki/Romance_(heroic_literature)" class="mw-redirect" title="Romance (heroic literature)">romance</a> similar to the Acts of other virgin martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-blunt_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blunt-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the traditional story, Lucy was born to rich and noble parents in the year 283 AD. Her father was of Roman origin,<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> but died when she was five years old,<sup id="cite_ref-scranton_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scranton-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> leaving Lucy and her mother without a protective guardian. Her mother's name <a href="/wiki/Eutychia" class="mw-disambig" title="Eutychia">Eutychia</a> seems to indicate that she came from a Greek background.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Like many of the early martyrs, Lucy had consecrated her virginity to God, and she hoped to distribute her dowry to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, Eutychia, not knowing of Lucy's promise, and suffering from a bleeding disorder, feared for Lucy's future. She arranged Lucy's marriage to a young man of a wealthy pagan family.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg/170px-Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="239" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg/255px-Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg/340px-Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="520" data-file-height="730" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Jacobello_del_Fiore_Santa_Luc%C3%ADa_en_el_sepulcro_de_Santa_Ag%C3%BCeda_PC_Fermo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Eutychia and Lucy at the Tomb of Saint Agatha</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Jacobello_del_Fiore" title="Jacobello del Fiore">Jacobello del Fiore</a></div></div></div>
<p>Saint <a href="/wiki/Agatha_of_Sicily" title="Agatha of Sicily">Agatha</a> had been martyred 52 years before during the <a href="/wiki/Decian_persecution" title="Decian persecution">Decian persecution</a>. Her shrine at Catania, less than 50 miles from Syracuse, attracted a number of pilgrims; many miracles were reported to have happened through her intercession. Eutychia was persuaded to make a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a>, in hopes of a cure. While there, St. Agatha came to Lucy in a dream and told her that because of her faith, her mother would be cured and that Lucy would be the glory of Syracuse, as she was of Catania. With her mother cured, Lucy took the opportunity to persuade her mother to allow her to distribute a great part of her riches among the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eutychia suggested that the sums would make a good bequest, but Lucy countered, "...whatever you give away at death for the Lord's sake you give because you cannot take it with you. Give now to the true Savior, while you are healthy, whatever you intended to give away at your death."<sup id="cite_ref-skeat_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-skeat-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>News that the patrimony and jewels were being distributed came to Lucy's betrothed, who denounced her to Paschasius, the Governor of Syracuse. Paschasius ordered her to burn a sacrifice to the emperor's image. When she refused, Paschasius sentenced her to be defiled in a brothel.
</p><p>The Christian tradition states that when the guards came to take her away, they could not move her even when they hitched her to a team of oxen. Bundles of wood were then heaped about her and set on fire, but would not burn. Finally, she met her death by the sword<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> thrust into her throat.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Lotto,_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Lotto%2C_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg/250px-Lotto%2C_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="314" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Lotto%2C_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg/375px-Lotto%2C_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Lotto%2C_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg/500px-Lotto%2C_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1902" data-file-height="2386" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Lotto,_pala_di_santa_lucia_00.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Lucy Before the Judge</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Lorenzo_Lotto" title="Lorenzo Lotto">Lorenzo Lotto</a>, 1523–32</div></div></div>
<p>Absent in the early narratives and traditions, at least until the fifteenth century, is the story of Lucia tortured by eye-gouging. According to later accounts, before she died she foretold the punishment of Paschasius and the speedy end of the persecution, adding that Diocletian would reign no more, and Maximian would meet his end.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This so angered Paschasius that he ordered the guards to remove her eyes. Another version has Lucy taking her own eyes out in order to discourage a persistent suitor who admired them. When her body was prepared for burial in the family mausoleum it was discovered that her eyes had been miraculously restored.<sup id="cite_ref-scranton_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scranton-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This is one of the reasons that Lucy is the patron saint of those with eye illnesses.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By the sixth century, her story was sufficiently widespread that she appears in the Sacramentary of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-blunt_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blunt-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She is also commemorated in the ancient Roman Martyrology.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> St. Aldhelm (English, died in 709) and later the Venerable Bede (English, died in 735) attest that her popularity had already spread to England, where her festival was kept in England until the Protestant Reformation, as a holy day of the second rank in which no work but tillage or the like was allowed.<sup id="cite_ref-scranton_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scranton-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lucy is honored in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/December_13" title="December 13">13 December</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Monk <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_of_Gembloux" title="Sigebert of Gembloux">Sigebert of Gembloux</a> (1030–1112) wrote a mid-eleventh century <i><a href="/wiki/Passio" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio">passio</a></i>, to support a local cult of Lucy at <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> formerly had a commemoration of Saints Lucy and Geminianus on 16 September. This was removed in 1969, as a duplication of the feast of her <i><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">dies natalis</a></i> on 13 December and because the Geminianus in question, mentioned in the <i>Passio</i> of Saint Lucy, seems to be a fictitious figure,<sup id="cite_ref-CR_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> unrelated to the <a href="/wiki/Geminianus" title="Geminianus">Geminianus</a> whose feast is on 31 January.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:224px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg/222px-Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg" decoding="async" width="222" height="321" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg/333px-Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg/444px-Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg 2x" data-file-width="720" data-file-height="1040" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Lucy_by_Domenico_di_Pace_Beccafumi.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Lucy by <a href="/wiki/Domenico_Beccafumi" title="Domenico Beccafumi">Domenico Beccafumi</a>, 1521, a High Renaissance recasting of a Gothic iconic image (<a href="/wiki/Pinacoteca_Nazionale_(Siena)" title="Pinacoteca Nazionale (Siena)">Pinacoteca Nazionale</a>, <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>)</div></div></div>
<p>Sigebert of Gembloux, in his <i>sermo de Sancta Lucia</i>, chronicled that her body lay undisturbed in Sicily for 400 years, before <a href="/wiki/Faroald_II_of_Spoleto" title="Faroald II of Spoleto">Faroald II, Duke of Spoleto</a>, captured the island and transferred the body to <a href="/wiki/Corfinium" title="Corfinium">Corfinium</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Abruzzo" title="Abruzzo">Abruzzo</a>, Italy. From there it was removed by the <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Otho I</a> in 972 to <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a> and deposited in the church of St. Vincent. It was from this shrine that an arm of the saint was taken to the monastery of Luitburg in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Speyer" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Speyer">Diocese of Speyer</a> – an incident celebrated by Sigebert in verse.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The subsequent history of the relics is not clear.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to Umberto Benigni, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II" title="Pope Stephen II">Stephen II</a> (768) sent the relics of St. Lucy to Constantinople for safety against the Saracen incursions.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> On their capture of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 1204, the French found some relics attributed to Saint Lucy in the city, and <a href="/wiki/Enrico_Dandolo" title="Enrico Dandolo">Enrico Dandolo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Doge_of_Venice" title="Doge of Venice">Doge of Venice</a>, secured them for the monastery of St. George at <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-lucia_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lucia-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In 1513 the Venetians presented to <a href="/wiki/Louis_XII_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XII of France">Louis XII of France</a> the saint's head, which he deposited in the cathedral church of <a href="/wiki/Bourges" title="Bourges">Bourges</a>. Another account, however, states that the head was brought to Bourges from Rome, where it had been transferred during the time when the relics rested in Corfinium.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Parts of the body are present in Sicily in particular in Syracuse, which has preserved them from antiquity.
The remainder of the relics remain in Venice: they were transferred to the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Geremia" title="San Geremia">San Geremia</a> when the church of Santa Lucia was demolished in 1861 to make way for the new railway terminus. A century later, on 7 November 1981, thieves stole all her bones, except her head. Police recovered them five weeks later, on her feast day. Other parts of the corpse have found their way to Rome, Naples, Verona, Lisbon, Milan, as well as Germany and France.<sup id="cite_ref-lucia_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lucia-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lucy's Latin name <i>Lucia</i> shares a root (<i>luc-</i>) with the Latin word for light, <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/lux#Latin" class="extiw" title="wikt:lux">lux</a></i>. A number of traditions incorporate symbolic meaning of St. Lucy as the bearer of light in the darkness of winter, her feast day being 13 December. Because some versions of her story relate that her eyes were removed, either by herself or by her persecutors, she is the patron saint of the blind.<sup id="cite_ref-Campbell_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Campbell-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is also the patron saint of authors, cutlers, glaziers, laborers, martyrs, peasants, saddlers, salesmen, stained glass workers, and of <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a>, Italy. She is invoked against hemorraghes, dysentery, diseases of the eye, and throat infections.<sup id="cite_ref-cathculture_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathculture-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Lucy is the patroness of Syracuse in Sicily, Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-cathculture_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathculture-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> At the Piazza Duomo in Syracuse, the church of Santa Lucia alla Badia houses the painting <i><a href="/wiki/Burial_of_St._Lucy_(Caravaggio)" title="Burial of St. Lucy (Caravaggio)">Burial of St. Lucy</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Caravaggio" title="Caravaggio">Caravaggio</a>. She is also the patron saint of the coastal town of Olón, Ecuador, Colombia, which celebrates with a week-long festival culminating on the feast day 13 December. She is also the patron saint of the town of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Guanes,_Santander&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guanes, Santander (page does not exist)">Guanes, Santander</a>, Colombia.
</p><p>The Caribbean island of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lucia" title="Saint Lucia">Saint Lucia</a>, one of the Windward Islands in the Lesser Antilles, is named after her.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg/220px-Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg/330px-Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg/440px-Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Francesco_del_Cossa_-_Saint_Lucy.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Lucy, by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_del_Cossa" title="Francesco del Cossa">Francesco del Cossa</a> (c. 1430 – c. 1477)</div></div></div>
<p>The emblem of eyes on a cup or plate apparently reflects popular devotion to her as protector of sight, because of her name, Lucia (from the Latin word "lux" which means "light").<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In paintings St. Lucy is frequently shown holding her eyes on a golden plate. Lucy was represented in Gothic art holding a dish with two eyes on it. She also holds the palm branch, symbol of martyrdom and victory over evil.<sup id="cite_ref-scranton_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scranton-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Other symbolic images include a lamp, dagger, sword or two oxen.<sup id="cite_ref-cathculture_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathculture-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_literature">In literature</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: In literature">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Dante">Dante</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Dante">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Lucy first appears in Canto 2 of <i><a href="/wiki/The_Divine_Comedy#Inferno" class="mw-redirect" title="The Divine Comedy">Inferno</a></i>, the first canticle of <a href="/wiki/Dante_Alighieri" title="Dante Alighieri">Dante Alighieri</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Divine_Comedy" title="Divine Comedy">Divine Comedy</a></i>, as the messenger sent to <a href="/wiki/Beatrice_Portinari" title="Beatrice Portinari">Beatrice</a> from "The blessed Dame" (the Virgin Mary), to rouse Beatrice to send Virgil to Dante's aid. <a href="/wiki/Henry_Fanshawe_Tozer" title="Henry Fanshawe Tozer">Henry Fanshawe Tozer</a> identifies Lucia as representing "illuminative grace".<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Robert_Pogue_Harrison" title="Robert Pogue Harrison">Robert Pogue Harrison</a> and Rachel Jacoff, Lucia's appearance in this intermediary role is to reinforce the scene in which Virgil tries to fortify Dante's courage to begin the journey through the inferno.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In <i><a href="/wiki/Purgatorio" title="Purgatorio">Purgatorio</a></i> 9.52–63, Lucy carries a sleeping Dante to the entrance to purgatory. Since Lucy represents light, her appearance in <i>Purgatorio</i> 9 mirrors her appearance in <i>Inferno</i> 2; both times she carries him out of darkness. Lucy's light symbolism also explains why Dante tells this evening scene in <i>Purgatorio</i> 9 through the lens of the dawn. She carries him both out of the literal darkness to a new day, as well as the figurative darkness to lead him to salvation.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then in <i><a href="/wiki/Paradiso_(Dante)" title="Paradiso (Dante)">Paradiso</a></i> 32, Dante places her opposite Adam within the Mystic Rose in Canto XXXII of the <i>Paradiso</i>. Lucy may also be seen as a figure of Illuminating Grace or Mercy or even Justice.<sup id="cite_ref-dc_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dc-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Donne">Donne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Lucy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Donne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages" title="Late Middle Ages">Late Middle Ages</a> the shortest day of the year usually fell on her feast day, and the two became associated, as in <a href="/wiki/John_Donne" title="John Donne">John Donne</a>'s poem, "A Nocturnal upon St. Lucie's Day, being the <a href="/wiki/Shortest_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Shortest day">shortest day</a>" (1627). The poem begins with: "'Tis the year's midnight, and it is the day's".<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Due to the inaccuracy of the <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>, the shortest day actually fell a day or two earlier in Donne's time.
</p><p>Lucia is also the protagonist of a Swedish novel: "<i>Ett ljus i mörkret</i>" ("A light in the darkness") by Agneta Sjödin.
</p>

<p>Lucy's feast is on 13 December, in <a href="/wiki/Advent" title="Advent">Advent</a>. Her feast once coincided with the Winter Solstice, the shortest day of the year, before calendar reforms, so her feast day has become a festival of light.<sup id="cite_ref-scranton_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scranton-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Lucy is the patron saint of the city of Syracuse (<a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>).  On 13 December a silver statue of St. Lucy containing her relics is paraded through the streets before returning to the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Syracuse" title="Cathedral of Syracuse">Cathedral of Syracuse</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>  Sicilians recall a legend that holds that a famine ended on her feast day when ships loaded with grain entered the harbor. Here, it is traditional to eat whole grains instead of bread on 13 December. This usually takes the form of <a href="/wiki/Cucc%C3%ACa" title="Cuccìa">cuccìa</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-alio_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alio-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> a dish of boiled wheat berries often mixed with ricotta and honey, or sometimes served as a savory soup with beans.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This is particularly seen in <a href="/wiki/Scandinavia" title="Scandinavia">Scandinavian</a> countries, with their long dark winters. There, a young girl dressed in a white dress and a red sash (as the symbol of martyrdom) carries palms and wears a crown or wreath of candles on her head. In Denmark, Norway, Sweden, and parts of Finland, girls dressed as Lucy carry <a href="/wiki/St._Lucia_buns" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Lucia buns">St. Lucia buns</a> in procession as songs are sung. It is said that to vividly celebrate St. Lucy's Day will help one live the long winter days with enough light.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A special devotion to St. Lucy is practiced in the Italian regions of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Emilia-Romagna" title="Emilia-Romagna">Emilia-Romagna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Friuli_Venezia_Giulia" title="Friuli Venezia Giulia">Friuli Venezia Giulia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trentino-Alto_Adige/S%C3%BCdtirol" title="Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol">Trentino-Alto Adige</a>, in the North of the country, and <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> and <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a>, in the South, as well as in the Croatian coastal region of <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatia</a>. The feast is a Catholic-celebrated holiday with roots that can be traced to Sicily. On the 13th of every December it is celebrated with large traditional feasts of home-made pasta and various other Italian dishes, with a special dessert called <a href="/wiki/Cucc%C3%ACa" title="Cuccìa">cuccìa</a>, made of wheatberries, butter, sugar, chocolate, and milk. The large grains of soft wheat are representative of her eyes and are a treat only to be indulged in once a year. In <a href="/wiki/North_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="North Italy">North Italy</a>, Saint Lucy <a href="/wiki/Christmas_gift-bringer" title="Christmas gift-bringer">brings gift to children</a> between 12 and 13 December. Traditionally a bouquet of hay is put outside of the house for Lucy's Donkey and food in the house for Lucy to refresh them after the long night bringing gifts to every kid. In small towns, a parade with Saint Lucy is held the evening of the 12th when she goes through the main streets of the town munching sweets and candy from her cart, always together with her donkey.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A Hungarian custom is to plant wheat in a small pot on St. Lucy's feast. By Christmas green sprouts appear, signs of life coming from death. The wheat is then carried to the manger scene as the symbol of Christ in the Eucharist.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, villagers from <a href="/wiki/Barangay" title="Barangay">Barangay</a> Sta. Lucia in <a href="/wiki/Magarao,_Camarines_Sur" class="mw-redirect" title="Magarao, Camarines Sur">Magarao</a>, Camarines Sur, hold a <a href="/wiki/Novena" title="Novena">novena</a> to St. Lucy nine days before her feast. A <a href="/wiki/Procession" title="Procession">procession</a> of the saint's image is held every morning at the <a href="/wiki/Poblacion" title="Poblacion">poblacion</a> or village centre during the nine days leading up to St. Lucy's Day, attracting devotees from other parts of the <a href="/wiki/Bicol_Region" title="Bicol Region">Bicol Region</a>. Hymns to the saint, known as the <i>Gozos</i>, as well as the Spanish version of the <a href="/wiki/Ave_Maria" class="mw-redirect" title="Ave Maria">Ave Maria</a> are chanted during the dawn procession, which is followed by a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The feast day is also commemorated in Barangay Sucad in <a href="/wiki/Apalit" title="Apalit">Apalit</a> Pampanga after the traditional nine-day novena, where a whole day celebration is observed through Eucharistic Masses, festivals and the procession of the religious sculpture of Sta. Lucia in the evening before the evening Mass.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
