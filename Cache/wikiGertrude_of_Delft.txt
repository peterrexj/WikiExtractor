					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Delft14.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Delft14.JPG/220px-Delft14.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Delft14.JPG/330px-Delft14.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Delft14.JPG/440px-Delft14.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2112" data-file-height="2816" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Delft14.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Gertrude van der Oosten in Delft</div></div></div>
<p><b>Gertrude van der Oosten</b> (or <b>Gertrude of Delft</b>; died 6 January 1358) was a Dutch <a href="/wiki/Beguine" class="mw-redirect" title="Beguine">Beguine</a> who was considered a <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a> and had received the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">Stigmata</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gertrude_van_der_Oosten&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gertrude was born in Voorburcht in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Holland" title="County of Holland">County of Holland</a>, to peasant parents, and entered <a href="/wiki/Domestic_service" class="mw-redirect" title="Domestic service">domestic service</a> at Delft. Her surname of <i>van Ooten</i>, or "of the East", came from her custom of singing a hymn which began: <i>Het daghet in den Oosten</i>, i.e., "Daylight breaks in the East", which she is thought to have composed herself. 
</p><p>After living a pious life for many years and being jilted by her fiancé,<sup id="cite_ref-Routledge_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Routledge-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Gertrude obtained admission into the <a href="/wiki/Beguinage" title="Beguinage">beguinage</a> in <a href="/wiki/Delft" title="Delft">Delft</a>. Here, though not a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, or bound by <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>, she profited by the ample opportunities for <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplation</a> afforded by life in this community. She had great devotion to the mysteries of the <a href="/wiki/Incarnation" title="Incarnation">Incarnation</a>, especially to the <a href="/wiki/Passion_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion (Christianity)">Passion</a> of Christ. She is believed to have received the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">Stigmata</a> in the Good Friday of the year 1340:<sup id="cite_ref-Routledge_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Routledge-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> "while she lay prostrate before a crucifix, red blood began to gush from her hands, feet, and side and continued to do so seven times that day for every <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_hours" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgy of the hours">canonical hour</a>, and for days after."<sup id="cite_ref-OUP_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OUP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She begged God that this grace might be withdrawn, and her prayer was granted to the degree that the blood ceased to flow, but the marks of the Stigmata remained.<sup id="cite_ref-OUP_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OUP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the same time she displayed the gift of <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecy</a>.
</p><p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, her breast became filled with milk when she meditated on the <a href="/wiki/Nativity_of_Jesus" title="Nativity of Jesus">Nativity of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gertrude died in Delft on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Epiphany_(holiday)" title="Epiphany (holiday)">Epiphany</a> and was buried in the Church of St. Hippolytus in Delft, as that beguinage did not have its own church or cemetery. Her name has never been inscribed in the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>, though she is commemorated in various others, and her <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)#Roman_Catholic_&#39;&#39;cultus&#39;&#39;" title="Cult (religious practice)"><i>cultus</i></a> is a purely local one.
</p>
