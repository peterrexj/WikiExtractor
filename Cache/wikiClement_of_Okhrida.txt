<p><b>Saint Clement</b> or <b>Kliment of Ohrid</b> (<a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_language" title="Bulgarian language">Bulgarian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Macedonian_language" title="Macedonian language">Macedonian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Serbian_language" title="Serbian language">Serbian</a>: <span lang="sr">Климент Охридски</span>, <i>Kliment Ohridski</i>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Κλήμης τῆς Ἀχρίδας</span></span>, <i>Klḗmēs tē̂s Akhrídas</i>; <a href="/wiki/Slovak_language" title="Slovak language">Slovak</a>: <i lang="sk">Kliment Ochridský</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;830</span><span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>– 916) was one of the first medieval <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgarian</a> saints,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> scholar, writer, and apostle to the <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was one of the most prominent disciples of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Cyril and Methodius</a> and is often associated with the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Glagolitic" class="mw-redirect" title="Glagolitic">Glagolitic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic scripts</a>, especially their popularisation among <a href="/wiki/Christianise" class="mw-redirect" title="Christianise">Christianised</a> Slavs. He was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Ohrid_Literary_School" title="Ohrid Literary School">Ohrid Literary School</a> and is regarded as a patron of education and language by some Slavic people. He is considered to be the first bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Bulgarian Orthodox Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-bakalov_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bakalov-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> one of the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Apostles_of_Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Seven Apostles of Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Seven Apostles of Bulgarian Orthodox Church</a>  since the 10th century, and one of the premier saints of modern <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The mission of Clement was the crucial factor which transformed the Slavs in then <a href="/wiki/Kutmichevitsa" title="Kutmichevitsa">Kutmichevitsa</a> (present-day Macedonia)<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> into Bulgarians.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Clement is also the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a>, the city of <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a><sup id="cite_ref-saints_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Macedonian_Orthodox_Church" title="Macedonian Orthodox Church">Macedonian Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg/170px-Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg/255px-Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg/340px-Climent_of_Ohrid.jpg 2x" data-file-width="471" data-file-height="629" /></a><figcaption>Icon of Saint Clement, located in the Mother of God Perybleptos church, <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius,_Kastoria.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius%2C_Kastoria.jpg/170px-St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius%2C_Kastoria.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="214" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius%2C_Kastoria.jpg/255px-St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius%2C_Kastoria.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius%2C_Kastoria.jpg/340px-St._Clement_of_Ohrid_in_the_church_of_St._Athanasius%2C_Kastoria.jpg 2x" data-file-width="726" data-file-height="914" /></a><figcaption>Fresco of St. Clement in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Athanasius_of_Mouzaki" title="Church of St. Athanasius of Mouzaki">Church of St. Athanasius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kastoria" title="Kastoria">Kastoria</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The exact date of his birth is unknown. Most probably, he joined Methodius as a young man following him later to the monastery on <a href="/wiki/Mysian_Olympus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mysian Olympus">Mysian Olympus</a>. According to his hagiography by <a href="/wiki/Theophylact_of_Ohrid" title="Theophylact of Ohrid">Theophylact of Ohrid</a>, Clement knew the life of Methodius like no other. That is why most scholars think he was born in the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> in the territory where Methodius served during his political career, i.e. that he was a <a href="/wiki/Slav" class="mw-redirect" title="Slav">Slav</a> from <a href="/wiki/Southern_Macedonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Southern Macedonia">Southern Macedonia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> This gives rise to some researchers to indicate the area of <a href="/wiki/Thessaloniki" title="Thessaloniki">Thessaloniki</a> as the possible place of birth of Clement. According to others, the area of Southern Macedonia, including the northern approach to Thessaloniki, where he may have been born, was then part of the <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">First Bulgarian Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Most of <a href="/wiki/Macedonia_(region)" title="Macedonia (region)">Macedonia</a> became part of Bulgaria between 830 and 840, i.e when Clement was born.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Short Life of St. Clement</i> by Theophylact of Ohrid testifies to his Slavic origin, calling him "the first bishop in the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_language" title="Bulgarian language">Bulgarian language</a>,"<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> while <i>The Ohrid Legend</i> written by <a href="/wiki/Demetrios_Chomatenos" title="Demetrios Chomatenos">Demetrios Chomatenos</a> describes him as one of the European <a href="/wiki/Moesians" class="mw-redirect" title="Moesians">Moesians</a>, commonly known as Bulgarians.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup> Because of that, some scholars label him a Bulgarian Slav,<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> while <a href="/wiki/Dimitri_Obolensky" title="Dimitri Obolensky">Dimitri Obolensky</a> calls Clement a Slav inhabitant of the Kingdom of Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> A fringe view on his origin postulates that Clement was born in <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a>. This view is based on the lexicographical analysis of Clement's works.<sup id="cite_ref-povod_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-povod-30">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Clement participated in the mission of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Cyril and Methodius</a> to <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a>. In 867 or 868 he became a priest in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, ordained along with two other disciples of Cyril and Methodius, Gorazd and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Naum" title="Saint Naum">Naum</a>, by bishops <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Formosus</a> and Gauderic. After the death of Cyril, Clement accompanied Methodius on his journey from <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to <a href="/wiki/Pannonia" title="Pannonia">Pannonia</a> and Great Moravia. After the death of Methodius himself in 885, Clement headed the struggle against the <a href="/wiki/Germans" title="Germans">German</a> clergy in Great Moravia along with Gorazd. After spending some time in jail, he was expelled from Great Moravia and in 885 or 886 reached <a href="/wiki/Belgrade" title="Belgrade">Belgrade</a>, then in the borders of Bulgaria, together with <a href="/wiki/Naum_of_Preslav" class="mw-redirect" title="Naum of Preslav">Naum of Preslav</a>, <a href="/wiki/Angelarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Angelarius">Angelarius</a> and possibly Gorazd (according to other sources, Gorazd was already dead by that time). Thereafter, the four of them were sent to the Bulgarian capital of <a href="/wiki/Pliska" title="Pliska">Pliska</a>, where they were commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Boris_I_of_Bulgaria" title="Boris I of Bulgaria">Boris I of Bulgaria</a> to instruct the future clergy of the state in the <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Old Slavonic language</a>.
</p><p>After the adoption of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> in 865, religious ceremonies in Bulgaria were conducted in <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> by clergy sent from the Byzantine Empire. Fearing growing Byzantine influence and weakening of the state, Boris viewed the adoption of the Old Slavonic language as a way to preserve the political independence and stability of Bulgaria. With a view thereto, Boris made arrangements for the establishment of two literary academies where theology was to be taught in the Slavonic language. The first of the schools was to be found in the capital, Pliska, and the second in the region of <a href="/wiki/Kutmichevitsa" title="Kutmichevitsa">Kutmichevitsa</a>.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Balkans850.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Balkans850.png/290px-Balkans850.png" decoding="async" width="290" height="239" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Balkans850.png/435px-Balkans850.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Balkans850.png/580px-Balkans850.png 2x" data-file-width="628" data-file-height="517" /></a><figcaption>Southeastern Europe in the late 9th century.</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to his hagiography by Theophylact of Ohrid, while Naum stayed in Pliska working on the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Preslav_Literary_School" title="Preslav Literary School">Pliska Literary School</a>, Clement was commissioned by Boris I to organise the teaching of theology to future clergymen in Old Church Slavonic in the southwestern part of the Bulgarian Empire, in the region then known as <a href="/wiki/Kutmichevitsa" title="Kutmichevitsa">Kutmichevitsa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> For a period of seven years (between 886 and 893) Clement taught some 3,500 disciples in the Slavonic language and the <a href="/wiki/Glagolitic_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Glagolitic alphabet">Glagolitic alphabet</a>. At that time, Clement translated <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> literature into Old Church Slavonic, and in this way, he and his co-workers laid the foundations of the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Bulgarian Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> In 893 he was ordained archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Drembica" class="mw-redirect" title="Drembica">Drembica</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Velika_(bishopric)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Velika (bishopric) (page does not exist)">Velika (bishopric)</a>. Upon his death in 916 he was buried in his monastery, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Panteleimon,_Ohrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Panteleimon, Ohrid">Saint Panteleimon</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a>. Soon after he was canonized as a saint by the Bulgarian Orthodox Church.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The development of Old Church Slavonic literacy had the effect of preventing the assimilation of the <a href="/wiki/South_Slavs" title="South Slavs">South Slavs</a> into neighbouring Byzantine culture, which promoted the formation of a distinct Bulgarian identity in the Empire.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> During the first quarter of the 10th century, the <a href="/wiki/Ethnonym" title="Ethnonym">ethnonym</a> “Bulgarians” was adopted by the Slavic tribes in most of Macedonia, while their names were abandoned.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> Clement's life's work played a significant role in this transformation.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG/175px-Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG" decoding="async" width="175" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG/263px-Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG/350px-Saint_Clement_and_Pantheleimon_Ohrid._Tomb_of_Clement.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2280" data-file-height="2955" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Saint Clement within the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saints_Clement_and_Panteleimon" title="Church of Saints Clement and Panteleimon">Church of Saints Clement and Panteleimon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Clement of Ohrid was one of the most prolific and important writers in <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Old Church Slavonic</a>. He is credited with the <i>Panonic Hagiography of Saint Cyril and Saint Methodius</i>. Clement also translated the <i>Flower Triode</i> containing church songs sung from Easter to Pentecost and is believed to be the author of the <i>Holy Service</i> and the <i>Life of St. Clement of Rome</i>, as well as of the oldest service dedicated to St. Cyril and St. Methodius. The invention of the Cyrillic alphabet is also usually ascribed to him although the alphabet is most likely to have been developed at the <a href="/wiki/Preslav_Literary_School" title="Preslav Literary School">Preslav Literary School</a> at the beginning of the 10th century (see <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic script</a>).
</p><p>Medieval frescoes of Clement exist throughout the modern-day territories of North Macedonia, Serbia and northern Greece, with the vast majority being located in North Macedonia.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Clement_of_Ohrid" title="Church of St. Clement of Ohrid">Church of St. Clement of Ohrid</a> is located in <a href="/wiki/Skopje" title="Skopje">Skopje</a> and is the largest cathedral of the <a href="/wiki/Macedonian_Orthodox_Church" title="Macedonian Orthodox Church">Macedonian Orthodox Church</a>.
</p><p>The first modern <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgarian</a> university, <a href="/wiki/Sofia_University" title="Sofia University">Sofia University</a>, was named after Clement upon its foundation in 1888. The Macedonian National and University Library, founded on November 23, 1944, also bears his name.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The University in <a href="/wiki/Bitola" title="Bitola">Bitola</a>, established in 1979, is named after Clement, as well as the Bulgarian scientific base, <a href="/wiki/St._Kliment_Ohridski_Base" title="St. Kliment Ohridski Base">St. Kliment Ohridski</a> on <a href="/wiki/Livingston_Island" title="Livingston Island">Livingston Island</a> in the <a href="/wiki/South_Shetland_Islands" title="South Shetland Islands">South Shetland Islands</a> of <a href="/wiki/Antarctica" title="Antarctica">Antarctica</a>.
</p><p>In November 2008, the <a href="/wiki/Macedonian_Orthodox_Church" title="Macedonian Orthodox Church">Macedonian Orthodox Church</a> donated part of Clement's relics to the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Bulgarian Orthodox Church</a> as a sign of good will.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In May 2018 was announced that in the ruins of a <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ravna_Monastery&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ravna Monastery (page does not exist)">Ravna Monastery</a>, near the village of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ravna,_Varna_Province&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ravna, Varna Province (page does not exist)">Ravna</a> in <a href="/wiki/Provadiya_Municipality" class="mw-redirect" title="Provadiya Municipality">Provadiya Municipality</a>, the signature of Clement was identified on a stone plate with a large amount of graffiti on it. The signature is dated as of April 24, 889. The finding gives reason to assume that the disciples of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Cyril and Methodius</a> were settled there at one stage, after being expelled from the Great Moravia and their reception in Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
