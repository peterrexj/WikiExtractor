<p><b>Nereus and Achilleus</b> are two Roman martyr saints. In the present <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">revised in 1969</a>, Nereus and Achilleus (together) are celebrated (optional memorials) on 12 May.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> had on 12 May a joint feast (semidouble rank) of Nereus, Achilleus and Pancras. The name of Domitilla was added in 1595.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The joint celebration of Nereus, Achilleus, Domitilla and Pancras continued with that <a href="/wiki/Ranking_of_liturgical_days_in_the_Roman_Rite" title="Ranking of liturgical days in the Roman Rite">ranking</a> (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1954" title="General Roman Calendar of 1954">General Roman Calendar of 1954</a>) until the <a href="/wiki/Code_of_Rubrics" title="Code of Rubrics">revision of 1960</a>, when it was reclassified as a third-class feast (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Nereus_and_Achilleus">Nereus and Achilleus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Nereus and Achilleus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The old Roman lists of the 5th century, which passed over into the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i>, contain the names of the two martyrs Nereus and Achilleus, whose grave was in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb_of_Domitilla" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb of Domitilla">Catacomb of Domitilla</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a>. The notice in the more complete version given by the Berne Codex reads: "<i>IIII id. Maii, Romae in coemeterio Praetextati natale Nerei et Achillei fratrum</i> (On 12 May at Rome in the cemetery of Praetextatus [an evident error for Domitilla?] the natal day of the brothers Nereus and Achilleus").<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the invocation of the Mass for 2 October in the "Sacramentarium Gelasianum", the names of Nereus and Achilleus alone are mentioned. In the fourth and following centuries a special votive Mass was celebrated on 12 May at the grave of Nereus and Achilleus on the Via Ardeatina.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Itineraries of the graves of the Roman martyrs, written in the 7th century, are unanimous in their indication of the resting-place of these saints (<a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_de_Rossi" title="Giovanni Battista de Rossi">Giovanni Battista de Rossi</a>, "Roma sotterranea", I, 180–83).
</p><p>The <i>basilica of Sts. Nereus and Achilleus</i> in the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a> (not to be confused with the church of the <a href="/wiki/Santi_Nereo_e_Achilleo" title="Santi Nereo e Achilleo">same name</a> near the <a href="/wiki/Baths_of_Caracalla" title="Baths of Caracalla">Baths of Caracalla</a> to which the relics of the saints were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> in the 6th century), was erected above the burial site of Nereus and Achilleus and is of the latter part of the 4th century; it is a three-naved basilica, which was abandoned in the mid 9th century<sup id="cite_ref-Guida817_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guida817-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and rediscovered in 1874<sup id="cite_ref-Guida817_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Guida817-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> by de Rossi in the Catacomb of Domitilla. Amongst the numerous objects found in the ruins were two pillars which had supported the ciborium ornamented with sculptures representing the death of the two saints by decapitation; one of these pillars is perfectly preserved, and the name of Achilleus is carved on it. There was also found a large fragment of a marble slab, with an inscription composed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus</a>, the text of which is well known from an ancient copy. This oldest historical mention of Nereus and Achilleus<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> tells how the two as soldiers were obedient to the tyrant, but suddenly being converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, joyfully resigned their commission and died the martyr's death; as to the date of their glorious confession we can make no inference. The acts of these martyrs, <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">legendary</a> even to a romantic degree, have no historical value for their life and death; they bring no fewer than thirteen different Roman martyrs into relation, amongst them even <a href="/wiki/Simon_Magus" title="Simon Magus">Simon Magus</a>, according to the <a href="/wiki/Apocrypha" title="Apocrypha">apocryphal</a> Petrine Acts, and place their death in the end of the first and beginning of the 2nd centuries. These Acts were written in Greek and Latin; according to Achelis (see below) the Greek was the original text, and written in Latin in the 6th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to these legends, Nereus and Achilleus were <a href="/wiki/Eunuch" title="Eunuch">eunuchs</a> and <a href="/wiki/Chamberlain_(office)" title="Chamberlain (office)">chamberlains</a> of <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Domitilla_(Catholic_saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavia Domitilla (Catholic saint)">Flavia Domitilla</a>, a niece of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Domitian" title="Domitian">Domitian</a>; with the Christian virgin, they had been banished to the island of <a href="/wiki/Ponza" title="Ponza">Ponza</a> (Pontia), and later on <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> in <a href="/wiki/Terracina" title="Terracina">Terracina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The graves of these two martyrs were on an estate of the Lady Domitilla near the Via Ardeatina, close to that of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petronilla" title="Saint Petronilla">Petronilla</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The author of this legend places the two saints quite differently from the poem of Pope Damasus: as Nereus and Achilleus were buried in a very ancient part of the Catacomb of Domitilla, built as far back as the beginning of the 2nd century, we may conclude that they are among the most ancient martyrs of the Roman Church, and stand in very near relation to the Flavian family, of which Domitilla, the foundress of the catacomb, was a member. In the <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Romans" title="Epistle to the Romans">Epistle to the Romans</a>, St. Paul mentions a Nereus with his sister, to whom he sends greetings (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Romans%2016:15&amp;version=nrsv">Romans 16:15</a>); perhaps even the martyr was a descendant of this disciple of the Apostle of the Gentiles<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>. Owing to the purely legendary character of these Acts, we cannot use them as an argument to aid in the controversy whether there were two Christians of the name of Domitilla in the family of the Christian Flavian, or only one: the wife of the Consul <a href="/wiki/Titus_Flavius_Clemens_(consul)" title="Titus Flavius Clemens (consul)">Titus Flavius Clemens</a>. As to other martyrs of the name Nereus, who are especially noted in the old martyrologies as martyrs of the faith in Africa, or as being natives of that country (e.g., in the Martyrologium Hieronymianum, 11 May, 15 or 16 October, 16 November) though there is one of the name in the present <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> under date of 16 October, nothing more is known.
</p><p>The relics of Nereus and Achilleus are housed since the 6th century together with the relics of Domitilla under the high altar of the church of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Nereo_e_Achilleo" title="Santi Nereo e Achilleo">Ss. Nereo e Achilleo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> had on 12 May a joint feast (semidouble rank) of Nereus, Achilleus and <a href="/wiki/Pancras_of_Rome" title="Pancras of Rome">Pancras</a>. The name of Domitilla was added in 1595.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The joint celebration of Nereus, Achilleus, Domitilla and Pancras continued with that ranking (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1954" title="General Roman Calendar of 1954">General Roman Calendar of 1954</a>) until reduced to that of simple in 1955 (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_Pope_Pius_XII#Rank_of_feasts" title="General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII">General Roman Calendar of Pope Pius XII</a>) and that of third-class feast in 1960 (see <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In the present <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">revised in 1969</a>, Saints <a href="/wiki/Nereus_and_Achilleus" class="mw-redirect" title="Nereus and Achilleus">Nereus and Achilleus</a> (together) and <a href="/wiki/Pancras_of_Rome" title="Pancras of Rome">Saint Pancras</a> have distinct celebrations (optional memorials) on 12 May. Saint Domitilla is not included in the revised calendar, because the liturgical honours once paid to her "have no basis in tradition".<sup id="cite_ref-CR_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The feast is celebrated on the same day by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
