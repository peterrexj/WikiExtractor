<p><b>Pauline Von Mallinckrodt</b> (3 June 1817 - 30 April 1881) was a <a href="/wiki/German_people" class="mw-redirect" title="German people">German</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> and the foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Christian_Charity" title="Sisters of Christian Charity">Sisters of Christian Charity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mallinckrodt was born into an aristocratic household as the daughter of a Lutheran father and Catholic mother and from her adolescence began to tend to the blind and sick. This venture expanded into what became her religious order which spread at a rapid pace; she herself travelled to a range of places to oversee the growth and development of her order.<sup id="cite_ref-IC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her beatification cause opened in 1958 and she later was beatified on 14 April 1985.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_Mallinckrodt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pauline von Mallinckrodt was born in Minden on 3 June 1817 as the eldest of four children to the politician Christian Detmar Karl von Mallinckrodt (04.12.1769-04.04.1842) and Marianna Bernhardina Katharina von Mallinckrodt (23.03-1787-17.08.1834). The distinguished parliamentarian <a href="/wiki/Hermann_von_Mallinckrodt" title="Hermann von Mallinckrodt">Hermann Josef Christian von Mallinckrodt</a> (05.02.1821-26.05.1874) was her little brother as was Georg Detmar Ignaz Franz Daniel Wilhem von Mallinckrodt (c.1819-21.03.1881) and sister Bertha von Mallinckrodt.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her father was Lutheran; her mother a devout Catholic; Pauline was baptized as "Maria Bernardine Sophia Pauline".<sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1826 her father was transferred to <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aix-la-Chapelle</a> where Mallinckrodt attended Saint Leonard's school. Her classmates included Clara Fey and <a href="/wiki/Mary_Frances_Schervier" title="Mary Frances Schervier">Mary Frances Schervier</a>; one of her teachers were <a href="/wiki/Luise_Hensel" title="Luise Hensel">Luise Hensel</a>. In the fall of 1832 she continued her studies at a French school in <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>. Following a tour through <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a> in 1833 with her parents she returned to Aix-la-Chapelle.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mallinckrodt received her <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(sacrament)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (sacrament)">Confirmation</a> in August 1835. Upon her return from Switzerland her parents wanted her to integrate into aristocratic circles and, despite finding social functions a distasteful affair, she did so in obedience to her parents. Her mother grew ill to the point where Pauline accompanied her to a health resort in Schwalbach.
</p><p>Upon her mother's death from <a href="/wiki/Typhus" title="Typhus">typhus</a> in 1834 Mallinckrodt, at seventeen, took over the management of her father's household and the education of her three little siblings. In 1840 her father retired from public service to his manor at Boeddekken near <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a> and it was at this stage she entertained notions of becoming a <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Vincentian Sister</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1835 her father paid for her to have a trip to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> and in 1836 she accompanied Bernard von Hartmann and his wife (her paternal uncle and his wife) on a trip across Belgium in places such as <a href="/wiki/Louvain,_Belgium" class="mw-redirect" title="Louvain, Belgium">Louvain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>; she also visited the site of the <a href="/wiki/Waterloo,_Belgium" title="Waterloo, Belgium">Waterloo</a> <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo" title="Battle of Waterloo">battle</a>. The poor of the village would ask her to tend the sick. In winter she and her father lived in Paderborn where there was a women's group that tended the sick in their homes and she soon joined this group. In 1840 the group opened a <a href="/wiki/Kindergarten" title="Kindergarten">kindergarten</a> to provide safekeeping and care for neglected children and placed it in her charge.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> From this developed an institution for blind children who were provided a home in a former <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> where she took up residence after the death of her father in 1842; she was at his bedside as he died.<sup id="cite_ref-IC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1842 she did the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">Spiritual Exercises</a> for the first time.
</p><p>In 1846 she went to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> to induce <a href="/wiki/Madeleine_Sophie_Barat" title="Madeleine Sophie Barat">Madeleine Sophie Barat</a> to place the Paderborn institution for the blind under the care of Barat's congregation. However, because the Prussian government would not permit a French-run religious order to operate in Prussia she found it prudent to establish her own order - the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Christian_Charity" title="Sisters of Christian Charity">Sisters of Christian Charity</a> - on 21 August 1849 and she was vested in the habit. Mallinckrodt was chosen as the order's first Mother Superior.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her profession was held on 4 November 1850. It spread at a rapid pace that in the time before the <a href="/wiki/Kulturkampf" title="Kulturkampf">Kulturkampf</a> (1871–78) - which saw a brief suspension of its growth - it ran 20 establishments and had 250 members in various parts of the nation. In 1867 she fell ill and was directed to refrain from work prompting the religious to keep her workload limited.<sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The order soon spread in the <a href="/wiki/United_States_of_America" class="mw-redirect" title="United States of America">United States of America</a> and first settled in <a href="/wiki/New_Orleans" title="New Orleans">New Orleans</a>; she arrived in the United States herself on 7 June 1873 to open their motherhouse and oversee further expansion. In 1874 she was at her brother Hermann's bedside as he died.
</p><p>For a long time she wanted to visit <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to see the pope and to visit the tombs of the Apostles; she left on 27 April 1876 and at the end of the month met <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> during a public audience; he stopped to speak with her for a minute. She also visited the tomb of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and attended a Mass in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> before leaving. En route back home she stopped off in <a href="/wiki/Loreto,_Marche" title="Loreto, Marche">Loreto</a> and also went to the Italian cities of <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>, <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> and <a href="/wiki/Trieste" title="Trieste">Trieste</a> while also stopping off in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1875, the government passed a law authorizing the government to take over administration of land and other properties that religious communities possessed. Therefore, she moved the motherhouse to <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>. In 1875 Bishop <a href="/wiki/Konrad_Martin" title="Konrad Martin">Konrad Martin</a> was incarcerated in the <a href="/wiki/Wesel" title="Wesel">Wesel</a> fortress because of the <a href="/wiki/Falk_Laws" title="Falk Laws">Falk Laws</a>. A few months later he succeeded in escaping and found refuge with the order at the new motherhouse in <a href="/wiki/Mont_St._Guibert" class="mw-redirect" title="Mont St. Guibert">Mont St. Guibert</a>. Upon his death in 1879 she accompanied his remains in secret across the border to Paderborn where the bishop was buried with full solemn honors. On 1 October 1879 she traveled to <a href="/wiki/Chile" title="Chile">Chile</a> and was suffering from poor health at the time which made the trip hard; she left Chile on 19 February 1880. From there she travelled via <a href="/wiki/Panama" title="Panama">Panama</a> to the United States to New York to visit new houses that had opened and arrived on 26 March 1880 - <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> - with Sister Christostoma before leaving on 21 August and arriving home on 3 September.<sup id="cite_ref-NA_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-IC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 25 April 1881 she caught a chill and a fever as her condition grew worse - her sisters confined her to bed but she preferred to rest on a sofa. The fever increased and she became restless in the night.<sup id="cite_ref-LM_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Mallinckrodt died from pneumonia on 30 April 1881 at 9:00am in Paderborn. In 1951 her order had 2300 religious and this expanded in 1960 to 2654 still operating across Europe and other continents.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Establishments">Establishments</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_Mallinckrodt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Establishments">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Her religious order owns and operates the "Haus Pauline von Mallinckrodt" <a href="/wiki/Retirement_home" title="Retirement home">retirement home</a> in Paderborn.
</p><p>The "Mallinckrodt Scholars Program" for undergraduates at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ann_Ida_Gannon,_BVM,_Center_for_Women_and_Leadership&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ann Ida Gannon, BVM, Center for Women and Leadership (page does not exist)">Ann Ida Gannon, BVM, Center for Women and Leadership</a> at  <a href="/wiki/Loyola_University_Chicago" title="Loyola University Chicago">Loyola University Chicago</a> is named in her honor and focuses on developing leadership reflecting Mallinckrodt's spirit of compassion and action in its students.
</p><p><br />
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pauline_Mallinckrodt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification cause started in an informative process in Paderborn that spanned from 1926 until 1933 while her writings received theological approval on two occasions on 6 December 1942 and a decade later on 23 December 1952; the formal introduction to the cause came on 29 May 1958 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> and she became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. An apostolic process was held from 1961 to 1963 and the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> validated the previous two processes on 6 March 1970. The C.C.S. officials and their consultants approved the cause on 2 December 1981 and the C.C.S. alone later approved it on 14 December 1982. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and named her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 13 January 1983.
</p><p>The miracle for beatification was investigated on a diocesan level and it received C.C.S. validation sometime later. Medical experts approved this miracle on 8 March 1994 and theologians likewise approved it on 26 June 1984 as did the C.C.S. on 6 November 1984. John Paul II approved this miracle on 14 December 1984 and beatified her several months later on 14 April 1985.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The second miracle for her to be sainted was investigated in <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit</a> from April to 30 May 2006 and was validated on 12 January 2007.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for this cause is Dr. Andrea Ambrosi.
</p>
