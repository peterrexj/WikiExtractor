<p><b>Saint Oda of Scotland</b> (c. 680 – c. 726) was a woman, supposedly of Scottish origin,<sup id="cite_ref-Realy_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Realy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  who became a holy recluse in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>. Her feast day is 23 October.<sup id="cite_ref-scotsman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scotsman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Oda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oda was born blind<sup id="cite_ref-Realy_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Realy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and her father sent her on <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a> to visit the relics of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lambert_(martyr)" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Lambert (martyr)">Saint Lambert</a>. While praying at his grave she was miraculously cured of her blindness, as recorded in the saint's 8th century <i><a href="/wiki/Vitae" class="mw-redirect" title="Vitae">vitae</a>.</i> Vowing to dedicate her life to God, she returned to Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-jongen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jongen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to records written in the 13th century, her father wanted to arrange a marriage for her. Because of her vow she and her maid fled across the <a href="/wiki/North_Sea" title="North Sea">North Sea</a>. After a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Monte_Sant%27Angelo_sul_Gargano" class="mw-redirect" title="Monte Sant&#39;Angelo sul Gargano">Monte Sant'Angelo sul Gargano</a>, Oda prayed in various villages in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a> and <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a> and finally settled in <a href="/wiki/Venray" title="Venray">Venray</a>, only to be repeatedly disturbed by <a href="/wiki/Magpie" title="Magpie">magpies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Seeking solitude, she fled from the magpies and the birds led her to an open space in the forest. There the villagers built a hut for her.<sup id="cite_ref-scotsman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-scotsman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In order to protect her humble shelter from the wind, hail, rain and snow and to hide it from the view of the world, Oda planted some bushes. The following day they had already grown into a thick hedge. Her father sought for her, and as in the story of St. <a href="/wiki/Dymphna" title="Dymphna">Dymphna</a>, her location was disclosed by her use of coins from her homeland. However, when he attempted to approach her hut, magpies repeatedly drove him off. Eventually he gave up and returned to Scotland without her.<sup id="cite_ref-jongen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jongen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She remained there as a hermitess.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Oda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Oda is usually depicted wearing a long blue gown with one shoulder bare and carying a staff or a book (symbolic of her cured blindness). She is always shown with a magpie on her hand and a crown under her feet (symbolic of her rejection of her earthly father's kingdom).
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg/220px-Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg/330px-Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg/440px-Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="326" data-file-height="217" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sint-Oedenrode_vlag.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Sint-Oedenrode flag</div></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Oda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Oda's death, her humble hut became a place of pilgrimage. "Saint Oda's place in the woods"  became the town of <a href="/wiki/Sint-Oedenrode" title="Sint-Oedenrode">Sint-Oedenrode</a>, Netherlands (in Dutch, a small manmade open space in the woods is called a <i>rode</i>). Like the flag of Scotland, the flag of Sint-Oedenrode is a white saltire on a blue field.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pieces of Saint Oda's skull and teeth are kept in the Saint Martin Church of Sint-Oedenrode. Various statues and paintings are kept in a chapel dedicated to Oda in the church's garden. <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">Pilgrims</a> visited Saint Oda for relief of sore eyes and other illnesses related to the head.
</p>
