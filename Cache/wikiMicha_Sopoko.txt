<p><b>Michael Sopoćko</b> (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Michał Sopoćko</i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="pl-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Polish" title="Help:IPA/Polish">[ˈmixaw sɔˈpɔt͡ɕkɔ]</a></span>; 1 November 1888 – 15 February 1975) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest and professor at <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_University" title="Vilnius University">Vilnius University</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOdell199884–85_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOdell199884–85-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is best known as the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual director</a> of <a href="/wiki/Faustina_Kowalska" title="Faustina Kowalska">Faustina Kowalska</a>. He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in 2008.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Sopo%C4%87ko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sopoćko was born to Polish parents in 1888 in Juszewszczyzna (also known as Nowosady) near <a href="/wiki/Valozhyn" title="Valozhyn">Valozhyn</a> within the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Belarus" title="Belarus">Belarus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Calloway_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calloway-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-VaticanBio_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanBio-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He entered <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Priest_Seminary" class="mw-redirect" title="Vilnius Priest Seminary">Vilnius Priest Seminary</a> in 1910 and was ordained in 1914. He was a priest in Vilnius (1914–1918) and then a chaplain in the army in Warsaw and Vilnius during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>. After obtaining his <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Theology" title="Doctor of Theology">doctorate in theology</a> in 1926, he became the spiritual director at the seminary in Vilnius and, in 1928, professor of <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_theology" title="Pastoral theology">pastoral theology</a> at <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Batory_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Stefan Batory University">Stefan Batory University</a>, in Vilnius.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Divine_Mercy">Divine Mercy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Sopo%C4%87ko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Divine Mercy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg/300px-Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="225" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg/450px-Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg/600px-Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="3000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Lomza_Holycross_fc03.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Paintings of Faustina and Sopoćko in <a href="/wiki/%C5%81om%C5%BCa" title="Łomża">Łomża</a></div></div></div>
<p>Sopoćko was very supportive of the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Mercy" title="Divine Mercy">Divine Mercy</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_devotion" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic devotion">devotion</a> of Faustina Kowalska and in her diary (Notebook V, item 1238) she stated: "This priest is a great soul, entirely filled with God." Since 1931 Kowalska had been trying (without success) to find someone to paint the <i><a href="/wiki/Divine_Mercy_image" title="Divine Mercy image">Divine Mercy image</a></i> until Sopoćko became her confessor in the middle of 1933. By January 1934, Sopoćko arranged for the artist <a href="/wiki/Eugeniusz_Kazimirowski" title="Eugeniusz Kazimirowski">Eugeniusz Kazimirowski</a> (who was also a professor at the university) to paint the image.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETorretto201084–107_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETorretto201084–107-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On Friday 26 April 1935 Sopoćko delivered the first sermon ever on the Divine Mercy – and Kowalska attended the sermon.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETorretto201016_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETorretto201016-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The first Mass during which the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Mercy_image" title="Divine Mercy image">Divine Mercy image</a> was displayed was on 28 April 1935, the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Mercy_Sunday" title="Divine Mercy Sunday">Divine Mercy Sunday</a> (the first after Easter). Sopoćko managed to obtain permission to place the painting within the <a href="/wiki/Gate_of_Dawn" title="Gate of Dawn">Gate of Dawn</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a> as he celebrated the Mass that Sunday.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETorretto201016_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETorretto201016-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOdell1998102–103_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOdell1998102–103-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the summer of 1936, Sopoćko wrote the first brochure on the Divine Mercy devotion and obtained the <a href="/wiki/Imprimatur" title="Imprimatur">imprimatur</a> of <a href="/wiki/Romuald_Ja%C5%82brzykowski" title="Romuald Jałbrzykowski">Archbishop Jałbrzykowski</a> for it. The brochure carried the Divine Mercy image on the cover.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOdell1998119_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOdell1998119-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Congregation_of_Sisters_of_Merciful_Jesus">Congregation of Sisters of Merciful Jesus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Sopo%C4%87ko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Congregation of Sisters of Merciful Jesus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1942 during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, Sopoćko and other professors and students had to go into hiding near Vilnius for about two years. However, he used the time to establish a new religious congregation, based on the Divine Mercy messages reported by Kowalska.<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanBio_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanBio-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After the war, Sopoćko wrote the constitution for the congregation and helped the formation of what is now the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Sisters_of_Merciful_Jesus" title="Congregation of the Sisters of Merciful Jesus">Congregation of the Sisters of Merciful Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-VaticanBio_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VaticanBio-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In an entry in <a href="/wiki/Diary:_Divine_Mercy_in_My_Soul" class="mw-redirect" title="Diary: Divine Mercy in My Soul">her diary</a> on 8 February 1935, (Notebook I, item 378), Kowalska had written that the Divine Mercy devotion would be suppressed for some time after her death but then be accepted again although Sopoćko would suffer for it.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Odell153_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odell153-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1959, the Vatican forbade the Divine Mercy devotion and censured Sopoćko.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 1965 <a href="/wiki/Karol_Wojty%C5%82a" class="mw-redirect" title="Karol Wojtyła">Karol Wojtyła</a>, then Archbishop of Kraków and later Pope John Paul II, opened a new investigation and submitted documents in 1968, which resulted in the reversal of the ban in 1978.<sup id="cite_ref-Odell153_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odell153-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">Second World War</a>, when the boundaries of Poland had changed so much, Sopoćko left Vilnius. Until 1962, he had been a professor of pastoral theology at the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok" title="Białystok">Białystok</a>. Meanwhile, he wrote a book <i>Miłosierdzie Boga w dziełach Jego</i> (<i>Mercy of God in His works</i>) in four volumes. He died on 15 February 1975 in <a href="/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok" title="Białystok">Białystok</a>, <a href="/wiki/People%27s_Republic_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="People&#39;s Republic of Poland">Poland</a>, and was buried there. In 1988, his remains were transferred to the Church of Divine Mercy in <a href="/wiki/Osiedle_Bia%C5%82ostoczek,_Bia%C5%82ystok" class="mw-redirect" title="Osiedle Białostoczek, Białystok">Białostoczek</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quote">Quote</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Sopo%C4%87ko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Quote">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The decisive factor in obtaining God’s Mercy is trust. Trust is the expectation of someone’s help. It does not constitute a separate virtue, but is an essential condition of the virtue of hope, and an integral part of the virtues of fortitude and generosity. Because trust springs from faith, it strengthens hope and love, and is, moreover, linked up, in one way or another, with the moral virtues. It may, therefore, be called the basis on which the theological virtues unite with the moral. The moral virtues, originally natural, become supernatural if we practice them with trust in God’s help.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Micha%C5%82_Sopo%C4%87ko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sopoćko's case for <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> was started at the Vatican in 1987.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOdell199884–85_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOdell199884–85-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 2004, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> issued a decree on Sopoćko's virtues.<sup id="cite_ref-Calloway_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Calloway-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In December 2007, Pope <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XVI">Benedict XVI</a> approved of a miracle through his intercession. His solemn beatification took place on 28 September 2008 at the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Mercy_Sanctuary_(Bia%C5%82ystok)" title="Divine Mercy Sanctuary (Białystok)">Sanctuary of Divine Mercy, in Białystok</a>. An estimated 80,000 people attended, including the Polish president, <a href="/wiki/Lech_Kaczy%C5%84ski" title="Lech Kaczyński">Lech Kaczyński</a>, and the speaker of the <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Poland" title="Parliament of Poland">Parliament of Poland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bronislaw_Komorowski" class="mw-redirect" title="Bronislaw Komorowski">Bronislaw Komorowski</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
