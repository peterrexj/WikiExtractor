<p><b>Simon the Zealot</b> (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Acts%201:13&amp;version=nrsv">Acts 1:13</a>, <span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+6%3A15&amp;version=NRSV">Luke 6:15</a></span>) or <b>Simon the Canaanite</b> or <b>Simon the Canaanean</b> (<span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+10%3A4&amp;version=NRSV">Matthew 10:4</a></span>, <span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+3%3A18&amp;version=NRSV">Mark 3:18</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Σίμων ὁ Κανανίτης</span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop">ⲥⲓⲙⲱⲛ ⲡⲓ-ⲕⲁⲛⲁⲛⲉⲟⲥ</span>; <a href="/wiki/Classical_Syriac_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Syriac language">Classical Syriac</a>: <span lang="syc" dir="rtl">ܫܡܥܘܢ ܩܢܢܝܐ</span>)<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> was one of the most obscure among the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">apostles of Jesus</a>. <h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Identity">Identity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_the_Zealot&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Identity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:106px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG/104px-SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG" decoding="async" width="104" height="318" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG/156px-SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG/208px-SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG 2x" data-file-width="358" data-file-height="1091" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SimonTheZealotWithSaw.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Simon the Zealot with his <a href="/wiki/Saint_symbology" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint symbology">attribute</a> of a saw</div></div></div>
<p>The name Simon occurs in all of the <a href="/wiki/Synoptic_Gospels" title="Synoptic Gospels">Synoptic Gospels</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Acts" class="mw-redirect" title="Book of Acts">Book of Acts</a> each time there is a list of apostles, without further details:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Simon, (whom he also named Peter,) and Andrew his brother, James and John, Philip and Bartholomew, Matthew and Thomas, James the son of Alphaeus, and Simon called Zelotes, And Judas the brother of James, and Judas Iscariot, which also was the traitor.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite><a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Luke#6:14" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Luke">Luke 6:14–16</a></cite></div></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Zealot">The Zealot</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_the_Zealot&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Zealot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>To distinguish him from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Simon Peter</a> he is called <i>Kananaios</i> or <i>Kananites</i>, depending on the manuscript (<span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+10%3A4&amp;version=NRSV">Matthew 10:4</a></span> <span class="nourlexpansion nowrap"><a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+3%3A18&amp;version=NRSV">Mark 3:18</a></span>), and in the list of apostles in Luke 6:15, repeated in Acts 1:13, <i>Zelotes,</i> the "<a href="/wiki/Zealots_(Judea)" class="mw-redirect" title="Zealots (Judea)">Zealot</a>". Both <i>Kananaios</i> and <i>Kananites</i> derive from the <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a> word <a href="/wiki/Kanai_(Judaism)" title="Kanai (Judaism)">קנאי</a> <i>qanai</i>, meaning <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/zealous" class="extiw" title="wikt:zealous">zealous</a></i>, although <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> and others mistook the word to signify the apostle was from the town of קנה <a href="/wiki/Cana" title="Cana">Cana</a>, in which case his <a href="/wiki/Epithet" title="Epithet">epithet</a> would have been "Kanaios", or even from the region of כנען <a href="/wiki/Canaan" title="Canaan">Canaan</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> As such, the translation of the word as "the Cananite" or "the Canaanite" is without contemporary extra-canonic parallel.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Robert_Eisenman" title="Robert Eisenman">Robert Eisenman</a> has argued that contemporary <a href="/wiki/Talmud" title="Talmud">talmudic</a> references to Zealots refer to them as <i>kanna'im</i> "but not really as a group—rather as avenging priests in the Temple".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eisenman's broader conclusions, that the zealot element in the original apostle group was disguised and overwritten to make it support the assimilative <a href="/wiki/Pauline_Christianity" title="Pauline Christianity">Pauline Christianity</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Gentile" title="Gentile">Gentiles</a>, are more controversial. <a href="/wiki/John_P._Meier" title="John P. Meier">John P. Meier</a> argues that the term "Zealot" is a mistranslation and in the context of the Gospels means "zealous" or "religious" (in this case, for keeping the Law of Moses), as the Zealot movement apparently did not exist until 30 to 40 years after the events of the Gospels.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> However, neither <a href="/wiki/S._G._F._Brandon" title="S. G. F. Brandon">Brandon</a><sup id="cite_ref-Brandon_1967_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brandon_1967-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> nor <a href="/wiki/Martin_Hengel" title="Martin Hengel">Hengel</a><sup id="cite_ref-Hengel_1989_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hengel_1989-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> support this view.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_identifications">Other identifications</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_the_Zealot&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Other identifications">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the gospels Simon the Zealot is not identified with <a href="/wiki/Simon,_brother_of_Jesus" title="Simon, brother of Jesus">Simon the brother of Jesus</a> mentioned in <a href="/wiki/Mark_6" title="Mark 6">Mark 6</a>:3:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary and brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon, and are not his sisters here with us?" And they took offense at him.</p></blockquote>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> suggests that Simon the Zealot may be the same person as <a href="/wiki/Simeon_of_Jerusalem" title="Simeon of Jerusalem">Simeon of Jerusalem</a> or <a href="/wiki/Simon_(brother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Simon (brother of Jesus)">Simon the brother of Jesus</a> or both. He would then be the cousin of Jesus or a son of Joseph from a previous marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Another tradition holds that this is the Simeon of Jerusalem who became the second bishop of Jerusalem, although he was born in Galilee.<sup id="cite_ref-Simon_New_Advent_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Simon_New_Advent-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_tradition">Later tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_the_Zealot&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:201px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena,_Italy.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena%2C_Italy.jpg/199px-Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena%2C_Italy.jpg" decoding="async" width="199" height="168" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena%2C_Italy.jpg/299px-Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena%2C_Italy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena%2C_Italy.jpg/398px-Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena%2C_Italy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1179" data-file-height="997" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Simon_the_Apostle._Detail_of_the_mosaic_in_the_Basilica_of_San_Vitale._Ravena,_Italy.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Simon the Apostle, detail of the mosaic in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Vitale" title="Basilica of San Vitale">Basilica of San Vitale</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, 6th century</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:201px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Simon_(Caravaggio).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Simon_%28Caravaggio%29.jpg/199px-Simon_%28Caravaggio%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="199" height="255" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Simon_%28Caravaggio%29.jpg/299px-Simon_%28Caravaggio%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Simon_%28Caravaggio%29.jpg/398px-Simon_%28Caravaggio%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="508" data-file-height="650" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Simon_(Caravaggio).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Simon the Zealot by <a href="/wiki/Caravaggio" title="Caravaggio">Caravaggio</a></div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Isidore_of_Seville" title="Isidore of Seville">Isidore of Seville</a> drew together the accumulated anecdotes of Simon in <i>De Vita et Morte</i>.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/The_Golden_Legend" class="mw-redirect" title="The Golden Legend">the Golden Legend</a>, which is a collection of <a href="/wiki/Hagiographies" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographies">hagiographies</a>, compiled by <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a> in the thirteenth century "Simon the Cananaean and <a href="/wiki/Judas_Thaddeus" class="mw-redirect" title="Judas Thaddeus">Judas Thaddeus</a> were brethren of <a href="/wiki/James_the_Less" title="James the Less">James the Less</a> and sons of <a href="/wiki/Mary_Cleophas" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary Cleophas">Mary Cleophas</a>, which was married to Alpheus."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Saint_Simon_the_Apostle2_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint_Simon_the_Apostle2-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the apocryphal <a href="/wiki/Arabic_Infancy_Gospel" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic Infancy Gospel">Arabic Infancy Gospel</a> a fact related to this apostle is mentioned. A boy named Simon is bitten by a snake in his hand; he is healed by Jesus, who told the child "you shall be my disciple". The mention ends with the phrase "this is Simon the Cananite, of whom mention is made in the Gospel."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In later tradition, Simon is often associated with <a href="/wiki/Jude_the_Apostle" title="Jude the Apostle">Jude the Apostle</a> as an evangelizing team; in Western Christianity, they share their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 28 October. The most widespread tradition is that after evangelizing in <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, Simon joined Jude in <a href="/wiki/Parthian_Empire" title="Parthian Empire">Persia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_(antiquity)" title="Kingdom of Armenia (antiquity)">Armenia</a> or <a href="/wiki/Beirut" title="Beirut">Beirut</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>, where both were martyred in 65 AD. This version is the one found in the <a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a>. He may have suffered crucifixion as the Bishop of Jerusalem. According to an Eastern tradition Simon travelled to Georgia on a missionary trip, died in Abkhazia and was buried in Nicopsia. His remains were later transferred to <a href="/wiki/New_Athos" title="New Athos">Anacopia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another tradition states that he traveled in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_East" title="Middle East">Middle East</a> and <a href="/wiki/Africa" title="Africa">Africa</a>.  Christian <a href="/wiki/People_of_Ethiopia" class="mw-redirect" title="People of Ethiopia">Ethiopians</a> claim that he was crucified in <a href="/wiki/Samaria" title="Samaria">Samaria</a>, while <a href="/wiki/Justus_Lipsius" title="Justus Lipsius">Justus Lipsius</a> writes that he was <a href="/wiki/Death_by_sawing" title="Death by sawing">sawn in half</a> at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Suanir&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Suanir (page does not exist)">Suanir</a>, <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_Simon_the_Apostle2_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint_Simon_the_Apostle2-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> However, <a href="/wiki/Moses_of_Chorene" class="mw-redirect" title="Moses of Chorene">Moses of Chorene</a> writes that he was martyred at Weriosphora in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iberia_(antiquity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Iberia (antiquity)">Caucasian Iberia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_Simon_the_Apostle2_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint_Simon_the_Apostle2-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Tradition also claims he died peacefully at <a href="/wiki/Edessa,_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Edessa, Mesopotamia">Edessa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Yet another tradition says he visited <a href="/wiki/Roman_Britain" title="Roman Britain">Roman Britain</a>. In this account, in his second mission to Britain, he arrived during the first year of <a href="/wiki/Boudica" title="Boudica">Boadicea</a>'s rebellion (60 AD). He was crucified 10 May 61 AD by the Roman Catus Decianus, at <a href="/wiki/Caistor" title="Caistor">Caistor</a>, modern-day <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>, Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-jowett_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jowett-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hippolytus_of_Rome" title="Hippolytus of Rome">Hippolytus of Rome</a>, Simon's first arrival in Britain was in the year A.D.44, during the <a href="/wiki/Roman_conquest_of_Britain" title="Roman conquest of Britain">Roman conquest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-jowett_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jowett-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_I_of_Constantinople" title="Nikephoros I of Constantinople">Nikephoros I of Constantinople</a> writes:<sup id="cite_ref-jowett_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jowett-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Simon born in Cana of Galilee who for his fervent affection for his Master and great zeal that he showed by all means to the Gospel, was surnamed Zelotes, having received the Holy Ghost from above, travelled through Egypt, and Africa, then through Mauretania and all Libya, preaching the Gospel. And the same doctrine he taught to the Occidental Sea, and the Isles called Britanniae.</p></blockquote>
<p>Another tradition, doubtless inspired by his title "the <a href="/wiki/Zealot" class="mw-redirect" title="Zealot">Zealot</a>", states that he was involved in the <a href="/wiki/First_Jewish%E2%80%93Roman_War" title="First Jewish–Roman War">First Jewish–Roman War</a> (66–73 AD).<sup id="cite_ref-Brandon_1967_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brandon_1967-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Hengel_1989_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hengel_1989-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The second century <i><a href="/wiki/Epistle_of_the_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Epistle of the Apostles">Epistle of the Apostles</a></i> (<i>Epistula Apostolorum</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> a polemic against <a href="/wiki/Gnosticism" title="Gnosticism">gnostics</a>, lists him among the apostles purported to be writing the letter (who include Thomas) as Judas Zelotes. Certain <a href="/wiki/Vetus_Latina" title="Vetus Latina">Old Latin translations</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew" title="Gospel of Matthew">Gospel of Matthew</a> substitute "Judas the Zealot" for <a href="/wiki/Saint_Jude" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Jude">Thaddeus/Lebbaeus</a> in Matthew 10:3. To some readers, this suggests that he may be identical with the "Judas not Iscariot" mentioned in <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=john%2014:22&amp;version=KJV">John 14:22</a>: "Judas saith unto him, not Iscariot, Our Lord, how is it that thou wilt manifest thyself unto us, and not unto the world?" As it has been suggested that Jude is identical with the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_(Apostle)" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas (Apostle)">Apostle Thomas</a> (see <a href="/wiki/Jude_Thomas" class="mw-redirect" title="Jude Thomas">Jude Thomas</a>), an identification of "Simon Zelotes" with Thomas is also possible. <a href="/wiki/Barbara_Thiering" title="Barbara Thiering">Barbara Thiering</a> identified Simon Zelotes with <a href="/wiki/Simon_Magus" title="Simon Magus">Simon Magus</a>; however, this view has received no serious acceptance. The <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a> records nothing more of Simon, aside from this multitude of possible but unlikely pseudonyms. He is buried in the same tomb as <a href="/wiki/Jude_the_Apostle" title="Jude the Apostle">Jude Thaddeus</a>, in the left transept of the St. Peter's Basilica in Rome, under the altar of St. Joseph.
</p><p>In Islam, Muslim exegesis and Quran commentary name the twelve apostles and include Simon amongst the disciples. <a href="/wiki/Hadith" title="Hadith">Muslim tradition</a> says that Simon was sent to preach the faith of <a href="/wiki/God_in_Islam" title="God in Islam">God</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Berber_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Berber people">Berbers</a>, outside <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-WheelerNoegel_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WheelerNoegel-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In art, Simon has the identifying attribute of a <a href="/wiki/Saw" title="Saw">saw</a> because he was traditionally martyred by <a href="/wiki/Death_by_sawing" title="Death by sawing">being sawn in half</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Simon_New_Advent_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Simon_New_Advent-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simon_the_Zealot&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Simon, like the other Apostles, is regarded as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Churches">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox Churches</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a>, the churches of the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran Church">Lutheran Church</a>. Simon is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> (with <a href="/wiki/Jude_the_Apostle" title="Jude the Apostle">Jude</a>) in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Festival (Anglicanism)">Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/October_28" title="October 28">28 October</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
