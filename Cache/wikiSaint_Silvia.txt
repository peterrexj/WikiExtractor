<p><b>Silvia</b>, or <b>Sylvia</b>, (c. 515 – c. 592) was the mother of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>. She is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> which names her a patroness of pregnant women.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Silvia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little biographical information about her exists.  Her native place is sometimes given as <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, sometimes as <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. Apparently she was of a distinguished family as her husband, the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> <i>regionarius</i>, Gordianus. She had, besides Gregory, a second son,<sup id="cite_ref-Löffler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Löffler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> whose name did not survive through the ages.
</p><p>Silvia was noted for her great <a href="/wiki/Piety" title="Piety">piety</a>, and she gave her sons an excellent education. After the death of her husband, around 573, she devoted herself entirely to religion in the "new cell by the gate of blessed Paul" (<i>cella nova juxta portam beati Pauli</i>). Gregory the Great had a <a href="/wiki/Mosaic" title="Mosaic">mosaic</a> <a href="/wiki/Portrait" title="Portrait">portrait</a> of his parents executed at the monastery of Saint Andrew; it is minutely described by <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Hymonides" title="Johannes Hymonides">Johannes Diaconus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Löffler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Löffler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Silvia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG/220px-Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG/330px-Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG/440px-Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1704" data-file-height="2272" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Celio_-_san_Gregorio_-_oratorio_s_Silvia_1740.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Oratorio S Silvia, San Gregorio Magno al Celio</div></div></div>
<p>The veneration of Silvia is of early date.<sup id="cite_ref-Löffler_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Löffler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was honoured by the Romans as a type of a Christian widow. Silvia had built a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> in her house. In 645, the monks from the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Mar_Saba" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Mar Saba">monastery of Mar Saba</a> (<a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>) settled in this house, and devoted it to the celebration of <a href="/wiki/Sabbas_the_Sanctified" title="Sabbas the Sanctified">Saint Sabas</a>. 
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> (1592–1605) inserted her name under 3 November in the Roman Martyrology. She is invoked by pregnant women for a safe delivery.<sup id="cite_ref-Löffler_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Löffler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two of her relatives, sisters-in-law <a href="/wiki/Trasilla_and_Emiliana" title="Trasilla and Emiliana">Trasilla and Emiliana</a>, are also venerated as saints, as well as her other sister-in-law Gordiana, and her husband Gordianus.
