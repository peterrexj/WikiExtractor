<p>Saint <b>Cynllo</b> (<small>Welsh pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="cy-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Welsh" title="Help:IPA/Welsh">[ˈkənɬɔ]</a></span>) is a <a href="/wiki/Britons_(Celtic_people)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (Celtic people)">British</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, who lived in the late 5th and early 6th centuries, generally described as a brother of Saint Teilo. Cynllo was known for "...the sanctity of his life and the austerity of his manners."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cynllo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cynllo is variously described in the genealogical <i><a href="/wiki/Bonedd_y_Saint" title="Bonedd y Saint">Bonedd y Saint</a></i> as the son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Issel" title="Saint Issel">Usyllt</a> and brother of <a href="/wiki/Teilo" class="mw-redirect" title="Teilo">Teilo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Later genealogies have him a grandson of <a href="/wiki/Coel_Hen" title="Coel Hen">Coel Hen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wade-Evans" title="Arthur Wade-Evans">Wade-Evans</a> thought he should be identified with <i>Kentinlau</i> who accompanied <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cadfan" title="Saint Cadfan">Saint Cadfan</a> from <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> to <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a>. References to him as <i>Cynllo Vrenin</i> (Cynllo the King) suggest that he was in possession of his ancestral dominions before devoting himself to religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cynllo's knee imprints, made as he said his devotions, are said to exist in a rock, near the farm Felin Gynllo, which lies just outside <a href="/wiki/Llangoedmor" title="Llangoedmor">Llangoedmor</a> in Ceredigion.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Middle_Welsh" title="Middle Welsh">Middle Welsh</a> poem, <i>The Consolations of Elffin</i>, attributed to the infant <a href="/wiki/Taliesin" title="Taliesin">Taliesin</a> includes the line, <i>Ni bydd coeg gweddi Cynllo</i>, "The prayer of Cynllo will not be in vain."
</p><p>Several churches are dedicated in his honour, but there is no reliable account of him.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Churches and chapels were dedicated to him over almost the whole of <a href="/wiki/Gwerthrynion" class="mw-redirect" title="Gwerthrynion">Gwerthrynion</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maelienydd" title="Maelienydd">Maelienydd</a>. 
</p><p>There is a church in <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a> commemorating Saint Cynllo at Llangynllo (said to stand on the site of his monastic cell)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Nantcwnlle" title="Nantcwnlle">Nantcwnlle</a>, and also the church of St Cynllo in the <a href="/wiki/Community_(Wales)" title="Community (Wales)">community</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nantmel" title="Nantmel">Nantmel</a> in the historic county of <a href="/wiki/Radnorshire" title="Radnorshire">Radnorshire</a>, now part of <a href="/wiki/Powys" title="Powys">Powys</a>, and at <a href="/wiki/Llangynllo" class="mw-redirect" title="Llangynllo">Llangynllo</a> west of <a href="/wiki/Knighton,_Powys" title="Knighton, Powys">Knighton</a>. <sup id="cite_ref-dwb_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dwb-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The latter was almost totally rebuilt in the late 18th century. Cynllo was so popular that even when the Normans changed the dedication of the church in Rhayader to Saint Clements, locals still held Cynllo’s feastday there.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Near St Cynllo's Church, <a href="/wiki/Llanbister" title="Llanbister">Llanbister</a> was a spring called <i>Pistyll Cynllo</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabine_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Williams_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cynllo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By the fifteenth century St. Cynllo's feast day had been set as 17 July,<sup id="cite_ref-church_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-church-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-dwb_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dwb-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> although <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Baring-Gould</a>, quoting the sixteenth century <i>Haford</i> MS, shows August 8.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>Cynllo does not appear on the Roman Catholic National Calendar for Wales, although every parish is encouraged to celebrate its patronal feast. The calendar indicates that "Where no other indication is given the celebration is an optional memorial."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>A new calendar for the Church in Wales was produced in 2003; Cynllo does not appear in that either although both the Catholic and Anglican calendars have a general commemoration in November for Welsh saints. Those parishes which continue to commemorate Cynllo appear to conform to the 17 July.
</p>
