<p><b>Victricius</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Victrice</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Vittricio</i>) also known as <b>Victricius of Rouen</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 330 – c. 407 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rouen">bishop of Rouen</a> (393–407), <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a>, and author.  His feast day is August 7.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victricius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Victricius was Gallic by birth, the son of a Roman <a href="/wiki/Roman_legion" title="Roman legion">legionnaire</a>. He also became a soldier and was posted to various locations around Gaul.<sup id="cite_ref-Villazala_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Villazala-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, when he became a <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a>, he refused to remain in the army. He was <a href="/wiki/Flogging" class="mw-redirect" title="Flogging">flogged</a> and sentenced to death, but managed to avoid  <a href="/wiki/Execution" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution">execution</a>. He proselytized amongst the tribes of <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_of_Hainaut" title="County of Hainaut">Hainault</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Brabant" title="Province of Brabant">Brabant</a>. He became bishop of Rouen around 386 or 393.  
</p><p>He was accused of <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresy</a> but was defended by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent I</a> and received from Innocent the important <a href="/wiki/Decretal" title="Decretal">decretal</a> of the <i>Liber Regularum</i>. 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="De_Laude_Sanctorum"><i>De Laude Sanctorum</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victricius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: De Laude Sanctorum">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 396, <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a> of Milan sent Victricius (as well as <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_Nola" title="Paulinus of Nola">Paulinus of Nola</a> and others) some relics of <a href="/wiki/Vitalis_and_Agricola" class="mw-redirect" title="Vitalis and Agricola">Vitalis and Agricola</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Victricius wrote a sermon, <i>De Laude Sanctorum</i> (<i>On the Praise of the Saints</i>), celebrating the arrival of the relics from Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He mentions that he had been away from Rouen (396), as he had been requested to travel to Britain to help resolve some doctrinal issues. Victricius describes Britain as a wild and hostile place dealing with paganism and heresy in contrast with Italy with its abundance of holy sites and relics of martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-Villazala_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Villazala-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
