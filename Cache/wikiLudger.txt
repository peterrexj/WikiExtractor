<p><b>Ludger</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ludgerus</i>; also <b>Lüdiger</b> or <b>Liudger</b>) (born at <a href="/wiki/Zuilen" title="Zuilen">Zuilen</a> near <a href="/wiki/Utrecht_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Utrecht (city)">Utrecht</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 742; died 26 March 809 at <a href="/wiki/Billerbeck" title="Billerbeck">Billerbeck</a>) was a <a href="/wiki/Missionary#Catholic_missions" title="Missionary">missionary</a> among the <a href="/wiki/Frisians" title="Frisians">Frisians</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saxons" title="Saxons">Saxons</a>, founder of <a href="/wiki/Werden_Abbey" title="Werden Abbey">Werden Abbey</a> and the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_M%C3%BCnster" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Münster">Bishop of Münster</a> in <a href="/wiki/Westphalia" title="Westphalia">Westphalia</a>. He has been called the "Apostle of Saxony".<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_to_ordination">Early life to ordination</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life to ordination">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ludger's parents, Thiadgrim and Liafburg, were wealthy Christian Frisians of noble descent. In 753 Ludger saw the great Apostle of <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface</a>, which, together with the subsequent martyrdom of the saint, made a deep impression on him. At his own request he was sent to the Utrecht Cathedral School (<i>Martinsstift</i>), founded by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Utrecht" title="Gregory of Utrecht">Gregory of Utrecht</a> in 756 or 757, and made good progress. In 767 Gregory, who did not wish to receive episcopal consecration himself, sent Alubert, who had come from England to assist him in his missionary work, to <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> to be consecrated bishop. Ludger accompanied him to be ordained into the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> (as he duly was, by <a href="/wiki/Ethelbert_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Ethelbert of York">Ethelbert of York</a>) and to study under <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a>, but after a year he returned to Utrecht. Some time later he was granted an opportunity to continue his studies at York, when he developed a friendship with Alcuin which lasted throughout life.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 772 friction arose between the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a> and the Frisians, and Ludger - for the sake of his personal safety - left for home, taking with him a number of valuable books. He remained in the <i>Martinsstift</i> until the death of Gregory in 775, in honour of whose memory he wrote the biography <i>Vita Gregorii</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was then sent to <a href="/wiki/Deventer" title="Deventer">Deventer</a> to restore the chapel destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a> Saxons and to find the <a href="/wiki/Relic#Christian_relics" title="Relic">relics</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lebuinus" title="Lebuinus">Lebuinus</a>, who had worked there as missionary, built the chapel, and died there in c. 775. Ludger succeeded, and returned to teach in the <i>Martinsstift</i>, after which he and others were sent north to destroy pagan places of worship west of the <a href="/wiki/Lauwers_Zee" class="mw-redirect" title="Lauwers Zee">Lauwers Zee</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Netherlands">The Netherlands</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Netherlands">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Ludger had been ordained at <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> on 7&#160;July 777, the missions of <i><a href="/wiki/Oostergo" class="mw-redirect" title="Oostergo">Ostergau</a></i> (or <i>Ostracha</i>, i.e., <a href="/wiki/East_Frisia" title="East Frisia">East Frisia</a>) were committed to his charge, of which missions <a href="/wiki/Dokkum" title="Dokkum">Dokkum</a>, the place of the martyrdom of Boniface, was made the centre. Every autumn however he came back to Utrecht to teach at the cathedral school. He worked in this way for about seven years, until <a href="/wiki/Widukind" title="Widukind">Widukind</a> in 784 persuaded the Frisians to drive out the missionaries, burn the churches, and return to the pagan gods.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg/220px-Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="192" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg/330px-Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg/440px-Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg 2x" data-file-width="879" data-file-height="768" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Liudger_bernlef_lochem.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ludger heals Frisian singer Bernlef from his blindness (statue at <a href="/wiki/Lochem" title="Lochem">Lochem</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gelderland" title="Gelderland">Gelderland</a>, The Netherlands).</div></div></div>
<p>Ludger escaped with his disciples, and in 785 visited <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he was well received by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_I" title="Pope Adrian I">Pope Adrian I</a>, who gave him much advice and special faculties. From Rome he went to Monte Cassino, where he lived according to the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Rule of Saint Benedict</a>, but did not bind himself by vows.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The news of Widukind's submission, and the arrival of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> at <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a> in 787, put an end to Ludger's peaceful retirement. He was appointed missionary to five districts east of the river <a href="/wiki/Lauwers" title="Lauwers">Lauwers</a>, around the estuaries of the Hunze, the <a href="/wiki/Fivel" title="Fivel">Fivel</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Ems_(river)" title="Ems (river)">Ems</a>, which were still occupied almost entirely by pagans. He began his work armed with characteristic energy and faith in God, and had a significant advantage in that he knew the language and habits of the people, and put this knowledge to good use in achieving their conversion.
</p><p>He worked in many places: the island of Bant, long since sunk beneath the sea, is mentioned as the scene of his apostolic work. He visited <a href="/wiki/Heligoland" title="Heligoland">Heligoland</a> (<i>Fossitesland</i>), where <a href="/wiki/Willibrord" title="Willibrord">Willibrord</a> had preached, where he destroyed the remains of paganism and built a Christian church. The well, formerly sacred to the pagan gods, he re-dedicated as his baptismal font. On his return he met the blind bard Bernlef, last of the Frisian <a href="/wiki/Skald" title="Skald">skalds</a>, cured his blindness, and made him a devout Christian.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Westphalia_and_Saxony">Westphalia and Saxony</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Westphalia and Saxony">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 793 Charlemagne wished to make Ludger <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Trier">Bishop of Trier</a>, but he declined, while declaring himself willing to undertake the evangelization of the Saxons. Charlemagne accepted the offer, and north-western Saxony was thus added to Ludger's missionary field. The monastery of <a href="/wiki/St._Ludger%27s_Abbey" title="St. Ludger&#39;s Abbey">St. Ludger's Abbey</a> at <a href="/wiki/Helmstedt" title="Helmstedt">Helmstedt</a> was founded as part of his missionary activity in this part of Germany. To meet necessary expenses the income of the Abbey of <a href="/wiki/Leuze-en-Hainaut" title="Leuze-en-Hainaut">Leuze</a>, in the present <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgian</a> province of <a href="/wiki/Hainaut_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Hainaut (province)">Hainaut</a>, was given him, and he was told to pick his fellow-workers from the members of that abbey.
</p><p>As <i>Mimigernaford</i> (also <i>Mimigardeford</i> or <i>Miningarvard</i>) had been designated the centre of the new district, Ludger built a monastery for canons regular there, from which the place took the name of <a href="/wiki/M%C3%BCnster" title="Münster">Münster</a>. Here he lived with his monks according to the rule of <a href="/wiki/Chrodegang" title="Chrodegang">Saint Chrodegang</a> of <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which in 789 had been made obligatory in the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> territories. He also built a chapel on the left bank of the <a href="/wiki/M%C3%BCnstersche_Aa" title="Münstersche Aa">Aa</a> in honour of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin">Blessed Virgin</a>, as well as the churches of Billerbeck, <a href="/wiki/Coesfeld" title="Coesfeld">Coesfeld</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld" class="mw-redirect" title="Hersfeld">Hersfeld</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nottuln" title="Nottuln">Nottuln</a> and others. Near the church of Nottuln he built a home for his sister, Gerburgis, who had consecrated herself to God. Many other women soon joined her, and so originated the first <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> in Westphalia (c. 803). Better known among his foundations, however, is the abbey at <a href="/wiki/Werden_Abbey" title="Werden Abbey">Werden</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> founded (after an abortive attempt to establish a religious house at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Wichmond&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Wichmond (page does not exist)">Wichmond</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Erft" title="Erft">Erft</a>) in c. 800 and consecrated in 804, on ground which Ludger himself had acquired, in fulfilment of his desire, formed since his stay at Monte Cassino, to found a Benedictine house.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alcuin recommended him to Charlemagne, who had <a href="/wiki/Hildebold,_Archbishop_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Hildebold, Archbishop of Cologne">Hildebold, Archbishop of Cologne</a>, consecrate Ludger as bishop of Munster on 30 March 805. Ludger's principal concern was to have a good and efficient clergy. To a great extent he educated his students personally, and generally took some of them on his missionary tours. He also founded the monastery of Helmstad, afterwards called Ludger-Clooster, or Ludger's Cloister, in the duchy of Brunswick.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was said of him that his peaceful methods were far more effective in promoting Christianity than the aggressive tactics of Charlemagne. He was criticised during his life for spending money on alms that should have gone towards the ornamentation of his churches. He suffered thereby, but was able to convince Charlemagne that this was no fault.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_relics">Death and relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On <a href="/wiki/Passion_Sunday" title="Passion Sunday">Passion Sunday</a> 809, Ludger heard <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> at Coesfeld early in the morning and preached, then went to Billerbeck, where at 9:00 he again preached, and said his last Mass. That evening he died peacefully in the company of his followers, at the age of sixty-six.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A dispute arose between Münster and Werden for the possession of his remains. His brother <a href="/wiki/Hildegrim" class="mw-redirect" title="Hildegrim">Hildegrim</a> was appealed to, and after consultation with the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">emperor</a>, decided in favour of Werden, where the relics still remain. Portions have, however, since been brought to Münster and Billerbeck.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The successive <i>Vitae</i>, beginning with the serious contemporary biographical work of <a href="/wiki/Altfrid" title="Altfrid">Altfrid</a> and passing through the <i>Vita Secunda</i> and <i>Vita Tertia</i> to the <i>Libellus Monasteriensis de miraculis sancti Liudgeri</i> (<i>The Little Book of Münster on the Miracles of Saint Ludger</i>) of c. 1170, demonstrate the growth of the legend. <a href="/wiki/Votive_office" title="Votive office">Votive practice</a> in Münster seems to have focused on a very large and elaborate cross containing a number of relics of the saint. The cult seems to have remained mostly local, and largely to have faded in the later Middle Ages. He is the patron saint of Werden, East Frisia, and Deventer.<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ludger is represented either as a bishop holding a church and a book<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> or as standing between two geese (occasionally described as swans). His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 26 March.
</p>
