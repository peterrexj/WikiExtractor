<p><b>Auguste Chapdelaine</b>, Chinese name <b>Mǎ Lài</b> (<a href="/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters" title="Traditional Chinese characters">Chinese</a>&#58; <span lang="zh-Hant">馬賴</span>; 6 February 1814 – 29 February 1856) was a French Christian <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a>. <a href="/wiki/Second_French_Empire" title="Second French Empire">France</a> used his death–– Chapdelaine was executed by Chinese officials–– as a <i><a href="/wiki/Casus_belli" title="Casus belli">casus belli</a></i> for its participation in the <a href="/wiki/Second_Opium_War" title="Second Opium War">Second Opium War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Agence_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agence-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Auguste_Chapdelaine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"><table class="box-More_citations_needed plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-Refimprove" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/50px-Question_book-new.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="39" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/75px-Question_book-new.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/100px-Question_book-new.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="399" /></a></span></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>needs additional citations for <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">verification</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Please help <a href="/wiki/Special:EditPage/Auguste_Chapdelaine" title="Special:EditPage/Auguste Chapdelaine">improve this article</a> by <a href="/wiki/Help:Referencing_for_beginners" title="Help:Referencing for beginners">adding citations to reliable sources</a>&#32;in this section. Unsourced material may be challenged and removed.<br /><small><span class="plainlinks"><i>Find sources:</i>&#160;<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?as_eq=wikipedia&amp;q=%22Auguste+Chapdelaine%22">"Auguste Chapdelaine"</a>&#160;–&#160;<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?tbm=nws&amp;q=%22Auguste+Chapdelaine%22+-wikipedia&amp;tbs=ar:1">news</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?&amp;q=%22Auguste+Chapdelaine%22&amp;tbs=bkt:s&amp;tbm=bks">newspapers</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?tbs=bks:1&amp;q=%22Auguste+Chapdelaine%22+-wikipedia">books</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Auguste+Chapdelaine%22">scholar</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.jstor.org/action/doBasicSearch?Query=%22Auguste+Chapdelaine%22&amp;acc=on&amp;wc=on">JSTOR</a></span></small></span>  <span class="date-container"><i>(<span class="date">December 2017</span>)</i></span><span class="hide-when-compact"><i> (<small><a href="/wiki/Help:Maintenance_template_removal" title="Help:Maintenance template removal">Learn how and when to remove this template message</a></small>)</i></span></div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Chapdelaine was born on a farm in <a href="/wiki/La_Rochelle-Normande" title="La Rochelle-Normande">La Rochelle-Normande</a>, France. By the age of twenty, he had entered the seminary at <a href="/wiki/Coutances" title="Coutances">Coutances</a>. He was ordained a priest in 1843 and in 1851 joined the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Institute of Foreign Missions</a> in Paris. He left from Antwerp in April 1852 to join the Catholic mission in the <a href="/wiki/Guangxi" title="Guangxi">Guangxi</a> province of China.<sup id="cite_ref-ANit_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ANit-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion" title="Taiping Rebellion">Taiping Rebellion</a> led to suspicion of Christians and foreigners were forbidden to enter the area.
</p><p>After a stay in <a href="/wiki/Guangzhou" title="Guangzhou">Guangzhou</a>, he moved to <a href="/wiki/Guiyang" title="Guiyang">Guiyang</a>, capital of the <a href="/wiki/Guizhou" title="Guizhou">Guizhou</a> province, in the spring of <a href="/wiki/1854" title="1854">1854</a>. In December, he went, together with <a href="/w/index.php?title=Lu_Tingmei&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Lu Tingmei (page does not exist)">Lu Tingmei</a>, to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Yaoshan,_Xilin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Yaoshan, Xilin (page does not exist)">Yaoshan</a> village, <a href="/wiki/Xilin_County" title="Xilin County">Xilin County</a> of Guangxi, where he met the local Catholic community of around 300 people. He celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">mass</a> there on 8 December 1854. He was arrested and thrown into the Xilin county <a href="/wiki/Prison" title="Prison">prison</a> ten days after his arrival, and was released after sixteen or eighteen days of captivity.
</p><p>Following personal threats, Chapdelaine returned to Guizhou in early 1855, and came back to Guangxi in December of the same year. He was denounced on February 22, 1856, by Bai San, a relative of a new convert, while the local tribunal was on holiday. He was arrested in Yaoshan, together with other Chinese Catholics, by orders of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Zhang_Mingfeng&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Zhang Mingfeng (page does not exist)">Zhang Mingfeng</a>, the new local <a href="/wiki/Mandarin_(bureaucrat)" title="Mandarin (bureaucrat)">mandarin</a> on 25 February 1856. Chapdelaine was accused of stirring up insurrection and refused to pay a bribe. Condemned to cage torture (<i>zhanlong</i>), he was first beaten one hundred times on the cheek by a leather thong, which caused his teeth to fly out, his face mutilated, and his jaw lacerated. He was locked into a small iron cage, which was hung at the gate of the jail. The planks he stood on were gradually removed, placing a strain in the muscles of the neck, and leading to a slow and painful death from suffocation.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He had already died when he was decapitated. His head was hung from a tree by his hair. Children were said to have thrown stones at the head until Chapdelaine's head fell to the ground and was devoured by street dogs and hogs.<sup id="cite_ref-Agence_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agence-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Diplomacy">Diplomacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Auguste_Chapdelaine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Diplomacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrerp_1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyrerp_1.jpg/220px-Martyrerp_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="170" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyrerp_1.jpg/330px-Martyrerp_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyrerp_1.jpg/440px-Martyrerp_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1392" data-file-height="1075" /></a><figcaption>Martyrdom of Auguste Chapdelaine.</figcaption></figure>
<p>His death was reported by the head of the French missions in Hong Kong on 12 July. The chargé d'affaires, de Courcy, in <a href="/wiki/Macau" title="Macau">Macau</a> learned of the execution on 17 July, and filed a vigorous protest on 25 July to the Chinese Imperial Viceroy <a href="/wiki/Ye_Mingchen" title="Ye Mingchen">Ye Mingchen</a>. On 30 July, he sent a report to the French foreign office of the execution. 
</p><p>The viceroy responded to de Courcy by pointing out that Chapdelaine had already violated Chinese law by preaching Christianity in the interior (the 1844 treaty signed with France only permitted for the propagation of Christianity in the five treaty ports opened to the French), he also claimed that the priest was in a rebel territory and that many of his converts had already been arrested for acts of treason, and the viceroy further claimed that Chapdelaine's mission had nothing in common with the propagation of religion.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Under French diplomatic pressure, the mandarin who ordered his death was later demoted. When Britain went to war with China in the same year (commencing the <a href="/wiki/Second_Opium_War" title="Second Opium War">Second Opium War</a> (1856–60)), France initially declared its neutrality, but de Courcy made it known that French sympathy was with the British due to the Chapdelaine incident.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1857, de Bourboulon, the French plenipotentiary, arrived in Hong Kong and attempted to negotiate reparations for the execution of Chapdelaine and to revise the treaty. He failed to reach an agreement with Yeh.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Talks continued into December of that year. Viceroy Yeh on 14 December stated that he had received a report that the person who was killed was a member of a <a href="/wiki/Triad_society" class="mw-redirect" title="Triad society">triad society</a> with a similar Chinese name to Chapdelaine was executed as a rebel in March, and that this was not the same person as Chapdelaine. He also complained that in the past many French citizens had gone into the interior to preach, and he cited the case of six missionaries who had been arrested and were handed over to French custody.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The French embassy found Yeh's reply to be evasive, derisory and a formal refusal of French demands. French military action began soon afterwards.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Second_Opium_War">The Second Opium War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Auguste_Chapdelaine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The Second Opium War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Second_Opium_War" title="Second Opium War">Second Opium War</a></div>
<p>According to historian <a href="/wiki/Anthony_E._Clark" title="Anthony E. Clark">Anthony Clark</a>, "there is no doubt Chapdelaine's death was exploited for imperialist gain".<sup id="cite_ref-Agence_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agence-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Second_French_Empire" title="Second French Empire">French Empire</a> had many times suffered the death of missionaries for which no military vengeance occurred. The political situation wherein Britain's victory was seen as inevitable and the French desire to make its own imperial gains in China, alongside the fact that the French did not have a policy elsewhere of punitive military expeditions to avenge the death of missionaries, has led many historians to conclude that the death of Chapdelaine was merely an excuse used in order to declare war so that France could build its empire.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<p><a href="/wiki/James_Bruce,_8th_Earl_of_Elgin" title="James Bruce, 8th Earl of Elgin">Lord Elgin</a>, the British High Commissioner for China commented on the French ultimatum given prior to France's entry to the war:</p><blockquote> <p><a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste-Louis_Gros" title="Jean-Baptiste-Louis Gros">Gros</a> [the French ambassador] showed me a <i>projet de note</i> [draft note] when I called on him some days ago. It is very long and very well written. The fact is, that he has had a much better case of quarrel than we; at least one that lends itself much better to rhetoric.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The Chinese version of Article Six in the Sino-French <a href="/wiki/Peking_Convention" class="mw-redirect" title="Peking Convention">Peking Convention</a>, signed at the end of the war, gave Christians the right to spread their faith in China and to French missionaries to hold property.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Recognition_and_controversy">Recognition and controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Auguste_Chapdelaine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Recognition and controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Chapdelaine was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1900<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2010)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on 1 October 2000, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, together with 120 Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> who had died in China between the 17th and 20th centuries. 
</p><p>Anthony Clark maintains that China's version of history is "largely contrived" and completely unsupported, and that notions that Chapdelaine was "a lascivious womanizer" and spy are "unsupportable in any historical records".<sup id="cite_ref-Agence_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agence-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
