<p><b>Pope Adrian I</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Hadrianus I</i>; c. 700 - 25 December 795) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 1 February 772 to his death.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the son of Theodore, a Roman nobleman.
</p><p>Adrian and his predecessors had to contend with periodic attempts by the Lombards to expand their holdings in Italy at the expense of the papacy. Not receiving any support from Constantinople, the popes looked for help to the Franks. Adrian's tenure saw the culmination of on-going territorial disputes between <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> and his brother <a href="/wiki/Carloman_I" title="Carloman I">Carloman I</a>. The Lombard king <a href="/wiki/Desiderius" title="Desiderius">Desiderius</a> supported the claims of Carloman's sons to their late father's land, and requested Pope Adrian crown Carloman's sons "Kings of the Franks". When the Pope failed to do so, Desiderius invaded Papal territory and seized the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_the_Pentapolis" title="Duchy of the Pentapolis">Duchy of the Pentapolis</a>. Charlemagne besieged <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> and took the Lombard crown for himself. He then restored the Pentapolis to the Papacy as well as some of the captured Lombard territory.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Start_of_papacy">Start of papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Start of papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Shortly after Adrian's accession in 772, the territory ruled by the papacy was invaded by <a href="/wiki/Desiderius" title="Desiderius">Desiderius</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a>, and Adrian was compelled to seek the assistance of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, who entered Italy with a large army. Charlemagne besieged Desiderius in his capital of <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>. After taking the town, he banished the Lombard king to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Corbie" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Corbie">Abbey of Corbie</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, and adopted the title "King of the Lombards" himself. The pope, whose expectations had been aroused, had to content himself with some additions to the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Rome" title="Duchy of Rome">Duchy of Rome</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna" title="Exarchate of Ravenna">Exarchate of Ravenna</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_the_Pentapolis" title="Duchy of the Pentapolis">Pentapolis</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Marches" class="mw-redirect" title="Marches">Marches</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which consisted of the "five cities" on the <a href="/wiki/Adriatic" class="mw-redirect" title="Adriatic">Adriatic</a> coast from <a href="/wiki/Rimini" title="Rimini">Rimini</a> to <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a> with the coastal plain as far as the mountains. He celebrated the occasion by striking the earliest papal coin,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and in a mark of the direction the mediaeval papacy was to take, no longer dated his documents by the Emperor in the east, but by the reign of Charles, king of the Franks.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He recognized the authority of Pope Adrian I, and in return the pope gave Charlemagne the title of "Patrician of Rome".
</p><p>A mark of such newly settled conditions in the Duchy of Rome is the <i><a href="/wiki/Domusculta_Capracorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Domusculta Capracorum">Domusculta Capracorum</a></i>, the central <a href="/wiki/Roman_villa" title="Roman villa">Roman villa</a> that Adrian assembled from a nucleus of his inherited estates and acquisitions from neighbors in the countryside north of <a href="/wiki/Veii" title="Veii">Veii</a>. The villa is documented in <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i>, but its site was not rediscovered until the 1960s, when excavations revealed the structures on a gently-rounded hill that was only marginally capable of self-defense, but fully self-sufficient for a mixed economy of grains and <a href="/wiki/Vineyard" title="Vineyard">vineyards</a>, <a href="/wiki/Olive" title="Olive">olives</a>, vegetable gardens and piggery with its own grain mill, smithies and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Tile-kiln&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Tile-kiln (page does not exist)">tile-kilns</a>. During the 10th century, villages were carved out of Adrian's Capracorum estate: <a href="/wiki/Campagnano" class="mw-redirect" title="Campagnano">Campagnano</a>, mentioned first in 1076; <a href="/wiki/Formello" title="Formello">Formello</a>, mentioned in 1027; <a href="/wiki/Mazzano_Romano" title="Mazzano Romano">Mazzano</a>, mentioned in 945; and Stabia (modern <a href="/wiki/Faleria" title="Faleria">Faleria</a>), mentioned in 998.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foreign_relations">Foreign relations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Foreign relations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lombards">Lombards</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Lombards">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>While the Lombards had always been openly respectful of the papacy, the popes distrusted them. The popes had sought aid from the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Empire">Eastern Roman Empire</a> to keep them in check. Adrian continued this policy. Because the East could offer no direct aid, Adrian then looked to the Franks to offset the power of the Lombards.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Upon the death of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_the_Short" title="Pepin the Short">Pepin the Short</a> in 768, his kingdom was left to his sons <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carloman_I" title="Carloman I">Carloman I</a>. Relations between the brothers is said to have been strained. In 770 Tassilo III, Duke of Bavaria married a Lombard princess, <a href="/wiki/Liutperga" title="Liutperga">Liutperga</a>, daughter of King Desiderius, to confirm the traditional alliance between Lombardy and Bavaria. That same year, Charlemagne concluded a treaty with Duke Tassilo, and married Liutperga's sister, <a href="/wiki/Desiderata_of_the_Lombards" title="Desiderata of the Lombards">Desiderata</a>, to surround Carloman with his own allies. Less than a year later, Charlemagne repudiated Desiderata and married <a href="/wiki/Hildegard_of_the_Vinzgau" title="Hildegard of the Vinzgau">Hildegard</a>, the daughter of Count <a href="/wiki/Gerold_of_Vinzgau" title="Gerold of Vinzgau">Gerold of Kraichgau</a> and his wife Emma, daughter, in turn, of <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Alemannia" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Alemannia">Duke</a> <a href="/wiki/Hnabi" title="Hnabi">Nebe (Hnabi)</a> of Alemannia.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Hildegard's father had extensive possessions in the territory under Carloman's dominion. This marriage was advantageous to Charlemagne because it allowed him to strengthen his position east of the Rhine and also bind the Alemannian nobility to his side.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> With Desiderata's return to her father's court at Pavia, Desiderius was grievously insulted, and appears to have made an alliance with Carloman against Charlemagne and the Papacy, which looked to the Franks for protection against Lombard incursions into Papal territory.<sup id="cite_ref-McKitterick_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McKitterick-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Italy">Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Iron_Crown.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Iron_Crown.JPG/220px-Iron_Crown.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="134" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Iron_Crown.JPG/330px-Iron_Crown.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Iron_Crown.JPG/440px-Iron_Crown.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1890" data-file-height="1150" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Iron_Crown_of_Lombardy" class="mw-redirect" title="Iron Crown of Lombardy">Iron Crown of Lombardy</a>, for centuries a symbol of the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Italy" title="King of Italy">Kings of Italy</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Carloman died in December 771, and when Charlemagne seized his brother's territory, Carloman's widow, <a href="/wiki/Gerberga,_wife_of_Carloman_I" title="Gerberga, wife of Carloman I">Gerberga</a>, and their two sons fled for refuge to the Lombard court at Pavia. Desiderius made overtures to Pope Adrian, requesting that he acknowledge Carloman's sons' right to succeed their father, and crown them as Kings of the Franks.<sup id="cite_ref-McKitterick_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McKitterick-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> With Charlemagne occupied with a campaign against the Saxons, Desiderius saw an opportunity to take all of Italy. He invaded the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_the_Pentapolis" title="Duchy of the Pentapolis">Duchy of the Pentapolis</a> which had been given to the papacy in 756 by Charlemagne's father. Desiderius's support of the claims of Carloman's sons posed a potential challenge to the legitimacy of Charlemagne's possession of his brother's lands. In 773, he cut short a military campaign near Paderborn, crossed the Alps, and laid siege to Pavia. In exchange for their lives, the Lombards surrendered and Desiderius was sent to the abbey of Corbie. Charlemagne assumed the title "King of the Lombards".
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Franks">Franks</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Franks">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg/220px-Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="164" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg/330px-Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg/440px-Charlemagne_and_Pope_Adrian_I.jpg 2x" data-file-width="556" data-file-height="414" /></a><figcaption>15th Century miniature depicting Adrian I greeting the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>From 781 Adrian began dating papal documents by the years of Charlemagne's reign, instead of the reign of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Byzantine Emperor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Friendly relations between pope and king were not disturbed by the theological dispute about the veneration of icons.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In 787, <a href="/wiki/Seventh_Ecumenical_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Seventh Ecumenical Council">Second Council of Nicaea</a>, approved by Pope Adrian, had confirmed the practice and excommunicated the <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm_(Byzantine)" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconoclasm (Byzantine)">iconoclasts</a>. Charlemagne, however, who had received the Council's decisions only in a bad Latin translation, consulted with his theologians and sent the Pope the <i>Capitulare contra synodum</i> (792), a response critical of several passages found in the council's acts. He also had his theologians, including <a href="/wiki/Theodulf_of_Orleans" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodulf of Orleans">Theodulf of Orleans</a>, compose the more comprehensive <i><a href="/wiki/Libri_Carolini" title="Libri Carolini">Libri Carolini</a></i>. Pope Adrian reacted to the Capitulare with a defense of the Council. In 794, a synod held at <a href="/wiki/Frankfurt" title="Frankfurt">Frankfurt</a> in 794 discussed the issue but refused to receive the Libri and contented itself with condemning extreme forms of veneration of icons.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="English">English</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: English">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 787 Adrian elevated the English <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lichfield" title="Bishop of Lichfield">diocese of Lichfield</a> to an archdiocese at the request of the English bishops and King <a href="/wiki/Offa_of_Mercia" title="Offa of Mercia">Offa of Mercia</a> to balance the ecclesiastic power in that land between <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>. He gave the <a href="/wiki/Lichfield" title="Lichfield">Lichfield</a> bishop <a href="/wiki/Hygeberht" title="Hygeberht">Hygeberht</a> the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> in 788.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Muslim_Spain">Muslim Spain</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Muslim Spain">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Regarding the Muslims, he maintained the prohibition of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zachary" title="Pope Zachary">Pope Zachary</a> of selling slaves to Muslims, whom Adrian described as "the unspeakable race of <a href="/wiki/Saracens" class="mw-redirect" title="Saracens">Saracens</a>,"<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> in order to guarantee a labor pool and to keep the power of Muslim rivals in check.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He also encouraged Charlemagne to lead his troops into Spain against <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">the Muslims there</a><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and was generally interested in expanding Christian influence and eliminating Muslim control.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The rise in the number of Christian girls being married to Muslims in <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">al-Andalus</a> prompted a letter of concern from Hadrian.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarton201521_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarton201521-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Hadrian's response was due to dispatches from bishop Egila who had been tasked with preaching the gospel in the peninsula.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarton201521_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarton201521-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Egila eventually fell in with the <a href="/wiki/Migetians" title="Migetians">Migetians</a>, a rigorist sect, provoking Adrian's condemnation.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECavadini198832_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECavadini198832-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_(titel_op_object)_Liber_Chronicarum_(serietitel),_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_%28titel_op_object%29_Liber_Chronicarum_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg/220px-Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_%28titel_op_object%29_Liber_Chronicarum_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="247" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_%28titel_op_object%29_Liber_Chronicarum_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg/330px-Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_%28titel_op_object%29_Liber_Chronicarum_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_%28titel_op_object%29_Liber_Chronicarum_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg/440px-Paus_Adrianus_I_Hadrianus_primus_%28titel_op_object%29_Liber_Chronicarum_%28serietitel%29%2C_RP-P-2016-49-58-8.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1204" data-file-height="1350" /></a><figcaption>Adrian I as depicted in the 15th century <a href="/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle" title="Nuremberg Chronicle">Nuremberg Chronicle</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Adrian restored some of the ancient <a href="/wiki/Aqueduct_(Roman)" class="mw-redirect" title="Aqueduct (Roman)">aqueducts</a> of Rome and rebuilt the churches of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Cosmedin" title="Santa Maria in Cosmedin">Santa Maria in Cosmedin</a>, decorated by Greek monks fleeing from the iconoclast persecutions, and of <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Marco_(Rome)" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di San Marco (Rome)">San Marco</a> in Rome. At the time of his death at the age of 95, his was the longest pontificate since <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> (the first pope) until it was surpassed by the 24-year papacy of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pius VI</a> in the late 18th century. Only three other popes&#160;– <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pius IX</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">John Paul II</a>&#160;– have reigned for longer periods since.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Epitaph">Epitaph</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Adrian_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Epitaph">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Adrian's epitaph was originally located in his burial chapel in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>, which was demolished in the mid-15th century as reconstruction works were initiated by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V" title="Pope Nicholas V">Pope Nicholas V</a>; since 1619 it has been preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Narthex" title="Narthex">portico</a> as rebuilt by <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Maderno" title="Carlo Maderno">Carlo Maderno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> It is placed high on the wall between the <a href="/wiki/Door_of_the_Dead_in_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Door of the Dead in St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">Door of the Dead</a> and the Door of Good and Evil.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> commissioned it in 796 and organized a literary competition for the text, won by <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a>; a competing entry by <a href="/wiki/Theodulf_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Theodulf of Orléans">Theodulf of Orléans</a> also survives.<sup id="cite_ref-Nelson_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nelson-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 347–351">&#58;&#8202;347–351&#8202;</span></sup> Alcuin's text,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> in which Charlemagne speaks affectionately of Adrian as his lost (spiritual) father, was carved in <a href="/wiki/Roman_square_capitals" title="Roman square capitals">Roman square capitals</a> on <a href="/wiki/Noir_Belge" title="Noir Belge">black limestone</a> from <a href="/wiki/Sclayn" title="Sclayn">Sclayn</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Meuse" title="Meuse">Meuse</a> valley, now in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Nelson_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nelson-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 350">&#58;&#8202;350&#8202;</span></sup> Its lettering has been described as "perfect and sharp" and is a notable exemplar of Carolingian monumental script.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The contemporaneous <a href="/wiki/Annales_laureshamenses" title="Annales laureshamenses">Annals of Lorsch</a> refer to Adrian's epitaph being made in <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a> and transported to Rome on Charlemagne's orders, and describe it as "written in gold letters on marble."<sup id="cite_ref-Nelson_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nelson-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page: 349">&#58;&#8202;349&#8202;</span></sup>
</p>
