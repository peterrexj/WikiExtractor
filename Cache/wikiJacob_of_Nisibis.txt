<p><b>Saint Jacob of Nisibis</b> (<a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>: <span lang="syr" dir="rtl">ܝܥܩܘܒ ܢܨܝܒܢܝܐ</span>, <i><span title="Semitic languages romanization"><i lang="sem-Latn">Yaʿqôḇ Nṣîḇnāyâ</i></span></i>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Ἅγιος Ἰάκωβος Ἐπίσκοπος Μυγδονίας; <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: Յակոբ Մծբնայ, <i><span title="Semitic languages romanization"><i lang="sem-Latn">Yagop Mdzpnay</i></span></i>), also known as <b>Saint Jacob of Mygdonia</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup> <b>Saint Jacob the Great</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and <b>Saint James of Nisibis</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Grazers_(Christianity)" title="Grazers (Christianity)">grazer</a> and the Bishop of Nisibis until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-:5_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:5-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was lauded as the "Moses of Mesopotamia", and was the spiritual father of the renowned writer and theologian Saint <a href="/wiki/Ephrem_the_Syrian" title="Ephrem the Syrian">Ephrem the Syrian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Saint Jacob was present at the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">first ecumenical council</a> at <a href="/wiki/Nicaea" title="Nicaea">Nicaea</a>, and is venerated as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_East" title="Church of the East">Church of the East</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodox Church">Oriental Orthodox Church</a>,  <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacob_of_Nisibis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mar_Jacob_Church,_Nisibis.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Mar_Jacob_Church%2C_Nisibis.jpg/300px-Mar_Jacob_Church%2C_Nisibis.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="172" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Mar_Jacob_Church%2C_Nisibis.jpg/450px-Mar_Jacob_Church%2C_Nisibis.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6a/Mar_Jacob_Church%2C_Nisibis.jpg/600px-Mar_Jacob_Church%2C_Nisibis.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2144" data-file-height="1232" /></a><figcaption>The newly excavated <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Jacob_of_Nisibis" title="Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis">Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Jacob was the son of prince Gefal,<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and was born in the city of <a href="/wiki/Nusaybin" title="Nusaybin">Nisibis</a> in <a href="/wiki/Mesopotamia" title="Mesopotamia">Mesopotamia</a> in the 3rd century AD.<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It is claimed that he was a relative of Saint <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator" title="Gregory the Illuminator">Gregory the Illuminator</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Saint Jacob became a <a href="/wiki/Confessor_of_the_Faith" title="Confessor of the Faith">Confessor of the Faith</a> for his suffering during persecution by Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Saint Jacob became an <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a> in c. 280 in the mountains near Nisibis where, according to Saint <a href="/wiki/Theodoret" title="Theodoret">Theodoret of Cyrrhus</a>, he survived on herbs and fruits, and chose to wear no clothes, build shelter, or light fires for warmth.<sup id="cite_ref-Hinson_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hinson-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The saint became famous, and received visits from Sheria, <a href="/wiki/Adiabene_(East_Syriac_Ecclesiastical_Province)#The_diocese_of_Erbil" class="mw-redirect" title="Adiabene (East Syriac Ecclesiastical Province)">Bishop of Arbela</a> (r. 304–316), according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Arbela" title="Chronicle of Arbela">Chronicle of Arbela</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Vööbus28_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vööbus28-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The saint resolved to climb <a href="/wiki/Mount_Judi" title="Mount Judi">Mount Qardu</a>, traditionally believed to be the resting place of <a href="/wiki/Noah%27s_Ark" title="Noah&#39;s Ark">Noah's Ark</a>, and recover a fragment of the ark upon hearing from the hermit Maroukeh that local people doubted the <a href="/wiki/Genesis_flood_narrative" title="Genesis flood narrative">Great Flood</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-AAC_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AAC-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Saint Jacob ascended the mountain and rested close to the summit; in his sleep, an angel placed a fragment of the ark close to him, and instructed him to awake.<sup id="cite_ref-AAC_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AAC-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The saint brought the relic to the hermit Maroukeh and, according to the saint's hagiography, a <a href="/wiki/Sacred_spring" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred spring">sacred spring</a> appeared where the saint had rested, reputed to have healing properties.<sup id="cite_ref-AAC_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AAC-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A number of miracles are credited to Saint Jacob by Saint Theodoret in <i>Historia Religiosa</i> (Religious History), in which the saint had a boulder explode beside a Persian judge who had given an unjust judgement.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Also, in one incident, Saint Jacob cursed boastful, promiscuous women by a spring so that their hair became white, and the spring disappeared.<sup id="cite_ref-SOCA_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOCA-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The women subsequently repented, and the spring returned, however, the women's hair remained white.<sup id="cite_ref-SOCA_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOCA-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> As well as this, a group of people attempted to deceive the saint whereby they asked for money to fund the burial of a man they had lain down and covered with a sheet with the illusion of death; consequently, the man died, and the people repented and thus the man was resurrected as a result of Saint Jacob's prayers.<sup id="cite_ref-SOCA_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOCA-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Disagreement exists as to the date of the saint's consecration as bishop of Nisibis as it is argued it took place in c. 300,<sup id="cite_ref-Hinson_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hinson-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and he is recorded as the city's first bishop by Saint <a href="/wiki/Ephrem_the_Syrian" title="Ephrem the Syrian">Ephrem the Syrian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bundy2000_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bundy2000-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> However, Saint Jacob is credited as the successor of Babu, the first bishop of Nisibis (r. 300–309), by the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> who Saint Ephrem states was in fact Saint Jacob's successor.<sup id="cite_ref-Bundy2000_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bundy2000-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In his <i>Chronography</i>, <a href="/wiki/Elijah_of_Nisibis" title="Elijah of Nisibis">Elijah of Nisibis</a> states that Saint Jacob was consecrated bishop in 308.<sup id="cite_ref-Vööbus28_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Vööbus28-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_Edessa" title="Chronicle of Edessa">Chronicle of Edessa</a></i> states that the saint constructed the <a href="/wiki/Mor_Yakup_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Mor Yakup Church">first church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nusaybin" title="Nusaybin">Nisibis</a> in c. 313–320.<sup id="cite_ref-Bundy_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bundy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Miles_(bishop_of_Susa)" title="Miles (bishop of Susa)">Miles, Bishop of Susa</a>, is said to have contributed a large quantity of silk from <a href="/wiki/Adiabene" title="Adiabene">Adiabene</a> to the church's construction.<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The foundation of the <a href="/wiki/School_of_Nisibis" title="School of Nisibis">School of Nisibis</a> is also attributed to Saint Jacob.<sup id="cite_ref-SOCA_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SOCA-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Saint Jacob attended the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">First Council of Nicaea</a> in 325, and opposed <a href="/wiki/Arius" title="Arius">Arius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bundy_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bundy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Saint Ephrem purportedly accompanied the saint to the council, however, this is considered apocryphal.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Saint Jacob attended the funeral of Saint <a href="/wiki/Metrophanes_of_Byzantium" title="Metrophanes of Byzantium">Metrophanes of Byzantium</a> in 326.<sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Jacob was present at the siege of Nisibis by <a href="/wiki/Shapur_II" title="Shapur II">Shapur II</a>, <i>Shahanshah</i> of <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_Empire" title="Sasanian Empire">Iran</a>, in 337/338,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;note 2&#93;</a></sup> and according to Saint Theodoret, with encouragement from the city's population and Saint Ephrem, Saint Jacob ascended the walls and prayed for the city, and cursed the besiegers.<sup id="cite_ref-Lightfoot124_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lightfoot124-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i> relates that he died on 15 July, the thirtieth day of the siege, according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicle_of_724" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicle of 724">Chronicle of 724</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Burgess_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burgess-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Gennadius and Saint Ephrem record that Saint Jacob was buried within the walls of Nisibis.<sup id="cite_ref-Burgess_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burgess-20">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Saint Theodoret adds that the Iranian army was afflicted by a swarm of gnats and flies summoned by the saint, and Shapur II subsequently abandoned the siege.<sup id="cite_ref-Lightfoot124_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lightfoot124-19">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The saint is counted amongst the signatories of the <a href="/wiki/Synods_of_Antioch" title="Synods of Antioch">Council of Antioch</a> in 341,<sup id="cite_ref-BARING-GOULD_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BARING-GOULD-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> however, his presence at the council is unrecorded in other sources.<sup id="cite_ref-Venables_8-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Venables-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 350, according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Paschale" title="Chronicon Paschale">Chronicon Paschale</a></i>, Saint Jacob helped defend Nisibis against <a href="/wiki/Shapur_II" title="Shapur II">Shapur II</a> again, and as he was wearing the imperial regalia, was confused for Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Whitby_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Whitby-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Shapur II challenged the saint to fight outside the city, where it was revealed he was an apparition and the Iranian army withdrew as a result.<sup id="cite_ref-Whitby_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Whitby-21">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacob_of_Nisibis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jacob_Tomb,_Nisibis.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Jacob_Tomb%2C_Nisibis.jpg/200px-Jacob_Tomb%2C_Nisibis.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="348" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Jacob_Tomb%2C_Nisibis.jpg/300px-Jacob_Tomb%2C_Nisibis.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Jacob_Tomb%2C_Nisibis.jpg/400px-Jacob_Tomb%2C_Nisibis.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1232" data-file-height="2144" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Saint Jacob of Nisibis of <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Jacob_of_Nisibis" title="Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis">Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The Tomb of Saint Jacob of Nisibis located at the newly excavated <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Jacob_of_Nisibis" title="Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis">Church of Saint Jacob of Nisibis</a>.
</p><p>The fragment of Noah's Ark discovered by Saint Jacob was later brought to <a href="/wiki/Etchmiadzin_Cathedral" title="Etchmiadzin Cathedral">Etchmiadzin Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-AAC_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AAC-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Theodoret relates that the bones of Saint Jacob were transferred from Nisibis to <a href="/wiki/Edessa" title="Edessa">Edessa</a> following the city's cession to Iran on 22 August 363.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The saint's relics were later moved to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 970,<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> according to the Menologion of the <a href="/wiki/Mekhitarists" title="Mekhitarists">Armenians at Venice</a>.
</p><p>Fragments of the skull of Saint Jacob were donated to <a href="/wiki/Hildesheim_Cathedral" title="Hildesheim Cathedral">Hildesheim Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> in 1367 by Lippold von Steinberg after the Battle of Dinklar (<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schlacht_von_Dinklar" class="extiw" title="de:Schlacht von Dinklar">de:Schlacht von Dinklar</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2018, relics of Saint Jacob were brought from the Armenian Church of Saint George in <a href="/wiki/Plovdiv" title="Plovdiv">Plovdiv</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>, where they were taken to the Armenian Church of Saint Gregory the Illuminator in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a> on 17 June, and the Armenian Church of the Holy Trinity in <a href="/wiki/Toronto" title="Toronto">Toronto</a> on 24 June.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacob_of_Nisibis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Several homilies previously attributed to Saint Jacob by Gennadius of Massilia and others are now understood to be the work of Saint <a href="/wiki/Aphrahat" title="Aphrahat">Aphraates</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bundy_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bundy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The misidentification arose from Aphraates' assumption of the name Jacob upon becoming bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Letters and canons, as well as other works, formerly attributed to the saint are known to be written in a later period.<sup id="cite_ref-Bundy_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bundy-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
