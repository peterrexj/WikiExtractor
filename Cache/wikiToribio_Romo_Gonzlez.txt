<p><b>Toribio Romo González</b>, known as <b>Saint Toribio Romo</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">santo Toribio Romo</i>, <small>Spanish pronunciation:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="es-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Spanish" title="Help:IPA/Spanish">[ˌsanto toˈɾiβjo ˈromo]</a></span>; April 16, 1900 – February 25, 1928) was a <a href="/wiki/Mexican_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Mexican people">Mexican</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who was killed during the <a href="/wiki/Anti-clericalism" title="Anti-clericalism">anti-clerical</a> persecutions of the <a href="/wiki/Cristero_War" title="Cristero War">Cristero War</a>. Beatified and later canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> along with 24 other <a href="/wiki/Saints_of_the_Cristero_War" title="Saints of the Cristero War">saints and martyrs of the Cristero War</a>, he is popularly venerated in Mexico and among <a href="/wiki/Emigration_from_Mexico" title="Emigration from Mexico">Mexican immigrants</a>, particularly for his reported miraculous appearances to migrants seeking to cross the <a href="/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border" title="Mexico–United States border">Mexico–United States border</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Toribio_Romo_Gonz%C3%A1lez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Toribio was born on April 16, 1900 to farmers Juana González Romo and Patricio Romo Pérez in the <i><a href="/wiki/Rancher%C3%ADa" title="Ranchería">ranchería</a></i> of Santa Ana de Guadalupe, located about 11 kilometres (6.8&#160;mi) from the municipal seat of <a href="/wiki/Jalostotitl%C3%A1n" title="Jalostotitlán">Jalostotitlán</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jalisco" title="Jalisco">Jalisco</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Orozco_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orozco-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He had two siblings: a sister, María, and a younger brother, Román, who would also go on to become a priest. 
</p><p>Though initially countered by his parents, in 1912, at age thirteen, he entered the Auxiliary Seminary of <a href="/wiki/San_Juan_de_los_Lagos" title="San Juan de los Lagos">San Juan de los Lagos</a>, before transferring to the Major Diocesan Seminary of <a href="/wiki/Guadalajara,_Jalisco" class="mw-redirect" title="Guadalajara, Jalisco">Guadalajara</a> in 1920. He was created a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> on September 22, 1922, and ordained a priest a few months later, on December 23, at the age of twenty-two, after being granted a <a href="/wiki/Dispensation_(canon_law)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dispensation (canon law)">dispensation</a> due to his exceedingly young age. He celebrated his first public mass on January 5, 1923.
</p><p>Toribio's ministry was characterized by a heavy emphasis on <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechesis</a> to the poor, as well as the centrality of the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">eucharist</a> to Christian life. During his brief priesthood, he served in parishes in the towns of <a href="/wiki/Sayula,_Jalisco" title="Sayula, Jalisco">Sayula</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tuxpan,_Jalisco" title="Tuxpan, Jalisco">Tuxpan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Yahualica_de_Gonz%C3%A1lez_Gallo" title="Yahualica de González Gallo">Yahualica</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cuqu%C3%ADo" title="Cuquío">Cuquío</a>, all of them in his native state of Jalisco.
</p><p>Starting in November 1926, after a revolt in the town of Cuquío against the <a href="/wiki/Anti-clericalism" title="Anti-clericalism">anti-clerical</a> persecutions of Mexican president <a href="/wiki/Plutarco_El%C3%ADas_Calles" title="Plutarco Elías Calles">Plutarco Elías Calles</a>, he was forced to take up an itinerant lifestyle along with Justino Orona, the parish priest of Cuquío. After relocating almost a dozen times, his final residence was the rural settlement of Agua Caliente, in the outskirts of the town of <a href="/wiki/Tequila,_Jalisco" title="Tequila, Jalisco">Tequila</a>, where he was sent to hide and was offered refuge by a local landowner. There, he was joined by his brother and sister, and he continued to secretly carry out his priestly ministry from an abandoned distillery and by visiting parishioners in the town of Tequila by night.<sup id="cite_ref-Orozco_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orozco-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Toribio_Romo_Gonz%C3%A1lez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On Friday, February 24, 1928, he spent his day organizing the parish registry. Two days before he had sent his brother away to safety. Toribio finished his work in the early hours of February 25 and went to bed. An hour later, government troops arrived and broke into the bedroom where he was sleeping. One of the soldiers reportedly shouted: "Here is the priest, kill him!" He said, "Here I am, but do not kill me."<sup id="cite_ref-Orozco_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Orozco-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another soldier, however, fired, and Toribio rose from his bed and took a few steps before a second bullet caused him to fall into the arms of his sister, who cried in a loud voice: "Courage, Father Toribio...merciful Christ, receive him! Long live Christ the King!"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Toribio_Romo_Gonz%C3%A1lez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Padre_Toribio.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Padre_Toribio.jpg/220px-Padre_Toribio.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Padre_Toribio.jpg/330px-Padre_Toribio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Padre_Toribio.jpg/440px-Padre_Toribio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3008" data-file-height="2000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Padre_Toribio.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Mural of Padre Toribio near Tequila, Jalisco</div></div></div>
<p>Father Toribio Romo was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on November 22, 1992 and canonized on May 21, 2000.<sup id="cite_ref-Corchado_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Corchado-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is May 21.
</p><p>Despite the fact that in 1920, fearing immigrants would lose their values, Father Toribio wrote a play titled "Let's go north!", a comedy that warned migrants against traveling to the States,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> there is a belief among some Mexicans that Toribio Romo has appeared to some who cross the border illegally to assist them in distress.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In the late 1970s migrants began telling stories about St. Toribio coming to their rescue.<sup id="cite_ref-Corchado_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Corchado-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Saint Toribio Romo Fund, which bears his name, supports the work of the immigration services department of Catholic Charities in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Monterey_in_California" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Monterey in California">Diocese of Monterey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
