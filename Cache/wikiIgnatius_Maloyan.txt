<p><b>Ignatius Shoukrallah Maloyan</b> (<a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Իգնատիոս Մալոյան</span>, b. April 8, 1869, <a href="/wiki/Mardin" title="Mardin">Mardin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> – d. June 11, 1915), was the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Catholic">Armenian Catholic</a> Archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Mardin" title="Mardin">Mardin</a> between 1911 and 1915, when he fell victim to the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Genocide">Armenian Genocide</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> as a martyr in 2001.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Shoukrallah Maloyan was born in 1869. When he was fourteen years old, he was sent by his parish priest to <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Elias_and_Saint_Gregory_the_Illuminator" title="Cathedral of Saint Elias and Saint Gregory the Illuminator">the Armenian Catholic Cathedral</a> at <a href="/wiki/Bzoummar" title="Bzoummar">Bzoummar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He completed his theological studies on 6 August 1896 and adopted the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of <b>Ignatius</b> in honor of St. <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch" title="Ignatius of Antioch">Ignatius of Antioch</a>. During the years 1897–1910, Maloyan served the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Eparchy_of_Alexandria" title="Armenian Catholic Eparchy of Alexandria">Armenian Catholic Eparchy of Alexandria</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cairo" title="Cairo">Cairo</a>. Maloyan began serving in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> as an assistant to the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Cilicia" title="Patriarchate of Cilicia">Armenian Catholic Patriarch</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_Petros_XII_Sabbaghian&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paul Petros XII Sabbaghian (page does not exist)">Paul Petros XII Sabbaghian</a> in 1904.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to historian Charles A. Frazee, the Patriarch chose to continue the policies of his predecessor and did not protest against Turkish and Kurdish massacres of his faithful, but instead focussed upon giving humanitarian relief to the survivors.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/Young_Turk_Revolution" title="Young Turk Revolution">Young Turk Revolution</a> in 1907, however, the Patriarch came under attack by some Catholic Armenians, who considered him, "an unworthy prelate". Having little desire for conflict, Patriarch Paul Petros submitted his resignation to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> in August 1910.<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_271_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_271-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The National Council of the Armenian Catholic Church accordingly met and, on April 23, 1911, unanimously selected the Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Adana" title="Adana">Adana</a> as <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paul_Petros_XIII_Terzian&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paul Petros XIII Terzian (page does not exist)">Paul Petros XIII Terzian</a>. During a visit to the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican</a> in order to receive the <i><a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">Pallium</a></i>, the Patriarch was told the Pope would choose the bishops for the vacant <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">Sees</a> out of a list of names chosen by the Patriarch and approved by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Propagation_of_the_Faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith">Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> also summoned the Armenian Catholic bishops to Rome, "to confer on the difficulties facing their Church."<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_271_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_271-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite the best efforts of Armenian Catholic dissenters and of the Ottoman State, Patriarch Paul Petros XIII accordingly convened the <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">Synod</a> in Rome with 13 bishops present on October 15, 1911.<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_271_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_271-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Following a Synod whose decrees greatly strengthened the independence and governing role the Armenian Catholic clergy at the expense of the Ottoman Government and the laity,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Fr. Maloyan was consecrated in Rome as <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Mardin" title="Armenian Catholic Archeparchy of Mardin">Archbishop of Mardin</a> on October 22, 1911.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In a 20th-century renewal of the <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">Investiture Controversy</a>, both the National Council and by Sultan <a href="/wiki/Mehmet_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Mehmet V">Mehmet V</a> declared, in retaliation for having convened a Synod outside of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> and for consecrating Bishops without asking first for the Government's approval, that Patriarch Paul Petros XIII was deposed. A Patriarchal <i><a href="/wiki/Locum_tenens" class="mw-redirect" title="Locum tenens">locum tenens</a></i>, Bishop Hatchadourian of <a href="/wiki/Malatia" class="mw-redirect" title="Malatia">Malatia</a>, was appointed instead and moved into the Patriarch's official residence in <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>. Although Pope Pius X immediately declared the new "Patriarch" suspended, the Vatican was unable to restore Patriarch Paul Petros XIII as long as the Ottoman Government supported his rival.<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_272_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_272-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For this reason, Archbishop Maloyan arrived in Mardin to find local Armenian Catholics in turmoil. He tried to restore order, while also encouraging devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Charles E. Frazee, "Thus the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church" title="Armenian Catholic Church">Armenian Catholic Church</a>, with about 140,000 members, entered the period of the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a> with a deep division in its ranks. Each Patriarch had his own partisans among the clergy and laity, and compromise seemed far away. Despite the fact that, of all Armenians, Catholics were the most loyal to the Ottoman Government, they were not to be spared the <a href="/wiki/Genocide" title="Genocide">genocide</a> that lay ahead."<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_272_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles_E._Frazee_1983_Page_272-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prelude_to_Genocide">Prelude to Genocide</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Prelude to Genocide">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/1915_genocide_in_Diyarbekir" title="1915 genocide in Diyarbekir">1915 genocide in Diyarbekir</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Great_War">The Great War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The Great War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The conscription and mobilization of adult males, including Christians, began in Mardin in August 1914. House searches and public executions of both real and imagined deserters became a daily occurrence. The <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottoman Army">Ottoman Army</a> also "requisitioned" both goods from Christian owned shops and draft animals for military use. The detailed <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic language</a> diary of <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Syriac Catholic">Syriac Catholic</a> priest Fr. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ishaq_Armal%C3%A9&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ishaq Armalé (page does not exist)">Ishaq Armalé</a> also records multiple cases of random violence and murders of Mardin's  Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On December 13, 1914, however, Hilmi Bey was appointed as <a href="/wiki/Mutasarrif" title="Mutasarrif">mutasarrif</a> of Mardin. Upon arrival, Hilmi Bey was polite and proper towards Archbishops Maloyan and <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_Gabriel_I_Tappouni" title="Ignatius Gabriel I Tappouni">Gabriel Tappouni</a> and promised to, "help them with their problems." Soon after, when accusations spread that more Christian deserters were hiding in the area, both Archbishops met with the Mutasarrif, the police chief, and the head of the military garrison and requested a list of names so that might help with the search. This was interpreted by Hilmi Bey as a, "sign of upright love of the state."<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_329_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_329-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On February 11, 1915, Archbishop Maloyan attended a dinner at Archbishop Tappouni's residence, which the Mutasarrif also attended.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_329_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_329-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Gallipoli_campaign" title="Gallipoli campaign">Gallipoli campaign</a> in March 1915, however, rumors reached Mardin of plans to exterminate the Empire's Christian population.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_329_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_329-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That same month, Dr. <a href="/wiki/Mehmed_Reshid" title="Mehmed Reshid">Mehmed Reshid</a> was appointed as the new <a href="/wiki/Diyarbekir_Vilayet" class="mw-redirect" title="Diyarbekir Vilayet">Vali of Diyarbekir</a> and arrived with a small private <a href="/wiki/Death_squad" title="Death squad">death squad</a> composed of <a href="/wiki/Circassian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Circassian people">Circassian</a> volunteers.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_321_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_321-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On March 28, which was <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a>, Turkish soldiers entered all of the churches for mass arrests of alleged deserters, including deacons, who previously been exempt from the draft. The military continued to harass both Armenian and Assyrian churchgoers all through <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week" title="Holy Week">Holy Week</a> and <a href="/wiki/Easter_Sunday" class="mw-redirect" title="Easter Sunday">Easter Sunday</a> was spent with, "scared hearts and fearful restless minds."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hoping to save lives, Archbishop Maloyan made every opportunity to express his Archdiocese's loyalty to the Ottoman Empire. For these statements, Maloyan was informed on April 6 that he was to receive a medal from Sultan <a href="/wiki/Mehmed_V" title="Mehmed V">Mehmed V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_329_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_329-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On April 13, 1915, a <a href="/wiki/Special_Organization_(Ottoman_Empire)" title="Special Organization (Ottoman Empire)">militia unit</a> was formed from local Muslim volunteers, which increased the worry of local Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_330_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_330-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On April 20, 1915, the medal arrived and was publicly presented to Archbishop Maloyan along with a proclamation from the Sultan. In his acceptance speech, the Archbishop expressed hope for the continued health of Sultan Mehmet and the success of his Ministers and Generals in leading the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> to victory over the <a href="/wiki/Allies_(World_War_I)" class="mw-redirect" title="Allies (World War I)">Allies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_329_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_329-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On April 22, a senior Ottoman official tipped off local Armenian and Assyrian clergy, saying, "hide the letters, papers, and books that you have which contain political news or are written in <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> or <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>. The government intends to search minutely for such material and to punish severely those who have such material in their possession."<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_330_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_330-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Meeting">The Meeting</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: The Meeting">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Soon after the <a href="/wiki/Deportation_of_Armenian_intellectuals_on_24_April_1915" title="Deportation of Armenian intellectuals on 24 April 1915">April 24th mass arrest of Armenian intellectuals in Istanbul</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Diyarbekir_Vilayet" class="mw-redirect" title="Diyarbekir Vilayet">Vali of Diyarbekir</a>, <a href="/wiki/Re%C5%9Fit_Bey" class="mw-redirect" title="Reşit Bey">Reşit Bey</a>, sent his close associate <a href="/wiki/Aziz_Feyzi_Pirin%C3%A7%C3%A7iz%C3%A2de" title="Aziz Feyzi Pirinççizâde">Aziz Feyzi Pirinççizâde</a> to convince the Turkish and Kurdish leaders of Mardin, who had refused to take part in the <a href="/wiki/Hamidian_massacres" title="Hamidian massacres">Hamidian massacres</a>, to participate in what would later be dubbed the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_genocide" title="Armenian genocide">Armenian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Assyrian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Assyrian Genocide">Assyrian Genocides</a>. According to <a href="/wiki/David_Gaunt" title="David Gaunt">David Gaunt</a>, Feyzi was the son of Arif Pirinççizâde, who had organized the <a href="/wiki/Massacres_of_Diyarbakir_(1895)" class="mw-redirect" title="Massacres of Diyarbakir (1895)">Hamidian massacres in Diyarbekir Vilayet</a>. Like his father, Feyzi was known to be, "a fanatic Pan-Turk nationalist."<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_321_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_321-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Fr. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacques_Rh%C3%A9tor%C3%A9&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jacques Rhétoré (page does not exist)">Jacques Rhétoré</a>, a French Dominican priest interned in Mardin, a large meeting was held in Mardin on May 15, 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Feyzi accused the previous Vali of, "being soft on the Armenians and of being a close friend of the Armenian Bishop."<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_321_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_321-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Fr. Ishaq Armalé, Feyzi declared, "Let no Christian remain! He who does not do this duty is no longer a Muslim."<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Feyzi added, "The time has come to save Turkey from its national enemies, that is, the Christians. It is clear that the states of Europe will not punish us, because Germany is on our side and helps us."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Feyzi also mocked those who objected to murdering Christians, "You surprise me. What is holding you back? Is it the fear of one day having to pay for this? But what happened to those who killed Armenians in <a href="/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_II" title="Abdul Hamid II">Abdul Hamid</a>’s time? Today Germany is with us and our enemies are its enemies. This will surely give us victory in this war, and we won’t have to answer to anyone. Let us get rid of the Christians so we can be masters in our own house. This is what the government wants."<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Everyone present at the meeting was required to sign a petition that Mardin's Christians were traitors and needed to be eliminated.<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Preparing_for_Martyrdom">Preparing for Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Preparing for Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On May 25, 1915, Resit Bey arrived in Mardin and verbally ordered Hilmi Bey, the <a href="/wiki/Mutasarrif" title="Mutasarrif">Mutasarrif</a>, to arrest the Christian leaders of Mardin. Hilmi Bey responded, "I see no reason for Mardin's Christians to be arrested. Therefore I cannot comply with your demand." Hilmi then added, "I am not without conscience and cannot cooperate in the massacre of Ottoman subjects who are innocent and loyal to the government." Reshid Bey left the meeting determined to have Hilmi Bey replaced.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_332_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_332-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On June 1, 1915, Archbishop Maloyan met with Mar <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_Gabriel_I_Tappouni" title="Ignatius Gabriel I Tappouni">Gabriel Tappouni</a> the <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Syriac Catholic">Syriac Catholic</a> Archbishop of Mardin. Maloyan read aloud the letter he had written to prepare his clergy and faithful for martyrdom, then folded it, and gave it to Tappouni, with the words, "Keep this testament on you." Although Tappouni then tried to console him, Maloyan replied, "I know for sure that I and my congregation will be condemned to torture and death. I expect them to come and arrest us any day. It is unavoidable... Pray for me. I suspect that this will be the last time I will see you."<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_332_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_332-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Archbishop's letter read, "I urge you first of all to strengthen your faith and trust in the Holy Church which goes back to the teaching received from <a href="/wiki/Peter_the_Apostle" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter the Apostle">St Peter</a> whom <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> chose to be the rock on which He built His Holy Church and made from the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">Apostles</a>' and <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Martyrs</a> blood it's&#32;&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Sic" title="Sic">sic</a></i>&#93; foundation; from where could we be granted this great gift where our blood, we sinners, become worthy to be mixed with the blood of those Holy Saints... My dear children I entrust you in God and ask you to pray for me so that He may grant me the strength and courage, till the shedding of my blood, so I would spend this perishable time in His grace and love."<sup id="cite_ref-armeniancatholic.org.au_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armeniancatholic.org.au-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_and_trial">Arrest and trial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Arrest and trial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On June 3, Hilmi Bey was lured away from Mardin and a delegation of officials arrived from Diyarbekir with orders from the Vali and long lists of Christian leaders to arrest.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Between 3 and 4 June 1915, Archbishop Maloyan and 420 of the other leading Christians of Mardin were arrested<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> and imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Mardin_Castle" title="Mardin Castle">Mardin Castle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When he was brought to trial, Maloyan stood accused of forming and leading a <a href="/wiki/Terrorist_organization" class="mw-redirect" title="Terrorist organization">terrorist organization</a> of <a href="/wiki/Armenian_nationalism" title="Armenian nationalism">Armenian nationalists</a> and of hiding two boxes of guns and ammunition inside his cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_332_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_332-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Although the Archbishop easily refuted these accusations, he was told, "<a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Islam" title="Conversion to Islam">Become Muslim</a> and <a href="/wiki/Shahada" title="Shahada">declare it</a> or death will be your fate."<sup id="cite_ref-:1_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>To the outrage of those present, the Archbishop replied, "Muslim? There is no way I would reject my religion and my Saviour. I have been brought up in the Holy Catholic Church, assimilated the base of its truthful teachings from a young age and became proficient in its undisputable facts until I unworthily become one of its pastors. I consider the shed of my blood for my faith to be the sweetest thing to my heart because I know for sure that if I get tortured for the love of the One who died for me I would become one of the happiest blessed people and I will see my Lord and my God in heaven. You can only beat me and cut me into pieces but I will never deny my religion."<sup id="cite_ref-armeniancatholic.org.au_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armeniancatholic.org.au-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of those present was heard to cry, "Do you disdain our religion?!" Another slapped the Archbishop and shouted, "I swear to Allah to torture you and give you a violent death!"<sup id="cite_ref-:1_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Similarly outraged that the Archbishop had refused to convert to <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a>, Mardin's chief of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Gendarmerie" title="Ottoman Gendarmerie">Ottoman Gendarmerie</a>, Mahmdouh Bey, ordered that Maloyan would first be beaten and then severely tortured by <a href="/wiki/Bastinado" class="mw-redirect" title="Bastinado">bastinado</a> and the extraction of his toenails. At every blow, the Archbishop was heard to cry, "Oh Lord, have mercy on me! Oh Lord, give me strength!"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_335_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_335-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On June 7, 1915, Hilmi Bey returned to Mardin and, "began energetic efforts to free the Christians, but he was quickly deposed and temporarily replaced by one of the conspirators, Khalil Adib, a judge of the criminal court and leader of the town's <a href="/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress" title="Committee of Union and Progress">Committee of Union and Progress</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_335_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_335-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On June 9, 1915, Archbishop Maloyan and all the other prisoners were informed that the Vali, <a href="/wiki/Re%C5%9Fit_Bey" class="mw-redirect" title="Reşit Bey">Reşit Bey</a>, had summoned them and that they would be leaving for <a href="/wiki/Diyarbekir" class="mw-redirect" title="Diyarbekir">Diyarbekir</a> the following day. At this point,Maloyan and all the other prisoners realized that they were going to die.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Under the direction of Mardin's chief of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Gendarmerie" title="Ottoman Gendarmerie">Ottoman Gendarmerie</a>, Mahmdouh Bey, Archbishop Maloyan, along with around 400 other Christians, was force-marched into the desert on the night of June 10, 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the front of the caravan marched Mahmdouh Bey. Many of the 400 prisoners had visible signs of having been tortured. Some had bleeding feet and fingers from nails that had been extracted, broken bones, and wounds upon their heads. Some had to be supported by other deportees to be able to walk at all. At the end of the procession walked Archbishop Maloyan, who was in shackles, barefoot, and walked with a limp after being repeatedly beaten on the soles of his feet.<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Fr. Ishaq Armalé, “At the fall of darkness, Mardin residents could see soldiers going up to the fort and then returning to the prison. They carried iron rings, chains, and thick ropes. They called out the names of the prisoners one by one, and they tied them with ropes so that they could not flee… Then those who were thought to be Armenians were taken from the others. Rings were pressed around their necks and chains around their wrists. In this way they were bound, drawn, and chained for several hours… After having arranged the men in rows, they forced them out through the prison gates. Above them weapons and swords shined. The prisoners were kept totally silent. And a town crier cried out, ‘The Christian residents who leave their houses will be amputated and put together with their co-religionists.’ Then they trudged along the main street 417 priests and other men. Young and old, <a href="/wiki/Armenian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian people">Armenians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Christianity" title="Syriac Christianity">Syriacs</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chaldean_Catholics" title="Chaldean Catholics">Chaldeans</a>, and Protestants. When they passed the Muslim quarter, the women came out and joked. They insulted the prisoners. Children threw stones. When the prisoners came to the Christian quarter, the residents could not go out to talk or say farewell. Many stood by the railings on their roofs and wept, praying to God. ... The Christians shuffled in silence like pupils on their way to school. They made no sound. ... When they came to the western city gate, those monks that were still free and the American missionaries went out on the roofs to see their friends for the last time and say farewell. They found them in a tragic state, so that blood could clot in their veins and terror hold them in its grip. There could not have been anything more difficult for the eye to see or more painful for the heart than standing there and looking down on the many chained co-religionists. Every time anyone cast a glance at that street, he would be reminded of the noble archbishop, the venerable priests, and the march of the dear Christians."<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The column of deportees was escorted to a <a href="/wiki/Kurds" title="Kurds">Kurdish</a> village, Aderchek, near Cheikhan (Sheikhan), where 100 of their number were taken by Turkish troops to nearby caves,<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> "a religious cult-place about six hours from Mardin."<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the Sheikhan Caves, Mahmdouh Bey read out what he said was an Imperial <i><a href="/wiki/Firman" title="Firman">Firman</a></i> charging all Christians with treason and sentencing them to death. Those prisoners who converted to Islam, however, would be set free and allowed to return to Mardin. Otherwise, they would be executed within the hour.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_336-337_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_336-337-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Archbishop Maloyan replied that he preferred to die as a Christian than live as a Muslim. The vast majority of the deportees agreed with the Archbishop and, the few who did not, were taken away by local Kurdish villagers and brought before their <a href="/wiki/Shayk" class="mw-redirect" title="Shayk">Shayk</a> to undergo <a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Islam" title="Conversion to Islam">conversion to Islam</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of the Turkish soldiers present at the scene later recalled, "We have never seen people so strong in their faith. If the Christians had captured us and offered us the same chance to convert, we all would have become Christians."<sup id="cite_ref-catholicphilly.com_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicphilly.com-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Archbishop Maloyan ordered his priests to circulate among the other prisoners to give them <a href="/wiki/Absolution" title="Absolution">Absolution</a> and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Communion" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Communion">Holy Communion</a>. Then, he watched as his priests and faithful were massacred in front of him.<sup id="cite_ref-Urfa._2006,_page_336-337_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Urfa._2006,_page_336-337-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Archbishop  Maloyan was again told by Mahmdouh Bey, as a religious duty during <a href="/wiki/Jihad" title="Jihad">Jihad</a>, that his life would be spared if he recited the <i><a href="/wiki/Shahada" title="Shahada">Shahada</a></i> and converted to <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a>. The Archbishop replied, "It's strange that you ask me this again although I told you before that I would live and die loyal to my faith and can only boast about my Lord's noble Cross."<sup id="cite_ref-armeniancatholic.org.au_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armeniancatholic.org.au-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> This enraged Mahmdouh Bey, who drew his revolver and personally executed Maloyan with a pistol shot.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The Archbishop's last words after being shot were reportedly, "My God, have mercy on me! Into Your hands I commend my spirit!"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The local <a href="/wiki/Kurdish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Kurdish people">Kurdish</a> population stripped the clothes from the bodies of the victims, which remained untouched for five hours until they were all collected, doused with <a href="/wiki/Petrol" class="mw-redirect" title="Petrol">petrol</a>, and burned.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Perpetrators">Perpetrators</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Perpetrators">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Prosecution_of_Ottoman_war_criminals" title="Prosecution of Ottoman war criminals">Prosecution of Ottoman war criminals</a></div>
<h3><span id="Re.C5.9Fit_Bey"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Reşit_Bey">Reşit Bey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Reşit Bey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Walter Holstein, the Imperial German Consul at <a href="/wiki/Mosul" title="Mosul">Mosul</a>, learned of the deportation and murder of Archbishop Maloyan and every other member of the caravan via a letter which no longer survives, but which was written either by Hilmi Bey or Shefik Bey. Holstein immediately informed the German Ambassador in Constantinople, <a href="/wiki/Hans_von_Wangenheim" title="Hans von Wangenheim">Hans von Wangenheim</a>, who in turn informed Berlin. The <a href="/wiki/German_Foreign_Office" class="mw-redirect" title="German Foreign Office">German Foreign Office</a> then issued a formal protest through Ambassador von Wangenheim to the <a href="/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_(Ottoman_Empire)" title="Ministry of the Interior (Ottoman Empire)">Ottoman Minister of the Interior</a>, <a href="/wiki/Talat_Pasha" class="mw-redirect" title="Talat Pasha">Talat Pasha</a>. Through Ambassador von Wangenheim, the Imperial German Government demanded that <a href="/wiki/Re%C5%9Fit_Bey" class="mw-redirect" title="Reşit Bey">Reşit Bey</a> be removed as Vali of Diyarbekir, "lest his murderous policies lead to the total extermination of the Christians in his area."<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> Instead, Talat Pasha chose to send an ambiguously worded reprimand ordering an end to the killing of non-Armenian Christians to Reşit Bey,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> who ignored it,<sup id="cite_ref-armenianweekly.com_13-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-armenianweekly.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> remained at his post until March 1916, and was never otherwise reprimanded for his role in the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Genocide">Armenian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Assyrian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Assyrian Genocide">Assyrian Genocides</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Even though he is now known as the "Butcher of Diyarbakir",<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Re%C5%9Fit_Bey" class="mw-redirect" title="Reşit Bey">Reşit Bey</a> claimed, during a conversation with <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottoman Army">Ottoman Army</a> officer <a href="/wiki/Rafael_de_Nogales" title="Rafael de Nogales">Rafael de Nogales</a>, to bear no legal or moral responsibility for the systematic massacre of Christians in his province, as he <a href="/wiki/Nuremberg_defense" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuremberg defense">only followed orders</a> from <a href="/wiki/Talat_Pasha" class="mw-redirect" title="Talat Pasha">Talat Pasha</a>. According to De Nogales, "Talat had ordered the slaughter by a circular telegram, if my memory is correct, containing a scant three words: <i>Yak - Vur - Oldur</i>, meaning, 'Burn, demolish, kill'. The authenticity of that terrible phrase was confirmed by the press of Constantinople after the Armistice with the publication of a certain telegram which the Ottoman commission engaged in investigating the massacres and deportations had discovered among the papers of the <a href="/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress" title="Committee of Union and Progress">Committee of Union and Progress</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/S%C3%BCleyman_Nazif" title="Süleyman Nazif">Süleyman Nazif</a>, the former <a href="/wiki/Mosul_Vilayet" class="mw-redirect" title="Mosul Vilayet">Vali of Mosul</a>, had a very different opinion and testified after the Armistice, "The catastrophic deportations and murders in Diyarbekir were Reshid's work. He alone is responsible. He recruited people from the outside in order to perpetrate the killings. He murdered the <a href="/wiki/Kaimakam" class="mw-redirect" title="Kaimakam">Kaimakams</a> in order to scare all other opposed Muslim men and women; he displayed the corpses of the Kaimakams in public."<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On July 16, 1915, the Consul at Mosul, Walter Holstein, alleged in a report to the German Embassy that Reşit Bey had ordered the recent assassination of the <a href="/wiki/Kaimakam" class="mw-redirect" title="Kaimakam">Kaimakam</a> of <a href="/wiki/Midyat" title="Midyat">Midyat</a>, for having, "refused to let the Christians in his district be massacred."<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Taner_Ak%C3%A7am" title="Taner Akçam">Taner Akçam</a>, Reşit Bey ordered the assassination of many other Muslims, including fellow Ottoman officials and many civilians, for opposing the Genocides, including <a href="/w/index.php?title=H%C3%BCseyin_Nesimi&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hüseyin Nesimi (page does not exist)">Hüseyin Nesimi</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Kaimakam" class="mw-redirect" title="Kaimakam">Kaimakam</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lice,_Turkey" title="Lice, Turkey">Lice</a>, Ferit, the Assistant-<a href="/wiki/Basra_Vilayet" class="mw-redirect" title="Basra Vilayet">Vali of Basra</a>, Bedri Nuri, the <a href="/wiki/Mutasarrif" title="Mutasarrif">Mutasarrif</a> of <a href="/wiki/Al-Muntafiq" title="Al-Muntafiq">Müntefak</a>, Sabit, the <a href="/wiki/Kaimakam" class="mw-redirect" title="Kaimakam">Kaimakam</a> of <a href="/wiki/Be%C5%9Firi" title="Beşiri">Beşiri</a>, and the journalist Īsmail Mestan.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>All jewellery and other possessions that Reşit Bey "requisitioned" from massacred Christians were, in theory, to be forwarded to the central government's treasury. Talat Pasha's concern for these valuables resulted in an investigation into Reşit for embezzlement, which found that he had amassed a personal fortune from the killings. For this reason, in 
March 1916, Talat Pasha had Reşit removed from his post.<sup id="cite_ref-Akçam_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Akçam-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/S%C3%BCleyman_Nazif" title="Süleyman Nazif">Süleyman Nazif</a> later commented, "Talat Pasha dismissed Reşit as a thief, while he adored him as murderer".<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 5 November 1918, a little less than a week after the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> surrendered to the <a href="/wiki/Allies_(World_War_I)" class="mw-redirect" title="Allies (World War I)">Allies</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Armistice_of_Mudros" title="Armistice of Mudros">Armistice of Mudros</a>, Reşit was arrested. His role in both the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Genocide">Armenian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Assyrian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Assyrian Genocide">Assyrian Genocides</a> was exposed in the Constantinople press, though he continued to deny having ever committed a crime. He escaped from imprisonment in January 1919, but when government authorities cornered him he committed suicide by shooting himself in the head.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Hans-Lukas_Kieser" title="Hans-Lukas Kieser">Hans-Lukas Kieser</a>, despite being one of the worst Genocide perpetrators Reşit "is perceived as a patriot and martyr in official Turkish-nationalist diction".<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Ankara" title="Ankara">Ankara</a>, a boulevard is named in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-anderson_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-anderson-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Aziz_Feyzi_Pirin.C3.A7.C3.A7iz.C3.A2de"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Aziz_Feyzi_Pirinççizâde">Aziz Feyzi Pirinççizâde</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Aziz Feyzi Pirinççizâde">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After the defeat of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> in 1918, <a href="/wiki/Aziz_Feyzi_Pirin%C3%A7%C3%A7iz%C3%A2de" title="Aziz Feyzi Pirinççizâde">Aziz Feyzi Pirinççizâde</a> transferred his support from Sultan <a href="/wiki/Mehmed_VI" title="Mehmed VI">Mehmed VI</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Government_of_the_Grand_National_Assembly" title="Government of the Grand National Assembly">Turkish Republican Government</a> based in <a href="/wiki/Ankara_Vilayet" class="mw-redirect" title="Ankara Vilayet">Ankara Vilayet</a>. On 15 January 1919, Feyzi was arrested and charged with involvement in the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Genocide">Armenian Genocide</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Her_Majesty%27s_Government" class="mw-redirect" title="Her Majesty&#39;s Government">British Government</a> deported him to the <a href="/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Malta" title="Crown Colony of Malta">Crown Colony of Malta</a> in May 1919, where Feyzi was held for two years awaiting <a href="/wiki/Prosecution_of_Ottoman_war_criminals" title="Prosecution of Ottoman war criminals">prosecution for war crimes</a> as part of <a href="/wiki/Malta_exiles" title="Malta exiles">Group A</a>, which was reserved for Turks and Kurds who were directly involved in the Genocides. In 1921, Feyzi escaped with 15 fellow prisoners, returned to Turkey, and joined the <a href="/wiki/Kemalism" title="Kemalism">Kemalists</a>. Later in life, Feyzi was a facilitator of massacres and forced resettlements of the <a href="/wiki/Kurdish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Kurdish people">Kurdish people</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Talat_Pasha">Talat Pasha</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Talat Pasha">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Talat_Pasha" class="mw-redirect" title="Talat Pasha">Talat Pasha</a>, who is widely considered the mastermind of the Armenian, Assyrian, and <a href="/wiki/Greek_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Genocide">Greek Genocides</a>, fled to the <a href="/wiki/Weimar_Republic" title="Weimar Republic">Weimar Republic</a> in November 1918. An <a href="/wiki/Istanbul_trials_of_1919%E2%80%931920" title="Istanbul trials of 1919–1920">Ottoman Special Military Tribunal</a> tried, convicted, and sentenced Talat to death <i><a href="/wiki/In_absentia" title="In absentia">in absentia</a></i> for subverting the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire" title="Constitution of the Ottoman Empire">Constitution</a>, <a href="/wiki/War_profiteering" title="War profiteering">war profiteering</a>, and organizing <a href="/wiki/Genocide" title="Genocide">Genocide</a> against <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Greeks" title="Ottoman Greeks">Ottoman Greeks</a> and Armenians. On March 15, 1921, Talat was <a href="/wiki/Assassination_of_Talat_Pasha" class="mw-redirect" title="Assassination of Talat Pasha">assassinated</a> in <a href="/wiki/Berlin" title="Berlin">Berlin</a> by <a href="/wiki/Soghomon_Tehlirian" title="Soghomon Tehlirian">Soghomon Tehlirian</a> as part of <a href="/wiki/Operation_Nemesis" title="Operation Nemesis">Operation Nemesis</a>, a worldwide plan by the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation" title="Armenian Revolutionary Federation">Armenian Revolutionary Federation</a> to hunt down and liquidate all surviving perpetrators of the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Genocide" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Genocide">Armenian Genocide</a>. After a two-day trial, a German court found Tehlirian <a href="/wiki/Acquittal" title="Acquittal">not guilty</a> of murder by reason of <a href="/wiki/Temporary_insanity" class="mw-redirect" title="Temporary insanity">temporary insanity</a>, and released him. As efforts to prosecute the Genocide's perpetrators had ceased by this time, Tehlirian is considered a <a href="/wiki/National_hero" class="mw-redirect" title="National hero">national hero</a> by Armenians.<sup id="cite_ref-sarah_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sarah-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatius_Maloyan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Donald_Attwater" title="Donald Attwater">Donald Attwater</a>, "During the massacres by Turks and Kurds in the war of 1914-1918, the Catholic Armenians lost seven bishops, over one hundred priests, forty-five nuns, and thirty-thousand lay folk; over eight hundred ecclesiastical buildings and schools were pillaged and destroyed, and a dozen dioceses laid waste. Moreover, the formation of a <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Socialist_Republic" class="mw-redirect" title="Soviet Socialist Republic">Soviet Socialist Republic</a> in <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Armenia" class="mw-redirect" title="Soviet Armenia">Russian Armenia</a> cut off an indeterminate number of Catholics from their fellows. A conference of Armenian bishops at Rome in 1928 reorganized their Church in view of these events and of the conditions now obtaining."<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Since 1928, the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Istanbul" title="Armenian Catholic Archeparchy of Istanbul">Armenian Catholic Archeparchy of Istanbul</a>, based at <a href="/wiki/St._Mary_of_Sak%C4%B1za%C4%9Fa%C3%A7_Cathedral,_Istanbul" title="St. Mary of Sakızağaç Cathedral, Istanbul">St. Mary of Sakızağaç Cathedral</a>, has spiritually looked after all Catholic Armenians who remain living in the <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkish Republic</a> and who, as of 2008, numbered only 3,650.
</p><p>Archbishop Ignatius Maloyan was beatified in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on October 7, 2001.<sup id="cite_ref-Pope_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pope-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In a sermon for the occasion, Pope John Paul said, 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Archbishop Ignatius Maloyan, who died a martyr when he was 46, reminds us of every Christian's spiritual combat, whose faith is exposed to the attacks of evil. It is in the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> that he drew, day by day, the force necessary to accomplish his priestly ministry with generosity and passion, dedicating himself to preaching, to a pastoral life connected with the celebration of the sacraments and to the service of the neediest. Throughout his existence, he fully lived the words of St Paul:  "God has not given us a spirit of fear but a spirit of courage, of love and self control" (II Tim 1,14. 7). Before the dangers of persecution, Bl. Ignatius did not accept any compromise, declaring to those who were putting pressure on him, "It does not please God that I should deny Jesus my Saviour. To shed my blood for my faith is the strongest desire of my heart". May his example enlighten all those who today wish to be witnesses of the Gospel for the glory of God and for the salvation of their neighbour.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City State</a> commemorated the centennial of Blessed Ignatius Maloyan's martyrdom with a  postage stamp issued on the 2 September 2015.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> Fr. <a href="/wiki/Leonard_Melki" title="Leonard Melki">Leonard Melki</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Lebanese_people" title="Lebanese people">Lebanese</a> <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> priest who was deported in the same convoy and martyred at the Sheikhan Caves alongside Archbishop Maloyan, will be <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> in <a href="/wiki/Beirut" title="Beirut">Beirut</a> on 4 June 2022.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicphilly.com_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicphilly.com-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
