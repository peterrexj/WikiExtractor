<p><b>Liborius of Le Mans</b> (c. 348–397) was the second <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Mans">Bishop of Le Mans</a>. He is the patron saint of the cathedral and archdiocese of <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>. The year of his birth is unknown; he died in 397, reputedly on 23 July.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Le_Mans_and_Paderborn">Le Mans and Paderborn</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Liborius_of_Le_Mans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Le Mans and Paderborn">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As for other fourth-century saints, little is known of his life. He was a <a href="/wiki/Gauls" title="Gauls">Gaul</a>, influenced by Latin culture. He is said to have been Bishop of Le Mans for 49 years. He built some churches in its neighbourhood, an indication that his missionary activity was limited to the <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> of his time. He is said to have ordained, in the course of 96 ordinations, 217 priests and 186 deacons. Saint <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a> assisted him when he was dying. He was buried in the Apostle Basilica of Le Mans, beside his predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Julian_of_Le_Mans" title="Julian of Le Mans">Julian</a>, the founder of the bishopric.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Miracles are said to have occurred at his tomb. In 835 Bishop Aldrich placed some relics of his body into an altar in the cathedral, and in the following year, on the instructions of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a>, sent the body to Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Badurad_of_Paderborn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Badurad of Paderborn (page does not exist)">Badurad of Paderborn</a><sup class="noprint" style="font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badurad" class="extiw" title="de:Badurad">de</a>&#93;</sup>, a diocese founded in 799 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_III" title="Pope Leo III">Pope Leo III</a> and Emperor <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> that had no saint of its own.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From this arose a "love bond of lasting brotherhood" that has survived all the hostilities of the succeeding centuries and is considered to be the oldest contract still in force. Both churches bound themselves to help each other by prayer and material assistance, as they have in fact done on more than one occasion.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In view of the power that veneration of Saint Liborius has had in binding peoples together, Archbishop Johannes Joachim Degenhardt of Paderborn established in 1977 the Saint Liborius Medal for Unity and Peace, which is conferred every five years on someone who has contributed to the unity of Europe on Christian principles.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage_and_symbology">Patronage and symbology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Liborius_of_Le_Mans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Patronage and symbology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Since Liborius died in the arms of his friend Martin of Tours, he is looked to as a patron of a good death.  Since the thirteenth century he is prayed to for assistance against that <a href="/wiki/Gallstone" title="Gallstone">gallstones</a> that are caused by the water of the limestone area; the first account of a healing of this kind concerns the cure of Archbishop Werner von Eppstein, who came on pilgrimage to the saint's shrine in 1267. This is the origin of the saint's attribute of three stones placed on a copy of the Bible. In the same period he became the patron of the cathedral and the archdiocese, rather than the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Kilian" title="Saint Kilian">Saint Kilian</a>, who were previously in first place. And as stated above, he is seen as a patron of peace and understanding among peoples.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is invoked against <a href="/wiki/Renal_colic" title="Renal colic">colic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Fever" title="Fever">fever</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gallstone" title="Gallstone">gallstones</a>.
</p><p>As well as being shown as a bishop carrying small stones on a book, Saint Liborius is also shown with the attribute of a <a href="/wiki/Peacock" class="mw-redirect" title="Peacock">peacock</a>, because of a legend that, when his body was brought to Paderborn, a peacock guided the bearers.
</p><p>The popularity of the saint in Paderborn is shown in the week-long yearly festival that begins on the Saturday after his 23 July feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Liturgy">Liturgy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Liborius_of_Le_Mans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Liturgy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St Liborius is a recognized <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> but his feast day was not included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>. It was added in 1702 as a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">commemoration</a> within the 23 July celebration of Saint <a href="/wiki/Apollinaris_of_Ravenna" title="Apollinaris of Ravenna">Apollinaris of Ravenna</a>. The 1969 <i><a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">Mysterii Paschalis</a></i> revision judged that he was not of sufficient universal importance for insertion in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> and that it should be left to local calendars to include him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Libori_Festival">Libori Festival</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Liborius_of_Le_Mans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Libori Festival">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Libori is a festival in <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a>, Germany, in honour of Saint Liborius, which takes place annually in the last week of July.
</p>
