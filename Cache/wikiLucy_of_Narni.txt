<p><b>Lucy Brocadelli</b> also known as <i>Lucy of Narni</i> or <i>Lucy of Narnia</i>, (13 December 1476, in Narni – 15 November 1544, in Ferrara) was a <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Dominican tertiary</a> who was famed as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Stigmatic" class="mw-redirect" title="Stigmatic">stigmatic</a>. She has been venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> since 1710. She is known for being the counselor of the <a href="/wiki/Dukes_of_Ferrara_and_of_Modena" class="mw-redirect" title="Dukes of Ferrara and of Modena">Duke of Ferrara</a>, for founding <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convents</a> in two different and distant <a href="/wiki/City-state" title="City-state">city-states</a> and for her remains being solemnly returned to her home city of Narni on 26 May 1935, 391 years after her death.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_Brocadelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lucy was born in 13 December 1476 on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Lucy" title="Saint Lucy">Saint Lucy</a>, the eldest of eleven children of Bartolomeo Brocadelli and Gentilina Cassio, in the town of <a href="/wiki/Narni" title="Narni">Narni</a> (then called <i>Narnia</i>) and in the region of <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>. A pious child, she is said to have received visions from an early age.<sup id="cite_ref-Bullivant_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bullivant-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When she was twelve years old, Lucy made a private <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">vow</a> of <a href="/wiki/Perpetual_virginity_of_Mary" title="Perpetual virginity of Mary">chastity</a>, and she determined to become a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>. 
</p><p>Circumstances, however, changed to make doing so impossible as her father died the following year, leaving her in the care of an uncle. This uncle, following the wish of her father while he was still alive, decided that the best course of action he could take would be to get Lucy married as quickly as possible. He made several attempts to do so. One of these included holding a large family party. He had invited the man he had chosen to become Lucy's husband to the party, with the intention of having the couple publicly <a href="/wiki/Betrothal" class="mw-redirect" title="Betrothal">betrothed</a>. He however had not informed Lucy of his intentions. The suitor made an attempt to put a ring on Lucy's finger, only to be slapped repeatedly by her for his efforts.<sup id="cite_ref-Narnia_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narnia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A later attempt involved <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">Count</a> Pietro di Alessio of Milan, an acquaintance of the family. Lucy was actually quite fond of him, but felt that her earlier vow of perpetual virginity made the marriage impossible. The strain Lucy felt as a result of the conflicting feelings made her seriously ill. During this time, the Virgin Mary and Saint Dominic again appeared to her, this time accompanied by St. <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>. They reportedly advised Lucy to contract a legal marriage to Pietro, but to explain that her vow of virginity would have to be respected and not violated. Pietro agreed to the terms, and the marriage was formalized.<sup id="cite_ref-Bullivant_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bullivant-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="A_spiritual_marriage">A spiritual marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_Brocadelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: A spiritual marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1491 Lucy became Pietro's legal wife and the mistress of his household, which included a number of servants and a busy social calendar. Despite her busy social schedule as a <a href="/wiki/Countess" class="mw-redirect" title="Countess">Countess</a>, Lucy made great efforts to instruct the servants in the Catholic faith and soon became well known locally for her charity to the poor. Pietro observed Lucy's behavior, and occasional quirks, quite indulgently. He never objected when she gave away clothing and food. Nor when she performed austere penances, which included regularly wearing a <a href="/wiki/Hair_shirt" class="mw-redirect" title="Hair shirt">hair shirt</a> under her garments and spending most of the night in prayer and helping the poor. He also seemed to have taken in stride the story he was told by the servants that Lucy was often visited in the evenings by Saint Catherine, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Agnes" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Agnes">Saint Agnes</a>, and Saint <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Montepulciano" title="Agnes of Montepulciano">Agnes of Montepulciano</a>, who helped her make bread for the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-Bullivant_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bullivant-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, when one of the servants came up to him one day and told him that Lucy was privately entertaining a handsome young man she appeared to be quite familiar with, he did react. He took up his sword and went to see who this person was. When he arrived, he found Lucy contemplating a large <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a>. The servant told him that the man he had seen Lucy with looked like the figure on the crucifix.<sup id="cite_ref-Narnia_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narnia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Later Lucy left one night for a local <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> friary, only to find it closed. She returned home the following morning, stating that she had been led back by two saints. That was enough for Pietro. He had her locked away for the bulk of one <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lenten</a> season. She was visited only by servants who brought her food. When <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> arrived, however, she managed to escape from Pietro back to her mother's house and, on 8 May 1494, became a Dominican tertiary. Pietro expressed his disapproval of this in a rather dramatic form—by burning down the monastery of the prior who had given her the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> of the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-Fullerton_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fullerton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Stigmata">Stigmata</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_Brocadelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Stigmata">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1495 Lucy went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and joined a group of Dominican tertiaries who were living in community. The next year she was sent to <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a> to establish a new convent and there she found she was frequently the object of unwanted attention. It was there, on 25 February 1496, that she is reported to have received the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Fullerton_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fullerton-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Lucy did her best to hide these marks, and was frequently in spiritual ecstasy. The house had a steady stream of visitors who came to speak to Lucy, and, often, just to stare at her. Even the other sisters were concerned about her, and at one point called in the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> who watched Lucy go through the drama of the <a href="/wiki/Passion_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion (Christianity)">Passion</a> for twelve hours straight.
</p><p>The bishop would not make a decision on Lucy, and called in the local <a href="/wiki/Inquisition" title="Inquisition">Inquisition</a>. Reports here vary, some indicating that he referred the case directly to the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, who is said to have spoken with her and, with the assistance of <a href="/wiki/Columba_of_Rieti" title="Columba of Rieti">Columba of Rieti</a> (another mystic of the Dominican Third Order), ultimately decided in her favor, telling her to go home and pray for him. At that time Pietro also came to her, making a final plea to persuade Lucia to return with him as his wife. She declined, and Pietro left alone. He would himself later become a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and a famous <a href="/wiki/Preacher" title="Preacher">preacher</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Narnia_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Narnia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Lucy returned to the convent in Viterbo, she found that the Duke of Ferrara, <a href="/wiki/Ercole_d%27Este_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Ercole d&#39;Este I">Ercole d'Este I</a>, had determined to build a convent in <a href="/wiki/Ferrara" title="Ferrara">Ferrara</a>, a city about 230 miles north, and that, having heard of her, he determined that she would be its <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">prioress</a>. In the summer of 1497 he invited her to be the founder of this new community.<sup id="cite_ref-Mariani_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariani-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Lucy, the Dominican Order, and the pope all agreed quickly to the new proposal. The municipal council of Viterbo, however, objected, not wanting to lose their celebrated mystic. She had already been praying for some time for a way to create a new community of stricter observance, however, and agreed to go to the new convent.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conflict">Conflict</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_Brocadelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Conflict">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lucy's decision precipitated a conflict between Ferrara and Viterbo which would continue for two years. Viterbo wanted to keep the famous mystic for themselves, and the Duke wanted her in Ferrara. After extensive correspondence between the parties, on April 15, 1499, Lucy escaped secretly from Viterbo and was officially received in Ferrara on May 7, 1499. Thirteen young girls immediately applied for admission to her new community; the construction of the monastery began in June and was completed two years later, in August 1501. It contained 140 cells for <a href="/wiki/Religious_sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sisters">religious sisters</a> and the novices, but to fill it with suitable vocations proved to be very difficult.
</p><p>Lucy expressed the wish to have there some of her former friends from Viterbo and Narni. Duke Ercole, in September 1501, sent his messenger to Rome asking for the help of the pope's daughter, <a href="/wiki/Lucrezia_Borgia" title="Lucrezia Borgia">Lucrezia Borgia</a>, who was preparing to marry the Duke's son, Alfonso. She collected all eleven candidates whom Lucy had indicated, and sent them as a special wedding present to Lucy and the Duke, a few days ahead of her bridal party. She herself solemnly entered Ferrara on 2 February 1502.<sup id="cite_ref-Gardner_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gardner-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Duke petitioned the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> for some help for Lucy in governing her new community, and the bishop sent ten women from a local monastery to join Lucia's convent. For better or worse, these were <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> of the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a>, who were thus permitted to wear black veils, something Lucy and the other Sisters of the Dominican Third Order community were not. The differences in canonical standing and requirements between the two groups was to cause friction in the convent.
</p><p>Tensions were heightened when one of these black veiled outsiders, Sister Maria da Parma, was made the prioress of the convent on September 2, 1503. When Duke Ercole died on 24 January 1505, the new prioress quickly found Lucy to be guilty of some unrecorded transgression (most probably of her open and public support for the <a href="/wiki/Savonarola#Reformer" class="mw-redirect" title="Savonarola">Savonarolan church reform</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-Herzig_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herzig-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and placed her on a strict penance. Lucy was not allowed to speak to any person but her confessor, who was chosen by the prioress. The local <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Prior Provincial</a> of the Dominican Order would also not permit any member of the Order to see her. There are records that at least one Dominican, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Racconigi" title="Catherine of Racconigi">Catherine of Racconigi</a>, did visit her, evidently by <a href="/wiki/Bilocation" title="Bilocation">bilocation</a>, and that Lucy's earlier visitations by departed saints continued. In response to Lucy's insistent prayer, her stigmata eventually disappeared, which caused some of the other members of the community to question whether they had ever been there at all. This punishment was to last her entire life, or at least until 1541, when a niece of Girolamo Savonarola was elected prioress. When Lucy died on November 15, 1544,<sup id="cite_ref-Mariani_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariani-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> many people were surprised to find that she had not died years earlier.
</p>
<h2><span id="Death.2C_veneration_and_beatification"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Death,_veneration_and_beatification">Death, veneration and beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_Brocadelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death, veneration and beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:BeataLucy.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/BeataLucy.jpg/214px-BeataLucy.jpg" decoding="async" width="214" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/BeataLucy.jpg/321px-BeataLucy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/BeataLucy.jpg/428px-BeataLucy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>A fresco in <a href="/wiki/Narni" title="Narni">Narni</a> depicting Blessed Lucy</figcaption></figure>
<p>Suddenly everything changed. When her body was laid out for burial so many people wanted to pay their last respects that her funeral had to be delayed by three days. Her tomb in the convent church was opened four years later and her perfectly preserved body was transferred to a glass case.
</p><p>When the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Army" title="French Revolutionary Army">French Revolutionary Army</a> suppressed the convent in 1797, her body was transferred to the Cathedral of Ferrara, and again, on 26 May 1935, to the former Cathedral of Narni, as the diocese had been merged with another. Lucy was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a> on 1 March 1710.<sup id="cite_ref-Mariani_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariani-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_Brocadelli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sister Lucia has a very important part in <i><a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Foxes" title="Prince of Foxes">Prince of Foxes</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Shellabarger" title="Samuel Shellabarger">Samuel Shellabarger</a>, a historical novel which dramatizes her escape from Viterbo and which some consider Shellabarger's best.
</p><p>She also was possibly a source of inspiration for <i><a href="/wiki/The_Chronicles_of_Narnia" title="The Chronicles of Narnia">The Chronicles of Narnia</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/C.S._Lewis" class="mw-redirect" title="C.S. Lewis">C.S. Lewis</a>, as Lucy, a girl that believes and can see many things that other people cannot see. <a href="/wiki/Walter_Hooper" title="Walter Hooper">Walter Hooper</a>, a biographer of Lewis, has referred to the possible connection.
</p>
