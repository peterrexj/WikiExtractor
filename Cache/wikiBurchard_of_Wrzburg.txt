<p><b>Burchard of Würzburg</b> (in German Burkard or Burkhard) was an Anglo-Saxon missionary who became the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_W%C3%BCrzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Würzburg">Bishop of Würzburg</a> (741–751).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Burchard_of_W%C3%BCrzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> Benedictine monk possibly of noble birth, and educated at <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury_Abbey" title="Malmesbury Abbey">Malmesbury Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He left <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> after the death of his parents and joined <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a> (who may have been a relative) in his missionary labors, some time after 732. Burchard lived for a time at the Abbey of St. Peter in <a href="/wiki/Fritzlar" title="Fritzlar">Fritzlar</a>, founded by Boniface. Later he left to become a missioner in <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Boniface organized bishoprics in <a href="/wiki/Middle_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Middle Germany">Middle Germany</a>, he placed Burchard over that of <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg" title="Würzburg">Würzburg</a>.  As was customary at the time, his appointment was approved by <a href="/wiki/Carloman_(mayor_of_the_palace)" title="Carloman (mayor of the palace)">Carloman (mayor of the palace)</a>, who endowed the diocese with a number of benefits. His consecration can not have occurred later than the summer of 741, since in the autumn of that year, he was documented as officiating as a bishop at the consecration of <a href="/wiki/Willibald" title="Willibald">Willibald</a> of Eichstädt.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Zachary" title="Pope Zachary">Pope Zachary</a> confirmed the new bishopric in 743. Burchard appears again as a member of the first German council in 742, and at the general ouncil of the Franks in 747. The following year, as an envoy to Rome from Boniface he presented the results of the council to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zachary" title="Pope Zachary">Pope Zachary</a>. In 750, with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fulrad" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Fulrad">Fulrad</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Denis,_Seine-Saint-Denis" title="Saint-Denis, Seine-Saint-Denis">Saint-Denis</a>, he brought to Zachary the famous question of <a href="/wiki/Pippin_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin the Younger">Pepin</a>, whose answer was supposed to justify the assumption of regal power by the <a href="/wiki/Carolingians" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingians">Carolingians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 751, he resigned his see in favor of <a href="/wiki/Megingoz_of_W%C3%BCrzburg" title="Megingoz of Würzburg">Megingoz</a>, a Benedictine monk from St. Peter's Abbey in Fritzlar,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and retired to a life of solitude. In 752, he dedicated the Abbey of St. Andrew in Würzburg (which was later renamed in his honor). He translated the relics of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Kilian" title="Saint Kilian">Saint Kilian</a> to <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg_Cathedral" title="Würzburg Cathedral">his own cathedral</a> on July 8, 752.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Burchard died in 753.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Burchard_of_W%C3%BCrzburg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His relics were translated to the Abbey of St. Andrew in 986.
His feast day is 14 October.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
