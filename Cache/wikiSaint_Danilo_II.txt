<p><b>Danilo II</b> (<a href="/wiki/Serbian_Cyrillic_alphabet" title="Serbian Cyrillic alphabet">Serbian Cyrillic</a>: <span lang="sr-Cyrl">Данило II</span>) was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Serbs" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Serbs">Archbishop of Serbs</a> 1324 to 1337, under the rule of Kings <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Uro%C5%A1_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen Uroš III">Stephen Uroš III</a> (1321–1331) and <a href="/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_the_Mighty" class="mw-redirect" title="Dušan the Mighty">Dušan the Mighty</a> (1331–1355, crowned Emperor in 1345). As a Serbian monk, he was also a <a href="/wiki/Chronicler" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicler">chronicler</a>, active in court and Church politics, holding the office during the zenith of the <a href="/wiki/Nemanji%C4%87_dynasty" title="Nemanjić dynasty">Nemanjić dynasty</a>-era; he wrote many biographies which are considered part of the most notable <a href="/wiki/List_of_medieval_Serbian_literature" class="mw-redirect" title="List of medieval Serbian literature">medieval Serbian literature</a>. He was proclaimed <b><a href="/wiki/List_of_Serbian_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Serbian saints">Saint</a> Danilo II</b> (Свети Данило II) of the <a href="/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church" title="Serbian Orthodox Church">Serbian Orthodox Church</a>, and is celebrated on the same day as Saint <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch" title="Ignatius of Antioch">Ignatius of Antioch</a> on 2 January&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 20 December&#93;.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_work">Life and work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Danilo_II_(Archbishop_of_Serbs)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:1330s_fresco_of_Archbishop_Danilo_II_with_Prophet_Daniel.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/1330s_fresco_of_Archbishop_Danilo_II_with_Prophet_Daniel.jpg/220px-1330s_fresco_of_Archbishop_Danilo_II_with_Prophet_Daniel.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="314" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cf/1330s_fresco_of_Archbishop_Danilo_II_with_Prophet_Daniel.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="250" data-file-height="357" /></a><figcaption>Danilo II with Prophet Daniel.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Born around 1270 in the <a href="/wiki/%C5%BDupa" title="Župa">župa</a> of Pilot (present-day <a href="/wiki/Pult" title="Pult">Pult</a>, in northern <a href="/wiki/Albania" title="Albania">Albania</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> his given name has not been recorded, only that he belonged to a <a href="/wiki/Serbian_nobility" title="Serbian nobility">Serbian noble family</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1993103_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1993103-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was endowed with a fine intellect and a noble disposition; he had received an excellent education at the hands of the most learned men in <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Serbia" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval Serbia">Medieval Serbia</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Byzantium" title="Byzantium">Byzantium</a>. After his education, he joined the court of the Serbian king <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Milutin" title="Stefan Milutin">Stefan Milutin</a>, which he left around 1300 to join the monastery of Končul on the <a href="/wiki/Ibar_(river)" title="Ibar (river)">Ibar</a> river.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After his novitiate, he went to <a href="/wiki/Pe%C4%87" class="mw-redirect" title="Peć">Peć</a> at the request of the Serbian archbishop <a href="/wiki/Jevstatije_II" title="Jevstatije II">Jevstatije</a>, where he was made a presbyter priest.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038-39_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198038-39-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After that, he progressed quickly in the ecclesiastical hierarchy of the Serbian Orthodox Church: first as abbot of <a href="/wiki/Hilandar" title="Hilandar">Hilandar</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1306-1311</span>), then as bishop of <a href="/wiki/Banjska_Monastery" title="Banjska Monastery">Banjska</a> (1312-1315), and finally as archbishop from 1324 until his death in 1337.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198039_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDaniel198039-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He died on 19 December 1337 and was buried in one of the churches he had built in the <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Pe%C4%87_(monastery)" title="Patriarchate of Peć (monastery)">monastery of Peć</a>, Our Lady Hodegetria.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEThomson1993106_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomson1993106-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Danilo wrote biographies of Serbian medieval kings and archbishops, including the biography of Jelena, the wife of King <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Uro%C5%A1_I_of_Serbia" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen Uroš I of Serbia">Stephen Uroš I of Serbia</a> (1243–1276). His monumental work is referred to in the poetry of Serbian folklore as <i>knjige starostavne</i> (the ancient books) and <i>knjige carostavne</i> (the royal books). As a result of his work, many historical details concerning both the rulers of medieval Serbia and the members of the Nemanjić dynasty have been preserved.
</p>
