<p><b>Hugh</b> (died 730) was the son of Duke <a href="/wiki/Drogo_of_Champagne" title="Drogo of Champagne">Drogo of Champagne</a> and his wife Anstrudis. He entered the church and became <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">archbishop of Rouen</a> in 722.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_of_Rouen_(died_730)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hugh was the grandson of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Heristal" class="mw-redirect" title="Pepin of Heristal">Pepin of Heristal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Plectrude" title="Plectrude">Plectrude</a> on his father's side,<sup id="cite_ref-Monks_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monks-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and of <a href="/wiki/Waratton" class="mw-redirect" title="Waratton">Waratton</a> and Ansfledis on his mother's. Both Waratton and Drogo were <a href="/wiki/List_of_Mayors_of_the_Palaces" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Mayors of the Palaces">mayors of the palaces</a>. He was brought up by his grandmother Ansfled, while his father Drogo was duke in distant Champagne. Paul Fouracre regards the education of Hugh as an example of the important role of women in Frankish family fortunes and in politics generally.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>While still a layman, Hugh was endowed with <a href="/wiki/Jumi%C3%A8ges_Abbey" title="Jumièges Abbey">Jumièges Abbey</a>, which he entered as a monk in 718 under Abbot Cochin. He later became vicar-general of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Metz">diocese of Metz</a>. In 722, Hugh was elected to the vacant archdiocese of Rouen. In 723, he accepted charge of <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Wandrille" title="Abbey of Saint Wandrille">Fontenelle Abbey</a>. In 724, he took on the administration, together with his own, of the dioceses of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Paris">Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Bayeux" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Bayeux">Bayeux</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ordericus_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ordericus-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He used the revenue from his various benefices to promote piety and learning.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Towards the end of his life, Hugh retired to Jumièges, where he died on 9 April 730.<sup id="cite_ref-roman_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-roman-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was interred at Jumièges. In the ninth century his relics were relocated to Belgium in order to save them from profanation at the hands of <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a>. He is regarded as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is kept on 9 April.<sup id="cite_ref-Monks_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Monks-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-roman_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-roman-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
