<p><b>La Chaise-Dieu</b> (<small>French pronunciation:&#160;&#8203;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[la ʃɛz djø]</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Auvergnat_dialect" class="mw-redirect" title="Auvergnat dialect">Auvergnat</a>: <i>La Chasa Dieu</i>) is a <a href="/wiki/Communes_of_France" title="Communes of France">commune</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Haute-Loire" title="Haute-Loire">Haute-Loire</a> <a href="/wiki/Departments_of_France" title="Departments of France">department</a> in south-central <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. Its inhabitants are called <i>Casadéens</i>, from the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> name of the city.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Geography">Geography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=La_Chaise-Dieu&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Geography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>La Chaise-Dieu occupies a 1082 m <a href="/wiki/Butte" title="Butte">butte</a> which dominates a plain between the mounts of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Livradois&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Livradois (page does not exist)">Livradois</a> and <a href="/wiki/Velay" title="Velay">Velay</a>.  The closest cities are <a href="/wiki/Brioude" title="Brioude">Brioude</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ambert" title="Ambert">Ambert</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Le_Puy-en-Velay" title="Le Puy-en-Velay">Le Puy-en-Velay</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Senouire" title="Senouire">Senouire</a> forms most of the commune's eastern and western borders.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=La_Chaise-Dieu&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>La Chaise-Dieu means "the Seat of God" in French (from the <a href="/wiki/Occitan_language" title="Occitan language">Occitan</a> "Chasa Dieu") and is a reference to the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which was founded on the site in 1043 by <a href="/wiki/Robert_de_Turlande" title="Robert de Turlande">Robert de Turlande</a>, a kinsman of <a href="/wiki/Gerald_of_Aurillac" title="Gerald of Aurillac">Gerald of Aurillac</a> and canon of <a href="/wiki/St._Julian_of_Brioude" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Julian of Brioude">Saint Julian's</a> church at Brioude, nearby. Robert served an apprenticeship at <a href="/wiki/Cluny_Abbey" title="Cluny Abbey">Cluny</a> under <a href="/wiki/Odilo_of_Cluny" title="Odilo of Cluny">Abbot Odilo</a>, then served as abbot in the community he founded in the wilderness here, initially in the company of a repentant knight, Stephen.   The traditional date of the founding is 28 December 1043. The abbey had over 300 monks and 42 outlying <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priories</a> depending on it when Robert de Turlande died, probably in 1067. After his death, Robert was quickly canonized (1095) as <b>Saint Robert de Turlande</b> (also known as Saint Robert of Chaise-Dieu).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Chaise-Dieu continued to grow throughout the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, becoming the motherhouse of further congregations of Black Monks. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VI" title="Pope Clement VI">Pope Clement VI</a> began his vocation as a monk at Chaise Dieu and was the patron of the vast abbey church (built 1344–1350), a suitable setting for his tomb.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_(6).JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_%286%29.JPG/300px-La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_%286%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="300" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_%286%29.JPG/450px-La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_%286%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_%286%29.JPG/600px-La-Chaise-Dieu_JPG0_%286%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>The abbey church</figcaption></figure>
<p>The monks were driven out and the abbey secularized during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. Clement's vast abbey church, his tomb and the abbey cloister remain. The fresco of the <a href="/wiki/Danse_Macabre" title="Danse Macabre">Dance of Death</a> (ca 1470) is a famous example of this motif, which gained wide currency following the visitations of the <a href="/wiki/Black_Death" title="Black Death">Black Death</a>. The partners of the figures are skeletons and the parade strictly follows precedence of contemporary society, with Adam and Eve preceding all:  the pope then the emperor, the legate, the prince, the cardinal, the High Constable, the patriarch, the knight, then the abbot, the townsman, the merchant, the lady, ending with the lawyer, the minstrel, the clerk, the ploughman, the monk, the innocent child and the pilgrim.
</p><p>Between 1727 and 1740, the <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenist</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Jean_Soanen" title="Jean Soanen">Jean Soanen</a> was exiled to the abbey.
</p>
