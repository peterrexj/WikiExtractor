<p><b>Franciszek Dachtera</b> (22 September 1910 – 22 August 1942) was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish people">Polish</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He was imprisoned in the Nazi concentration camps at <a href="/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp" title="Buchenwald concentration camp">Buchenwald</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp" title="Dachau concentration camp">Dachau</a>, where he died after being tortured in medical experiments. He is one of the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Martyrs of World War II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Faith">Faith</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Franciszek_Dachtera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Faith">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Dachtera was ordained as a priest in 1933.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="War">War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Franciszek_Dachtera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In August 1939 he was mobilized as a <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> to the 62nd Wielkopolska Infantry Regiment in <a href="/wiki/Bydgoszcz" title="Bydgoszcz">Bydgoszcz</a>. During the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Bzura" title="Battle of the Bzura">Battle of the Bzura</a> he was taken prisoner by the Nazis, and was a prisoner in Oflag IX C Rotenburg starting on 10 December 1939. In April 1940 he was stripped of status as a POW and taken to <a href="/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp" title="Buchenwald concentration camp">Buchenwald concentration camp</a>, where he arrived on 25 April 1940, then in July 1942 he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp" title="Dachau concentration camp">Dachau</a>. Despite his suffering, he remained faithful and maintained a cheerful demeanor, earning the nickname Cherubnik. In December 1942 he was selected for cruel medical experiments done by war criminal <a href="/wiki/Claus_Schilling" title="Claus Schilling">Claus Schilling</a>. He was intentionally infected with malaria and not given treatment, resulting in him suffering from jaundice, liver failure, spleen failure, and severe pain. Greatly weakened by the experiments, he died on 22 August 1944, probably from a lethal injection. His body was burned in the crematorium.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Posthumous_honors">Posthumous honors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Franciszek_Dachtera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Posthumous honors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was awarded the „Za udział w wojnie obronnej 1939” medal in 1985, <a href="/wiki/Cross_of_Merit_(Poland)" title="Cross of Merit (Poland)">Silver Cross of Merit with a rosette</a> in 1992. There are plaques bearing his name on several churches in Poland, and a street was named after him in 2016. Two survivors of the Dachau concentration camp who knew him nominated him for the <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> title when the beatification process was announced, and in 1999 he became one of the <a href="/wiki/108_Martyrs_of_World_War_II" title="108 Martyrs of World War II">108 Blessed Polish Martyrs</a>.
</p>
