<p><b>Mary Euphrasia Pelletier</b> (31 July 1796 in <a href="/wiki/Noirmoutier-en-l%27%C3%8Ele" title="Noirmoutier-en-l&#39;Île">Noirmoutier-en-l'Île</a> – 24 April 1868 in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>), born <b>Rose Virginie Pelletier</b>, was a French <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, best known as the foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity_of_the_Good_Shepherd" title="Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd">Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd</a>.
</p><p>Pelletier was born on an island off the coast of France where her parents had been exiled by the French Revolutionaries. At the age of eighteen, she joined the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity" title="Order of Our Lady of Charity">Order of Our Lady of Charity</a> who cared for girls and women in difficulty. Some of the girls were abandoned by their families or orphaned, some had turned to prostitution in order to survive. The Sisters of Our Lady of Charity provided shelter, food, vocational training and an opportunity for these girls and women to turn their lives around. Pelletier formed the Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd to expand this apostolate to wherever needed.
</p><p>Pelletier died in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, in 1868 and was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> in 1940. Her feast day is 24 April.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg/160px-Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="224" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg/240px-Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg/320px-Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Young.jpg 2x" data-file-width="572" data-file-height="800" /></a><figcaption>Mary Euphrasia Pelletier in her youth.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Pelletier was born on 31 July 1796 on <a href="/wiki/Noirmoutier" title="Noirmoutier">Noirmoutier</a> a small island off the northwest coast of France. Her parents had fled there thinking that they could escape the violence of the French Revolution.<sup id="cite_ref-granada_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granada-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was the 8th child of Julian and Anne Pelletier.<sup id="cite_ref-nz_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nz-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her father died when she was ten years old. In 1810 her mother placed Pelletier in a boarding school in Tours. Her mother died in 1813.<sup id="cite_ref-Clarke_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clarke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Near the boarding school was the convent of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity" title="Order of Our Lady of Charity">Order of Our Lady of Charity of the Refuge</a>, a religious Congregation founded by <a href="/wiki/John_Eudes" title="John Eudes">John Eudes</a> to provide care and protection for women and girls who were homeless and at risk of exploitation. Despite her guardian's reservations Pelletier was allowed to join the sisters provided that she not make her vows before she turned 21.<sup id="cite_ref-nz_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nz-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She made her profession in 1816, taking the name of <i>Mary of Saint Euphrasia</i>. The sisters of the community had been dispersed at one point during the revolution; the majority had been imprisoned. Pelletier joined what was a community of elderly weary sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-oshea_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oshea-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A short time after her profession, she became first mistress of the penitents, and about eight years later was made superioress of the house of <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She founded a community, the "Sisters Magdalen" for women who wanted to lead a contemplative and <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">enclosed</a> life and would support, by their ministry of prayer, the different works of the Congregation.<sup id="cite_ref-granada_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granada-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is now known as the <i>Contemplatives of the Good Shepherd</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sisters_of_the_Good_Shepherd">Sisters of the Good Shepherd</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sisters of the Good Shepherd">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg/160px-Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="224" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg/240px-Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/21/Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg/320px-Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier_-_Sister.jpg 2x" data-file-width="572" data-file-height="800" /></a><figcaption>Mary Euphrasia Pelletier as Mother Superior of the Good Shepherd Sisters</figcaption></figure>
<p>The city of Angers asked that Pelletier establish a Convent of Refuge there. She established a house in an old factory and called it "Bon Pasteur" (Good Shepherd). In 1831 she was appointed as Mother Superior of the house in Angers. The congregation in Tours did not wish to expand to Angers, nor did the house in Nantes. Eudes had established his houses as separate and autonomous. Pelletier came to believe that if the work was to grow, that each house should be under the direction of a generalate. She founded additional convents in Le Mans, Poitiers, Grenoble and Metz.<sup id="cite_ref-pamphlets_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-pamphlets-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In April 1835, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> granted approval of the Mother-House at Angers for the institute known as <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity_of_the_Good_Shepherd" title="Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd">Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd of Angers</a>. Convents that developed for Angers would be part of the institute while those houses that did not attach themselves to the General Administration would remain Refuges. The development of the Generalate made possible the sending of the sisters to wherever they were needed. Convents were also established in Italy, Belgium, Germany, and England. The institute is directly subject to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>; Cardinal Odescalchi was its first cardinal-protector.<sup id="cite_ref-oshea_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oshea-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For some time, Pelletier had to deal with the opposition of the Bishop Angebault of Angers, who wished to exercise the authority of Superior General, although the constitutions of the Order did not provide for this. She was accused of ambition, of innovation, and of disobedience. Sometimes she was put in the position of addressing conflicting instructions from Rome and the bishop. Although she had the support of Rome, the local clergy tended to keep their distance from someone who had incurred the bishop's displeasure. According to Sister Norma O'Shea, the bishop's opposition, coupled with the deaths of a number of sisters and longtime supporters, made Pelletier's last years very lonely.<sup id="cite_ref-oshea_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oshea-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pelletier devoted herself to the work entrusted to her. By 1868, she was Superior General of 3,000 religious, in 110 convents, in thirty-five countries.<sup id="cite_ref-na_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-na-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> She died of cancer on 24 April 1868.<sup id="cite_ref-oshea_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oshea-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She is buried on the property of the Motherhouse of the Sisters of the Good Shepherd in Angers, France.<sup id="cite_ref-gs_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gs-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonisation">Canonisation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Canonisation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg/220px-Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg/330px-Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg/440px-Abbaye_st_Nicolas_d%27Angers.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>The Abbey of Saint Nicholas in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a> is the Mother-House of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Our_Lady_of_Charity_of_the_Good_Shepherd" title="Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd">Congregation of Our Lady of Charity of the Good Shepherd</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>On 11 December 1897, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> declared Pelletier "Venerable." She was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 30 April 1933 and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on
2 May 1940 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quotations">Quotations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mary_Euphrasia_Pelletier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Quotations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li><i>"Though old stars burn out and die, look to the new and even beyond."</i><sup id="cite_ref-cin_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cin-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></li>

<li><i>"Do well all that you do."</i></li></ul>
