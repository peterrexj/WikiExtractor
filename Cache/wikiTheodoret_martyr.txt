<p><b>Saint Theodoret</b> or <b>Saint Theodoritus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Θεοδώρητος</span>, "God given"; died October 22, 362) was a Greek-speaking <a href="/wiki/Syria_(Roman_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Syria (Roman province)">Syrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> who died a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> in <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> during the reign of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>. His life is recorded only by apologetic works. His relics were later taken to <a href="/wiki/Uz%C3%A8s" title="Uzès">Uzès</a> in the south of France and placed in the <a href="/wiki/Uz%C3%A8s_Cathedral" title="Uzès Cathedral">cathedral</a>, which is dedicated to him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodoret_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Emperor <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>, who was the nephew of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a>, made his uncle Julian a count and governor of the East. Hearing that in the treasury of one of churches of Antioch there was much wealth, Count Julian was determined to seize it and published a proclamation banning the clergy. Theodoret, a priest, who was keeper of the sacred vessels, refused to abandon his flock and continued publicly to celebrate the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">Divine Liturgy</a>. Count Julian ordered that he should be arrested and brought before him, with his hands bound behind his back. Count Julian accused him of having destroyed the statues of the gods in a previous reign. Theodoret retorted by reproaching the Count with his <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostasy</a>. Count Julian now ordered that Theodoret should be tortured, and in consequence, the most atrocious torments were heaped upon this heroic priest, who bore them all with courage for the sake of his <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">faith</a>. It was said that Theodoret spoke these words to Count Julian and to the magistrate: "O most wretched man," he said, "you know well that at the day of judgment the crucified God Whom you blaspheme will send you and the tyrant whom you serve to hell."<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was later <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on October 22, 362.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
