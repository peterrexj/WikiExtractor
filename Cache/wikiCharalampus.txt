<p><b>Saint Charalampos</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Ἅγιος Χαράλαμπος</span>) (also variously <b>Charalampas</b>, <b>Charalampus</b>, <b>Charalambos</b>, <b>Haralampus</b>, <b>Haralampos</b>, <b>Haralabos</b> or <b>Haralambos</b>) was an <a href="/wiki/Early_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christianity">early Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> in <a href="/wiki/Magnesia_on_the_Maeander" title="Magnesia on the Maeander">Magnesia on the Maeander</a>, a city in <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_province" title="Roman province">diocese</a> of the same name. His name <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Χαράλαμπος</span></span> means <i>glowing with joy</i> in <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>. He lived during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Septimius_Severus" title="Septimius Severus">Septimius Severus</a> (193–211), when Lucian was <a href="/wiki/Proconsul" title="Proconsul">Proconsul</a> of Magnesia. According to one source, at the time of his martyrdom in 202, Charalambos was 113 years old.<sup id="cite_ref-serb_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_martyrdom">Life and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charalambos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Charalambos was Bishop of Magnesia also known as the Assyrian Saint Mar <a href="/wiki/Zayya" title="Zayya">Zayya</a><sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and spread the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> in that region for many years. However, when news of his preaching reached the authorities of the area, the proconsul Lucian and military commander Lucius, the saint was arrested and brought to trial, where he confessed his faith in Christ and refused to offer sacrifice to <a href="/wiki/Idolatry" title="Idolatry">idols</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoam_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoam-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite his advanced age, he was tortured mercilessly. They lacerated his body with iron hooks, and scraped all the skin from his body.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoam_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoam-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The saint had only one thing to say to his tormentors: "Thank you, my brethren, for scraping off the old body and renewing my soul for new and eternal life."<sup id="cite_ref-serb_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the saint's <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, upon witnessing Charalambos' endurance of these tortures, two soldiers, Porphyrius and Baptus, openly <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">confessed their faith in Christ</a>, for which they were immediately <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> with a sword. Three women who were watching the sufferings of Charalambos also began to glorify Christ, and were quickly martyred as well.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoam_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoam-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The legend continues to say that Lucius, enraged, seized the instruments of torture and began to torture Charalambos himself, but suddenly his <a href="/wiki/Forearm" title="Forearm">forearms</a> were cut off as if by a sword. The governor Lucian then <a href="/wiki/Spitting" title="Spitting">spat</a> in the face of the saint, and immediately Lucian's head was turned around so that he faced backwards.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoam_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoam-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Apparently, Lucian and Lucius both prayed for mercy, and were healed by the saint, and became Christians.
</p><p>More tortures, the legend says, were wrought upon the saint after he was brought to <a href="/wiki/Septimius_Severus" title="Septimius Severus">Septimius Severus</a> himself. Condemned to death and led to the place of execution, Charalambos prayed that God grant that the place where his relics would repose would never suffer famine or disease.<sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After praying this, the saint gave up his soul to God even before the <a href="/wiki/Execution" class="mw-redirect" title="Execution">executioner</a> had laid his sword to his neck. Tradition says that Severus' daughter Gallina<sup id="cite_ref-gallina_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gallina-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> was so moved by his death, that she was converted and buried Charalambos herself.<sup id="cite_ref-serb_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charalambos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Skull" title="Skull">skull</a> of Saint Charalambos is kept at the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Saint_Stephen_(Meteora)" title="Monastery of Saint Stephen (Meteora)">Monastery of Saint Stephen</a> at <a href="/wiki/Meteora" title="Meteora">Meteora</a>. Many miracles are traditionally attributed to the fragments of his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, which are to be found in many places in <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> and elsewhere. The miracles have made this saint, considered the most aged of all the martyrs, especially dear to the people of Greece.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoarch_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoarch-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On some <a href="/wiki/Greek_islands" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek islands">Greek islands</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kourbania" title="Kourbania">bulls are sacrificed</a> on his feast day. "This festival is the most important popular activity of the village of <a href="/wiki/Agia_Paraskevi_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Agia Paraskevi (disambiguation)">Agia Paraskevi</a> and it combines a variety of happenings that regard the ritual of the bull' s sacrifice. [An agricultural group] revived this ancient custom in 1774. It was established as a reverence to St Haralambos, the protector of [the] agricultural group that organises [the] festival".
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Saint Charalambos is normally commemorated on <a href="/wiki/February_10_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="February 10 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">February 10</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> the exception being when this date falls on the <a href="/wiki/Saturday_of_Souls" title="Saturday of Souls">Saturday of Souls</a> preceding <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a> or on <a href="/wiki/Clean_Monday" title="Clean Monday">Clean Monday</a> (the first day of Lent), in which case the feast is celebrated on 9 February.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoarch_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoarch-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He is also revered in <a href="/wiki/Comit%C3%A1n,_Chiapas" class="mw-redirect" title="Comitán, Chiapas">Comitán, Chiapas</a>, México (in Spanish: <i>San Caralampio</i>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Charalambos&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In Greek <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> and <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a>, Charalambos is regarded as a priest, while <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russian</a> sources seem to regard him as a bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-orthoam_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthoam-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
