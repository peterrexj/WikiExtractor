					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Andrew Wouters</b> (<a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch:</a> Andries Wouters) was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Netherlands" title="Catholic Church in the Netherlands">Dutch Catholic</a> priest who served as pastor in <a href="/wiki/Heinenoord" title="Heinenoord">Heinenoord</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hoeksche_Waard" title="Hoeksche Waard">Hoeksche Waard</a>. He was among the 19 <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_Gorkum" title="Martyrs of Gorkum">Martyrs of Gorkum</a> in 1572.
</p><p>Wouters and his fellow martyrs were canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> on June 29, 1867, after being beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a> in 1675. Wouters' feast day is 9 July.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Wouters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wouters was born in 1542. He was ordained a priest and lived a very scandalous life. He was a known <a href="/wiki/Promiscuity" title="Promiscuity">womanizer</a>, and fathered many children.
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_Revolt" class="mw-redirect" title="Dutch Revolt">Dutch revolt</a> against the Spanish, a <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinist</a> rebel group set themselves up to battle the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Netherlands" title="Catholic Church in the Netherlands">Catholic Church in the Netherlands</a>. In June 1572, <a href="/wiki/Gorinchem" title="Gorinchem">Gorkum</a> fell to the Dutch, and the mentioned rebels captured nine <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> friars, two lay brothers, and also a parish priest with his assistant. They joined two other imprisoned clerics. Four more, including Wouters, were added to the fifteen already captured in mid-June.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Imprisoned at Gorkum from 26 June to 6 July 1572, the nineteen were moved to <a href="/wiki/Brielle" title="Brielle">Brielle</a>, arriving on 8 July. The captured were forced to abandon their belief in <a href="/wiki/Transubstantiation" title="Transubstantiation">Transubstantiation</a>, and also their belief in <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">Papal supremacy</a>. All refused to renounce the faith. A letter from <a href="/wiki/William_the_Silent" title="William the Silent">William the Silent</a>, saying that all those in authority should leave the priests and brothers unmolested, was ignored by the Calvinists. The martyrs were hanged in 1572 from the roof of a turfshed.
</p><p>Wouters' last words were "Fornicator I always was; heretic I never was", referencing that he was a known womanizer, but refused to renounce the Catholic faith.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrew_Wouters&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wouters and his 18 fellow martyrs were beatified just over a century after their deaths, after many miraculous things were attributed to their intercession, including the curing of hernias in particular. Their canonizations were on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">Feast of Saints Peter and Paul</a> in 1867, as a part of the grand celebrations for the 1800th anniversary of the two apostles' martyrdoms.
</p><p>Brielle, the place of the martyrdom, was a site of many pilgrimages and processions before the martyrs' relics were transferred to the Church of Saint Nicholas in <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels, Belgium</a>.
</p>
