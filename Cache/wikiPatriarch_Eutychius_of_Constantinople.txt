<p><b>Eutychius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ευτύχιος</span>, <i>Eutychios</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 512 – 5 April 582), considered a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> Christian traditions, was the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">patriarch of Constantinople</a> from 552 to 565, and from 577 to 582. His feast is kept by the Orthodox Church on 6 April, and he is mentioned in the Catholic Church's "<a href="/wiki/Corpus_Juris_Canonici" title="Corpus Juris Canonici"><i>Corpus Juris</i></a>".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His terms of office, occurring during the reign of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Justinian the Great">Justinian the Great</a>, were marked by controversies with both imperial and papal authority.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eutychius_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eutychius' career is well documented: a full biography, composed by his chaplain Eustathius of Constantinople, was preserved intact.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Eutychius was born at Theium in <a href="/wiki/Phrygia" title="Phrygia">Phrygia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His father, Alexander, was a general under the famous Byzantine commander <a href="/wiki/Belisarius" title="Belisarius">Belisarius</a>. Eutychius became a monk at <a href="/wiki/Amasya" title="Amasya">Amasea</a> at the age of 30. As an <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">archimandrite</a> at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, Eutychius was well respected by <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Menas_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Menas of Constantinople">Menas</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a>. Eventually, on the day Menas died, Eutychius was nominated by <a href="/wiki/Justinian_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Justinian the Great">Justinian the Great</a> as Patriarch.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="First_patriarchate">First patriarchate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eutychius_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: First patriarchate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">Pope Vigilius</a> was in Constantinople when Eutychius became patriarch.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eutychius sent him the usual announcement of his own appointment and a completely orthodox profession of the then-united Church. At the same time, the Pope urged him to summon and preside over the Church Council summoned to deal with the <a href="/wiki/Three-Chapter_Controversy" title="Three-Chapter Controversy">Three-Chapter Controversy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Vigilius first gave, and then withdrew, his consent to the Council.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In spite of the Pope's refusal, the council met on 5 May 553 at Constantinople, and Eutychius shared the first place in the assembly with <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Apollinarius_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch Apollinarius of Alexandria">Apollinarius of Alexandria</a> and Domninus of Antioch (called "<a href="/w/index.php?title=Domnus_III&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Domnus III (page does not exist)">Domnus III</a>", see <a href="/wiki/List_of_Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchs_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Greek Orthodox Patriarchs of Antioch">List of Greek Orthodox Patriarchs of Antioch</a>). At the second session, the pope excused himself again on the grounds of ill health. The subscription of Eutychius to the Acts of this synod, which was later recognized as the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Constantinople" title="Second Council of Constantinople">Fifth General Council</a> and which concluded on 2 June 553, is a summary of the decrees against the Three Chapters.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eutychius had, so far, stood by the Emperor throughout. He composed the decree of the Council against The Chapters. In 562, he consecrated the new church of <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, Eutychius came into violent collision with Justinian in 564, when the Emperor adopted the tenets of the <a href="/wiki/Aphthartodocetae" title="Aphthartodocetae">Aphthartodocetae</a>, a sect of <a href="/wiki/Non-Chalcedonians" class="mw-redirect" title="Non-Chalcedonians">Non-Chalcedonians</a> who believed that <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>'s body on earth was incorruptible (<span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">’aphthorá</i></span>) and subject to no pain.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eutychius, in a long address, argued the incompatibility of the Aphthartodocetic beliefs with Scripture. Emperor Justinian insisted that he subscribe to it anyway. When Eutychius refused to compromise, Justinian ordered his arrest. On 22 January 565, Eutychius was celebrating the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Timothy" title="Saint Timothy">Saint Timothy</a> in <a href="/wiki/Little_Hagia_Sophia" title="Little Hagia Sophia">the church</a> adjoining the <a href="/wiki/Hormisdas_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Hormisdas Palace">Hormisdas Palace</a> when soldiers broke into the patriarchal residence, entered the church, and carried him away.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_and_exile">Arrest and exile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eutychius_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Arrest and exile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eutychius was first removed to a monastery called Choracudis, and the next day to the monastery of <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Osias&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Osias (page does not exist)">St. Osias</a> near <a href="/wiki/Chalcedon" title="Chalcedon">Chalcedon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFortescue1909-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eight days later Justinian called an assembly of princes and prelates, to which he summoned Eutychius. The charges against him were trivial: that he used ointments, ate "delicate meats", and prayed for long periods.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> After being summoned three times, Eutychius replied that he would only come if he were to be judged canonically, in his own dignity, and in command of his clergy. Condemned by default, he was sent to an island in the <a href="/wiki/Propontis" class="mw-redirect" title="Propontis">Propontis</a> named <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Principus</a> ("Prince's Island"), and later to his old monastery at <a href="/wiki/Amasya" title="Amasya">Amasea</a>, where he spent 12 years and 5 months.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_and_second_patriarchate">Return and second patriarchate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eutychius_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Return and second patriarchate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon the death of <a href="/wiki/John_Scholasticus" title="John Scholasticus">John Scholasticus</a>, whom Justinian had put in the patriarchal chair, the people of Constantinople demanded the return of Eutychius. <a href="/wiki/Justin_II" title="Justin II">Justin II</a> had succeeded Justinian in 565 and had associated with himself the young <a href="/wiki/Tiberius_II_Constantine" title="Tiberius II Constantine">Tiberius</a>, who was  made <a href="/wiki/Caesar_(title)" title="Caesar (title)">Caesar</a> in 574. In October 577 the two emperors sent a delegation to Amasea to bring Eutychius back to Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Contemporary reports claim that as he entered the city, a large group of people met him, shouting aloud, "Blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord," and "Glory to God in the highest, on earth peace".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In imitation of the entrance of Jesus into Jerusalem (recorded in <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthew" title="Gospel of Matthew">Matthew</a> 21:1–11 and <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">John</a> 12:12–18), he entered the city on an ass's colt, over garments spread on the ground, the crowd carrying palms, dancing, and singing. The whole city was illuminated, public banquets were held, and new buildings were inaugurated.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The next day he met with the two emperors and was given "conspicuous honor" at the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Mary_of_Blachernae_(Istanbul)" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Mary of Blachernae (Istanbul)">Church of the Virgin</a> in <a href="/wiki/Blachernae" title="Blachernae">Blachernae</a>. He then proceeded to <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a>, mounted the pulpit, and blessed the many people. It took him six hours to distribute the communion because all of the people wished to receive it from his own hands.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Late_beliefs_and_death">Late beliefs and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eutychius_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Late beliefs and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Toward the end of his life, Eutychius maintained an opinion that after the resurrection the body will be "more subtle than air" and no longer a tangible thing.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This was considered heretical, because it was taken as a denial of the doctrine of physical, corporeal resurrection. The future Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, then residing at Constantinople as <a href="/wiki/Apocrisiarius" title="Apocrisiarius">Apocrisiarius</a>, opposed this opinion, citing <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Luke" title="Gospel of Luke">Luke</a> 24:39. Emperor Tiberius talked to the disputants separately, and tried to reconcile them, but the breach was persistent.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESinclair1911_3-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESinclair1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eutychius died quietly on the Sunday after <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>, at the age of 70. Some of his friends later told Pope Gregory that a few minutes before his death he touched the skin of his hand and said, "I confess that in this flesh we shall rise again",<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> (a rough quote of <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Job" title="Book of Job">Job</a> 19:26).
</p><p>Among his pupils was <a href="/wiki/Eustratios_of_Constantinople" title="Eustratios of Constantinople">Eustratios of Constantinople</a> who wrote a tract against <a href="/wiki/Soul_sleep" class="mw-redirect" title="Soul sleep">soul sleep</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
