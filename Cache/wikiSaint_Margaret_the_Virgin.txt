<p><b>Margaret</b>, known as <b>Margaret of Antioch</b> in the West, and as <b>Saint Marina the Great Martyr</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἁγία Μαρίνα</span></span>) in the East, is celebrated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> on 20 July in <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western Christianity</a>, on 17 July (<a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a>) by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and on <a href="/wiki/Epip" title="Epip">Epip</a> 23 and <a href="/wiki/Hathor_(month)" title="Hathor (month)">Hathor</a> 23 in the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church" title="Coptic Orthodox Church">Coptic Orthodox Church</a>.
</p><p>She was reputed to have promised very powerful <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgences</a> to those who wrote or read her <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">life</a>, or invoked her <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercessions</a>; these no doubt helped the spread of her following.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Margaret is one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>, and is one of the saints <a href="/wiki/Joan_of_Arc" title="Joan of Arc">Joan of Arc</a> claimed to have spoken with.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a 9th-century martyrology of <a href="/wiki/Rabanus_Maurus" title="Rabanus Maurus">Rabanus Maurus</a>, she suffered at <a href="/wiki/Antioch_in_Pisidia" class="mw-redirect" title="Antioch in Pisidia">Antioch in Pisidia</a> (in what is now Turkey) in around 304, during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic persecution</a>. She was the daughter of a pagan priest named Aedesius. Her mother having died soon after her birth, Margaret was nursed by a Christian woman five or six leagues (6.9–8.3 miles) from Antioch. Having embraced Christianity and consecrated her <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virginity</a> to God, Margaret was disowned by her father, adopted by her nurse, and lived in the country keeping sheep with her foster mother.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Olybrius, Governor of the Roman <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_the_East" title="Diocese of the East">Diocese of the East</a>, asked to marry her, but with the demand that she <a href="/wiki/Apostacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostacy">renounce</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. Upon her refusal, she was cruelly <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a>, during which various miraculous incidents occurred.
One of these involved being swallowed by <a href="/wiki/Satan" title="Satan">Satan</a> in the shape of a <a href="/wiki/European_dragon" title="European dragon">dragon</a>, from which she escaped alive when the <a href="/wiki/Cross" title="Cross">cross</a> she carried irritated the dragon's innards. Eventually, she was decapitated.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica" title="Encyclopædia Britannica">Encyclopædia Britannica</a></i>, Margaret's story is "generally regarded to be fictitious".<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> states that "even the century to which she belonged is uncertain".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Doubts about her story are not new: already in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographer</a> <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a> (author of the well-known <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i>) considered her martyrology to be too fantastic and remarked that the part where she is eaten by the dragon was to be considered a legend.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Greek Marina came from Antioch, Antioch in Pisidia (as opposed to <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> of <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>), but this distinction was lost in the West. From the east her veneration spread towards England, France, and Germany, in the eleventh century, during the Crusades.
</p><p>In 1222, the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Oxford" title="Council of Oxford">Council of Oxford</a> added her to the list of feast days, and so her cult acquired great popularity. Many versions of the story were told in 13th-century England, in Anglo-Norman (including one ascribed to <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Bozon" title="Nicholas Bozon">Nicholas Bozon</a>), English, and Latin,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and more than 250 churches are dedicated to her in England, most famously, <a href="/wiki/St._Margaret%27s,_Westminster" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Margaret&#39;s, Westminster">St. Margaret's, Westminster</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish church</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> of the British <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster" title="Palace of Westminster">Houses of Parliament</a> in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There is also a <a href="/wiki/Saint_Margaret_Shrine" title="Saint Margaret Shrine">Saint Margaret Shrine</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bridgeport,_Connecticut" title="Bridgeport, Connecticut">Bridgeport, Connecticut</a> in the United States.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>She was recognised as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, being listed as such in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> for 20 July.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
She was also included from the 12th to the 20th century among the saints to be commemorated wherever the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> was celebrated,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> but was then removed from <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">the general calendar</a> along with a number of other European saints through the apostolic letter <i><a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">Mysterii Paschalis</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> knows Margaret as Saint Marina, and celebrates her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 17 July. Margaret is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/July_20" title="July 20">20 July</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Every year on Epip 23 the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox church">Coptic Orthodox church</a> celebrates her martyrdom day, and on Hathor 23 the Coptic church celebrates the dedication of a church to her name.
<a href="/wiki/Saint_Mary_Church_(Haret_Elroum)" title="Saint Mary Church (Haret Elroum)">Saint Mary church</a> in Cairo holds a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> believed to be Margaret's right hand, previously moved from the Angel Michael Church (modernly known as Haret Al Gawayna) following its destruction in the 13th century AD.
</p><p>In 2022, Margaret was officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day she shares with <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria" title="Catherine of Alexandria">Catherine of Alexandria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Barbara" title="Saint Barbara">Barbara of Nicomedia</a> on 24 November.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Margaret of Antioch is a patroness of pregnant women, servant maids, and against diabolical infestations.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_the_Virgin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In art, she is often represented as a shepherdess, or pictured escaping from, or standing above, a dragon. While Western iconography typically depicts St. Margaret emerging from the dragon, Eastern Byzantine iconography tends to focus on her battle with the demon in her cell and depicts her grabbing him by his hair and swinging a copper hammer at his face.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
