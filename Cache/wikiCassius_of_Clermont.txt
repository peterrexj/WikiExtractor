<p><b>Saint Cassius of Clermont</b> is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the 3rd century. He was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Senate" title="Roman Senate">senator</a> who was converted to Christianity by <a href="/wiki/Austromoine" title="Austromoine">Austromoine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cassius was killed with Victorinus (a pagan priest who had also been converted by Austremonius),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Maximus, Anatolius, Linguinus, and others at <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont-Ferrand</a> by <a href="/wiki/Chrocus" title="Chrocus">Chrocas</a>, the chieftain of the <a href="/wiki/Alemanni" title="Alemanni">Alemanni</a>, who were invading <a href="/wiki/Roman_Gaul" title="Roman Gaul">Roman Gaul</a> at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Chrocus" title="Chrocus">Chrocas</a> is said to have killed a total of 6,266 Christians at Clermont at this time, according to tradition.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory of Tours mentions a Church of Saint Cassius the Martyr at Clermont.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
