<p><b>Germanus I</b> (c. 634 – 733 or 740) was the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a> from 715 to 730. He is regarded as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, by both the Orthodox and Roman Catholic Churches, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of 12 May.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He had been ecumenically preceded by Patriarch <a href="/wiki/John_VI_of_Constantinople" title="John VI of Constantinople">John VI of Constantinople</a>, and was succeeded in Orthodox rite by patriarch <a href="/wiki/Constantine_II_of_Constantinople" title="Constantine II of Constantinople">Constantine II of Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_I_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Theophanes_the_Confessor" title="Theophanes the Confessor">Theophanes the Confessor</a>, Germanus was a son of <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(ancient_Rome)" title="Patrician (ancient Rome)">patrician</a> Justinian, who was executed in 668.<sup id="cite_ref-ocafs.oca.org_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocafs.oca.org-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Justinian was reportedly involved in the murder of <a href="/wiki/Constans_II" title="Constans II">Constans II</a> and usurpation of the throne by <a href="/wiki/Mezezius" class="mw-redirect" title="Mezezius">Mezezius</a>. <a href="/wiki/Constantine_IV" title="Constantine IV">Constantine IV</a>, son of Constans II, defeated his rival and punished the supporters of Mezezius. Germanus survived the persecutions, but was made a <a href="/wiki/Eunuch" title="Eunuch">eunuch</a> by the victors.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Germanus was sent to a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>. He resurfaces as <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cyzicus" title="Cyzicus">Cyzicus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ocafs.oca.org_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ocafs.oca.org-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He took part in the 712 Council of Constantinople where decisions favored <a href="/wiki/Monothelitism" title="Monothelitism">Monothelitism</a>, abolishing the canons of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Council_of_Constantinople" title="Third Council of Constantinople">Third Council of Constantinople</a> (680-681). The Council followed the religious preferences of <a href="/wiki/Philippikos_Bardanes" class="mw-redirect" title="Philippikos Bardanes">Philippikos Bardanes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 713, Philippikos Bardanes was deposed by <a href="/wiki/Anastasios_II_(emperor)" class="mw-redirect" title="Anastasios II (emperor)">Anastasios II</a>. Anastasios soon reversed all religious decisions of his predecessor. <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_John_VI_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch John VI of Constantinople">Patriarch John VI</a>, strongly associated with Monothelitism, was eventually dismissed. On 11 August 714/715, Germanus was elected Patriarch of Constantinople. Germanus later helped negotiate Anastasius' surrender terms to <a href="/wiki/Theodosios_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodosios III">Theodosios III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 715, Germanus organized a new council propagating <a href="/wiki/Dyothelitism" title="Dyothelitism">Dyothelitism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anathema" title="Anathema">anathematizing</a> various leaders of the opposing faction. He attempted to improve relations with the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenian Apostolic Church</a> with a view towards reconciliation. The major issue of his term would, however, be the emerging <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm" title="Byzantine Iconoclasm">Byzantine Iconoclasm</a>, propagated by <a href="/wiki/Leo_III_the_Isaurian" title="Leo III the Isaurian">Leo III the Isaurian</a>. Germanus was an <a href="/wiki/Iconodule" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconodule">iconodule</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and played an important role in defending the use of sacred images during the iconoclastic crisis of his day, suffering exile for his opposition to the emperor, who considered reverence for these images a form of idolatry.<sup id="cite_ref-ncr_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After an apparently successful attempt to enforce the <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> of all <a href="/wiki/Jew" class="mw-redirect" title="Jew">Jews</a> and <a href="/wiki/Montanism" title="Montanism">Montanists</a> in the empire (722), Leo issued a series of edicts against the worship of images (726&#8211;729).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> A letter by the patriarch Germanus written before 726 to two Iconoclast bishops says that "now whole towns and multitudes of people are in considerable agitation over this matter" but we have very little evidence as to the growth of the debate.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Germanus either resigned or was deposed following the ban. Surviving letters Germanus wrote at the time say little of theology. According to Patricia Karlin-Hayter, what worried Germanus was that the ban of icons would prove that the Church had been in error for a long time and therefore play into the hands of Jews and Muslims.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford1_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford1-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Tradition depicts Germanus as much more determined in his position, even winning a debate on the matter with Constantine, Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Nacoleia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nacoleia">Nacoleia</a>, a leading Iconoclast. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_II" title="Pope Gregory II">Pope Gregory II</a> (term 715–731), a fellow iconodule, praised Germanus' "zeal and steadfastness".<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Germanus was replaced by <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Anastasius_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Anastasius of Constantinople">Anastasios</a>, more willing to obey the emperor. Germanus retired to the residence of his family. He died a few years at an advanced age in 740.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  He was buried at the <a href="/wiki/Chora_Church" title="Chora Church">Chora Church</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Nicaea" title="Second Council of Nicaea">Second Council of Nicaea</a> (787) included Germanus in the <a href="/wiki/Diptych" title="Diptych">diptychs</a> of the saints. He has since been regarded a saint by both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>Several of his writings have been preserved.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His <i>Historia Ecclesiastica</i> was a popular work in Greek and Latin translations for many centuries, and remains often quoted by scholars. Parts of it were published in English in 1985 as <i>On the Divine Liturgy</i>, described by its publishers as "for centuries the quasi-official explanation of the Divine Liturgy for the Byzantine Christian world".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  However the <a href="/wiki/Johann_Peter_Kirsch" title="Johann Peter Kirsch">Johann Peter Kirsch</a> is dubious that the work is actually by Germanus.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent.org_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent.org-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Influence">Influence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Germanus_I_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Influence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pope Pius XII included one of his texts in the apostolic constitution proclaiming Mary's assumption into heaven a dogma of the Church.<sup id="cite_ref-ncr_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncr-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Among his writings was the hymn "Μέγα καί παράδοξον θαῦμα" translated by <a href="/wiki/John_Mason_Neale" title="John Mason Neale">John Mason Neale</a> as "A Great and Mighty Wonder",<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> although Neale misattributed this to <a href="/wiki/Anatolius_of_Constantinople" title="Anatolius of Constantinople">Anatolius of Constantinople</a>.
</p>
