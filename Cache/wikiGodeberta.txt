<p><b>Godeberta</b> (Gothic meaning "fervor", 640–April 9 or 11, 670; also called <b>Gotheberta</b> and <b>Gothoberta</b>)<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-362_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-362-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>. She was born of "pious parents"<sup id="cite_ref-gould-163_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gould-163-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens, France</a>, north of <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>. Her parents were nobles attached to the king's court, so she was carefully educated.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When Godeberta was old enough, her father took her to the king's court to obtain permission to "marry her to someone of suitable rank and fortune".<sup id="cite_ref-gould-163_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gould-163-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Eligius" title="Saint Eligius">Saint Eligius</a>, who was present at court, was able to see that she wanted to "dedicate her virginity to God",<sup id="cite_ref-gould-163_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gould-163-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and took off his episcopal ring and placed it on her finger in the presence of the king, pronouncing her a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>. Godeberta refused offers of marriage by her noble suitors, and the king, impressed with her zeal and conduct, endowed Godeberta with a small palace in nearby <a href="/wiki/Noyon" title="Noyon">Noyon</a>, north of France, with a chapel dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a>. She turned her home into a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, where she was abbess for 12 young women. She chose Eligius as her spiritual guide.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-362_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-362-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-gould-163_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gould-163-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Godeberta lived a life of prayer and solitude at the monastery in Noyon for the rest of her life, leaving occasionally when "the call of charity or religion brought her forth among the people",<sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> many of whom were pagans. She also practiced penances, fasts, and believed in the efficacy of the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">sign of the cross</a>. According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>, in 676, when Noyon was threatened with fire, she made the sign of the cross over the flames and they were immediately extinguished, saving the town from destruction.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Dunbar reported that "her sanctity was shown by many miracles".<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-362_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-362-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Godeberta died in 670 at Noyon,<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-362_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-362-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> probably on June 11, although the exact date of her death is unknown. Her body was interred in the church of St. George, which was renamed for her after her death. In 1168, her body was translated from the church, which was in ruins, to the <a href="/wiki/Noyon_Cathedral" title="Noyon Cathedral">Noyon Cathedral</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, which included a bell that she used in her convent and the ring presented to her by Eligius, had survived. During the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, a "pious townsman"<sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> buried her relics near the cathedral, and were returned to the cathedral after the Revolution ended. Even though little historical details remain about Godeberta, she was seen as a protector during times of "plagues and catastrophes".<sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Catholic Encyclopedia</i> reports that in 1866, during an outbreak of <a href="/wiki/Typhoid_fever" title="Typhoid fever">typhoid fever</a> in Noyon, a leading citizen of the town whose child had died asked that Godeberta's relics be exposed, that a <a href="/wiki/Novena" title="Novena">novena</a> of intercession be practiced, and the town's inhabitants observe a three-day fast and wear sackcloth and ashes. The outbreak ended, and a few weeks later, her relics were paraded through the town and a statue of Godeberta was made to commemorate the miracle.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-362_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-362-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Godeberta's feast is celebrated on June 11; it is also celebrated in Noyon on the fifth Sunday after <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicencyclopedia_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicencyclopedia-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> of Noyon, France and is invoked against pestilence and rain. She is represented holding a ring.<sup id="cite_ref-dunbar-362_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dunbar-362-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1449, <a href="/wiki/Flemish_people" title="Flemish people">Flemish</a> artist <a href="/wiki/Petrus_Christus" title="Petrus Christus">Petrus Christus</a> painted <i><a href="/wiki/A_Goldsmith_in_His_Shop,_Possibly_Saint_Eligius" class="mw-redirect" title="A Goldsmith in His Shop, Possibly Saint Eligius">St. Eligius in His Workshop</a></i> (also known as <i>The Legend of Saint Eligius and Saint Godeberta</i>), which depicts Saint Eligius, Godeberta, and the king in Eligius' goldsmithing workshop, providing Godeberta the ring that espoused her to Christ. The painting is currently held at the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art" title="Metropolitan Museum of Art">Metropolitan Museum of Art</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
