					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Christians executed by Romans in present-day Tunisia (180 AD)</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above fn org" style="background-color:gold;">Scillitan Martyrs</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">2nd-century, North Africa</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">17 July 180, <a href="/wiki/Scillium" title="Scillium">Scillium</a>, <a href="/wiki/Africa_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Africa Province">Africa Proconsularis</a>; <br />Now within <a href="/wiki/Kasserine_Governorate" title="Kasserine Governorate">Kasserine Governorate</a>, Tunisia</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Martyred by</th><td class="infobox-data">Vigellius Saturninus</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Pre-congregation</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">17 July</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The <b>Scillitan Martyrs</b> were a company of twelve North African Christians who were executed for their beliefs on 17 July 180 AD. The martyrs take their name from Scilla (or <a href="/wiki/Scillium" title="Scillium">Scillium</a>), a town in <a href="/wiki/Numidia" title="Numidia">Numidia</a>. The <i><a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles_(genre)" title="Acts of the Apostles (genre)">Acts</a> of the Scillitan Martyrs</i> are considered to be the earliest documents of the <a href="/wiki/Christianity_in_Africa" title="Christianity in Africa">church of Africa</a> and also the earliest specimen of Christian <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-smith_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was the last of the persecutions during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>, which is best known from the sufferings of the churches of <a href="/wiki/Vienne" title="Vienne">Vienne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a> in South <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>. Marcus Aurelius died on 17 March of the year in question, and persecution ceased sometime after the accession of his son <a href="/wiki/Commodus" title="Commodus">Commodus</a>. A group of sufferers called the Madaurian martyrs seems to belong to the same period; in the correspondence of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">St Augustine</a>, Namphamo, one of their number, is spoken of as an "archimartyr," which appears to mean a <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a> of Africa.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_account">The account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Scillitan_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Acts of their martyrdom are of interest, as being among the most ancient Acts extant for the Roman Province of Africa.<sup id="cite_ref-Hassett_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hassett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The martyrs' trial and execution took place in <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a> under the <a href="/wiki/Proconsul" title="Proconsul">proconsul</a> <a href="/wiki/Publius_Vigellius_Saturninus" title="Publius Vigellius Saturninus">Publius Vigellius Saturninus</a>, whom <a href="/wiki/Tertullian" title="Tertullian">Tertullian</a> declares to have been the first persecutor of Christians in Africa. The trial is notable among the trials of early martyrs inasmuch as the accused were not subjected to torture.<sup id="cite_ref-Hassett_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hassett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Scillitan sufferers were twelve in all—seven men and five women. Their names were <b>Speratus, Nartzalus, Cintinus (Cittinus), Veturius, Felix, Aquilinus,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Laetantius, Januaria, Generosa, Vestia, Donata, and Secunda</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Two of these bear <a href="/wiki/Punic_language" title="Punic language">Punic</a> names (Nartzalus, Cintinus), but the rest are Latin names. Six had already been tried: of the remainder, to whom these <i>Acta</i> primarily relate, Speratus was the principal spokesman. He claimed for himself and his companions that they had lived a quiet and moral life, paying their dues and doing no wrong to their neighbors. But when called upon to swear by the name of the emperor, he replied "I recognize not the empire of this world; but rather do I serve that God whom no man hath seen, nor with these eyes can see."<sup id="cite_ref-smith_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The response was a reference to the language of <a href="/wiki/First_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="First Epistle to Timothy">1 Tim.</a> vi. 16. In reply to the question, "What are the things in your satchel?", he said "Books and letters of Paul, a just man." 
</p><p>The dialogue between the Proconsul and the martyrs shows that the former entertained no prejudices against the Christians. He exhorts them to comply with the law, and when they decline he suggests that they take time to think on the matter.<sup id="cite_ref-Hassett_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hassett-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The martyrs were offered a delay of 30 days to reconsider their decision, which they all refused. They were then put to death by the sword.
</p><p>The fame of the martyrs led to the building of a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> in their honor at <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and their annual commemoration required that the brevity and obscurity of their <i>Acta</i> should be supplemented and explained to make them suitable for public recitation.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Scillitan_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Agobard" title="Agobard">Agobard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lyons" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lyons">archbishop of Lyons</a> (c. 779–840) stated that the relics of Speratus, and those of Cyprian, were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> by <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne's</a> orders from Carthage to Lyons.<sup id="cite_ref-smith_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The historical questions connected with these martyrs were addressed by bishop <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Barber_Lightfoot" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseph Barber Lightfoot">Joseph Barber Lightfoot</a> in <i>Epistles of Ignatius and Polycarp</i>, 1885.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
