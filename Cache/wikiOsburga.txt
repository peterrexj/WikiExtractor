<p><b>Osburh</b> or <b>Osburga</b> (also <b>Osburga Oslacsdotter</b>) was the first wife of King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelwulf_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelwulf of Wessex">Æthelwulf of Wessex</a> and mother of King <a href="/wiki/Alfred_the_Great" title="Alfred the Great">Alfred the Great</a>. Alfred's biographer, <a href="/wiki/Asser" title="Asser">Asser</a>, described her as "a most religious woman, noble in character and noble by birth".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Osburh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Osburh's existence is known only from Asser's <i><a href="/wiki/Life_of_King_Alfred" class="mw-redirect" title="Life of King Alfred">Life of King Alfred</a></i>. She is not named as witness to any charters, nor is her death reported in the <i><a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle" title="Anglo-Saxon Chronicle">Anglo-Saxon Chronicle</a></i>. So far as is known, she was the mother of all Æthelwulf's children, his five sons <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelstan_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelstan of Wessex">Æthelstan</a>, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelbald_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelbald of Wessex">Æthelbald</a>, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelberht_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelberht of Wessex">Æthelberht</a>, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="Æthelred of Wessex">Æthelred</a> and Alfred, and his daughter <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelswith" title="Æthelswith">Æthelswith</a>, wife of King <a href="/wiki/Burgred_of_Mercia" title="Burgred of Mercia">Burgred of Mercia</a>.
</p><p>Osburh is best known from Asser's story about a book of Saxon songs which she showed to her sons, offering to give the book to whoever could first memorise it, a challenge which Alfred took up and won. This exhibits high-status ninth-century women's interest in books and their role in educating their children.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Osburh was the daughter of Oslac (who is also only known from Asser's <i>Life</i>), King Æthelwulf's <i>pincerna</i> (butler), an important figure in the royal court and household.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Oslac is described as a descendant of King <a href="/wiki/Cerdic" class="mw-redirect" title="Cerdic">Cerdic</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Jutes" title="Jutes">Jutish</a> nephews, Stuf and Wihtgar, who conquered the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
