<p><b>Saint Exuperius</b> (also <b>Exsuperius</b>) (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Exupéry, Saint Soupire<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></i>) (died c. 410) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Toulouse">Bishop of Toulouse</a> at the beginning of the 5th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Exuperius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His place and date of birth are unknown. Upon succeeding <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sylvius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Sylvius">Saint Sylvius</a> as bishop of Toulouse,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he ordered the completion of the basilica of <a href="/wiki/Saturnin" title="Saturnin">St. Saturnin</a>, a part of which was incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Sernin,_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Sernin, Toulouse">Basilica of St. Sernin</a>. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Jerome" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Jerome">Saint Jerome</a> praised Exuperius "for his munificence to the monks of <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Libya" title="Libya">Libya</a>, and for his charity to the people of his own diocese, who were then suffering from the attacks of the <a href="/wiki/Vandals" title="Vandals">Vandals</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alans" title="Alans">Alans</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Suevi" class="mw-redirect" title="Suevi">Suevi</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On behalf of the poor in his diocese he sold the basilica's altar vessels and was therefore compelled to carry the <a href="/wiki/Sacramental_bread" title="Sacramental bread">Sacred Offering</a> in an <a href="/wiki/Salix_viminalis" title="Salix viminalis">osier</a> basket and the <a href="/wiki/Precious_Blood" class="mw-redirect" title="Precious Blood">Precious Blood</a> in a vessel of glass. In respect of his virtues and in gratitude for his gifts, Saint Jerome dedicated his <i>Commentary on <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Zechariah" title="Book of Zechariah">Zacharias</a></i> to him.
</p><p>Exuperius is best known in connection with the <a href="/wiki/Christian_biblical_canons" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian biblical canons">Canon of the Sacred Scriptures</a>. He had written to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent I</a> for instructions concerning the canon and several points of ecclesiastical behaviour. In reply, the pope honoured him with the letter <i>Consulenti Tibi</i>, dated February 405, which contained a list of the canonical scriptures.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The opinion of <a href="/wiki/Baronius" class="mw-redirect" title="Baronius">Baronius</a>, that bishop Exuperius was the same person as the rector with the same name, is usually rejected, as the rector was a teacher of <a href="/wiki/Hannibalianus" title="Hannibalianus">Hannibalianus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dalmatius" title="Dalmatius">Dalmatius</a>, nephews of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine the Great</a>, and therefore from an earlier period than the bishop. From Jerome's letter to Furia in 394, and from the epistle of <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_Nola" title="Paulinus of Nola">Saint Paulinus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Amandus_of_Bordeaux" title="Amandus of Bordeaux">Amandus of Bordeaux</a> in 397, it seems probable that Exuperius was a priest at <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and later at <a href="/wiki/Bordeaux" title="Bordeaux">Bordeaux</a> before he was raised to the episcopate—though it is possible that in both of these letters reference is made to a different person. 
</p><p>The precise date of his promotion to the bishop is unknown. Evidence suggests that he occupied the See of Toulouse in February 405 (as is evident from the letter of Innocent I mentioned above). It is sometimes said that Jerome reproached him in a letter to Riparius, a priest of <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, for tolerating the heretic <a href="/wiki/Vigilantius" title="Vigilantius">Vigilantius</a>; but as Vigilantius did not belong to the diocese of Toulouse, Jerome was probably speaking of another bishop.
</p><p>Exuperius was venerated as a saint from early times. In the time of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> he was held in equal veneration with <a href="/wiki/Saturnin" title="Saturnin">Saint Saturninus</a>. His feast occurs on 28 September. The first martyrologist to assign it to this date was <a href="/wiki/Usuard" title="Usuard">Usuard</a>, who wrote towards the end of the 9th century.
</p>
