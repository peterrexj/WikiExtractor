<p><b>Solanus Casey</b>, OFM Cap. (November 25, 1870 – July 31, 1957), born <b>Bernard Francis Casey</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> and was a professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</a>. He was known during his lifetime as a healer, for his great faith and his abilities as a spiritual counselor, but especially for his great attention to the sick, for whom he celebrated special <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Masses</a>. The friar was much sought-after and came to be revered especially in <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit</a>, where he resided. He was also a noted lover of the <a href="/wiki/Violin" title="Violin">violin</a>, a trait he shared with his eponym, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Francis_Solanus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Francis Solanus">Saint Francis Solanus</a>.
</p><p>The cause for his sainthood, initiated by the laity who so loved him, commenced a few years after his death; he was officially recognized as <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">servant of God</a> in 1982, and he received the title of <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 1995. After a miraculous healing attributed to him was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> in 2017, he was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in Detroit at <a href="/wiki/Ford_Field" title="Ford Field">Ford Field</a> on November 18, 2017.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solanus_Casey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_studies">Childhood and studies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solanus_Casey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood and studies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bernard Francis Casey (nicknamed "Barney") was born on November 25, 1870, on a farm in the town of <a href="/wiki/Oak_Grove,_Pierce_County,_Wisconsin" title="Oak Grove, Pierce County, Wisconsin">Oak Grove</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pierce_County,_Wisconsin" title="Pierce County, Wisconsin">Pierce County, Wisconsin</a>, the sixth of sixteen children born to <a href="/wiki/Irish_diaspora" title="Irish diaspora">Irish immigrants</a> Bernard James Casey and Ellen Elizabeth Murphy. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on December 18, 1870. <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He contracted <a href="/wiki/Diphtheria" title="Diphtheria">diphtheria</a> in 1878, which permanently damaged his voice, leaving it wispy and slightly impaired; two of his siblings died from the disease during that year. The family later moved to <a href="/wiki/Hudson,_Wisconsin" title="Hudson, Wisconsin">Hudson, Wisconsin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1878, he began school at Saint Mary's, but this was cut short in October 1882 when the family relocated again, to <a href="/wiki/Burkhardt,_Wisconsin" title="Burkhardt, Wisconsin">Burkhardt</a> in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Croix_County" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Croix County">Saint Croix County</a>. In 1887, he left the farm for a series of jobs in his home state and in nearby <a href="/wiki/Minnesota" title="Minnesota">Minnesota</a>, working as a <a href="/wiki/Lumberjack" title="Lumberjack">lumberjack</a>, a hospital orderly, a guard in the Minnesota state prison, and a <a href="/wiki/Streetcar" class="mw-redirect" title="Streetcar">street car</a> operator in <a href="/wiki/Superior,_Wisconsin" title="Superior, Wisconsin">Superior</a>. His time as a prison guard saw him befriend a couple of <a href="/wiki/Jesse_James" title="Jesse James">Jesse James'</a> cohorts.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> At first, he desired married life, but the mother of a girl to whom he had proposed suddenly sent her off to a <a href="/wiki/Boarding_school" title="Boarding school">boarding school</a>.
</p><p>While working at his last job on the trolleys in Superior, Wisconsin he witnessed a violent act that caused him to evaluate his life and future. Some reports say that he saw a drunken <a href="/wiki/Sailor" title="Sailor">sailor</a> standing over a bleeding woman with a knife in his hand.  He then acted on a <a href="/wiki/Vocational_Discernment_in_the_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Vocational Discernment in the Catholic Church">call to the priesthood</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Due to his limited formal education, he enrolled at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Francis_de_Sales_Seminary" title="Saint Francis de Sales Seminary">Saint Francis High School Seminary</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milwaukee" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Milwaukee">Archdiocese of Milwaukee</a>, in January 1891, hoping to become a <a href="/wiki/Diocesan_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocesan priest">diocesan priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FM_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FM-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Classes there were taught in either <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a> or <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, neither of which he knew. In due course, he was advised that he should consider joining a <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">religious order</a> if he wanted to become a priest due to his academic limitations. He returned home before deciding to make his application.
</p><p>While reflecting before a statue of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, he heard her spiritual voice telling him to "go to Detroit".<sup id="cite_ref-CO_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He then applied to the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</a> in that city, and was received into it on January 14, 1897. He was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of "Solanus" after <a href="/wiki/Saint_Francis_Solanus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Francis Solanus">Saint Francis Solanus</a>; both men shared a love of the <a href="/wiki/Violin" title="Violin">violin</a>. He made his vows on July 21, 1898.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He struggled through his studies, but received <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> to the priesthood on July 24, 1904, from Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Sebastian_Messmer" class="mw-redirect" title="Sebastian Messmer">Sebastian Messmer</a> at Saint Francis of Assisi Church in <a href="/wiki/Milwaukee" title="Milwaukee">Milwaukee</a>. Because he had not performed well enough in his studies, he was ordained as a "simplex" priest.<sup id="cite_ref-Michigan_Catholic_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Michigan_Catholic-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FG_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FG-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He celebrated his first Mass on July 31, 1904, in <a href="/wiki/Appleton,_Wisconsin" title="Appleton, Wisconsin">Appleton</a>, with his family present.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry">Ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solanus_Casey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He served for two decades in a succession of friaries in <a href="/wiki/New_York_(state)" title="New York (state)">New York</a>. His first assignment was at Sacred Heart Friary in <a href="/wiki/Yonkers,_New_York" title="Yonkers, New York">Yonkers</a>. He was later transferred to <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a>, where he first served at <a href="/wiki/St._John_the_Baptist_Church_(Manhattan)" title="St. John the Baptist Church (Manhattan)">Saint John's Church</a> next to <a href="/wiki/Penn_Station" class="mw-redirect" title="Penn Station">Penn Station</a> and later at <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_Queen_of_Angels_(New_York_City)" title="Church of Our Lady Queen of Angels (New York City)">Our Lady Queen of Angels</a> in <a href="/wiki/Harlem" title="Harlem">Harlem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-MC_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was recognized as a healer and gifted spiritual counselor. In August 1924, he was transferred to the <a href="/wiki/St._Bonaventure_Monastery" title="St. Bonaventure Monastery">Saint Bonaventure convent</a> in Detroit, where he worked until 1945. During this time, he mostly served as the simple <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">porter</a> (or receptionist and doorkeeper).<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Each Wednesday afternoon, he conducted well-attended services for the sick, and through these services, he became known for his great compassion and simple holiness. People considered him instrumental in cures and other blessings.<sup id="cite_ref-CERC_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CERC-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FM_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FM-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He loved to kneel before the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> in the quiet of the night.
</p><p>During his time at St. Bonaventure's, Casey was involved in the formation of the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Soup_Kitchen" title="Capuchin Soup Kitchen">Capuchin Soup Kitchen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The soup kitchen was founded in 1929 to provide food for Detroit's poor during the Great Depression. Casey is considered one of the founders of the soup kitchen, which is still in operation today.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a violinist, he loved to play Irish songs for his fellow friars during recreation time, but was not a very gifted instrumentalist and had a poor singing voice, attributed to having suffered from diphtheria as a child - a disease that took the life of two of his sisters.  Often Solanus could be found playing his violin in the chapel for the <a href="/wiki/Tabernacle" title="Tabernacle">tabernacle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He ate little. Until his late seventies, he joined the younger religious in games of <a href="/wiki/Tennis" title="Tennis">tennis</a> and <a href="/wiki/Volleyball" title="Volleyball">volleyball</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Declining_health_and_death">Declining health and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solanus_Casey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Declining health and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg/220px-Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg/330px-Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7c/Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="384" data-file-height="512" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fr_Solanus_Casey%27s_Tomb_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The friar's tomb from 1987 to 2017; it now has a plexiglass dome.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1946, in failing health and suffering from <a href="/wiki/Eczema" class="mw-redirect" title="Eczema">eczema</a> over his entire body, he was transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Victory_Noll-St._Felix_Friary_Historic_District" class="mw-redirect" title="Victory Noll-St. Felix Friary Historic District">Capuchin novitiate of Saint Felix</a> in <a href="/wiki/Huntington,_Indiana" title="Huntington, Indiana">Huntington</a>, <a href="/wiki/Indiana" title="Indiana">Indiana</a>, where he lived until a 1956 hospitalization in Detroit.<sup id="cite_ref-MC_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MC-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FM_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FM-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In 1957, he was rushed to the hospital for <a href="/wiki/Food_poisoning" class="mw-redirect" title="Food poisoning">food poisoning</a>; upon his release, friars noted that he was walking much more slowly and scratching his legs; it turned out that his skin was raw and infected, prompting a return to the hospital. The doctors diagnosed him with <a href="/wiki/Erysipelas" title="Erysipelas">erysipelas</a> or possibly <a href="/wiki/Psoriasis" title="Psoriasis">psoriasis</a>, which was beyond treatment, and they considered limb <a href="/wiki/Amputation" title="Amputation">amputation</a>, but the <a href="/wiki/Ulcers" class="mw-redirect" title="Ulcers">ulcers</a> began to heal.
</p><p>On July 2, 1957, he was readmitted to the hospital for the final time due to his skin deterioration. He was given <a href="/wiki/Oxygen_therapy" title="Oxygen therapy">oxygen therapy</a>. Casey's sister, Martha, came to visit him after being notified of the seriousness of his condition; the two prayed the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> together.
</p><p>He died from <a href="/wiki/Erysipelas" title="Erysipelas">erysipelas</a> at 11:00 am on July 31, 1957, at <a href="/wiki/St._John_Providence_Health_System" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John Providence Health System">Saint John Hospital</a> in Detroit, with only his nurse at his side. A commemorative plaque was placed outside the door of the room. His last words were reportedly: "I give my soul to <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-solanuscasey.org_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-solanuscasey.org-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> An estimated 20,000 people filed past his coffin prior to his funeral and burial in the cemetery of his Detroit monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-Father_Solanus_Casey_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Father_Solanus_Casey-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> On July 8, 1987, his remains were exhumed and reinterred inside the chapel at Saint Bonaventure Monastery, which eventually became part of the Solanus Casey Center; his remains showed no signs of the skin disease that afflicted him at the end of his life.. His remains were clothed in a new habit before reinternment in a steel casket at the <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">north transept</a>. A range of miraculous cures has been attributed to his intercession, both during his earthly life and death.<sup id="cite_ref-CERC_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CERC-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> although only one has been officially recognized by Rome
</p>

Solanus Casey</div></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Exhumation">Exhumation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solanus_Casey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Exhumation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His remains were exhumed to collect <a href="/wiki/Relic#Classifications_and_prohibitions_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Relic">first- and second-class relics</a> on August 1, 2017. They were then placed in a new black casket and reinterred with a plexiglass dome so the new casket is now visible.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Solanus_Casey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His beatification cause commenced in Detroit in 1976 with an investigation involving witness interrogatories and documentation compiling. The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> validated this phase on November 7, 1986; around 1995, it received the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier from <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulation</a> officials. The theological advisors approved the dossier on April 7, 1995; the cardinal and bishop members of the congregation did so on June 20, 1995. On July 11, 1995, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, in a private audience with congregation prefect <a href="/wiki/Alberto_Bovone" title="Alberto Bovone">Alberto Bovone</a>, confirmed that Casey had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and titled him <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For a person to be beatified - unless they are a martyr - ordinarily a miracle (normally, healing) must be approved following confirmation that science could not explain it. Numerous cases were investigated, including one considered by the congregation on April 3, 1998, which was later dismissed. Another purported miracle was investigated by the Archdiocese of Detroit; its favorable report was completed and forwarded to Rome in 2015. This received congregation validation on October 12, 2015; approval by a panel of medical experts on September 22, 2016; and theological consulters' approval on January 19, 2017.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The congregation approved the miracle on May 2, 2017, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> did so two days later, meaning that the late friar would be beatified. On November 18, 2017, the beatification took place at <a href="/wiki/Ford_Field" title="Ford Field">Ford Field</a> in Detroit in front of an estimated crowd of 70,000.<sup id="cite_ref-BeatifiedMilwaukeeJournalSentinel_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BeatifiedMilwaukeeJournalSentinel-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The Mass and beatification rite was led by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> for the congregation. Casey now has the title "Blessed" and is one step closer to sainthood.<sup id="cite_ref-did_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-did-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Casey's confirmed miracle involved the curing of Paula Medina Zarate's <a href="/wiki/Ichthyosis" title="Ichthyosis">ichthyosis</a>, a genetic skin condition.<sup id="cite_ref-did_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-did-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The miracle occurred at <a href="/wiki/St._Bonaventure_Monastery" title="St. Bonaventure Monastery">St. Bonaventure Monastery</a> in Detroit in 2012.<sup id="cite_ref-did_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-did-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The postulator for Casey's cause was his fellow Capuchin friar Carlo Calloni. The current vice-postulator is <a href="/wiki/Edward_Foley,_Capuchin" title="Edward Foley, Capuchin">Edward Foley</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
