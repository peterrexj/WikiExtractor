<p><b>Saint Corentin</b> (Corentinus; in <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>, <i>Sant Kaourintin</i>) (d. 460 AD) is a <a href="/wiki/Breton_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton people">Breton</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. He was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Quimper" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Quimper">bishop of Quimper</a>. Corentin was a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> at <a href="/wiki/Plomodiern" title="Plomodiern">Plomodiern</a> and was regarded as one of the <a href="/wiki/Brittany#Religion" title="Brittany">seven founding saints of Brittany</a>. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cornouaille" title="Cornouaille">Cornouaille</a>, Brittany, and is also the patron saint of seafood. His feast day is December 12.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History_and_tradition">History and tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Corentin_of_Quimper&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History and tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Corentin is one of the "Seven Saints" who evangelized Brittany. The others are: Saint Tugdual de Tréguier, Saint Paterne de Vannes, Saint Samson of Dol, Saint Pol de Léon, Saint Malo and Saint Brieuc. The <i>Tro Breiz</i>, which in Breton means "tour of Brittany", is a Catholic pilgrimage that connects the cities of the seven legendary saints of Brittany, monks from Wales and Cornwall who brought Christianity to Armorica and founded the first bishoprics in the fifth century and sixth century. 
</p><p>Corentin's life is told in the book <i>Vita de saint Corentin</i>, written by Dom Plaine around 1220–1235. This publication was revised and comments were added to it, particularly after the discovery of a book called <i>The Ancient Life of Saint Corentin</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Chanson_d%27Aiquin" class="mw-redirect" title="Chanson d&#39;Aiquin">song of Aiquin</a> also evokes his life.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <i><a href="/wiki/Albert_le_Grand" class="mw-redirect" title="Albert le Grand">Albert le Grand</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_le_Grand_(%C3%A9crivain)" class="extiw" title="fr:Albert le Grand (écrivain)">fr</a>&#93;</span></i>, Saint Corentin was born in a region called <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cornouaille_armoricaine&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cornouaille armoricaine (page does not exist)">Cornouaille armoricaine</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royaume_de_Cornouaille" class="extiw" title="fr:Royaume de Cornouaille">fr</a>&#93;</span>. He became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in the city of <a href="/wiki/Plomodiern" title="Plomodiern">Plomodiern</a>, adjacent to Ménez-Hom, where he was already changing lives through his devotion to people. His selflessness was renowned across France, and through the medium of scriptures and word of mouth, his humanitarianism was recognised in Europe also.
</p><p>Legend has it that near where he was living, a miraculous fish would present itself to Saint Corentin in a fountain. He would cut a small piece of the fish to feed himself, and that piece would regrow<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (he would otherwise feed himself with herbs and roots found in the forest). 
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/Gradlon" title="Gradlon">King Gradlon</a> decided to create the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> of <a href="/wiki/Quimper" title="Quimper">Quimper</a>, he called on Corentin and asked him to become the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. He sent Corentin to the city of <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a> so that he could be consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Saint Martin</a>. He was accompanied by his disciples, Saint Guénolé, founder of <a href="/wiki/Land%C3%A9vennec_Abbey" title="Landévennec Abbey">Landévennec Abbey</a>, and Saint Tudy were contemporaries and disciples of Corentin.
</p><p>Gradlon also gave him a palace on the location where the <a href="/wiki/Quimper_Cathedral" title="Quimper Cathedral">Quimper Cathedral</a> now stands.
</p><p>After his death, he was buried in front of the altar of the Quimper Cathedral. An abbey was dedicated to him in 1201 by King <a href="/wiki/Philippe-Auguste" class="mw-redirect" title="Philippe-Auguste">Philippe Auguste</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Mantes-la-Ville" title="Mantes-la-Ville">Mantes</a>; it was called Saint Corentin Abbey.
</p><p>He is also known in Cornwall, where <a href="/wiki/St_Corentine%27s_Church,_Cury" title="St Corentine&#39;s Church, Cury">St. Corentine's Church, Cury</a> is dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend_of_Ys">Legend of Ys</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Corentin_of_Quimper&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend of Ys">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris,_Souvestre.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris%2C_Souvestre.jpg/220px-Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris%2C_Souvestre.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="149" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris%2C_Souvestre.jpg/330px-Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris%2C_Souvestre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris%2C_Souvestre.jpg/440px-Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris%2C_Souvestre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="443" data-file-height="300" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gradlon_et_Corentin_-_Keris,_Souvestre.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gradlon et Corentin</div></div></div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Ys" title="Ys">Ys</a></div>
<p>In the Breton legend of the city of <a href="/wiki/Ys" title="Ys">Ys</a>, Corentin is the saint who observed the fall of Ys and warned <a href="/wiki/Gradlon" title="Gradlon">King Gradlon</a> of the sin committed by his daughter, <a href="/wiki/Dahut" title="Dahut">Dahut (Ahes)</a>. The Christianization of the Celts was concurrent with the fall of Rome, and so the mercy of Corentin towards Gradlon symbolized the cultural transition. Prior to Christianity, the Celtic lifestyle was based around estuarine aquaculture dependent upon the pattern of the tides. In lowland environments where flooding is a major hazard, megaliths served as an astronomical calendar to predict the movement of water. Coastal Celts (also called <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armoricans</a>) used a system of dikes and locks to provide irrigation on an alternating basis, allowing separate plots of land to switch between producing cereals and shellfish. Corentin is a patron saint of seafood and, through him, inland aquaculture demonstrates the sustainability of <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Celtic Christianity</a> over prior practices.
</p><p>Later tales blamed the caprice of Princess <a href="/wiki/Dahut" title="Dahut">Dahut</a> for the cataclysm at Ys. Some tales refer to her as a descendant of faeries sent to beguile King <a href="/wiki/Gradlon" title="Gradlon">Gradlon</a> into ruin. Others depict her as a princess seduced by the devil into opening the floodgates. The tales share a common plot point: King Gradlon and Princess Dahut magically escaping to shore on horseback where they are waylaid by Saint Corentin, (or in some versions Saint Guénolé) who decries the excess of Dahut, causing her to fall into the water and become a <a href="/wiki/Morgen_(mythological_creature)" title="Morgen (mythological creature)">morgen</a> or <a href="/wiki/Siren_(mythology)" title="Siren (mythology)">siren</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Corentin then absconds to his hermitage while the king embarks on a hunting party. Gradlon becomes lost and hungry enough to request food when he stumbles upon Corentin's hermitage. Corentin offers the king a morsel of his miraculous, regenerative fish, symbolizing the gift of Christianity.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Corentin_of_Quimper&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is represented with a whole fish or a half of fish (reminder of the fish he shared with the hunter) that he holds in his hand or at his feet where he is sometimes associated with a fountain.     
</p>
