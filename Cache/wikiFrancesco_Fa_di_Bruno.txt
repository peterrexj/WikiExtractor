<p><b>Francesco Faà di Bruno</b> (7 March 1825 – 25 March 1888) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> and advocate of the poor, a leading <a href="/wiki/Mathematician" title="Mathematician">mathematician</a> of his era and a noted religious musician. In 1988 he was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-beatif_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-beatif-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is the <a href="/wiki/Eponym" title="Eponym">eponym</a> of <a href="/wiki/Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno%27s_formula" title="Faà di Bruno&#39;s formula">Faà di Bruno's formula</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francesco_Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francesco_Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Faà di Bruno was born in <a href="/wiki/Alessandria" title="Alessandria">Alessandria</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia" title="Kingdom of Sardinia">Kingdom of Sardinia</a>, on 7 March 1825. He was of noble birth, being the twelfth and youngest child of the <a href="/wiki/Marchese" class="mw-redirect" title="Marchese">Marchese</a> Luigi Faà di Bruno and the Lady Carolina Sappa de' Milanesi. He was raised in a home marked by happiness, the arts and a concern for the poor arising from the parents' strong Catholic faith.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As a young man, he entered the Royal Army and held, at one time, the rank of <a href="/wiki/Staff_(military)" title="Staff (military)">Staff Officer</a>. He resigned his commission, and went to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, where he did doctoral studies in mathematics under <a href="/wiki/Augustin_Cauchy" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustin Cauchy">Augustin Cauchy</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Urbain_Le_Verrier" title="Urbain Le Verrier">Urbain Le Verrier</a>, who both shared in the discovery of the planet <a href="/wiki/Neptune" title="Neptune">Neptune</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-linehan_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-linehan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was in close contact with the mathematicians <a href="/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois-Napol%C3%A9on-Marie_Moigno" title="François-Napoléon-Marie Moigno">François-Napoléon-Marie Moigno</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_Hermite" title="Charles Hermite">Charles Hermite</a>.
</p><p>On his return to Turin, he took up the position of Professor of Mathematics at the local university. In recognition of his achievements as a mathematician, the degree of Doctor of Science was conferred on him by the Universities of Paris and Turin.<sup id="cite_ref-linehan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-linehan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Social_reformer">Social reformer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francesco_Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Social reformer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>While carrying out his career responsibilities, Faà di Bruno also became actively involved in the social outreach to the poor being developed by leading figures of the Catholic Church in Turin. He became a close friend of <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">John Bosco</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and helped establish refuges for the elderly and the poor. He oversaw the construction of a church in Turin, <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_di_Nostra_Signora_del_Suffragio" class="extiw" title="it:Chiesa di Nostra Signora del Suffragio">Our Lady of Suffrage</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Priest_and_founder">Priest and founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francesco_Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Priest and founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Somewhat late in his life, Faà di Bruno came to feel that pursuing <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> would help him in his religious activities, and commenced the necessary studies in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>. What he found, however, was that the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Turin" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Turin">Archbishop of Turin</a> at that time would not accept an older man for ordination, Faà di Bruno being in his late 40s at that time. For centuries, the traditional route for this profession began in a boy's mid-teens.
</p><p>Faà di Bruno appealed to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> and received his support, finally being ordained at age 51. He founded the <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suore_Minime_di_Nostra_Signora_del_Suffragio" class="extiw" title="it:Suore Minime di Nostra Signora del Suffragio">Minim Sisters of St. Zita</a> in 1881 to provide help for maids and domestic servants, later expanding its outreach to include others, such as unmarried mothers. With their help, he also established another refuge, one dedicated to taking in <a href="/wiki/Prostitute" class="mw-redirect" title="Prostitute">prostitutes</a>.
</p><p>Faà di Bruno died in Turin on 25 March 1888.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francesco_Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The cause for the <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of Faà di Bruno opened in the early 20th century by the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Turin" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Turin">Archdiocese of Turin</a> and he was declared a Servant of God. He was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1971, and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on the centennial of his death in 1988.<sup id="cite_ref-beatif_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-beatif-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In an address to the Minim Sisters in 2002, Pope John Paul II said, ""Francesco Faà di Bruno", I said, is "a giant of faith and charity", for his message of light and love, "far from being exhausted, seems timelier than ever, urging all those who have Gospel values at heart to action"" (Homily, 25 September, n. 4; ORE, 24 October 1988, p. 15).<sup id="cite_ref-va_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Research_in_mathematics">Research in mathematics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francesco_Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Research in mathematics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In addition to some <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetical</a> writings, the composition of some sacred melodies, and the invention of some scientific apparatus, Faà di Bruno made numerous and important contributions to mathematics.  Today, he is best known for <a href="/wiki/Fa%C3%A0_di_Bruno%27s_formula" title="Faà di Bruno&#39;s formula">Faà di Bruno's formula</a> on derivatives of composite functions although it is now certain <a href="/wiki/Louis_Fran%C3%A7ois_Antoine_Arbogast" title="Louis François Antoine Arbogast">Louis François Antoine Arbogast</a> had priority for its discovery and use. Faà di Bruno should be credited only for the formula's <a href="/wiki/Determinant" title="Determinant">determinant form</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> However, his work is mainly related to <a href="/wiki/Elimination_theory" title="Elimination theory">elimination theory</a> and to the theory of <a href="/wiki/Elliptic_function" title="Elliptic function">elliptic functions</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was the author of about 40 original articles published in the "Journal de Mathématiques" (edited by <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Liouville" title="Joseph Liouville">Joseph Liouville</a>), <a href="/wiki/Crelle%27s_Journal" title="Crelle&#39;s Journal">Crelle's Journal</a>, "American Journal of Mathematics" (<a href="/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University" title="Johns Hopkins University">Johns Hopkins University</a>), "Annali di Tortolini", "Les Mondes", "<a href="/wiki/Comptes_rendus_de_l%27Acad%C3%A9mie_des_sciences" class="mw-redirect" title="Comptes rendus de l&#39;Académie des sciences">Comptes rendus de l'Académie des sciences</a>", etc.; the first half of an exhaustive treatise on the theory and applications of <a href="/wiki/Elliptic_function" title="Elliptic function">elliptic functions</a> which he planned to complete in three volumes; "Théorie générale de l'élimination" (Paris, 1859); "Calcolo degli errori" (Turin, 1867), translated into French under the title of "Traité élémentaire du calcul des erreurs" (Paris, 1869); and most important of all, "Théorie des formes binaires" (Paris, 1876), translated into German (Leipzig, 1881). For a list of the memoirs of Faà di Bruno, see the "Catalogue of Scientific Papers of the Royal Society: (London, 1868, 1877, 1891), t. II, vii, and ix.
</p><p>Among his students were the noted mathematicians <a href="/wiki/Corrado_Segre" title="Corrado Segre">Corrado Segre</a> and <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Peano" title="Giuseppe Peano">Giuseppe Peano</a>.
</p>
