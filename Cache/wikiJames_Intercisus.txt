<p><b>Saint James Intercisus</b>, also called <b>James the Mutilated</b> or <b>James the Persian</b> (<a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a><b>:</b> <i><a href="/wiki/Mar" title="Mar">Mor</a> Yakob M'phasko Sahada</i>; Latin: <i>Sanctus Jacobus Intercisus</i>), (died in 420 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Syriac_Christianity" title="Syriac Christianity">Syriac</a> Christian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> born in <a href="/wiki/Beth_Huzaye_(East_Syriac_ecclesiastical_province)" title="Beth Huzaye (East Syriac ecclesiastical province)">Beth Huzaye</a> (<a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>: ܒܝܬ ܗܘܙܝܐ) in <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Epithet" title="Epithet">epithet</a>, <i>Intercisus</i>, is derived from the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> word for "cut into pieces," which refers to the manner of his <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>: he was slowly <a href="/wiki/Lingchi" title="Lingchi">cut into twenty-eight pieces</a>. His death, along with the persecution of other Christians in the <a href="/wiki/Sasanian_Empire" title="Sasanian Empire">Sasanid Empire</a>, started the <a href="/wiki/Roman-Sassanid_War_(421-422)" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman-Sassanid War (421-422)">Roman-Sassanid War (421-422)</a>.
</p><p>His feast day is November 27.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Intercisus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tradition states that he was a military officer and <a href="/wiki/Courtier" title="Courtier">courtier</a> to <a href="/wiki/Yazdegerd_I" title="Yazdegerd I">Yazdegerd I</a> who had <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostatized</a> after this ruler began to persecute Christians. Under the influence of his Christian family, however, he expressed his faith to Yazdegerd's successor, <a href="/wiki/Bahram_V" title="Bahram V">Bahram V</a>, leading to his execution.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Intercisus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was killed in Beth Lapat (<a href="/wiki/Gundishapur" class="mw-redirect" title="Gundishapur">Gundishapur</a>). The ruins of this city are near <a href="/wiki/Dezful" title="Dezful">Dezful</a>, <a href="/wiki/Iran" title="Iran">Iran</a>.
</p><p>At his execution, he survived the loss of limbs until he was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>. His followers requested his body parts as relics, but this request was denied, so they stole the body parts,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which were somehow sent to the Portuguese cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a> and put into a sarcophagus in the Relics Chapel.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Intercisus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James' story is recounted in <i><a href="/wiki/The_Golden_Legend" class="mw-redirect" title="The Golden Legend">The Golden Legend</a></i>.
</p><p>According to Katherine Rabenstein, he may be a composite character of James of Beit (who, having renounced Christianity under Yazdegerd, was shamed by his parents and changed his mind, becoming a martyr under the persecution of Bahram); Mar Peros (similarly shamed by his parents and martyred in 448); and James of Karka (a 20-year-old notary to Bahram, tortured alongside many others after casually remarking that he'd rather be cut into pieces than renounce God).<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Holy_Relics.2C_Churches_and_Monasteries"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Holy_Relics,_Churches_and_Monasteries">Holy Relics, Churches and Monasteries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Intercisus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Holy Relics, Churches and Monasteries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/w/index.php?title=Guillermus_Ludovicus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guillermus Ludovicus (page does not exist)">Guillermus Ludovicus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salpi" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Salpi">bishop of Salpi</a>, gifted to the <a href="/wiki/Cormery_Abbey" title="Cormery Abbey">abbey of St Paul in Cormery</a>, the place where he had been a monk, several relics, including the head of St James, on July 19th 1103.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A piece of bone from the finger of St. James the Mutilated (Mor Yakob M'phasko Sahada) is kept in a golden casket in the holy cross (kurishupalli) dedicated to the saint in the Orthodox Syrian Old Church of St Peter &amp; Paul in <a href="/wiki/Pengamuck" title="Pengamuck">Pengamuck</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kerala" title="Kerala">Kerala</a>, <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>. It was dedicated by <a href="/wiki/Mar" title="Mar">Mor</a> <a href="/wiki/Gregorios_Geevarghese" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorios Geevarghese">Gregorios Geevargese</a> (Parumala Thirumeni)] and metropolitan Mor Dionysious Joseph (Pulikkottil II), a native of Pengamuck, who had received the bone at his consecration as Metropolitan by the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">patriarch of Antioch</a> Ignatius Yakoob II.
</p><p>Several churches and monasteries are dedicated to St. James:
</p>
<ul><li>Church of St. James Intercisus in the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Quarter" title="Armenian Quarter">Armenian Quarter</a> of <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Monastery of St. James the Persian in Sireți, <a href="/wiki/Str%C4%83%C8%99eni" title="Strășeni">Strășeni</a> <a href="/wiki/Moldova" title="Moldova">Moldova</a></li>
<li>Monastery of St. James the Persian in <a href="/wiki/Deddeh" title="Deddeh">Deddeh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Saint_James_the_Mutilated_(Qara)" title="Monastery of Saint James the Mutilated (Qara)">Monastery of St James the Mutilated</a> in <a href="/wiki/Qara,_Syria" title="Qara, Syria">Qara, Syria</a></li></ul>
