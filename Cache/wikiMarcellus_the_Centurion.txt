<p><b>Saint Marcellus of Tangier</b> or <b>Saint Marcellus the Centurion</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Marcelo</i>) (c. mid 3rd century – 298 <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_people" title="Roman people">Roman</a> centurion who is today venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">martyr-saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on October 30.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellus_of_Tangier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Marcellus was said to have been a <a href="/wiki/Centurion" title="Centurion">centurion</a> stationed at <a href="/wiki/Tingis" class="mw-redirect" title="Tingis">Tingis</a> (modern-day <a href="/wiki/Tangiers" class="mw-redirect" title="Tangiers">Tangiers</a>) who refused to participate in the general birthday celebrations of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>, which would have entailed sacrifice to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_mythology" title="Roman mythology">Roman gods</a>. Throwing off his military belt, weapons, and <a href="/wiki/Vine_staff" title="Vine staff">vine staff</a> (the insignia of his rank),<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Marcellus was soon brought before a judge named <a href="/w/index.php?title=Fortunatus_the_judge&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Fortunatus the judge (page does not exist)">Fortunatus</a>. The judge remanded the saint to lay his case before Maximian and Constantius; the latter was friendly to Christians. However, Marcellus was taken to the deputy Praetorian prefect <a href="/w/index.php?title=Aurelius_Agricolanus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Aurelius Agricolanus (page does not exist)">Aurelius Agricolanus</a> instead. Marcellus pleaded guilty to repudiating his allegiance to an earthly leader.
</p><p>Marcellus was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Sword" title="Sword">sword</a> by the deputy <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Guard" title="Praetorian Guard">Praetorian</a> prefect.
</p><p>Afterwards, it is said that the official <a href="/wiki/Shorthand" title="Shorthand">shorthand</a> writer, a man named <a href="/wiki/Cassian_of_Tangier" title="Cassian of Tangier">Saint Cassian</a>, was so angry at the sentence that he refused to record the court proceedings and was martyred as well.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St Marcellus' <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were later brought to and enshrined at <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">León</a>, and he became a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the city. The Plaza de San Marcelo in this city is named for him. The church of San Marcelo dates from the 10th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Alternative_version">Alternative version</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellus_of_Tangier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Alternative version">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>An alternative version of his legend states that he was a centurion of the <a href="/wiki/Legio_VII_Gemina" title="Legio VII Gemina">Legio VII Gemina Pia Felix</a>, and was born in what is now <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">León</a> in the middle of the third century.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> This version, however, has been shown to be largely apocryphal.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> The story relates that Marcellus lived close to the <a href="/wiki/City_walls" class="mw-redirect" title="City walls">city walls</a>. During the birthday celebrations for the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a>, in July 298, Marcellus publicly demonstrated his Christian faith by throwing down the insignia of his rank, and proclaimed that he only venerated one God. He was then sent to Tangiers to be judged by the vice-prefect (vicar), Agricolanus. He was condemned to death and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a> on October 29, 298, together with his wife Nona and his twelve sons (Claudius, Lupercus, Victorius, <a href="/wiki/Facundus_and_Primitivus" title="Facundus and Primitivus">Facundus, Primitivus</a>, Servandus, Germanus, Faustus, Januarius, and Martial). Included in this group of sons are the martyrs <a href="/wiki/Emeterius_and_Celedonius" title="Emeterius and Celedonius">Emeterius and Celedonius</a>, though their connection to Marcellus is probably <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a>. <a href="/wiki/Servandus_and_Germanus" class="mw-redirect" title="Servandus and Germanus">Servandus and Germanus</a> (or Cermanus) were venerated separately at <a href="/wiki/Cadiz" class="mw-redirect" title="Cadiz">Cadiz</a>, and their association with Marcellus is also apocryphal.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Claudius.2C_Lupercus.2C_Victorius"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Claudius,_Lupercus,_Victorius">Claudius, Lupercus, Victorius</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellus_of_Tangier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Claudius, Lupercus, Victorius">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG/200px-Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG/300px-Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG/400px-Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1536" data-file-height="2048" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Papones_Santo_Cristo_Bienaventuranza_Le%C3%B3n.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Procession of the Confraternity of Santo Cristo de la Bienaventuranza walking past the church of San Claudio in León, 2005.</div></div></div>
<p><b>Saints Claudius, Lupercus</b> or <b>Lupercio</b>, and <b>Victorius</b> or <b>Victoricus</b> (d. c. 300) are said to have been the sons of St Marcellus. They were said to have been martyred at <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">León</a>, Spain, during the reign of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>. Their relationship to Marcellus is probably <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a>, though it was accepted in subsequent <a href="/wiki/Canonical_hours" title="Canonical hours">breviaries</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hagiographies" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographies">hagiographies</a> as well as the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>, which placed all four saints under October 30.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> However, it is quite possible that Claudius, Lupercus, and Victorius were soldiers of Spanish origin who were killed at León, as tradition states. Many churches in Spain were dedicated to them, including the ancient <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbey of San Claudio, in <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Spain)" title="Galicia (Spain)">Galicia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
The town of <a href="/wiki/San_Claudio" title="San Claudio">San Claudio</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Oviedo" title="Oviedo">Oviedo</a>, takes its name from this group of martyrs.
</p><p>Their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> several times in succeeding centuries. King <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Castile" class="mw-redirect" title="Ferdinand I of Castile">Ferdinand I of Castile</a> transported some of their relics to the church of San Isidoro in León. In 1173, the relics were translated to a new church dedicated to the three martyrs. This church was destroyed in 1834 and the relics were moved to the church of San Marcelo, dedicated to their presumed father. Their feast day is celebrated in Spain and Portugal on October 30.
</p><p>Victorius or Victoricus should not be confused with the <a href="/wiki/Victoricus,_Fuscian,_and_Gentian" title="Victoricus, Fuscian, and Gentian">French saint of the same name</a>. <a href="/wiki/Luperculus" title="Luperculus">Another Lupercus or Luperculus</a> was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Eauze" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Eauze">bishop of Eauze</a> who was venerated as a martyr, and sometimes identified as an uncle of the Spanish saint <a href="/wiki/Saint_Engratia" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Engratia">Saint Engratia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
