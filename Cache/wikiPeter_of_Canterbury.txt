					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">7th-century missionary and abbot in Britain</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other uses, see <a href="/wiki/List_of_people_named_Peter" title="List of people named Peter">List of people named Peter</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color: #DFB0DF;"><div style="display:inline" class="fn">Peter of Canterbury</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-subheader role">Abbot of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Personal details</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">early 7th century</td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:#DDDDDD;">Sainthood</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Feast day</th><td class="infobox-data">6 January</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Peter of Canterbury</b><sup id="cite_ref-Walsh482_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh482-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Petrus</b><sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;607</span> or after 614) was the first abbot of the monastery of SS. Peter and Paul in Canterbury (later <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a>) and a companion of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Canterbury" title="Augustine of Canterbury">Augustine</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian mission</a> to Kent. Augustine sent Peter as an emissary to Rome around 600 to convey news of the mission to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a>. Peter's death has traditionally been dated to around 607, but evidence suggests that he was present at a church council in Paris in 614, so he probably died after that date.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is presumed that Peter was a native of Italy, like the other members of the Gregorian mission.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair87_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair87-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This mission was dispatched by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a> in 596 to <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianize</a> the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxons</a> from their native <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a>. It landed in Kent in 597, and soon converted King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelberht_of_Kent" title="Æthelberht of Kent">Æthelberht of Kent</a>, who gave Augustine the land on which he founded the abbey that later became St Augustine's, Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-Mayr61_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mayr61-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The medieval chronicler <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> records that sometime after the mission's arrival in England,<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks9_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks9-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> probably in late 600,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Peter, along with fellow-missionary <a href="/wiki/Laurence_of_Canterbury" title="Laurence of Canterbury">Laurence</a>, was sent back to Gregory.  This deputation was to relay the news of Augustine's successes in Kent, and to request more missionaries.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks9_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks9-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They also conveyed to the pope a number of inquiries from Augustine about how to proceed with the mission, and when they returned in 601, they brought back Gregory's replies to Augustine.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter became the abbot of the monastery that Æthelberht founded in Canterbury, originally dedicated to the saints <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Paul">Paul</a>, but later rededicated as St Augustine's, after the leader of the mission.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bede describes Peter as both abbot and <i><a href="/wiki/Presbyter" title="Presbyter">presbyter</a></i>, a word usually translated as priest.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks88_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks88-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter drowned while crossing the <a href="/wiki/English_Channel" title="English Channel">English Channel</a> on the way to Gaul,<sup id="cite_ref-Blair74_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair74-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> at a place called Ambleteuse, near Boulogne.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At first he was buried hastily nearby, but Bede reports that after a light illuminated the grave every night, the locals realised Peter was a saint and exhumed him and re-interred him in Boulogne. The actual date of death is unknown, and since his feast day was celebrated on two different days, 30 December or 6 January, that information does not clear up the mystery. The date of his death is reported to have been 1 year, 7 months and 3 weeks after Augustine's, by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_of_Elmham" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas of Elmham">Thomas of Elmham</a>, a 15th-century chronicler. If this is true, this would give a year of death between 605 and 611.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This information, however, is contradicted by the fact that Peter was present at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Paris_(614)" title="Council of Paris (614)">Council of Paris</a> in 614, convened by <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_II" title="Chlothar II">Chlothar II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wood7_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wood7-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It is possible that he died during his return from the Council of Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-Hayward204_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hayward204-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter is a considered a saint, with a feast day on 6 January. His cult was <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Roman_Catholic_procedure_since_1983" title="Canonization">confirmed</a> in 1915.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh482_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh482-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A <i>Vita Petri</i>, or <i>Life of Peter</i>, written by <a href="/wiki/Eadmer" title="Eadmer">Eadmer</a> in the 12th century, exists in manuscript form, but it is unreliable.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> There is evidence that Peter was the object of veneration in Boulogne in the 15th century, and a church in that town was associated with Peter, although probably not from the start of his cult.<sup id="cite_ref-Hayward206_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hayward206-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
