<p><b>Robert of Geneva</b>, (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Robert de Genève</i>; 1342 – 16 September 1394) elected to the papacy as <b>Clement VII</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Clément VII</i>) by the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinals</a> who opposed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a>, was the first <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> residing in <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. His election led to the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a>.
</p><p>The son of <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_III,_Count_of_Geneva" class="mw-redirect" title="Amadeus III, Count of Geneva">Amadeus III, Count of Geneva</a>, Robert became <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Cambrai">Archbishop of Cambrai</a> and was made a cardinal in 1371. As legate, during the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Eight_Saints" title="War of the Eight Saints">War of the Eight Saints</a>, he is said to have authorized the massacre of over 2,000 civilians at <a href="/wiki/Cesena" title="Cesena">Cesena</a> in 1377. He was elected pope the following year by the cardinals who opposed Urban VI and established himself at Avignon.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Clement_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert was born in the <a href="/wiki/Chateau_d%27Annecy" class="mw-redirect" title="Chateau d&#39;Annecy">Chateau d'Annecy</a> in 1342, the son of <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_III_of_Geneva" title="Amadeus III of Geneva">Amadeus III, Count of Geneva</a>, and Mahaut de Boulogne.<sup id="cite_ref-Dictionnaire_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dictionnaire-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Guy_de_Boulogne" class="mw-redirect" title="Guy de Boulogne">Guy de Boulogne</a> was his maternal uncle. Robert studied at <a href="/wiki/College_of_Sorbonne" title="College of Sorbonne">La Sorbonne</a> in Paris. In 1359, he was appointed prothonotary Apostolic, became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Th%C3%A9rouanne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Thérouanne">Bishop of Thérouanne</a> in 1361, Archbishop of Cambrai in 1368, and a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> on 30 May 1371.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> From 1373 he held the position of <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon_of_Dorset" title="Archdeacon of Dorset">Archdeacon of Dorset</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and from 1374 also <a href="/wiki/Prebendary" title="Prebendary">Prebend</a> of All Saints Parish Church in <a href="/wiki/Middle_Woodford" class="mw-redirect" title="Middle Woodford">Middle Woodford</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> leaving both positions in 1378. From 1375, he held a living as rector of <a href="/wiki/Sunderland_Minster" title="Sunderland Minster">Bishopwearmouth</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Durham" title="County Durham">County Durham</a>, England, and instead used the income from that highly prized living for his papal election expenses.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1377, while serving as papal legate in upper Italy (1376–1378), in order to put down a rebellion in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWeber1912_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeber1912-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> known as the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Eight_Saints" title="War of the Eight Saints">War of the Eight Saints</a>, he personally commanded troops lent to the papacy by the <i><a href="/wiki/Condottiere" class="mw-redirect" title="Condottiere">condottiere</a></i> <a href="/wiki/John_Hawkwood" title="John Hawkwood">John Hawkwood</a> to reduce the small city of <a href="/wiki/Cesena" title="Cesena">Cesena</a> in the territory of <a href="/wiki/Forl%C3%AC" title="Forlì">Forlì</a>, which resisted being added to the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Patrimony of Peter</a> for the second time in a generation; there he authorized the massacre of 3,000–8,000 civilians, an atrocity even by the rules of war at the time, which earned him the nickname <i>butcher of Cesena.</i><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1392, at the death of his brother, <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Geneva" title="Peter of Geneva">Pierre</a>, he inherited the title of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Geneva" title="County of Geneva">Count of Geneva</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Dictionnaire_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dictionnaire-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> his four brothers having each died without issue before him. The title then passed from him through his eldest sister Mary to her son, Humbert de Thoire.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_election_and_reign">Papal election and reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antipope_Clement_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papal election and reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert was elected pope at <a href="/wiki/Fondi" title="Fondi">Fondi</a> on 20 September 1378 by the cardinals who opposed the return of the Papacy from <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and the election of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a> in the latter town.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He chose the regnal name of Clement VII, and became the first of the line of 'popes' (now counted as antipopes) of the so-called <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a>, the second of the two periods referred to as the Great Schism, which lasted until 1417.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Clement had the immediate support of Queen <a href="/wiki/Joanna_I_of_Naples" title="Joanna I of Naples">Joanna I of Naples</a> and of several of the Italian barons. <a href="/wiki/Charles_V_of_France" title="Charles V of France">Charles V of France</a>, who seems to have been sounded beforehand on the choice of the Roman pontiff, soon became his warmest protector. Clement eventually succeeded in winning to his cause Castile, Aragon, Navarre, a great part of the Latin East, and Flanders. Scotland supported Clement because England supported Urban.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He had adherents, besides, scattered through Germany, while Portugal on two occasions acknowledged him, but afterwards forsook him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEValois1911485_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEValois1911485-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Burgundy<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and Savoy also acknowledged his authority.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Unable to maintain himself in Italy, he took up his residence at <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> in the southern French <a href="/wiki/Comtat_Venaissin" title="Comtat Venaissin">Comtat Venaissin</a>, where he became dependent on the French court.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWeber1912_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeber1912-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg/220px-Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg/330px-Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg/440px-Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="663" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cl%C3%A9ment_VII_c%C3%A9l%C3%A9brant_la_messe.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>14th century miniature depicting Clement VII celebrating mass</div></div></div>
<p>By the bait of a kingdom to be carved expressly out of the States of the Church and to be called the kingdom of Adria, coupled with the expectation of succeeding to Queen Joanna, Clement incited <a href="/wiki/Louis_I,_Duke_of_Anjou" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis I, Duke of Anjou">Louis I, Duke of Anjou</a>, the eldest of the brothers of Charles V, to take arms in his favour. These tempting offers gave rise to a series of expeditions into Italy carried out almost exclusively at Clement's expense, in the first of which Louis went to war with some 40,000 troops.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The campaign was unsuccessful: Louis suddenly died at <a href="/wiki/Bisceglie" title="Bisceglie">Bisceglie</a> on 20 September 1384. Still, these enterprises on several occasions planted Angevin domination in the south of the Italian peninsula, and their most decisive result was the assuring of Provence to the dukes of Anjou and afterwards to the kings of France. After the death of Louis, Clement hoped to find equally brave and interested champions in Louis' son and namesake <a href="/wiki/Louis_II_of_Anjou" title="Louis II of Anjou">Louis II of Anjou</a>, to which he donated the larger part of the Pontifical States. Clement then tried to ally with <a href="/wiki/Louis_I,_Duke_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Louis I, Duke of Orléans">Louis I, Duke of Orléans</a>, the brother of <a href="/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France" title="Charles VI of France">Charles VI</a>; with Charles VI himself; and with <a href="/wiki/John_III,_Count_of_Armagnac" title="John III, Count of Armagnac">John III, Count of Armagnac</a>. The prospect of his brilliant progress to Rome was ever before Clement's eyes; and in his thoughts force of arms, of French arms, was to be the instrument of his glorious triumph over his competitor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEValois1911485_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEValois1911485-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There came a time, however, when Clement and more particularly his following had to acknowledge the vanity of these elusive dreams; and at the end of his life he realized the impossibility of overcoming by brute force an opposition which was founded on the convictions of the greater part of Catholic Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEValois1911485_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEValois1911485-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Moreover, his ambitions and the financial needs of his court had resorted to <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a>, the loss of land and extortion which discerned among his adherents the germs of disaffection.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEValois1911485_12-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEValois1911485-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He had created excellent cardinals, but he seems never to have sincerely desired the termination of the schism.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died at Avignon on 16 September 1394.
</p><p>Eventually it was determined that he would be recorded as an antipope rather than as a pope. Uncertainty over who the legitimate pope might be during the time of the Western Schism gave rise to the legal theory called <a href="/wiki/Conciliarism" title="Conciliarism">Conciliarism</a>, which claimed that a general council of the church was superior to the pope and could therefore judge between rival claimants.
</p>
