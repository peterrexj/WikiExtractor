<p><b>Saint Blandina</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr"><b>Blandine</b></i>, c. 162–177 <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>) was a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who died in <a href="/wiki/Lugdunum" title="Lugdunum">Lugdunum</a> (modern Lyon, France) during the reign of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Context">Context</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Blandina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Context">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the first two centuries of the Christian era, it was the local Roman officials who were largely responsible for the persecution of Christians. In the second century, the emperors treated Christianity as a local problem to be dealt with by their subordinates.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The number and severity of persecutions of Christians in various locations of the empire seemingly increased during the reign of Marcus Aurelius. The extent to which Marcus Aurelius himself directed, encouraged, or was aware of these persecutions is unclear and much debated by historians.<sup id="cite_ref-McLynn2010295_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McLynn2010295-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Traditional_history">Traditional history</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Blandina&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Traditional history">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The traditional account regarding Blandina is reported by <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Historia Ecclesiastica</a></i>.
</p><p>She belongs to the band of <a href="/wiki/Persecution_in_Lyon" title="Persecution in Lyon">martyrs of Lyon</a> who, after some of their number had endured frightful <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortures</a>, suffered martyrdom in 177 in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>. Almost all we know of Blandina comes from a letter sent from the Church of Lyon to the Churches of Asia Minor.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Eusebius gives significant space of her life and death in his book as he quotes from the aforementioned epistle to <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  The fanaticism of the Roman populace in Lyon had been excited <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians_in_the_Roman_Empire" title="Persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire">against the Christians</a> so that the latter, when they ventured to show themselves publicly, were harassed and ill-treated.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While the imperial <a href="/wiki/Legatus" title="Legatus">legate</a> was away, the <a href="/wiki/Chiliarch" title="Chiliarch">chiliarch</a>, a military commander, and the <a href="/wiki/Duumvir" class="mw-redirect" title="Duumvir">duumvir</a>, a civil magistrate, threw a number of Christians, who confessed their faith, into prison.  When the legate returned, the imprisoned believers were brought to trial.  Among these Christians was Blandina, a <a href="/wiki/Slavery_in_ancient_Rome" title="Slavery in ancient Rome">slave</a>, who had been taken into custody along with her master, also a Christian.  Her companions greatly feared that on account of her bodily frailty she might not remain steadfast under torture.  But although the legate caused her to be tortured in a horrible manner, so that even the executioners became exhausted "as they did not know what more they could do to her", still she remained faithful and repeated to every question "I am a Christian, and we commit no wrongdoing."<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:302px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg/300px-Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="225" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg/450px-Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg/600px-Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Amphiteatre_Trois_Gaules_Lyon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i><a href="/wiki/Amphitheatre_of_the_Three_Gauls" title="Amphitheatre of the Three Gauls">Amphithéâtre des Trois-Gaules</a></i>, in Lyon. The pole in the arena is a memorial to the martyrs, including Blandina.</div></div></div> 
<p>Through fear of torture slaves had testified against their masters that the Christians when assembled committed cannibalism and incest, and the legate desired to wring confession of this misconduct from the Christian prisoners. In his report to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">emperor</a> the legate stated that those who held to their Christian belief were to be executed and those who denied their faith were to be released, and the legate received instructions from the Emperor Marcus Aurelius allowing the <a href="/wiki/Roman_citizenship" title="Roman citizenship">Roman citizens</a> who persisted in the faith to be executed by beheading,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> but those without citizenship were to be tortured.  Blandina was therefore subjected to new tortures with a number of companions in the town's <a href="/wiki/Amphitheater" class="mw-redirect" title="Amphitheater">amphitheater</a> (now known as the <a href="/wiki/Amphitheatre_of_the_Three_Gauls" title="Amphitheatre of the Three Gauls">Amphitheatre of the Three Gauls</a>) at the time of the public games.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was bound to a stake and wild beasts were set on her.  According to legend, they did not, however, touch her.  After enduring this for a number of days, in an effort to persuade her to recant, she was led into the arena to see the sufferings of her companions.  Finally, as the last of the martyrs, she was <a href="/wiki/Scourge" title="Scourge">scourged</a>, placed on a red-hot grate, enclosed in a net and thrown before a wild <a href="/wiki/Cattle" title="Cattle">steer</a> who tossed her into the air with his horns. In the end, she was killed with a dagger.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
