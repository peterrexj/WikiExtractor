<p>Saint <b>Maximus of Évreux</b> (died ca. 384), called <b>Saint Mauxe</b> locally, is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.  His legend states that he was the second <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_%C3%89vreux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Évreux">bishop of Évreux</a>, and that he died a martyr at <a href="/wiki/Acquigny" title="Acquigny">Acquigny</a> with his brother, who was his <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>.  His brother is called <b>Venerandus</b> (Vénérand) or <b>Victorinus</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_of_%C3%89vreux&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their legend states that they were natives of <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>.  Maximus was a bishop and Victorinus was a deacon.  They attempted to make converts to Christianity amongst the ranks of the barbarian armies, but failed.  The brothers were sent by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a> to preach in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> instead, to continue the work of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Taurinus" title="Saint Taurinus">Saint Taurinus</a> (Taurin) in the region.  Taurinus is considered the first bishop of Évreux.
</p><p>They traveled to Gaul with two priests, Mark and Etherius, passing through <a href="/wiki/Auxerre" title="Auxerre">Auxerre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> until they reached Évreux, where Maximus became bishop (the years of his episcopate are sometimes given as 411–440, which conflict with the traditional date of his martyrdom).
</p><p>At Acquigny they were captured by either <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagans</a> or <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arians</a> and were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.  Mark and Etherius escaped.  They returned to bury the two brothers.  Etherius later became bishop of Évreux.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_of_May_25_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_of_May_25-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximus_of_%C3%89vreux&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were re-discovered around 960 by a man named Amalbert.  He attempted to carry the relics off, leaving behind only the heads of Maximus and Victorinus.  However, as he was crossing the <a href="/wiki/Seine" title="Seine">Seine</a> near <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Wandrille" title="Abbey of Saint Wandrille">Fontenelle Abbey</a>, he fell sick.  He left the relics at the monastery, where <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_Normandy" title="Richard I of Normandy">Richard I of Normandy</a> built a chapel to house them.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_of_May_25_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_of_May_25-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The relics were later burnt by the <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Huguenots</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_of_May_25_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_of_May_25-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The saints’ heads, however, remained at Acquigny.  A <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> church was built over their tomb, but it fell into decay.  The relics were translated to the parish church and deposited under the high altar.  The saints were invoked against <a href="/wiki/Drought" title="Drought">drought</a> in 1559, 1615 and 1726, when they were carried after the head of <a href="/wiki/Swithun" title="Swithun">Saint Swithun</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Saints_of_May_25_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saints_of_May_25-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A sanctuary dedicated to him may have existed from the fifth century.  In the forest of <a href="/wiki/Bizy" class="mw-redirect" title="Bizy">Bizy</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Vernon,_Eure" title="Vernon, Eure">Vernon</a>, one can see the supposed tomb of Maximus.  It carries the inscription: "This monument of piety was restored by the <a href="/wiki/Duchess_of_Orleans" class="mw-redirect" title="Duchess of Orleans">Duchess of Orleans</a>.  1816."  On its southern face is a carved image of a saint carrying the <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a> of a bishop.  The monument is very degraded.
</p>
