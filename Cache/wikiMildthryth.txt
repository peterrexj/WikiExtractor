<p>Saint <b>Mildrith</b>, also <b>Mildthryth</b>, <b>Mildryth</b> and <b>Mildred</b>, (<a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Mildþrȳð</i></span>) (born c. 660, died after 732), was a 7th- and 8th-century <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of the Abbey at <a href="/wiki/Minster-in-Thanet" title="Minster-in-Thanet">Minster-in-Thanet</a>, Kent. She was declared a saint after her death, and, in 1030, her remains were moved to Canterbury.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_family">Life and family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mildrith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mildrith was the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Merewalh" title="Merewalh">Merewalh</a> of <a href="/wiki/Magonsaete" class="mw-redirect" title="Magonsaete">Magonsaete</a>, an area similar to the present day <a href="/wiki/Herefordshire" title="Herefordshire">Herefordshire</a>, a sub-kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>. Her mother was <a href="/wiki/Domne_Eafe" title="Domne Eafe">Domne Eafe</a> (also sometimes named as Saint Eormenburga),<sup id="cite_ref-book_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-book-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> herself a great-granddaughter of <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelberht_of_Kent" title="Æthelberht of Kent">Æthelberht of Kent</a>, and as such appearing in the so-called <a href="/wiki/Kentish_Royal_Legend" title="Kentish Royal Legend">Kentish Royal Legend</a>.
</p><p>Her sisters <a href="/wiki/Mildburh" title="Mildburh">Milburga of Much Wenlock</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mildgytha" class="mw-redirect" title="Mildgytha">Mildgyth</a> were also considered saints, and Mildrith, along with her extended family, features in the Kentish Royal Legend, also known as the "Mildrith Legend".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHollis199842_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHollis199842-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In the 11th century, <a href="/wiki/Goscelin" title="Goscelin">Goscelin</a> wrote a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> of Mildrith, the <i>Vita Mildrethae</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Rollason_1982_p._16_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rollason_1982_p._16-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Another work, the <i>Nova Legenda Anglie</i> of 1516, gives an extensive account of her life.
</p><p>Mildrith's maternal family had close ties to the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> rulers of <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Gaul</a>, and Mildrith is said to have been educated at the prestigious Merovingian royal <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Chelles" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Chelles">abbey of Chelles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-book_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-book-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She entered <a href="/wiki/Minster_in_Thanet_Priory" title="Minster in Thanet Priory">the abbey</a> of <a href="/wiki/Minster-in-Thanet" title="Minster-in-Thanet">Minster-in-Thanet</a>, which her mother had established, and became abbess there by 694. A number of dedications to Mildrith exist in the <a href="/wiki/Pas-de-Calais" title="Pas-de-Calais">Pas-de-Calais</a>, including at <a href="/wiki/Millam" title="Millam">Millam</a>, thereby suggesting that ties to Gaul were maintained. Mildrith died at Minster-in-Thanet some time after 732 and was buried there in the Abbey Church of St Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-Rollason_1982_p._16_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rollason_1982_p._16-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relic_remains">Relic remains</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mildrith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relic remains">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG/220px-Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG/330px-Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG/440px-Bronze_casket_for_St_Mildred%27s_relics.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1704" data-file-height="2272" /></a><figcaption>Bronze casket for St Mildred's relics in Minster Abbey, made (1955) by Sister <a href="/wiki/Concordia_Scott" title="Concordia Scott">Concordia Scott</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Mildrith's successor as abbess, Eadburg (also styled <a href="/wiki/Edburga_of_Minster-in-Thanet" title="Edburga of Minster-in-Thanet">Edburga of Minster-in-Thanet</a>, a correspondent of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a>), built a new abbey church, also at Minster-in-Thanet, dedicated to Ss Peter and Paul, and translated Mildrith's remains there not later than 748.<sup id="cite_ref-Rollason_1982_p._16_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rollason_1982_p._16-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The shrine within the abbey became a popular place of local pilgrimage, with Mildrith becoming a much-loved local patron saint.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The last abbess of Minster in Thanet was Leofruna, who was captured by Danes in 1011. The abbey was abandoned and the church downgraded to a parish church.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Mildrith's remains, despite fierce local opposition,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> in 1030,<sup id="cite_ref-book_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-book-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> an event commemorated on 18 May. <a href="/wiki/St_Mildred%27s_Church,_Canterbury" title="St Mildred&#39;s Church, Canterbury">St Mildred's Church, Canterbury</a>, within the town walls, dates back to this time.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some of her relics were given, in the 11th century, to a church at <a href="/wiki/Deventer" title="Deventer">Deventer</a>, Netherlands. In 1881 the feast day of St Mildred was officially reinstated by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-chron_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chron-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In 1882, following a refounding of a Benedictine monastery at Minster in Thanet, the nuns petitioned the Archbishop of Utrecht, who granted their return to Thanet.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It became a private house until 1937, when it was purchased by Benedictine nuns from St Walburga's Abbey in <a href="/wiki/Eichst%C3%A4tt" title="Eichstätt">Eichstätt</a>, Bavaria, as a refuge from persecution and became a dependent priory. In 1953, a relic of St Mildred was brought there.<sup id="cite_ref-chron_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chron-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_tree">Family tree</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mildrith&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Family tree">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The family tree of this part of the royal family of Kent in the 7th century is derived from the later Old English and Latin accounts. Eadbold became king in 616 A.D, succeeded by Eorcantberht in 640 A.D. (possibly co-ruling with his brother Eormenred, Mildrith's grandfather). Ecgberht came to the throne in 664 and died in 673 A.D.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
