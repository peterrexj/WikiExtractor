<p><b>Pope Innocent VII</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Innocentius VII</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Innocenzo VII</i>; 1339<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> – 6 November 1406), born <b>Cosimo de' Migliorati</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> from 17 October 1404 to his death in November 1406. He was pope during the period of the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> (1378–1417), and was opposed by the <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a> claimant <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Benedict_XIII" title="Antipope Benedict XIII">Benedict XIII</a>. Despite good intentions, he did little to end the schism, owing to the troubled state of affairs in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and his distrust of the sincerity of Benedict XIII, and King <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_of_Naples" title="Ladislaus of Naples">Ladislaus of Naples</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cosimo de' Migliorati was born to a simple family of <a href="/wiki/Sulmona" title="Sulmona">Sulmona</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Abruzzi" class="mw-redirect" title="Abruzzi">Abruzzi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELightbown2004216_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELightbown2004216-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He distinguished himself by his learning in both civil and <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">Canon Law</a>, which he taught for a time at <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>. His teacher <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_da_Legnano" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni da Legnano">Giovanni da Legnano</a> sponsored him at Rome, where <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a> (1378–89) took him into the <a href="/wiki/Curia" title="Curia">Curia</a>, sent him for ten years as papal collector to <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> made him <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bologna" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bologna">Bishop of Bologna</a> in 1386 at a time of strife in that city, and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> in 1387.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> made him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a>-priest of S. Croce in Gerusalemme (1389) and sent him as legate to Lombardy and Tuscany in 1390.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When Boniface IX died, there were present in Rome delegates from the rival pope at Avignon, Benedict XIII. The Roman cardinals asked these delegates whether their master would abdicate if the cardinals refrained from holding an election. When they were bluntly told that Benedict XIII would never abdicate (indeed he never did), the cardinals proceeded to an election. First, however, they each undertook a solemn oath to leave nothing undone, and, if need be, lay down the <a href="/wiki/Papal_tiara" title="Papal tiara">tiara</a> to end the schism.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_(FindID_602224).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_%28FindID_602224%29.jpg/220px-Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_%28FindID_602224%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="110" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_%28FindID_602224%29.jpg/330px-Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_%28FindID_602224%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_%28FindID_602224%29.jpg/440px-Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_%28FindID_602224%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4072" data-file-height="2027" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Papal_bulla_of_Pope_Innocent_VII_(FindID_602224).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Papal_bulla" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal bulla">Papal bulla</a> of Innocent VII</div></div></div>
<p>Migliorati was <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1404" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1404">unanimously chosen</a> – by eight cardinals – on 17 October 1404 and took the name of Innocent VII. There was a general riot by the <a href="/wiki/Ghibelline" class="mw-redirect" title="Ghibelline">Ghibelline</a> party in Rome when news of his election got out, but peace was maintained by the aid of King <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_of_Naples" title="Ladislaus of Naples">Ladislaus of Naples</a>, who hastened to Rome with a band of soldiers to assist the Pope in suppressing the insurrection. For his services the king extorted various concessions from Innocent VII, among them the promise that Ladislaus' claim to Naples would not be compromised,<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which claim had been challenged until very recently by <a href="/wiki/Louis_II_of_Anjou" title="Louis II of Anjou">Louis II of Anjou</a>. That suited Innocent VII, who had no intention of reaching an agreement with Avignon that would compromise his claims to the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>. Thus Innocent VII was laid under embarrassing obligations, from which he freed himself.
</p><p>Innocent VII had made the great mistake of elevating his highly unsuitable nephew <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ludovico_Migliorati&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ludovico Migliorati (page does not exist)">Ludovico Migliorati</a> – a colorful <a href="/wiki/Condottiero" title="Condottiero">condottiero</a> formerly in the pay of <a href="/wiki/Giangaleazzo_Visconti" class="mw-redirect" title="Giangaleazzo Visconti">Giangaleazzo Visconti</a> of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> – to be Captain of the Papal Militia, an act of nepotism that cost him dearly.<sup id="cite_ref-treccani.it_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-treccani.it-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Innocent further named him the rector of Todi in April 1405.<sup id="cite_ref-treccani.it_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-treccani.it-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In August 1405, Ludovico Migliorati, using his power as head of the militia, seized eleven members of the obstreperous Roman partisans on their return from a conference with the Pope, had them murdered in his own house, and had their bodies thrown from the windows of the hospital of Santo Spirito into the street. There was an uproar. Pope, court and cardinals, with the Migliorati faction, fled towards <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>. Ludovico took the occasion of driving off cattle that were grazing outside the walls, and the Papal party were pursued by furious Romans, losing thirty members, whose bodies were abandoned in the flight, including the Abbot of Perugia, struck down under the eyes of the Pope.
</p><p>Innocent's protector Ladislaus sent a squad of troops to quell the riots, and by January 1406 the Romans again acknowledged Papal temporal authority, and Innocent VII felt able to return. But Ladislaus, not content with the former concessions, desired to extend his authority in Rome and the Papal States. To attain his end he aided the Ghibelline faction in Rome in their revolutionary attempts in 1405. A squad of troops which King Ladislaus had sent to the aid of the <a href="/wiki/Colonna" class="mw-redirect" title="Colonna">Colonna</a> faction was still occupying the <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Castle of Sant' Angelo</a>, ostensibly protecting the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a>, but making frequent sorties upon Rome and the neighbouring territory. Only after Ladislaus was <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> did he yield to the demands of the Pope and withdraw his troops.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after his accession in 1404 Innocent VII had taken steps to keep his oath by proclaiming a council to resolve the Western Schism. King <a href="/wiki/Charles_VI_of_France" title="Charles VI of France">Charles VI of France</a>, theologians at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>, such as <a href="/wiki/Pierre_d%27Ailly" title="Pierre d&#39;Ailly">Pierre d'Ailly</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jean_Gerson" title="Jean Gerson">Jean Gerson</a>, and King <a href="/wiki/Rupert_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="Rupert of Germany">Rupert of Germany</a>, were all urging such a meeting. However, the troubles of 1405 furnished him with a pretext for postponing the meeting, claiming that he could not guarantee safe passage to his rival Benedict XIII if he came to the council in Rome. Benedict, however, made it appear that the only obstacle to the end of the Schism was the unwillingness of Innocent VII. Innocent VII was unreceptive to the proposal that he as well as Benedict XIII should resign in the interests of peace.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Innocent_VII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Innocent died in Rome on 6 November 1406.<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It is said that Innocent VII planned the restoration of the Roman University, but his death brought an end to such talk.
