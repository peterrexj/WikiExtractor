<p>Born on 25 November 1914 in <a href="/wiki/Pozoblanco" title="Pozoblanco">Pozoblanco</a>, <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba_(Spanish_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Córdoba (Spanish province)">Córdoba Province</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, Blanco was orphaned as a child, and raised by his aunt and uncle with whom he worked.  He was an excellent student, studying under the tutelage of the <a href="/wiki/Salesian" class="mw-redirect" title="Salesian">Salesians</a>.  He also served as a <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a> <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechist</a>, and at 18 was elected youth secretary of Catholic Action in Pozoblanco.<sup id="cite_ref-Hartman_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hartman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Arrest_and_trial">Arrest and trial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolom%C3%A9_Blanco&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Arrest and trial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 18 August 1936, Blanco was imprisoned while on military leave for refusing to be mobilised in the government's armed forces against <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Franco" title="Francisco Franco">Franco</a>'s military rebellion of July; on 24 September he was moved to a prison in Jaén.  There he was held with fifteen priests and other laymen. During his trial, Blanco remained true to his faith and his religious convictions. He did not protest his death sentence and told the court that if he lived he would continue being an active Catholic. He was judged, condemned to death and shot on 2 October 1936, charged with refusing to serve in the army in time of war.<sup id="cite_ref-Hartman_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hartman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Letters he wrote on the eve of his death to his family and to his girlfriend Maruja show his profound faith.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“May this be my last will: forgiveness, forgiveness, forgiveness; but indulgence, which I wish to be accompanied by doing them as much good as possible.  Therefore, I ask you to avenge me with the vengeance of a Christian: returning much good to those that have tried to do me evil,” he wrote to his relatives.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsagency.com_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsagency.com-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>According to documents supporting his cause for <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>, Blanco went to the site of his execution barefooted, "in order to be more conformed to Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsagency.com_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsagency.com-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>He kissed his handcuffs, surprising the guards that cuffed him.  He refused to be shot from behind.  “Whoever dies for Christ should do so facing forward and standing straight.  Long live Christ the King!” he shouted as he fell to the ground under a shower of bullets.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsagency.com_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsagency.com-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Blanco was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> 28 October 2007 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a>.
</p>
