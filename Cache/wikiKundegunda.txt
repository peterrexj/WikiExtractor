<p><b>Kinga of Poland</b> or <b>Kinga of Hungary</b>, also <b>Saint Kinga</b> (also known as <b>Cunegunda</b>; <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Święta Kinga</i>, <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Szent Kinga</i>) (5 March 1224<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKristó2003248_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKristó2003248-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002439_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002439-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>&#8211; 24 July 1292) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Patronage" title="Patronage">patroness</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kinga_of_Poland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born in <a href="/wiki/Esztergom" title="Esztergom">Esztergom</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a>, the daughter of King <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV_of_Hungary" title="Béla IV of Hungary">Béla IV of Hungary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maria_Laskarina" title="Maria Laskarina">Maria Laskarina</a>. She was a niece of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary" title="Elizabeth of Hungary">Elizabeth of Hungary</a> and great-niece of <a href="/wiki/Hedwig_of_Andechs" class="mw-redirect" title="Hedwig of Andechs">Hedwig of Andechs</a>. Kinga's sisters were <a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Hungary" title="Margaret of Hungary">Margaret of Hungary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jolenta_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Jolenta of Poland">Jolenta of Poland</a>. She reluctantly married <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_V_the_Chaste" title="Bolesław V the Chaste">Bolesław V</a> ("the Chaste") and became princess when her husband ascended the throne as <a href="/wiki/High_Duke_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="High Duke of Poland">High Duke of Poland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Sokol_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sokol-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Despite the marriage, the devout couple took up a vow of <a href="/wiki/Chastity" title="Chastity">chastity</a>. The marriage was largely arranged by, and the vow of chastity patterned after that of Bolesław's sister, <a href="/wiki/Salomea_of_Poland" title="Salomea of Poland">Salomea of Poland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002242–243_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKlaniczay2002242–243-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During her reign Kinga got involved in charitable works such as visiting the poor and helping the lepers. When her husband died in 1279, she sold all her material possessions and gave the money to the poor. She soon did not want any part in governing the kingdom which was left to her, and decided to become a <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clare">Poor Clare</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> in the monastery at Sandec (<a href="/wiki/Stary_S%C4%85cz" title="Stary Sącz">Stary Sącz</a>). She would spend the rest of her life in contemplative prayer and did not allow anyone to refer to her past role as Grand Duchess of Poland. She died on 24 July 1292, aged 68.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Alexander_VIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexander VIII">Alexander VIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> Kinga in 1690. In 1695, she was made chief <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patroness</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a>. On 16 June 1999, she was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kinga_of_Poland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Legend has it that Kinga threw her engagement ring into the <a href="/wiki/Aknaszlatina" class="mw-redirect" title="Aknaszlatina">Aknaszlatina</a> salt mine in what was then Hungary. The ring miraculously traveled along with salt deposits to <a href="/wiki/Wieliczka" title="Wieliczka">Wieliczka</a>, where it was rediscovered. On this spot the miners erected a statue of Saint Kinga, carved entirely from salt, which is 101 meters under the Earth's surface.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
