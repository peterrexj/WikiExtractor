<p><b>Ana Petra Pérez Florido</b> (6 December 1845 – 16 August 1906), also known as <b>Petra of Saint Joseph</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Spain" title="Catholic Church in Spain">Spanish Catholic</a> nun.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She established the Congregation of the Mothers of the Abandoned to care for the abandoned as well as the elderly and infirm.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 16 October 1994.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ana_Petra_P%C3%A9rez_Florido&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ana Petra Pérez Florido was born on December 6, 1845 in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and was the last of five children to José Perez and Maria Florido; she was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> with the name of "Ana Josefa".
</p><p>Her mother died when she was three and her paternal grandmother, Teresa Reina, was interested in her education and so assumed control of her education. From her she learnt about the importance of the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> as well as a devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">Mother of God</a> as well as a special devotion to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Twice two men of good families asked her for marriage but her father rejected such proposals for political reasons. It was all to her relief for she said: "I have no vocation for marriage". True to her word she declined all future opportunities for marriage and her parents did not respond well to her refusals and were even harsher when she informed her parents of her desire to join religious life as a servant of <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>.
</p><p>Her parents attempted to prevent her from responding to her vocation and she could not enter the Congregation of the Little Sisters of the Poor due to their interference. In 1872 her father relented and gave her his blessing for joining the religious life; her father died on 11 January 1875. Florido at once began catering to the needs of the old and the abandoned and soon town officials requested that she open a house for the old which she did this on 19 March 1875; the home was named "The Porch of Bethlehem". But she later became a member of the Mercedarians in the 1870s (accepted their habit on 1 November 1878) though soon decided to leave and establish her own congregation and approval was granted on 25 December 1880 for her to do so; her first profession saw her assume the name "Petra of Saint Joseph" in 1891.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The local bishop granted his approval of the order at the beginning of 1883 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> granted his formal approval of the order in 1891. On 23 March 1891 she had set off for <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to ask the pope to grant papal approval to her order and on arrival met with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Isidoro_Verga" title="Isidoro Verga">Isidoro Verga</a>. Her group then met with the pope and attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> where she received the Eucharist from him. Her perpetual profession was on 15 October 1891. Florido travelled to Rome in 1905.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1883 she opened both a kindergarten and hospital in <a href="/wiki/Ronda,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Ronda, Spain">Ronda</a> and later opened two houses; one in <a href="/wiki/Andujar" class="mw-redirect" title="Andujar">Andujar</a> in 1885 and one in <a href="/wiki/Martos,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Martos, Spain">Martos</a> in 1887.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Florido died on 16 August 1906 in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> and her funeral was held on 18 August. Her remains were transferred elsewhere on 5 November 1920 but disappeared during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The remains were found in 1983 and reburied on 10 June 1984.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ana_Petra_P%C3%A9rez_Florido&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG/190px-SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG" decoding="async" width="190" height="143" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG/285px-SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG/380px-SJosepMuntanya-tombaBeataJosefa.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1944" /></a><figcaption><b>Ana Petra Pérez Florido's coffin under the Altar</b></figcaption></figure>
<p>The Beatification process commenced in the Barcelona archdiocese in an informative process that opened in 1932 concluded its work in 1933 which was the accumulation of all documentation and witness testimonies. This all occurred despite the fact that the cause did not open on a formal level on 3 December 1944 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> which conferred upon him the posthumous title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>; her spiritual writings received theological approval on 26 February 1943. The second process in Barcelona continued the work of the first process in 1948 and it concluded in 1949. The formal decree of ratification of both processes came from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> on 4 May 1952 and it ensured that the cause proceeded to the next stage.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> and their consultants approved the cause on 16 December 1969 while the C.C.S. alone approved it sometime later on 15 December 1970. Florido was proclaimed to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 14 June 1971 after <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> recognized that she had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The miracle for beatification was investigated and had received C.C.S. validation on 18 October 1991. The medical experts approved this case on 4 February 1993 as did the theologians on 7 May 1993 and the C.C.S. on 15 June 1993. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved the miracle on 6 July 1993 and beatified Florido in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 16 October 1994.
</p>
