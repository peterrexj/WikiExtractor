<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p><b>William of Æbelholt</b> (also known as Vilhelm of Æbelholt, William of Eskilsø and William of Paris) (d. <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> Sunday, 1203) was a <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a>-born churchman of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Denmark">Denmark</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_%C3%86belholt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William was born of a noble French family and educated by his uncle Hugh, forty-second abbot of <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Germain-des-Pr%C3%A9s" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés">Saint-Germain-des-Prés</a> at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, and, having been ordained subdeacon, received a <a href="/wiki/Prebend" class="mw-redirect" title="Prebend">prebend</a> in the church of <a href="/wiki/Sainte-Genevi%C3%A8ve-du-Mont" class="mw-redirect" title="Sainte-Geneviève-du-Mont">Sainte-Geneviève-du-Mont</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-wmp_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wmp-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> William reportedly sought entry into a stricter house (either a <a href="/wiki/Cluniac" class="mw-redirect" title="Cluniac">Cluniac</a> or a <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> monastery) while still in his youth, though he decided to remain at Ste-Geneviève. According to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographic</a> sources, his exemplary life did not commend him to his fellow canons, who tried to rid themselves of his presence, and even prevented by slander his ordination to the diaconate by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Paris">Bishop of Paris</a>. William obtained this order from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Senlis" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Senlis">Bishop of Senlis</a> by his uncle's intercession, and was soon afterwards presented by the canons to the little <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> of <a href="/wiki/%C3%89pinay,_Eure" title="Épinay, Eure">Épinay</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1148, by order of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III" title="Pope Eugene III">Pope Eugene III</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Secular_canons" class="mw-redirect" title="Secular canons">secular canons</a> of Ste-Geneviève were replaced by <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a> from the Parisian <a href="/wiki/St._Victor%27s_Abbey,_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Victor&#39;s Abbey, Paris">monastery of St. Victor</a>, whose prior, Odo, was made abbot of Ste-Geneviève. William soon afterwards joined the new community and was made sub-prior.<sup id="cite_ref-wmp_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wmp-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In this position he showed great zeal for the religious life, and on one occasion opposed the entry of a new prior who had obtained his position irregularly. For this he was punished by Abbot Garin, successor of Odo, but his action was finally supported by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  Such actions apparently put William at odds with his abbot, who subjected him to humiliating discipline, about which the canon complained bitterly to the pope.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Work_in_Denmark">Work in Denmark</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_%C3%86belholt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Work in Denmark">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1161 <a href="/wiki/Absalon" title="Absalon">Absalon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Roskilde" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Roskilde">bishop of Roskilde</a> (and later <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lund" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lund">archbishop of Lund</a>) in Denmark, sent to Paris the <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a> of his cathedral (almost surely the Danish historian <a href="/wiki/Saxo_Grammaticus" title="Saxo Grammaticus">Saxo Grammaticus</a>) to obtain canons regular for the reform of the <a href="/wiki/Canonry" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonry">canonry</a> of St. Thomas at <a href="/wiki/Eskils%C3%B8_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Eskilsø Abbey">Eskilsø</a>.  Absalon and William were said to have formed a close friendship when the former was studying at the schools of Paris.  In 1165 William journeyed to Denmark with three companions, and became abbot of that house.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When Abbot William arrived there were only six religious left at Eskilsø, two of whom were dismissed when they refused to submit to the new rule.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Denmark was an unwelcome destination for these French churchmen.  Reiterating themes from the history of early-medieval Christian missions, William and his men were frightened by the culture and language of their new home, and the new abbot's original companions soon left him and returned to France, a permission denied to William.
</p><p>Nevertheless, despite difficulties arising from poverty and opposition on the part of the community, he reformed the monastery<sup id="cite_ref-wmp_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wmp-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and in 1176 transferred it to <a href="/wiki/%C3%86belholt_Abbey" title="Æbelholt Abbey">Æbelholt</a>, dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Paraclete" title="Paraclete">Paraclete</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Sj%C3%A6lland" class="mw-redirect" title="Sjælland">Sjælland</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Region_Hovedstaden" class="mw-redirect" title="Region Hovedstaden">Region Hovedstaden</a>), near the present-day town of <a href="/wiki/Hiller%C3%B8d" title="Hillerød">Hillerød</a>.  As abbot, William worked to institute the standards of religious discipline emerging from reform centers in the heartlands of <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Christendom</a>.  These included a stricter insistence on <a href="/wiki/Claustration" class="mw-redirect" title="Claustration">claustration</a>.  William also worked to establish closer links among Danish church institutions similarly committed to ecclesiastical rigor.  These included other communities in the <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian tradition</a> (such as <a href="/wiki/Vestervig_Abbey" title="Vestervig Abbey">Vestervig</a>), but also extended to <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> houses, most notably the monks of nearby <a href="/wiki/Esrum_Abbey" title="Esrum Abbey">Esrum Abbey</a>. In 1183 there were 25 monks and the monastery also functioned as a hostel for visitors. Sometimes more than a 100 persons were fed and the board and lodging was free.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>William also continued to serve as an intermediary between Denmark and France. He likely had a hand in arranging for Absalon's relative <a href="/w/index.php?title=Peder_Sunesen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peder Sunesen (page does not exist)">Peder Sunesen</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q12331202#sitelinks-wikipedia" class="extiw" title="d:Special:EntityPage/Q12331202"><span title="&quot;Peder Sunesen&quot; in other languages">Wikidata</span></a>&#93;</span> (who replaced Absalon as bishop in Roskilde in 1192) to study at Ste-Geneviève.  Peder continued to enjoy a closer relationship with William after his return.
</p><p>Nowhere was William's role as a conduit between Gallic and Danish society more apparent, however, than in the "Ingeborg Affair."  Working apparently on behalf of King <a href="/wiki/Knud_VI_of_Denmark" class="mw-redirect" title="Knud VI of Denmark">Knud VI of Denmark</a>, and Absalon, William intervened in the case of <a href="/wiki/Philip_Augustus" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Augustus">Philip Augustus</a> of France who was attempting to repudiate his wife <a href="/wiki/Ingeborg_of_Denmark,_Queen_of_France" title="Ingeborg of Denmark, Queen of France">Ingeborg</a> (1175–1236), daughter of <a href="/wiki/Valdemar_I_of_Denmark" title="Valdemar I of Denmark">Valdemar I of Denmark</a>.  It was on this occasion that he composed a genealogy of the <a href="/wiki/Danish_kings" class="mw-redirect" title="Danish kings">Danish kings</a>, intended to disprove the alleged impediment of <a href="/wiki/Consanguinity" title="Consanguinity">consanguinity</a> between Ingeborg and Philip.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_canonization">Death and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_%C3%86belholt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the <i>Vita</i> composed to support his candidacy for <a href="/wiki/Sainthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Sainthood">sainthood</a>, William died on Easter Sunday 1202, though the actual year was 1203.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Numerous <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> were reported at his grave, and in 1218 the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lund" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lund">Archbishop of Lund</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anders_Sunesen" title="Anders Sunesen">Anders Sunesen</a>, requested that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> appoint a local commission to investigate the claims for William's sanctity.  Their report was presumably sent to Rome, where it languished for some time.  Then, in January 1224, William was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by Honorius,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> who acted on additional information provided by Cardinal Gregorius de Crescentio, recently returned from a papal legation to Denmark.  A new church was constructed at Æbelholt Abbey and William's remains were moved there in 1238. In time, relics of St. William were distributed to Roskilde Cathedral, Lund Cathedral, the Church of Our Lady (Vor Frue Kirke) and Greyfriars Church in Copenhagen, and Greyfriars Church in Roskilde.
</p>

<h2><span id="Sources_for_William.27s_life_and_career"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources_for_William's_life_and_career">Sources for William's life and career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_%C3%86belholt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sources for William&#039;s life and career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William of Æbelholt was the subject of a saint's <i>Vita et miracula</i>, composed most likely as part of his canonization process.  It was printed in M.C. Gertz, ed., <i>Vitae Sanctorum Danorum</i> (Copenhagen, 1908–12), pp.&#160;285–369.  Gertz also included in that edition a brief treatise supposedly written by William on the authenticity of the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of <a href="/wiki/Genevieve" title="Genevieve">Geneviève</a> (pp.&#160;378–82).  William's genealogy of the Danish kings can be found in M.C. Gertz, ed., <i>Scriptores Miniores Historiae Danicae Medii Aevi</i>, vol. 1 (Copenhagen, 1970; reprint of Copenhagen, 1917–18), pp.&#160;176–85.  William also left a substantial collection of letters, published most recently in the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Diplomatarium_Danicum&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Diplomatarium Danicum (page does not exist)">Diplomatarium Danicum</a></i>, vol. 3,pt. 2, ed. C.A. Christensen, Herluf Nielsen, and Lauritz Weibull (Copenhagen, 1977).  Further evidence can be found in charters dealing with Æbelholt (mostly transmitted through the archives of Æbelholt Abbey and its closest neighbor, Esrum).  These have been published in the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Diplomatarium_Danicum&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Diplomatarium Danicum (page does not exist)">Diplomatarium Danicum</a></i>.
</p>
