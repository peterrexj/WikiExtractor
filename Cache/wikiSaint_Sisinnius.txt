<p><b>Vigilius of Trent</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Vigilio di Trento</i>, <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Vigilius von Trient</i>; c. 353 – 26 June 405) is venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trent" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trent">bishop of Trent</a>. He should not be confused with the <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vigilius" title="Pope Vigilius">pope of the same name</a>. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vigilius_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, he was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Patrician_(ancient_Rome)" title="Patrician (ancient Rome)">patrician</a>, the son of Maxentia and a man whose name is sometimes given as Theodosius. His brothers, Claudian and Magorian, are also venerated as saints.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Vigilius was educated at <a href="/wiki/Athens" title="Athens">Athens</a> and seems to have been a friend of <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>. 
</p><p>In 380, Vigilius settled in the city of <a href="/wiki/Trento" title="Trento"> Trent</a> and was chosen as the city's bishop. He may have been consecrated by either <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a> of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> or Valerian (Valerianus) of Aquileia. Ambrose donated the episcopal insignia and showed a paternal solicitude for Vigilius. As bishop, Vigilius attempted to convert <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arians</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagans</a> to Nicene <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and is said to have founded thirty parishes in his diocese. He is traditionally regarded as the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Santa_Maria_Maggiore,_Trento" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Santa Maria Maggiore, Trento"> Church of Santa Maria Maggiore, Trent</a>. A letter attributed to Ambrose encourages Vigilius to oppose marriages between Christians and pagans.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Vigilius also preached in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a>, which lay outside of his diocese.
</p><p>His companions during his missions were Sisinnius, Martyrius and Alexander, who were sent by Ambrose to assist Vigilius.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Tradition makes these three natives of <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>. A work called <i>De Martyrio SS. Sisinnii, Martyrii et Alexandri</i> is attributed to Vigilius.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 397, Sisinnius, Martyrius and Alexander (<i>Sisinio, Martirio e Alessandro</i>) were killed at <a href="/wiki/Sanzeno" title="Sanzeno">Sanzeno</a> after they attempted to convert the local population there to Christianity. Vigilius forgave their killers and had the remains of the three men sent to John Chrysostom in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, as well as to <a href="/wiki/Simplician" title="Simplician">Simplician</a>, Ambrose's successor, in Milan. Milan would later give some of those relics back to Sanzeno in the 20th century, where they rest in the (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Basilica_dei_Santi_Martiri&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Basilica dei Santi Martiri (page does not exist)">Basilica dei Santi Martiri</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_dei_Santi_Martiri" class="extiw" title="it:Basilica dei Santi Martiri">it</a>&#93;</span>).<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vigilius is associated with the legend of <a href="/wiki/Romedius" title="Romedius">Romedius</a>, who is often depicted alongside or astride a <a href="/wiki/Bear" title="Bear">bear</a>. According to Romedius' <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, Romedius once wished to visit Vigilius, a friend of his youth, but Romedius' horse was torn to pieces by a wild bear. Romedius, however, had the bear bridled by his disciple David (Davide). The bear became docile and carried Romedius on its back to <a href="/wiki/Trento" title="Trento">Trento</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vigilius_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg/300px-Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="203" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg/450px-Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg/600px-Punta_San_Vigilio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1023" data-file-height="691" /></a><figcaption>Punta San Vigilio, where Vigilius is said to have been killed</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to a much later tradition,<sup id="cite_ref-santi_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Vigilius, who had been accompanied by his brothers Claudian and Magorian as well as a priest named Julian, was killed in the present-day parish of <a href="/wiki/Rendena" title="Rendena">Rendena</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Val_Rendena" title="Val Rendena">Rendena Valley</a>, where he had been preaching to the locals there, who worshipped the god <a href="/wiki/Saturn_(mythology)" title="Saturn (mythology)">Saturn</a>. Vigilius said <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> and overturned a statue of the god into the <a href="/wiki/Sarca" title="Sarca">Sarca River</a>. As punishment, he was <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoned to death</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> near <a href="/wiki/Lake_Garda" title="Lake Garda">Lake Garda</a> at the area called Punta San Vigilio.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ironically, a statue of the god <a href="/wiki/Neptune_(mythology)" title="Neptune (mythology)">Neptune</a> stands in front of Vigilius' shrine in Trent today.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vigilius_of_Trent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg/220px-Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="284" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg/330px-Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg/440px-Trento_Teatro_sociale_per_la_fiera_di_s._Vigilio_primavera_1857.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4600" data-file-height="5945" /></a><figcaption>Theatre playbill for the S. Vigilio Fair in 1857, preserved in the Municipal Library of Trento</figcaption></figure>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Trento_Duomo.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/Trento_Duomo.jpg/320px-Trento_Duomo.jpg" decoding="async" width="320" height="251" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/Trento_Duomo.jpg/480px-Trento_Duomo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/Trento_Duomo.jpg/640px-Trento_Duomo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="981" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>Trent Cathedral with the Fountain of Neptune</figcaption></figure>
<p>Vigilius was buried at a church that he built at Trent, later expanded by his successor Eugippius, and dedicated to Vigilius. This became <a href="/wiki/Trento_Cathedral" title="Trento Cathedral">Trento Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was immediately venerated after his death, and the acts of his life and death were sent to Rome, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent I</a>, according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i>, "seems to have made a formal <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>, for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Benedict XIV</a> calls Vigilius the first martyr canonized by a pope."<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vigilius' arm was removed as a separate relic and placed into its own reliquary in 1386.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He is venerated in <a href="/wiki/County_of_Tyrol" title="County of Tyrol">Tyrol</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  A <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a> farmers' saying associated with a 2nd feast day of 31 January was: "Friert es zu Vigilius / im März die Eiseskälte kommen muss!" ("If it freezes on St. Vigilius' Day, frost will come in March!").<sup id="cite_ref-OH_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OH-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  There are similar sayings associated with other "<a href="/wiki/Weather_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Weather saints">weather saints</a>".
</p>
