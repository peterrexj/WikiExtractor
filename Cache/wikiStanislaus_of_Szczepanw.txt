<p><b>Stanislaus of Szczepanów</b> (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Stanisław ze Szczepanowa</i>; 26 July 1030&#160;– 11 April 1079) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Krak%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Kraków">Bishop of Kraków</a> known chiefly for having been martyred by the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Polish_monarchs" title="List of Polish monarchs">Polish King</a> <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_II_the_Generous" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolesław II the Generous">Bolesław II the Generous</a>. Stanislaus is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> as <b>Saint Stanislaus the Martyr</b> (as distinct from the 16th-century <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_Kostka" title="Stanislaus Kostka">Stanislaus Kostka</a>).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, Stanislaus, or Stanisław in Polish, was born at <a href="/wiki/Szczepan%C3%B3w,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship" title="Szczepanów, Lesser Poland Voivodeship">Szczepanów</a>, a village in <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Poland" title="Lesser Poland">Lesser Poland</a>, the only son of the noble and pious Wielisław and Bogna. He was educated at a cathedral school in <a href="/wiki/Gniezno" title="Gniezno">Gniezno</a> (then the capital of Poland) and later, probably at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On his return to Poland, Stanislaus was ordained a priest by <a href="/wiki/Lambert_Su%C5%82a" title="Lambert Suła">Lambert II Suła</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Krak%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Kraków">Bishop of Kraków</a>. Following his ordination, he was given a canonry in Kraków and became known for his preaching. He was subsequently made pastor of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Czembocz&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Czembocz (page does not exist)">Czembocz</a> near <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, canon and preacher at the cathedral, and later, vicar-general.
</p><p>After the bishop's death (1072), Stanislaus was elected his successor<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but accepted the office only at the explicit command of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a>. Stanislaus was one of the earliest native Polish bishops. He also became a ducal advisor and had some influence on Polish politics.
</p><p>Stanislaus' major accomplishments included bringing <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legates</a> to Poland, and reestablishment of a <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_see" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan see">metropolitan see</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gniezno" title="Gniezno">Gniezno</a>. The latter was a precondition for Duke Bolesław's coronation as king, which took place in 1076. Stanislaus then encouraged King Bolesław to establish <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monasteries to aid in the <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianization</a> of Poland.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/c0/Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg/220px-Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="170" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/c0/Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg/330px-Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/c/c0/Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg/440px-Swiety_Stanislaw_prowadzi_Piotrowina_przed_sad_krolewski.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1312" data-file-height="1011" /></a><figcaption>Saint Stanislaus leads Piotr before the royal tribunal.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Stanislaus initial conflict with King Bolesław was over a land dispute. Stanislaus  had purchased for the diocese a piece of land on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Vistula" title="Vistula">Vistula</a> River near <a href="/wiki/Lublin" title="Lublin">Lublin</a> from a certain Peter (<i>Piotr</i>), but after Piotr's death the land had been claimed by his family. The King ruled for the claimants, but according to legend, Stanislaus  resurrected Piotr so that he could confirm that he had sold the land to the bishop.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Augustin_Calmet" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustin Calmet">Augustin Calmet</a>, an 18th-century <a href="/wiki/Bible_scholar" class="mw-redirect" title="Bible scholar">Bible scholar</a>, Stanislaus asked the King for three days to produce his witness, Piotr. The King and the court were said to have laughed at the absurd request, but the King granted Stanislaus the three days. Stanislaus spent them in ceaseless prayer and, dressed in full bishop's regalia, went with a procession to the cemetery where Piotr had been buried three years earlier. He had Piotr's grave dug up until his remains were discovered. Then, before a multitude of witnesses, Stanislaus made Piotr rise, and Piotr did so.
</p><p>Piotr was then dressed in a cloak and brought before King Bolesław to testify on Stanislaus' behalf. The dumbfounded court heard Piotr reprimand his three sons and testify that Stanislaus had indeed paid for the land. Unable to give any other verdict, the King dismissed the suit against the bishop. Stanislaus asked Piotr whether he would remain alive, but Piotr declined and so was laid to rest once more in his grave and was reburied.
</p>
<h2><span id="Chastisement_of_Boles.C5.82aw"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Chastisement_of_Bolesław">Chastisement of Bolesław</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Chastisement of Bolesław">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg/250px-Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="363" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg/375px-Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg/500px-Saintstanislausanjoulegendarium.jpg 2x" data-file-width="700" data-file-height="1015" /></a><figcaption>1- Saint Stanislaus being ordained as bishop. 2- Saint Stanislaus resurrects Peter. 3-King Bolesław murders Saint Stanislaus. 4-Stanislaus' body is cut into pieces. Image from the <a href="/wiki/Hungarians" title="Hungarians">Hungarian</a> Kings' <a href="/wiki/Anjou_Legendarium" title="Anjou Legendarium">Anjou Legendarium</a> of the 14th century.</figcaption></figure>
<p>A more substantial conflict with King Bolesław  arose after a prolonged war in <a href="/wiki/Ruthenia" title="Ruthenia">Ruthenia</a>, when weary warriors deserted and went home, alarmed at tidings that their overseers were taking over their estates and wives. According to <a href="/wiki/Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek" title="Wincenty Kadłubek">Wincenty Kadłubek</a>, the King punished the soldiers' faithless wives very cruelly and was criticized for it by Bishop Stanislaus. <a href="/wiki/Jan_D%C5%82ugosz" title="Jan Długosz">Jan Długosz</a>, however, writes that the bishop had in fact criticized the King for his own sexual immorality. <a href="/wiki/Gallus_Anonymus" title="Gallus Anonymus">Gallus Anonymus</a>, in his laconic account, only condemned both the "traitor bishop" and the violent king.
</p><p>Whatever the actual cause of the conflict between them, the result was that the bishop <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> King Bolesław,<sup id="cite_ref-EB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> which included forbidding the saying of the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a> by the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ccnon_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ccnon (priest) (page does not exist)">canons</a> of <a href="/wiki/Krakow_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Krakow Cathedral">Krakow Cathedral</a> in case Bolesław attended.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The excommunication aided the King's political opponents, and the King accused Bishop Stanislaus of treason and the royal court found him guilty.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg/170px-File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="261" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg/255px-File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg/340px-File-Gothic_stained_glass_window_from_Dominican_Monastery_in_Krak%C3%B3w02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1508" data-file-height="2312" /></a><figcaption>13th-century effigy of Saint Stanislaus</figcaption></figure>
<p>King Bolesław sent his men to execute Bishop Stanislaus but when they didn't dare to touch the bishop, the King decided to kill the bishop himself.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He is said to have slain Stanislaus while he was celebrating <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ska%C5%82ka" title="Skałka">Skałka</a> outside the walls of Kraków.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to Paweł Jasienica: <i>Polska Piastów</i>, it was actually in the <a href="/wiki/Wawel" class="mw-redirect" title="Wawel">Wawel</a> castle. The guards then cut the bishop's body into pieces and scattered them to be devoured by wild beasts.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to the legend, his members miraculously reintegrated while the pool was guarded by four eagles.
</p><p>The exact date of Stanislaus's death is uncertain. According to different sources, it was either 11 April or 8 May 1079.
</p><p>The murder stirred outrage throughout the land and led to the dethronement of King <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_II_the_Bold" title="Bolesław II the Bold">Bolesław II the Bold</a>, who had to seek refuge in <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungary</a><sup id="cite_ref-EB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was succeeded by his brother, <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_I_Herman" title="Władysław I Herman">Władysław I Herman</a>.
</p><p>Whether Stanislaus should be regarded as a traitor or a hero remains one of the classic unresolved questions of Polish history. His story has a parallel in the murder of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a> in 1170 by henchmen of England's King <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Original_sources">Original sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Original sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is little information about Stanislaus's life. The only near-contemporary source was a chronicle of <a href="/wiki/Gallus_Anonymus" title="Gallus Anonymus">Gallus Anonymus</a>, but the author evaded writing details about a conflict with the king. Later sources are the chronicles of <a href="/wiki/Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek" title="Wincenty Kadłubek">Wincenty Kadłubek</a>, and two <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographies</a> by <a href="/wiki/Wincenty_of_Kielcza" title="Wincenty of Kielcza">Wincenty of Kielcza</a>. All contain hagiographic matter.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_as_a_saint">Veneration as a saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration as a saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cult of Saint Stanislaus the <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">Martyr</a> began immediately upon his death. In 1245 his relics were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> (<i>i.e.</i>, moved) to Kraków's <a href="/wiki/Wawel_Cathedral" title="Wawel Cathedral">Wawel Cathedral</a>. In the early 13th century, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Iwo_Odrow%C4%85%C5%BC" title="Iwo Odrowąż">Iwo Odrowąż</a> initiated preparations for Stanislaus' <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> and ordered Wincenty of Kielce to write the martyr's <i>vita</i>. Stanislaus of Krakow was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> at <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a> in 1253.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> did not include the saint's feast day in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> for use throughout the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. Subsequently, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> inserted it, setting it for 7 May, but Kraków observes it on 8 May, a supposed date of the saint's death, having done so since 8 May 1254, when it was attended by many Polish bishops and princes. In 1969, the Church moved the feast to 11 April, considered to be the date of his death in 1079.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG/220px-Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG/330px-Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG/440px-Konfesja_sw_Stanislawa2.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a><figcaption>Silver sarcophagus of St. Stanislaus in the Wawel Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<p>As the first native Polish saint, Stanislaus is the patron of Poland and Kraków,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and of some Polish dioceses. He shares the patronage of Poland with Saint <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Prague" title="Adalbert of Prague">Adalbert of Prague</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Florian" title="Saint Florian">Florian</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary_(Roman_Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary (Roman Catholic)">Our Lady</a> the Queen of Poland.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Wawel_Cathedral" title="Wawel Cathedral">Wawel Cathedral</a>, which holds the saint's relics, became a principal national shrine. Almost all of the Polish kings from <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_I_the_Elbow-high" class="mw-redirect" title="Władysław I the Elbow-high">Władysław I the Elbow-high</a> were crowned while they knelt before his sarcophagus, which stands in the middle of the cathedral. In the 17th century, King <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_IV_Vasa" title="Władysław IV Vasa">Władysław IV Vasa</a> commissioned an ornate silver coffin to hold the saint's relics. It was destroyed by Swedish troops during the <a href="/wiki/Deluge_(history)" title="Deluge (history)">Deluge</a> but was replaced with a new one c. 1670.
</p><p>Saint Stanislaus' veneration has had great patriotic importance. In the period of Poland's <a href="/wiki/History_of_Poland_during_the_Piast_dynasty#Fragmentation_of_the_realm_(1138–ca._1314)" title="History of Poland during the Piast dynasty">feudal fragmentation</a>, it was believed that Poland would one day reintegrate as had the members of his body. Half a millennium after Poland had indeed reintegrated, while yet another dismemberment of the polity was underway in the <a href="/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland" title="Partitions of Poland">Partitions of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a>, the framers of the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_of_3_May_1791" title="Constitution of 3 May 1791">Polish Constitution of 3 May 1791</a>, would dedicate this progressive political document to Saint Stanislaus, whose feast day fell close to the date of the Constitution's adoption.
</p><p>Each year on the first Sunday after 8 May, a procession, led by the Bishop of Kraków, goes out from <a href="/wiki/Wawel" class="mw-redirect" title="Wawel">Wawel</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Ska%C5%82ka" title="Skałka">Church on the Rock</a>. The procession, once a local event, was popularized in the 20th century by Polish <a href="/wiki/Primate_(bishop)" title="Primate (bishop)">Primate</a> <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Wyszy%C5%84ski" title="Stefan Wyszyński">Stefan Wyszyński</a> and Archbishop of Kraków, <a href="/wiki/Karol_Wojty%C5%82a" class="mw-redirect" title="Karol Wojtyła">Karol Wojtyła</a>. Wojtyla, as <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, called Saint Stanislaus the patron saint of moral order and wanted his first papal return to Poland to occur in April 1979 in observance of the 900th anniversary to the day of his martyrdom, but the Communist rulers of that time blocked this, causing the visit to be delayed until June of that year.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Roman Catholic churches belonging to <a href="/wiki/Polish_diaspora" title="Polish diaspora">Polish communities</a> outside Poland are often dedicated to Saint Stanislaus.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a>, Saint Stanislaus is usually depicted as a bishop holding a <a href="/wiki/Sword" title="Sword">sword</a>, the instrument of his martyrdom, and sometimes with Piotr rising from the dead at his feet.
</p>
