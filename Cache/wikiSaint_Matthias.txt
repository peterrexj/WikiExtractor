<p><b>Matthias</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="&#39;m&#39; <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop">ⲙⲁⲑⲓⲁⲥ</span>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;AD 80</span>) was, according to the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a> (written <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;AD 63</span>), chosen by God through the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Twelve Apostles">apostles</a> to replace <a href="/wiki/Judas_Iscariot" title="Judas Iscariot">Judas Iscariot</a> following the latter's <a href="/wiki/Betrayal_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Betrayal of Jesus">betrayal of Jesus</a> and his subsequent death.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His calling as an apostle is unique, in that his appointment was not made personally by Jesus, who had already <a href="/wiki/Ascension_of_Jesus" title="Ascension of Jesus">ascended into heaven</a>, and it was also made before the descent of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Spirit (Christianity)">Holy Spirit</a> upon the early Church.

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthias_the_Apostle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is no mention of a Matthias among the lists of disciples or followers of Jesus in the three <a href="/wiki/Synoptic_gospel" class="mw-redirect" title="Synoptic gospel">synoptic gospels</a>, but according to <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts</a>, he had been with Jesus from his <a href="/wiki/Baptism_of_Jesus" title="Baptism of Jesus">baptism</a> by <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John</a> until his <a href="/wiki/Ascension_of_Jesus" title="Ascension of Jesus">Ascension</a>. In the days following, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> proposed that the assembled disciples, who numbered about 120, nominate two men to replace Judas. They chose <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Barsabbas" title="Joseph Barsabbas">Joseph called Barsabas</a> (whose surname was Justus) and Matthias. Then they prayed, "Thou, Lord, which knowest the hearts of all [men], shew whether of these two thou hast chosen, That he may take part of this ministry and apostleship, from which Judas by transgression fell, that he might go to his own place."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Then they <a href="/wiki/Cleromancy" title="Cleromancy">cast lots</a>, and the lot fell to Matthias; so he was numbered with the eleven apostles.<sup id="cite_ref-Jacquier_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jacquier-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>No further information about Matthias is to be found in the <a href="/wiki/Biblical_canon" title="Biblical canon">canonical New Testament</a>. Even his name is variable: the Syriac version of <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> calls him throughout not Matthias but "Tolmai", not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle" title="Bartholomew the Apostle">Bartholomew</a> (which means Son of Tolmai), who was one of the twelve original Apostles; <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Alexandria" title="Clement of Alexandria">Clement of Alexandria</a> refers once to <a href="/wiki/Zacchaeus" title="Zacchaeus">Zacchaeus</a> in a way which could be read as suggesting that some identified him with Matthias;<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> the <i><a href="/wiki/Clementine_literature" title="Clementine literature">Clementine Recognitions</a></i> identify him with <a href="/wiki/Barnabas" title="Barnabas">Barnabas</a>; <a href="/wiki/Adolf_Bernhard_Christoph_Hilgenfeld" title="Adolf Bernhard Christoph Hilgenfeld">Adolf Bernhard Christoph Hilgenfeld</a> thinks he is the same as <a href="/wiki/Nathanael_(follower_of_Jesus)" title="Nathanael (follower of Jesus)">Nathanael</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">Gospel of John</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry">Ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthias_the_Apostle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The tradition of the Greeks says that St. Matthias planted the faith about <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a> and on the coasts of the <a href="/wiki/Caspian_Sea" title="Caspian Sea">Caspian Sea</a>, residing chiefly near the port <a href="/wiki/Issus_(Cilicia)" title="Issus (Cilicia)">Issus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_Kallistos_Xanthopoulos" title="Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos">Nicephorus</a> (<i>Historia eccl.</i>, 2, 40), Matthias first preached the Gospel in <a href="/wiki/Iudaea_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Iudaea Province">Judaea</a>, then in Aethiopia (by the region of <a href="/wiki/Colchis" title="Colchis">Colchis</a>, now in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a>) and was crucified.<sup id="cite_ref-Jacquier_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jacquier-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> An extant <a href="/wiki/Copt" class="mw-redirect" title="Copt">Coptic</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Andreae_et_Matthiae_apud_Anthropophagos" title="Acta Andreae et Matthiae apud Anthropophagos">Acts of Andrew and Matthias</a></i>, places his activity similarly in "the city of the cannibals" in <a href="/wiki/Ethiopia_(mythology)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ethiopia (mythology)">Aethiopia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A marker placed in the ruins of the Roman fortress at <a href="/wiki/Gonio" class="mw-redirect" title="Gonio">Gonio</a> (Apsaros) in the modern Georgian region of <a href="/wiki/Adjara" title="Adjara">Adjara</a> claims that Matthias is buried at that site.
</p><p>The <i>Synopsis of Dorotheus</i> contains this tradition: "Matthias preached the Gospel to barbarians and meat-eaters in the interior of Ethiopia, where the sea harbor of Hyssus is, at the mouth of the river Phasis. He died at Sebastopolis, and was buried there, near the Temple of the Sun."<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alternatively, another tradition maintains that Matthias was stoned at Jerusalem by the local populace, and then beheaded (cf. <a href="/wiki/Louis-S%C3%A9bastien_Le_Nain_de_Tillemont" title="Louis-Sébastien Le Nain de Tillemont">Tillemont</a>, <i>Mémoires pour servir à l'histoire ecclesiastique des six premiers siècles</i>, I, 406–7).<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Hippolytus_of_Rome" title="Hippolytus of Rome">Hippolytus of Rome</a>, Matthias died of old age in Jerusalem.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Alexandria" title="Clement of Alexandria">Clement of Alexandria</a> observed (<i>Stromateis</i> vi.13.):
</p>
<blockquote><p>Not that they became apostles through being chosen for some distinguished peculiarity of nature, since also Judas was chosen along with them. But they were capable of becoming apostles on being chosen by Him who foresees even ultimate issues. Matthias, accordingly, who was not chosen along with them, on showing himself worthy of becoming an apostle, is substituted for Judas.</p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthias_the_Apostle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Surviving fragments of the lost <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Matthias" title="Gospel of Matthias">Gospel of Matthias</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> attribute it to Matthias, but Early <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Church Fathers</a> attributed it to <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretical</a> writings in the 2nd century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matthias_the_Apostle&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Giustina_(Padua)_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Santa_Giustina_%28Padua%29_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg/220px-Santa_Giustina_%28Padua%29_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Santa_Giustina_%28Padua%29_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg/330px-Santa_Giustina_%28Padua%29_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Santa_Giustina_%28Padua%29_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg/440px-Santa_Giustina_%28Padua%29_-_Tomb_of_Saint_Matthias.jpg 2x" data-file-width="7360" data-file-height="4425" /></a><figcaption>His reliquary in <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg/170px-Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="257" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg/255px-Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg/340px-Saint_Matthias_Helsinki_Cathedral.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2010" data-file-height="3036" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Saint Matthias by <a href="/wiki/Hermann_Schievelbein" title="Hermann Schievelbein">Hermann Schievelbein</a> at the roof of the <a href="/wiki/Helsinki_Cathedral" title="Helsinki Cathedral">Helsinki Cathedral</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The feast of Saint Matthias was included in the Roman Calendar in the 11th century and celebrated on the sixth day to the Calends of March (24 February usually, but 25 February in leap years). In the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">revision</a> of the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1969, his feast was transferred to 14 May, so as not to celebrate it in <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> but instead in <a href="/wiki/Eastertide" title="Eastertide">Eastertide</a> close to the <a href="/wiki/Ascension_of_Jesus" title="Ascension of Jesus">Solemnity of the Ascension</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> the event after which the Acts of the Apostles recounts that Matthias was selected to be ranked with the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles" class="mw-redirect" title="Twelve Apostles">Twelve Apostles</a>.
</p><p>The Eastern Rites of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> celebrate his feast on 9 August. Yet the <a href="/wiki/Western_Rite_Orthodoxy" title="Western Rite Orthodoxy">Western Rite</a> parishes of the Orthodox Church continues the old Roman Rite of 24 and 25 February in leap years.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Book_of_Common_Prayer" title="Book of Common Prayer">Book of Common Prayer</a></i>, as well as other older common prayer books in the Anglican Communion,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> celebrates Matthias on 24 February. According to the newer <i><a href="/wiki/Common_Worship" title="Common Worship">Common Worship</a></i> liturgy, Matthias is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Festival (Anglicanism)">Festival</a> on 14 May,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> although he may be celebrated on 24 February, if desired.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopal Church</a> as well as some in the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Lutheran Church">Lutheran Church</a>, including the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod" title="Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod">Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Canada" title="Lutheran Church–Canada">Lutheran Church–Canada</a>, his feast remains on 24 February.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In <i><a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Worship" title="Evangelical Lutheran Worship">Evangelical Lutheran Worship</a></i>, used by the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_America" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church in America">Evangelical Lutheran Church in America</a> as well as the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_Canada" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church in Canada">Evangelical Lutheran Church in Canada</a>, the feast date for Matthias is on 14 May.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is claimed that St Matthias the Apostle's remains were brought to Italy through Empress <a href="/wiki/Helena_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Helena of Constantinople">Helena</a>, mother of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a> (the Great); part of these relics would be interred in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Santa_Giustina" title="Abbey of Santa Giustina">Abbey of Santa Giustina</a>, <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>, and the remaining in the <a href="/wiki/St._Matthias%27_Abbey,_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Matthias&#39; Abbey, Trier">Abbey of St. Matthias</a>, <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>, Germany. According to Greek sources, the remains of the apostle are buried in the castle of <a href="/wiki/Gonio" class="mw-redirect" title="Gonio">Gonio-Apsaros</a>, <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
