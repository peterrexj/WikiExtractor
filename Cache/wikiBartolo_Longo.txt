<p><b>Bartolo Longo</b> (February 10, 1841 – October 5, 1926) was an <a href="/wiki/Italians" title="Italians">Italian</a> lawyer who has been <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the Roman Catholic Church. He was a former <a href="/wiki/Satanism" title="Satanism">Satanic</a> priest who returned to the Catholic faith and became a <a href="/wiki/Third_order" title="Third order">third order</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a>, dedicating his life to the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">Rosary</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>. He was eventually awarded a <a href="/wiki/Papal_knighthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal knighthood">papal knighthood</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Order of the Holy Sepulchre">Order of the Holy Sepulchre</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolo_Longo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bartolo Longo, was born into a wealthy family on February 10, 1841 in the small town of Latiano, near <a href="/wiki/Brindisi" title="Brindisi">Brindisi</a>, in southern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-rum_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rum-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents were devout <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholics</a>. In 1851, Longo's father died and his mother remarried a lawyer. Despite Longo's stepfather wanting him to become a teacher, Longo was set on becoming a lawyer. In 1861, Longo succeeded in convincing his stepfather and was sent to the University of Naples to study law.<sup id="cite_ref-brown_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brown-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 1860s, the Catholic Church in Italy found itself at odds with a strong <a href="/wiki/Nationalism" title="Nationalism">nationalistic</a> movement. General <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Garibaldi" title="Giuseppe Garibaldi">Giuseppe Garibaldi</a>, who played a key role in <a href="/wiki/Italian_unification" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian unification">Italian unification</a>, saw the Pope as an antagonist to Italian nationalism and actively campaigned for the elimination of the papal office altogether.<sup id="cite_ref-gg_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gg-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic Church in Europe was also competing with a growing popularity in <a href="/wiki/Spiritualism" title="Spiritualism">Spiritualism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Occult" title="Occult">Occultism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-conan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Because of this, many students at the University of Naples took part in demonstrations against the pope, dabbled in witchcraft and consulted Neapolitan mediums.<sup id="cite_ref-conan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-sullivan_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sullivan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Longo became involved with a movement that he claimed led him into a Satanist cult. After some study and several "spiritual" experiences Longo said that he was ordained as a satanic priest.<sup id="cite_ref-stagnaro_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stagnaro-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion">Conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolo_Longo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg/220px-Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="344" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b7/Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg/330px-Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg 2x" data-file-width="363" data-file-height="567" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bartolo_Longo_A_22_anni.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bartolo Longo at age 22</div></div></div>
<p>In the following years, Longo's life became one of "depression, nervousness, and confusion".<sup id="cite_ref-stagnaro_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stagnaro-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Bothered by paranoia and anxiety, he turned to a hometown friend, Vincenzo Pepe, for guidance. It was Pepe who convinced him, in Longo's account, to abandon Satanism and introduced him to the Dominican priest Alberto Radente who led him to a devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">Rosary</a>. On October 7, 1871, Longo became a Dominican tertiary and took the name "Rosario". Around this time, he reportedly visited a séance and held up a rosary, declaring, "I renounce spiritualism because it is nothing but a maze of error and falsehood." He also came to know some Franciscans with whom he helped the poor and incurably ill for two years. Bartolo also kept up his law practice, which took him to the nearby village of <a href="/wiki/Pompei" title="Pompei">Pompei</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sullivan_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sullivan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He went to Pompei to take care of the affairs of Countess Marianna Farnararo De Fusco.<sup id="cite_ref-rum_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rum-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Pompei, Longo later recounted, he was shocked at the erosion of the people's faith. He wrote, "Their religion was a mixture of superstition and popular tradition. ... For their every need, ... they would go to a witch, a sorceress, in order to obtain charms and witchcraft." Through talking to the citizens, Bartolo came to recognize their severe lack of catechesis. When he asked one man if there was only one God, the fellow answered, "When I was a child, I remember people telling me there were three. Now, after so many years, I don't know if one of them is dead or one has married."<sup id="cite_ref-sullivan_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sullivan-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Longo wrote of his personal struggles with mental illness, paranoia, depression and anxiety. At one point, he noted struggling with suicidal thoughts, but rejected them by recalling the promise of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Dominic</a>, <i>"he who propagates my Rosary will be saved."</i> Longo wrote that this promise is what convinced him to encourage public devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-landry_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-landry-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrine_of_Our_Lady_of_Pompei">Shrine of Our Lady of Pompei</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolo_Longo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Shrine of Our Lady of Pompei">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Shrine_of_Our_Lady_of_Pompei" class="mw-redirect" title="Shrine of Our Lady of Pompei">Shrine of Our Lady of Pompei</a></div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Icona_Madonna_Pompei.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/Icona_Madonna_Pompei.jpg/220px-Icona_Madonna_Pompei.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="304" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d5/Icona_Madonna_Pompei.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="254" data-file-height="351" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Icona_Madonna_Pompei.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Our Lady of the Rosary with St. Dominic and St. Catherine of Siena</div></div></div>
<p>With the help of Countess Mariana di Fusco, he inaugurated a confraternity of the Rosary and in October 1873 started restoring a dilapidated church. He sponsored a festival in honor of Our Lady of the Rosary.
</p><p>In 1875, Longo obtained as a gift a painting portraying <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary" title="Our Lady of the Rosary">Our Lady of the Rosary</a>, with Saint Dominic and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Catherine_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Catherine of Siena">Saint Catherine of Siena</a>. M. Concetta de Litala, a <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sister (Catholic)">religious sister</a> of the Monastery of the Rosary at Porta Medina, had been holding it for the Dominican priest Alberto Radente. Radente had acquired it from a junk-shop dealer in Naples for a very small sum. The painting was in bad condition and Longo wrote of his immediate distaste of the poor artistic quality when he first saw it. However, he accepted the gift to conserve funds and to not insult Concetta. Longo raised funds to restore the image and placed it in the church in an effort to encourage pilgrimages.<sup id="cite_ref-cruz_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-longo1_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-longo1-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alleged miracles began to be reported and people began flocking in droves to the church. Longo was encouraged by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Nola" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Nola">Bishop of Nola</a> to begin the construction of a larger church—the cornerstone being laid on May 8, 1876. The church was consecrated in May 1891 by Cardinal La Valletta (representing <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-longo1_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-longo1-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In 1939, the church was enlarged to a basilica, known today as the <a href="/wiki/Shrine_of_the_Virgin_of_the_Rosary_of_Pompei" title="Shrine of the Virgin of the Rosary of Pompei">Basilica of Our Lady of the Most Holy Rosary of Pompei</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life_and_death">Later life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolo_Longo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg/220px-Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg/330px-Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg/440px-Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1071" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Bartolo_Longo_at_Pompei.jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Remains of Blessed Longo</div></div></div>
<p>At the suggestion of Pope Leo XIII, Bartolo Longo and the Countess Mariana di Fusco were married on April 7, 1885. The couple remained continent (abstained from intercourse),<sup id="cite_ref-cna_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and continued to do many charitable works and provided for orphaned children and the children of prisoners, which was radical for its time.<sup id="cite_ref-anuletta_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-anuletta-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1906 they donated the entire property of the Pompei shrine to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. Longo continued promoting the Rosary until his death on October 5, 1926, at the age of 85. The piazza on which his basilica stands has since been named in memory of Longo. His body is encased in a glass tomb and he is wearing the <a href="/wiki/Mantle_(clothing)" title="Mantle (clothing)">mantle</a> of a Knight of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Order of the Holy Sepulchre">Order of the Holy Sepulchre</a>, a papal order of knighthood.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bartolo_Longo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On October 26, 1980, he was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, who would call him the "Apostle of the Rosary" and mentioned him specifically in his apostolic letter "Rosarium Virginis Mariae" (The Rosary of the Virgin Mary).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On October 7, 2003, Pope John Paul II prayed for world peace at the basilica. More than 30,000 people were waiting to greet him as he flew in by helicopter.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
