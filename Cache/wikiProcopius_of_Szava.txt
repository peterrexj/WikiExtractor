<p><b>Saint Procopius of Sázava</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Procopius Sazavensis</i>, <a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Prokop Sázavský</i>; died 25 March 1053) was a <a href="/wiki/Czechs" title="Czechs">Czech</a> <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, canonized as a saint of the Catholic church in 1204.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Procopius_of_S%C3%A1zava&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little about his life is known with certainty. According to hagiographical tradition, he was born in 970, in a Central Bohemian village of <a href="/wiki/Chr%C3%A1%C5%A1%C5%A5any_(Kol%C3%ADn_District)" title="Chrášťany (Kolín District)">Chotouň</a> near <a href="/wiki/Kou%C5%99im" title="Kouřim">Kouřim</a>. He studied in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> there.
</p><p>He was married and had a son, called Jimram (Emmeram), but later entered the Benedictine order, presumably at <a href="/wiki/B%C5%99evnov_Monastery" title="Břevnov Monastery">Břevnov Monastery</a>, and eventually retired to the wilderness as a hermit, living in a cave on the banks of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A1zava_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Sázava River">Sázava River</a>, where over time he attracted a group of fellow hermits.
The community of hermits was incorporated as a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery by the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bohemia">duke of Bohemia</a> in 1032/3, now known as <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A1zava_Monastery" title="Sázava Monastery">Sázava Monastery</a>, or St Procopius Monastery, where he served as the first abbot for the span of twenty years until his death.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Procopius_of_S%C3%A1zava&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Local veneration of Procopius as a saint is recorded for the 12th century when the first biography <i>Vita minor</i> has been written. He was canonized in 1204; however, there is still much debate on how his canonization was performed. It is stated that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> canonized him in 1204<sup id="cite_ref-mortesen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mortesen-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> or that during a liturgical elevation and translation of his body to the altar in Sázava his canonization took place. This was at that time the equivalent to canonization<sup id="cite_ref-kubin_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kubin-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his canonization, he became greatly venerated throughout Bohemia, to the point of his being considered the <a href="/wiki/National_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="National saint">national saint</a>  of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia" title="Kingdom of Bohemia">Kingdom of Bohemia</a>. His life and wonders were described by the <i>Vita antiqua</i> from the second half of the 13th century, and <i>Vita maior</i> from the 14th century. His remains were transferred to <a href="/wiki/All_Saints_Church_(Prague_Castle)" title="All Saints Church (Prague Castle)">All Saints Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Prague_Castle" title="Prague Castle">Prague Castle</a> in 1588.
</p><p>The Cyrillic portion of the <a href="/wiki/Reims_Gospel" title="Reims Gospel">Reims Gospel</a> manuscript (since 1554 kept in Reims, France) were attributed to Procopius in the 14th century, and <a href="/wiki/Charles_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles IV</a> commissioned an extension of the manuscript in <a href="/wiki/Glagolitic_script" title="Glagolitic script">Glagolitic script</a> in 1395.
</p><p>Sázava Monastery had been destroyed in the Hussite Wars, but the church was re-established in the 17th century, as well as the monastery buildings changed in a castle. The Baroque-era frescos "The Meeting of Hermit Procopius with Prince Oldřich" and "Abbot Procopius Giving Alms" besides other frescos  depicting scenes the saint's life and the history of the monastery, were discovered there (under layers of 19th-century paint) in the 2000s.
</p><p>Hugo Fabricius, a monk at Sázava, wrote a new life of St. Procopius in the 18th century, <i>Požehnaná Památka Welikého Swěta Diwotworce Swatýho Prokopa</i> ("The Blessed Legacy of the Great <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgy" title="Thaumaturgy">Miracle Worker</a> of the World, St. Procopius").
</p><p>Numerous churches in Bohemia are dedicated to him, and many Baroque-era statues and paintings of the saint are extant. Among these is the early 18th century <a href="/wiki/Statues_of_Saints_Vincent_Ferrer_and_Procopius,_Charles_Bridge" title="Statues of Saints Vincent Ferrer and Procopius, Charles Bridge">Procopius statue</a> on <a href="/wiki/Charles_Bridge" title="Charles Bridge">Charles Bridge</a> by <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_Brokoff" title="Ferdinand Brokoff">Ferdinand Brokoff</a>. Modern retellings of the saint's life were published by Czech poets <a href="/wiki/Jaroslav_Vrchlick%C3%BD" title="Jaroslav Vrchlický">Jaroslav Vrchlický</a> and <a href="/wiki/V%C3%ADt%C4%9Bzslav_Nezval" title="Vítězslav Nezval">Vítězslav Nezval</a>.
</p><p>The "Cave of St. Procopius", the supposed site of his original hermitage, was discovered by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Method_Klement&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Method Klement (page does not exist)">Method Klement</a> OSB in the 1940s.
</p><p>On 9 March 2017, by the decision of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Synod_of_the_Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Holy Synod of the Russian Orthodox Church">Holy Synod of the Russian Orthodox Church</a>, the name of "Venerable Procopius, Abbot of Sázava" was added to the <a href="/wiki/Menologium" title="Menologium">Menologium</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
