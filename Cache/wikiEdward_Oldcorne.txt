<p><b>Edward Oldcorne</b> alias <i>Hall</i> (1561 &#8211; 7 April 1606) was an English <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> priest. He was known to people who knew of the <a href="/wiki/Gunpowder_Plot" title="Gunpowder Plot">Gunpowder Plot</a> to destroy the <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_England" title="Parliament of England">Parliament of England</a> and kill <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="James I of England">King James I</a>; and although his involvement is unclear, he was caught up in the subsequent investigation. He is a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> martyr, and was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1929. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Oldcorne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oldcorne was born in <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a> in 1561, the son of John Oldcorne, a bricklayer, and his wife Mary.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father was a <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestant</a>, and his mother a Catholic who had spent some time in prison due to her faith. He was educated at St Peter's School in York; school friends were <a href="/wiki/John_Wright_(Gunpowder_Plot)" class="mw-redirect" title="John Wright (Gunpowder Plot)">John</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Wright_(plotter)" class="mw-redirect" title="Christopher Wright (plotter)">Christopher Wright</a> and <a href="/wiki/Guy_Fawkes" title="Guy Fawkes">Guy Fawkes</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-gun_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gun-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oldcorne was educated as a doctor, but later decided to enter the priesthood. He went to the English College at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, then to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> where after ordination in 1587, he became a Jesuit in 1588.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="On_the_English_mission">On the English mission</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Oldcorne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: On the English mission">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In late 1588 Oldcorne returned to England, in the company of Father <a href="/wiki/John_Gerard_(Jesuit)" title="John Gerard (Jesuit)">John Gerard</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In early 1589 he went with Father <a href="/wiki/Henry_Garnet" title="Henry Garnet">Henry Garnet</a> to the <a href="/wiki/West_Midlands_(region)" title="West Midlands (region)">West Midlands</a>, visiting <a href="/wiki/Coughton,_Warwickshire" title="Coughton, Warwickshire">Coughton, Warwickshire</a> and settling at <a href="/wiki/Baddesley_Clinton" title="Baddesley Clinton">Baddesley Clinton</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McCoog2012_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCoog2012-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He then worked chiefly in <a href="/wiki/Worcestershire" title="Worcestershire">Worcestershire</a> for 17 years. <a href="/wiki/Oswald_Tesimond" title="Oswald Tesimond">Oswald Tesimond</a> assisted him after 1596;<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Father <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Lister_(Jesuit)" title="Thomas Lister (Jesuit)">Thomas Lister</a>, another Jesuit, also supported Oldcorne's mission but found the requirements of the covert life difficult.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oldcorne sometimes stayed with <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Abington" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Abington">Thomas Abington</a>, whose house at <a href="/wiki/Hindlip_Hall" title="Hindlip Hall">Hindlip Hall</a> was near Baddesley Clinton. There he converted Thomas's sister Dorothy.<sup id="cite_ref-McCoog2012_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McCoog2012-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The house was then was adapted by <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Owen_(martyr)" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicholas Owen (martyr)">Nicholas Owen</a> to help conceal Catholic priests.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="From_1601_to_1605">From 1601 to 1605</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Oldcorne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: From 1601 to 1605">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 3 November 1601, Oldcorne went on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/St_Winefride%27s_Well" title="St Winefride&#39;s Well">St Winefride's Well</a> at <a href="/wiki/Holywell,_Flintshire" title="Holywell, Flintshire">Holywell</a> in north Wales to obtain a cure for a cancer of the throat. The cancer cleared up and in 1605 about thirty people returned with him to give thanks for his recovery. Amongst this group were the priests <a href="/wiki/Oswald_Tesimond" title="Oswald Tesimond">Oswald Tesimond</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Ashley" title="Ralph Ashley">Ralph Ashley</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Henry_Garnet" title="Henry Garnet">Henry Garnet</a>, as well as Nicholas Owen and John Gerard .
</p><p>Also in the group was plotter <a href="/wiki/Everard_Digby" title="Everard Digby">Everard Digby</a> and his wife, whose priest was Oldcorne. The timing of this second pilgrimage and the people involved later aroused suspicion. The government investigation used this gathering as circumstantial evidence to implicate some of those there in the plot.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Aftermath_of_the_Plot">Aftermath of the Plot</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Oldcorne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Aftermath of the Plot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When the <a href="/wiki/Gunpowder_Plot" title="Gunpowder Plot">Gunpowder Plot</a> was discovered, Oldcorne was at Hindlip Hall, his base for fourteen years. In December, he was joined there by Nicholas Owen, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Garnet" title="Henry Garnet">Henry Garnet</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Ashley" title="Ralph Ashley">Ralph Ashley</a> who were hiding because they were under suspicion of involvement. Hindlip was searched in January but the four were not discovered: Garnet and Oldcorne were in one hiding place while the two lay brothers were in another. Their conditions were poor, and after eight days they surrendered.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Oldcorne was arrested with Garnet<sup id="cite_ref-cath_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Sir_Henry_Bromley" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Henry Bromley">Sir Henry Bromley</a> and held briefly at the castle at <a href="/wiki/Holt,_Worcestershire" title="Holt, Worcestershire">Holt</a> in Worcestershire before being taken to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>. It has been said that Bromley would have abandoned his search much earlier but he had information from <a href="/wiki/Humphrey_Littleton_(plotter)" class="mw-redirect" title="Humphrey Littleton (plotter)">Humphrey Littleton</a> that Oldcorne and possibly Garnet were hiding there.<sup id="cite_ref-humph_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-humph-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg/220px-Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg/330px-Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg/440px-Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Holt_castle_850843_62546082.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Holt Castle (in 2008), where Oldcorne was briefly held</div></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Trial_and_execution">Trial and execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Oldcorne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Trial and execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oldcorne was tortured, but no evidence was found to connect him to the Gunpowder Plot. He recounted under interrogation that on 8 November 1605 there arrived Tesimond from Robert Wintour's who told Mr (H)Abington and himself that "he brought them the worst news that they had ever heard, and they were all undone." Tesimond said that certain people had intended to blow up the parliament house but they had been discovered a few days before it was meant to happen.<sup id="cite_ref-state_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-state-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Edward_Oldcorne;_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Edward_Oldcorne%3B_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg/220px-Edward_Oldcorne%3B_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="314" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Edward_Oldcorne%3B_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg/330px-Edward_Oldcorne%3B_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Edward_Oldcorne%3B_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg/440px-Edward_Oldcorne%3B_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2400" data-file-height="3428" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Edward_Oldcorne;_Nicholas_Owen_by_Gaspar_Bouttats.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Edward Oldcorne and Nicholas Owen, engraving by <a href="/wiki/Gaspar_Bouttats" title="Gaspar Bouttats">Gaspar Bouttats</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Some allege that Oldcorne was executed just for his priesthood.<sup id="cite_ref-cath_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Others suppose that it may have been because he was notorious or because he had provided safe refuge through <a href="/wiki/John_Jones_(martyr)" title="John Jones (martyr)">Father Jones</a> for the plotters, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Wintour" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Wintour">Robert Wintour</a> and Stephen Littleton (<a href="/wiki/Stephen_Lyttelton" title="Stephen Lyttelton">Stephen Lyttelton</a>); or for providing a hiding place for his superior Henry Garnet at Hindlip.<sup id="cite_ref-letter_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> At his trial, Humphrey Littleton asked for his forgiveness and it was said that he believed he deserved to die for revealing his friend's whereabouts.<sup id="cite_ref-humph_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-humph-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Two letters of his are at <a href="/wiki/Stonyhurst" title="Stonyhurst">Stonyhurst</a>, the second written from prison. On the day before his execution <a href="/wiki/John_Floyd_(Jesuit)" title="John Floyd (Jesuit)">John Floyd</a>, a fellow Jesuit, was arrested for trying to visit him.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Oldcorne was executed at <a href="/wiki/Red_Hill,_Worcester" title="Red Hill, Worcester">Red Hill, Worcester</a>, together with <a href="/wiki/John_Wintour_(Gunpowder_Plot)" class="mw-redirect" title="John Wintour (Gunpowder Plot)">John Wintour</a>, Humphrey Littleton and Ralph Ashley, his servant.<sup id="cite_ref-gun_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gun-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was hanged, drawn, and quartered; it is said that, as Oldcorne waited on the ladder to die, Ashley kissed his feet and said, "What a happy man am I to follow in the steps of my sweet father". Oldcorne died with the name of <a href="/wiki/St_Winifred" class="mw-redirect" title="St Winifred">St Winifred</a> on his lips.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> When Ashley came to die he prayed and asked for forgiveness and noted that like Oldcorne he was dying for his religion and not as a traitor.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edward_Oldcorne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Oldcorne's portrait was painted after his death for the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Ges%C3%B9" title="Church of the Gesù">Church of the Gesù</a>. A number of his relics survived<sup id="cite_ref-cath_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cath-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> including one of his <a href="/wiki/Human_eye" title="Human eye">eyes</a><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> which he lost when the executioner decapitated him: it is said that the force of the blow was so great that his eye flew out of its socket.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> A secondary school, <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Edward_Oldcorne_Catholic_College" title="Blessed Edward Oldcorne Catholic College">Blessed Edward Oldcorne Catholic College</a>, named in his honour, is in <a href="/wiki/Worcester,_England" title="Worcester, England">Worcester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> His right eye and the rope that bound him are kept as relics at <a href="/wiki/Stonyhurst_College" title="Stonyhurst College">Stonyhurst College</a>. They believe that the eye was taken by a Catholic sympathiser while his body was being parboiled after he was quartered.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Abington's wife Mary was the sister of <a href="/wiki/William_Parker,_4th_Baron_Monteagle" title="William Parker, 4th Baron Monteagle">William Parker, 4th Baron Monteagle</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-lives_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Lord Monteagle was later to become a pivotal figure in the capture of the gunpowder plotters.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The authorship of Monteagle's letter has been a significant problem to historians. One of the candidates put forward is Oldcorne.<sup id="cite_ref-letter_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-letter-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
