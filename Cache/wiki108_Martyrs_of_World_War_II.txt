<p>The <b>108 Martyrs of World War II</b>, known also as the <b>108 Blessed Polish Martyrs</b> (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">108 błogosławionych męczenników</i>), were <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholics</a> from <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> killed during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> by <a href="/wiki/Nazi_Germany" title="Nazi Germany">Nazi Germany</a>.
</p><p>Their liturgical <a href="/wiki/Festival" title="Festival">feast</a> day is 12 June. The 108 were <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 13 June 1999 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a>, Poland. The group comprises 3 bishops, 79 priests, 7 male <a href="/wiki/Religious_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious (Catholicism)">religious</a>, 8 female religious, and 11 <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay people</a>. There are two parishes named for the 108 Martyrs of World War II in <a href="/wiki/Powiercie" title="Powiercie">Powiercie</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ko%C5%82o_County" title="Koło County">Koło County</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Malbork" title="Malbork">Malbork</a>, Poland.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
