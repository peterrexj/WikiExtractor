<p><b>Raphael of Brooklyn</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">القديس رفائيل من بروكلين</span>), born <b>Rufāʾīl Hawāwīnī</b> (<b>Raphael Hawaweeny</b>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">رفائيل الهواويني</span>; November 20, 1860 – February 27, 1915), was bishop of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Auxiliary_bishop" title="Auxiliary bishop">auxiliary bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Brooklyn" title="Brooklyn">Brooklyn</a>, vicar of the Northern-American diocese, and head of the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Antioch" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch">Antiochian Levantine Christian</a> mission. He was the first Orthodox Christian bishop consecrated on American soil.<sup id="cite_ref-straphaelofbrooklyn_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-straphaelofbrooklyn-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_of_Brooklyn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in modern-day Lebanon to Damascene <a href="/wiki/Syrians" title="Syrians">Syrian</a> (<a href="/wiki/Antiochian_Greek_Christians" title="Antiochian Greek Christians">Antiochian Greek Christian</a>) parents of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy_in_Syria" title="Eastern Orthodoxy in Syria">Antiochian Orthodox</a> faith who had come to Beirut<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> fleeing the <a href="/wiki/1860_civil_conflict_in_Mount_Lebanon_and_Damascus" title="1860 civil conflict in Mount Lebanon and Damascus">1860 Syrian Civil War</a> in <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Newman_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Newman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was first educated at the Damascus <a href="/wiki/Patriarchy" title="Patriarchy">Patriarchal</a> School that had become the leading <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church" title="Greek Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox</a> institution of higher learning in the Levant under the leadership of <a href="/wiki/Joseph_of_Damascus" title="Joseph of Damascus">Joseph of Damascus</a>. He furthered his study of Christian theology at the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Patriarchal</a> <a href="/wiki/Halki_seminary" title="Halki seminary">Halki seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, and at the Theological Academy in <a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kiev</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>).
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Raphael_of_Brooklyn.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Raphael_of_Brooklyn.jpg/220px-Raphael_of_Brooklyn.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="290" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fe/Raphael_of_Brooklyn.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="265" data-file-height="349" /></a><figcaption>Archimandrite Raphael upon his arrival in America</figcaption></figure> 
<p>Father Raphael was sent to <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> in 1895 by Tsar <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia" title="Nicholas II of Russia">Nicholas II of Russia</a> to administer the local Orthodox Christian community which then included mainly <a href="/wiki/Russians" title="Russians">Russian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a>, and Levantine immigrants.
</p><p>In 1904, he became the first <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> to be consecrated in <a href="/wiki/North_America" title="North America">North America</a>; the consecration was performed in <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Tikhon_of_Moscow" title="Patriarch Tikhon of Moscow">Tikhon (Bellavin)</a> and Bishop Innocent (Pustynsky). He served as Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Brooklyn" title="Brooklyn">Brooklyn</a> until his death.
</p><p>During the course of his ministry as an auxiliary bishop of the Russian Orthodox Church in America, Raphael founded the present-day <a href="/wiki/St._Nicholas_Antiochian_Orthodox_Cathedral_(Brooklyn)" title="St. Nicholas Antiochian Orthodox Cathedral (Brooklyn)">St. Nicholas Antiochian Orthodox Cathedral (Brooklyn)</a>, established thirty parishes and assisted in the founding of St. Tikhon's Orthodox Monastery.
</p><p>Bishop Raphael founded the official magazine of the Antiochian Orthodox Archdiocese, <i>The Word</i>, in 1905 in Arabic (الكلمة).<sup id="cite_ref-The_Word_magazine_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-The_Word_magazine-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Glorification_and_honors">Glorification and honors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_of_Brooklyn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Glorification and honors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bishop Raphael was originally buried in <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York</a> until August 1988, when his relics were translated to the <a href="/wiki/Antiochian_Village" title="Antiochian Village">Antiochian Village</a> Camp in <a href="/wiki/Ligonier,_Pennsylvania" title="Ligonier, Pennsylvania">Ligonier, Pennsylvania</a>, on property of the Antiochian Archdiocese, along with several other bishops and clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Raphael was <a href="/wiki/Glorification#Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Glorification">glorified</a> by the Holy Synod of the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a> (OCA) in its March 2000 session. He is commemorated by the OCA on February 27, the anniversary of his death and by the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Antioch" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch">Antiochian Orthodox Church</a> on the first Saturday of November near the Synaxis of the Archangels Michael, Gabriel, Raphael and all the bodiless powers of heaven.
</p><p>In 2015, the Antiochian Archdiocese, OCA and <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_of_Russia" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia">ROCOR</a> celebrated the 100th Anniversary of the dormition of St. Raphael.<sup id="cite_ref-straphaelofbrooklyn_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-straphaelofbrooklyn-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is commemorated on 14 February<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and on 27 October - Synaxis of All Saints of Kiev Theological Academy and Kiev Theological Seminary in <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_(Moscow_Patriarchate)" title="Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate)">Ukrainian Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
