<p><b>Isidore the Labourer</b>, also known as <b>Isidore the Farmer</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Isidro Labrador</i>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1070</span> – 15 May 1130), was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Farmworker" title="Farmworker">farmworker</a> known for his piety toward the poor and animals. He is the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Farmer" title="Farmer">farmers</a>, and of <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/El_Gobernador" title="El Gobernador">El Gobernador</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jalisco" title="Jalisco">Jalisco</a>, <a href="/wiki/La_Ceiba" title="La Ceiba">La Ceiba</a>, <a href="/wiki/Honduras" title="Honduras">Honduras</a>, and of <a href="/wiki/Tocoa,_Col%C3%B3n" title="Tocoa, Colón">Tocoa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Honduras" title="Honduras">Honduras</a>. His feast day is celebrated on 15 May.
</p><p>The Spanish profession name <i>labrador</i> comes from the verb <i>labrar</i> ("to till", "to plow" or, in a broader sense, "to work the land"). Hence, to refer to him as simply a "laborer" is a poor translation of the Spanish <i>labrador</i> as it makes no reference to the essential farming aspect of his work and his identity.  His real name was Isidro de Merlo y Quintana.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Isidore was born in Madrid, around year 1070 or 1082,<sup id="cite_ref-Forense_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Forense-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> to poor but very devout parents, and was christened Isidore after name of their patron, <a href="/wiki/St._Isidore_of_Seville" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Isidore of Seville">St. Isidore of Seville</a>.
In 1083 or 1085, the troops of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VI_of_Le%C3%B3n_and_Castile" title="Alfonso VI of León and Castile">Alfonso VI of León and Castile</a> conquered Madrid from the Muslim <a href="/wiki/Taifa_of_Toledo" title="Taifa of Toledo">taifa of Toledo</a>.
Isidore spent his life as a hired hand in the service of the wealthy Madrilenian landowner, Juan de Vargas, on a farm in the city's vicinity.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He shared what he had, even his meals, with the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-sicc_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicc-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Juan de Vargas would later make him <a href="/wiki/Bailiff" title="Bailiff">bailiff</a> of his entire estate of Lower Caramanca.
</p><p>Isidore married <a href="/wiki/Maria_Torribia" title="Maria Torribia">Maria Torribia</a>, known as <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es-ES">Santa María de la Cabeza</i></span> in Spain; she has never been canonized, pending confirmation by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>. Isidore and Maria had one son.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On one occasion, their son fell into a deep well, and at the prayers of his parents, the water of the well is said to have risen miraculously to the level of the ground, bringing the child with it. In thanksgiving, Isidore and Maria then vowed sexual abstinence and lived in separate houses. Their son later died in his youth.
</p><p>Isidore died on 15 May 1130,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> at his birthplace close to Madrid, although the only official source places his death in the year 1172.<sup id="cite_ref-abc.es_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abc.es-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2022, a team from the <a href="/wiki/Complutense_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Complutense University">Complutense University</a> undertook a forensic study of Isidore's remains.
A coin with a lion was found in the throat.
It was conjectured that it could date from the age of <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_Castile" title="Henry IV of Castile">Henry IV of Castile</a>.
The only active illness found was dental abscesses.
The researchers proposed them as a cause of death.
The age of death ranges between 35 and 45 years old.
The height was estimated between 167 and 186&#160;cm.
The cranium shows a predominance of African features.<sup id="cite_ref-Casado_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Casado-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Forense_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Forense-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracle_stories">Miracle stories</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Miracle stories">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SIsidroImage.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/SIsidroImage.jpg/220px-SIsidroImage.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="337" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/SIsidroImage.jpg/330px-SIsidroImage.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/SIsidroImage.jpg/440px-SIsidroImage.jpg 2x" data-file-width="458" data-file-height="702" /></a><figcaption>St Isidore and St. Maria</figcaption></figure>
<p>In the morning before going to work, Isidore would usually attend Mass at one of the churches in Madrid. One day, his fellow farm workers complained to their master that Isidore was always late for work in the morning. Upon investigation the master found Isidore at prayer while an angel was doing the ploughing for him.<sup id="cite_ref-sicc_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicc-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On another occasion, his master saw an angel ploughing on either side of him, so that Isidore's work was equal to that of three of his fellow field workers. Isidore is also said to have brought back to life his master's deceased daughter, and to have caused a fountain of fresh water to burst from the dry earth to quench his master's thirst.<sup id="cite_ref-sicc_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicc-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One snowy day, when going to the mill with wheat to be ground, he passed a flock of wood-pigeons scratching vainly for food on the hard surface of the frosty ground. Taking pity on the poor animals, he poured half of his sack of precious wheat upon the ground for the birds, despite the mocking of witnesses. When he reached the mill, however, the bag was full, and the wheat, when it was ground, produced double the expected amount of flour.
</p><p>Isidore's wife, Maria, always kept a pot of stew on the fireplace in their humble home as Isidore would often bring home anyone who was hungry. One day he brought home more hungry people than usual. After she served many of them, Maria told him that there simply was no more stew in the pot. He insisted that she check the pot again, and she was able to spoon out enough stew to feed them all.<sup id="cite_ref-ncrlc_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncrlc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 2 April 1212, after torrential rains had exhumed cadavers from cemeteries in Madrid, his body was discovered in an apparent state of <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorruptibility</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-abc.es_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abc.es-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is said to have appeared to <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_VIII_of_Castile" title="Alfonso VIII of Castile">Alfonso VIII of Castile</a>, and to have shown him the hidden path by which he surprised the Moors and gained the victory of <a href="/wiki/Las_Navas_de_Tolosa" class="mw-redirect" title="Las Navas de Tolosa">Las Navas de Tolosa</a>, in 1212.<sup id="cite_ref-sicc_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sicc-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> When King <a href="/wiki/Philip_III_of_Spain" title="Philip III of Spain">Philip III of Spain</a> was cured of a deadly disease after touching the <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of the saint, the king replaced the old <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> with a costly silver one and instigated the process of his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-secretosdemadrid.es_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secretosdemadrid.es-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Throughout history, other members of the royal family would seek curative powers from the saint.
</p><p>The number of miracles attributed to him has been counted as 438.<sup id="cite_ref-secretosdemadrid.es_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secretosdemadrid.es-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The only original source of <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> on him is a fourteenth century <a href="/wiki/Codex" title="Codex">codex</a> called <i>Códice de Juan Diácono</i> which relates five of his miracles: 
</p>
<ol><li>The pigeons and the grain.</li>
<li>The angels ploughing.</li>
<li>The saving of his donkey, through prayer, from a wolf attack.</li>
<li>The account of his wife's pot of food.</li>
<li>A similar account of his feeding the brotherhood.</li></ol>
<p>The codex also attests to the incorruptible state of his body, stating it was exhumed 40 years after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg/220px-Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg/330px-Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg/440px-Sa_Isidro_Labrador-Madrid.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3888" /></a><figcaption>San Isidro Labrador-Madrid</figcaption></figure>
<p>Isidore was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in Rome on 2 May 1619 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a>. He was canonized nearly three years later by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XV" title="Pope Gregory XV">Pope Gregory XV</a>, along with Saints <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a>, <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>, <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila" title="Teresa of Ávila">Teresa of Ávila</a> and <a href="/wiki/Philip_Neri" title="Philip Neri">Philip Neri</a>, on 12 March 1622.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1696, his relics were moved to the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Alcazar_of_Madrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Royal Alcazar of Madrid">Royal Alcazar of Madrid</a> to intervene on behalf of the health of <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_Spain" title="Charles II of Spain">Charles II of Spain</a>. While there, the King's locksmith pulled a tooth from the body and gave it to the monarch, who slept with it under his pillow until his death. This was not the first, nor the last time his body was allegedly mutilated out of religious fervour. For example, it was reported one of the ladies in the court of <a href="/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile" title="Isabella I of Castile">Isabella I of Castile</a> bit off one of his toes.
</p><p>In 1760, his body was brought to the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Madrid" title="Royal Palace of Madrid">Royal Palace of Madrid</a> during the illness of <a href="/wiki/Maria_Amalia_of_Saxony" title="Maria Amalia of Saxony">Maria Amalia of Saxony</a>.
</p><p>In 1769, <a href="/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain" title="Charles III of Spain">Charles III of Spain</a> had the remains of Saint Isidore and his wife Maria relocated to the <a href="/wiki/San_Isidro_Church,_Madrid" class="mw-redirect" title="San Isidro Church, Madrid">San Isidro Church, Madrid</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-abc.es_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abc.es-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> has nine locks and only the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Spain">King of Spain</a> has the master key. The opening of the sepulcher must be performed by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Madrid" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Madrid">Archbishop of Madrid</a> and authorized by the King himself. Consequently, it has not been opened since 1985.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast day is celebrated on 15 May in the Catholic Church,<sup id="cite_ref-ott_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and in the <a href="/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church" title="Philippine Independent Church">Philippine Independent Church</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Isidore is widely venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of farmers, peasants, day laborers and agriculture in general, as well as brick layers. His hometown of <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, the Spanish cities of <a href="/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Spain" title="León, Spain">Leon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zaragoza" title="Zaragoza">Zaragoza</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, and various locales in the former <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Empire" title="Spanish Empire">Spanish Empire</a> honour him as their patron saint. The US National Catholic Rural Life Conference claims him as its patron.<sup id="cite_ref-ball_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ball-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/San_Ysidro,_California" class="mw-redirect" title="San Ysidro, California">San Ysidro, California</a>, and <a href="/wiki/San_Ysidro,_New_Mexico" title="San Ysidro, New Mexico">San Ysidro, New Mexico</a>, were named after him.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Isidore is often portrayed as a peasant holding a sickle and a sheaf of corn. He might also be shown with a sickle and staff; as an angel plows for him; or with an angel and white oxen near him.<sup id="cite_ref-ncrlc_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncrlc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In Spanish art, his emblems are a spade or a plough.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Isidore.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Saint_Isidore.jpg/220px-Saint_Isidore.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="306" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Saint_Isidore.jpg/330px-Saint_Isidore.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Saint_Isidore.jpg/440px-Saint_Isidore.jpg 2x" data-file-width="725" data-file-height="1010" /></a><figcaption>This is in the chapel built where he lived and died, in the Saint Isidore Museum in Saint Andrew's square in Madrid.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The story of St. Isidore is a reminder of the <a href="/wiki/Dignity_of_work" class="mw-redirect" title="Dignity of work">dignity of work</a>, and that ordinary life can lead to holiness.<sup id="cite_ref-ball_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ball-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Legends about angel helpers and mysterious oxen indicate that his work was not neglected and his duties did not go unfulfilled. St. Isidore's life demonstrates that: If you have your spiritual self in order, your earthly commitments will fall into order also."<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The house of his master, Juan de Vargas, in Madrid is now a museum, popularly known as the "Casa de San Isidro". It houses temporary exhibitions on the history of Madrid, as well as on the life of the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-gomadrid_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gomadrid-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> It is not to be confused with the aforementioned San Isidro Church. Not only does this museum contain a chapel built upon the place where Isidore lived and died, but also the well where his son fell and was saved.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day_celebrations_and_festivals">Feast day celebrations and festivals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Feast day celebrations and festivals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The date of his liturgical feast, which, though not included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, has been celebrated for centuries in several countries and dioceses, is 15 May.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Many towns venerate Saint Isidore and his wife <a href="/wiki/Maria_Torribia" title="Maria Torribia">Blessed María Torribia</a> with processions in which the fields are blessed.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Spain">Spain</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Spain">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg/220px-Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg/330px-Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg/440px-Procesi%C3%B3n_San_Isidro_Labrador_-Madrid.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="3888" /></a><figcaption>Procesión San Isidro Labrador -Madrid</figcaption></figure>
<dl><dd><dl><dd>see also: <a href="/wiki/Real_Cortijo_de_San_Isidro" title="Real Cortijo de San Isidro">Real Cortijo de San Isidro</a></dd></dl></dd></dl>
<p>One of the most celebrated holidays of Madrid is held on 15 May, the liturgical feast of Isidore who is the city's patron saint as well as of farmers. The traditional festival is held in an open-air area known as the <i>Pradera del Santo</i>. In the afternoon, the images of San Isidro and his wife, Santa María de la Cabeza, are paraded through the streets, from Calle del Sacramento to the Plaza de la Villa, passing through Calle del Cordón.<sup id="cite_ref-gomadrid_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gomadrid-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The feast in honor of San Isidro is declared of National Tourist Interest in <a href="/wiki/Andalusia" title="Andalusia">Andalusia</a> and is one of the most important celebrations in province of <a href="/wiki/Malaga" class="mw-redirect" title="Malaga">Malaga</a>. The fiesta is very popular in <a href="/wiki/Alameda,_Andalusia" title="Alameda, Andalusia">Alameda</a> because San Isidro is a co-patron of the town.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Celebrations honoring both saints are also held elsewhere in Spain. For years, the Alicantine locality of Castalla has been celebrating the Fair of San Isidro, where numerous companies display their products in a playful and festive atmosphere. A medieval <a href="/wiki/Swap_meet" class="mw-redirect" title="Swap meet">swap meet</a> and mechanical attractions are especially popular.
</p><p>A large celebration is held in <a href="/wiki/Estepona" title="Estepona">Estepona</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Marbella" title="Marbella">Marbella</a> along the <i><a href="/wiki/Costa_del_Sol" title="Costa del Sol">Costa del Sol</a></i> in Andalucia, where locals celebrate the day by drinking a mix of brandy and a popular energy drink which is named in his honour. This has led to Saint Isidore often being termed the “patron saint of krunk” (the name of this combination drink in the United States).
</p><p>The Romería festival in Almogia, a <i>pueblo blanco</i> in the campo halfway between Malaga to the south and Antequera, celebrates San Isidro, its patron saint, on the weekend in the middle of May with a fiesta carnival. Floats from the surrounding farming communities, accompanied by traditionally dressed ladies in <i>flamenco</i> dresses and caballeros on dancing horses, sing and dance from Almogia to the Romería ground a few kilometres north of the village and the festival includes music, traditional horse races, a bar for horses as well as their riders, and much parading of costume and finery. The best-dressed float is awarded a prize.
</p>

<p>15 May is San Isidro Day in <a href="/wiki/Cuz_Cuz,_Chile" class="mw-redirect" title="Cuz Cuz, Chile">Cuz-Cuz</a>, about five kilometers from the city of Illapel, Choapa province, in the Coquimbo region of Chile. <h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Nicaragua">Nicaragua</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Nicaragua">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>San Isidro, Matagalpa: The town celebrates it patron saint from 1 to 15 May.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Peru">Peru</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Peru">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG/250px-Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG/375px-Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG/500px-Primera_caudrilla_sanisidro.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a><figcaption>First Group of Shippers of <b> San Isidro Labrador</b> in Lima, Peru.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The residents of <b>San Isidro de Carampá</b> of Ayacucho in the city of Lima celebrate a San Isidro festival. The First Society of San Isidro de Carampá organizes the festival, along with the Butler and the Adornante festivals. In the evening, after the celebration of the Mass, a procession moves to the house of the Adornante. On the next day, Central Day, another Mass is said, this time celebrated by the Butler. Another procession is held, followed by a festival.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Philippines">Philippines</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Philippines">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Many festivals are held in honour of Saint Isidore on or around 15 May in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, which is mostly agricultural and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines" title="Catholic Church in the Philippines">predominantly Catholic</a>.
</p>
<ul><li>The <i>Sikoy-Sikoy</i> Festival, The sea festival of <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando,_Cebu" title="San Fernando, Cebu">San Fernando, Cebu</a>, It is in honor of St. Isidore the Farmer. The name of the festival is a Cebuano word Sikoy, which is a fishing method using nets done during rough tides. The festival celebrates the rich and bountiful sea life of San Fernando, which are considered blessings of God's love to the town. It is celebrated every Sunday before the Feast day which is on May 15. The festivity comes with thanksgiving rituals and petition prayers for guidance, protection, and abundance in their farm and sea harvest.</li>
<li>The <i>Apit</i> Festival of <a href="/wiki/Allacapan,_Cagayan" class="mw-redirect" title="Allacapan, Cagayan">Allacapan, Cagayan</a>, done in praise and thanksgiving for the harvest gathered throughout the year. The Apit Festival is celebrated on 10–15 May.</li>
<li>The <i>Sabugan ng Biyaya</i> Festival (often shortened to <i>Sabugan</i> Festival) of <a href="/wiki/Agdangan,_Quezon" class="mw-redirect" title="Agdangan, Quezon">Agdangan, Quezon</a>, is a thanksgiving event for the blessings that the town has received.</li>
<li>The <a href="/wiki/Lucban,_Quezon#Pahiyas_Festival" class="mw-redirect" title="Lucban, Quezon"><i>Pahiyás</i> Festival</a> is held in honour of Saint Isidore and Blessed María in <a href="/wiki/Lucban,_Quezon" class="mw-redirect" title="Lucban, Quezon">Lucban, Quezon</a>, in thanksgiving for a bountiful harvest. It is noted as local houses are decorated with produce and other agricultural products, which are given away after.</li>
<li>The <i>Kangga</i> Festival is held on his feast day in <a href="/wiki/Mogpog" title="Mogpog">Mogpog</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marinduque" title="Marinduque">Marinduque</a> (the island also known for its <a href="/wiki/Moriones_Festival" title="Moriones Festival">Moriones Festival</a> every <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week_in_the_Philippines" title="Holy Week in the Philippines">Holy Week</a>). The festivities highlight farming traditions, and give thanks for a good harvest and the town's continuing prosperity.</li>
<li>The <i>Bariw</i> Festival in <a href="/wiki/Nabas,_Aklan" title="Nabas, Aklan">Nabas, Aklan</a>, held every 12–15 May as Saint Isidore is the town's patron. The feast also showcases the town's <i><a href="/wiki/Pandanus_spiralis" title="Pandanus spiralis">bariw</a></i> products such as hats and mats as well as unique local attractions.</li>
<li>In <a href="/wiki/Pulilan,_Bulacan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pulilan, Bulacan">Pulilan, Bulacan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Carabao" title="Carabao">carabaos</a> are often dressed in clothes or painted, and trained to kneel on their forelegs before the Church of San Isidro Labrador during the fiesta. The custom, unique to the town, is performed as an act of veneration to the saint.</li>
<li>Saint Isidore is celebrated in the towns of <a href="/wiki/San_Isidro,_Nueva_Ecija" title="San Isidro, Nueva Ecija">San Isidro</a> and <a href="/wiki/Talavera,_Nueva_Ecija" title="Talavera, Nueva Ecija">Talavera</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nueva_Ecija" title="Nueva Ecija">Nueva Ecija</a>. The province is often referred to as the "Rice Granary of the Philippines", as rice is its principal crop aside from maize and onion. Celebrations begin a week before the feast day itself, including daily novenas, Masses, processions, public entertainment and a <a href="/wiki/Funfair" class="mw-redirect" title="Funfair">funfair</a> (<i>perya</i>).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/San_Isidro,_Lupao,_Nueva_Ecija" class="mw-redirect" title="San Isidro, Lupao, Nueva Ecija">Barangay San Isidro</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lupao,_Nueva_Ecija" class="mw-redirect" title="Lupao, Nueva Ecija">Lupao, Nueva Ecija</a> also celebrates Saint Isidore as its patron saint.</li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roxas,_Talugtug,_Nueva_Ecija&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Roxas, Talugtug, Nueva Ecija (page does not exist)">Roxas, Talugtug, Nueva Ecija</a> in <a href="/wiki/Talugtug,_Nueva_Ecija" class="mw-redirect" title="Talugtug, Nueva Ecija">Talugtug, Nueva Ecija</a> also celebrates the feast of Saint Isidore for 15 days until 15 May at the Church of <a href="/wiki/Iglesia_Filipina_Independiente" class="mw-redirect" title="Iglesia Filipina Independiente">Iglesia Filipina Independiente</a></li>
<li>Saint Isidore's feast is also celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Lezo,_Aklan" title="Lezo, Aklan">Lezo, Aklan</a> every 14–15 May.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Barangay" title="Barangay">Barangay</a> Teguis in <a href="/wiki/Poro,_Cebu" title="Poro, Cebu">Poro, Cebu</a> celebrates Saint Isidore's feast on 15 May, as well as the day after.</li>
<li>The towns of Tubigon, San Isidro and Bilar in <a href="/wiki/Bohol" title="Bohol">Bohol</a> celebrates Saint Isidore's feast day on 15 May.</li>
<li>Sitio Canipa-an in Barangay Banquerohan, <a href="/wiki/Cadiz,_Negros_Occidental" title="Cadiz, Negros Occidental">Cadiz, Negros Occidental</a>, also celebrates the feast annually in May. The first time the saint was honoured was in 1918.</li>
<li>In Pampanga, the Feast of San Isidro is celebrated every 15 May in different barangays. <a href="/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga" title="San Fernando, Pampanga">City of San Fernando</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bacolor,_Pampanga" class="mw-redirect" title="Bacolor, Pampanga">Bacolor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Macabebe,_Pampanga" class="mw-redirect" title="Macabebe, Pampanga">Macabebe</a>, and other towns have barangays named after the saint.</li>
<li>In <a href="/wiki/Hinunangan,_Southern_Leyte" class="mw-redirect" title="Hinunangan, Southern Leyte">Hinunangan, Southern Leyte</a>, Barangay Calag-itan, which has Saint Isidore the Farmer Chapel, and Barangay Catublian, which hosts the Parish of Saint Isidore the Farmer, as well as Silago also venerate Saint Isidore. These three belong to the <a href="/wiki/Iglesia_Filipina_Independiente" class="mw-redirect" title="Iglesia Filipina Independiente">Iglesia Filipina Independiente</a>, a <a href="/wiki/National_church" title="National church">national church</a> that split from the Catholic Church after the <a href="/wiki/Philippine_Revolution" title="Philippine Revolution">Philippine Revolution</a> and recognises pre-schism saints.</li>
<li>In Barangay <a href="/wiki/Salawag" class="mw-redirect" title="Salawag">Salawag</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dasmari%C3%B1as" title="Dasmariñas">Dasmariñas</a>, the Feast of San Isidro is celebrated along with the Feast of the Arrival of the Virgin of Salawag. Saint Isidore is the principal patron of the village, and secondary patron of the local parish since 1987.</li>
<li>Saint Isidore is the Patron Saint of Barangay San Roque, and also entitled as the 2nd Patron Saint of the whole municipality of <a href="/wiki/Naic" title="Naic">Naic</a>. On May 15, the Feast day of Saint Isidore, the Parish together with the whole town celebrates the BIHISAKAHAN Festival wherein the native dishes and local products are decorated and served for everyone.</li>
<li>Saint Isidore's feast is also celebrated in some places in Northern Samar particularly in the municipality of Palapag where in mostly of its barangays honored Saint Isidore the farmer as their patron saint. These barangay includes Cabariwan, Nipa, Campedico, Matambag, Natawo, Nagbobtac, Napo, Bangon, Jangtud, Mabaras, Magsaysay, Manajao, and Sangay.</li>
<li>Saint Isidore the Farmer is the Patron Saint of Zamboanguita, Negros Oriental and celebrates its feast day on 15 May.</li>
<li>San Isidro Labrador Parish <a href="/wiki/Las_Pinas_City" class="mw-redirect" title="Las Pinas City">Las Pinas City</a> of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Paranaque" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Paranaque">Diocese of Paranaque</a> celebrate its feast day every year on 15th of May.</li>
<li>San Isidro Labrador is the Second Patron of the Diocesan Shrine of the FIVE WOUNDS of Our Lord Jesus Christ Parish. Under this Parish a chapel of San Isidro Labrador, in Carmencita Admiral, known to be the first chapel in Las Pinas established under the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Manila" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Manila">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Manila</a> dedicated to San Isidro. Together the Shrine and the chapel celebrated the saint feast day every 15th of May.</li>
<li>In Lumban, Laguna, among its sixteen barangays, San Isidro Labrador is the patron saints of 5 barangay which the parish differs the feast day of those barangays due to lack of priest before if they'll going to celebrate its feast according to feast date. May 15- Brgy. Lewin and Brgy. Wawa, May 20- Brgy. Maytalang 1, May 22- Brgy. Caliraya and 2nd sunday of May of Brgy. Maytalang 2.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="United_States">United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1947, at the request of the National Catholic Rural Life Conference, he was officially named patron of <a href="/wiki/Farmer" title="Farmer">farmers</a>, with a feast day on 10 May in all dioceses of the United States, with a proper Mass and Office. When St Isidore's feast was first inserted into the calendar for the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> in the year 1947, the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_de_la_Salle" class="mw-redirect" title="Jean-Baptiste de la Salle">Saint John Baptist de La Salle</a> was still being celebrated on 15 May, with the result that the celebration of his feast was assigned to 22 March.
</p><p>With the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, the memorial of Saint John Baptist de la Salle was moved to his day of death, 7 April, and Saint Isidore's was restored to the 15 May date and is celebrated as an optional <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a>. In some places within the US and Canada, his feast is celebrated on 25 October, and other locations and some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholics">Traditionalist Catholics</a> in that area, though not elsewhere, keep the 22 March date.
</p>
<h4><span id="Corrales.2C_New_Mexico"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Corrales,_New_Mexico">Corrales, New Mexico</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Isidore_the_Laborer&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Corrales, New Mexico">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Corrales,_New_Mexico" title="Corrales, New Mexico">Corrales, New Mexico</a>, the town celebrates the San Ysidro Feast day on 15 May. <a href="/wiki/Matachines" title="Matachines">Matachines</a> dance through the streets and the fiesta is a big part of the celebrations in the city.
</p>
