<p><b>Benedict Biscop</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;628</span>&#160;– 690), also known as <b>Biscop Baducing</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and founder of <a href="/wiki/Monkwearmouth-Jarrow_Priory" class="mw-redirect" title="Monkwearmouth-Jarrow Priory">Monkwearmouth-Jarrow Priory</a> (where he also founded the famous library) and was considered a saint after his death.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Biscop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_career">Early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Biscop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Benedict, born of a noble <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> family, was for a time a <a href="/wiki/Thegn" title="Thegn">thegn</a> of King <a href="/wiki/Oswiu_of_Northumbria" class="mw-redirect" title="Oswiu of Northumbria">Oswiu of Bernicia</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#8201;642–670</span>) At the age of 25 (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 653) Benedict made the first of his five trips to Rome, accompanying his friend <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Saint Wilfrid the Elder</a>. However Wilfrid was detained in <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a> <i>en route</i>. Benedict completed the journey on his own, and when he returned to England was "full of fervour and enthusiasm ... for the good of the English Church".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benedict made a second journey to Rome twelve years later. <a href="/wiki/Alchfrith_of_Deira" class="mw-redirect" title="Alchfrith of Deira">Alchfrith of Deira</a>, a son of King Oswiu, intended to accompany him, but the king refused to grant permission. On this trip Biscop met <a href="/wiki/Acca_of_Hexham" title="Acca of Hexham">Acca</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>. On his return journey to England Benedict stopped at <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">Lérins</a>, a monastic island off the Mediterranean coast of Provence, which had by then adopted the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Rule of St. Benedict</a>. During his two-year stay there, from 665 to 667, he underwent a course of instruction, taking monastic vows<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and the name of "Benedict".
</p><p>Following the two years in Lérins Benedict made his third trip to Rome. At this time <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vitalian" title="Pope Vitalian">Pope Vitalian</a> commissioned him to accompany Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore of Tarsus</a> back to <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> in 669. On their return Archbishop Theodore appointed Benedict as abbot of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">SS. Peter and Paul's</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a>, a role he held for two years.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bibliophile">Bibliophile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Biscop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bibliophile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Benedict Biscop, the Bibliophile, assembled a library from his travels. His second trip to Rome had been a book-buying trip.  Overall, the collection had an estimated 250 titles of mostly service books. The library included scripture, classical, and secular works.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Biscop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Monkwearmouth%E2%80%93Jarrow_Abbey" title="Monkwearmouth–Jarrow Abbey">Monkwearmouth–Jarrow Abbey</a></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ecgfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Ecgfrith of Northumbria">Ecgfrith of Northumbria</a> granted Benedict land in 674 for the purpose of building a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>. He went to the Continent to bring back masons who could build a monastery in the <a href="/wiki/Pre-Romanesque_art_and_architecture" title="Pre-Romanesque art and architecture">Pre-Romanesque style</a>. Benedict made his fifth and final trip to Rome in 679 to bring back books for a library, saintly relics, stonemasons, glaziers, and a grant from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Agatho" title="Pope Agatho">Pope Agatho</a> granting his monastery certain privileges. Benedict made five overseas voyages in all to stock the library.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Attwater_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Attwater-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 682 Benedict appointed <a href="/wiki/Eosterwine" title="Eosterwine">Eosterwine</a> as his <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">coadjutor</a> and the King was so delighted at the success of St Peter's, he gave him land in Jarrow and urged him to build a second monastery. Benedict erected a sister foundation (St Paul) at <a href="/wiki/Jarrow" title="Jarrow">Jarrow</a>. He appointed <a href="/wiki/Ceolfrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Ceolfrid">Ceolfrid</a> as the superior, who left Wearmouth with 20 monks to start the foundation in Jarrow. <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, one of Benedict's pupils, tells us that he brought builders and glass-workers from <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a> to erect the buildings in stone.<sup id="cite_ref-Attwater_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Attwater-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He drew up a rule for his community, based on that of Benedict and the customs of seventeen monasteries he had visited. He also engaged Abbot John, Arch-cantor of St. Peter's in Rome, to teach Roman chant at these monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 685, Ecgfrith granted the land south of the River Wear to Biscop. Separated from the monastery, this would be known as the "sundered land," which in time would become the name of the wider urban area.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benedict's idea was to build a model monastery for England, sharing his knowledge of the experience of the Church in Europe. It was the first ecclesiastical building in Britain to be built in stone, and the use of glass was a novelty for many in 7th-century England. It eventually possessed what was a large library for the time&#160;– several hundred volumes&#160;– and it was here that Benedict's student Bede wrote his famous works. The library became world-famous and manuscripts that had been copied there became prized possessions throughout Europe,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> including especially the <a href="/wiki/Codex_Amiatinus" title="Codex Amiatinus">Codex Amiatinus</a>, the earliest surviving manuscript of the complete Bible in the Latin Vulgate version.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Biscop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>For the last three years of his life Benedict was bed-ridden. He suffered his affliction with great patience and faith.<sup id="cite_ref-Attwater_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Attwater-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He died on 12 January 690.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Biscop&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A sermon of <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> (Homily 17) indicates that there was a very early public cult of Biscop; for his feast, but it became more widespread only after the translation of his relics to Thorney under Ethelwold <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;980</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He is recognised as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the Christian Church, which holds his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 12 January.
</p><p>Benedict is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/January_12" title="January 12">12 January</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> venerates him as a saint and celebrates his feast day on 12th January on the New Calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHutchison-Hall201347_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHutchison-Hall201347-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><br />
</p>
