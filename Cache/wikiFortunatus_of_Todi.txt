<p><b>Saint Fortunatus</b> (died 537) was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Todi" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Todi">bishop of Todi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  According to tradition, he defended <a href="/wiki/Todi" title="Todi">Todi</a> during a <a href="/wiki/Gothic_Wars_(6th_century)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gothic Wars (6th century)">Gothic</a> siege.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Todi.  He is praised by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, who calls him a man of great virtue who took great care in attending to the sick.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Gregory, who was born around the time that Fortunatus died, was greatly interested in Fortunatus' life.  Gregory writes that "a certain poor old man was brought to me –because I always love to talk with such men- of whom I inquired his country, and hearing that he was of the city of Todi, I asked him whether he knew Bishop Fortunatus.  He said he knew him very well.  'Then I beseech you,' said I, 'tell me whether you know of any miracles that he did, and, since I am very desirous to know, explained to me what manner of man he was.'"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fortunatus_of_Todi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The church of <i>San Fortunato</i> in Todi is dedicated to him and holds his relics.  San Fortunato began as a <a href="/wiki/Palaeo-Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Palaeo-Christian">Palaeo-Christian</a> temple (7th century) and in 1292 the construction of a new Gothic edifice was begun by the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscans</a>, with a "hall" structure. The crypt houses a <a href="/wiki/Sepulchre" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulchre">sepulchre</a> containing the remains of Fortunatus and other saints, as well as the tomb of <a href="/wiki/Jacopone_da_Todi" title="Jacopone da Todi">Jacopone da Todi</a>.
</p>
