<p><b>Benjamin</b> (AD&#160;329&#160;– <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;424</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyred</a> circa 424 in <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a>. Benjamin was executed during a period of persecution of Christians that lasted forty years and through the reign of two Persian kings: <a href="/wiki/Yazdegerd_I" title="Yazdegerd I">Isdegerd I</a>, who died in 421, and his son and successor, <a href="/wiki/Bahram_V" title="Bahram V">Varanes V</a>. King Varanes carried on the persecution with such great fury that Christians were submitted to the most cruel tortures.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benjamin_the_Deacon_and_Martyr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benjamin was imprisoned for a year for his Christian faith, and later released with the condition that he abandon preaching or speaking of his religion. His release was obtained by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Emperor">Eastern Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a> through an ambassador. However, Benjamin declared that it was his duty to preach about Christ and that he could not be silent. As a consequence, Benjamin was tortured mercilessly until his death in the year 424, specifically, "sharpened reeds [were] stuck under the nails of his fingers and toes."<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to his hagiography, when the king was apprised that Benjamin refused to stop preaching, he "...&#160;caused reeds to be run in between the nails and the flesh, both of his hands and feet, and to be thrust into other most tender parts,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and drawn out again, and this to be frequently repeated with violence. Lastly, a knotty stake was thrust into his bowels, to rend and tear them, in which torment he expired...."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benjamin_the_Deacon_and_Martyr&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benjamin's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on October 13<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a> <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a>. Benjamin's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on March 31 by the Roman Catholic Church. He is mentioned also in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> but has not been included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>.
</p>
