<p><b>Anselm of Lucca</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Anselmus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Anselmo</i>; 1036&#160;– 18 March 1086), born <b>Anselm of Baggio</b> (<i><span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Anselmo da Baggio</i></span></i>), was a <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">medieval</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lucca" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lucca">bishop of Lucca</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_(HRE)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Italy (HRE)">Italy</a> and a prominent figure in the <a href="/wiki/Investiture_Controversy" title="Investiture Controversy">Investiture Controversy</a> amid the fighting in central Italy between <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Tuscany" title="Matilda of Tuscany">Matilda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Countess_of_Tuscany" class="mw-redirect" title="Countess of Tuscany">countess of Tuscany</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_(HRE)" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry IV (HRE)">Henry&#160;IV</a>. His uncle Anselm preceded him as bishop of Lucca before being elected to the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander&#160;II</a>; owing to this, he is sometimes distinguished as <span class="nowrap"><b>Anselm the Younger</b></span> or <span class="nowrap"><b>Anselm II</b>.</span>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Anselm_of_Lucca&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was educated there in grammar and dialectic. Anselm was a nephew of Anselm of Lucca the Elder, who became <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a> in 1061 and designated Anselm to succeed him in his former position as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lucca" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lucca">Bishop of Lucca</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Alexander sent him to <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> advising him to take investiture from <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry IV">Emperor Henry IV</a>. Alexander II, may have elevated him to the cardinalate ca. 1062.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anselm went to Germany, but was loath to receive the insignia of spiritual power from a <a href="/wiki/State_(polity)" title="State (polity)">temporal</a> ruler and returned without <a href="/wiki/Investiture" title="Investiture">investiture</a>. In 1073, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>, again appointed Anselm Bishop of Lucca, but advised him not to accept investiture from Henry IV. For some reason, Anselm did so this time, but soon felt such remorse that he resigned his bishopric, and entered the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> Order at Padilirone, a <a href="/wiki/Cluniac" class="mw-redirect" title="Cluniac">Cluniac</a> monastery near Mantua.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory VII ordered him to return to Lucca, and he reluctantly obeyed, but continued to lead the life of a monk. In the years 1077–79, he accepted the transfer of several castles from Countess Matilda, in preparation for Henry's expected campaign, which was carried out in 1081–84. Meanwhile, he attempted to impose the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Rule of Saint Augustine">Rule of Saint Augustine</a> upon the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons of his cathedral</a>. Most of the canons refused to submit to the new regulations though they were interdicted by the pope. Anselm was expelled from Lucca around 1080, with the help of Emperor Henry and <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Guibert, Antipope Clement III</a>, after the defeat of the papal defender, the Countess <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Tuscany" title="Matilda of Tuscany">Matilda of Tuscany</a> at the Battle of Volta Mantovana (October 1080).<sup id="cite_ref-Livingstone_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Livingstone-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Anselm fled first to the shelter of Moriana, an episcopal stronghold only a few miles up the <a href="/wiki/Arno" title="Arno">Arno</a> from Lucca— accompanied by Bardo, a priest who later wrote his <i><a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">vita</a></i>—then retired to <a href="/wiki/Canossa" title="Canossa">Canossa</a> as spiritual guide to Countess Matilda. Bishop <a href="/wiki/Benzo_of_Alba" title="Benzo of Alba">Benzo of Alba</a>, Henry IV's fiercely partisan supporter, tells how Matilda and Anselm stripped the monasteries to send gold and silver to Gregory in Rome. Because through his prayers was obtained the rout of the enemies of Gregory VII, he is represented before an army in confusion.
</p><p>Some time later Gregory VII made him <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Livingstone_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Livingstone-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> with authorization to rule over all the dioceses which had been left without bishops due to the conflict between pope and emperor.
</p><p>Anselm was well versed in scripture and wrote some important works attacking lay investiture and defending Pope Gregory against <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Antipope Clement III</a> and <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Henry IV</a>. He spent his last years assembling a collection of ecclesiastical law canons in 13 books, which formed the earliest of the collections of canons (<i>Collectio canonum</i>) supporting the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_reform" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian reform">Gregorian reforms</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Livingstone_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Livingstone-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which afterwards were incorporated into the well-known <i>Decretum</i> of the jurist <a href="/wiki/Gratian_(jurist)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gratian (jurist)">Gratian</a>. The <i>Collectio canonum</i> most notably revived the <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian</a>’s <i><a href="/wiki/Novellae_Constitutiones" title="Novellae Constitutiones">Novellae</a></i>, which set the basis for Roman law in the middle ages.
</p><p>Anselm died in Mantua on 18 March 1086, and is regarded as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of that city.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Two biographies were written about the bishop-saint shortly after his death: Pseudo-Bardone’s <i>Vita Anselmi episcopi Lucensis</i> and Bishop Rangerius of Lucca’s, <i>Vita metrica of S. Anselmi lucensis episcopi.</i> Anselm was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_III" title="Pope Victor III">Pope Victor III</a> in 1087.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
