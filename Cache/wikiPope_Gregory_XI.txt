<p><b>Pope Gregory XI</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gregorius</i>, born <b>Pierre Roger de Beaufort</b>; c. 1329 – 27 March 1378) was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> from 30 December 1370 to his death, in March 1378. He was the seventh and last <a href="/wiki/Avignon_pope" class="mw-redirect" title="Avignon pope">Avignon pope</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and the most recent <a href="/wiki/French_pope" class="mw-redirect" title="French pope">French pope</a> recognized by the modern Catholic Church. In 1377, Gregory XI returned the Papal court to Rome, ending nearly 70 years of papal residency in <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. His death shortly after was followed by the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> involving two Avignon-based <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipopes</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pierre Roger de Beaufort was born at Maumont, France, around 1330. His uncle, Pierre Cardinal Roger, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of Rouen, was elected pope in 1342 and took the name <a href="/wiki/Clement_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Clement VI">Clement VI</a>. Clement VI bestowed a number of <a href="/wiki/Benefices" class="mw-redirect" title="Benefices">benefices</a> upon his nephew and in 1348, created the eighteen-year-old a cardinal deacon. The young cardinal attended the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Perugia" title="University of Perugia">University of Perugia</a>, where he became a skilled canonist and theologian.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1909_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott_1909-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conclave_1370">Conclave 1370</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Conclave 1370">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/1370_papal_conclave" title="1370 papal conclave">1370 papal conclave</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_(couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_%28couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne%29.jpg/170px-07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_%28couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="219" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_%28couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne%29.jpg/255px-07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_%28couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_%28couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne%29.jpg/340px-07_Gr%C3%A9goire_XI_%28couronn%C3%A9_par_Guy_de_Boulogne%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1181" data-file-height="1518" /></a><figcaption>Coronation of Gregory XI</figcaption></figure>
<p>After the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_V" title="Pope Urban V">Pope Urban V</a> (December 1370), eighteen cardinals assembled at Avignon entered the conclave on 29 December. Cardinal Roger was unanimously elected on 30 December.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Though initially opposing his own election, Roger eventually accepted and took the name of Gregory XI. On 4 January 1371 he was ordained to the priesthood by the Dean of the College of Cardinals, <a href="/wiki/Guy_de_Boulogne" class="mw-redirect" title="Guy de Boulogne">Guy de Boulogne</a>, and on 5 January was consecrated Bishop of Rome and crowned by the new protodeacon Rinaldo Orsini in the cathedral <a href="/wiki/Notre_Dame_des_Doms" class="mw-redirect" title="Notre Dame des Doms">Notre Dame des Doms</a> in Avignon.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Immediately on his accession he attempted to reconcile the Kings of France and England, but failed. Gregory confirmed a <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Villeneuve" title="Treaty of Villeneuve">treaty between Sicily and Naples</a> at <a href="/wiki/Villeneuve-l%C3%A8s-Avignon" title="Villeneuve-lès-Avignon">Villeneuve-lès-Avignon</a> on 20 August 1372, which brought about a permanent settlement between the rival kingdoms, which were both papal fiefs.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1373, Pope Gregory issued the "<a href="/w/index.php?title=R%C3%A8gle_d%27idiom&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Règle d&#39;idiom (page does not exist)">règle d'idiom</a>", a commandment for the Catholic clergy to embrace the concept of <a href="/wiki/Language_revival" class="mw-redirect" title="Language revival">language revival</a> and to communicate with their flocks in the local <a href="/wiki/Vernacular" title="Vernacular">vernacular</a>, instead of allowing the Church to become a tool of <a href="/wiki/Linguistic_imperialism" title="Linguistic imperialism">linguistic imperialism</a> and coercive <a href="/wiki/Language_death" title="Language death">language death</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory also tried to undertake a crusade due to pleas from <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a> in 1376<sup id="cite_ref-:12_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:12-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> by continuing Pope Urban V’s call for Christians to stop fighting other Christians, which Urban called for in November of 1366.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Efforts were also made to reform corrupt practices in the various monastic orders, such as collecting fees from persons visiting holy sites and the exhibiting of faux relics of saints.<sup id="cite_ref-:12_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:12-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Many of these acts were through the persistence of letters from Catherine of Siena.
</p><p>Like the preceding popes of Avignon, Gregory XI made the mistake of appointing Frenchmen, who did not understand the Italians and whom the Italians hated, as legates and governors of the ecclesiastical provinces in Italy. Italian city states opposed the move of the papacy back to Rome and specifically Florence opposed to the move due to Gregory wanting to expand the Papal states upon the papal return to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Before moving to Rome, however, he had to give his entire attention to the turbulent affairs of Italy. Duke <a href="/wiki/Bernabo_Visconti" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernabo Visconti">Bernabo Visconti</a> of Milan, had, in 1371, made himself master of Reggio and other places that were feudatory to the Holy See. Gregory XI excommunicated him and later declared war on him in 1372 against a Florentine led coalition of Italian city states, which later became known as the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Eight_Saints" title="War of the Eight Saints">War of the Eight Saints</a> (1375-1378). After war broke out, Gregory excommunicated the city and placed the city under interdict on 31 March 1376<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> in an attempt to quell the rebellion. Catherine of Siena tried to convince Gregory to stop the war on behalf of the Florentine state.<sup id="cite_ref-Ott_1909_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ott_1909-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This proved to be futile as the war did not end until after Gregory’s death. The war ended with a peace treaty concluded at <a href="/wiki/Tivoli,_Lazio" title="Tivoli, Lazio">Tivoli</a> in July 1378, negotiated with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a> following the death of Gregory XI.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_to_Rome">Return to Rome</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Return to Rome">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg/220px-Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="110" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg/330px-Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg/440px-Bolognino_Gregorio_XI.jpg 2x" data-file-width="750" data-file-height="376" /></a><figcaption>A <i><a href="/wiki/Bolognino" title="Bolognino">bolognino</a></i> of Gregory XI</figcaption></figure><p>The return to Rome from Avignon has been an issue since <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_V" title="Pope Clement V">Pope Clement V</a> moved the papacy to Avignon in 1309.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> From Popes Clement V to Urban V, the popes of the Avignon Papacy had their reasons to stay in France and not return to Rome. After 68 years of papal rule from France, Gregory XI moved the papacy back to its former seat of power of Rome in 1377. &#160;
</p><p>Gregory was constantly receiving pleas and threats from Catherine of Siena through letters. In total, she wrote 14 letters between 1375 and 1378 until Gregory died. These letters dealt with different matters such as peace, church reform, and moving the papacy back to Rome. Catherine persuades him by saying that it is easier to achieve Gregory’s goal of peace among the city states in Italy by expanding the influence of the Papal states if the papacy is back in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-:12_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:12-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Catherine’s goals of church reform in her eyes seems more plausible if the papacy is in Rome as well. &#160;
</p><p>The return of the Curia to Rome began on 13 September 1376. Despite the protests of the French king and the majority of the cardinals, Gregory left Avignon on that day and made his way to <a href="/wiki/Marseilles" class="mw-redirect" title="Marseilles">Marseilles</a>, where he boarded a ship on 2 October. Arriving at <a href="/wiki/Corneto" class="mw-redirect" title="Corneto">Corneto</a> on 6 December, he decided to remain there until arrangements were made in Rome concerning its future government. On 13 January 1377, he left Corneto, landed at <a href="/wiki/Ostia_(Rome)" title="Ostia (Rome)">Ostia</a> the next day, and from there sailed up the Tiber to the monastery of <a href="/wiki/San_Paolo" title="San Paolo">San Paolo</a>. On 17 January he left the monastery to make a solemn entrance into Rome that same day.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gregory XI did not survive much longer after his move to Rome. He died on 27 March 1378 aged 48–49.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Pope Urban VI, an Italian, was elected to the papacy after his death. However, his decision to move the papacy back to Rome led to the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> and the rise of the Antipopes. Most of Europe supported <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_VII" title="Antipope Clement VII">Clement VII</a> (now considered an antipope) as the true pope.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> While the papacy was in Rome following Gregory’s death, it took another century of popes fighting to restore the papacy to its former glory and to firmly establish itself back in Rome.
</p><p>Subsequently, the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> created by the selection of rival popes forced Europe into a dilemma of papal allegiance. This schism was not resolved fully until the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Constance" title="Council of Constance">Council of Constance</a> (1414–1418).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
