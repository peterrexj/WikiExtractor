<p><b>Adela of Normandy</b>, <b>of Blois</b>, or <b>of England</b> (c. 1067 – 8 March 1137),<sup id="cite_ref-Women_and_Gender_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Women_and_Gender-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> also known as <span class="nowrap"><b>Saint Adela</b></span> in Roman Catholicism,<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was a daughter of <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror</a> and <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Flanders" title="Matilda of Flanders">Matilda of Flanders</a> who later became the <a href="/wiki/Counts_of_Blois" title="Counts of Blois">countess of Blois</a>, <a href="/wiki/List_of_counts_and_dukes_of_Chartres" title="List of counts and dukes of Chartres">Chartres</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Meaux" title="Meaux">Meaux</a> by marriage to <a href="/wiki/Stephen_II_of_Blois" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen II of Blois">Stephen II of Blois</a>. Her husband greatly benefited from the increased social status and prestige that came with such a marriage. She brought with her not only her bloodline but a <a href="/wiki/Dowry" title="Dowry">dowry</a> of money and other movable goods from the prodigious store of Anglo-Norman wealth. She was <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">regent</a> of Blois during the absence of her spouse in 1096–1100 and 1101–02, and during the minority of her son from 1102 until 1120.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Adela was the mother of King <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen of England">Stephen of England</a> and Bishop <a href="/wiki/Henry_of_Blois" title="Henry of Blois">Henry</a> of Winchester. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adela_of_Normandy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is generally believed that Adela was born between 1066 and 1070 after her father's accession to the <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">English</a> throne.<sup id="cite_ref-Women_and_Gender_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Women_and_Gender-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her royal blood marked her as noble in the eyes of her peers. She was the favorite sister of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry I of England</a>, and they were probably the youngest of the Conqueror's children.<sup id="cite_ref-Anglo-Norman_Card_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anglo-Norman_Card-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Adela was a high-spirited and educated woman with a knowledge of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She had three older brothers and one younger, and thus she would not inherit her father's honors; however her bloodline would be a valuable asset to a spouse.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adela married <a href="/wiki/Stephen_II,_Count_of_Blois" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen II, Count of Blois">Stephen Henry</a>, son and heir to the <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Blois" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Blois">count of Blois</a>, between 1080 and 1083, around her fifteenth birthday.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Stephen was nearly twenty years her senior.<sup id="cite_ref-Women_and_Gender_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Women_and_Gender-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Stephen inherited Blois, Chartres and Meaux upon his father's death in 1089, as well as lands and right in parts of Berry and Burgundy.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The Thibaudian dynasty had other possessions east of Paris, and by the end of Adela's life these were coalescing into the county of Champagne.  While Theobald IV takes most of the credit for the emergence of that principality, Adela helped lay its foundations.<sup id="cite_ref-Daniel._1134_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Daniel._1134-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Adela and her husband had a relationship based on trust and mutual respect, if not affection, and she made decisions along with Stephen.<sup id="cite_ref-Evergates,_Theodore_1999_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Evergates,_Theodore_1999-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>  She swore, for instance, to bind herself and her husband to protect the <a href="/wiki/List_of_bishops_of_Chartres" title="List of bishops of Chartres">bishop of Chartres</a>, then in a dispute with the king of France.<sup id="cite_ref-Evergates,_Theodore_1999_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Evergates,_Theodore_1999-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="First_regency">First regency</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adela_of_Normandy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: First regency">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Stephen-Henry joined the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a> in 1096, along with his brother-in-law <a href="/wiki/Robert_Curthose" title="Robert Curthose">Robert Curthose</a>. Adela filled in as regent for her husband during his extended absence as a leader of the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a> (1095–1098)<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> as well as during his second expedition in 1101. Stephen's letters to Adela form a uniquely intimate insight into the experiences of the Crusade's leaders<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and show that he trusted Adela to rule as regent while he was on crusade.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adela's regency included granting monks the right to build new churches, as well as other charters. Adela also worked with <a href="/wiki/Ivo_of_Chartres" title="Ivo of Chartres">Ivo of Chartres</a> at various points, exchanging letters throughout her regency<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> to discuss matters such as the control of misbehaving nuns and larger issues such as disputes about sworn oaths.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> While her husband was away, Adela would continue to tour their lands, settling disputes, promoting economic growth, and even commanding knights to go to battle with the king.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This however was not unique, as during the crusades it was common for noble women to take upon themselves the duties of their male counterparts.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Count of Blois returned to France in 1100 bringing with him several cartloads of maps, jewels and other treasures, which he deposited at Chartres. According to <a href="/wiki/Orderic_Vitalis" title="Orderic Vitalis">Orderic Vitalis</a>, when Stephen-Henry abandoned the First Crusade returning to France in ignominy, "Adela constantly berated him, even during their love-making", urging him to return to the Holy Land.<sup id="cite_ref-Daniel._1134_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Daniel._1134-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He was under an obligation to the pope for agreements made years earlier and returned to Antioch to participate in the <a href="/wiki/Crusade_of_1101" title="Crusade of 1101">crusade of 1101</a>. He was ultimately killed in a last stand after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Ramla_(1102)" title="Battle of Ramla (1102)">Battle of Ramla</a> in 1102.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The image of Adela persuading her ease-loving husband to redeem his reputation through action has proved popular with historians attempting to account for the crusader motivations.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_regency">Second regency</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adela_of_Normandy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Second regency">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adela continued to act as regent after her husband's death and through her son Thibaud's early rule until her retirement in 1120.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Even after Thibaud came of age and no longer needed a regent, Adela continued to issue charters and act as co-ruler of many parts of their land.  Adela did not secure a marriage alliance for Thibaud, who did not get married until after Adela's retirement, which helped to maintain her power and influence over both her son and her lands.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adela, a devout Benedictine sympathizer, employed several high-ranking tutors to educate her children.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>  Her youngest son, Henry, was conceived during the single year Stephen was in France between crusading duties.  At two years of age Henry was pledged to the Church at Cluny Abbey, Saône-et-Loire, France, as an oblate child, that is, he was dedicated to the service of God, according to medieval practice. Henry went on to be appointed Abbot of Glastonbury and Bishop of Winchester. In that capacity, he sponsored hundreds of constructions including bridges, canals, palaces, forts, castles, and whole villages. In addition, Bishop Henry built dozens of abbeys and chapels and sponsored books including the treasured <a href="/wiki/Winchester_Bible" title="Winchester Bible">Winchester Bible</a>.
</p><p>In 1105, after <a href="/wiki/St_Anselm" class="mw-redirect" title="St Anselm">St Anselm</a> visited her during a sickness, she was responsible for communicating the archbishop's earnestness in threatening <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunication</a> to her brother <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry&#160;I</a>. Orderic Vitalis praises her as a "<i>wise and spirited woman</i>" who ably governed her husband's estates and her own.<sup id="cite_ref-Familial_Alliances_7-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Familial_Alliances-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Adela's power and interests are reflected in letters collected, they demonstrate her religiosity and intellect.  In one such letter to the public from 1104, Adela gifts a monastery a portion of land with all the wildlife inhabiting it, but she reserves the power to pass judgment upon crimes committed in the area.  She also makes sure to mark her place as a woman, tying herself to not only her late husband but her sons.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Adela quarrelled with her eldest son William and despite his previously being named heir-designate,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> she appointed his younger brother Theobald to replace him as heir in 1107. Another son, Stephen of Blois, moved to London in 1111 to join the court of his uncle, King Henry I (Beauclerc), and became his favorite. Upon Beauclerc's death in Normandy (1135), Stephen seized the English throne from  Holy Roman Empress Dowager Matilda, King Henry I's daughter, whom the monarch had named as his successor.  This started a protracted civil war in England that lasted nearly twenty years.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Retirement">Retirement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adela_of_Normandy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Retirement">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adela retired to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcigny_Convent&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Marcigny Convent (page does not exist)">Marcigny Convent</a> in 1120. Though she may have considered retiring to an abbey in Normandy where members of her family, including sisters and nieces, may have already been living, Adela was drawn to and chose the larger, more prestigious convent at Marcigny near her son Henry at <a href="/wiki/Cluny_Abbey" title="Cluny Abbey">Cluny Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Adela may have acted as prioress within the community at Marcigny, though this is not certain.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> She continued to interact and communicate with her children and the ecclesiastical leaders of lands that she had once ruled, maintaining her influence over the area.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In one instance, Adela sent letters to both her son Thibaud <sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> and Geoffrey, bishop of Chartres, reminding them of her settlement of a monastic case.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> In these letters she reminds her son how his father and she felt about alms gifting to monasteries.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Later that same year, her daughter <a href="/wiki/Lucia-Mahaut" class="mw-redirect" title="Lucia-Mahaut">Lucia-Mahaut</a> drowned in the wreck of the <i><a href="/wiki/White_Ship" title="White Ship">White Ship</a></i> alongside her husband. Adela lived long enough to see her son Stephen on the English throne, though any response she may have had to this development has been lost.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> She likely took pride in the ascension of her youngest child, Henry of Blois, to the bishopric of Winchester in 1129.<sup id="cite_ref-Anglo-Norman_Card_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anglo-Norman_Card-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> After her death in 1137 at Marcigny, prayers were offered at a number of churches that she had endowed personally or had recognized at some point during her life.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Her money, influence, and entry into the convent resulted in Adela becoming a saint of the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adela_of_Normandy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adela is a saint in the Roman Catholic church. Her feast day is 24 February.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic.org_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic.org-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was deeply religious and supported scholars and poets. Throughout her life she generously endowed various abbeys and churches, helping to expand and preserve the culture and art of her time. <sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
