<p><b>William Petty Fitzmaurice, 1st Marquess of Lansdowne</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a></span> (2 May 1737&#160;&#8211;&#32;7 May 1805; known as <b>the Earl of Shelburne</b> between 1761 and 1784, by which title he is generally known to history), was an Anglo-Irish <a href="/wiki/Whig_(British_political_party)" class="mw-redirect" title="Whig (British political party)">Whig</a> statesman who was the first <a href="/wiki/Home_secretary" class="mw-redirect" title="Home secretary">home secretary</a> in 1782 and then <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">prime minister</a> from 1782 to 1783 during the final months of the <a href="/wiki/American_War_of_Independence" class="mw-redirect" title="American War of Independence">American War of Independence</a>. He succeeded in securing peace with America and this feat remains his most notable legacy.<sup id="cite_ref-www.number10.gov.uk_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-www.number10.gov.uk-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lord Shelburne was born in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> and spent his formative years in Ireland. After attending <a href="/wiki/Oxford_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford University">Oxford University</a> he served in the British Army during the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War" title="Seven Years&#39; War">Seven Years' War</a>. As a reward for his conduct at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Kloster_Kampen" title="Battle of Kloster Kampen">Battle of Kloster Kampen</a>, Shelburne was appointed an <a href="/wiki/Aide-de-camp" title="Aide-de-camp">aide-de-camp</a> to <a href="/wiki/George_III" title="George III">George III</a>. He became involved in politics, becoming a member of parliament in 1760. After his father's death in 1761, he inherited his title and entered the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>.
</p><p>In 1766, Shelburne was appointed as <a href="/wiki/Southern_Secretary" class="mw-redirect" title="Southern Secretary">Southern Secretary</a>, a position which he held for two years. He departed office during the <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Crisis" title="Corsican Crisis">Corsican Crisis</a> and joined the Opposition. Following the fall of the North government, Shelburne joined its replacement under <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Lord Rockingham</a>. Shelburne was made Prime Minister in 1782 following Rockingham's death, with the American War still being fought.
</p><p>He lost his authority and influence after being driven out of office at the age of 45 in 1783. Shelburne lamented that his career had been a failure, despite the many high offices he held over 17 years, and his undoubted abilities as a debater. He blamed his poor education—although it was as good as that of most peers—and said the real problem was that "it has been my fate through life to fall in with clever but unpopular connections".
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The future Marquess of Lansdowne was born <b>William Fitzmaurice</b> in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>, the first son of <a href="/wiki/John_Petty,_1st_Earl_of_Shelburne" title="John Petty, 1st Earl of Shelburne">John Fitzmaurice</a>, who was the second surviving son of the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Fitzmaurice,_1st_Earl_of_Kerry" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Fitzmaurice, 1st Earl of Kerry">1st Earl of Kerry</a>. Lord Kerry had married Anne Petty, the daughter of Sir <a href="/wiki/William_Petty" title="William Petty">William Petty</a>, <a href="/wiki/Surveyor_General_of_Ireland" title="Surveyor General of Ireland">Surveyor General of Ireland</a>, whose elder son had been created Baron Shelburne in 1688 and (on the elder son's death) whose younger son had been created Baron Shelburne in 1699 and Earl of Shelburne in 1719.
</p><p>On the younger son's death, the Petty estates passed to the aforementioned John Fitzmaurice, who changed his branch of the family's surname to "Petty" in place of "Fitzmaurice", and was created Viscount Fitzmaurice later in 1751 and Earl of Shelburne in 1753 (after which his elder son John was styled <b>Viscount Fitzmaurice</b>). His grandfather, Lord Kerry, died when he was four, but Fitzmaurice grew up with other people's grim memories of the old man as a "tyrant", whose family and servants lived in permanent fear of him.
</p><p>Fitzmaurice spent his childhood "in the remotest parts of the south of Ireland,"<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> and, according to his own account, when he entered <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>, in 1755, he had "both everything to learn and everything to unlearn".
</p><p>From a <a href="/wiki/Tutor" class="mw-redirect" title="Tutor">tutor</a> whom he describes as "narrow-minded" he received advantageous guidance in his studies, but he attributes his improvement in manners and in knowledge of the world chiefly to the fact that, as was his "fate through life", he fell in "with clever but unpopular connexions".<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_career_and_election_to_Parliament">Military career and election to Parliament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Military career and election to Parliament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years%27_War" title="Great Britain in the Seven Years&#39; War">Great Britain in the Seven Years' War</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg/220px-Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg/330px-Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg/440px-Caton-Woodville_Battle_of_Minden_1759.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="360" /></a><figcaption>Shelburne served with distinction during the Seven Years' War participating in engagements such as the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Minden" title="Battle of Minden">Battle of Minden</a> in 1759.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Shortly after leaving the university, he served in <a href="/wiki/20th_Foot" class="mw-redirect" title="20th Foot">20th Foot</a> regiment commanded by <a href="/wiki/James_Wolfe" title="James Wolfe">James Wolfe</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War" title="Seven Years&#39; War">Seven Years' War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He became friends with one of his fellow officers <a href="/wiki/Charles_Grey,_1st_Earl_Grey" title="Charles Grey, 1st Earl Grey">Charles Grey</a> whose career he later assisted.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1757 he took part in the <a href="/wiki/Amphibious_warfare" title="Amphibious warfare">amphibious</a> <a href="/wiki/Raid_on_Rochefort" title="Raid on Rochefort">Raid on Rochefort</a> which withdrew without making any serious attempt on the town. The following year he was sent to serve in Germany and distinguished himself at <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Minden" title="Battle of Minden">Minden</a> and <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Kloster-Kampen" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Kloster-Kampen">Kloster-Kampen</a>. For his services he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Aide-de-camp" title="Aide-de-camp">aide-de-camp</a> to the new King, <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">George III</a>, with the rank of <a href="/wiki/Colonel_(United_Kingdom)" title="Colonel (United Kingdom)">colonel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This brought protests from several members of the cabinet as it meant he was promoted ahead of much more senior officers.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In response to the appointment the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Lennox,_3rd_Duke_of_Richmond" title="Charles Lennox, 3rd Duke of Richmond">Duke of Richmond</a> resigned a post in the royal household.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Though he had no active military career after this,<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> his early promotion as colonel meant that he would be further promoted through seniority to <a href="/wiki/Major-general_(British_Army)" class="mw-redirect" title="Major-general (British Army)">major-general</a> in 1765,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Lieutenant-general_(British_Army)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lieutenant-general (British Army)">lieutenant-general</a> in 1772<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/General_(British_Army)" class="mw-redirect" title="General (British Army)">general</a> in 1783.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 2 June 1760, while still abroad, Fitzmaurice had been returned to the <a href="/wiki/British_House_of_Commons" class="mw-redirect" title="British House of Commons">British House of Commons</a> as a member for <a href="/wiki/Wycombe_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Wycombe (UK Parliament constituency)">Wycombe</a>. He was re-elected unopposed at the <a href="/wiki/1761_British_general_election" title="1761 British general election">general election of 1761</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-HoP_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HoP-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and was also elected to the <a href="/wiki/Irish_House_of_Commons" title="Irish House of Commons">Irish House of Commons</a> for <a href="/wiki/County_Kerry_(Parliament_of_Ireland_constituency)" title="County Kerry (Parliament of Ireland constituency)">County Kerry</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, on 14 May 1761, before either Parliament met, he succeeded on his father's death as the second <b>Earl of Shelburne</b> in the <a href="/wiki/Peerage_of_Ireland" title="Peerage of Ireland">Peerage of Ireland</a> and the second <b>Baron Wycombe</b> in the <a href="/wiki/Peerage_of_Great_Britain" title="Peerage of Great Britain">Peerage of Great Britain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HoP_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HoP-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> As a result, he lost his seat in both Houses of Commons and moved up to the House of Lords, though he would not take his seat in the <a href="/wiki/Irish_House_of_Lords" title="Irish House of Lords">Irish House of Lords</a> until April 1764.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded in Wycombe by one of his supporters Colonel <a href="/wiki/Isaac_Barr%C3%A9" title="Isaac Barré">Isaac Barré</a> who had a distinguished war record after serving with <a href="/wiki/James_Wolfe" title="James Wolfe">James Wolfe</a> in Canada.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Economics">Economics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Economics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Shelburne, who was a descendant of the father of quantitative economics, <a href="/wiki/William_Petty" title="William Petty">William Petty</a>, displayed a serious interest in economic reform, and was a proselytizer for free trade. He consulted with numerous English, Scottish, French and American economists and experts. He was on good terms with <a href="/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin" title="Benjamin Franklin">Benjamin Franklin</a> and <a href="/wiki/David_Hume" title="David Hume">David Hume</a>. He met in Paris with leading French economists and intellectuals.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> By the 1770s Shelburne had become the most prominent British statesmen to advocate <a href="/wiki/Free_trade" title="Free trade">free trade</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-www.bowood.org.uk_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-www.bowood.org.uk-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Shelburne said his conversion from mercantilism to free trade ultimately derived from long conversations in 1761 with <a href="/wiki/Adam_Smith" title="Adam Smith">Adam Smith</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In 1795 he described this to <a href="/wiki/Dugald_Stewart" title="Dugald Stewart">Dugald Stewart</a>:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I owe to a journey I made with Mr Smith from Edinburgh to London, the difference between light and darkness through the best part of my life. The novelty of his principles, added to my youth and prejudices, made me unable to comprehend them at the time, but he urged them with so much benevolence, as well as eloquence, that they took a certain hold, which, though it did not develop itself so as to arrive at full conviction for some few years after, I can fairly say, has constituted, ever since, the happiness of my life, as well as any little consideration I may have enjoyed in it.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Ritcheson is dubious on whether the journey with Smith actually happened, but provides no evidence to the contrary. There is proof that Shelburne did consult with Smith on at least one occasion, and Smith was close to Shelburne's father and his brother.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career">Early political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"><table class="box-More_citations_needed plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-Refimprove" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/50px-Question_book-new.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="39" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/75px-Question_book-new.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/100px-Question_book-new.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="399" /></a></span></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>needs additional citations for <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">verification</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Please help <a href="/wiki/Special:EditPage/William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne" title="Special:EditPage/William Petty, 2nd Earl of Shelburne">improve this article</a> by <a href="/wiki/Help:Referencing_for_beginners" title="Help:Referencing for beginners">adding citations to reliable sources</a>&#32;in this section. Unsourced material may be challenged and removed.<br /><small><span class="plainlinks"><i>Find sources:</i>&#160;<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?as_eq=wikipedia&amp;q=%22William+Petty%2C+2nd+Earl+of+Shelburne%22">"William Petty, 2nd Earl of Shelburne"</a>&#160;–&#160;<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?tbm=nws&amp;q=%22William+Petty%2C+2nd+Earl+of+Shelburne%22+-wikipedia&amp;tbs=ar:1">news</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?&amp;q=%22William+Petty%2C+2nd+Earl+of+Shelburne%22&amp;tbs=bkt:s&amp;tbm=bks">newspapers</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?tbs=bks:1&amp;q=%22William+Petty%2C+2nd+Earl+of+Shelburne%22+-wikipedia">books</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22William+Petty%2C+2nd+Earl+of+Shelburne%22">scholar</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.jstor.org/action/doBasicSearch?Query=%22William+Petty%2C+2nd+Earl+of+Shelburne%22&amp;acc=on&amp;wc=on">JSTOR</a></span></small></span>  <span class="date-container"><i>(<span class="date">July 2023</span>)</i></span><span class="hide-when-compact"><i> (<small><a href="/wiki/Help:Maintenance_template_removal" title="Help:Maintenance template removal">Learn how and when to remove this template message</a></small>)</i></span></div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Shelburne's new military role close to the King brought him into communication with <a href="/wiki/John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute" title="John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute">Lord Bute</a>, who was the King's closest advisor and a senior minister in the government. In 1761 Shelburne was employed by Bute to negotiate for the support of <a href="/wiki/Henry_Fox,_1st_Baron_Holland" title="Henry Fox, 1st Baron Holland">Henry Fox</a>. Fox held the lucrative but unimportant post of <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a>, but commanded large support in the House of Commons and could boost Bute's power base. Shelburne was opposed to Pitt, who had resigned from the government in 1761. Under instructions from Shelburne, Barré made a vehement attack on Pitt in the House of Commons.
</p><p>In 1762 negotiations for a peace agreement went on in London and Paris. Eventually, a deal was agreed but it was heavily criticised for the perceived leniency of its terms as it handed back a number of captured territories to France and Spain. Defending it in the House of Lords, Shelburne observed "the security of the British colonies in North America was the first cause of the war" asserting that security "has been wisely attended to in the negotiations for peace".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Led by Fox, the government was able to push the peace treaty through parliament despite opposition led by Pitt. Shortly afterwards, Bute chose to resign as Prime Minister and retire from politics and was replaced by <a href="/wiki/George_Grenville" title="George Grenville">George Grenville</a>.
</p><p>Shelburne joined the <a href="/wiki/George_Grenville" title="George Grenville">Grenville</a> ministry in 1763 as <a href="/wiki/President_of_the_Board_of_Trade" title="President of the Board of Trade">First Lord of Trade</a>. By this stage, Shelburne had changed his opinion of Pitt and become an admirer of him. After failing to secure Pitt's inclusion in the Cabinet he resigned office after only a few months. Having moreover on account of his support of Pitt on the question of <a href="/wiki/John_Wilkes" title="John Wilkes">John Wilkes</a>'s expulsion from the House of Commons incurred the displeasure of the King, he retired for a time to his estate.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Southern_Secretary">Southern Secretary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Southern Secretary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After Pitt's return to power in 1766, he became <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Southern_Department" title="Secretary of State for the Southern Department">Southern Secretary</a>, but during Pitt's illness his conciliatory policy towards America was completely thwarted by his colleagues and the King, and in 1768 he was dismissed from office.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During the <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Crisis" title="Corsican Crisis">Corsican Crisis</a>, sparked by the <a href="/wiki/French_Conquest_of_Corsica" class="mw-redirect" title="French Conquest of Corsica">French invasion of Corsica</a>, Shelburne was the major voice in the cabinet who favoured assisting the <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Republic" title="Corsican Republic">Corsican Republic</a>. Although secret aid was given to the Corsicans it was decided not to intervene militarily and provoke a war with France, a decision made easier by the departure of the hard-line Shelburne from the cabinet.
</p><p>In June 1768 the General Court incorporated the district of <a href="/wiki/Shelburne,_Massachusetts" title="Shelburne, Massachusetts">Shelburne, Massachusetts</a> from the area formerly known as "Deerfield Northeast" and in 1786 the district became a town. The town was named in honour of Lord Shelburne, who, in return sent a <a href="/wiki/Church_bell" title="Church bell">church bell</a>, which never reached the town.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Opposition">Opposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Opposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Shelburne went into Opposition where he continued to associate with <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham</a>. They were both critical of the policies of the <a href="/wiki/North_Ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="North Ministry">North government</a> in the years leading up to the outbreak of the <a href="/wiki/American_War_of_Independence" class="mw-redirect" title="American War of Independence">American War of Independence</a> in 1775. As the war progressed Shelburne cooperated with the <a href="/wiki/Rockingham_Whigs" title="Rockingham Whigs">Rockingham Whigs</a> to attack the government of Lord North. After a British army was compelled to surrender at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Saratoga" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Saratoga">Battle of Saratoga</a> in 1777, Shelburne joined other leaders of the Opposition to call for a total withdrawal of British troops.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Shelburne_ministry" title="Shelburne ministry">Shelburne ministry</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg/220px-Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="279" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg/330px-Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg/440px-Joshua_Reynolds_-_William_Petty.jpg 2x" data-file-width="709" data-file-height="900" /></a><figcaption>A portrait of Lord Shelburne by Sir <a href="/wiki/Joshua_Reynolds" title="Joshua Reynolds">Joshua Reynolds</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In March 1782, following the downfall of the <a href="/wiki/North_ministry" title="North ministry">North ministry</a>, Shelburne agreed to take office under <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Lord Rockingham</a> on condition that the King would recognise the United States. Following the sudden and unexpected death of Lord Rockingham on 1 July 1782, Shelburne succeeded him as Prime Minister. Shelburne's appointment by the King provoked <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox" title="Charles James Fox">Charles James Fox</a> and his supporters, including <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Burke" title="Edmund Burke">Edmund Burke</a>, to resign their posts on 4 July 1782.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Burke scathingly compared Shelburne to his predecessor Rockingham. One of the figures brought in as a replacement was the 23-year-old <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt</a>, son of Shelburne's former political ally, who became <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a>. That year,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers"><span title="Need the year this event took place (December 2022)">year&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Shelburne was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter" title="Order of the Garter">Order of the Garter</a> as its 599th Knight.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Peace_negotiations">Peace negotiations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Peace negotiations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Peace_of_Paris_(1783)" title="Peace of Paris (1783)">Peace of Paris (1783)</a></div>
<p>Shelburne's government continued to negotiate for peace in Paris using <a href="/wiki/Richard_Oswald_(merchant)" title="Richard Oswald (merchant)">Richard Oswald</a> as the chief negotiator. Shelburne entertained a French peace envoy <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Matthias_G%C3%A9rard_de_Rayneval" title="Joseph Matthias Gérard de Rayneval">Joseph Matthias Gérard de Rayneval</a> at his country estate in Wiltshire, and they discreetly agreed on a number of points which formed a basis for peace. Shelburne's own envoys negotiated a separate peace with American commissioners which eventually led to an agreement on American independence and the borders of the newly created United States. Shelburne agreed to generous borders in the <a href="/wiki/Illinois_Country" title="Illinois Country">Illinois Country</a>, but rejected demands by <a href="/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin" title="Benjamin Franklin">Benjamin Franklin</a> for the cession of Canada and other territories. Historians have often commented that the treaty was very generous to the United States in terms of greatly enlarged boundaries. Historians such as Alvord, Harlow and Ritcheson have emphasized that British generosity was based on Shelburne's statesmanlike vision of close economic ties between Britain and the United States. The concession of the vast trans-Appalachian areas was designed to facilitate the growth of the American population and create lucrative markets for British merchants, without any military or administrative costs to Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The point was the United States would become a major trading partner. As the French foreign minister <a href="/wiki/Charles_Gravier,_comte_de_Vergennes" title="Charles Gravier, comte de Vergennes">Vergennes</a> later put it, "The English buy peace rather than make it".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Downfall">Downfall</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Downfall">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Priestly-4.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Dedication to Shelburne in volume I of Joseph Priestly&#39;s Experiments and Observations on Different Kinds of Air (1774)" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Priestly-4.jpg/120px-Priestly-4.jpg" decoding="async" width="120" height="201" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Priestly-4.jpg/180px-Priestly-4.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Priestly-4.jpg/240px-Priestly-4.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2079" data-file-height="3481" /></a><figcaption>Dedication to Shelburne in volume I of <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Priestley" title="Joseph Priestley">Joseph Priestly's</a> <i>Experiments and Observations on Different Kinds of Air</i> (1774)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Fox's departure led to the unexpected creation of <a href="/wiki/Fox-North_Coalition" class="mw-redirect" title="Fox-North Coalition">a coalition</a> involving Fox and <a href="/wiki/Frederick_North,_Lord_North" title="Frederick North, Lord North">Lord North</a> which dominated the Opposition.
</p><p>In April 1783 the Opposition forced Shelburne's resignation. The major achievement of Shelburne's time in office was the agreement of peace terms which formed the basis of the <a href="/wiki/Peace_of_Paris_(1783)" title="Peace of Paris (1783)">Peace of Paris</a> bringing the American War of Independence to an end.
</p><p>His fall was perhaps hastened by his plans for the reform of the <a href="/wiki/Civil_Service_(United_Kingdom)" title="Civil Service (United Kingdom)">public service</a>. He had also in contemplation a Bill to promote free trade between Britain and the United States.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">Pitt</a> became Prime Minister in 1784, Shelburne, instead of receiving a place in the Cabinet, was created <b>Marquess of Lansdowne</b>. Though giving general support to the policy of Pitt, he from this time ceased to take an active part in public affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was elected a Foreign Honorary Member of the <a href="/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences" title="American Academy of Arts and Sciences">American Academy of Arts and Sciences</a> in 1803.<sup id="cite_ref-AAAS_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AAAS-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> A <a href="/wiki/Local_and_personal_Acts_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom)" title="Local and personal Acts of Parliament (United Kingdom)">personal act</a> of 1797 relieved him "from disabilities in consequence of his having sat and voted in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> without having made <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Allegiance_(United_Kingdom)#Origins" title="Oath of Allegiance (United Kingdom)">the necessary oaths and declarations</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1762, he founded the <a href="/wiki/Boodle%27s" title="Boodle&#39;s">Boodle's Club</a>, which would later have as members <a href="/wiki/Adam_Smith" title="Adam Smith">Adam Smith</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Wellington" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a>, Sir <a href="/wiki/Winston_Churchill" title="Winston Churchill">Winston Churchill</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Ian_Fleming" title="Ian Fleming">Ian Fleming</a>, among others, and is now the second oldest club in the world.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lord Lansdowne was twice married:
</p><p>First to Lady Sophia Carteret (26 August 1745&#160;&#8211;&#32;5 January 1771), daughter of <a href="/wiki/John_Carteret,_2nd_Earl_Granville" title="John Carteret, 2nd Earl Granville">John Carteret, 2nd Earl Granville</a>, through whom he obtained the Lansdowne estates near <a href="/wiki/Bath,_Somerset" title="Bath, Somerset">Bath</a>. They had at least one child:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/John_Petty,_2nd_Marquess_of_Lansdowne" title="John Petty, 2nd Marquess of Lansdowne">John Henry Petty, 2nd Marquess of Lansdowne</a> (6 December 1765&#160;&#8211;&#32;15 November 1809), sat from 1786 in his father's interest House of Commons for Chipping Wycombe. After witnessing revolutionary events in Paris, he began to establish an independent reputation as a critic of the war with France and of the suppression of democratic agitation at home. In Ireland, to which he repaired in 1797, he was suspected by the <a href="/wiki/Dublin_Castle_administration" title="Dublin Castle administration">Dublin Castle administration</a> of being a party to <a href="/wiki/Robert_Emmet" title="Robert Emmet">Robert Emmet</a>'s conspiracy to renew the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Rebellion_of_1798" title="Irish Rebellion of 1798">United Irish rebellion of 1798</a> with a rising Dublin. He died, without issue, within four years of his father's death in 1809, aged 43.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>Secondly, to <a href="/wiki/Louisa_Petty,_Marchioness_of_Lansdowne" class="mw-redirect" title="Louisa Petty, Marchioness of Lansdowne">Lady Louisa FitzPatrick</a> (1755&#160;&#8211;&#32;7 August 1789), daughter of the <a href="/wiki/John_FitzPatrick,_1st_Earl_of_Upper_Ossory" title="John FitzPatrick, 1st Earl of Upper Ossory">1st Earl of Upper Ossory</a>. They had at least two children:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Henry_Petty-Fitzmaurice,_3rd_Marquess_of_Lansdowne" title="Henry Petty-Fitzmaurice, 3rd Marquess of Lansdowne">Henry Petty-Fitzmaurice, 3rd Marquess of Lansdowne</a> (1780–1863), who succeeded his half-brother in the title.</li>
<li>Lady Louisa Fitzmaurice (born bef. 1789)</li></ul>
<p>Lord Lansdowne's brother, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Fitzmaurice_(MP)" title="Thomas Fitzmaurice (MP)">The Hon. Thomas Fitzmaurice</a> (1742–1793) of <a href="/wiki/Cliveden" title="Cliveden">Cliveden</a>, was also a <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament" class="mw-redirect" title="Member of Parliament">Member of Parliament</a>.
</p>
