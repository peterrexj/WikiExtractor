<p><b>Victoricus</b> (or <b>Victorice</b>, <b>Victoric</b>), <b>Fuscian</b> (or <b>Fulcian</b>, <b>Fulcien</b>, <b>Fuscien</b>) and <b>Gentian</b> (or <b>Gentien</b>) (died circa 287–303) were three Christian martyrs later venerated as <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> saints. Their <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> falls on 11 December.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victoricus,_Fuscian,_and_Gentian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to tradition, Victoricus and Fuscian were missionaries from the city of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> were preaching the Christian religion in the city of <a href="/wiki/Therouanne" class="mw-redirect" title="Therouanne">Therouanne</a>, and in the areas inhabited by the people known as the <a href="/wiki/Morini" title="Morini">Morini</a>. They were followers of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Quentin" title="Saint Quentin">Saint Quentin</a>, as well as of <a href="/wiki/Crispin_and_Crispinian" title="Crispin and Crispinian">Crispin and Crispinian</a>.
</p><p>Near <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a>, they met Gentian, who warned them that Christians were being killed for their faith. Later, the governor <a href="/wiki/Rictius_Varus" title="Rictius Varus">Rictius Varus</a> (Rictiovarus) questioned Gentian about the whereabouts of Victoricus and Fuscian. Gentian refused to tell him and was consequently <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>. According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i>, the governor later brought Victoricus and Fuscian to Amiens. "Then took spears of iron and put them through their ears and through their nostrils, and had them decapitated. And by the will and power of our Lord, they arose up, and <a href="/wiki/Cephalophore" title="Cephalophore">took their heads in their hands</a>, and bare them two miles far from the place where they had been beheaded."<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It is said that all three were buried at the place called <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fuscien" title="Saint-Fuscien">Saint-Fuscien</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victoricus,_Fuscian,_and_Gentian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_(partie_sup%C3%A9rieure).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_%28partie_sup%C3%A9rieure%29.jpg/200px-Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_%28partie_sup%C3%A9rieure%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="267" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_%28partie_sup%C3%A9rieure%29.jpg/300px-Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_%28partie_sup%C3%A9rieure%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_%28partie_sup%C3%A9rieure%29.jpg/400px-Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_%28partie_sup%C3%A9rieure%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2112" data-file-height="2816" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois_%C3%A9glise_tombeau_(partie_sup%C3%A9rieure).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of the three saints in the church of <a href="/wiki/Sains-en-Ami%C3%A9nois" title="Sains-en-Amiénois">Sains-en-Amiénois</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>It is said that <a href="/wiki/Honoratus_of_Amiens" title="Honoratus of Amiens">Honoratus of Amiens</a>, seventh <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Amiens" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Amiens">bishop of Amiens</a> (d. ca. 600), had discovered in his diocese the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of these martyrs. <a href="/wiki/Childebert_II" title="Childebert II">Childebert</a> attempted to possess these <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, but was prevented from removing them. Subsequently, the king made generous gifts to endow the cult of the three saints and sent <a href="/wiki/Goldsmith" title="Goldsmith">goldsmiths</a> to fashion decorative pieces in their honour.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Statues of Fuscian, Gentian and Victoricus stand in the left portal of <a href="/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral" title="Amiens Cathedral">Amiens Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the 7th century, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Audomare" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Audomare">Saint Audomare</a> (Omer) re-evangelized the same area.
</p>
