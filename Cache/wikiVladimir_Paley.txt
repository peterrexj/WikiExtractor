<p><b>Prince Vladimir Pavlovich Paley</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Князь Владимир Павлович Палей</span>; 9 January 1897 – 18 July 1918) was a <a href="/wiki/Russians" title="Russians">Russian</a> nobleman and <a href="/wiki/Poet" title="Poet">poet</a> who was murdered by the <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks">Bolsheviks</a> when he was 21 years old.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vladimir_Paley&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born as <b>Vladimir von Pistohlkors</b> in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petersburg" title="Saint Petersburg">Saint Petersburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a>, on 9 January 1897. His father was <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Paul_Alexandrovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Paul Alexandrovich of Russia">Grand Duke Paul Alexandrovich of Russia</a>, the youngest child of <a href="/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Russia" title="Alexander II of Russia">Emperor Alexander II</a>. His mother was his father's mistress, <a href="/wiki/Princess_Olga_Paley" title="Princess Olga Paley">Olga Valerianovna Karnovich</a>, who was still married to Erich Gerhard von <a href="/wiki/Pistolekors" title="Pistolekors">Pistohlkors</a> at that time.
</p><p>In 1902, Grand Duke Paul—-a widower after his short marriage to <a href="/wiki/Alexandra_Georgievna_of_Greece" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexandra Georgievna of Greece">Princess Alexandra of Greece and Denmark</a>, with whom he had two children before her premature death in childbirth-—wed Olga <a href="/wiki/Morganatic" class="mw-redirect" title="Morganatic">morganatically</a>. In 1904, she was created Countess von Hohenfelsen by <a href="/wiki/Luitpold,_Prince_Regent_of_Bavaria" title="Luitpold, Prince Regent of Bavaria">Prince Regent Luitpold of Bavaria</a>, thus providing Vladimir with the title of <b>Count Vladimir von Hohenfelsen</b>. In 1915, Olga was created <a href="/wiki/Princess_Paley" title="Princess Paley">Princess Paley</a> by <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia" title="Nicholas II of Russia">Nicholas II</a>, which allowed Vladimir to use the title of <a href="/wiki/Prince_Paley" title="Prince Paley">Prince Paley</a>.
</p><p>Prince Vladimir had two elder half-siblings from his father's marriage to <a href="/wiki/Alexandra_Georgievna_of_Greece" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexandra Georgievna of Greece">Alexandra Georgievna of Greece</a>, <i>née</i> Princess Alexandra of Greece and Denmark: <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchess_Maria_Pavlovna_of_Russia_(1890-1958)" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duchess Maria Pavlovna of Russia (1890-1958)">Grand Duchess Maria Pavlovna of Russia</a> and Grand Duke <a href="/wiki/Dmitri_Pavlovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia">Dmitri Pavlovich of Russia</a>. He had two full sisters, both of whom eventually were styled as <a href="/wiki/Her_Serene_Highness" class="mw-redirect" title="Her Serene Highness">Her Serene Highness</a> Princess Paley: Irina Pavlovna and Natalia Pavlovna. He also had three half-siblings from his mother's first marriage: <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Pistohlkors" title="Alexander Pistohlkors">Alexander Erikovich von Pistohlkors</a>, Olga Erikovna von Pistohlkors, and <a href="/wiki/Marianne_Pistohlkors" title="Marianne Pistohlkors">Marianna Erikovna (or Marianne) von Pistohlkors</a>.
</p><p>He spent his childhood in Paris and later graduated from the <a href="/wiki/Corps_des_Pages" class="mw-redirect" title="Corps des Pages">Corps des Pages</a>, an aristocratic military school in Saint Petersburg.<sup id="cite_ref-saenz_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saenz-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In December 1914, he entered the regiment of the Emperor’s Hussars and fought with the Russian army in the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a>. He was promoted to lieutenant and decorated as a war hero with the Order of Saint Anne.
</p><p>Even as a teenager, he showed remarkable talent as a poet. He published two volumes of poetry (1916 and 1918) and wrote several plays and essays, as well as a French translation of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Konstantin_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" title="Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovich of Russia">Grand Duke Konstantin Konstantinovitch</a>'s play, <i>The King of the Jews</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-saenz_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saenz-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the summer of 1917, he and his family were placed for a short time under house arrest by the Provisional Government, because of a poem he wrote about <a href="/wiki/Aleksandr_Kerensky" class="mw-redirect" title="Aleksandr Kerensky">Aleksandr Kerensky</a>. In March 1918, he was arrested by the <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks">Bolsheviks</a> and sent to exile in <a href="/wiki/Kirov,_Kirov_Oblast" title="Kirov, Kirov Oblast">Vyatka</a>, and later in <a href="/wiki/Ekaterinburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Ekaterinburg">Ekaterinburg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alapaevsk" class="mw-redirect" title="Alapaevsk">Alapaevsk</a>. He was brutally murdered in a mineshaft near Alapaevsk, together with his cousins <a href="/wiki/Prince_John_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" title="Prince John Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Ioann Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_Konstantine_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Konstantine Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Konstantine Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prince_Igor_Konstantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince Igor Konstantinovich of Russia">Prince Igor Konstantinovich of Russia</a>, and other relatives. Their bodies were recovered by the Imperial White Army and placed in coffins, then moved around during fighting with the Red Army. They were brought and buried months later at the Russian Mission Orthodox cemetery in <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a>, <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>, the site of which was destroyed during the <a href="/wiki/Cultural_Revolution" title="Cultural Revolution">Cultural Revolution</a> and is now a parking area.
</p><p>A biography of Prince Vladimir Paley by Andrey Baranovsky was published in 1997 in Russian, and another (<i>A Poet Among The Romanovs</i>) by Jorge F. Saenz was published in 2004, in both Russian and English. He is now venerated by the Russian Orthodox Church as a Saint and martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
