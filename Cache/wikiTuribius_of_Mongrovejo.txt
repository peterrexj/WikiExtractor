<p><b>Toribio Alfonso de Mogrovejo</b> (16 November 1538 – 23 March 1606) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> of the Catholic Church who served as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lima" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lima">Archbishop of Lima</a> from 1579 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He first studied in the humanities and law before serving as a professor and later as the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Inquisitor" title="Grand Inquisitor">Grand Inquisitor</a> at the behest of <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">King Philip II</a>. His piety and learning had reached the ears of the king who appointed him to that position which was considered unusual since he had no previous government or judicial experience.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His noted work for the Inquisition earned him praise from the king who nominated him for the vacant Lima archdiocese. The pope confirmed this despite his protests.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mogrovejo was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> in 1578 and was later consecrated as an archbishop in 1580 before setting off for <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a> to begin his mission. He was a noted and charismatic preacher who set about <a href="/wiki/Baptizing" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptizing">baptizing</a> and catechizing the natives while confirming almost half a million people; these included <a href="/wiki/Rose_of_Lima" title="Rose of Lima">Rose of Lima</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martin_de_Porres" title="Martin de Porres">Martin de Porres</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The archbishop was a staunch advocate for archdiocesan reform and set to work reforming the diocesan priests from impurities and scandals while instituting new educational procedures for <a href="/wiki/Seminaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminaries">seminaries</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He predicted the exact date and hour of his death. His reputation for holiness and learning was never forgotten, and it led to calls for his canonization. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a> beatified the late archbishop and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> canonized him as a saint on 10 December 1726.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Turibius_of_Mogrovejo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg/180px-Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="240" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg/270px-Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg/360px-Monumento_Santo_Toribio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>Monument in his birthplace.</figcaption></figure>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Turibius_of_Mogrovejo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Toribio Alfonso de Mogrovejo was born on 16 November 1538 in the <a href="/wiki/Valladolid_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Valladolid (province)">Valladolid</a> province of <a href="/wiki/Habsburg_Spain" title="Habsburg Spain">Habsburg Spain</a>, to the nobles Luis Alfonso de Mogrovejo (1510–1568) and Ana de Roblès i Morán (1515–???); his sister was Grimanese de Mogrovejo i Robledo (1545–1635). He was named after <a href="/wiki/Turibius_of_Astorga" title="Turibius of Astorga">Turibius of Astorga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was noted as a pious child with a strong devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin">Blessed Virgin</a>. He fasted once a week in her honor and recited <a href="/wiki/Rosaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Rosaries">rosaries</a> often.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He received an education befitting a noble at the time; he entered <a href="/wiki/University_of_Valladolid" title="University of Valladolid">the college</a> at Valladolid in 1550 where he studied humanities.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He became a professor teaching law to students at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Salamanca" title="University of Salamanca">reputed college</a> in <a href="/wiki/Salamanca" title="Salamanca">Salamanca</a>. His uncle Juan de Mogrovejo served as a professor there as well as at the San Salvador High School in <a href="/wiki/Oviedo" title="Oviedo">Oviedo</a>, before <a href="/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal" title="John III of Portugal">King Juan III</a> invited him to teach at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Coimbra" title="University of Coimbra">the college</a> in <a href="/wiki/Coimbra" title="Coimbra">Coimbra</a>. Toribio accompanied his uncle there and studied at the college in Coimbra before returning to Salamanca sometime later. His uncle died not long after he returned to Salamanca for his studies.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His learning and virtuous reputation led to <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">King Philip II</a> appointing him as the Grand Inquisitor on the Inquisition Court stationed at <a href="/wiki/Granada" title="Granada">Granada</a> in February 1571.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He remained in that position until 1576 but not without impressing the king with his work.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Turibius_of_Mogrovejo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>During this time Philip II nominated him for the vacant Lima archbishopric, despite his strong protests. He used his knowledge of canon law to remind the king and the pope that priests alone could be designated with ecclesial dignities, but the pope overruled him.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Preparations were made for him to be <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> before the formal announcement could be made.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> in 1578 in Granada (after four consecutive weeks of receiving the <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XIII" title="Pope Gregory XIII">Pope Gregory XIII</a> named him on 16 May 1579 as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lima" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lima">Archbishop of Lima</a>; he received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> in August 1580 from the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Seville" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Seville">Archbishop of Seville</a> <a href="/wiki/Crist%C3%B3bal_Rojas_Sandoval" title="Cristóbal Rojas Sandoval">Cristóbal Rojas Sandoval</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In September 1580 he departed for <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a> alongside his sister and her husband.
</p><p>The new archbishop first arrived in <a href="/wiki/Paita" title="Paita">Paita</a> on 12 May 1581 which was 600 miles – or 970 kilometers – from Lima. He began his new mission travelling to Lima on foot while he <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> and taught the natives. He was enthroned in his new <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">see</a> a week later.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His favorite topic was: "Time is not our own and we must give a strict account of it". He traversed his entire archdiocese three times on foot and alone, exposed to tempests and torrents as well as the wild beasts and tropical heat. He also had to deal with <a href="/wiki/Fever" title="Fever">fevers</a>, and often with threats from hostile tribes. He countered these, all the while baptizing and confirming almost one half million people, including <a href="/wiki/Rose_of_Lima" title="Rose of Lima">Rose of Lima</a>, <a href="/wiki/Martin_de_Porres" title="Martin de Porres">Martin de Porres</a>, <a href="/wiki/Francis_Solano" class="mw-redirect" title="Francis Solano">Francis Solano</a> (who later became a close friend), and <a href="/wiki/John_Macias" title="John Macias">Juan Masías</a>, all of whom would be <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He built roads and schoolhouses as well as chapels and hospitals, and established <a href="/wiki/Convents" class="mw-redirect" title="Convents">convents</a> for them to live in. In 1591 he founded the first <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Western_hemisphere" class="mw-redirect" title="Western hemisphere">Western Hemisphere</a> and mandated that learning indigenous languages was a prerequisite in their formation.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He inaugurated the first part of the third <a href="/wiki/Lima_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Lima Cathedral">Lima Cathedral</a> on 2 February 1604. He also assembled thirteen diocesan synods and three provincial councils during his tenure. He was seen as a champion of the rights of the natives against the Spanish masters. He learnt the local dialects and was seen as a champion for rights and liberties, in challenge to the Peruvian governors' power and control.
</p><p>Mogrovejo sought the reformation of diocesan priests and found that some of their behavior had grown too scandalous to be continued. There were those priests who came to resent him for this, though <a href="/wiki/Francisco_de_Toledo" title="Francisco de Toledo">Francisco de Toledo</a> supported his reform efforts and rendered him assistance.<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the request of Philip II he also oversaw the Third Provincial Council from 1582 to 1583. He served as the council's president, guiding rather than leading it; he drafted important conciliar documents. 
</p><p>Mogrovejo also worked to implement the decrees from the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a> and made evangelization a core theme in his episcopal career.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He produced a trilingual catechism in Spanish/<a href="/wiki/Quechuan_languages" title="Quechuan languages">Quechuan</a>/<a href="/wiki/Aymara_language" title="Aymara language">Aymara</a> in 1584, implementing Trent's call for preaching in indigenous languages.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He endorsed the council's decree which prescribed <a href="/wiki/Excommunication_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunication (Catholic Church)">excommunication</a> of clerics who engaged in business ventures, since these often exploited natives for work and profit. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V" title="Pope Sixtus V">Pope Sixtus V</a> in 1588 confirmed the acts of the Third Council of Lima which implemented Trent's decrees. Many dioceses in South America adopted these acts from Lima.
</p><p>Mogrovejo held two more provincial councils in 1591 and in 1601. Mogrovejo made three pastoral visitations that were all extensive in time. He visited each parish and would first inspect all objects for divine worship (to be in good condition) before talking to the parish priest about the life of the parish. He would then check the parish registers and also see if the priest had the missal that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> had mandated over a decade prior.<sup id="cite_ref-CM_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CM-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Turibius_of_Mogrovejo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He predicted the exact date and hour of his death. It was in <a href="/wiki/Pacasmayo" title="Pacasmayo">Pacasmayo</a> during a pastoral visit that he contracted a fever but continued labouring to the last and arrived at <a href="/wiki/Za%C3%B1a" title="Zaña">Zaña</a> in a critical condition.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He dragged himself to receive the <a href="/wiki/Viaticum" title="Viaticum">Viaticum</a> and died not long after this on 23 March 1606 (<a href="/wiki/Holy_Thursday" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Thursday">Holy Thursday</a>) at 3:30 pm at the Saint Augustine convent. His final words were those of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> on the Cross: "Lord, into Thy hands I commend my spirit".<sup id="cite_ref-TA_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TA-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His remains are interred in the archdiocesan cathedral.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Turibius_of_Mogrovejo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His beatification was celebrated under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a> in 1679 (ratified in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> "<i>Laudeamus</i>"), and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> canonized him as a saint on 10 December 1726 through the papal bull "<i>Quoniam Spiritus</i>".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His liturgical feast was once celebrated on 27 April but is now celebrated on 23 March. His cult was once confined for the most part to <a href="/wiki/South_America" title="South America">South America</a> but is now more widespread because of his pioneering reforms. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaimed him the patron saint for the Latin American episcopate in 1983.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
