<p><b>William Henry Cavendish Cavendish-Bentinck, 3rd Duke of Portland</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society" title="Fellow of the Royal Society">FRS</a></span> (14 April 1738&#160;&#8211;&#32;30 October 1809) was a British <a href="/wiki/British_Whig_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig Party">Whig</a> and then a <a href="/wiki/Tory" title="Tory">Tory</a> politician during the late <a href="/wiki/Georgian_era" title="Georgian era">Georgian era</a>. He served as <a href="/wiki/List_of_Chancellors_of_the_University_of_Oxford" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Chancellors of the University of Oxford">Chancellor of the University of Oxford</a> (1792–1809) and as Prime Minister of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain" title="Kingdom of Great Britain">Great Britain</a> (1783) and then of the <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a> (1807–1809). The gap of 26 years between his two terms as Prime Minister is the longest <a href="/wiki/List_of_Prime_Ministers_of_the_United_Kingdom_by_tenure" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Prime Ministers of the United Kingdom by tenure">of any British Prime Minister</a>. He was also the fourth great-grandfather of <a href="/wiki/Charles_III" title="Charles III">King Charles III</a> through his great-granddaughter <a href="/wiki/Cecilia_Bowes-Lyon,_Countess_of_Strathmore_and_Kinghorne" title="Cecilia Bowes-Lyon, Countess of Strathmore and Kinghorne">Cecilia Bowes-Lyon, Countess of Strathmore and Kinghorne</a>.
</p><p>Portland was known before 1762 by the <a href="/wiki/Courtesy_title" title="Courtesy title">courtesy title</a> Marquess of Titchfield. He held a title for every degree of British nobility: duke, marquess, earl (<a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Portland" title="Earl of Portland">Earl of Portland</a>), viscount (Viscount Woodstock), and baron (Baron Cirencester). He was the leader of the Portland Whigs faction, which broke with the <a href="/wiki/Whig_Party_(UK)" class="mw-redirect" title="Whig Party (UK)">Whig</a> leadership of <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox" title="Charles James Fox">Charles James Fox</a> and joined with <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a> in the wake of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William Henry, Lord Titchfield, was born on April 14, 1738 at <a href="/wiki/Bulstrode_Park" title="Bulstrode Park">Bulstrode Park</a> in <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="Buckinghamshire">Buckinghamshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the eldest son of <a href="/wiki/William_Bentinck,_2nd_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Bentinck, 2nd Duke of Portland">William Bentinck, 2nd Duke of Portland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Cavendish-Harley" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret Cavendish-Harley">Margaret Cavendish-Harley</a> and inherited many lands from his mother and his maternal grandmother,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/John_Holles,_1st_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="John Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle">John Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was educated at <a href="/wiki/Westminster_School" title="Westminster School">Westminster School</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>, where he graduated <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts" title="Master of Arts">MA</a> in 1757.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>On 8 November 1766, Portland married <a href="/wiki/Dorothy_Bentinck,_Duchess_of_Portland" title="Dorothy Bentinck, Duchess of Portland">Lady Dorothy Cavendish</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/William_Cavendish,_4th_Duke_of_Devonshire" title="William Cavendish, 4th Duke of Devonshire">William Cavendish, 4th Duke of Devonshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charlotte_Cavendish,_Marchioness_of_Hartington" title="Charlotte Cavendish, Marchioness of Hartington">Charlotte Boyle</a>. They were parents of six children:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/William_Bentinck,_4th_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Bentinck, 4th Duke of Portland">William Bentinck, 4th Duke of Portland</a> (24 June 1768&#160;&#8211;&#32;27 March 1854).</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Lord_William_Bentinck" title="Lord William Bentinck">Lord William Henry Cavendish-Bentinck</a> (14 September 1774&#160;&#8211;&#32;17 June 1839).</li>

<li><a href="/wiki/Lord_Frederick_Cavendish-Bentinck" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Frederick Cavendish-Bentinck">Lord Frederick Cavendish-Bentinck</a> (2 November 1781&#160;&#8211;&#32;11 February 1828) married Lady Mary Lowther (died 1863), daughter of <a href="/wiki/William_Lowther,_1st_Earl_of_Lonsdale" title="William Lowther, 1st Earl of Lonsdale">William Lowther, 1st Earl of Lonsdale</a>, 16 September 1820; had issue: <a href="/wiki/George_Cavendish-Bentinck" title="George Cavendish-Bentinck">George Cavendish-Bentinck</a>, ancestor of the 8th and 9th dukes of Portland.</li>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_and_public_offices">Political and public offices</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Political and public offices">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Portland was elected to sit in the <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain" title="Parliament of Great Britain">Parliament of Great Britain</a> for <a href="/wiki/Weobley_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Weobley (UK Parliament constituency)">Weobley</a> in 1761 before he entered the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> after he succeeded his father as <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Portland" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Portland">Duke of Portland</a> the next year. He was associated with the aristocratic Whig Party of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Lord Rockingham</a> and served as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chamberlain_of_the_Household" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chamberlain of the Household">Lord Chamberlain of the Household</a> in Rockingham's first government (1765–1766).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland">Lord Lieutenant of Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Lord Lieutenant of Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Portland served as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Ireland" title="Lord Lieutenant of Ireland">Lord Lieutenant of Ireland</a> in Rockingham's <a href="/wiki/Second_Rockingham_Ministry" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Rockingham Ministry">second ministry</a> (April–August 1782). He faced strong demands for conciliatory measures following years of coercion and taxation brought about by the British government's engagement in the <a href="/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War" title="American Revolutionary War">American Revolutionary War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wilkinson_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilkinson-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  Portland resolved to make concessions and, overcoming the resistance of <a href="/wiki/Lord_Shelburne" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Shelburne">Lord Shelburne</a>, the Home Secretary to whom he reported, convinced Parliament to repeal the <a href="/wiki/Declaratory_Act_1719" title="Declaratory Act 1719">Declaratory Act</a> and to modify <a href="/wiki/Poynings%27_Law_(on_certification_of_acts)" title="Poynings&#39; Law (on certification of acts)">Poynings' Law</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  Following Rockingham's death, Portland resigned from Lord Shelburne's ministry along with other supporters of <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox" title="Charles James Fox">Charles James Fox</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="First_government">First government<span class="anchor" id="First_premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: First government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Fox%E2%80%93North_coalition" title="Fox–North coalition">Fox–North coalition</a></div>
<p>In April 1783, Portland was selected as the titular head of a coalition government as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime Minister of Great Britain">Prime Minister</a>, whose real leaders were Charles James Fox and <a href="/wiki/Frederick_North,_Lord_North" title="Frederick North, Lord North">Lord North</a>. He served as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury" title="First Lord of the Treasury">First Lord of the Treasury</a> in the ministry until its fall in December that same year. During his tenure, the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783)" title="Treaty of Paris (1783)">Treaty of Paris</a> was signed, which formally ended the American Revolutionary War. The government was brought down after it had lost a vote in the House of Lords on its proposed reform of the <a href="/wiki/East_India_Company" title="East India Company">East India Company</a> after <a href="/wiki/George_III" title="George III">George III</a> had let it be known that any peer voting for the measure would be considered his personal enemy.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1789, Portland became one of several vice presidents of <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Foundling_Hospital" title="Foundling Hospital">Foundling Hospital</a>. The charity had become one of the most fashionable of the time, with several notables serving on its board. At its creation, 50 years earlier, Portland's father, <a href="/wiki/William_Bentinck,_2nd_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Bentinck, 2nd Duke of Portland">William Bentinck, 2nd Duke of Portland</a>, had been one of the founding governors, as listed on the charity's <a href="/wiki/Royal_charter" title="Royal charter">royal charter</a> granted by <a href="/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain" title="George II of Great Britain">George II</a>. The hospital had a mission to care for the abandoned children in London, and it achieved rapid fame through its poignant mission, its art collection donated from supporting artists and the popular benefit concerts by <a href="/wiki/George_Frideric_Handel" title="George Frideric Handel">George Frideric Handel</a>. In 1793, Portland took over the presidency of the charity from Lord North.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Home_secretary">Home secretary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Home secretary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Along with many other conservative Whigs such as <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Burke" title="Edmund Burke">Edmund Burke</a>, Portland was deeply uncomfortable with the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>; he broke with Fox over that issue and joined Pitt's government as <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Secretary of State for the Home Department</a> in 1794. In that role he oversaw the administration of patronage and financial inducements, which were often secret, to secure the passage of the <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Union_1800" class="mw-redirect" title="Act of Union 1800">Act of Union 1800</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> He continued to serve in the cabinet until Pitt's death in 1806, from 1801 to 1805 as <a href="/wiki/Lord_President_of_the_Council" title="Lord President of the Council">Lord President of the Council</a><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and then as a <a href="/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio_(United_Kingdom)" title="Minister without portfolio (United Kingdom)">Minister without Portfolio</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_government">Second government<span class="anchor" id="Second_premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Second government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Second_Portland_ministry" title="Second Portland ministry">Second Portland ministry</a></div>
<p>In March 1807, after the collapse of the <a href="/wiki/Ministry_of_all_the_Talents" class="mw-redirect" title="Ministry of all the Talents">Ministry of all the Talents</a>, Pitt's supporters returned to power, and Portland was once again an acceptable figurehead for a fractious group of ministers that included <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a>, <a href="/wiki/Viscount_Castlereagh" class="mw-redirect" title="Viscount Castlereagh">Lord Castlereagh</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Banks_Jenkinson,_2nd_Earl_of_Liverpool" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Banks Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool">Lord Hawkesbury</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spencer_Perceval" title="Spencer Perceval">Spencer Perceval</a>.
</p><p>Portland's second government saw the United Kingdom's complete isolation on the continent but also the beginning of its recovery with the start of the <a href="/wiki/Peninsular_War" title="Peninsular War">Peninsular War</a>. In late 1809, with Portland's health poor and the ministry rocked by the scandalous duel between Canning and Castlereagh, Portland resigned and died shortly thereafter.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Recorder_of_Nottingham" title="Recorder of Nottingham">Recorder of Nottingham</a> until his death.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg/220px-3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg/330px-3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg/440px-3DukePortlandPlaque.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>Memorial to the 3rd Duke of Portland at the family vault in <a href="/wiki/St_Marylebone_Parish_Church" title="St Marylebone Parish Church">St Marylebone Parish Church</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>He died on 30 October 1809 at <a href="/wiki/Burlington_House" title="Burlington House">Burlington House</a>, <a href="/wiki/Piccadilly" title="Piccadilly">Piccadilly</a>, after an operation for the <a href="/wiki/Calculus_(medicine)" title="Calculus (medicine)">stone</a>, and was buried at <a href="/wiki/St_Marylebone_Parish_Church" title="St Marylebone Parish Church">St Marylebone Parish Church</a>, London.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He had lived expensively: with an income of £17,000 a year (worth £577,000 in 2005),<sup id="cite_ref-nacc_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nacc-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> he had debts at his death computed at £52,000 (£1.76 million in 2005),<sup id="cite_ref-nacc_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nacc-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> which were paid off by his succeeding son by selling off some property, including <a href="/wiki/Bulstrode_Park" title="Bulstrode Park">Bulstrode Park</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-odnb_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odnb-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Along with <a href="/wiki/Sir_Robert_Peel" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Robert Peel">Sir Robert Peel</a>, <a href="/wiki/George_Hamilton_Gordon,_4th_Earl_of_Aberdeen" class="mw-redirect" title="George Hamilton Gordon, 4th Earl of Aberdeen">Lord Aberdeen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Benjamin_Disraeli" title="Benjamin Disraeli">Benjamin Disraeli</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Gascoyne-Cecil,_3rd_Marquess_of_Salisbury" title="Robert Gascoyne-Cecil, 3rd Marquess of Salisbury">Marquess of Salisbury</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sir_Henry_Campbell-Bannerman" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Henry Campbell-Bannerman">Sir Henry Campbell-Bannerman</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bonar_Law" title="Bonar Law">Bonar Law</a> and <a href="/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain" title="Neville Chamberlain">Neville Chamberlain</a>, he was the first of eight British prime ministers to die while his direct successor was in office.
</p>

<p>The <a href="/wiki/Portland_Vase" title="Portland Vase">Portland Vase</a> of Roman glass was given its name because it was owned by Portland at his family residence at Bulstrode Park.
</p><p>Portland Parish, in Jamaica, was named after him. The Titchfield School, founded in 1786, is in the parish and is also named in his honour. The school's crest is derived from his personal crest.
</p><p>Two major streets in Marylebone are named after him: <a href="/wiki/Portland_Place" title="Portland Place">Portland Place</a> and <a href="/wiki/Great_Portland_Street" title="Great Portland Street">Great Portland Street</a>. Both were built on land that he once owned.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/North_Bentinck_Arm" title="North Bentinck Arm">North Bentinck Arm</a> and <a href="/wiki/South_Bentinck_Arm" title="South Bentinck Arm">South Bentinck Arm</a> were named for the <a href="/wiki/Bentinck_family" title="Bentinck family">Bentinck family</a> by <a href="/wiki/George_Vancouver" title="George Vancouver">George Vancouver</a> in 1793, along with other names on the British Columbia Coast, such as <a href="/wiki/Portland_Canal" title="Portland Canal">Portland Canal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Portland_Channel" class="mw-redirect" title="Portland Channel">Portland Channel</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Portland_Bay" title="Portland Bay">Portland Bay</a> in Victoria, Australia was named in 1800 by the British navigator <a href="/wiki/James_Grant_(navigator)" title="James Grant (navigator)">James Grant</a>. The city of <a href="/wiki/Portland,_Victoria" title="Portland, Victoria">Portland</a> is located on the bay.
</p><p>The department of <a href="/wiki/Manuscripts_and_Special_Collections,_The_University_of_Nottingham" class="mw-redirect" title="Manuscripts and Special Collections, The University of Nottingham">Manuscripts and Special Collections, The University of Nottingham</a> holds a number of papers relating to him. His personal and political papers (Pw F) are part of the Portland (Welbeck) Collection, and the Portland (London) Collection (Pl) contains his correspondence and official papers, especially in series Pl C.
</p><p>The Portland Estate Papers held at Nottinghamshire Archives also contain items relating to the 3rd Duke's properties.
</p><p>The Portland Collection<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> of fine and decorative art includes pieces owned and commissioned by him, including paintings by George Stubbs.
</p>

<h3><span id="Second_Ministry.2C_March_1807_.E2.80.93_October_1809"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_Ministry,_March_1807_–_October_1809">Second Ministry, March 1807&#160;&#8211;&#32;October 1809</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Second Ministry, March 1807 – October 1809">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
