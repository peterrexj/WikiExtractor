<p><br />
<b>Sherwin</b> (25 October 1550 &#8211; 1 December 1581) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest, executed in 1581. He is a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years_and_education">Early years and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Sherwin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sherwin was born at <a href="/wiki/Rodsley" title="Rodsley">Rodsley</a>, <a href="/wiki/Derbyshire" title="Derbyshire">Derbyshire</a> to John and Constance Sherwin and christened in Longford church. He was educated at Eton college. In 1568, he was nominated by Sir <a href="/wiki/William_Petre" title="William Petre">William Petre</a> to one of the eight <a href="/wiki/Fellow" title="Fellow">fellowships</a> which he had founded at <a href="/wiki/Exeter_College,_Oxford" title="Exeter College, Oxford">Exeter College, Oxford</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Herald_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herald-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> probably influenced by Sherwin's uncle, John Woodward, who from 1556 to 1566 had been <a href="/wiki/Rector_(ecclesiastical)" title="Rector (ecclesiastical)">rector</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ingatestone" title="Ingatestone">Ingatestone</a>, <a href="/wiki/Essex" title="Essex">Essex</a>, where Petre lived. A talented <a href="/wiki/Classics" title="Classics">classical</a> scholar, Sherwin obtained his <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts" title="Bachelor of Arts">Bachelor of Arts</a> in 1571 and <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts" title="Master of Arts">Master of Arts</a> on 2 July 1574, and the following year converted to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a>. He soon made for the English College at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>, where he was ordained a priest by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cambrai">Bishop of Cambrai</a> on 23 March 1577. On 2 August 1577, he left for <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he stayed at the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Rome" title="English College, Rome">English College, Rome</a> for nearly three years.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On 18 April 1580, Sherwin and thirteen companions left Rome for England as missionaries. He got into England in early August and started his successful ministry in different parts of the country but not for long.<sup id="cite_ref-kater_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kater-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prison_and_death">Prison and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Sherwin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Prison and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 9 November 1580, he was arrested while preaching in the house of Nicholas Roscarrock in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> and imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>, where he converted many fellow prisoners, and on 4 December was transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>, where he was tortured on the rack and then laid out in the snow. Later he was put into isolation cell, without food. He is said to have been personally offered a <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">bishopric</a> by <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I</a> if he converted, but refused.<sup id="cite_ref-Herald_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herald-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After spending a year in prison he was finally brought to trial with <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> on a charge of treasonable conspiracy. He was convicted in <a href="/wiki/Westminster_Hall" class="mw-redirect" title="Westminster Hall">Westminster Hall</a> on 20 November 1581. Eleven days later he was taken to <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a> on a hurdle along with <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Briant" title="Alexander Briant">Alexander Briant</a> and <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a>, where the three martyrs were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>. On the scaffold Ralph Sherwin again "professed his innocence, proclaimed his Catholic faith, and prayed for the Queen". Sherwin's last words were <i>"Iesu, Iesu, Iesu, esto mihi Iesus!"</i><sup id="cite_ref-shercath_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shercath-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Sherwin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:264px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg/262px-Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg" decoding="async" width="262" height="183" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg/393px-Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg/524px-Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2296" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Ralph_Sherwin_Plaque_Rodsley_Derbyshire.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A plaque in his birthplace erected by the Shewin Society</div></div></div>
<p>Sherwin was the first member of the English College in Rome to be martyred. During the years 1581–1681 over forty more students were martyred for their faith. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 29 December 1886 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> on 25 October 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a> with a common <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of 25 October. His individual feast day is celebrated on 1 December, the day of his martyrdom.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A Catholic church in the <a href="/wiki/Chellaston" title="Chellaston">Chellaston</a> area of Derby, registered in January 1981, was dedicated to Ralph Sherwin.<sup id="cite_ref-LG-48508-1353_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LG-48508-1353-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>  The church was demolished in 2018 to make space for a Lidl supermarket.
</p><p>There is a Sherwin Football Club based in the Normanton area of Derby. Founded in 1973, the club provides football and social opportunities for all ages and both genders in the local community. The club colours are purple with yellow trim.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
