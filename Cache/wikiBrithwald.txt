<p><b>Berhtwald</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died 731) was the ninth <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a> in England. Documentary evidence names Berhtwald as abbot at Reculver before his election as archbishop. Berhtwald begins the first continuous series of native-born Archbishops of Canterbury, although there had been previous Anglo-Saxon archbishops, they had not succeeded each other until Berhtwald's reign.
</p><p>Berhtwald's period as archbishop coincided with the end of <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid" title="Wilfrid">Wilfrid</a>'s long struggle to regain the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Bishopric of York</a>, and the two-year delay between <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore</a>'s death and Berhtwald's election may have been due to efforts to select Wilfrid for Canterbury. After his election, Berhtwald went to Gaul for consecration and then presided over two councils that attempted to settle the Wilfrid issue, finally succeeding at the second council in 705. Berhtwald also was the recipient of the first surviving <a href="/wiki/Letters_close" title="Letters close">letter close</a> in Western Europe.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Berhtwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known of Berhtwald's ancestry or his early life, but he was born around the middle of the seventh century.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> By 679, he was made abbot of the monastery at <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Church,_Reculver" title="St Mary&#39;s Church, Reculver">Reculver</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>, and a <a href="/wiki/Charter" title="Charter">charter</a> dated May 679 names Berhtwald as abbot. This charter, from <a href="/wiki/Hlothere" class="mw-redirect" title="Hlothere">Hlothere</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Kent" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Kent">King of Kent</a>, is the earliest surviving original <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_charter" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon charter">Anglo-Saxon charter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Election_as_archbishop">Election as archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Berhtwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Election as archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/See_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Canterbury">see of Canterbury</a> was vacant for two years after the death of Theodore before Berhtwald was elected to the office on 1 July 692.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby104_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby104-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The long vacancy resulted from the disturbed conditions in the kingdom of Kent at the time, as various kings fought for control.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks76_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks76-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The succession to the kingdom was disputed between rival claimants <a href="/wiki/Oswine_of_Kent" title="Oswine of Kent">Oswine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wihtred" class="mw-redirect" title="Wihtred">Wihtred</a>, and various outside kings, including <a href="/wiki/Caedwalla" class="mw-redirect" title="Caedwalla">Caedwalla</a> and <a href="/wiki/Swaefheard" class="mw-redirect" title="Swaefheard">Swaefheard</a> raided and plundered Kent. Eventually, Wihtred secured the throne, around 691 or early 692, as Bede names Wihtred as King of Kent, along with Swaefheard, at the time of Berhtwald's election. Swaerfheard, however, is not named as king of Kent after this date.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby104a_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby104a-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The vacancy may also have occurred because Wilfrid, who was at that point having problems in Northumbria, desired to become Archbishop of Canterbury. A contemporary biographer of Wilfrid, <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_Ripon" title="Stephen of Ripon">Stephen of Ripon</a>, says that Theodore had wished for Wilfrid to succeed Theodore at Canterbury. <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_of_Mercia" title="Æthelred of Mercia">Æthelred of Mercia</a> may have supported Wilfrid's <a href="/wiki/Translation_(ecclesiastical)" title="Translation (ecclesiastical)">translation</a> to Canterbury also, but despite these desires, the translation did not happen.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks76_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks76-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Berhtwald was consecrated on 29 June 693,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook213_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook213-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> having travelled to France for his consecration as archbishop of Canterbury by Godwin, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lyon">Archbishop of Lyon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bede282_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede282-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Berhtwald went to the continent for consecration probably because he feared that his election was not supported by all of the kings and bishops. After his consecration, Berhtwald travelled to Rome to obtain the support of Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Sergius I</a>, who wrote to a number of Anglo-Saxon kings and bishops in support of the archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks76_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks76-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Two of these letters survive, and their authenticity has been doubted, mainly because they are only preserved as part of the post-<a href="/wiki/Norman_Conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman Conquest of England">Norman Conquest</a> <a href="/wiki/Canterbury-York_dispute" class="mw-redirect" title="Canterbury-York dispute">Canterbury-York dispute</a>. Historians have since come to regard the two letters as genuine. Sergius also gave Berhtwald a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>, the symbol of an archbishop's authority.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop">Archbishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Berhtwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Berhtwald appears to have been involved in the governance of the church, establishing the bishopric of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sherborne_(historic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sherborne (historic)">Sherborne</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> and it was during his tenure that <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sussex" title="Kingdom of Sussex">Sussex</a>, the last pagan kingdom in England, was converted to Christianity. He also consecrated the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Selsey" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Selsey">Bishop of Selsey</a>. During his time in office, King <a href="/wiki/Wihtred_of_Kent" title="Wihtred of Kent">Wihtred of Kent</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Law_of_Wihtred" title="Law of Wihtred">Law of Wihtred</a> exempted the church from taxation.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE142_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE142-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Berhtwald was a proponent of his predecessor's view of the archbishops of Canterbury as primates of the entire island of Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby18_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby18-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Berhtwald co-operated closely with Wihtred in the kingdom, and secured the exemption of the church from taxation under Wihtred's laws issued in 695. The law code also dealt with other ecclesiastical matters, including marriage, Sunday observance, and pagan worship.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby105_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby105-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> This law code resulted from a royal council that was held at <a href="/wiki/Bearsted" title="Bearsted">Bearsted</a>. Further privileges for the church were issued in 699, and may have been composed by Berhtwald before being promulgated. Another privilege, usually referred to as the "Privilege of Wihtred", is claimed to be a grant from Wihtred to the monasteries of Kent of exemption from non-clerical control. However, this is actually a ninth-century forgery.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Much of Berhtwald's time in office coincided with the efforts of Wilfrid to regain the see of York, and to reverse the division of York into smaller dioceses. Berhtwald was opposed to Wilfrid's desire to restore some separated bishoprics to the bishopric of York as well as regaining his old see.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE142_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE142-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Wilfrid's problems had begun during the archbishopric of Berhtwald's predecessor, Theodore of Tarsus, when Wilfrid had quarreled with the King of Northumbria, <a href="/wiki/Ecgfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Ecgfrith of Northumbria">Ecgfrith</a>, and was expelled from the north. Theodore had taken the opportunity to divide the large see of York into a number of smaller dioceses, and Wilfrid had appealed to the papacy in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-John33_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-John33-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Berhtwald inherited the dispute and presided at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Austerfield" title="Council of Austerfield">Council of Austerfield</a> in 702, at which Wilfrid's biographer relates the story that King <a href="/wiki/Aldfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Aldfrith of Northumbria">Aldfrith of Northumbria</a>, Berhtwald, and the other enemies of Wilfrid conspired to deprive Wilfrid of all his offices and possessions. A more likely story is that Berhtwald managed to secure concessions from the Northumbrians, and tried to broker a compromise. The offer in the end was that Wilfrid would retire to <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ripon_and_Leeds" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ripon and Leeds">Ripon</a> and cease acting as a bishop. Wilfrid rejected this compromise and once more appealed to the pope. Three years later, at a further Council, it was arranged that Wilfrid should receive the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hexham" title="Bishop of Hexham">Bishopric of Hexham</a> in place of that of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks76_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks76-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This was the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Council_of_Nidd&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Council of Nidd (page does not exist)">Council of Nidd</a>, usually dated to 706, and it was held in Northumbria.<sup id="cite_ref-Councils290_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Councils290-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Bede also mentions that Berhtwald consecrated a number of bishops, including <a href="/wiki/Tobias_(bishop_of_Rochester)" title="Tobias (bishop of Rochester)">Tobias</a> as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester" title="Bishop of Rochester">Bishop of Rochester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Read175_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Read175-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of Berhtwald's letters has been preserved, sent to <a href="/wiki/Forthhere" title="Forthhere">Forthhere</a>, Bishop of Sherborne, and asking Forthhere to intercede with Beorwold, the Abbot of Glastonbury, to ransom a slave. Another letter, this one addressed to Berhtwald, from <a href="/wiki/Waldhere_(bishop)" title="Waldhere (bishop)">Waldhere</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_London" title="Bishop of London">Bishop of London</a>, also survives. The main interest in the second letter is that it is the oldest surviving <a href="/wiki/Letters_close" title="Letters close">letter close</a> in Western Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This second letter also relates that Waldhere and Berhtwald had attended a synod which can be dated to sometime between 703 and 705, where the kingdom of Wessex was threatened with excommunication.<sup id="cite_ref-Councils260_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Councils260-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> A charter witnessed by Berhtwald which mentions a supposed 706 council, numbered 54 by Sawyer, is now known to be a fake, although the witness list may be based on a legitimate 8th century charter that no longer survives.<sup id="cite_ref-Councils262_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Councils262-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Likewise, a charter with Berhtwald as a witness and relating to the 716 <a href="/wiki/Councils_of_Clovesho" title="Councils of Clovesho">Council of Clofesho</a> is also known to be a 9th-century forgery, although again it may have been based on actual documents from the council.<sup id="cite_ref-Councils264_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Councils264-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Berhtwald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Berhtwald died on 13 January 731.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook213_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook213-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> An epitaph to him in verse survives, and may have been placed over his tomb,<sup id="cite_ref-ASE_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> which was at Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair517_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair517-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Subsequently he was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> with a feast day of 9 January.<sup id="cite_ref-DictSaint_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictSaint-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Little evidence of extensive cult activity exists, however, and the main evidence for his sainthood is a late medieval entry in a St Augustine's calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS55_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS55-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Berhtwald is the first of the continuous series of native-born archbishops in England, although there had been two previous Anglo-Saxon archbishops at Canterbury—<a href="/wiki/Deusdedit_of_Canterbury" title="Deusdedit of Canterbury">Deusdedit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wighard" title="Wighard">Wighard</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Intro142_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Intro142-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
