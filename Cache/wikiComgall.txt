					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Mediaeval Irish abbot and saint</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the 6th-century king in Dál Riata, see <a href="/wiki/Comgall_mac_Domangairt" title="Comgall mac Domangairt">Comgall mac Domangairt</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>

<p><b>Saint Comgall</b> (c. 510–520 – 597/602), an early Irish saint, was the founder and abbot of the great Irish monastery at <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a> in Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-maccaffrey_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maccaffrey-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Comgall&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Comgall was born sometime between 510 and 520 in <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_nAraidi" title="Dál nAraidi">Dál nAraidi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a> according to the Irish <a href="/wiki/Annals" title="Annals">annals</a> near the place now known as <a href="/wiki/Magheramorne" title="Magheramorne">Magheramorne</a> in present-day <a href="/wiki/County_Antrim" title="County Antrim">County Antrim</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-maccaffrey_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maccaffrey-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Comgall's father was Setna, a <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Pictish</a> warrior; his mother's name was Briga.<sup id="cite_ref-Lapa_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lapa-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>After serving as a soldier in his early life, he was educated under <a href="/wiki/Fintan_of_Clonenagh" title="Fintan of Clonenagh">Fintan of Clonenagh</a> and also studied under <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Movilla" title="Finnian of Movilla">Finnian of Movilla</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mobh%C3%AD_Cl%C3%A1rainech" title="Mobhí Clárainech">Mobhí Clárainech</a> at Glasnevin, and <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Ciarán of Clonmacnoise</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He was ordained deacon and priest by Bishop Lugidius, either at <a href="/wiki/Clonmacnoise" title="Clonmacnoise">Clonmacnoise</a> or Connor. He lived for a while in Ulster on an island on <a href="/wiki/Lough_Erne" title="Lough Erne">Lough Erne</a>, accompanied by a few friends who followed a severe form of monastic life. The regime was so austere that seven companions died of cold and hunger.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Initially intending to go to <a href="/wiki/Sub-Roman_Britain" title="Sub-Roman Britain">Britain</a>, Comgall was dissuaded by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Lugidius&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Lugidius (page does not exist)">Lugidius</a>, the bishop who ordained him, at whose advice he remained in Ireland to spread the monastic life throughout the country. He founded a <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">monastery at Bangor</a>, County Down on the southern shore of <a href="/wiki/Belfast_Lough" title="Belfast Lough">Belfast Lough</a>, directly opposite <a href="/wiki/Carrickfergus" title="Carrickfergus">Carrickfergus</a>. 
According to the Irish annals, Bangor was founded no later than 552, though <a href="/wiki/James_Ussher" title="James Ussher">James Ussher</a> and most of the later writers on the subject assign the foundation to the year 555.<sup id="cite_ref-maccaffrey_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maccaffrey-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Webb places it at 559.<sup id="cite_ref-Webb_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webb-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He is said to have governed in Bangor and other houses over four thousand monks; all which religious men were employed in tillage or other manual labour.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Life in the monasteries was severe. Food was scant and plain. Herbs, water, and bread was customary. Even milk was considered an indulgence. At Bangor only one meal was allowed, and that not until evening. Confession was in public before the community. Severe acts of penance were frequent. Silence was observed at meals and at other times also, conversation being restricted to the minimum. Fasting was frequent and prolonged.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Adamnan's <i>Life of Columba</i>, there was a close connection between Comgall and <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">Columba</a>, though there does not appear to be sufficient authority for stating that Comgall was the disciple of Columba in any strict sense. Comgall was a friend to future saints Cormac, <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Cainnech_of_Aghaboe" title="Cainnech of Aghaboe">Canice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-maccaffrey_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maccaffrey-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
It is believed that among the monks trained by Comgall at Bangor, were <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a> of <a href="/wiki/Luxeuil-les-Bains" title="Luxeuil-les-Bains">Luxeuil-les-Bains</a> (21 or 23 November) and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Moluag" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Moluag">Saint Moluag</a> (25 June).<sup id="cite_ref-oca_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After a period of intense suffering, Comgall received the Eucharist from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fiacre" title="Saint Fiacre">Saint Fiacre</a> and died in the monastery at Bangor. The year of his death was either 602, according to <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Tigernach" title="Annals of Tigernach">Annals of Tigernach</a></i> and <i><a href="/wiki/Chronicon_Scotorum" title="Chronicon Scotorum">Chronicon Scotorum</a></i>, or 597, according to <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Inisfallen" title="Annals of Inisfallen">Annals of Inisfallen</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-maccaffrey_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-maccaffrey-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His relics, which were kept at Bangor, were scattered during Viking raids in 822.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Role">Role</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Comgall&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Role">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Comgall belonged to what is known as the Second Order of Irish Saints. These flourished in the Irish Church during the sixth century. They were for the most part educated in Britain, or received their training from those who had grown up under the influence of the British Schools. They were the founders of the great Irish monastic schools, and contributed much to the spread of monasticism in the <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Irish Church</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Antiphonary_of_Bangor" title="Antiphonary of Bangor">Antiphonary of Bangor</a> of the seventh century claimed that Comgall was 'strict, holy and constant';<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and there has come down to us a Rule of Saint Comgall in Irish,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> but the evidence would not warrant us in saying that as it stands at present it could be attributed to him. The fact, however, that Columbanus, a disciple of Comgall and himself a monk of Bangor, drew up for his Continental monasteries a <i>Regula Monachorum</i> would lead us to believe that there had been a similar organisation in Bangor in his time. This, however, is not conclusive, since Columbanus might have derived inspiration from the Benedictine Rule then widely spread over the <a href="/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula" title="Iberian Peninsula">Iberian Peninsula</a>.
</p><p>St. Comgall is mentioned in the "Life of Columbanus" by Jonas, as the superior of Bangor, under whom St. Columbanus had studied. He is also mentioned under 10 May, his feast-day in the "Felire" of <a href="/wiki/%C3%93engus_of_Tallaght" title="Óengus of Tallaght">Óengus of Tallaght</a> published by <a href="/wiki/Whitley_Stokes" class="mw-redirect" title="Whitley Stokes">Whitley Stokes</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Henry_Bradshaw_Society" title="Henry Bradshaw Society">Henry Bradshaw Society</a> (2nd ed.), and his name is commemorated in the <a href="/wiki/Stowe_Missal" title="Stowe Missal">Stowe Missal</a> (MacCarthy), and in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Tallaght" title="Martyrology of Tallaght">Martyrology of Tallaght</a></i>.
</p>
