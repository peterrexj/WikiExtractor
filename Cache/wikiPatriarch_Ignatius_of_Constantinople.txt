<p>St. <b>Ignatius</b> or <b>Ignatios</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ιγνάτιος</span>), (c. 798 &#8211; 23 October 877) was a <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a> from July 4, 847, to October 23, 858, and from November 23, 867, to his death on October 23, 877.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDvornik1948_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDvornik1948-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches, he is regarded as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of October 23.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ignatios_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ignatius_of_Constantinople_(Menologion_of_Basil_II)2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Ignatius_of_Constantinople_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%292.jpg/270px-Ignatius_of_Constantinople_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%292.jpg" decoding="async" width="270" height="187" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Ignatius_of_Constantinople_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%292.jpg/405px-Ignatius_of_Constantinople_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%292.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Ignatius_of_Constantinople_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%292.jpg/540px-Ignatius_of_Constantinople_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%292.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1076" data-file-height="746" /></a><figcaption>Painting showing the death of Ignatius from the <a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a> (c. 1000 AD)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ignatius, originally named Niketas, was a son of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_I_Rangabe" title="Michael I Rangabe">Michael I Rangabe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prokopia" title="Prokopia">Prokopia</a>. His maternal grandfather was <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_I" title="Nikephoros I">Nikephoros I</a>. Although he was still a child, Niketas had been appointed nominal commander of the new corps of imperial guards, the <i><a href="/wiki/Hikanatoi" title="Hikanatoi">Hikanatoi</a></i>. He was <a href="/wiki/Political_mutilation_in_Byzantine_culture" title="Political mutilation in Byzantine culture">forcibly castrated</a> (and thus made ineligible for becoming emperor, since the emperor could not be a <a href="/wiki/Eunuch" title="Eunuch">eunuch</a>) and tonsured after his father's deposition in 813. He founded three monasteries on the <a href="/wiki/Princes%27_Islands" title="Princes&#39; Islands">Princes' Islands</a>, a favourite place for exiling <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> members of the imperial house.
</p><p>Empress <a href="/wiki/Theodora_(9th_century)" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodora (9th century)">Theodora</a> appointed Ignatius, a staunch opponent of <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">Iconoclasm</a>, to succeed <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Methodius_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Methodius I of Constantinople">Methodios I</a> as patriarch of Constantinople in 847.  Ignatius soon became embroiled in the conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Studites" class="mw-redirect" title="Studites">Stoudites</a> and the moderates in the Church, the issue being whether or not to depose clergymen who had cooperated with iconoclast policies in the past.  Ignatius took the side of the conservative Stoudites and deposed the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Syracuse" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Syracuse">archbishop of Syracuse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_Asbestas" title="Gregory Asbestas">Gregory Asbestas</a>, the leader of the moderate party. Asbestas appealed for redress to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IV" title="Pope Leo IV">Pope Leo IV</a> and thus inaugurated a period of friction in relations between the Roman and Constantinopolitan churches.
</p><p>A fervent critic of the <a href="/wiki/Caesar_(title)" title="Caesar (title)">Caesar</a> <a href="/wiki/Bardas" title="Bardas">Bardas</a>, Ignatius lost support after Emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_III" title="Michael III">Michael III</a> and Bardas removed Theodora from influence in 857. Ignatius was forced to resign in 858 and was replaced by the layman <a href="/wiki/Photius_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Photius I of Constantinople">Photios</a>. Those questions were discussed at councils held in Constantinople in 859, and again in 861.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDvornik194870–90_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDvornik194870–90-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When Photios reversed some of his predecessor's policies, Ignatius's supporters appealed to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a>, who at first tried to stay out of the controversy, but then condemned Photios (863). The immediate issues in the conflict were the question of papal precedence over the patriarch, and jurisdiction over newly converted <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>.
</p><p>In 867 <a href="/wiki/Basil_I_the_Macedonian" class="mw-redirect" title="Basil I the Macedonian">Basil I the Macedonian</a> usurped the throne and, seeking an alliance with Nicholas I and <a href="/wiki/Louis_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis II, Holy Roman Emperor">Louis II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, banished Photios and restored Ignatius on the patriarchal throne.  Reinstated, Ignatius persuaded the Bulgarian prince to expel the hierarchy of the Latin rite from Bulgaria in 870.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Since Ignatius and Photios pursued the same policy, the latter was recalled and reinstated as tutor to the emperor's children. 
</p><p>Of him the Roman Martyrology recorded the following: "At Constantinople St. Ignatius, Bishop, who, when he had reproved Bardas the Cæsar for having repudiated his wife, was attacked by many injuries and sent into exile; but having been restored by the Roman Pontiff Nicholas, at last he went to his rest in peace."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When he died in October 877, Photios was reinstated as patriarch and contributed to Ignatius's canonisation.
</p>
