<p><b>Medardus</b> or <b>Medard</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_(language)" class="mw-redirect" title="French (language)">French</a>: <i>Médard</i> or <i>Méard</i>) (ca. 456–545) was the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Noyon" title="Ancient Diocese of Noyon">Bishop of Noyon</a>.  He moved the seat of the diocese from <a href="/wiki/Vermand" title="Vermand">Vermand</a> to Noviomagus Veromanduorum (modern <a href="/wiki/Noyon" title="Noyon">Noyon</a>) in northern France. Medardus was one of the most honored bishops of his time, often depicted laughing, with his mouth wide open, and therefore he was invoked against <a href="/wiki/Toothache" title="Toothache">toothache</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Medardus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Medardus was born around 456 at <a href="/wiki/Salency" title="Salency">Salency</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oise" title="Oise">Oise</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardy</a>. His father, Nectaridus, was a noble of <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> origin, while his mother Protagia was <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-clugnet_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clugnet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>  includes the fanciful tale  that <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gildard" title="Saint Gildard">Gildard</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rouen">Bishop of Rouen</a>, was his brother, '"born on the same day, consecrated bishops on the same day, and on the same day withdrawn from this life." However, there is no mention of Gildard in the earliest lives of Medardus, and Gildard attended the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="First Council of Orléans">First Council of Orléans</a> in 511, while Medardus was not consecrated until 530.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Pious_fiction" title="Pious fiction">pious fiction</a> links his childhood to his future bishoprics: "He often accompanied his father on business to <a href="/wiki/Vermand" title="Vermand">Vermand</a> and Tornacum (modern <a href="/wiki/Tournai" title="Tournai">Tournai</a>), where he frequented the schools, carefully avoiding all worldly dissipation".<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Medardus lived during the immediate aftermath of the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire" title="Fall of the Western Roman Empire">fall of the Western Roman Empire</a>.  The last <a href="/wiki/Deposition_of_Romulus_Augustus" title="Deposition of Romulus Augustus">Western Roman emperor was deposed</a> in 476.  During 481–511, the <a href="/wiki/Salian_Franks" title="Salian Franks">Salian Frankish</a> king <a href="/wiki/Clovis_I" title="Clovis I">Clovis I</a> conquered and united several <a href="/wiki/Barbarian_kingdoms" title="Barbarian kingdoms">Germanic successor states</a> to form the Kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a>, the predecessor of modern France and Germany.
</p><p>He was ordained at the age of 33. His piety and knowledge, considerable for that time, caused Bishop Alomer of Vermand to confer on him Holy Orders. At the death of Alomer in 530, Medardus was chosen to succeed him as bishop of Vermand. Despite his objections, but the people insisted, so he accepted the responsibilities.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg/280px-St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg" decoding="async" width="280" height="210" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg/420px-St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg/560px-St_Medard%27s_Tympanum.jpg 2x" data-file-width="653" data-file-height="490" /></a><figcaption>Romanesque <a href="/wiki/Tympanum_(architecture)" title="Tympanum (architecture)">tympanum</a> at church of St Medard &amp; St Gildard, <a href="/wiki/Little_Bytham" title="Little Bytham">Little Bytham</a>, UK (the dedication is unique in Britain). The circular opening may once have held a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of the saint. The birds on either side are probably eagles; one is said to have sheltered Medardus from a rainstorm</figcaption></figure>
<p>He is held to have removed the seat of his bishopric from Vermand, a little city with no defences, to Noviomagus Veromanduorum (modern <a href="/wiki/Noyon" title="Noyon">Noyon</a>), the strongest place in that region of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>, in 531.<sup id="cite_ref-clugnet_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clugnet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  That year <a href="/wiki/Chlothar_I" title="Chlothar I">Clotaire</a> marched against the <a href="/wiki/Thuringii" title="Thuringii">Thuringii</a> with his brother <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_I" title="Theuderic I">Theuderic I</a>, but struggles with the <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundians</a> also troubled Merovingian Neustria. Medardus was a councillor to Clotaire, the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> king at Noviodunum (modern <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>).
</p><p>It is also claimed that in 532, at the death of <a href="/wiki/Eleutherius_of_Tournai" title="Eleutherius of Tournai">Eleutherius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Tournai" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Tournai">bishop of Tornacum</a>, Medardus was invited to assume the direction of that diocese also. He refused at first, but being urged by Clotaire himself, he at last accepted. The union of the two dioceses of Noviomagus/Noyon and Tornacum/Tournai lasted until 1146, when they were again separated.<sup id="cite_ref-clugnet_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-clugnet-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Tornacum was a center for evangelizing the pagan <a href="/wiki/Flemish_people" title="Flemish people">Flemings</a>. There, Medardus accepted <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Radegund of Thuringia</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Deaconess" title="Deaconess">deaconess</a> and <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, until she moved to her own foundation at <a href="/wiki/Saix,_Vienne" title="Saix, Vienne">Saix</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Medardus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>King Clotaire, who had paid Medardus a last visit at Noviomagus, where the bishop died, had his body transferred to his own manor of Croviacum (modern <a href="/wiki/Crouy" title="Crouy">Crouy</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> at the gates of the royal city of Noviodunum; there over his tomb was erected the celebrated <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine abbey</a> which bears his name, the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-M%C3%A9dard_de_Soissons" title="Abbey of Saint-Médard de Soissons">Abbey of Saint-Médard</a>. The selection of the site was given authenticity through a familiar <a href="/wiki/Trope_(literature)" title="Trope (literature)">trope</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>:
</p>
<dl><dd><i>When the procession reached Crouy, which is about three miles from Soissons, the bier became wholly immovable. The king then promised to give half the borough of Crouy to the new church. On trying again to lift the bier, it was found that the half facing the part given to the church was loose and could be moved, but the other half was as fast as ever. Clotaire now promised the whole borough to the church. The bier instantly became so light that it could be lifted and carried without any trouble to its final destination.</i> (Walsh 1897)</dd></dl>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Medardus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_medardus.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/d/d9/St_medardus.jpg" decoding="async" width="112" height="136" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="112" data-file-height="136" /></a><figcaption>Coat of arms of <a href="/wiki/L%C3%BCdenscheid" title="Lüdenscheid">Lüdenscheid</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Medardus was one of the most honored bishops of his time. His memory has always been popularly venerated, first in the north of France, then in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> and extending to western <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and he became the hero of numerous legends. His <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a></i> is mentioned by both <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">Venantius Fortunatus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on June 8. It is believed that, as with <a href="/wiki/Swithun" title="Swithun">Swithun</a>, whatever the weather on his feast day, it will continue for the forty days following, unless the weather changes on the feast of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Barnabas" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Barnabas">Saint Barnabas</a> (11 June).<sup id="cite_ref-WikipediaFr_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WikipediaFr-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was often depicted laughing, with his mouth wide open, therefore he was invoked against <a href="/wiki/Toothache" title="Toothache">toothache</a>. He is also invoked against bad weather (but also for rain), sterility and imprisonment. He is patron saint of vineyards, brewers, captives and prisoners, the mentally ill, and peasants.<sup id="cite_ref-aquinas_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aquinas-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The coat of arms of the Dutch municipality of <a href="/wiki/Wessem" title="Wessem">Wessem</a> and that of the German municipality of <a href="/wiki/L%C3%BCdenscheid" title="Lüdenscheid">Lüdenscheid</a> feature St. Medardus.<sup id="cite_ref-HeraldryOfTheWorld_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HeraldryOfTheWorld-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The parish church of <a href="/wiki/St_Medardus_and_St_Gildardus_Church,_Little_Bytham" title="St Medardus and St Gildardus Church, Little Bytham">St. Medardus and St. Gildardus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> is jointly dedicated to him and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gildard" title="Saint Gildard">Saint Gildard</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends_and_customs">Legends and customs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Medardus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legends and customs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a child, Medardus was said to have once been sheltered from rain by an <a href="/wiki/Eagle" title="Eagle">eagle</a> which hovered over him. This is how he was most commonly depicted, and is why he is associated with weather, good or bad, and why he is held to protect those who work in the open air.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
The weather legend concerning Medardus is similar to <a href="/wiki/Swithun" title="Swithun">Swithun</a> in England.  The French rhyme is: <i>Quand il pleut à la Saint-Médard, il pleut quarante jours plus tard</i> (If it rains on St Medardus' Day, it rains for forty days more).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In Czech, the same weather lore is also found, "Medardovˇe kápˇe, 40 dní kape."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Every year at <a href="/wiki/Salency" title="Salency">Salency</a> (Medardus' birthplace) near <a href="/wiki/Noyon" title="Noyon">Noyon</a>, France (and certain other villages) "the most virtuous young girl of the year" of the commune is elected the <i>Rosière</i>. The custom is said to have been started by Medardus himself, and the first <i>Rosière</i> is said to have been his sister, Médrine.<sup id="cite_ref-aquinas_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aquinas-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WikipediaFr_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WikipediaFr-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Clothed in a long white dress, the <i>Rosière</i> is escorted to <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> by 12 young girls dressed in white and 12 young boys. After <a href="/wiki/Vespers" title="Vespers">vespers</a>, accompanied by two godmothers, she is crowned with a crown of 12 <a href="/wiki/Rose" title="Rose">roses</a>, decorated with a blue ribbon and a silver ring, at the chapel of St Médard. Then she goes to receive a bouquet of roses from the mayor, who also presents her with two arrows, two tennis balls and a whistle. She blows the whistle three times and throws nuts to the assembled crowd. The procession is followed by a fair with rides, stalls and fireworks.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
