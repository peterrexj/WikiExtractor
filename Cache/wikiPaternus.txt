<p><b>Padarn</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Paternus, Padarnus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Padarn</i>; <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>: <i lang="br">Padern</i>;&#160;? – <span title="circa">c.</span> 550 AD)<sup id="cite_ref-parish_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an early 6th century <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">British Christian</a> abbot-bishop who founded St Padarn's Church in<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ceredigion" title="Kingdom of Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>. He appears to be one and the same with the first bishop of <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a> and Saint Paternus of Avranches in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>. Padarn built a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Vannes" title="Vannes">Vannes</a> and is considered one of the <a href="/wiki/Brittany#Religion" title="Brittany">seven founding saints of Brittany</a>. Padarn's early <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> is one of five insular and two <a href="/wiki/Bretons" title="Bretons">Breton</a> saints' lives that mention <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">King Arthur</a> independently of <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth" title="Geoffrey of Monmouth">Geoffrey of Monmouth</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Regum_Britanniae" title="Historia Regum Britanniae">Historia Regum Britanniae</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Padarn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Vita Sancti Paterni</i>, a major source for biographical details of Padarn, may be an epitome of a previous and more extensive source. According to the <i>Vita Sancti Paterni</i>, Padarn is <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armorican</a> by race, born to "Petran, his father, and Guean, his mother".<sup id="cite_ref-parish_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents "dedicated themselves to Christ" and Petran left Letavia (modern <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>) for Ireland. Thomas Wakeman names Padarn a nephew of <a href="/wiki/Hoel" class="mw-redirect" title="Hoel">Hoel</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cornouaille" title="Cornouaille">Cornouaille</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wakeman_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wakeman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> However, Canon <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Hunter_Doble" title="Gilbert Hunter Doble">G. H. Doble</a> believes Padarn was likely Welsh, and was later confused with Breton saints of the same name.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Around the year 524 Padarn traveled to join his father in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. He joined a fellowship of monks led by his cousin, St. Cadvan, who were travelling to <a href="/wiki/Britannia" title="Britannia">Britannia</a>. At this time, as in many saints' lives of the era, there appears to be an aristocratic military function in Padarn's career, for among the travellers were Padarn's cousins, who appointed him as the fourth leader of the troop, saying "you should rule over people for example of life".<sup id="cite_ref-Wakeman_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wakeman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale,_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale%2C_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg/220px-Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale%2C_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="285" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale%2C_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg/330px-Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale%2C_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale%2C_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg/440px-Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale%2C_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg 2x" data-file-width="721" data-file-height="934" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Vannes_-_cath%C3%A9drale,_vitrail_des_saints_Patern_et_M%C3%A9riadec_Detail_Padarn.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>1878 <a href="/wiki/Stained_glass_window" class="mw-redirect" title="Stained glass window">stained glass window</a> of St. Paternus at the Cathedral of Vannes in <a href="/wiki/Morbihan" title="Morbihan">Morbihan</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Padarn became a student at <a href="/wiki/Illtud" title="Illtud">Illtud</a>'s school, <a href="/wiki/Cor_Tewdws" class="mw-redirect" title="Cor Tewdws">Cor Tewdws</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wakeman_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wakeman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Padarn later founded a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at Llanbadarn Fawr, near Aberystwyth, which became the seat of a new diocese, with him as its first bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He then left it in the charge of a trusted steward and proceeded to Ireland. Padarn's spiritual countenance was sufficient to calm the armies of kings of two provinces.<sup id="cite_ref-Maddem_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maddem-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Padarn returned to Llanbadarn Fawr, <a href="/wiki/Maelgwn_Gwynedd" title="Maelgwn Gwynedd">Maelgwn Gwynedd</a> tried to cheat him out of property belonging to the monastery. Two of Maelgwn's evil heralds were undone by the <a href="/wiki/Trial_by_ordeal" title="Trial by ordeal">trial by ordeal</a> of boiling water; scalded and defeated, "Their souls in raven-forms fly to the riverbed, which unto this day by the name of one of them is called, to wit, Graban."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Maelgwn Gwynedd himself is first cursed and then cured of his sickness and blindness when he comes on bended knee to ask forgiveness, and to bestow lands on Padarn's community, which are laid out with the exactitude of a deed: "a quantity of land, that is, from the mouth of the river Rheidiol upwards until it touches at its head the limit of the river Clarach; and along the length of the same river as far as the sea is the limit prolonged."<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <i>Vita Sancti Paterni</i> Padarn travels on a pilgrimage to Jerusalem with <a href="/wiki/Saint_David" title="Saint David">Saint David</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Teilo" title="Saint Teilo">Saint Teilo</a> for all three to be ordained bishops by the patriarch. Along the way they acquired the gift of tongues so that whomever they spoke to understood them in his own language.<sup id="cite_ref-Maddem_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maddem-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The patriarch gave Paternus two gifts, a <a href="/wiki/Crosier" title="Crosier">crozier</a> and a finely woven tunic. On their return, they amicably divided <a href="/wiki/Britannia" title="Britannia">Britannia</a> into three bishoprics.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Padarn finally returned to Letavia, where his fame filled the region and "made peace" with the bishop Samson in Vannes, where Padarn his built a monastery and subsequently made a peace with the six bishops of <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armorica</a>, of which he now made a seventh.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Padarn_and_Arthur">Padarn and Arthur</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Padarn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Padarn and Arthur">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Paternus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Paternus.jpg/220px-Paternus.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="155" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Paternus.jpg/330px-Paternus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Paternus.jpg/440px-Paternus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="918" data-file-height="648" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Paternus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>1878 sketch from <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Little_Pictorial_Lives_of_the_Saints&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Little Pictorial Lives of the Saints (page does not exist)">Little Pictorial Lives of the Saints</a></i>,  by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Benzinger_Brothers&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Benzinger Brothers (page does not exist)">Benzinger Brothers</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>In the most celebrated episode, King Arthur tries to steal Padarn's tunic and subsequently becomes Christian. It's too late to be reliable. But the episode was probably meant to increase Padarn's prestige and credibility as a saint by being granted credit for "Christianizing" the semi-historical leader who allegedly defeated the Anglo-Saxons with the help of Jesus Christ and the Virgin Mary in the battle of Mount Badon according to the 9th century ad monk Nennius. The author also seemed to confuse this Padarn with <a href="/wiki/Padarn_Beisrudd" title="Padarn Beisrudd">Padarn Redcoat</a> whose coat was one of the <a href="/wiki/Thirteen_Treasures_of_the_Island_of_Britain" title="Thirteen Treasures of the Island of Britain">Thirteen Treasures of the Island of Britain</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-Maddem_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maddem-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<blockquote><p>When Padarn was in his church resting after so much labour at sea, a certain tyrant, Arthur by name, was traversing the regions on either side, who one day came to the cell of saint Padarn the bishop. And while he was addressing Padarn, he looked at the tunic, which he, being pierced with the zeal of avarice, sought for his own. The saint answering said, "This tunic is not fitting for the habit of any malign person, but for the habit of the clerical office." He went out of the monastery in a rage. And again he returns in wrath, that he might take away the tunic against the counsels of his own companions. One of the disciples of Padarn seeing him returning in fury, ran to saint Padarn and said,  "The tyrant, who went out from here before, is returning. Reviling, stamping, he levels the ground with his feet". Padarn answers "Nay rather, may the earth swallow him." With the word straightway the earth opens the hollow of its depth, and swallows Arthur up to his chin. He immediately acknowledging his guilt begins to praise both God and Padarn, until, while he begs forgiveness, the earth delivered him up. From that place on bent knees he begged the saint for indulgence, whom the saint forgave. And he took Padarn as his continual patron, and so departed.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Paternus_of_Avranches">Paternus of Avranches</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Padarn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Paternus of Avranches">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The British Padarn appears to be the same person as Saint Paternus of Avranches in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>. By tradition, it is said that he was born in <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>, became a monk at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Saint-Jouin_de_Marnes&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Saint-Jouin de Marnes (page does not exist)">Abbey of Saint-Jouin de Marnes</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbaye_Saint-Jouin_de_Marnes" class="extiw" title="fr:Abbaye Saint-Jouin de Marnes">fr</a>&#93;</span> in France, and retreated with his fellow monk, Saint Scubilion, to the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Forest_of_Scissy&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Forest of Scissy (page does not exist)">forest of Scissy</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/For%C3%AAt_de_Scissy" class="extiw" title="fr:Forêt de Scissy">fr</a>&#93;</span> in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Coutances" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Coutances">diocese of Coutances</a> before the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Coutances" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Coutances">Bishop of Coutances</a> made him a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> in 512.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Padarn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><b>Feast day</b>: April 16 <sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Three days were kept to honour Padern in Armorica; the Armoricans celebrate three solemnities of his, namely, that day of the Kalends of November, when he formed perpetual unity with the six chief saints of Letia, the day of his obit, and the day he received the order of the episcopate, the twelfth before the Kalends of the month of July.
</p><p>As well as the church at Llanbadarn Fawr, there is a second church dedicated to him, in <a href="/wiki/St_Padarn%27s_Church,_Llanberis" title="St Padarn&#39;s Church, Llanberis">Llanberis</a> in Gwynedd.
</p><p>St Padarn’s Institute, located at St Michael’s Conference Centre, Llandaff, handles training for ministry in the Church in Wales.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
