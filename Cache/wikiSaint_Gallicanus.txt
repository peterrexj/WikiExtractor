					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other persons and saints of the same name, see <a href="/wiki/Gallicanus_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Gallicanus (disambiguation)">Gallicanus</a>.</div>
<p><b>Saint Gallicanus</b> was a Roman martyr in <a href="/wiki/Aegyptus_(Roman_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Aegyptus (Roman province)">Egypt</a> in 363 AD, during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Julian_(emperor)" title="Julian (emperor)">Julian</a>. According to his "Acta" (in <i>Acta SS.</i>, June, VII, 31), he was a distinguished general in the <a href="/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Persian_Wars" title="Roman–Persian Wars">war against the Persians</a>. He was also consul with <a href="/wiki/Aurelius_Valerius_Tullianus_Symmachus" class="mw-redirect" title="Aurelius Valerius Tullianus Symmachus">Symmachus</a> in 330 and perhaps also once before with <a href="/wiki/Caesonius_Bassus" title="Caesonius Bassus">Caesonius Bassus</a> in 317. After his conversion to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> he retired to <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica_(archaeological_site)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ostia Antica (archaeological site)">Ostia</a>, founded a hospital where he worked with Saint <a href="/wiki/Hilarinus" title="Hilarinus">Hilarinus</a> and endowed a church built by <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a>. Under Julian he was banished to Egypt, and lived with the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a> in the desert. A small church was built in his honour in the <a href="/wiki/Trastevere" title="Trastevere">Trastevere</a> of Rome. His <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> are at Rome in the church of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Andrea_della_Valle" title="Sant&#39;Andrea della Valle">Sant'Andrea della Valle</a>. The legend of his conversion was dramatized in the tenth century by the nun <a href="/wiki/Roswitha" class="mw-redirect" title="Roswitha">Roswitha</a>. He is commemorated on 25 June.
</p>
