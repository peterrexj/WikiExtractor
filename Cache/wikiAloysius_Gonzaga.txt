<p><b>Aloysius de Gonzaga</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Luigi Gonzaga</i>; 9 March 1568&#160;&#8211;&#32;21 June 1591) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Aristocracy_(class)" title="Aristocracy (class)">aristocrat</a> who became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>. While still a student at the <a href="/wiki/Roman_College" title="Roman College">Roman College</a>, he died as a result of caring for the victims of a serious epidemic. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1605 and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1726.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aloysius_Gonzaga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gonzaga was born the eldest of eight children, at his family's castle in <a href="/wiki/Castiglione_delle_Stiviere" title="Castiglione delle Stiviere">Castiglione delle Stiviere</a>, between <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a> in northern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> in what was then part of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Mantua" title="Duchy of Mantua">Duchy of Mantua</a>, into a cadet branch of the illustrious <a href="/wiki/House_of_Gonzaga" title="House of Gonzaga">House of Gonzaga</a>. "Aloysius" is the Latin form of his given name in Italian, "Luigi".<sup id="cite_ref-univ2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-univ2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Gonzaga was the son of <a href="/wiki/Ferrante_Gonzaga_(1544-1586)" title="Ferrante Gonzaga (1544-1586)">Ferrante de Gonzaga (1544–1586)</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marquis" class="mw-redirect" title="Marquis">Marquis</a> of Castiglione, and Dona Marta Tana di <a href="/wiki/Santena" title="Santena">Santena</a>, daughter of a baron of the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmontese</a> <a href="/wiki/Della_Rovere" title="Della Rovere">Della Rovere</a> family.  His mother was a <a href="/wiki/Lady-in-waiting" title="Lady-in-waiting">lady-in-waiting</a> to <a href="/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Valois" title="Elisabeth of Valois">Isabel</a>, the wife of <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a>.
</p><p>As the first-born son, he was in line to inherit his father's title and status of Marquis.<sup id="cite_ref-univ2_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-univ2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father assumed that Gonzaga would become a soldier, as that was the norm for sons of the aristocracy and the family was often involved in the minor wars of the period. As early as age four, Luigi was given a set of miniature guns and accompanied his father on training expeditions so that the boy might learn "the art of arms".<sup id="cite_ref-martin_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At age five, Gonzaga was sent to a military camp to get started on his training. His father was pleased to see his son marching around camp at the head of a platoon of soldiers. His mother and his tutor were less pleased with the vocabulary he picked up there.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Aloysius_Gonzaga_child.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Aloysius_Gonzaga_child.jpg/170px-Aloysius_Gonzaga_child.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="238" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Aloysius_Gonzaga_child.jpg/255px-Aloysius_Gonzaga_child.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1a/Aloysius_Gonzaga_child.jpg 2x" data-file-width="300" data-file-height="420" /></a><figcaption>Aloysius de Gonzaga as a boy</figcaption></figure>
<p>He grew up amid the violence and brutality of <a href="/wiki/Renaissance" title="Renaissance">Renaissance</a> Italy  and witnessed the murder of two of his brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-univ2_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-univ2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag may need clarification or removal of jargon. (April 2022)">clarification needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 1576, at age 8, he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> along with his younger brother, Rodolfo, to serve at the court of the Grand Duke <a href="/wiki/Francesco_I_de%27_Medici" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco I de&#39; Medici">Francesco I de' Medici</a> and to receive further education.<sup id="cite_ref-oconor_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oconor-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  While there, he fell ill with a disease of the kidneys, which troubled him throughout his life. While he was ill, he took the opportunity to read about the saints and to spend much of his time in prayer. In November 1579, the brothers were sent to the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Mantua" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Mantua">Duke of Mantua</a>. Gonzaga was shocked by the violent and frivolous lifestyle he encountered there.
</p><p>Gonzaga returned to Castiglione where he met <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Charles_Borromeo" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a>, and from him received First Communion on 22 July 1580.<sup id="cite_ref-oconor_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oconor-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After reading a book about <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> missionaries in <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>, Gonzaga felt strongly that he wanted to become a missionary. He started practicing by teaching <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> classes to young boys in Castiglione in the summers. He also repeatedly visited the houses of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friars</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Barnabite" class="mw-redirect" title="Barnabite">Barnabites</a> located in <a href="/wiki/Casale_Monferrato" title="Casale Monferrato">Casale Monferrato</a>, the capital of the Gonzaga-ruled <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Montferrat" title="Duchy of Montferrat">Duchy of Montferrat</a> where the family spent the winter. He also adopted an <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> lifestyle.
</p>

<p>The family was called to Spain in 1581 to assist <a href="/wiki/Maria_of_Austria,_Holy_Roman_Empress" title="Maria of Austria, Holy Roman Empress">Maria of Austria, Holy Roman Empress</a>. They arrived in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> in March 1582, where Gonzaga and Rodolfo became pages for the young <a href="/wiki/Infante" title="Infante">Infante</a> <a href="/wiki/Diego,_Prince_of_Asturias" title="Diego, Prince of Asturias">Diego</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oconor_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oconor-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Gonzaga started thinking in earnest about joining a <a href="/wiki/Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Order (religious)">religious order</a>. He had considered joining the Capuchins, but he had a Jesuit confessor in Madrid and decided instead to join that order. His mother agreed to his request, but his father was furious and prevented him from doing so.
</p><p>In July 1584, a year and a half after the Infante's death, the family returned to Italy. Gonzaga still wanted to become a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, but several members of his family worked hard to persuade him to change his mind. When they realized there was no way to make him give up his plan, they tried to persuade him to become a <a href="/wiki/Secular_clergy" title="Secular clergy">secular priest</a> and offered to arrange for a <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">bishopric</a> for him. If he were to become a Jesuit he would renounce any right to his inheritance or status in society.<sup id="cite_ref-coulson_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coulson-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His family's attempts to dissuade him failed; Gonzaga was not interested in higher office and still wanted to become a missionary.
</p>

<p>In November 1585, Gonzaga gave up all rights of inheritance, which was confirmed by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">emperor</a>. He went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and, because of his noble birth, gained an audience with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V" title="Pope Sixtus V">Pope Sixtus V</a>. Following a brief stay at the <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Aragona_Gonzaga" title="Palazzo Aragona Gonzaga">Palazzo Aragona Gonzaga</a>, the Roman home of his cousin, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Scipione_Gonzaga" title="Scipione Gonzaga">Scipione Gonzaga</a>, on 25 November 1585 he was accepted into the Society of Jesus in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-gonzagau_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gonzagau-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> During this period, he was asked to moderate his asceticism somewhat and to be more social with the other novices.
</p><p>Gonzaga's health continued to cause problems. In addition to the kidney disease, he also had a skin disease, chronic headaches and <a href="/wiki/Insomnia" title="Insomnia">insomnia</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He was sent to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> for studies, but after some time he was sent back to Rome because of his health. On 25 November 1587, he took the three religious <a href="/wiki/Vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Vows">vows</a> of <a href="/wiki/Chastity,_poverty_and_obedience" class="mw-redirect" title="Chastity, poverty and obedience">chastity, poverty and obedience</a>. In February and March 1588, he received <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a> and started studying <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> to prepare for <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordination</a>. In 1589, he was called to <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a> to mediate between his brother Rodolfo and the Duke of Mantua. He returned to Rome in May 1590. It is said that, later that year, he had a vision in which the <a href="/wiki/Gabriel" title="Gabriel">Archangel Gabriel</a> told him that he would die within a year.
</p><p>In 1591, a <a href="/wiki/Pandemic" title="Pandemic">plague</a> broke out in Rome. The Jesuits opened a hospital for the stricken, and Gonzaga volunteered to work there.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> After begging alms for the victims, Gonzaga began working with the sick, carrying the dying from the streets into a hospital founded by the Jesuits. There he washed and fed the plague victims, preparing them as best he could to receive the sacraments. But though he threw himself into his tasks, he privately confessed to his spiritual director, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine" title="Robert Bellarmine">Robert Bellarmine</a>, that his constitution was revolted by the sights and smells of the work; he had to work hard to overcome his physical repulsion.
</p><p>At the time, many of the younger Jesuits had become infected with the disease, and so Gonzaga's superiors forbade him from returning to the hospital. But Gonzaga—long accustomed to refusals from his father—persisted and requested permission to return, which was granted. Eventually he was allowed to care for the sick, but only at another hospital, called Our Lady of Consolation, where those with contagious diseases were not admitted. While there, Gonzaga was infected. He grew ill and was bedridden by 3 March 1591, a few days before his 23rd birthday.
</p><p>Gonzaga declined for many weeks. It seemed certain that he would die in a short time, and he was given <a href="/wiki/Anointing_of_the_Sick" class="mw-redirect" title="Anointing of the Sick">Extreme Unction</a>. He spoke several times with his confessor, the cardinal and later saint, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine" title="Robert Bellarmine">Robert Bellarmine</a>. Gonzaga told several people that he would die on the <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">Octave</a> of the feast of <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Christi_(feast)" class="mw-redirect" title="Corpus Christi (feast)">Corpus Christi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> On that day, 21 June 1591, as he began to grow weak, Bellarmine gave him the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">last rites</a>. He died just before midnight.<sup id="cite_ref-craughwell_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-craughwell-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aloysius_Gonzaga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg/170px-Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="239" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg/255px-Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg/340px-Tiepolo_Gonzaga.jpg 2x" data-file-width="498" data-file-height="700" /></a><figcaption><i>Saint Aloysius Gonzaga in Glory</i> by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo" title="Giovanni Battista Tiepolo">Giovanni Battista Tiepolo</a>, incomplete provenance</figcaption></figure>
<p>Gonzaga was buried in the Church of the Most Holy Annunciation, which later became the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Ignatius_of_Loyola,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Saint Ignatius of Loyola, Rome">Church of Saint Ignatius of Loyola</a> (Sant'Ignazio) in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-dublin2_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dublin2-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> His name was changed to "Robert" before his death, in honor of his confessor. Many people considered him to be a saint soon after his death, and his remains were moved into the Sant'Ignazio church, where they now rest in an urn of <a href="/wiki/Lapis_lazuli" title="Lapis lazuli">lapis lazuli</a> in the Lancellotti Chapel. His head was later translated to the basilica bearing his name in <a href="/wiki/Castiglione_delle_Stiviere" title="Castiglione delle Stiviere">Castiglione delle Stiviere</a>. He was beatified only fourteen years after his death by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a>, on 19 October 1605. On 31 December 1726,<sup id="cite_ref-gonzagau_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gonzagau-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> he was canonized together with another Jesuit novice, <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_Kostka" title="Stanislaus Kostka">Stanislaus Kostka</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a>.
Purity was his notable virtue.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene_de%27_Pazzi" title="Mary Magdalene de&#39; Pazzi">Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi</a>, claimed to have had a vision of him on 4 April 1600. She described him as radiant in glory because of his "interior works," a hidden martyr for his great love of God.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aloysius_Gonzaga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1729, Pope Benedict XIII declared Aloysius de Gonzaga to be the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of young students. In 1926, he was named patron of all Christian youth by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-gonzagau_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gonzagau-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Owing to the manner of his death, he has been considered a patron saint of plague victims. For his compassion and courage in the face of an incurable disease, Gonzaga has become the patron both of <a href="/wiki/AIDS" class="mw-redirect" title="AIDS">AIDS</a> patients and their caregivers.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Gonzaga is also the patron of <a href="/wiki/Valmontone" title="Valmontone">Valmontone</a>, a town in <a href="/wiki/Lazio" title="Lazio">Lazio</a>.
</p><p>Gonzaga is also celebrated in a small south Italy town called <a href="/wiki/Alezio" title="Alezio">Alezio</a>, as a patron of the town, celebrated on June 21.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aloysius_Gonzaga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In art, Gonzaga is shown as a young man wearing a black <a href="/wiki/Cassock" title="Cassock">cassock</a> and <a href="/wiki/Surplice" title="Surplice">surplice</a>, or as a <a href="/wiki/Page_(occupation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Page (occupation)">page</a>. His attributes are a <a href="/wiki/Lily" class="mw-redirect" title="Lily">lily</a>, referring to innocence; a <a href="/wiki/Cross" title="Cross">cross</a>, referring to piety and sacrifice; a skull, referring to his early death; and a <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a>, referring to his devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-martin_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martin-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> St. Joseph's Church in <a href="/wiki/Gelsenkirchen" title="Gelsenkirchen">Gelsenkirchen</a>, the location of German soccer club <a href="/wiki/FC_Schalke_04" title="FC Schalke 04">Schalke 04</a>, has a glass window of the saint with a soccer ball.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aloysius_Gonzaga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png/220px-St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="250" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png/330px-St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png/440px-St._Aloysius_Gonzaga_Church.png 2x" data-file-width="1449" data-file-height="1649" /></a><figcaption>St. Aloysius Gonzaga Church on the campus of <a href="/wiki/Gonzaga_University" title="Gonzaga University">Gonzaga University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spokane,_Washington" title="Spokane, Washington">Spokane, Washington</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Saint Aloysius' <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 21 June, the date of his death.
</p><p>Many schools and colleges are named after Aloysius Gonzaga. <a href="/wiki/Gonzaga_University" title="Gonzaga University">Gonzaga University</a> in Spokane, Washington, is an example.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
