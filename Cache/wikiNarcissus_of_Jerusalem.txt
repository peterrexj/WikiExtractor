<p><b>Saint Narcissus of Jerusalem</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;March 9, AD 99</span> – c. 216) was an early <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarchate_of_Jerusalem" title="Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem">patriarch of Jerusalem</a>. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by both the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Western</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern</a> Churches. In the Roman Catholic Church, his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on October 29, while in the Eastern Orthodox Church it is celebrated on August 7.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Narcissus_of_Jerusalem&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is inferred that the average reign of the bishops of Jerusalem was short, as evidenced by the episcopal reigns of those who followed <a href="/wiki/Simeon_of_Jerusalem" title="Simeon of Jerusalem">Saint Simeon</a>, the second bishop of Jerusalem, who was martyred in the year 117 by the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Trajan" title="Trajan">Trajan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-LS423_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LS423-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Of Greek origin, tradition holds that Narcissus was born in the year 99 and was at least 80 when he was made the 30th bishop of <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, bishop of <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima" title="Caesarea Maritima">Caesarea</a> in Palestine, presided over a council held by the bishops of Palestine in Caesarea, and it was decreed that <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> was to be always kept on a <a href="/wiki/Sunday" title="Sunday">Sunday</a>, and not with the Jewish <a href="/wiki/Passover" title="Passover">Passover</a>. According to <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a>, the bishop performed many miracles. One miracle of note, as Eusebius testified, had occurred during the <a href="/wiki/Easter_Vigil" title="Easter Vigil">Easter Vigil</a> when Narcissus changed water into oil to supply all the lamps of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-LS424_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LS424-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Narcissus was the subject of several serious allegations made by members of the Christian community, but these proved to be false. He forgave his accusers, but left Jerusalem and lived in seclusion for several years.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Three bishops governed the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">See</a> of Jerusalem in succession during his absence. Upon his return to Jerusalem, the people unanimously sought him out and asked him to resume his episcopal duties. This he did, but owing to his extreme age and the weight of his duties, he made <a href="/wiki/Alexander_of_Jerusalem" title="Alexander of Jerusalem">Saint Alexander</a> his <a href="/wiki/Coadjutor_bishop" title="Coadjutor bishop">coadjutor bishop</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-LS424_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LS424-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> St Narcissus continued to serve his flock and other churches outside his jurisdiction by his constant prayer and his exhortations to the faithful for unity and peace.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
