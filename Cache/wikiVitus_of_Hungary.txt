<p><b>Vitus of Hungary</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vitus Pannonius</i> or <i>Vitus de Hungaria</i>, <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Magyarországi Vid</i>; died 1297) was a Hungarian <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Order of Saint Augustine">Augustinian</a> friar in the 13th century, who was later <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_life">Monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitus_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Contemporary sources have preserved neither his name nor activities. 18th-century historiographical works – <i>Ungaricae sanctitatis indicia</i> and the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandists</a>' <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum Ungariae I.</a></i> – state that Vitus was a member of the Augustinian Hermits.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012204_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012204-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is possible he was born in <a href="/wiki/Oradea" title="Oradea">Várad</a> (present-day Oradea, Romania), as a 17th-century author Nicolaus Crusenius claimed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012210–211_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012210–211-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to his legend, Vitus lived in the Augustinian St. Anne monastery of <a href="/wiki/Esztergom" title="Esztergom">Esztergom</a>. He took an active part in the conversion of the pagan <a href="/wiki/Cumans" title="Cumans">Cumans</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEF._Romhányi201823–24_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEF._Romhányi201823–24-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> after <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> <a href="/wiki/Philip_III,_Bishop_of_Fermo" title="Philip III, Bishop of Fermo">Philip of Fermo</a> persuaded the Hungarian elite to adopt the so-called Cuman laws in 1279.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012212_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012212-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A non-authentic royal charter from 1278 refers to a certain frater Vitus, along with Augustinian provincial prior Elijah and abbots Nicholas of Esztergom and James of Mezősomlyó. In the document, <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_IV_of_Hungary" title="Ladislaus IV of Hungary">Ladislaus IV of Hungary</a> donated a mill to the abbey of <a href="/wiki/G%C4%83taia" title="Gătaia">Mezősomlyó</a> (present-day Gătaia, <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEF._Romhányi201823–24_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEF._Romhányi201823–24-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The document narrates that Vitus was present in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_on_the_Marchfeld" title="Battle on the Marchfeld">Battle on the Marchfeld</a> took place in that year. The fact that he precedes the two abbots in the order of the Augustinians (without any title) in the document indicates his honorable status.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012209_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012209-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitus_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ignatius Brentano Cimarolo's  <i>Epitome chronologica mundi christiany</i> (1727) put his death year to 1297.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012209_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012209-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 14th-century theologians <a href="/wiki/Jordan_of_Quedlinburg" title="Jordan of Quedlinburg">Jordan of Quedlinburg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Henry_of_Friemar" title="Henry of Friemar">Henry of Friemar</a> already listed Vitus among the most respected Augustinian friars, but without details of his activity.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012210–211_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012210–211-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 16th-century historian Joseph Panfilo claimed that Vitus was a holy man whose cult was connected with the resurrection of the dead. 17th-century scholar Johannes Naevius claimed that Vitus protected <i>Pannonia</i> (i.e. Hungary) from the epidemics.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012210–211_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012210–211-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to the 17th-century <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> scholar <a href="/wiki/Martin_Szentiv%C3%A1nyi" title="Martin Szentiványi">Márton Szentiványi</a>, his <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> was held on 3 May.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012204_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012204-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An unidentified <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Florence" title="Republic of Florence">Florentine</a> chronicler, who compiled his work in 1336, lists his three miracles, which, however, contain valueless hagiographical topoi without any identifiable geographical or historical data. One of them says that 13 years after his death, his grave was excavated, after which the miracle was performed. Based on this, Slovak historian Miroslav Huťka considered his beatification took place around 1310, with the contribution of papal legate <a href="/wiki/Gentile_Portino_da_Montefiore" title="Gentile Portino da Montefiore">Gentile Portino da Montefiore</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012213_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012213-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Beside Hungary, Vitus' cult slightly spread into <a href="/wiki/Northern_Italy" title="Northern Italy">Northern Italy</a>; still in 1466, one of the images of the St. Nicholas altar of the Chiesa di Santa Maria della Cella in <a href="/wiki/Sampierdarena" title="Sampierdarena">Sampierdarena</a> (today an area of <a href="/wiki/Genoa" title="Genoa">Genoa</a>) depicted Vitus along with several other Augustinian saints. After the end of the 15th century, however, the cult and knowledge of his person faded.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012215_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHuťka2012215-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
