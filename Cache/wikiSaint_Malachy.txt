<p><b>Malachy</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="/æ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;bad&#39;">æ</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;k&#39; in &#39;kind&#39;">k</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span></span>/</a></span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Middle_Irish_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Middle Irish language">Middle Irish</a>: <i lang="mga">Máel Máedóc Ua Morgair</i>; Modern <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Maelmhaedhoc Ó Morgair</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Malachias</i>) (<a href="/wiki/11th_century_in_Ireland" title="11th century in Ireland">1094</a>&#160;– 2 November <a href="/wiki/1148_in_Ireland" title="1148 in Ireland">1148</a>) is an Irish saint who was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh" title="Archbishop of Armagh">Archbishop of Armagh</a>, to whom were attributed several <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> and an alleged vision of 112 popes later attributed to the apocryphal (i.e. of doubtful authenticity) <a href="/wiki/Prophecy_of_the_Popes" title="Prophecy of the Popes">Prophecy of the Popes</a>.
</p><p>Malachy was the first native-born Irish saint to be formally canonised. His brother was Gilla Críst Ua Morgair, who later became Bishop <a href="/wiki/Christian_of_Clogher" title="Christian of Clogher">Christian of Clogher</a> from 1126 to 1138.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Máel Máedóc, whose surname was Ua Morgair, was born in <a href="/wiki/Armagh" title="Armagh">Armagh</a> in 1094. <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> describes him as having noble birth.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was baptised Máel Máedóc, meaning 'devotee or servant' of Máedóc (<a href="/wiki/M%C3%A1ed%C3%B3c_of_Ferns" title="Máedóc of Ferns">Máedóc of Ferns</a>) which was rendered <i>Malachus</i> in Latin (and subsequently as <i>Malachy</i> in English) and was trained under the famous <a href="/wiki/Recluse" title="Recluse">recluse</a> Imhar <a href="/wiki/O%27Hagan" title="O&#39;Hagan">O'Hagan</a>, subsequently <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">Abbot</a> of Armagh. <a href="/wiki/Mac_%C3%8Domhair#Etymology" title="Mac Íomhair">Imhar</a> was in sympathy with the aims of those who sought to reform the Irish church, and it was probably through his influence that Malachy became imbued with their principles. After a long course of studies Malachy was ordained priest by <a href="/wiki/Cellach_of_Armagh" title="Cellach of Armagh">Cellach of Armagh</a> (Celsus) in 1119.
</p><p>Shortly afterwards Cellach made the young priest his vicar. For the next year or two it was Malachy's duty to administer the diocese of Armagh. He established in all the churches the apostolic sanctions and the decrees of the holy fathers, and the customs and practices of the Roman Church. He introduced the Roman method of <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">chanting</a> the services of the canonical hours and instituted a new confession, confirmation and marriage contract, of which his parishioners were either ignorant or uninterested.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawlor_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawlor-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> With the consent of Cellach and Imar, he went to study under <a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1el_%C3%8Dsu_Ua_hAinmere&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Máel Ísu Ua hAinmere (page does not exist)">Máel Ísu Ua hAinmere</a> (Malchus, first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Waterford" title="Bishop of Waterford">Bishop of the Norse city of Waterford</a>), who had by this time retired from the archbishopric of Cashel and was settled at <a href="/wiki/Lismore,_County_Waterford" title="Lismore, County Waterford">Lismore</a>. He spent three years there.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Bangor">Abbot of Bangor</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Abbot of Bangor">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1123 the <a href="/wiki/Coarb" title="Coarb">coarb</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">Bangor Abbey</a> died. Bangor was the principal religious site in the north-east of Ireland. Since he ended his days at Lismore, it may be assumed that he was a friend of Malchus, and of the movement with which he was identified. His successor, who was Malachy's uncle, expressed his willingness to surrender his office and the site of the monastery to his nephew. Malachy became Abbot of Bangor Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawlor_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawlor-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This became an opportunity to implement one of the canons of the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_R%C3%A1th_Breasail" title="Synod of Ráth Breasail">Synod of Rathbreasail</a>, which by establishing the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Down_and_Connor" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Down and Connor">diocese of Connor</a>. Cellach, as coarb of Patrick, and consecrated bishop, had been able to organize the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Armagh" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Armagh">diocese of Armagh</a> in accordance with the Rathbreasail plan. With the prestige which belonged to the coarb of Comgall, Malachy, if consecrated bishop, could probably succeed in organizing the diocese of Connor. In 1124 Malachy journeyed to Bangor, was installed as abbot, and was made bishop by Cellach.<sup id="cite_ref-Lawlor_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lawlor-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1132, he was promoted to the <a href="/wiki/Primate_of_All_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="Primate of All Ireland">primacy</a> of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh" title="Archbishop of Armagh">Armagh</a>.
</p><p>Bernard provides many interesting anecdotes regarding Malachy and highly praises Malachy's zeal for religion both in Connor and Armagh. In 1127, Malachy paid a second visit to Lismore and acted for a time as confessor to Cormac MacCarthy, Prince of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Desmond" title="Kingdom of Desmond">Desmond</a>. While Bishop of Down and Connor, Malachy continued to reside at <a href="/wiki/Bangor,_County_Down" title="Bangor, County Down">Bangor</a>, and when some of the native princes sacked the two dioceses of Down and Connor, Malachy brought the Bangor monks to <a href="/wiki/Iveragh_Peninsula" title="Iveragh Peninsula">Iveragh</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Kerry" title="County Kerry">County Kerry</a>, where they were welcomed by now King Cormac. On the death of Celsus (who was buried at Lismore in 1129), Malachy was appointed Archbishop of Armagh, 1132, which dignity he accepted with great reluctance. Owing to intrigues, he was unable to take possession of his <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">See</a> for two years; even then he had to purchase the <a href="/wiki/Bachal_Isu" title="Bachal Isu">Bachal Isu</a> (Staff of Jesus) from Niall, the usurping <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a>-primate.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Malachy's influence in Irish ecclesiastical affairs has been compared with that of <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a> in Germany. During three years at Armagh, as <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> writes, Malachy restored the discipline of the church, grown lax during the intruded rule of a series of <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">lay</a>-abbots, and had the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church#Liturgy" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Liturgy</a> adopted.<sup id="cite_ref-grattan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-grattan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Malachy worked zealously to restore ecclesiastical discipline, restored marriage, renewed the practices of confession and confirmation, and introduced Roman chants in the liturgy. He was also known for his care to the needy as a miracle worker and healer. In his lifetime, he planted apple trees throughout Ireland during time of famine.<sup id="cite_ref-kennedytownship_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kennedytownship-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bernard continues: Having extirpated barbarism and re-established Christian morals, and seeing all things tranquil, Malachy began to think of his own peace. He therefore resigned the Sees of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh" title="Archbishop of Armagh">Armagh</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Connor" title="Bishop of Connor">Connor</a>, in 1136 or 1137, but retained as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Down" title="Bishop of Down">Bishop of Down</a>. He founded a <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> of Austin Canons at <a href="/wiki/Downpatrick" title="Downpatrick">Downpatrick</a>, and was unceasing in his episcopal labours. Early in 1139 he journeyed to Rome, via <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, England, and France, visiting <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard</a> at <a href="/wiki/Clairvaux_Abbey" title="Clairvaux Abbey">Clairvaux</a>, <a href="/wiki/Champagne_(province)" title="Champagne (province)">Champagne</a>. He petitioned <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_II" title="Pope Innocent II">Pope Innocent II</a> for <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallia</a> for the Sees of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh" title="Archbishop of Armagh">Armagh</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cashel" title="Archbishop of Cashel">Cashel</a>, and was appointed <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legate</a> for Ireland. On his return visit to Clairvaux he obtained five monks for a foundation in Ireland, under Christian, an Irishman, as superior: thus arose the great <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Mellifont" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Mellifont">Abbey of Mellifont</a> in 1142. Malachy set out on a second journey to Rome in 1148, but on arriving at Clairvaux, he fell sick and died in the arms of Bernard, on 2 November 1148.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Malachy</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1190 by Pope Clement III</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">3 November</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Archdiocese of Armagh, Diocese of Down and Connor</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>In the book <i>Life of Saint Malachy</i>, his biographer Bernard of Clairvaux says Malachy was distinguished by his meekness, humility, obedience, modesty, and true diligence in his studies. <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a> praised Malachy for attending to the needy, bringing the holy <a href="/wiki/Sacraments" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacraments">sacraments</a> to all alike and renewing the fervor of the people in receiving them.<sup id="cite_ref-burlington_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-burlington-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Malachy's <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast</a> is celebrated on 3 November, so as not to clash with <a href="/wiki/All_Souls_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="All Souls Day">All Souls Day</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Visio_Tnugdali">Visio Tnugdali</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Visio Tnugdali">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Visio Tnugdali written c.1149 refers to Malachy as follows- "When Saint Ruadan had fallen silent, Tundale looked happily about him and saw Saint Patrick of Ireland, dressed in shining robes alongside many bishops decked out in their finest regalia. They were all joyful and there was no sound of any sighing! Among that blessed company Tundale could see four bishops whom he recognised. They were all good men; one of them was Saint Cellach, a former archbishop of Armagh, who did much good for the sake of Our Lord. Another was <b>Malachias O'Moore</b>, who had become archbishop of Armagh after him and gave everything that he had to the poor. He founded a large number of churches and colleges, as many as forty-four in all, endowed them with land and rents and so allowed many men of religion to serve God devotedly, although he hardly retained enough for himself to live on."
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG/220px-RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG/330px-RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG/440px-RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1944" data-file-height="2592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:RelicsOfSaintMalachy.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bone fragment of St Malachy, <a href="/wiki/Clairvaux_Abbey" title="Clairvaux Abbey">Clairvaux Abbey</a></div></div></div>
<p>Malachy's body remained at <a href="/wiki/Clairvaux_Abbey" title="Clairvaux Abbey">Clairvaux Abbey</a> and eventually was placed in a tomb near <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a>'s, after the abbot's own death. The tomb was moved several times with the rebuilding of the church. Portions of his remains were sent to Ireland in 1194 and deposited at <a href="/wiki/Mellifont_Abbey" title="Mellifont Abbey">Mellifont Abbey</a> and other abbeys of the <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a>. At some point at Clairvaux, part of Malachy's arm and part of his skull were removed and placed in special <a href="/wiki/Reliquaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Reliquaries">reliquaries</a> in the abbey's treasury. His arm was kept in a silver case decorated with precious stones. A portion of his skull was kept in a bust reliquary of gilt silver, also decorated with precious stones and topped by a <a href="/wiki/Mitre" title="Mitre">mitre</a>. These reliquaries may have been enameled or painted. During the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, the reliquaries themselves were destroyed, although the relics were preserved. Malachy's head is now preserved in a reliquary in the treasury of <a href="/wiki/Troyes_Cathedral" title="Troyes Cathedral">Troyes Cathedral</a>, not far from the site of Clairvaux. The tombs of the two friends were destroyed in the aftermath of the revolution, and the bones were commingled and distributed to various parishes in the district of Clairvaux.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Ph. Guignard published an account of the relics in the <i>Patrologia Latina.</i><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Malachy.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/St._Malachy.jpg/220px-St._Malachy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="328" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/St._Malachy.jpg/330px-St._Malachy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/St._Malachy.jpg/440px-St._Malachy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2296" data-file-height="3428" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Malachy.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Malachy, <a href="/wiki/Sterling_Heights,_Michigan" title="Sterling Heights, Michigan">Sterling Heights, Michigan</a></div></div></div>
<p>Malachy is <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Armagh" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Armagh">Archdiocese of Armagh</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Down_and_Connor" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Down and Connor">Diocese of Down and Connor</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Carlingford_Abbey" title="Carlingford Abbey">Dominican Abbey at Carlingford</a> (est. 1305) was dedicated to him and his legacy. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Malachy%27s_Church,_Belfast" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Malachy&#39;s Church, Belfast">Saint Malachy's Church, Belfast</a> was intended to be the Cathedral Church of <a href="/wiki/Down_and_Connor" class="mw-redirect" title="Down and Connor">Down and Connor</a> dedicated in honour of the Diocesan Patron. However, the <a href="/wiki/Great_Famine_(Ireland)" title="Great Famine (Ireland)">Irish Famine</a> broke out and the grand plans for the Saint Malachy's Cathedral were shelved to divert funds to the needy.
</p><p>A number of parishes are dedicated to Malachy, including those in Tehachapi, California; <a href="/wiki/Brownsburg,_Indiana" title="Brownsburg, Indiana">Brownsburg, Indiana</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Burlington,_Massachusetts" title="Burlington, Massachusetts">Burlington, Massachusetts</a>; Kennedy Township, New York City (The Actors' Chapel);<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="Philadelphia, Pennsylvania">Philadelphia, Pennsylvania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Geneseo,_Illinois" title="Geneseo, Illinois">Geneseo, Illinois</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rantoul,_Illinois" title="Rantoul, Illinois">Rantoul, Illinois</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prophecy_of_the_Popes">Prophecy of the Popes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Prophecy of the Popes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Prophecy_of_the_Popes" title="Prophecy of the Popes">Prophecy of the Popes</a></div>
<p>A "Prophecy of the Popes" is attributed to Malachy, which is claimed to predict that there would be only 112 more <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">popes</a> before the <a href="/wiki/Last_Judgment" title="Last Judgment">Last Judgment</a>. <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Arnold_de_Wyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Arnold de Wyon">Arnold de Wyon</a> discovered and published the so-called "Doomsday <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">Prophecy</a>" in 1590. Most scholars consider the document a 16th-century elaborate hoax.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  James Weiss, a professor of church history at <a href="/wiki/Boston_College" title="Boston College">Boston College</a>, has stated: "It is widely thought ... given who the author was and his relationship, [that the prophecies] were published to establish the case for election of one particular <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-huff_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huff-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Groome" title="Thomas Groome">Thomas Groome</a>, chair of the Department of Religious Education and Pastoral Ministry at Boston College, has a similar notion: "For myself – and even as a native Irishman – the 'Prophecies of St. Malachy' are a grand old fun tale that have about as much reliability as the morning horoscope".<sup id="cite_ref-huff_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huff-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Thomas J. Reese of Georgetown University, had only this to say: "St. Malachy's prophecy is nonsense."<sup id="cite_ref-huff_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huff-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Malachy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Malachy, his "Doomsday Prophecy", and the conflicts between the Christians and pagans are important plot points in <a href="/wiki/James_Rollins" title="James Rollins">James Rollins</a>' sixth Sigma Force novel, <i><a href="/wiki/James_Rollins#Book_6:_The_Doomsday_Key_.282009.29" title="James Rollins">The Doomsday Key</a></i> (2009), particularly in Chapter 21.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
