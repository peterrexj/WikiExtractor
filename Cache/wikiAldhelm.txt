<p><b>Aldhelm</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Ealdhelm</i></span>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Aldhelmus Malmesberiensis</i>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;639</span>&#160;&#8211;&#32;25 May 709), <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Malmesbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Malmesbury">Abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury_Abbey" title="Malmesbury Abbey">Malmesbury Abbey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sherborne_(ancient)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sherborne (ancient)">Bishop of Sherborne</a>, and a writer and scholar of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> poetry, was born before the middle of the 7th century. He is said to have been the son of Kenten, who was of the royal house of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh21_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh21-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was certainly not, as his early biographer <a href="/wiki/Faritius" title="Faritius">Faritius</a> asserts, the brother of King <a href="/wiki/Ine_of_Wessex" title="Ine of Wessex">Ine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After his death he was venerated as a saint, his feast day being the day of his death, 25 May.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Aldhelm received his first education in the school of the Irish scholar and monk <a href="/wiki/Maildubh" class="mw-redirect" title="Maildubh">Máeldub</a> (also <i>Maildubh</i>, <i>Maildulf</i> or <i>Meldun</i>) (died <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;675</span>),<sup id="cite_ref-museum_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-museum-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> who had settled in the British stronghold of Bladon (or <i>Bladow</i>) on the site of the town called Mailduberi, Maldubesburg, Meldunesburg, etc., and finally <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury,_Wiltshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Malmesbury, Wiltshire">Malmesbury</a>, after him.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 668, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Vitalian" title="Pope Vitalian">Pope Vitalian</a> sent <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore of Tarsus</a> to be <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. At the same time the North African scholar <a href="/wiki/Adrian_of_Canterbury" title="Adrian of Canterbury">Hadrian</a> became abbot of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's</a> at <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a>. Aldhelm was one of his disciples,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh21_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh21-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> for he addresses him as the 'venerable preceptor of my rude childhood.' He must, nevertheless, have been thirty years of age when he began to study with Hadrian. His studies included <a href="/wiki/Roman_law" title="Roman law">Roman law</a>, astronomy, astrology, the art of reckoning and the difficulties of the calendar. He learned, according to the doubtful statements of the early lives, both Greek and <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>. He certainly introduces many Latinized Greek words into his works.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ill health compelled Aldhelm to leave Canterbury and he returned to Malmesbury Abbey, where he was a monk under Máeldub for fourteen years, dating probably from 661 and including the period of his studies with Hadrian.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Malmesbury">Abbot of Malmesbury</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Abbot of Malmesbury">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When Máeldub died, Aldhelm was appointed in 675,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh21_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh21-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> according to a charter of doubtful authenticity cited by <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Leuthere" title="Leuthere">Leuthere</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a> (671–676), to succeed to the direction of the monastery, of which he became the first <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-museum_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-museum-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aldhelm introduced the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St Benedict">Benedictine rule</a> and secured the right of the election of the abbot by the monks themselves. The community at Malmesbury increased, and Aldhelm was able to found two other monasteries as centres of learning, at <a href="/wiki/Frome" title="Frome">Frome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a> and at <a href="/wiki/Bradford-on-Avon" title="Bradford-on-Avon">Bradford-on-Avon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>. Following a pilgrimage to Rome, he was given permission by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Pope Sergius I</a> in a Papal Bull of 701 to establish the monastery at Frome, where he had already built a church circa 685.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Anglo-Saxon building of <a href="/wiki/St_Laurence%27s_Church,_Bradford-on-Avon" title="St Laurence&#39;s Church, Bradford-on-Avon">St Laurence's Church, Bradford-on-Avon</a> dates back to his time, and may safely be regarded as his. At Malmesbury he built a new church to replace Máeldub's modest building,<sup id="cite_ref-museum_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-museum-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and obtained considerable grants of land for the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Aldhelm held this post until circa 705 when he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg/220px-Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="298" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg/330px-Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg/440px-Aldhelm.plaque.arp.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1008" data-file-height="1365" /></a><figcaption>Wall plaque at the Catholic Church of St Aldhelm, <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury" title="Malmesbury">Malmesbury</a>. <h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Easter_controversy">Easter controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Easter controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Aldhelm was deputed by a synod of the church in Wessex to remonstrate with the <a href="/wiki/Britons_(historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Britons (historical)">Britons</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a> (<a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>) on the <a href="/wiki/Easter_controversy" title="Easter controversy">Easter controversy</a>. British Christians followed a unique system of calculation for the date of Easter and also bore a distinctive tonsure; these customs are generally associated with the practice known as <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Celtic Christianity</a>. Aldhelm wrote a long and rather acrimonious letter to king <a href="/wiki/Geraint_of_Dumnonia" title="Geraint of Dumnonia">Geraint of Dumnonia</a> (Geruntius) achieving ultimate agreement with Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Sherborne">Bishop of Sherborne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Bishop of Sherborne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 705, or perhaps earlier, <a href="/wiki/H%C3%A6dde" title="Hædde">Hædde</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>, died, and the diocese was divided into two parts.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sherborne" title="Sherborne">Sherborne</a> was the new see, of which Aldhelm became the first bishop around 705.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook222_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook222-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He wished to resign the abbey of Malmesbury which he had governed for thirty years, but yielding to the remonstrances of the monks he continued to direct it until his death. He was now an old man, but he showed great activity in his new functions. The <a href="/wiki/Sherborne_Abbey#Saxon" title="Sherborne Abbey">cathedral church which he built at Sherborne</a>, though replaced later by a Norman church, is described by William of Malmesbury.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In his capacity as bishop, he displayed a great deal of energy. This included going into public places where he would sing hymns and passages from the gospels interspersed with bits of clowning to draw attention to his message.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Organ">Organ</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Organ">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Rogers has Aldhelm claiming to have built an innovative <a href="/wiki/Organ_(music)" title="Organ (music)">organ</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-memorials-p49_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-memorials-p49-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <i>"a mighty instrument, with innumerable tones, blown with belows, and enclosed in a gilded case."</i>  (It is not clear from the source cited whether the device was innovative for the premises, the locale, or a fundamental advance on existing known technologies.)
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg/220px-Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg/330px-Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5d/Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg/440px-Statue_of_St_Aldhelm_in_Sherborne_Abbey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2448" data-file-height="3264" /></a><figcaption>Conceptual statue of St Aldhelm in <a href="/wiki/Sherborne_Abbey" title="Sherborne Abbey">Sherborne Abbey</a> by Marzia Colonna</figcaption></figure>
<p>Aldhelm was on his rounds in his diocese when he died at the church in <a href="/wiki/Doulting" title="Doulting">Doulting</a> village in 709, the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Aldhelm,_Doulting" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St Aldhelm, Doulting">Church of St Aldhelm</a> and <a href="/wiki/St_Aldhelm%27s_Well" title="St Aldhelm&#39;s Well">St Aldhelm's Well</a> in the village are dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook222_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook222-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The body was taken to Malmesbury, and crosses were set up by his friend, <a href="/wiki/Egwin" class="mw-redirect" title="Egwin">Egwin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a>, at the various stopping-places.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in the church of St Michael at <a href="/wiki/Malmesbury_Abbey" title="Malmesbury Abbey">Malmesbury Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair512_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair512-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> His biographers relate miracles due to his sanctity worked during his lifetime and at his shrine.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The cape in <a href="/wiki/Dorset" title="Dorset">Dorset</a> commonly known as <a href="/wiki/St_Alban%27s_Head" title="St Alban&#39;s Head">St Alban's Head</a> is more properly called St. Aldhelm's Head in his honour.
</p><p>Aldhelm was revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> after his death, with his feast day being celebrated on 25 May.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh21_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh21-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were translated in 980 by <a href="/wiki/Dunstan" title="Dunstan">Dunstan</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair512_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair512-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He is commemorated by a statue in <a href="/wiki/Table_of_the_Statuary_of_the_West_Front_of_Salisbury_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Table of the Statuary of the West Front of Salisbury Cathedral">niche 124</a> of the West Front of <a href="/wiki/Salisbury_Cathedral" title="Salisbury Cathedral">Salisbury Cathedral</a>. There is also a statue in <a href="/wiki/Sherborne_Abbey" title="Sherborne Abbey">Sherborne Abbey</a> of Aldhelm, created in 2004 by Marzia Colonna.<sup id="cite_ref-BathAC_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BathAC-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg/220px-Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg/330px-Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg/440px-Flag_of_Saint_Aldhelm.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="307" /></a><figcaption>Flag of Saint Aldhelm</figcaption></figure>
<p>Aldhelm's flag may be flown in his celebration. The flag, a white cross on a red background, is a colour reversed version of <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>'s St. George flag.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aldhelm is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on 25 May.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Aldhelm's collected works were edited by Rudolf Ehwald, <i>Aldhelmi opera</i> (Berlin, 1919). An earlier edition by <a href="/wiki/J._A._Giles" class="mw-redirect" title="J. A. Giles">J. A. Giles</a>, <i>Patres eccl. Angl.</i> (Oxford, 1844) was reprinted by <a href="/wiki/Jacques_Paul_Migne" title="Jacques Paul Migne">J. P. Migne</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/Patrologia_Latina" title="Patrologia Latina">Patrologiae Cursus</a>,</i> vol. 89 (1850).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Contemporary_reputation">Contemporary reputation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Contemporary reputation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Aldhelm's fame as a scholar spread to other countries. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Artwil&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Artwil (page does not exist)">Artwil</a>, the son of an Irish king, submitted his writings for Aldhelm's approval, and <a href="/wiki/Cellanus" title="Cellanus">Cellanus</a>, an Irish monk from <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9ronne,_Somme" title="Péronne, Somme">Peronne</a>, was one of his correspondents. Aldhelm was the first <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a>, so far as is known, to write in <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> verse, and his letter to Acircius (<a href="/wiki/Aldfrith" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldfrith">Aldfrith</a> or Eadfrith, king of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a>) is a treatise on Latin <a href="/wiki/Prosody_(poetry)" class="mw-redirect" title="Prosody (poetry)">prosody</a> for the use of his countrymen. In this work he included his most famous productions, <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_riddles#Aldhelm" title="Anglo-Saxon riddles">one hundred and one riddles</a> in Latin hexameters. Each of them is a complete picture, and one of them, <i><a href="/wiki/De_creatura" title="De creatura">De creatura</a></i>, runs to 83 lines.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That Aldhelm's merits as a scholar were early recognised in his own country is shown by the encomium of <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> (<i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum</a></i> 5.18), who speaks of him as a wonder of erudition. His fame reached Italy, and at the request of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Pope Sergius I</a> he paid a visit to Rome, of which, however, there is no notice in his extant writings. On his return, bringing with him privileges for his monastery and a magnificent altar, he received a popular ovation.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aldhelm wrote in elaborate, grandiloquent and very difficult <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, known as <a href="/wiki/Hermeneutic_style" title="Hermeneutic style">hermeneutic style</a>. This <i>verborum garrulitas</i> shows the influence of Irish models and became England's dominant Latin style for centuries,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> though eventually it came to be regarded as barbarous.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911535-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> His works became standard school texts in monastic schools, until his influence declined around the time of the Norman Conquest.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern_reputation">Modern reputation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Modern reputation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Modern historians have contrasting views of his writings. <a href="/wiki/Peter_Hunter_Blair" title="Peter Hunter Blair">Peter Hunter Blair</a> compares him unfavourably to Bede: "In the mind of his older contemporary, Aldhelm, learning of equal depth produced little more than an extravagant form of intellectual curiosity...Like Bede he drank deeply from the streams of Irish and Mediterranean scholarship, but their waters produced in him a state of intellectual intoxication which delighted its beholders, but which left little to posterity."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHunter_Blair2003326_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHunter_Blair2003326-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> However, <a href="/wiki/Michael_Lapidge" title="Michael Lapidge">Michael Lapidge</a> praises his immense learning, observing that his knowledge of Latin texts is greater than any other pre-Conquest Anglo-Saxon writer, and that "the originality and importance of his corpus of Latin writings well justifies his status as the first English man of letters".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELapidge2004_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELapidge2004-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Prose">Prose</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Prose">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><i><b>De Laude Virginitatis</b></i> (the prose <i>De Virginitate</i>), a Latin treatise on virginity addressed to the nuns of the double monastery at <a href="/wiki/Barking_Abbey" title="Barking Abbey">Barking</a>, is Aldhelm's best-known work. After a long preface extolling the merits of virginity, he commemorates a great number of male and female saints. Aldhelm later wrote a shorter, poetic version (see below).</li>
<li><i><b><a href="/wiki/Epistola_ad_Acircium" title="Epistola ad Acircium">Epistola ad Acircium</a></b></i>, a Latin treatise dedicated to one Acircius, understood to be King <a href="/wiki/Aldfrith_of_Northumbria" title="Aldfrith of Northumbria">Aldfrith of Northumbria</a> (r. 685-704/5). The chief source of his <i>Epistola ad Acircium</i> (ed. A. Mai, <i>Class. Auct.</i> vol. V) is <a href="/wiki/Priscian" title="Priscian">Priscian</a>. The acrostic introduction gives the sentence, 'Aldhelmus cecinit millenis versibus odas,' whether read from the initial or final letters of the lines.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After an address to King Aldfrith, the letter consists of three treatises:
<ul><li><i>De septenario</i>, treatise on the number seven in <a href="/wiki/Arithmology" class="mw-redirect" title="Arithmology">arithmology</a></li>
<li><i>De metris</i>, treatise on metre, including the <i>Enigmata</i> (see below).</li>
<li><i>De pedum regulis</i>, didactive treatise on <a href="/wiki/Metrical_feet" class="mw-redirect" title="Metrical feet">metrical feet</a>, such as iambs and spondees.</li></ul></li>
<li>Other <b>Letters</b>. Correspondents include Bishop <a href="/wiki/Leuthere" title="Leuthere">Leuthere</a>, <a href="/wiki/Adrian_of_Canterbury" title="Adrian of Canterbury">Hadrian</a>, King <a href="/wiki/Geraint_of_Dumnonia" title="Geraint of Dumnonia">Geraint of Dumnonia</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eahfrid&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Eahfrid (page does not exist)">Eahfrid</a>, Cellanus, Sergius and Aldhelm's pupils Wihtfrith and Æthelwald who was responsible for part of the <i>Carmen rhythmicum</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
<ul><li>The letter to King <a href="/wiki/Geraint_of_Dumnonia" title="Geraint of Dumnonia">Geraint of Dumnonia</a> was supposed to have been destroyed by the Britons (<a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/Gesta_pontificum_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Gesta pontificum Anglorum">Gesta pontificum Anglorum</a></i> p.&#160;361), but was discovered with others of Aldhelm's in the correspondence of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">St Boniface</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">archbishop of Mainz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>A long letter to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Eahfrid&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Eahfrid (page does not exist)">Eahfrid</a>, a scholar just returned from Ireland (first printed in Usher, <i>Veterum Epistt. Hiber. Sylloge,</i> 1632), is of interest as casting light on the relations between English and Irish scholars.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li></ul></li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Poetry">Poetry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Poetry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li><i><b>Carmen de virginitate</b></i> (the poetic <i>De Virginitate</i>). Aldhelm wrote a shorter, poetic version of <i>De Laude Virginitatis</i>, which closes with a battle of the virtues against the vices, the <i>De octo principalibus vitiis</i> (first printed by Delrio, Mainz, 1601). The two works are what is sometimes called an <i>opus geminatum</i> or "twin work".</li>
<li><i><b>Carmen rhythmicum</b></i>, rhythmic poem which describes a travel through western England and the way a wooden church was affected by a storm.</li>
<li><i><b>Carmina ecclesiastica</b></i> (modern title), i.e. a number of Latin <i>tituli</i> designed for inscription on a church or altar. They are: (1) <i>In Basilica Sanctorum Petri et Pauli</i>, for a church dedicated to St Peter and St Paul, possibly the church which Aldhelm founded at Malmesbury, (2) <i>In Basilica Beatae Mariae Semper Virginis</i>, St Mary's Church, possibly also at Malmesbury, (3) <i>In Ecclesia Mariae a Bugge Extructa</i>, for the church built by Bugga, that is <a href="/wiki/Edburga_of_Minster-in-Thanet" title="Edburga of Minster-in-Thanet">Eadburh of Minster-in-Thanet</a>, a royal lady of the house of Wessex, (4) the twelve <i>tituli</i> known collectively as <i>In Duodecim Apostolorum Aris</i> and (5) <i>In sancti Matthiae Apostoli Ecclesia</i>.</li>
<li><i><b>Aenigmata</b></i>, one hundred riddles included in the <i><a href="/wiki/Epistola_ad_Acircium" title="Epistola ad Acircium">Epistola ad Acircium</a></i>.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lost_works">Lost works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aldhelm&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Lost works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>, Aldhelm also wrote poetry in <a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a> and set his own compositions to music, but none of his songs, which were still popular in the time of <a href="/wiki/Alfred_the_Great" title="Alfred the Great">Alfred</a>, have survived. Finding his people slow to come to church, he is said to have stood at the end of a bridge singing songs in the vernacular, thus collecting a crowd to listen to exhortations on sacred subjects.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
