<p><b>Frederick North, 2nd Earl of Guilford</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a></span> (13 April 1732&#160;&#8211;&#32;5 August 1792), better known by his <a href="/wiki/Courtesy_titles_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="Courtesy titles in the United Kingdom">courtesy title</a> <b>Lord North</b>, which he used from 1752 to 1790, was <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime Minister of Great Britain">Prime Minister of Great Britain</a> from 1770 to 1782. He led <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain" title="Kingdom of Great Britain">Great Britain</a> through most of the <a href="/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War" title="American Revolutionary War">American War of Independence</a>. He also held a number of other cabinet posts, including <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a>.
</p><p>North's reputation among historians has varied wildly, reaching its lowest point in the late 19th century, when he was depicted as a creature of the king and an incompetent who lost the American colonies. In the early 20th century, a revised view emerged which emphasised his strengths in administering the Treasury, handling the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Great_Britain" title="House of Commons of Great Britain">House of Commons</a>, and in defending the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>. Historian <a href="/wiki/Herbert_Butterfield" title="Herbert Butterfield">Herbert Butterfield</a>, however, argued that his indolence was a barrier to efficient crisis management; he neglected his role in supervising the entire war effort.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Birth_and_family">Birth and family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Birth and family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>North was born in London on 13 April 1732 at the family house at <a href="/wiki/Albemarle_Street" title="Albemarle Street">Albemarle Street</a>, just off <a href="/wiki/Piccadilly" title="Piccadilly">Piccadilly</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He spent much of his youth at <a href="/wiki/Wroxton_Abbey" title="Wroxton Abbey">Wroxton Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a>. North's strong resemblance to King <a href="/wiki/George_III" title="George III">George III</a> suggested to contemporaries that George III's father, <a href="/wiki/Frederick,_Prince_of_Wales" title="Frederick, Prince of Wales">Frederick, Prince of Wales</a>, might have been North's real father, making North the king's half-brother, a theory compatible with the prince's reputation but supported by little else other than the circumstantial evidence.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>King <a href="/wiki/George_IV" title="George IV">George IV</a> remarked that "either his royal grandfather or North's mother must have played her husband false",<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> North's father, <a href="/wiki/Francis_North,_1st_Earl_of_Guilford" title="Francis North, 1st Earl of Guilford">Francis North, 1st Earl of Guilford</a>, was from 1730 to 1751 <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_the_Bedchamber" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of the Bedchamber">Lord of the Bedchamber</a> to Frederick, Prince of Wales, who stood as <a href="/wiki/Godparent" title="Godparent">godfather</a> to the infant, christened Frederick, possibly in honour of his real father.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>North was descended from <a href="/wiki/Henry_Montagu,_1st_Earl_of_Manchester" title="Henry Montagu, 1st Earl of Manchester">Henry Montagu, 1st Earl of Manchester</a>, paternal uncle of <a href="/wiki/Edward_Montagu,_1st_Earl_of_Sandwich" title="Edward Montagu, 1st Earl of Sandwich">Edward Montagu, 1st Earl of Sandwich</a> and was related to <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Pepys" title="Samuel Pepys">Samuel Pepys</a> and the <a href="/wiki/John_Stuart,_3rd_Earl_of_Bute" title="John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute">3rd Earl of Bute</a>. He at times had a slightly turbulent relationship with his father Francis North, yet they were very close. In his early years, the family was not wealthy, though their situation improved in 1735 when his father inherited property from his cousin.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Frederick's mother, Lady Lucy Montagu, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/George_Montagu,_1st_Earl_of_Halifax" title="George Montagu, 1st Earl of Halifax">George Montagu, 1st Earl of Halifax</a> and his first wife, Ricarda Posthuma Staltonstall, died in 1734. His father remarried, but his stepmother, Elizabeth Kaye, widow of George Legge, Viscount Lewisham, eldest son of <a href="/wiki/William_Legge,_1st_Earl_of_Dartmouth" title="William Legge, 1st Earl of Dartmouth">William Legge, 1st Earl of Dartmouth</a> and his wife, Lady Anne Finch, third daughter of <a href="/wiki/Heneage_Finch,_1st_Earl_of_Aylesford" title="Heneage Finch, 1st Earl of Aylesford">Heneage Finch, 1st Earl of Aylesford</a>, died in 1745, when Frederick was thirteen. One of his stepbrothers was <a href="/wiki/William_Legge,_2nd_Earl_of_Dartmouth" title="William Legge, 2nd Earl of Dartmouth">William Legge, 2nd Earl of Dartmouth</a>, who remained a close friend for life.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was educated at <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a> between 1742 and 1748 and at <a href="/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford" title="Trinity College, Oxford">Trinity College, Oxford</a>, where in 1750 he was awarded an <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Arts_(Oxbridge)" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of Arts (Oxbridge)">MA</a>. After leaving <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">Oxford</a>, he travelled in Europe on a <a href="/wiki/Grand_Tour" title="Grand Tour">Grand Tour</a> with Lord Dartmouth. They stayed in <a href="/wiki/Leipzig" title="Leipzig">Leipzig</a> for nearly nine months, studying under the constitutional scholar <a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Jacob_Mascov" class="extiw" title="de:Johann Jacob Mascov">Johann Jacob Mascov</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> They continued through Austria and Italy, staying in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> from December 1752 to Easter 1753, then through Switzerland to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, returning to England in early 1754.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_political_career">Early political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Early political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Member_of_Parliament">Member of Parliament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Member of Parliament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 15 April 1754, North, then 22, was elected unopposed as the <a href="/wiki/Member_of_parliament" title="Member of parliament">member of parliament</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Banbury_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Banbury (UK Parliament constituency)">constituency of Banbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He served as an MP from 1754 to 1790 and joined the government as a junior <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_the_Treasury" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord of the Treasury">Lord of the Treasury</a> on 2 June 1759 during the <a href="/wiki/Pitt%E2%80%93Newcastle_ministry" title="Pitt–Newcastle ministry">Pitt–Newcastle ministry</a> (an alliance between the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pelham-Holles,_1st_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle">Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle</a> and <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Elder" class="mw-redirect" title="William Pitt the Elder">William Pitt the Elder</a>). He soon developed a reputation as a good administrator and parliamentarian and was generally liked by his colleagues. Although he initially considered himself a <a href="/wiki/Whig_(British_political_faction)" class="mw-redirect" title="Whig (British political faction)">Whig</a>, he did not closely align with any of the Whig factions in Parliament, and it became obvious to many contemporaries that his sympathies were largely <a href="/wiki/Tory" title="Tory">Tory</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was appointed <a href="/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel_(United_Kingdom)" title="Lieutenant colonel (United Kingdom)">Lieutenant-Colonel</a> of the <a href="/wiki/1st_Somerset_Militia" title="1st Somerset Militia">1st Somerset Militia</a> on 23 June 1759 when it was embodied for fulltime service, and commanded it in the West Country for <a href="/wiki/John_Poulett,_2nd_Earl_Poulett" title="John Poulett, 2nd Earl Poulett">Earl Poulett</a>, the colonel, who was also <a href="/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_Somerset" title="Lord Lieutenant of Somerset">Lord Lieutenant of Somerset</a>. However, North resigned in November 1761 and concentrated on his political career.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In November 1763, he was chosen to speak for the government concerning radical MP <a href="/wiki/John_Wilkes" title="John Wilkes">John Wilkes</a>. Wilkes had made a savage attack on both the Prime Minister and the king in his newspaper <i><a href="/wiki/The_North_Briton" title="The North Briton">The North Briton</a></i>, which many thought libellous. North's motion that Wilkes be expelled from the House of Commons passed by 273 votes to 111. Wilkes' expulsion took place in his absence, as he had already fled to France following a duel.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_government">In government</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: In government">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When a government headed by the Whig magnate <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Charles Watson-Wentworth, Lord Rockingham</a> came to power in 1765, North left his post and served for a time as a <a href="/wiki/Backbencher" title="Backbencher">backbench MP</a>. He turned down an offer by Rockingham to rejoin the government, not wanting to be associated with the Whig <a href="/wiki/Grandee" title="Grandee">grandees</a> that dominated the Ministry.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He returned to office when Pitt returned to head a second government in 1766. North was appointed Joint <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a> in Pitt's ministry and became a <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of Great Britain">Privy Counsellor</a>. As Pitt was constantly ill, the government was effectively run by <a href="/wiki/Augustus_FitzRoy,_3rd_Duke_of_Grafton" title="Augustus FitzRoy, 3rd Duke of Grafton">Augustus FitzRoy, 3rd Duke of Grafton</a>, with North as one of its most senior members.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In December 1767, he succeeded <a href="/wiki/Charles_Townshend" title="Charles Townshend">Charles Townshend</a> as <a href="/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer" title="Chancellor of the Exchequer">Chancellor of the Exchequer</a>. With the resignation of the secretary of state <a href="/wiki/Henry_Seymour_Conway" title="Henry Seymour Conway">Henry Seymour Conway</a> in early 1768, North became <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Commons" title="Leader of the House of Commons">Leader of the Commons</a> as well. He continued to serve when Pitt was succeeded by Grafton in October.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointment">Appointment</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Appointment">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When the Duke of Grafton resigned as Prime Minister, North formed a government on 28 January 1770. His ministers and supporters tended to be known as Tories, though they were not a formal grouping and many had previously been Whigs. He took over with Great Britain in a triumphant state following the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Years%27_War" title="Seven Years&#39; War">Seven Years' War</a>, which had seen the <a href="/wiki/First_British_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="First British Empire">First British Empire</a> expand to a peak by taking in vast new territories on several continents. Circumstances forced him to keep many members of the previous cabinet in their jobs, despite their lack of agreement with him.<sup id="cite_ref-Rodger_p._329_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rodger_p._329-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In contrast to many of his predecessors, North enjoyed a good relationship with George III, partly based on their shared <a href="/wiki/Patriotism" title="Patriotism">patriotism</a> and desire for decency in their <a href="/wiki/Private_sphere" title="Private sphere">private lives</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Falklands_Crisis">Falklands Crisis</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Falklands Crisis">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Falklands_Crisis_of_1770" title="Falklands Crisis of 1770">Falklands Crisis of 1770</a></div>
<p>North's ministry had an early success during the <a href="/wiki/Falklands_Crisis_of_1770" title="Falklands Crisis of 1770">Falklands Crisis</a> in 1770, in which Great Britain faced down a Spanish attempt to seize the <a href="/wiki/Falkland_Islands" title="Falkland Islands">Falkland Islands</a>, nearly provoking a war.<sup id="cite_ref-Rodger_p._329_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rodger_p._329-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Both France and Spain had been left unhappy by Great Britain's perceived dominance following the <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years%27_War" title="Great Britain in the Seven Years&#39; War">British victory in the Seven Years War</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2010)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Spanish forces seized the British settlement on the Falklands and expelled the small British garrison. When Britain opposed the seizure, Spain sought backing from her ally France. King <a href="/wiki/Louis_XV" title="Louis XV">Louis XV</a> of France did not believe his country was ready for war, however, and in the face of a strong mobilisation of the British fleet, the French compelled the Spanish to back down. Louis also dismissed the <a href="/wiki/%C3%89tienne_Fran%C3%A7ois,_duc_de_Choiseul" class="mw-redirect" title="Étienne François, duc de Choiseul">Duc de Choiseul</a>, the hawkish Chief Minister of France, who had advocated war and a large invasion of Great Britain by the French.
</p><p>The British government's prestige and popularity were enormously boosted by the incident. It had successfully managed to drive a wedge between France and Spain and demonstrated the power of the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Navy" title="Royal Navy">Royal Navy</a>, although it was suggested by critics that this gave Lord North a degree of complacency and an incorrect belief that the European powers would not interfere in British colonial affairs. This was contrasted with the previous administration's failure to prevent France <a href="/wiki/French_Conquest_of_Corsica" class="mw-redirect" title="French Conquest of Corsica">from annexing</a> the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Corsica" class="mw-redirect" title="Republic of Corsica">Republic of Corsica</a>, a British ally, during the <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Crisis" title="Corsican Crisis">Corsican Crisis</a> two years earlier. Using his newly found popularity, North took a chance and appointed <a href="/wiki/John_Montagu,_4th_Earl_of_Sandwich" title="John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich">Lord Sandwich</a> to the cabinet as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Admiralty" title="First Lord of the Admiralty">First Lord of the Admiralty</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="American_War_of_Independence">American War of Independence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: American War of Independence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg/220px-Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="160" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg/330px-Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg/440px-Milord_North_et_la_femme_du_gouverneur_de_la_Grenade_1779.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1050" data-file-height="764" /></a><figcaption>French caricature on the government of Frederick North after the defeat of Grenada (1779).</figcaption></figure>
<p>Most of North's government was focused first on the growing problems with the American colonies. Later on, it was preoccupied with conducting the <a href="/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War" title="American Revolutionary War">American War of Independence</a> that broke out in 1775 with the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Lexington" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Lexington">Battle of Lexington</a>. Following the <a href="/wiki/Boston_Tea_Party" title="Boston Tea Party">Boston Tea Party</a> in 1773, Lord North proposed a number of legislative measures that were supposed to punish the Bostonians. These measures were known as the <a href="/wiki/Coercive_Acts" class="mw-redirect" title="Coercive Acts">Coercive Acts</a> in Great Britain, while dubbed the <a href="/wiki/Intolerable_Acts" title="Intolerable Acts">Intolerable Acts</a> in the colonies. By shutting down the Boston government and cutting off trade, he hoped they would keep the peace and dispirit the rebellious colonists. Instead, the acts further inflamed Massachusetts and the other colonies, eventually resulting in open war during the <a href="/wiki/Boston_campaign" title="Boston campaign">Boston campaign</a> of 1775–76.
</p><p>North deferred the overall strategy of the war to his key subordinates <a href="/wiki/George_Germain,_1st_Viscount_Sackville" title="George Germain, 1st Viscount Sackville">Lord George Germain</a> and the Earl of Sandwich. Despite a series of victories and the <a href="/wiki/New_York_and_New_Jersey_campaign" title="New York and New Jersey campaign">capture of New York</a> and <a href="/wiki/Capture_of_Philadelphia" class="mw-redirect" title="Capture of Philadelphia">Philadelphia</a>, the British were unable to secure a decisive victory. In 1778, the French allied themselves with the American rebels, and Spain joined the war in 1779 as an ally of France, followed by the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_Republic" title="Dutch Republic">Dutch Republic</a> in 1780. The British found themselves fighting a global war on four continents without a single ally. After 1778, the British switched the focus of their efforts to the defence of the <a href="/wiki/West_Indies" title="West Indies">West Indies</a>, as their sugar wealth made them much more valuable to Great Britain than the <a href="/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies" title="Thirteen Colonies">Thirteen Colonies</a>. In 1779, Great Britain was faced with the prospect of a major Franco-Spanish invasion, but the <a href="/wiki/Armada_of_1779" title="Armada of 1779">Armada of 1779</a> was ultimately a failure. Several peace initiatives fell through, and an attempt by <a href="/wiki/Richard_Cumberland_(dramatist)" title="Richard Cumberland (dramatist)">Richard Cumberland</a> to negotiate a separate peace with Spain ended in frustration.
</p><p>The country's problems were augmented by the <a href="/wiki/First_League_of_Armed_Neutrality" title="First League of Armed Neutrality">First League of Armed Neutrality</a>, which was formed to counter the British blockade strategy, and threatened British naval supplies from the <a href="/wiki/Baltic_Sea" title="Baltic Sea">Baltic</a>. With severe manpower shortages, North's government passed an act abandoning previous statutes placing restrictions on Catholics serving in the military. This provoked an upsurge of anti-Catholic feelings and the formation of the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Association" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Association">Protestant Association</a> that led to the <a href="/wiki/Gordon_Riots" title="Gordon Riots">Gordon Riots</a> in London in June 1780.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> For around a week, the city was in the control of the mob until the military was called out and <a href="/wiki/Martial_law" title="Martial law">martial law</a> imposed.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Public opinion, especially in middle-class and elite circles, repudiated anti-Catholicism and violence, and rallied behind the North government. Demands were made for a London police force.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Britain's fortunes in the war in America had temporarily improved following the failure of a <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Rhode_Island" title="Battle of Rhode Island">Franco-American attack on Newport</a> and the prosecution of a Southern strategy that saw <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Charleston" title="Siege of Charleston">the capture of Charleston, South Carolina</a> and its garrison. During 1780 and 1781, the North government gained strength in the House of Commons.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In October 1781, British forces under <a href="/wiki/Charles_Cornwallis,_1st_Marquess_Cornwallis" title="Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis">Lord Cornwallis</a> surrendered at the conclusion of the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown" title="Siege of Yorktown">Siege of Yorktown</a>, dealing a crushing blow to British morale. When the news reached North, he took it "as he would have taken a ball in his breast", and exclaimed repeatedly "Oh God! It is all over!"<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Resignation">Resignation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Resignation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg/220px-Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="110" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg/330px-Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg/440px-Motion_of_no_confidence_against_Lord_North_1782_Silver_Medal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1764" data-file-height="885" /></a><figcaption>This modern American silver medallion commemorates the motion of no confidence against North on 27 February 1782 to end the American War of Independence. North resigned a month later.</figcaption></figure>
<p>North was the second British Prime Minister to be forced out of office by a <a href="/wiki/Motion_of_no_confidence" title="Motion of no confidence">motion of no confidence</a>; the first was Sir <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a> in 1742. Lord North resigned on 20 March 1782 on account of the British defeat at <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Yorktown" title="Siege of Yorktown">Yorktown</a> the year before. In an attempt to end the war, he proposed the Conciliation Plan, in which he promised that Great Britain would eliminate all disagreeable acts if the colonies ended the war. The colonies rejected the plan, as their goal had become full independence.
</p><p>In April 1782, it was suggested in cabinet by <a href="/wiki/William_Petty,_2nd_Earl_of_Shelburne" title="William Petty, 2nd Earl of Shelburne">Lord Shelburne</a> that North should be brought to public trial for his conduct of the American War, but the prospect was soon abandoned.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Ironically, the war began to turn in Great Britain's favour again in 1782 through naval victories, owing largely to policies adopted by Lord North and the Earl of Sandwich. The British naval victory at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_the_Saintes" title="Battle of the Saintes">Battle of the Saintes</a> took place after the government's fall. Despite predictions that <a href="/wiki/Gibraltar" title="Gibraltar">Gibraltar</a>'s fall was imminent, <a href="/wiki/Great_Siege_of_Gibraltar" title="Great Siege of Gibraltar">that fortress managed to hold out and was relieved</a>. Great Britain was able to make a much more favourable peace in 1783 than had appeared likely at the time when North had been ousted. In spite of this, North was critical of the terms agreed by the Shelburne government which he felt undervalued the strength of the British negotiating position.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Post-premiership">Post-premiership</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Post-premiership">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Fox.E2.80.93North_coalition"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Fox–North_coalition">Fox–North coalition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Fox–North coalition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Fox%E2%80%93North_coalition" title="Fox–North coalition">Fox–North coalition</a></div>
<p>In April 1783, North returned to power as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Home_Department" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State for the Home Department">Home Secretary</a> in an unlikely coalition with the radical Whig leader <a href="/wiki/Charles_James_Fox" title="Charles James Fox">Charles James Fox</a> known as the Fox–North Coalition under the nominal leadership of the <a href="/wiki/William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Cavendish-Bentinck, 3rd Duke of Portland">Duke of Portland</a>. King <a href="/wiki/George_III" title="George III">George III</a>, who detested the radical and republican Fox, never forgave this supposed betrayal, and North never again served in government after the ministry fell in December 1783. One of the major achievements of the coalition was the signing of the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1783)" title="Treaty of Paris (1783)">Treaty of Paris</a>, which formally ended the <a href="/wiki/American_War_of_Independence" class="mw-redirect" title="American War of Independence">American War of Independence</a>.
</p><p>The new Prime Minister, <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a>, was not expected to last long, and North, a vocal critic, still entertained hopes of regaining high office. In this, he was to be frustrated, as Pitt dominated the British political scene for the next twenty years, leaving both North and Fox in the political wilderness.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years">Later years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Later years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>North was an active speaker until he began to go blind in 1786. He succeeded his father as 2nd Earl of Guilford on 4 August 1790 and entered the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>, by which time he had entirely lost his sight.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Lord Guilford died in <a href="/wiki/Mayfair" title="Mayfair">Mayfair</a>, England (now part of London), and was buried at All Saints' Church, <a href="/wiki/Wroxton" title="Wroxton">Wroxton</a> (Oxfordshire), near his family home of <a href="/wiki/Wroxton_Abbey" title="Wroxton Abbey">Wroxton Abbey</a>. His memorial was sculpted by <a href="/wiki/John_Flaxman" title="John Flaxman">John Flaxman</a> RA.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His son <a href="/wiki/George_North,_3rd_Earl_of_Guilford" title="George North, 3rd Earl of Guilford">George North, Lord North</a>, took over the constituency of Banbury, and in 1792 acceded to his father's title. Wroxton Abbey is now owned by <a href="/wiki/Fairleigh_Dickinson_University" title="Fairleigh Dickinson University">Fairleigh Dickinson University</a>, ironically an American college, and the modernised abbey serves as a location for American students to study abroad in England.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lord North is today predominantly remembered as the Prime Minister "who lost America".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Both <a href="/wiki/Lord_North_Street" title="Lord North Street">Lord North Street</a> and <a href="/wiki/Guilford_Street" title="Guilford Street">Guilford Street</a> in London are named after him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frederick_North,_Lord_North&amp;action=edit&amp;section=16" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>On 20 May 1756 Lord North married Anne Speke (before 1741&#160;&#8211;&#32;1797), daughter of <a href="/wiki/George_Speke_(politician,_died_1753)" title="George Speke (politician, died 1753)">George Speke</a> MP, of <a href="/wiki/Whitelackington" title="Whitelackington">Whitelackington</a> in Somerset. She was the sole heiress of the Devonshire estates of the Drake family of <a href="/wiki/Ash,_Musbury" title="Ash, Musbury">Ash</a>, which subsequently were sold piecemeal by Lord North.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> By Anne he had seven children:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/George_Augustus_North,_3rd_Earl_of_Guilford" class="mw-redirect" title="George Augustus North, 3rd Earl of Guilford">George Augustus North, 3rd Earl of Guilford</a> (11 September 1757&#160;&#8211;&#32;20 April 1802), who married, firstly, Maria Frances Mary Hobart-Hampden (died 23 April 1794), daughter of the 3rd <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Buckinghamshire" title="Earl of Buckinghamshire">Earl of Buckinghamshire</a>, on 30 September 1785 and had issue. He married, secondly, Susan Coutts (died 24 September 1837), on 28 February 1796.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Catherine_Douglas,_Baroness_Glenbervie" title="Catherine Douglas, Baroness Glenbervie">Catherine Anne North</a> (1760–1817), who married <a href="/wiki/Sylvester_Douglas,_1st_Baron_Glenbervie" title="Sylvester Douglas, 1st Baron Glenbervie">Sylvester Douglas, 1st Baron Glenbervie</a>, and had no children.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Francis_North,_4th_Earl_of_Guilford" title="Francis North, 4th Earl of Guilford">Francis North, 4th Earl of Guilford</a> (1761–1817)</li>
<li>Lady Charlotte North (died 25 October 1849), who married Lt. Col. The Hon. John Lindsay (15 March 1762&#160;&#8211;&#32;6 March 1826), son of the 5th <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Balcarres" title="Earl of Balcarres">Earl of Balcarres</a>, on 2 April 1800.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Frederick_North,_5th_Earl_of_Guilford" title="Frederick North, 5th Earl of Guilford">Frederick North, 5th Earl of Guilford</a> (1766–1827)</li>
<li>Lady Anne North (before 1783&#160;&#8211;&#32;18 January 1832), who married the <a href="/wiki/John_Baker-Holroyd,_1st_Earl_of_Sheffield" class="mw-redirect" title="John Baker-Holroyd, 1st Earl of Sheffield">1st Earl of Sheffield</a> on 20 January 1798 and had two children</li></ul>
