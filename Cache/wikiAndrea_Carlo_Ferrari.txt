<p><b>Andrea Ferrari</b> (13 August 1850 &#8211; 2 February 1921) – later adopting the middle name "<b>Carlo</b>" – was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> who served as a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> and as the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Milan" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> from 1894 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Ferrari was a well-regarded pastor and theologian who led two dioceses before being appointed to the prestigious Milanese archdiocese which he led until his death. But he was later accused of <a href="/wiki/Modernism_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Modernism in the Catholic Church">Modernism</a> which led to a strained relationship with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> who finally reconciled with Ferrari in 1912.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3G_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3G-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The cause for his canonization opened after his death in 1963 and he became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. He was named <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 1975, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified him in 1987.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education_and_priesthood">Education and priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education and priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg/210px-Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg" decoding="async" width="210" height="129" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg/315px-Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg/420px-Blessed_Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari_-_Duomo_-_Milan_2014.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2368" data-file-height="1460" /></a><figcaption>Cardinal Ferrari's remains housed in the Cathedral of Milan.</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Andrea Ferrari</b> was born on 13 August 1850 in Lalatta (now Palanzano) in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Parma" title="Province of Parma">Parma province</a> as the eldest of four children to the <a href="/wiki/Shoemaker" class="mw-redirect" title="Shoemaker">shoemaker</a> Giuseppe Ferrari and Maddalena Longarini; his <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> was celebrated on 14 August.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His two paternal uncles Abbondio and Pietro were priests serving in <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He received his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> in 1860 from Father Giovanni Agostini and received his <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Christian_sacrament)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Christian sacrament)">Confirmation</a> in 1866.
</p><p>He felt called to serve as a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> and was educated in <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a> where he was to obtain a <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theological studies</a> in 1883.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ferrari received the first two minor orders on 18 September 1869 and the other two on 23 September 1871.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He received the subdiaconate on 21 September 1872 and the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> on 15 December 1872. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> on 20 December 1873 for the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Parma" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Parma">Diocese of Parma</a> where he served from 1874 until 1890. He also served as the <a href="/wiki/Archpriest" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a> of <a href="/wiki/Fornovo_di_Taro" title="Fornovo di Taro">Fornovo di Taro</a> from 1874 until 1875 when he was made the vicar curate for the San Leonardo church.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ferrari served as the <a href="/wiki/Rector_(ecclesiastical)" title="Rector (ecclesiastical)">Vice-Rector</a> for seminarians in Parma and served also as a professor of mathematics and natural sciences in 1875 and later became its rector in 1877. He served also as a professor of <a href="/wiki/Fundamental_theology" title="Fundamental theology">theological fundamentals</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_history" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecclesiastical history">ecclesial historical sciences</a> as well as educating them in <a href="/wiki/Moral_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Moral theology">moral theological</a> subjects in 1878. It was later that he published the "<i>Summula theologiae dogmaticae generalis</i>" in 1885 which proved to be a respected work in the field at that time and it was reprinted several times.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In mid-1890 he was appointed as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Guastalla" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Guastalla">Bishop of Guastalla</a> and he received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">bishop</a> on 29 June 1890 from Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Lucido_Parocchi" title="Lucido Parocchi">Lucido Parocchi</a> in the church of the Sacred Heart of Jesus in Villa Lante. The co-consecrators were Vincenzo Leone Sallua and Giovanni Maria Majoli.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He took possession of his new diocese on 3 October 1890 and was later transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Como" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Como">Diocese of Como</a> in mid-1891 after a brief tenure in Guastalla. In Como he was noted for his dedication to the people and made several pastoral visits to see all his parishes. In 1894 the newspaper <i><a href="/wiki/Corriere_della_Sera" title="Corriere della Sera">Corriere della Sera</a></i> noted his "meticulous visits" as proper diocesan management and attentiveness while noting that "he talks well with a good voice".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1893 he supported the appointment of Giuseppe Melchiorre Sarto – future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> – as <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice" title="Patriarch of Venice">Patriarch of Venice</a> and was successful in securing the appointment.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinalate">Cardinalate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cardinalate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ferrari was elevated to the cardinalate in 1894 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> named him as the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_Sant%27Anastasia_al_Palatino" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di Sant&#39;Anastasia al Palatino">Cardinal-Priest of Santa Anastasia</a> (the title and red hat were conferred a week after the elevation).<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It was just a week after his elevation that he was transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Milan">Archdiocese of Milan</a> and was granted the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> prior to his departure; he also took <b>Carlo</b> as a middle name in honour of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a> who was a predecessor during the <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a> period. His private assistant while in Milan was Father Giovanni Rossi.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Ferrari was a strong supporter and promoter of <i><a href="/wiki/Rerum_Novarum" class="mw-redirect" title="Rerum Novarum">Rerum Novarum</a></i> and espoused the core themes of <a href="/wiki/Social_justice" title="Social justice">social justice</a> that the pope highlighted in that document. He also enlisted the aid of <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Toniolo" title="Giuseppe Toniolo">Giuseppe Toniolo</a> to promote it and make it a theme of his professorship.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His main mission in Milan was to preserve the faith of the people through <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechesis</a> and he made four pastoral visits as archbishop. He pushed for the publication of the <a href="/wiki/Catechism_of_Saint_Pius_X" title="Catechism of Saint Pius X"><i>Catechism of Pius X</i></a> in Milan as a step towards this aim. He also visited all parishes in his archdiocese and was attentive to the social circumstances of each parish. In addition Ferrari held several episcopal conferences to discuss matters of ecclesial life. In 1895 he held the Archdiocesan Eucharistic Congress from 1–5 September 1895.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ferrari participated in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1903" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1903">papal conclave in 1903</a> that elected Pope Pius X, and had been considered to be "<a href="/wiki/Papabile" title="Papabile">papabile</a>" for his pastoral qualities.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Ferrari petitioned the cardinals to support a pastoral candidate to become pope and began casting his votes for his old colleague Sarto. He tried to persuade Sarto to accept the election if chosen though the latter insisted that he should not be voted for and that he would not accept. But Ferrari insisted that the refusal could become harmful for the Church and painful for Sarto for the remainder of his life. But <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Satolli" title="Francesco Satolli">Francesco Satolli</a> convinced Sarto of the ramifications of his refusal which prompted Sarto's acceptance of the pontificate.<sup id="cite_ref-3G_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3G-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Ferrari returned to Milan on 10 August and that month travelled to <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> to meet with its archbishop Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Anton_Hubert_Fischer" title="Anton Hubert Fischer">Anton Hubert Fischer</a>.
</p><p>In 1908 he was in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> at <a href="/wiki/Westminster" title="Westminster">Westminster</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Eucharistic_Congress" class="mw-redirect" title="Eucharistic Congress">nineteenth Eucharistic Congress</a> held from 9–13 September.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1910 he organized festivities for the third centennial of the canonization of Charles Borromeo.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1918 he founded the Women's Youth for <a href="/wiki/Azione_Cattolica" title="Azione Cattolica">Azione Cattolica</a> and entrusted its direction to <a href="/wiki/Armida_Barelli" title="Armida Barelli">Armida Barelli</a>. During <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> he formed a group that was dedicated to caring for soldiers and prisoners and was awarded in 1919 with the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saints_Maurizio_and_Lazzaro" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saints Maurizio and Lazzaro">Grand Cross of the Order of Saints Maurizio and Lazzaro</a> for his efforts.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ferrari was accused of "<a href="/wiki/Modernism" title="Modernism">Modernism</a>" in 1907 which was an accusation that Pope Pius X had accepted. He was no Modernist and denounced them in a pastoral letter he issued in 1908. Despite this the accusations put him in a negative position with <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and he decided to keep quiet so as not to attract the ire of Pius X. He had been accused of excessive liberalism and defended his archdiocese against misunderstandings that Rome held though this prompted a 1911 canonical investigation.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1912 the pope realized the mistake he had made and received the cardinal after this matter was resolved for a reconciliation. In 1912 he promoted the establishment of the newspaper <i>L'Italia</i> which replaced <i>L'Unione</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He also partook in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1914" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1914">conclave in 1914</a> that elected <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a>. Ferrari was on good terms with Angelo Giuseppe Roncalli – the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>. The two knew the other well and Roncalli was the one who celebrated his funeral. He was also close with Achille Ratti who was his successor in Milan and the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>. Ferrari ordained as priests the future cardinals <a href="/wiki/Camillo_Caccia_Dominioni" title="Camillo Caccia Dominioni">Camillo Caccia Dominioni</a> (1899) and <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Confalonieri" title="Carlo Confalonieri">Carlo Confalonieri</a> (1916) in addition to Bishop Giorgio Giovanni Elli (1903) and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Mario_Giardini" title="Mario Giardini">Mario Giardini</a> (1904).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ferrari died in 1921 at 5:55<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>pm after he finished the recitation of one of the <a href="/wiki/Rosaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Rosaries">rosaries</a> he himself started due to <a href="/wiki/Throat_cancer" class="mw-redirect" title="Throat cancer">throat cancer</a>, and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Milan_Cathedral" title="Milan Cathedral">archdiocesan cathedral</a> under the Sacred Heart altar.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The first sign of his ailment around 1918 was simple hoarseness, later diagnosed as throat cancer. His old friend Roncalli would refer to him later as an "authentic saint".<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Legnano" title="Legnano">Legnano</a> a church was constructed from 1987 to 1989 and dedicated to him. Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Maria_Martini" title="Carlo Maria Martini">Carlo Maria Martini</a> consecrated the church in 1991.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrea_Carlo_Ferrari&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Roma_(Q._Ostiense)_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Roma_%28Q._Ostiense%29_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG/220px-Roma_%28Q._Ostiense%29_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Roma_%28Q._Ostiense%29_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG/330px-Roma_%28Q._Ostiense%29_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/47/Roma_%28Q._Ostiense%29_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG/440px-Roma_%28Q._Ostiense%29_-_S._Benedetto_11.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>Plaque in honour of Ferrari, <a href="/wiki/San_Benedetto_fuori_Porta_San_Paolo" title="San Benedetto fuori Porta San Paolo">San Benedetto fuori Porta San Paolo</a>, Rome</figcaption></figure>
<p>The Milanese came to revere Ferrari for his strong holiness and his old friend Pope John XXIII opened his cause for canonization on 10 February 1963. This came after his Milanese successor <a href="/wiki/Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster" title="Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster">Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster</a> opened the informative phase of investigation for his beatification in 1951. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> (another Milanese successor) proclaimed him to be Venerable on 1 February 1975 in recognition of his life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified Ferrari on 10 May 1987 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for this cause is the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> priest Giovangiuseppe Califano.
</p>
