<p><b>Gotthard</b> (or <b>Godehard</b>) (960 – 5 May 1038 <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gotthardus, Godehardus</i>), also known as <i>Gothard</i> or <i>Godehard the Bishop</i>, was a German bishop venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gotthard_of_Hildesheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gotthard was born in 960 near <a href="/wiki/Niederalteich" title="Niederalteich">Niederaltaich</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Passau" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Passau">diocese of Passau</a>.  Gotthard studied the humanities and theology at <a href="/wiki/Niederaltaich_Abbey" title="Niederaltaich Abbey">Niederaltaich Abbey</a>, where his father Ratmund was a vassal of the canons.  While at the abbey, Gotthard became a canon under Abbot Erkanbert. Gotthard then continued his studies at the archiepiscopal court of <a href="/wiki/Salzburg" title="Salzburg">Salzburg</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Mariendom_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariendom-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> where he served as an ecclesiastical administrator. After traveling through various countries, including <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, Gotthard completed his advanced studies under the guidance of Liutfrid in the cathedral school at <a href="/wiki/Passau" title="Passau">Passau</a>. He then joined the canons at Niederaltaich and was appointed <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry II of Bavaria">Henry II of Bavaria</a> decided to transform the <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">chapter house</a> of Niederaltaich into a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery Gotthard remained there as a novice, subsequently becoming a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> there in 990 under the abbot Ercanbertn. In 993, Gotthard was ordained a priest, and later became prior and rector of the monastic school. Duke Henry was present at Gotthard's installation as abbot in 996. 
</p><p>Gotthard was a dedicated reformer and introduced the <a href="/wiki/Cluniac_reforms" class="mw-redirect" title="Cluniac reforms">Cluniac reforms</a> at Niederaltaich.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He helped revive the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Rule of St. Benedict</a>, and then trained abbots for the abbeys of <a href="/wiki/Tegernsee_Abbey" title="Tegernsee Abbey">Tegernsee</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld_Abbey" title="Hersfeld Abbey">Hersfeld</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kremsm%C3%BCnster_Abbey" title="Kremsmünster Abbey">Kremsmünster</a> to restore Benedictine observance, under the patronage of <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>.
</p><p>He succeeded <a href="/wiki/Bernward_of_Hildesheim" title="Bernward of Hildesheim">Bernward</a> as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hildesheim" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Hildesheim">bishop of Hildesheim</a> on 2 December 1022,<sup id="cite_ref-Mariendom_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariendom-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> being consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Aribo,_Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Aribo, Archbishop of Mainz">Aribo, Archbishop of Mainz</a>. During the 15 years of his episcopal government, while earning the respect of the clergy. He was particularly interested in the education of the young clergy, and he started several schools. The cathedral school in Hildesheim became under him a center for learning. Gotthard ordered the construction of some 30 churches. He founded a monastery beside the chapel on the Zierenberg about 1025 and had a church built there which was dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Maurice" title="Saint Maurice">Saint Maurice</a> and consecrated in 1028.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite his advanced age, he defended the rights of his diocese vigorously.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  After a brief sickness, he died on <a href="/wiki/5_May" class="mw-redirect" title="5 May">5 May</a> 1038 at the "Mauritiusstift", the <a href="/wiki/Hostel" title="Hostel">hospice</a> for travelers which he had founded.<sup id="cite_ref-Mariendom_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mariendom-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gotthard_of_Hildesheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg/200px-Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="103" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg/300px-Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg/400px-Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="935" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hildesheim_StGodehard_Tympanon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Tympanum_(architecture)" title="Tympanum (architecture)">Tympanum</a> depicting <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>, Gotthard and <a href="/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Pavia" title="Epiphanius of Pavia">Epiphanius</a>.  Hildesheim, St. Godehard Basilica.</div></div></div>
<p>Gotthard's successors in the episcopate of Hildesheim, Bertold (1119–30) and <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Hildesheim" title="Bernard of Hildesheim">Bernhard I</a> (1130–53), pushed for his <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This was accomplished during the episcopate of Bernard, in 1131, and it took place at a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims</a>. There, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_II" title="Pope Innocent II">Pope Innocent II</a>, in the presence of Bernard and <a href="/wiki/Norbert_of_Xanten" title="Norbert of Xanten">Norbert of Xanten</a>, officially made Gotthard a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg/220px-Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="146" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg/330px-Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg/440px-Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4922" data-file-height="3276" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Godehardschrein_in_der_Krypta_des_Hildesheimer_Doms.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Shrine of Godehard in the crypt of <a href="/wiki/Hildesheim_Cathedral" title="Hildesheim Cathedral">Hildesheim Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>On 4 May 1132, Bernard <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> Gotthard's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> from the abbatial church to the cathedral at Hildesheim. On 5 May the first liturgical festivity in honor of Gotthard was celebrated.  <a href="/wiki/Miracles" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracles">Miracles</a> were attributed to the relics. The Gotthard shrine is one of the oldest remaining relic shrines of the Middle Ages, dating from about 1140.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>Veneration of Gotthard spread to <a href="/wiki/Scandinavia" title="Scandinavia">Scandinavia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Europe" title="Eastern Europe">Eastern Europe</a>. Gotthard was invoked against fever, <a href="/wiki/Dropsy" class="mw-redirect" title="Dropsy">dropsy</a>, childhood sicknesses, <a href="/wiki/Hailstones" class="mw-redirect" title="Hailstones">hailstones</a>, the pain of childbirth, and <a href="/wiki/Gout" title="Gout">gout</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Niederaltaich_Abbey" title="Niederaltaich Abbey">Niederaltaich Abbey</a> made its famous abbot the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the abbey's well-known grammar school, the <i>St.-Gotthard-Gymnasium</i>.
</p><p>Gotthard also became the patron saint of traveling merchants, and thus many churches and chapels were dedicated to him in the <a href="/wiki/Alps" title="Alps">Alps</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>According to an ancient <a href="/wiki/Ticino" title="Ticino">Ticinese</a> tradition, the little church in <a href="/wiki/St._Gotthard_Pass" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Gotthard Pass">St. Gotthard Pass</a> (<i>San Gottardo</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Swiss_Alps" title="Swiss Alps">Swiss Alps</a> was founded by <a href="/wiki/Galdino" class="mw-redirect" title="Galdino">Galdino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> (r. 1166-76).  Goffredo da Bussero, however, attributes the founding of the church to <a href="/wiki/Enrico_da_Settala" title="Enrico da Settala">Enrico da Settala</a>, Bishop of Milan from 1213 to 1230.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  The hospice was entrusted to the care of the <a href="/wiki/Capuchin_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Capuchin Order">Capuchin Order</a> in 1685 by <a href="/wiki/Federico_Visconti" title="Federico Visconti">Federico Visconti</a>, and later passed under the control of a <a href="/wiki/Confraternity" title="Confraternity">confraternity</a> of Ticino.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Gottardo_di_Hildesheim-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
