<p><b>John Cantius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Joannes Cantius</i>; <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Jan z Kęt</i> or <span title="Polish-language text"><i lang="pl">Jan Kanty</i></span>; 23 June 1390 &#8211; 24 December 1473) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Priest_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priest (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scholasticism" title="Scholasticism">scholastic philosopher</a>, <a href="/wiki/Physicist" title="Physicist">physicist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Theologian" class="mw-redirect" title="Theologian">theologian</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Cantius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Cantius was born in <a href="/wiki/K%C4%99ty" title="Kęty">Kęty</a>, a small town near <a href="/wiki/O%C5%9Bwi%C4%99cim" title="Oświęcim">Oświęcim</a>, Poland, to Anna and Stanisław Kanty. He attended the <a href="/wiki/Jagiellonian_University" title="Jagiellonian University">Kraków Academy</a> at which he attained <a href="/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree" title="Bachelor&#39;s degree">bachelor</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Licentiate_(degree)" title="Licentiate (degree)">licentiate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-godrycz_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-godrycz-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1418 he became a Doctor of Philosophy.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Upon graduation he spent the next three years conducting philosophy classes at the university, while preparing for the priesthood.
</p><p>Upon his ordination, he became rector at the school of the Canons Regular of the Most Holy Sepulcher in Miechow.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> While there, he was offered a professorship of <i>Sacra Scriptura</i> (Holy Scripture) back at his <a href="/wiki/Alma_mater" title="Alma mater">alma mater</a>, the Kraków Academy, which would later be named the <a href="/wiki/Jagiellonian_University" title="Jagiellonian University">Jagiellonian University</a>. He attained a doctorate in theology and eventually became director of the theology department. He held the professorship until his death in 1473. Cantius spent many hours copying manuscripts of the Holy Scriptures, theological tracts, and other scholarly works.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Physics" title="Physics">physics</a>, he helped develop <a href="/wiki/Jean_Buridan" title="Jean Buridan">Jean Buridan</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Theory_of_impetus" title="Theory of impetus">theory of impetus</a>, which anticipated the work of <a href="/wiki/Galileo" class="mw-redirect" title="Galileo">Galileo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Isaac_Newton" title="Isaac Newton">Newton</a>.
</p><p>During his time in Kraków, Cantius became well known in the city for his generosity and compassion toward the poor, especially needy students at the university. He subsisted on what was strictly necessary to sustain his life, giving alms regularly to the poor. He made one pilgrimage to Jerusalem and four pilgrimages on foot to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-godrycz_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-godrycz-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Michael Miechowita, the medieval Polish historian and Cantius's first biographer, described Cantius's extreme humility and charity; he took as his motto:
</p>
<dl><dd><i>Conturbare cave: non est placare suave</i>,</dd>
<dd><i>Infamare cave; nam revocare grave.</i><sup id="cite_ref-godrycz_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-godrycz-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></dd>
<dd>(<i>Beware disturbing: it's not sweetly pleasing</i>,</dd>
<dd><i>Beware speaking ill: for taking back words is burdensome.</i>)</dd></dl>
<p>He died while living in retirement at his alma mater on 24 December 1473, aged 83. His remains were interred in the Collegiate Church of St Anne, where his tomb became and remains a popular pilgrimage site.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is the patron of the diocese of Bielsko-Żywiec (since 1992), and of the students.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Cantius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Cantius was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a> on 28 March 1676. He was named patron of <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> in the year 1737.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Ninety-one years after his beatification, John Cantius was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 16 July 1767, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Breviary" title="Roman Breviary">Roman Breviary</a> distinguishes him with three hymns; he is the only <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> not a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> who has been given this honor in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_liturgy" title="Catholic liturgy">Catholic liturgy</a>.
</p><p>St. John Cantius is a popular <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>.  A number of churches and schools founded by <a href="/wiki/Polish_diaspora" title="Polish diaspora">Polish diaspora</a> communities throughout <a href="/wiki/North_America" title="North America">North America</a> are named in his honor, in cities as far-ranging as <a href="/wiki/Cleveland" title="Cleveland">Cleveland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ohio" title="Ohio">Ohio</a>; <a href="/wiki/Winnipeg" title="Winnipeg">Winnipeg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manitoba" title="Manitoba">Manitoba</a>; <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit</a>, <a href="/wiki/Michigan" title="Michigan">Michigan</a>; <a href="/wiki/Chicago" title="Chicago">Chicago</a>, <a href="/wiki/Illinois" title="Illinois">Illinois</a>; Rolling Prairie, <a href="/wiki/Indiana" title="Indiana">Indiana</a>, <a href="/wiki/Milwaukee" title="Milwaukee">Milwaukee</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wisconsin" title="Wisconsin">Wisconsin</a>; <a href="/wiki/St._Cloud,_Minnesota" title="St. Cloud, Minnesota">St. Cloud</a>, <a href="/wiki/Minnesota" title="Minnesota">Minnesota</a>; <a href="/wiki/Wilno,_Minnesota" title="Wilno, Minnesota">Wilno, Minnesota</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia" title="Philadelphia">Philadelphia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania" title="Erie, Pennsylvania">Erie</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Windber" class="mw-redirect" title="Windber">Windber</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pennsylvania" title="Pennsylvania">Pennsylvania</a>; <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> and <a href="/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York" title="Buffalo, New York">Buffalo</a>, <a href="/wiki/New_York_(state)" title="New York (state)">New York</a>.
</p><p>"John Cantius" has been used as a first and middle name&#8212;see, for example, <a href="/wiki/John_Cantius_Garand" class="mw-redirect" title="John Cantius Garand">John Cantius Garand</a>.
</p><p>In 1998, a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> was founded, based in <a href="/wiki/Chicago" title="Chicago">Chicago</a>, which took St. John Cantius as their <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>. Thus they are the <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular_of_Saint_John_Cantius" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular of Saint John Cantius">Canons Regular of Saint John Cantius</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Cantius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When St. John Cantius's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was first inserted into the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1770, it was initially assigned to 20 October, but in the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">calendar reform of 1969</a> it was moved to 23 December, the day before the anniversary of his death, which occurred on <a href="/wiki/Christmas_Eve" title="Christmas Eve">Christmas Eve</a> 1473.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Those who, as authorized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in <i><a href="/wiki/Summorum_Pontificum" title="Summorum Pontificum">Summorum Pontificum</a></i>, use the <a href="/wiki/1962_Roman_Missal" class="mw-redirect" title="1962 Roman Missal">1962 Roman Missal</a> continue to celebrate it on 20 October as a III Class Feast.
</p>
