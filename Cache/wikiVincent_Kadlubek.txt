<p><b>Wincenty Kadłubek</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1150 &#8211; 8 March 1223) was a <a href="/wiki/Polish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Polish people">Polish</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> and professed <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> who served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Krak%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Kraków">Bishop of Kraków</a> from 1208 until his resignation in 1218.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His episcopal mission was to reform the diocesan priests to ensure their holiness and invigorate the faithful and cultivate greater participation in ecclesial affairs on their part.<sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Wincenty was much more than just a bishop; he was a leading scholar in Poland from the twelfth and thirteenth centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was also a lawyer, historian, church reformer, monk, <a href="/wiki/Magisterium" title="Magisterium">magister</a>,  and the father of Polish culture and national identity.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The process of his canonization proved quite slow despite the initial momentum to see him proclaimed as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The cause languished for several centuries until 1764 when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a> beatified him.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known about Kadłubek's early life, but we do know he was born around 1160 to parents of elite status.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Eleventh and Twelfth century Poland was a complicated and turbulent place, shaping Kadłubek's works and life. This period of time is characterized by territorial divisions between branches of the Piast family and civil war. <sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Kadłubek first studied in <a href="/wiki/Stopnica" title="Stopnica">Stopnica</a><sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> before he studied at the cathedral school in <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was while at the latter that he studied under Mateusz Cholewa, who encouraged Kadłubek to be sent abroad for further studies.<sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He completed his studies in 1185 and returned home where he was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> before 1189 and served as the <a href="/wiki/Provost_(religion)" title="Provost (religion)">provost</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Sandomierz_Cathedral" title="Sandomierz Cathedral">Sandomierz Cathedral</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> some sources suggest that he served as the principal of the cathedral school in Kraków.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> &#160;He studied in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">the college</a> in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> as well as at <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">the college</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>. Some sources suggest that he met the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> at Bologna when the two were students<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and it was further said he once encountered <a href="/wiki/John_of_Salisbury" title="John of Salisbury">John of Salisbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Kadłubek was considerably educated for the time period. He was knowledgeable of medieval and classical literature, spoke Greek, French, and Latin, and had a meticulous knowledge of Roman and Canon law.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At the time, you could only learn about Roman and Canon law at schools in Paris and Bologna. He was the first known Polish lawyer to contribute to Polish legal scholarship.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Kadłubek's work, the Chronica Polonorum, or the Chronicle of Poles, confirms that he was a lawyer. Through the Chronicle of Poles, he was the first Polish person to chronicle Polish history, making him a cardinal source for the early medieval history of Poland.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Time_as_a_bishop">Time as a bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Time as a bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Kadłubek was elected to be a bishop in 1208.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was after the death of Fulko - the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Krak%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Kraków">Bishop of Kraków</a> - that he was chosen to succeed him as the new bishop.  This was the first time that the Krakow Chapter elected a bishop on its own, with the help of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a>, instead of through secular power. Pope Innocent III had to take action because the Krakow Chapter could not decide between Kadłubek and Bishop Gedko of Plok. Kadłubek was eventually chosen to be a bishop because of his avid support of church reforms and potential friendship with Pope Innocent III, as It is possible that Pope Innocent III and Kadłubek met in Paris while studying.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Pope Innocent III confirmed the decision in a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> on 28 March 1208 and Kadłubek received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Gniezno" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Gniezno">Archbishop of Gniezno</a> two months later. Innocent III's bull referred to Kadłubek's wisdom as the motivation for his selection while referring to him as a "master and preacher". 
</p><p>He set out to reform the diocesan priesthood to ensure their holiness while also seeking to invigorate the faithful to active participation in ecclesial affairs.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As Bishop, he took action to make sure the church was independent of the power of the duke. In addition to being the bishop, he was the legislator of the diocese in Krakow and a member of the first Polish delegation to the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Fourth Council of the Lateran">Fourth Lateran Council</a>, causing him to affect their reforms and resolutions in Krakow. He participated in diocesan synods and donated to the diocese of Krakow. He also founded and donated to monasteries, in addition to being a benefactor of the <a href="/wiki/J%C4%99drzej%C3%B3w_Abbey" title="Jędrzejów Abbey">Cistercian monastery in Jędrzejów</a>, which was not uncommon for bishops at the time. He was the first in Poland to begin the custom of lighting a vigil lamp prior to the Blessed Sacrament, which he was able to accomplish by allocating funds from the Krakow Chapter to provide wax for the vigil lamp in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Wenceslas_Cathedral" title="Saint Wenceslas Cathedral">Saint Wenceslas Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He also consecrated Saint Florian's Basilica and was said to have once been the chaplain to <a href="/wiki/Casimir_II_the_Just" title="Casimir II the Just">Casimir II the Just</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Kadłubek was ordained as a priest the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> <a href="/wiki/Ceslaus" title="Ceslaus">Ceslaus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bishop was noted for his linguistic skills and his charismatic preaching; Kadłubek was known also for his expertise in <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a>, his approach to philosophical subjects, as well as for his expertise in rhetoric.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was well educated on the natural sciences since he had learned about them while in Paris and Bologna. Yet, it was while in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> that he started reading into the life and works of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Bernard_of_Clairvaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Bernard of Clairvaux">Saint Bernard of Clairvaux</a> and became enthralled with the charism of the <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a> whom he granted favors to as bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He followed <a href="/wiki/Gallus_Anonymus" title="Gallus Anonymus">Gallus Anonymus</a> in further developing the idea of the Latin proverb "<a href="/wiki/Vox_populi#Vox_populi,_vox_Dei" title="Vox populi">vox populi vox dei</a>" ("the voice of the people is the voice of God") and argued that the ruler (king) should follow a council that includes bishops and representatives of clans since not the ruler but the council has higher power originating from the laws that God instituted. He also claimed that the council should elect the ruler and that rulers abusing their power should be removed from their position.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1217, Kadłubek decided to resign from his duties as a bishop. He spent his last years at the Cistercian monastery in Jędrzejów, making him the first Bishop of Krakow to join the Cistercian order after voluntarily abdicating. This also made him the first Polish person in the Cistercian monastery in Jędrzejów, which was composed of exclusively French monks at the time. The fire of the Jędrzejów monastery in 1800 in combination with the turbulence of Polish history at the time makes it difficult to understand the remaining years of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He likely continued to write the Chronica Polonorum into this time.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Kadlubek died on March 8, 1223, retaining his bishop's pallium, buried in the Jędrzejów monastery,<sup id="cite_ref-:1_5-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> his remains were interred before the high altar of the convent church. His remains were exhumed on 26 April 1633 with his <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> found intact though his remains had become skeletal. Measurements were taken and it was surmised that he was of "fair height".<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His remains were moved to a new location before the high altar on the following 16 August. Kadłubek's remains were again exhumed and reinterred in mid-1765 and some were moved to Sandomierz in 1845 for veneration. Other parts to his remains were moved in 1903 to Wawel and placed in a silver urn.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:102px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg/100px-Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg" decoding="async" width="100" height="189" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg/150px-Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/66/Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg/200px-Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg 2x" data-file-width="264" data-file-height="500" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wincenty_kad%C5%82ubek.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Painting in the <a href="/wiki/Sandomierz_Cathedral" title="Sandomierz Cathedral">Sandomierz Cathedral</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1634 the Polish episcopate made a petition to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> requesting his canonization. In 1650 the Bishop of Kraków Piotr Gembicki appointed a tribunal to hear witnesses for the cause but the commission never began its work which suspended the cause.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Jan Małachowski - in 1683 - petitioned <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a> to canonize the late bishop. Innocent XI requested the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> to begin an investigation which took place from 1688 to 1692. But the congregation concluded that there was no reliable evidence to prove his <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> which suspended the cause once again. In 1682 the king <a href="/wiki/Jan_III_Sobieski" class="mw-redirect" title="Jan III Sobieski">Jan III Sobieski</a> petitioned for his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Order_of_C%C3%AEteaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Cîteaux">Order of Cîteaux</a> made similar request in 1699 at their General Chapter.<sup id="cite_ref-WR_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-WR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Polish episcopate made another request to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> to canonize him but the process that the pope requested came to the same conclusion as the previous one.
</p><p>In 1761 the king <a href="/wiki/Augustus_III_of_Poland" title="Augustus III of Poland">Augustus III</a> sent two letters to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a> requesting the canonization which prompted the pope to order another process of investigation. That investigation proved to be successful for Clement XIII issued a papal bull in which he  beatified the late bishop on 18 February 1764.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On 9 June 1764 the pope issued another bull that allowed for a Mass and the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a> to be said in his honor in Kraków and among the Cistercians.
</p><p>He is referred to in Poland as a saint despite the fact that he has not been canonized as such; Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Dziwisz" title="Stanisław Dziwisz">Stanisław Dziwisz</a> referred to Kadłubek as a "saint" in 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Since 2 January 2016 he has been the patron for <a href="/wiki/J%C4%99drzej%C3%B3w" class="mw-redirect" title="Jędrzejów">Jędrzejów</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Efforts_to_resume_the_cause">Efforts to resume the cause</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Efforts to resume the cause">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There had been several efforts since his beatification to resume the cause and achieve his canonization though each attempt failed. Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Stefan_Wyszy%C5%84ski" title="Stefan Wyszyński">Stefan Wyszyński</a> and Karol Józef Wojtyła (the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>) both supported his cause in 1964 with Wojtyła referring to the late bishop as the "Father of Polish culture".<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1972 the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> concluded that there was no reliable or available documentation that could confirm Kadłubek's heroic virtues thus suspending the cause which has not continued since.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wincenty_Kad%C5%82ubek&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Kadlubek.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Kadlubek.jpg/180px-Kadlubek.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="135" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Kadlubek.jpg/270px-Kadlubek.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Kadlubek.jpg/360px-Kadlubek.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Kadlubek.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relics in Jędrzejów.</div></div></div>
<p>His best-known work - <i><a href="/wiki/Chronica_seu_originale_regum_et_principum_Poloniae" title="Chronica seu originale regum et principum Poloniae">Chronica seu originale regum et principum Poloniae</a></i> (<i>Chronicles of the Kings and Princes of Poland</i>) - is a historical compendium of Poland in four volumes.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The first three volumes take the form of a dialogue between Archbishop John of Gniezno and Bishop Mateusz. The first volume's sources are legends while the second is based on the chronicle of Gallus and the last two are based upon Kadłubek's own experiences. Writing this work made him the first Polish native to chronicle Polish history.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This work had a huge impact on the Polish political doctrine of the 14th and 15th centuries co-authored by <a href="/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_of_Skarbimierz" title="Stanisław of Skarbimierz">Stanisław of Skarbimierz</a> as well as on later works of <a href="/wiki/Wawrzyniec_Grzyma%C5%82a_Go%C5%9Blicki" class="mw-redirect" title="Wawrzyniec Grzymała Goślicki">Wawrzyniec Grzymała Goślicki</a>. These ideas led to the <a href="/wiki/Golden_Liberty" title="Golden Liberty">Nobles' Democracy</a> in Poland for it is in his works that for the first time the terms <i><a href="/wiki/Res_publica" title="Res publica">res publica</a></i> (see <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth" title="Commonwealth">Commonwealth</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rzeczpospolita" title="Rzeczpospolita">Rzeczpospolita</a>) were used in the context of Poland. Some suggest that the book was written at the request of Prince <a href="/wiki/Casimir_II_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Casimir II of Poland">Casimir II</a>; others suggest that it was made at the request of Prince <a href="/wiki/Leszek_I_the_White" class="mw-redirect" title="Leszek I the White">Leszek</a> while Kadłubek was a bishop; still others claim that it was not written until after his retirement.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
