<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>

<p><b>Peter the Hermit</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1050 – 8 July 1115 or 1131), also known as <b>Little Peter</b>, <b>Peter of Amiens</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">fr.</a> <i>Pierre d'Amiens</i>) or <b>Peter of Achères</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">fr.</a> <i>Pierre d'Achères</i>), was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest of <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a> and a key figure during the military expedition from France to Jerusalem, known as the <a href="/wiki/People%27s_Crusade" title="People&#39;s Crusade">People's Crusade</a>. He is by some called Blessed Peter the Hermit,<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> although he has not been <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is called <b>Pierre l'Ermite</b> in French. The structure of this name in French unlike in <a href="/wiki/English_language" title="English language">English</a> has led some <a href="/wiki/Francophone" class="mw-redirect" title="Francophone">francophone</a> scholars to treat l'Ermite as a surname rather than a title.
</p><p>According to some authors, he was born around 1050 and was the son of Renauld L'Ermite of Auvergne, and his wife Alide Montaigu, de <a href="/wiki/Picardy" title="Picardy">Picardie</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nominis_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nominis-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Others claim he was a member of the <i>L'Hermite</i> family of Auvergne in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> These claims are disputed by other authors, who argue that nothing can confirm that "the Hermit" was an actual surname and that surnames had not developed until after his day.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Before_1096">Before 1096</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Before 1096">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Anna_Komnene" title="Anna Komnene">Anna Komnene's</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Alexiad" title="Alexiad">Alexiad</a></i> (1148), <sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Peter attempted a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> before 1096, but was prevented by the <a href="/wiki/Seljuk_Turks" class="mw-redirect" title="Seljuk Turks">Seljuk Turks</a> from reaching his goal and was reportedly mistreated. This experience caused him to preach inflammatory statements about the Turks to upset Christians. However, doubts remain whether he ever made such a journey.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before the events of the first crusade, Peter the Hermit witnessed scenes that disturbed him during an earlier pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. When he finally reached the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Church of the Holy Sepulchre">Church of the Holy Sepulchre</a> "he saw many forbidden and wicked things occurring there… so he sought out the patriarch of the holy church of Jerusalem and asked why gentiles and evil men were able to pollute holy places and steal away offerings from the faithful, using the church as if a stable, beating up Christians, despoiling pilgrims through unjust fees, and inflicting on them many sufferings." The frustrated patriarch threw up his hands in exasperation: "Why do you reprimand me and disturb me in the midst of my fatherly cares? I have but the strength and power of a tiny ant when compared to those proud men. We have to redeem our lives here by regular tribute payments (<i><a href="/wiki/Jizya" title="Jizya">jizya</a></i>) or else face death-dealing punishment".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sources differ as to whether he was present at <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Clermont" title="Council of Clermont">Council of Clermont</a> in 1095, where the Pope made his military strategy public. It is certain that he was one of the preachers of the armed pilgrimage in France afterwards, and his own experience may have helped to give fire to the Roman Catholic cause. Tradition in <a href="/wiki/Huy" title="Huy">Huy</a> holds that he was there when the call for war was announced and he began his preaching at once. He soon leapt into fame as an emotional <a href="/wiki/Revivalist_(person)" title="Revivalist (person)">revivalist</a>, and the vast majority of sources and historians agree that thousands of serfs and peasants eagerly took the cross at his bidding, partly because of promises he did make.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This part of the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a> was also known as the <a href="/wiki/People%27s_Crusade" title="People&#39;s Crusade">Crusade of the Paupers</a>. Peter organized and guided the paupers as a spiritually purified and holy group of pilgrims who would, supposedly, be protected by the Holy Ghost. Peter did not train his army, nor did he supply weapons or food. Some historians think it possible that the People's Crusade also included well-armed soldiers and nobles.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> (A list of known participants in Peter's army can be found at The Digital Humanities Institute.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>)
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Crusade_to_the_Holy_Land">Crusade to the Holy Land</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Crusade to the Holy Land">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The leader of the Roman Catholic Church, <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> Urban II commissioned Peter to command an armed pilgrimage to Jerusalem.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Before Peter went on his military expedition he received permission from Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Simeon_II_of_Jerusalem" title="Simeon II of Jerusalem">Simeon II of Jerusalem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Peter was able to recruit from England, Lorraine, France, and Flanders.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  Peter the Hermit arrived in Cologne, Germany, on Holy Saturday, 12 April in 1096.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Massacre_of_Jewish_civilians">Massacre of Jewish civilians</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Massacre of Jewish civilians">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the spring of 1096 Peter was one of the prominent  involved in the <a href="/wiki/Rhineland_massacres" title="Rhineland massacres">Rhineland massacres</a> against civilians adherent to the Jewish religion.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>  Peter and his troops participated in the torture and slaughter of Jews in <a href="/wiki/Lorraine" title="Lorraine">Lorraine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Worms,_Germany" title="Worms, Germany">Worms</a>, <a href="/wiki/Speyer" title="Speyer">Speyer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In Mainz, Peter's followers killed a large group of Jews that had been granted refuge by a local bishop in exchange for money. Death toll estimations vary from 2,000 - 2,800 civilians, some speak about 5,000 or even 10,000 people killed. These killings are known as <i>Gzerot Tatnó</i> (Hebrew: גזרות תתנ"ו, Edicts of 4856).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Another group had been led by the knight count <a href="/wiki/Emicho" title="Emicho">Emicho of Leiningen</a> who provoked the group to the massacres.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Hungary.2C_Belgrade.2C_Sofia"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hungary,_Belgrade,_Sofia">Hungary, Belgrade, Sofia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Hungary, Belgrade, Sofia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Leading the first of the five sections of the People's Crusade to the destination of their pilgrimage, the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Church of the Holy Sepulchre">Church of the Holy Sepulchre</a>, he started (with 40,000 men and women) from <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> in April 1096, and arrived (with 30,000 men and women) at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> at the end of July. <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Alexios_I_Komnenos" title="Alexios I Komnenos">Alexios I Komnenos</a> was less than pleased with their arrival, for along with the head of the Eastern Orthodox Church, <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Nicholas_III_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Nicholas III of Constantinople">Patriarch Nicholas III of Constantinople</a>, he was now required to provide for the care and sustenance of the vast host of paupers for the remainder of their journey.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SiegeNish1096.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/SiegeNish1096.jpg/170px-SiegeNish1096.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="187" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/SiegeNish1096.jpg/255px-SiegeNish1096.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a6/SiegeNish1096.jpg 2x" data-file-width="297" data-file-height="326" /></a><figcaption>1096 Siege of Nish (1337)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Before reaching Constantinople though, Peter and his followers began to experience difficulty.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Zemun" title="Zemun">Zemun</a>, the governor, who was descendant of a Ghuzz Turk, and a colleague, were frightened by the army's size and decided to tighten regulations on a frontier. This would have been fine if a dispute about the sale of a pair of shoes had not occurred.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> This led to a riot and against Peter's wishes the town was attacked and the citadel was stormed. This resulted in 4000 Hungarians being killed and many provisions being stolen. Then on 26 June 1096 Peter's army was able to cross the <a href="/wiki/Sava" title="Sava">Sava</a> river. Then the army marched into <a href="/wiki/Belgrade" title="Belgrade">Belgrade</a>, set the town on fire, and pillaged it.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The army then made its way into and through Nish (<a href="/wiki/Ni%C5%A1" title="Niš">Niš</a>) after an eight-day delay. After riding through Nish, the Crusaders made their way towards Sofia, when they were attacked on the road. The army took heavy losses. They lost a quarter of their men but arrived in <a href="/wiki/Sofia" title="Sofia">Sofia</a> on 12 July nonetheless.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_14-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Constantinople">Constantinople</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Constantinople">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The forces then arrived in Constantinople on 1 August 1096. After a while, they arrived at a castle called Xerigordon and captured it. They captured the castle by taking possession of the castle's spring and well. After setting off to Civetot, they had set up camp near a village called Dracon. This is where the Turks ambushed Peter and his forces. This was the final battle of the People's Crusade that Peter led.
</p><p>Most of the paupers failed to make their way out of Catholic jurisdiction. The majority were incapable of being provided for by the various lordships and dioceses along the way and either starved, returned home or were put into servitude, while a substantial number were captured and sold into slavery by the various Slavic robber barons in the <a href="/wiki/Balkans" title="Balkans">Balkans</a>, kindling the view of the Balkan Slavs as unredeemed robbers and villains.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2009)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Peter joined the only other section which had succeeded in reaching Constantinople,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> that of <a href="/wiki/Walter_Sans_Avoir" title="Walter Sans Avoir">Walter Sans Avoir</a>, into a single group and encamped the still numerous pilgrims around Constantinople while he negotiated the shipping of the People's Crusade to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. The Emperor meanwhile had failed to provide for the pilgrims adequately and the camp made itself a growing nuisance, as the increasingly hungry paupers turned to pilfering the imperial stores.
</p><p>Alexios, worried at the growing disorder and fearful of his standing before the coming armed Crusader armies, quickly concluded negotiations and shipped them across the <a href="/wiki/Bosporus" title="Bosporus">Bosporus</a> to the Asiatic shore at the beginning of August, with promises of guards and passage through the Turkish lines. He warned the People's Crusade to await his orders, but in spite of his warnings, the paupers entered Turkish territory. The Turks began skirmishing with the largely unarmed host. Peter returned in desperation to Constantinople, seeking the Emperor's help.
</p><p>In Peter's absence, the pilgrims were ambushed and cut to pieces in detail by the Turks, who were more disciplined, at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Civetot" title="Battle of Civetot">Battle of Civetot</a>. Despite Peter's pronunciations of divine protection, the vast majority of the pilgrims were slaughtered by the swords and arrows of the Turks or were enslaved. Left in Constantinople with the small number of surviving followers, during the winter of 1096–1097, with little hope of securing Byzantine support, the People's Crusade awaited the coming of the armed crusaders as their sole source of protection to complete the pilgrimage.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="To_Jerusalem">To Jerusalem</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: To Jerusalem">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg/220px-Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="69" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg/330px-Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg/440px-Roman_du_Chevalier_du_Cygne_f176v_Pierre_l%27Ermite.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1021" data-file-height="318" /></a><figcaption>Peter the Hermit shows the crusaders the way to Jerusalem. French illumination (about 1270).</figcaption></figure>
<p>When the princes arrived, Peter joined their ranks as a member of the council in May 1097, and with the little following which remained they marched together through <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a> to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>. While his "paupers" never regained the numbers previous to the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Civetot" title="Battle of Civetot">Battle of Civetot</a>, his ranks were increasingly replenished with disarmed, injured, or bankrupted crusaders. Nonetheless, aside from a few rousing speeches to motivate the Crusaders, he played a subordinate part in the remaining history of the First Crusade which at this point clearly settled on a military campaign as the means to secure the pilgrimage routes and holy sites in <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>.
</p><p>Peter appears, at the beginning of 1098, as attempting to escape from the privations of the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Antioch" title="Siege of Antioch">siege of Antioch</a>—showing himself, as <a href="/wiki/Guibert_of_Nogent" title="Guibert of Nogent">Guibert of Nogent</a> says, a "fallen star." Guibert and other sources go on to write that Peter was responsible for the speech before the half-starved and dead Crusaders which motivated their sally from the gates of Antioch and their subsequent victory over the overwhelmingly superior Muslim army besieging the city. Thus, having recovered his stature, in the middle of the year he was sent by the princes to invite <a href="/wiki/Kerbogha" title="Kerbogha">Kerbogha</a> to settle all differences via a duel,<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which the emir subsequently declined.
</p><p>In 1099 Peter appears as the treasurer of the alms at the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Arqa" class="mw-redirect" title="Siege of Arqa">siege of Arqa</a>, and as leader of the supplicatory processions around the walls of Jerusalem <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_(1099)" title="Siege of Jerusalem (1099)">before it fell</a>, and later within Jerusalem which preceded the Crusaders' surprising victory at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Ascalon" title="Battle of Ascalon">Battle of Ascalon</a> (August). At the end of 1099, Peter went to <a href="/wiki/Latakia" title="Latakia">Latakia</a>, and sailed thence for the West. From this time he disappears from the historical record. <a href="/wiki/Albert_of_Aix" title="Albert of Aix">Albert of Aix</a> records that he died in 1131, as prior of a church of the Holy Sepulchre which he had founded in France.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Role_in_preaching_First_Crusade">Role in preaching First Crusade</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Role in preaching First Crusade">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Albert_of_Aix" title="Albert of Aix">Albert of Aix</a> in his <i>Historia Hierosolymitanae Expeditionis</i><sup id="cite_ref-Aix_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Aix-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> claims that Peter the Hermit was the true author and originator of the First Crusade. This story later appears in the pages of <a href="/wiki/William_of_Tyre" title="William of Tyre">William of Tyre</a>, which indicates that even a few generations after the crusade, the descendants of the crusaders believed Peter was its originator. This myth was dismissed by modern historians beginning with <a href="/wiki/Heinrich_von_Sybel" title="Heinrich von Sybel">Heinrich von Sybel</a>.<i><sup id="cite_ref-:02_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:02-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup></i> Various historical sources also recount that during an early visit to Jerusalem sometime before 1096, Jesus appeared to Peter the Hermit in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre" title="Church of the Holy Sepulchre">Church of the Holy Sepulchre</a>, and bade him preach the crusade. The origins of these legend is a matter of some interest. Von Sybel suggested that in the camp of the paupers (which existed side by side with that of the knights, and grew increasingly large as the crusade took a more and more heavy toll on the purses of the crusaders) some idolization of Peter the Hermit had already begun, parallel to the similar glorification of <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_of_Bouillon" title="Godfrey of Bouillon">Godfrey of Bouillon</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Lorraine_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorraine (province)">Lorrainers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is very little concrete record for his life after returning to Europe and much of what is known is speculation or legend. However, Albert of Aix records that he died in 1131, as <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of a church of the Holy Sepulchre which he had founded in France<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas_Dudley_Fosbroke_1843_p_317_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas_Dudley_Fosbroke_1843_p_317-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-William_Hurd_1799_p255_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Hurd_1799_p255-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>.  It is thought that during the Siege of Antioch during the days of famine and cold weather,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Peter attempted to flee only to be captured by the Norman Tancred and placed back on the battlefield in 1097. Peter also held services of intercession for Latin and native recruits. Peter advised Greeks and Latins to form processions as well.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg/220px-Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg/330px-Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg/440px-Tombeau_de_Pierre_l%27ermite_%C3%A0_Huy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2816" data-file-height="2112" /></a><figcaption>The Tomb of Pierre l'ermite</figcaption></figure>
<p>It is generally quoted that he founded an <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> named for the Church of the Holy Sepulchre. However, it was actually in Flanders<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas_Dudley_Fosbroke_1843_p_317_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas_Dudley_Fosbroke_1843_p_317-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-William_Hurd_1799_p255_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Hurd_1799_p255-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> at Neufmoustier near Huy,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> or Huy itself which may have been his home town. His tomb is in <a href="/wiki/Neufmoustier_Abbey" title="Neufmoustier Abbey">Neufmoustier Abbey</a>, so it is presumed that this was his abbey but in another tradition the nearby <a href="/wiki/Soli%C3%A8res_Abbey" title="Solières Abbey">Solières Abbey</a> claims that it was his foundation.
</p><p>Peter's obituary is in the chronicle of Neufmoustier Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> On its page entry of 8 July 1115 the chronicle says that this day saw "the death of Dom Pierre, of pious memory, venerable priest and hermit, who deserved to be appointed by the Lord to announce the first to the holy Cross" and the text continues with "after the conquest of the holy land, Pierre returned to his native country" and also that "he founded this church ... and chooses them a decent burial". This record further supports Neufmoustier's claim as his foundation.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_the_Hermit&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG/170px-0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="258" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG/255px-0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG/340px-0_Saint_Pierre_l%27Ermite_-_Amiens.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1557" data-file-height="2366" /></a><figcaption>A statue of Peter the Hermit in <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Since his death various legends have sprung up around Peter. One legend has its roots in the writings of <a href="/wiki/Jacques_de_Vitry" title="Jacques de Vitry">Jacques de Vitry</a>, who found it convenient to convince people from the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Li%C3%A8ge" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Liège">bishopric of Liège</a> of  the merits of participating in the <a href="/wiki/Albigensian_Crusade" title="Albigensian Crusade">Albigensian Crusade</a> by manipulating the story of Peter.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another legend is given in the 14th century by the French <a href="/wiki/Troubadour" title="Troubadour">troubadour</a> Jehan-de-Bouteiller, who sings the memory of "a dict Peter the Hermit deschendant a count of Clairmont by a Sieur d'Herrymont [who] married a Montagut". Peter the Hermit's parents would, therefore, be Renauld de Hérimont and Aleidis Montaigu (Aleidis is known in Huy as the "mother of Dom Pierre, with a home in Huy").<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also a strong and old tradition that Peter the Hermit was the first to introduce the use of the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">Rosary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Thomas_Dudley_Fosbroke_1843_p_317_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thomas_Dudley_Fosbroke_1843_p_317-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-William_Hurd_1799_p255_20-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-William_Hurd_1799_p255-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> It follows that he began this tradition in about 1090.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
