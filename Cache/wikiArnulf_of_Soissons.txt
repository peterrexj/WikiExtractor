<p><b>Arnold (Arnoul) of <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a></b> or <b>Arnold</b> or <b>Arnulf of <a href="/wiki/Oudenburg" title="Oudenburg">Oudenburg</a></b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (c. 1040–1087) is a saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hops" title="Hops">hop-pickers</a>, Belgian <a href="/wiki/Brewing" title="Brewing">brewers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arnold_of_Soissons&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Arnold, born in <a href="/wiki/Brabant_(landgraviat)" class="mw-redirect" title="Brabant (landgraviat)">Brabant</a>, the son of a certain Fulbertus<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> was first a career soldier before settling at the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/St._Medard%27s_Abbey,_Soissons" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Medard&#39;s Abbey, Soissons">St. Medard's Abbey, Soissons</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. He spent his first three years as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, but later rose to be <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the monastery. His <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> states that he tried to refuse this honor and flee, but was forced by a <a href="/wiki/Wolf" title="Wolf">wolf</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> to return. He then became a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and in 1080, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Soissons" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Soissons">bishop of Soissons</a>, another honor that he sought to avoid. When his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> was occupied by another bishop, rather than fighting, he took the opportunity to retire from public life, founding the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Peter_in_Oudenburg" title="Abbey of St. Peter in Oudenburg">Abbey of St. Peter in Oudenburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As abbot in Oudenburg, Arnold <a href="/wiki/Brewery" title="Brewery">brewed beer</a>, as essential in medieval life as water.  He encouraged local peasants to drink beer, instead of water, due to its "gift of health". During the process of brewing the water was boiled and thus freed of pathogens, making the beer safer to drink.  The beer normally consumed at breakfast and during the day at this time in Europe was called <a href="/wiki/Small_beer" title="Small beer">small beer</a>, having a very low alcohol content, and containing spent yeast. It is likely that people in the local area normally consumed small beer from the monastery, or made their own small beer at the instructions of Arnold and his fellow monks. During one outbreak of sickness, Arnold advised the local people to avoid consuming water, in favor of beer, which advice effectively saved lives.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One miracle tale says, at the time of an epidemic, rather than stand by while the local people fell ill from drinking water, Arnold had them consume his monastery brews. Because of this, many people in his church survived the plague.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> This same story is also told of <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_of_Metz" title="Arnulf of Metz">Arnulf or Arnold of Metz</a>, another patron of brewers.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arnold_of_Soissons&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are many depictions of St. Arnold with a <a href="/wiki/Mash_rake" title="Mash rake">mashing rake</a> in his hand, to identify him.<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> For example, the label on "Steenbrugge Abbey" beers has a picture of St Arnold holding a mash rake.<sup id="cite_ref-amdt_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-amdt-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Arnold is honoured in July with a parade in Brussels on the "Day of Beer."<sup id="cite_ref-dcb_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dcb-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">Miracles</a> that were reported at his tomb were investigated and approved by a council at <a href="/wiki/Beauvais" title="Beauvais">Beauvais</a> in 1121; in 1131 Arnold's relics were translated to the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Church_of_St._Peter,_Oldenburg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Church of St. Peter, Oldenburg (page does not exist)">Church of St. Peter</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter_(Oldenburg)" class="extiw" title="de:St. Peter (Oldenburg)">de</a>&#93;</span> in <a href="/wiki/Aldenburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldenburg">Aldenburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> St. Arnold's feast day is 14 August.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
