<p><b>Madeleine Sophie Barat</b>, RSCJ, (12 December 1779 – 25 May 1865), was a <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> who founded the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Society of the Sacred Heart">Society of the Sacred Heart</a>, a worldwide <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> of educators.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_family">Early life and family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Barat was born on the night of 12 December 1779, in <a href="/wiki/Joigny" title="Joigny">Joigny</a>, <a href="/wiki/Early_modern_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Early modern France">France</a>, next door to a house <a href="/wiki/Fire" title="Fire">fire</a> at a neighbor's home. The stress and the terror of the fire caused Sophie's mother, Madeleine Fouffé Barat (1740–1822), then pregnant with her third child, to go into labour. Born two months premature, Madeleine Sophie was considered so fragile that she was baptised early the next morning in Sainy Thibault Church, just a few yards from the Barat family home. Although her parents had arranged <a href="/wiki/Godparent" title="Godparent">godparents</a> in advance, there was no time to call them to the church and so at five o'clock on the morning of 13 December 1779, Louise-Sophie Cédor, a local woman on her way to early Mass, and Sophie's older brother, Louis, stood in as her godparents.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Barat was born into a financially-comfortable family whose ancestors had lived in Joigny for generations and were proud of their roots in the region. Her father, Jacques Barat (1742–1809), was a <a href="/wiki/Cooper_(profession)" title="Cooper (profession)">cooper</a> and vine-grower.<sup id="cite_ref-network_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-network-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Both professions were respected trades, with centuries of French culture and spirituality behind them. The Barats were <a href="/wiki/Jansenist" class="mw-redirect" title="Jansenist">Jansenist</a> Catholics, which is often said to have shaped Sophie's spirituality profoundly.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
She was always willing to help people in need and to pray for others.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Barat's older brother Louis was a serious boy and a brilliant student. Their parents encouraged his interest in studies and employed a tutor for him at home. Shortly after entering the Collège Saint-Jacques in Joigny at the age of nine, Louis decided to become a Catholic priest. In 1784, at the age of 16, Louis left Joigny to begin his studies for the priesthood at the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> at <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a>. Louis was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, but because he was too young to be ordained, he was obliged to return home until he was 21. Louis became a teacher of mathematics at his old school and decided to take on Sophie's education. He taught her Latin, Greek, history, natural science, Spanish, and Italian providing Sophie with an education that was rarely available to young women and girls at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="French_Revolution">French Revolution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: French Revolution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>In 1789, Louis Barat became involved in the debate surrounding the <a href="/wiki/Civil_Constitution_of_the_Clergy" title="Civil Constitution of the Clergy">Civil Constitution of the Clergy</a>, passed in July 1790 which required all priests to swear allegiance to the new revolutionary state. Louis took the oath of loyalty in January 1791, but on learning that the pope had condemned the Constitution, he renounced his oath in May 1792. That renunciation had immediate consequences. Louis first tried to hide in his family's attic but soon fled to Paris, for the danger had become too great both for himself and his family. In Paris, he was arrested in May 1793, imprisoned there for two years and escaped the <a href="/wiki/Guillotine" title="Guillotine">guillotine</a> only through the brave intervention of a friend.
</p><p>After her brother was released in 1795, he briefly returned home to Joigny. Louis then went back to Paris to seek <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordination</a> and exercise his <a href="/wiki/Christian_ministry" title="Christian ministry">ministry</a> in secret.  He brought Sophie with him to further her education.<sup id="cite_ref-network_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-network-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After arriving in Paris, Sophie and Louis lived in a safe house belonging to one Madame Duval. Sophie worked as a seamstress and became an excellent embroidress.  Louis continued to say <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> and teach Sophie the <a href="/wiki/Fathers_of_the_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Fathers of the Church">Fathers of the Church</a>, mathematics, Latin, and the Scriptures. While living in Paris, at about the age of 18, Sophie decided to become a Carmelite <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>. That would be impossible, however, for the <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelites</a> had, along with many other religious communities, been abolished in 1790. For five years, she lived in Paris a life of prayer and study and taught catechism in secret to the children of <a href="/wiki/The_Marais" title="The Marais">The Marais</a> quarter. In 1800, Sophie briefly returned home to help her family with the vine harvest.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founding_of_Society_of_the_Sacred_Heart">Founding of Society of the Sacred Heart</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Founding of Society of the Sacred Heart">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When Barat returned to Paris, she was introduced to <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Varin" class="extiw" title="fr:Joseph Varin">Joseph Varin</a>. He wanted to found a religious order of women devoted to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred Heart of Jesus">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> and involved in the education of young women that would complement the work of the Fathers of the Faith. On 21 November 1800, at the age of 20, she abandoned her dream of becoming a Carmelite and, along with three other women living in the Paris safe-house, took her <a href="/wiki/Vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Vows">vows</a> as one of the first members of this new <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a>, marking the foundation of the Society of the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, because the French authorities had prohibited <a href="/wiki/Catholic_devotions" title="Catholic devotions">devotion</a> to the Sacred Heart of Jesus, the society was initially known as <i>Dames de la Foi</i> ("Women of Faith") or <i>de l’Instruction chrétienne</i> ("Christian instructors").
</p><p>The first school was opened in <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a> in northern France in September 1801 and Sophie travelled to this important provincial city in order to teach. Sophie made her vows, 7 June 1802.<sup id="cite_ref-Power_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Power-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The new community and school grew quickly. A school giving classes to the poor of the town was opened. In December 1802, at the age of twenty-three, Sophie became Superior of the Society of the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-network_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-network-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Expansion">Expansion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Expansion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In November 1804, Barat traveled to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Sainte-Marie-d%E2%80%99en-Haut&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sainte-Marie-d’en-Haut (page does not exist)">Sainte-Marie-d’en-Haut</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Grenoble" title="Grenoble">Grenoble</a>, in southeastern France, to receive a community of <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Visitation_of_Holy_Mary" title="Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary">Visitation nuns</a> into the Society. Among them was <a href="/wiki/Philippine_Duchesne" class="mw-redirect" title="Philippine Duchesne">Philippine Duchesne</a>, who would later introduce the Society to America and was canonized a saint in 1988. A second school was then established at Grenoble,<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> followed by a third at <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a> in western France. Father Varin envisioned an entire network of such schools and, after the first establishments in France, foundations were established in North America (1818), Italy (1828), Switzerland (1830), Belgium (1834), Algiers (1841), England (1842), Ireland (1842), Spain (1846), Holland (1848), Germany (1851), South America (1853), Austria (1853) and Poland (1857).
</p><p>Barat is credited with the twofold gift of intuition in the choice of persons fitted for office and trust of those in responsible posts. New foundations were always entrusted to other hands.<sup id="cite_ref-Power_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Power-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In January 1806, Barat was elected <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> of the Society of the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-uscanada_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-uscanada-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1820, she called all if the superiors together in a council at Paris to establish a uniform course of studies for the quickly expanding network of Sacred Heart schools.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The studies were to be serious, to cultivate the mind, and to create young women who would be devoted to the Sacred Heart of Jesus and perform good deeds in God's name. As foundations continued to multiply, Sophie saw the need for a greater degree of unity and sought the approval of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. By 1826, the Society of the Sacred Heart had received its decree of approbation from Rome.
</p><p>In 1832 Barat founded at Lyons the "Congregation of the Children of Mary" for former pupils and other ladies.<sup id="cite_ref-Power_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Power-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1840, Barat averted a potential <a href="/wiki/Schism_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Schism (religion)">schism</a> between the Vatican and the Archbishop of Paris. All of her sisters pressured her to choose sides, Sophie refused to do so and was able to heal the breach. 
</p>
<pre>Over the course of her 65 years as the superior general, Sophie and her Society survived the regime of <a href="/wiki/Napoleon" title="Napoleon">Napoleon</a>, saw France undergo two more revolutions, and witnessed Italy's struggle to become a full-fledged nation.
</pre>
<p>The Sacred Heart schools quickly earned an excellent reputation.,She dreamed of educating all children regardless of their parents' financial means. For almost every new school established, a corresponding “free” school was opened to provide the poorer children of the area with a high-quality education, which they would not otherwise have received.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Barat-incor.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Barat-incor.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Barat-incor.jpg/220px-Barat-incor.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="153" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Barat-incor.jpg/330px-Barat-incor.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9c/Barat-incor.jpg 2x" data-file-width="440" data-file-height="305" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Barat-incor.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div></div></div></div>
<p>During her 65-year leadership, the Society of the Sacred Heart grew to include more than 3,500 members educating women in Europe, North Africa, North and South America.<sup id="cite_ref-society_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-society-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Barat died at the general motherhouse in Paris on <a href="/wiki/Ascension_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascension Day">Ascension Day</a>, May 25, 1865.  In 1879, she was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a> and was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on May 24, 1908. On May 24, 1925, she was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by Pope Pius XI.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Barat's mortal remains are located in an ornate <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Fran%C3%A7ois-Xavier,_Paris" title="Saint-François-Xavier, Paris">Saint-François-Xavier, Paris</a>.
</p><p>Barat was known to refuse to have her photograph or portrait taken, and it had been believed that no portrait of her existed from her lifetime,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> but there is photograph of her on her deathbed. In 1992, a portrait was discovered during a restoration at the convent of Sante Rufina e Seconda in Rome by the Sisters of the Immaculate Conception of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Ivrea" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Ivrea">Ivrea</a>. It is believed to have been painted prior to her beatification and sent from Italy to the Mother House in Paris in 1879. It currently hangs in the General Archives of the Society of the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of Barat's earliest biographers was <a href="/wiki/Louis_Baunard" title="Louis Baunard">Louis Baunard</a>, who wrote <i>Histoire de la vénérable Mère Madeleine-Sophie Barat, fondatrice de la Société du Sacré-Cœur de Jésus</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quotes">Quotes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Quotes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>“We don’t live with angels; we have to put up with human nature and forgive it.”
</p><p>“Before making any change take counsel.... Prudence and a wise slowness are necessary in the beginning.”
</p><p>“More is gained by indulgence than by severity.”
</p><p>"Be humble, be simple, bring joy to others."<sup id="cite_ref-quotes_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quotes-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"For the sake of one child, I would have founded the Society."
</p><p>"Your example, even more than your words, will be an eloquent lesson to the world."
</p><p>"Give only good example to the children; never correct them when out of humor or impatient. We must win them by an appeal to their piety and to their hearts. Soften your reprimands with kind words; encourage and reward them. That is, in short, our way of educating."
</p><p>"Let us leave acts, not words. Nobody will have time to read us."<sup id="cite_ref-quotes_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-quotes-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>“It was her way to think well of people until forced to do otherwise.”
</p><p>“She loved people through their faults to the core of their best selves.”
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG/220px-Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="333" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG/330px-Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG/440px-Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3044" data-file-height="4608" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-Thibault_Vitrail_Barat.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Stained glass window in Saint Thibault Church, in <a href="/wiki/Joigny" title="Joigny">Joigny</a>, where Barat was baptized in 1779.</div></div></div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/List_of_Schools_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="List of Schools of the Sacred Heart">List of Schools of the Sacred Heart</a></div>
<p>A number of Barat's students founded their own religious congregations. <a href="/wiki/Eug%C3%A9nie_Smet" class="mw-redirect" title="Eugénie Smet">Eugénie Smet</a>, a pupil of Lille, founded the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_the_Helpers_of_the_Holy_Souls" title="Society of the Helpers of the Holy Souls">Society of the Helpers of the Holy Souls</a>. Anne de Meeûs, a pupil in Paris, wrote the constitutions of her congregation of <a href="/wiki/Religious_of_Perpetual_Adoration" title="Religious of Perpetual Adoration">Religious of Perpetual Adoration</a> at the little Château in Jette. <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henriette_Le_Forestier_d%27Osseville" class="extiw" title="fr:Henriette Le Forestier d&#39;Osseville">Henriette d'Osseville</a>, a pupil in Paris, founded the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Our_Lady_of_Fidelity" title="Sisters of Our Lady of Fidelity">Sisters of Our Lady of Fidelity</a>.
</p><p>The legacy of Barat can be found in the more than 100 schools operated by her Society of the Sacred Heart, institutions known for the quality of the education made available to the young. She founded the Society of the Sacred Heart, which would focus on schools for the poor as well as boarding schools for young women of means. Today, <a href="/wiki/Co-educational" class="mw-redirect" title="Co-educational">co-educational</a> Sacred Heart schools can be found as well as schools exclusively for boys.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Sophie Barat School in <a href="/wiki/Hamburg" title="Hamburg">Hamburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, an independent non-fee-paying co-educational grammar school, is run by the Society of the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Barat_College" title="Barat College">Barat College</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Lake_Forest,_Illinois" title="Lake Forest, Illinois">Lake Forest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Illinois" title="Illinois">Illinois</a>, the descendant of the Academy of the Sacred Heart, was founded on Wabash Avenue in 1858 and relocated to Lake Forest in 1904. It received its charter from the State of Illinois in 1918.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Barat College merged with <a href="/wiki/DePaul_University" title="DePaul University">DePaul University</a> in 2001 and was closed in 2005.
</p><p>The Barat Education Foundation, an independent nonprofit organization, was established in 2000 prior to the sale of Barat College to DePaul University. At the time, the Foundation was charged with the development and support of innovative educational programs and services that reflected the values and educational tradition of Barat College. When DePaul University closed Barat College in June 2005, the Board of Directors voted to perpetuate and build upon the Barat legacy of education, leadership and advocacy. Today, the Barat Education Foundation is committed to continuing and adapting the heritage and legacy of Barat College to the 21st-century world.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Madeleine_Sophie_Barat_Tribute_and_Memorial_Scholarship&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Madeleine Sophie Barat Tribute and Memorial Scholarship (page does not exist)">Saint Madeleine Sophie Barat Tribute and Memorial Scholarship</a> is available annually to two new or returning students attending <a href="/wiki/Oak_Hill_School" title="Oak Hill School">Oak Hill School</a> in <a href="/wiki/St._Louis" title="St. Louis">St. Louis</a>, <a href="/wiki/Missouri" title="Missouri">Missouri</a>. The scholarship is funded by tribute and memorial contributions, and the amount of assistance varies each year.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Madeleine_Sophie_Barat&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Sophie_the_Giraffe" title="Sophie the Giraffe">Sophie the Giraffe</a>, a French children's toy, was named after Madeleine Sophie Barat because the first toy went into production on May 25, 1961, St. Sophie's day.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
