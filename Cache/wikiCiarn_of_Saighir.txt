<p><b>Ciarán of Saigir</b><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Pronunciation" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Pronunciation"><span title="IPA transcription needed for this word. You can help!">needs  IPA</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> (5th century&#160;&#8211; <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;530</span>), also known as <b>Ciarán mac Luaigne</b> or <b>Saint Kieran</b> (<a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Cieran</i>), was one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a><sup id="cite_ref-Flood_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flood-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and is considered the first saint to have been born in Ireland,<sup id="cite_ref-conline_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-conline-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> although the legend that he preceded <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a> is questionable. Ciarán was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ossory" title="Bishop of Ossory">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saighir" title="Saighir">Saighir</a> (Seir-Kieran) and remains the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of its successor, the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Ossory" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Ossory">diocese of Ossory</a>.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 5 March.  He is sometimes called <b>Saint Ciarán&#160;the&#160;Elder</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Kyaranus</i> or <i>Ciaranus Maior</i>) to distinguish him from the other 6th-century Irish <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Ciarán the Younger">Saint Ciarán</a>, who was <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Abbot of Clonmacnoise">abbot of Clonmacnoise</a>.  He shares the feast date of 5 March with his mother, St. Liadán, and his disciple and episcopal successor, St. <a href="/wiki/Carthage_the_Elder" title="Carthage the Elder">Carthach the Elder</a>.
</p><p>His identity is sometimes equated with that of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran" title="Saint Piran">Saint Piran</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Ciaran_window.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/St_Ciaran_window.jpg/220px-St_Ciaran_window.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="764" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/St_Ciaran_window.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="259" data-file-height="900" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Ciaran_window.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ciarán of Saigir depicted in <a href="/wiki/Stained_glass" title="Stained glass">stained glass</a>, <a href="/wiki/St_Mary%27s_Cathedral,_Kilkenny" title="St Mary&#39;s Cathedral, Kilkenny">St Mary's Cathedral, Kilkenny</a></div></div></div>
<p>Various medieval traditions about the saint are recorded in a number of <a href="/wiki/Hagiographic" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographic">hagiographic</a> works: two <i>Lives</i> in Latin, both of uncertain date, and two <i>Lives</i> in Irish. The shortest Latin <i>Life</i> is preserved in the <i><a href="/wiki/Codex_Salmanticensis" title="Codex Salmanticensis">Codex Salmanticensis</a></i>, while the longer one is found in the Codex Kilkenniensis. The latter was rendered into Irish and a second Irish <i>Life</i> was produced after the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a>. The latter, though the latest of the four, is thought to draw on the oldest traditions when it deals sympathetically with the <a href="/wiki/Osraige" title="Osraige">Osraige</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Primarily intended to edify, educate and entertain, it is unclear to what extent they are an accurate representation of events.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_background_and_early_career">Family background and early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family background and early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>, notably the <i>Félire Óengusso</i>, and medieval Irish genealogies identify Ciarán's father as Lugna (also Laighne), a nobleman of the <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_Birn" title="Dál Birn">Dál Birn</a> rulers of <a href="/wiki/Osraige" title="Osraige">Osraige</a>, and his mother as Liadán, of the <a href="/wiki/Corcu_Lo%C3%ADgde" title="Corcu Loígde">Corcu Loígde</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
Before he was conceived Ciarán's mother had a dream that a star fell into her mouth. She related this dream to the druids who were knowledgeable of such things, and they told her that she would bear a son whose fame and virtues would be known as far as the world's end.<sup id="cite_ref-oconor_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oconor-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Cape_Clear_Island" title="Cape Clear Island">Cape Clear Island</a> south west of <a href="/wiki/County_Cork" title="County Cork">County Cork</a> is regarded as his birthplace and it is said that a church was built by him on the island.
</p><p>Ciarán's biography is full of obscurities. It is commonly said, however, that he left Ireland before the arrival of St Patrick. Already a Christian, and of royal Osraige blood, he had determined to study for the Church; hence, he secured an education at <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-schaff_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaff-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_Saighir">Foundation of Saighir</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundation of Saighir">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By one account Patrick sent Ciarán to precede him and directed him to build a monastery at the site of a well. When Ciarán asked how he should find this well, Patrick gave him a little bell, that would not ring until he reached the well.<sup id="cite_ref-oconor_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oconor-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On his return from Rome, he built himself a little cell in the woods of <a href="/wiki/Upper_Ossory" title="Upper Ossory">Upper Ossory</a>. He settled as a hermit at <a href="/wiki/Saighir" title="Saighir">Saighir</a> (alternately called <i><a href="/wiki/Saighir" title="Saighir">Seir Kieran</a></i>, or just <i>Seir</i>) near to the <a href="/wiki/Slieve_Bloom_Mountains" title="Slieve Bloom Mountains">Slieve Bloom Mountains</a>, but soon disciples were attracted to him and a large monastery grew up round his cell, which became the chosen burial place for the <a href="/wiki/Kings_of_Osraige" title="Kings of Osraige">Kings of Osraige</a>. A tradition shared by all four <i>Lives</i> describes Ciarán as a holy man wearing skins, whose first pupils are animals in the forest.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>This corresponds to the image of him as a Western <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>, wearing skins and dwelling in the wilderness, seemingly as a <a href="/wiki/Precursor_(religion)" title="Precursor (religion)">forerunner</a> to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a> as John was to <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>. His mother, Liadan, is said to have gone to Saighir with a group of women who devoted their lives to the service of God and the members of her son's community.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sier Kieran became the chief church of the Osraighe, a center for the preaching of the Gospel and a large industrial community noted for its wealth.<sup id="cite_ref-schaff_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaff-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It was superseded by the later monastic foundation of St <a href="/wiki/Cainnech_of_Aghaboe" title="Cainnech of Aghaboe">Canice</a> at nearby Aghaboe.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pre-Patrician_Arrival">Pre-Patrician Arrival</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Pre-Patrician Arrival">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Like the saints <a href="/wiki/Ailbe_of_Emly" title="Ailbe of Emly">Ailbe of Emly</a>, <a href="/wiki/Decl%C3%A1n_of_Ardmore" title="Declán of Ardmore">Declán of Ardmore</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A1n" title="Abbán">Abbán</a>, Ciarán is credited with a pre-Patrician career in Munster, though the <i>Lives</i> hardly refer to these putative contemporaries. This tradition may reflect interaction with Christians of south Wales before St Patrick came to Ireland.
</p><p>Ciarán is said to have met Patrick in Italy and made allegiance to him. Some writers say that when <a href="/wiki/St._Patrick" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Patrick">St. Patrick</a> arrived in Ireland, Ciarán was already a bishop, having been ordained while on the continent. It seems more likely, however, that he was one of the twelve men that Patrick, on his arrival, consecrated as helpers.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He became the first <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Ossory" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Ossory">bishop of Ossory</a>.
</p><p>There is long-standing academic disagreement in the dating of the life of St. Ciarán of Saighir. Traditional Irish sources (his vitae, the <a href="/wiki/F%C3%A9lire_%C3%93engusso" class="mw-redirect" title="Félire Óengusso">Félire Óengusso</a>, etc.) ascribe his missionary activity as before St Patrick, but assign no dates to his life. If true, he would have likely been born somewhere near the end of the 4th century and evangelizing in the 5th, and some writers accept this (Plummer, Hogan, Kenny).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>According to W. O'Halloran, the <i><a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Inisfallen" title="Annals of Inisfallen">Annals of Inisfallen</a></i> have Ciarán born at <a href="/wiki/Cape_Clear_Island" title="Cape Clear Island">Cape Clear</a> in Cork in 352.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Others such as Baring-Gould, Sharpe, O'Riain, and Sperber push his life forward variously into the 5th and even 6th centuries. <a href="/wiki/John_Lanigan_(historian)" title="John Lanigan (historian)">Lanigan</a> and Leslie Stephen place him in the 5th century, based on anecdotes that make him a contemporary of Ciarán of Clonmacnoise, <a href="/wiki/Brendan_of_Birr" title="Brendan of Birr">Brendan of Birr</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Brendan_of_Clonfert" class="mw-redirect" title="Brendan of Clonfert">Brendan of Clonfert</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Lanigan suggests that Ciarán of Saighir was one of <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian of Clonard</a>'s first students, and indicates that he was likely bishop sometime prior to 544.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Legends attribute remarkable miracles to Ciarán.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> One day when Ciarán was still yet a child he made a beginning of his miracles; for in the air right over him a kite came soaring and, swooping down before his face, lifted a little bird that sat upon her nest. Compassion for the little bird took Ciarán, and he deemed it an ill thing to see it in such plight; thereupon the kite turned back and in front of him deposited the bird half dead, sore hurt; but Ciarán bade it rise and be whole. The bird arose, and went whole upon its nest again.<sup id="cite_ref-oconor_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oconor-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One such relates how the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Justices_(Ireland)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Justices (Ireland)">Lord Justice</a> of Ireland, <a href="/wiki/Riste%C3%A1rd_de_Ti%C3%BAit" title="Risteárd de Tiúit">Risteárd de Tiúit</a>, went to Athlone, with the intention of sending his brothers to Limerick, Waterford, and Wexford, that he himself might reside in Dublin and Athlone (alternately); but it happened, through the miracles of God, St Peter and St Ciarán, that some of the stones of the castle of Athlone fell upon his head, killing him, his priest and a number of his people.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is reputed in the Lives as  having miraculously performed abortion in a raped nun called Bruinnech.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The Catholic News Agency cites  <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Charles-Edwards" title="Thomas Charles-Edwards">Thomas Charles-Edwards</a>, "...[t]hese accounts need to be put in context. In these examples the saint's intervention is directed towards restoring the honor of the woman concerned. ...The evidence of saints’ lives concerns miracles as conceived by later hagiographers. It is usually bad evidence for what they actually did, better evidence for what later writers could imagine happening.”<sup id="cite_ref-jones_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jones-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Maeve Callan suggest that abortion was seen as a lesser sin at the time. <sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another story is that he blessed a well so that “it had the taste of wine or honey for everyone who drank it got drunk as well as filled”.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Folklore also relates many charming tales of St Ciarán's influence on wild animals.  Tales tell of a fox, badger and wolf who worked with Ciarán and his monks to cut wood and build huts for the brothers.  One day the fox stole Ciarán's shoes; upon which Ciarán ordered the badger to retrieve them. The badger found the fox, and bound him from head to tail, returning him to his master; the saint ordered the fox to repent for his sin as a monk would, and to return to his tasks as before.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Scholia in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Oengus" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrology of Oengus">Martyrology of Oengus</a> states that he foretold of the sanctity of Conall and <a href="/wiki/Fachtna_of_Rosscarbery" title="Fachtna of Rosscarbery">Fachtna of Rosscarbery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Ciarán's date of death is uncertain but he is thought to have died at an advanced age from natural causes.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Saigir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The ruins of Ciarán's monastery - which were long the burial place of the <a href="/wiki/Kings_of_Osraige" title="Kings of Osraige">Kings of Osraige</a> - still remain to this day. He is also associated with <a href="/wiki/St._Kieran%27s_Church" title="St. Kieran&#39;s Church">a monastic site</a> near <a href="/wiki/Errill" title="Errill">Errill</a>. Another site exists at the island of <a href="/wiki/Cape_Clear_Island" title="Cape Clear Island">Cape Clear</a>, which is said to have been his birthplace and the hermitage of his youth. Church ruins and a well exist here of considerable age.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Saint Ciarán is venerated in England, Brittany, Wales, and Scotland, on 5 March.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>  <a href="/wiki/St._Kieran%27s_College" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Kieran&#39;s College">St. Kieran's College</a> (est. 1782) is the oldest Roman Catholic secondary school in Ireland, and is named for the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is sometimes listed as one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>, although in the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Oengus" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrology of Oengus">Martyrology of Oengus</a>, Ciarán of Saighir is not enumerated as such, and his association with the students of St. Finnian may be a persistent confusion.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Ciarán of Saigir was the subject of <a href="/w/index.php?title=New_Hagiography&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="New Hagiography (page does not exist)">New Hagiography</a>'s 5 March 2018 release of "Mr. Fox Felt Really Bad"; a reference to the stealing of the saint's leather shoe by one of his first vulpine monastic recruits.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is traditionally identified with the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Piran" title="Saint Piran">Saint Piran</a> who is venerated in Cornwall, Wales, and Brittany.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNBMunster_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNBMunster-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-schaff_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-schaff-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> although <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A1draig_%C3%93_Riain" title="Pádraig Ó Riain">Pádraig Ó Riain</a> considers this "groundless".<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
