<p><b>Scholastica</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;480</span> – 10 February AD 543) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>. She was born in Italy, and a ninth-century tradition makes her the twin sister of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">Saint Benedict of Nursia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-engs_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-engs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is 10 February, Saint Scholastica's Day.<sup id="cite_ref-Mazar2000_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mazar2000-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Scholastica is traditionally regarded as the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictine nuns</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Scholastica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the late 6th century <i><a href="/wiki/Dialogues_(Pope_Gregory)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dialogues (Pope Gregory)">Dialogues</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, Scholastica was born c. 480 in <a href="/wiki/Nursia" class="mw-redirect" title="Nursia">Nursia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, of wealthy parents (Anicius Eupropius and his wife Claudia Abondantia Reguardati). While Gregory states that Scholastica was Benedict's sister, a later tradition says she was his twin (whether this is meant biologically or spiritually, or both is unclear). Gregory also says she was dedicated to God from a young age. She and her brother Benedict were brought up together until the time he left to pursue studies in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>A young Roman woman of Scholastica's class and time would likely have remained in her father's house until marriage (likely arranged) or entry into consecrated life. On occasion several <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">consecrated virgins</a> would live together in a household and form a community.<sup id="cite_ref-engs_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-engs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benedictine tradition holds that Scholastica established a hermitage about five miles from <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a> and that this was the first convent of Benedictine nuns.<sup id="cite_ref-osb_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-osb-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, it is possible that Scholastica lived in a hermitage with one or two other consecrated virgins in a cluster of houses at the base of Mount Cassino where there is an ancient church under her patronage Monastero di Santa Scolastica. <a href="/wiki/Ruth_Clifford_Engs" class="mw-redirect" title="Ruth Clifford Engs">Ruth Clifford Engs</a> notes that since <i>Dialogues</i> indicates that Scholastica was dedicated to God at an early age, perhaps she lived in her father's house with other religious women until his death and then moved nearer to Benedict.<sup id="cite_ref-engs_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-engs-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative_from_the_Dialogues">Narrative from the <i>Dialogues</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Scholastica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Narrative from the Dialogues">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg/240px-Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg/360px-Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg/480px-Klosterkirche_Elchingen_10.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3449" data-file-height="2313" /></a><figcaption>Saints Benedict and Scholastica in conversation, Klosterkirche Elchingen</figcaption></figure>
<p>The most commonly told story about her is that Scholastica would, once a year, go and visit her brother at a place near his abbey, and they would spend the day worshiping together and discussing sacred texts and issues.<sup id="cite_ref-Gregory_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gregory-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One day they had supper and continued their conversation. When Benedict indicated it was time for him to leave, Scholastica, perhaps sensing that the time of her death was drawing near, asked him to stay with her for the evening so they could continue their discussions. Not wishing to break <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">his own Rule</a>, Benedict refused, insisting that he needed to return to his cell. At that point, Scholastica closed her hands in prayer, and after a moment, a wild storm started outside of the guest house in which they were staying. Benedict asked, "What have you done?", to which she replied, "I asked you and you would not listen; so I asked my God and he did listen. So now go off, if you can; leave me and return to your monastery." Benedict was unable to return to his monastery, and they spent the night in discussion.<sup id="cite_ref-Gregory_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gregory-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Three days later, from his cell, he saw his sister's <a href="/wiki/Soul_(spirit)" class="mw-redirect" title="Soul (spirit)">soul</a> leaving the earth and ascending to heaven in the form of a shining white dove.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Benedict had her body brought to his monastery, where he had it laid in the tomb which he had prepared for himself.<sup id="cite_ref-Gregory_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gregory-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Anglo-Saxon bishop and scholar <a href="/wiki/Aldhelm" title="Aldhelm">Aldhelm</a> recounts the story in both the <i>De Laude Virginitatis</i>, written for the nuns at <a href="/wiki/Barking_Abbey" title="Barking Abbey">Barking</a>, and in the shorter <i>Carmen de virginitate</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Studies">Studies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Scholastica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Studies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>What is known of Scholastica derives from the <i>Dialogues</i> of Gregory the Great. Early calendars and place names in the area around Monte Cassino support the historical accuracy of St. Gregory the Great's account of her life.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Gregory names as his sources four of Benedict's contemporaries. A contemporary, <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_of_Arles" title="Caesarius of Arles">Caesarius of Arles</a>, wrote the <i>Regula virginum</i> (Rule for Virgins), a rule drawn up for virgins living in community, for a community which was headed by his sister, Caesaria.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Scholastica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Scholastica is the patron saint of Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>, education, and <a href="/wiki/Convulsion" title="Convulsion">convulsive</a> children, and is invoked against storms and rain. 
Her feast is celebrated on 10 February.<sup id="cite_ref-Mazar2000_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mazar2000-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Saint Scholastica's Day bears special importance in the Benedictine monastic calendar.<sup id="cite_ref-Webber2008_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webber2008-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In iconography, Scholastica is represented in a Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a>, often as an <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>, and holding the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Benedict" title="Rule of Saint Benedict">Rule of Saint Benedict</a>, with a crucifix or an ascending dove.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Scholastica was selected as the main motif for a high value commemorative coin: the <a href="/wiki/Euro_gold_and_silver_commemorative_coins_(Austria)#2002_coinage" title="Euro gold and silver commemorative coins (Austria)">Austria €50 'The Christian Religious Orders'</a>, issued 13 March 2002. On the obverse (head) side of the coin Scholastica is depicted alongside Benedict. 
</p><p>Scholastica is honored on the calendars of the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> on 10 February. The calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Aachen" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Aachen">Diocese of Aachen</a> has an additional feast of the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translation of Scholastica's relics</a> on 6 February, the <a href="/wiki/Premonstratensians" title="Premonstratensians">Premonstratensians</a> commemorate her on 9 February.
</p>
