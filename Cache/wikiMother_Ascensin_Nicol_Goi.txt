<p><b>María Ascensión Nicol y Goñi</b>, <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">O.P.</a>, (14 March 1868 &#8211; 24 February 1940) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sister</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Third Order of St. Dominic</a>. She co-founded and was the first <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">Prioress</a> General of the <b>Congregation of Dominican Missionary Sisters of the Rosary</b>, which she helped to found in <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ascensi%C3%B3n_Nicol_y_Go%C3%B1i&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ascensi%C3%B3n_Nicol_y_Go%C3%B1i&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>She was born <b>Florentina Nicol y Goñi</b> on 14 March 1868 in <a href="/wiki/Tafalla" title="Tafalla">Tafalla</a>, <a href="/wiki/Navarre" title="Navarre">Navarre</a>, the youngest of the four daughters of Juan Nicol y Zalduendo, a shopkeeper specializing in farming items, and of Águeda Goñi y Vidal, who died in 1872. As a child, she had many duties, including helping her family with the household chores.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1878 a cousin of her father, who was a <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">cloistered</a> <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, offered to educate his middle two daughters at the boarding school of her monastery, the oldest having already married. Agreeing, he sent the girls to study. They later entered the monastic community themselves as Carmelite nuns.<sup id="cite_ref-MDO_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In December 1881 Nicol was enrolled by her father at the boarding school of the Beaterio (Convent) of Santa Rosa in <a href="/wiki/Huesca" title="Huesca">Huesca</a>, a religious community of <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">cloistered Sisters</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Third Order of St. Dominic</a>, which was considered a prestigious school in the region. It was there that she was able to experience for herself the <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated life (Catholic Church)">religious life</a>, which raised questions in her mind about her future. Her father and stepmother withdrew her from the school in February 1883, considering that she had received sufficient education for a female. During that time, however, she had felt called to join the Dominican Sisters who had taught her, but she returned home to reflect on her choices.<sup id="cite_ref-MDO_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By the following October Nicol had received the permission of her father to enter the convent. He then took her back to Huesca, where they visited her two sisters who were Carmelite nuns, after which she entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> of the beaterio. In 1886 she professed <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>, taking the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of <b>Mary Ascension of the Sacred Heart</b>. She then became a teacher at that school and served in that capacity for the next 27 years. Under the <a href="/wiki/Anti-clerical" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-clerical">anti-clerical</a> laws promulgated in the early 20th century, however, the Spanish government took over the school and expelled the Sisters in 1913.<sup id="cite_ref-MDR_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Deprived of their traditional ministry, the Sisters decided to act on a proposal they had long considered, namely, <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> service, about which they had learned from the periodicals issued by various missionary congregations. They wrote to <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> authorities in both America and the Philippines, seeking a field where they could help the poorest of the poor. The response came from the <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> Ramón Zubieta, a former missionary in the Philippines  who had just been appointed by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicar" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicar">Apostolic Vicar</a> of a new <a href="/wiki/Vicariate" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicariate">Vicariate</a> in the Peruvian <a href="/wiki/Amazons" title="Amazons">amazons</a>. The friar had traveled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for his <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">consecration</a> as a bishop. After his stay in Rome, he stopped in Huesca to speak with the Sisters to see if he would be able to get some of the community to help in his new responsibility. Five of the Sisters who volunteered for this mission were chosen, and Nicol was chosen to lead them.<sup id="cite_ref-MDR_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="New_horizons">New horizons</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ascensi%C3%B3n_Nicol_y_Go%C3%B1i&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: New horizons">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The five Dominican Sisters left Huesca during November 1913, accompanied by the bishop and three other friars who were to serve his territory, landing in Peru at the Port of <a href="/wiki/Callao" title="Callao">Callao</a> on 30 December 1913. When they arrived in Lima, they were given hospitality by the Dominican Sisters of the convent at the Shrine of Our Lady of the Patronage (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Patrocinio</i>), of which they took possession the following year. After a two-year period of cultural <a href="/wiki/Acclimation" class="mw-redirect" title="Acclimation">acclimation</a> and preparation for the mission, Nicol set off in 1915 with two other Sisters for their final destination in the mountain forests. After a journey of 24 days crossing the Andes to a region where white women had never before traveled, they arrived in <a href="/wiki/Puerto_Maldonado" title="Puerto Maldonado">Puerto Maldonado</a>, a small village in the <a href="/wiki/Amazon_basin" title="Amazon basin">Amazon basin</a>, situated between two large rivers, the <a href="/wiki/Madre_de_Dios_River" title="Madre de Dios River">Madre de Dios</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Tambopata_River" title="Tambopata River">Tambopata</a>, along which all communication took place.<sup id="cite_ref-MDR_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Within three days of their arrival, the Sisters began teaching the girls of the region and started the construction of a school. Soon girls from the <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas" title="Indigenous peoples of the Americas">indigenous</a> Baraya <a href="/wiki/Tribe" title="Tribe">tribe</a> starting coming from the forest to receive the education they offered. Nicol made it clear that they would be welcome in their classrooms. This was despite the hostility of the white plantation workers who formed the population of the town.<sup id="cite_ref-MDR_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The lack of any organized health care led the poor and the sick to come to the Sisters for care. The Sisters responded to this need, caring for them in their own convent, when necessary. They began to visit the sick in their homes, and provide whatever rudimentary care they could. Eventually, the Sisters would expand into medical care as a new <a href="/wiki/Apostolates" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolates">apostolate</a>. This pattern was repeated as other communities of Sisters were established in the region.<sup id="cite_ref-MDR_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder">Founder</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ascensi%C3%B3n_Nicol_y_Go%C3%B1i&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Founder">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1917 the Catholic Church established a new <a href="/wiki/1917_Code_of_Canon_Law" title="1917 Code of Canon Law">Code of Canon Law</a>, which was the first organized juridical codification of the regulations for many aspects of the church's life and functioning. One of the effects was reinforcing the separation of cloistered communities. These new regulations would have severely hampered the work the nuns were undertaking. At the recommendation of the <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">Master of the Order of Preachers</a>, Ludwig Theissling, who was visiting Peru, the Sisters, under the auspices of Bishop Zubieta, decided to separate from the monastic community from which they had come and to form a new and independent congregation of Religious Sisters of the Dominican Third Order Regular.<sup id="cite_ref-MDO_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The new congregation was formally established on 5 October 1918 at the Convent of Our Lady of the Patronage in Lima. Nicol was elected as the first <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">Prioress</a> General of the congregation, and served in that office the rest of her life. She also served as the <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">Mistress of novices</a>, training the candidates to the congregation at their novitiate in Spain. During her generalship, she led her Sisters to establish themselves in other countries. In 1932 she led the founding community to mainland China, establishing what is to be the nucleus of their presence in Asia. She also established her congregation in Spain where they were able to recruit and form many missionary vocations. The General <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">Motherhouse</a> of the congregation was established in <a href="/wiki/Pamplona" title="Pamplona">Pamplona</a>, Navarre, Spain, and became her base.<sup id="cite_ref-MDO_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDO-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1938 Nicol increasingly frail and wanted to retire to prepare herself for her final days. Nevertheless, she accepted her unanimous re-election for a third term as Prioress General at the congregation's <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a> of 1939. She died on 24 February 1940.<sup id="cite_ref-MDR_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MDR-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ascensi%C3%B3n_Nicol_y_Go%C3%B1i&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Today the congregation has 785 Sisters serving in 21 nations on five continents. Its General Motherhouse is now in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Among its members, the congregation counts four Sisters who are considered to be <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> for the faith, having been tortured and murdered in the former <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_the_Congo_(L%C3%A9opoldville)" title="Republic of the Congo (Léopoldville)">Republic of the Congo</a> on 25 November 1964, in the course of the <a href="/wiki/Simba_Rebellion" class="mw-redirect" title="Simba Rebellion">Simba Rebellion</a>, after they refused to leave the patients in their hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ascensi%C3%B3n_Nicol_y_Go%C3%B1i&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The formal process for the <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of Nicol was opened in Pamplona in September 1962. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> declared her <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 2003. The following year a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> was declared to have taken place through her <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercession</a>, due to which <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> authorized the process to proceed. Thus, on 15 May 2005, in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a>, Nicol was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in a ceremony presided over by <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saraiva_Martins" title="José Saraiva Martins">José Saraiva Martins</a>. She was beatified along with another missionary religious sister, <a href="/wiki/Marianne_Cope" title="Marianne Cope">Marianne Cope</a>, who worked among the lepers of <a href="/wiki/Molokai" title="Molokai">Molokai</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hawaii" title="Hawaii">Hawaii</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
