<p><b>Saints Victor and Corona</b> (also known as <b>Saints Victor and Stephanie</b>) are two <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian martyrs</a>. Victor was a Roman soldier who was tortured and killed; Corona was killed for comforting him. Corona is invoked as a patron of causes involving money; she was not historically associated with pandemics or disease, but has been invoked against the <a href="/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic" title="COVID-19 pandemic">coronavirus pandemic</a>.
</p>

<p>Their legend states that Victor was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman empire">Roman</a> soldier of Italian ancestry, who was tortured, including having his eyes gouged out, and was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>. Most sources state that he and Corona were killed in <a href="/wiki/Roman_Syria" title="Roman Syria">Roman Syria</a> during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a> (around the 160s-170s AD), but various <a href="/wiki/Hagiographical" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographical">hagiographical</a> texts disagree about the site of their martyrdom, with some stating that it was <a href="/wiki/Damascus" title="Damascus">Damascus</a>, while <a href="/wiki/Copt" class="mw-redirect" title="Copt">Coptic</a> sources state that it was <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>. Some Western sources state that <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> or <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> was their place of martyrdom. They also disagree about the date of their martyrdom. They may have been martyred during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Antoninus_Pius" title="Antoninus Pius">Antoninus Pius</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, while the <i>Roman Martyrology</i> states that it was in the third century when they met their death.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While he was suffering from the tortures, the sixteen-year-old wife of another soldier, named Corona or Stephanie (or <i>Stefania</i> or <i>Stephana</i>, from Greek <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CF%83%CF%84%CE%AD%CF%86%E1%BE%B0%CE%BD%CE%BF%CF%82" class="extiw" title="wikt:στέφᾰνος">στέφᾰνος</a>, <i>stéphanos</i>, "crown", the Greek version of her Latin name, which also means "crown")<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> comforted and encouraged him. For this, she was arrested and interrogated. According to the <i>passio</i> of Corona, Corona was bound to two bent <a href="/wiki/Palm_trees" class="mw-redirect" title="Palm trees">palm trees</a> and torn apart as the trunks were released; the <i>passio</i> is considered largely fictional, and she herself may also be fictional.<sup id="cite_ref-EMMiller_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EMMiller-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Other sources state that Victor and Corona were husband and wife.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is also debate as to where Corona was from; differing accounts place her in <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Marseille" title="Marseille">Marseille</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victor_and_Corona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Victor and Corona's memorial day is 24 November (11 November in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_liturgical_calendar" title="Eastern Orthodox liturgical calendar">Orthodox church calendar</a>). Their feast day is 14 May. Outside the town of <a href="/wiki/Feltre" title="Feltre">Feltre</a> in northern Italy, on the slopes of Mount Miesna, is the church of SS. Vittore e Corona, erected by the <a href="/wiki/Crusaders" class="mw-redirect" title="Crusaders">Crusaders</a> from Feltre after the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a>.
</p><p>Corona is especially venerated in <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> and eastern <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>. There is a chapel dedicated to her in <a href="/wiki/Sauerlach" title="Sauerlach">Sauerlach</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> There are two churches named after her in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Passau" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Passau">Roman Catholic Diocese of Passau</a> and two towns named after her in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Austria" title="Lower Austria">Lower Austria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> A statue of her stands in the <a href="/wiki/M%C3%BCnster_Cathedral" title="Münster Cathedral">Münster Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1000 AD <a href="/wiki/Otto_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto III">Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor</a> brought Corona's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> to <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a> in western Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Her relics were rediscovered during excavation work at <a href="/wiki/Aachen_Cathedral" title="Aachen Cathedral">Aachen Cathedral</a> in 1910. The relics were removed from a crypt and placed in a shrine inside the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-raleigh_7-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-raleigh-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Corona is the patroness of causes involving money, such as <a href="/wiki/Gambling" title="Gambling">gambling</a> and treasure hunting, a result of a later treasure hunter who credited his success to invoking her.<sup id="cite_ref-EMMiller_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EMMiller-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-catholic_online_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic_online-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Hopfensperger_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hopfensperger-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> She is called upon by a treasure hunter to bring treasure, and then sent away through a similarly elaborate ritual.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> She was not historically a patron saint of or invoked against pandemics or disease, but has been invoked against the <a href="/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic" title="COVID-19 pandemic">COVID-19 pandemic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EMMiller_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EMMiller-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Hopfensperger_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hopfensperger-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Her relics will be available for public veneration once the pandemic has passed.<sup id="cite_ref-Hopfensperger_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hopfensperger-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
