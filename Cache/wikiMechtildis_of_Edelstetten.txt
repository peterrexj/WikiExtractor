<p><b>Mechtildis of Edelstetten</b>, also known as <b>Mechtildis of Diessen</b> or <b>Mechtildis of Andechs</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> and, according to legend, a renowned miracle worker. Mechtildis was the  daughter of <a href="/wiki/Berthold_II,_Count_of_Andechs" title="Berthold II, Count of Andechs">Count Berthold</a> of <a href="/wiki/Counts_of_Andechs" title="Counts of Andechs">Andechs</a>, whose wife, Sophie, founded a monastery on their estate at <a href="/wiki/Diessen" title="Diessen">Diessen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, and placed their daughter there at the age of five.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1153, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Augsburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Augsburg">Bishop of Augsburg</a> placed her as <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">Abbess</a> of Edelstetten Abbey. Mechtildis was known for her mystical gifts and miracles. She died at <a href="/wiki/Diessen" title="Diessen">Diessen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, on 31 May 1160. Her adorned remains lie in a glass shrine within the <a href="/wiki/Marienm%C3%BCnster_church" title="Marienmünster church">Marienmünster church</a> of that town.
</p>
