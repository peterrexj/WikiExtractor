<p><b>Juliana Falconieri, O.S.M.</b>, (1270 – 19 June 1341) was the <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sisters">Religious Sisters</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order">Third Order</a> of <a href="/wiki/Servite" class="mw-redirect" title="Servite">Servites</a> (<a href="/wiki/Mantellate_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Mantellate Sisters">Mantellate Sisters</a> or the Servite Tertiaries).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juliana_Falconieri&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Juliana belonged to the noble Falconieri family of <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>. Her uncle, <a href="/wiki/Alexis_Falconieri" class="mw-redirect" title="Alexis Falconieri">Alexis Falconieri</a>, was one of the seven founders of the <a href="/wiki/Servite_Order" title="Servite Order">Servite Order</a>. Under his influence, she decided at a young age to follow the <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">consecrated life</a>. After her father's death, she received c. 1285 the habit of the Third Order of the Servites from <a href="/wiki/Philip_Benizi" class="mw-redirect" title="Philip Benizi">Philip Benizi</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">Prior</a> General of that Order. She remained at home following the rule Benizi had given her until her mother's death, when Juliana and several companions moved into a house of their own in 1305. This became the first <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of the Sisters of the Third Order of Servites. Juliana would serve as <a href="/wiki/Superior_(hierarchy)" title="Superior (hierarchy)">Superior</a> until the end of her life.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>
The Servites' dress consisted of a black gown, secured by a leather girdle, and a white veil. Because the gown had short sleeves to facilitate work, people called the sisters of the new Order <i>"Mantellate."</i> The sisters devoted themselves especially to the care of the sick and other works of mercy. It is said<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> </p><blockquote><p>she would often fall in to long moments and hours of ecstacy... She was daily caring for the sick in the streets, homes, and in hospitals and was known for using her own lips to suck out the infection of her patients open sores without fear of contracting any illness. Truly a magnificent act. Juliana directed the community of Servite Tertiaries for 35 years and was more of a servant to her subordinates than a mistress.</p></blockquote>
<p>A putative miracle mentioned in the liturgical texts for her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a>, is said to have occurred at Juliana's death. At this time, unable to receive <a href="/wiki/Eucharist_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Eucharist (Catholic Church)">Holy Communion</a> because of constant vomiting, she requested the priest to spread a <a href="/wiki/Corporal_(liturgy)" title="Corporal (liturgy)">corporal</a> upon her chest and lay the <a href="/wiki/Sacramental_bread" title="Sacramental bread">Eucharistic host</a> on it. Shortly thereafter, the host disappeared and Juliana died on 19 June 1341.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The image of a cross, just like the one on the host, was found on her breast.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Immediately after her death she was honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Servite_Order" title="Servite Order">Servite Order</a> was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_V" title="Pope Martin V">Pope Martin V</a> in the year 1420. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> recognized the devotion long paid to her and granted the Servites permission to celebrate the feast of the Blessed Juliana. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XII" title="Pope Clement XII">Pope Clement XII</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> her in the year 1737, and extended the celebration of her feast day (June 19) to the entire Church. Juliana is usually represented in the habit of her Order with a host upon her breast.
</p>
