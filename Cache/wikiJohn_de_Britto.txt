<p><b>John de Britto</b>, SJ (also spelled <i>Brito</i>; <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">João de Brito</i>), also known as <b>Arul Anandar</b>, (born in <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> on 1 March 1647 – died at <a href="/wiki/Orur" title="Orur">Orur</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Tamil_Nadu" title="Tamil Nadu">Tamil Nadu</a>, <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> on 4 February 1693) was a <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portuguese</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>, often called "the Portuguese <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">St Francis Xavier</a>" by <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_India" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism in India">Indian Catholics</a>. He is also called the John the Baptist of India.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_missionary_work">Early life and missionary work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_de_Britto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and missionary work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John de Britto was the scion of a powerful aristocratic Portuguese family; his father, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Salvador_de_Britto_Pereira&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Salvador de Britto Pereira (page does not exist)">Salvador de Britto Pereira</a>, died while serving as Viceroy of the Portuguese <a href="/wiki/Colonial_Brazil" title="Colonial Brazil">colony of Brazil</a>.  He joined the Jesuits in 1662, studying at the famous <a href="/wiki/University_of_Coimbra" title="University of Coimbra">University of Coimbra</a>. He travelled to the missions of <a href="/wiki/Madurai" title="Madurai">Madurai</a>, in Southern India, present-day <a href="/wiki/Tamil_Nadu" title="Tamil Nadu">Tamil Nadu</a>, in 1673 and preached the Christian religion in the region of the Maravar country. He renamed himself <i><b>Arul Anandar</b></i> (அருளானந்தர்) in <a href="/wiki/Tamil_language" title="Tamil language">Tamil</a>. The ruler of the Maravar country imprisoned him in 1684. Having been expelled, he returned to Lisbon in 1687 and worked as a missions procurator. King <a href="/wiki/Peter_II_of_Portugal" title="Peter II of Portugal">Pedro II</a> wanted him to stay, but in 1690 he returned to the Maravar country with 24 new missionaries.
</p><p>The Madurai Mission was a bold attempt to establish an Indian Catholic Church that was relatively free of European cultural domination.  As such, Britto learned the native languages, went about dressed in yellow cotton, and lived like a <a href="/wiki/Tamils" title="Tamils">Tamil</a> Thuravi/Sannyasi, abstaining from every kind of animal food and from <a href="/wiki/Wine" title="Wine">wine</a>. Britto tried to teach the Catholic faith in categories and concepts that would make sense to the people he taught. This method, proposed and practised by <a href="/wiki/Roberto_de_Nobili" title="Roberto de Nobili">Roberto de Nobili</a>, met with remarkable success. Britto remained a strict <a href="/wiki/Veganism" title="Veganism">vegan</a> until the end of his life, rejecting meat, fish, eggs and alcohol, and living only on legumes, fruits and herbs.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_de_Britto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mart%C3%ADrio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Mart%C3%ADrio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito.png/220px-Mart%C3%ADrio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="296" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Mart%C3%ADrio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito.png/330px-Mart%C3%ADrio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6b/Mart%C3%ADrio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito.png 2x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="538" /></a><figcaption>Martyrdom of Saint John de Britto Killed by Sethupathi King of Ramnad</figcaption></figure>
<p>John de Britto's preaching led to the <a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity" title="Conversion to Christianity">conversion</a> of Thadiyathevan (தடியத் தேவன்), a Maravar prince who had several wives. When Thadiyathevan was required to dismiss all his wives but one, a serious problem arose. One of the wives was a niece of the neighbouring king, the <a href="/wiki/Raghunatha_Kilavan" title="Raghunatha Kilavan">Raghunatha Kilavan</a> <a href="/wiki/Sethupathi" title="Sethupathi">Sethupathi</a> (சேதுபதி), <a href="/wiki/Ramnad_estate" title="Ramnad estate">the King of Ramnad</a> (Ramanathapuram), who took up her quarrel and began a general persecution of Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Britto and the catechists were taken and carried to the capital, <a href="/wiki/Ramanathapuram" title="Ramanathapuram">Ramnad</a>. Thence he was led to <a href="/wiki/Orur" title="Orur">Orur</a> (ஓரியூர்), some 30 miles northward along the coast, where he was executed on 4 February 1693.
</p><p>Britto was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> on 21 August 1853. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on 22 June 1947. Saint John de Britto's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 4 February.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Red_Sand">Red Sand</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_de_Britto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Red Sand">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>This seashore sightseeing location is one of the most venerable pilgrim centres of Christians in the world over, as it is said to be the site of Britto's martyrdom. It was at this place where Britto is said to have been beheaded in 1693. The sand dune here is believed to have been stained by his blood. There is a shrine constructed in Portuguese style containing a statue of Britto, known locally as 'Arul Anandar' who had modestly offered his neck to the executioner.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_de_Britto_(indipetens).JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/John_de_Britto_%28indipetens%29.JPG/220px-John_de_Britto_%28indipetens%29.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/John_de_Britto_%28indipetens%29.JPG/330px-John_de_Britto_%28indipetens%29.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/John_de_Britto_%28indipetens%29.JPG/440px-John_de_Britto_%28indipetens%29.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="4608" /></a><figcaption>Letter of John de Britto requesting to be sent to the Missions</figcaption></figure>
<p>The red sand dune here in this shrine where the blood of Britto was spilled has great significance. Numerous incurable diseases are said to have cured by the application of the red sand on the respective body parts. Couples are believed to have blessed with children on visiting the shrine and praying to the departed soul. During festivities, pilgrims mainly from Tamil Nadu and Kerala participate irrespective of their caste, creed and religion. Thus, together with Christians, Hindus and Muslims also come to worship at the shrine in thousands, to mark respect to a unique holy man who shed his life and blood at that spot. The occasion appears to be more as a social gathering rather than a religious festival. The auspicious ceremony is a rare opportunity for these simple people to bring gaiety and enthusiasm in their life. The strong faith and enviable ability to combine pleasure and righteousness on a pilgrimage gives a divine atmosphere to the Orur festival.
</p><p>Devotees from other dioceses and districts visit the shrine on specific dates. In February, believers from <a href="/wiki/Dindigul" title="Dindigul">Dindigul</a> arrive while in June, they are from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Karunguli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Karunguli (page does not exist)">Karunguli</a> and <a href="/wiki/Nagapattinam" title="Nagapattinam">Nagapattinam</a>. During September more than 25,000 pilgrims used to visit the shrine for dedicating prayers and offerings. In October, nearly 25,000 pilgrims arrive from the neighbouring <a href="/wiki/Sivagangai" class="mw-redirect" title="Sivagangai">Sivagangai</a> district and in December, visitors are from <a href="/wiki/Madurai" title="Madurai">Madurai</a> and <a href="/wiki/Melur" title="Melur">Melur</a>. Throughout the year, thousands of pilgrims from <a href="/wiki/Sakthikulangara" title="Sakthikulangara">Sakthikulangara</a>, a Catholic parish in Kerala, visit the shrine.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Portugal">In Portugal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_de_Britto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In Portugal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His name was given to the Jesuit-run <a href="https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col%C3%A9gio_de_S%C3%A3o_Jo%C3%A3o_de_Brito" class="extiw" title="pt:Colégio de São João de Brito">Colégio de São João de Brito</a> (Saint John de Britto College), located in <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_India">In India</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_de_Britto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In India">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There is a shrine to Britto in the village of <a href="/wiki/Orur" title="Orur">Orur</a> (also spelled Oreiour), where he is a significant figure revered by the <a href="/wiki/Kallar_(caste)" title="Kallar (caste)">Kallar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Maravar" title="Maravar">Maravar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Agamudayar" title="Agamudayar">Agamudayar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is only one church in Coimbatore dedicated to John de Britto and located at R S Puram, and it is one of the largest parishes in the diocese of <a href="/wiki/Coimbatore" title="Coimbatore">Coimbatore</a>. There is also a church in Subramaniapuram in <a href="/wiki/Tiruchirapalli" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiruchirapalli">Tiruchirapalli</a> district of Tamil Nadu dedicated to him. In 2016, a Church was built in honour of St. John de Britto at Laggere, Bangalore (Karnataka). There is also a Church dedicated <a href="/wiki/John_de_Britto_Church,_Sesurajapuram" class="mw-redirect" title="John de Britto Church, Sesurajapuram">John de Britto</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sesurajapuram" title="Sesurajapuram">Sesurajapuram</a>, Krishnagiri district of Tamilnadu.
</p><p>One of the four houses in the Jesuit school, St Xavier's, Durgapur, is named after John de Britto. In the <a href="/wiki/Campion_School,_Mumbai" title="Campion School, Mumbai">Campion School of Mumbai</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Xavier%27s_Hazaribag&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Xavier&#39;s Hazaribag (page does not exist)">St Xavier's Hazaribag</a>  there is a house named after Britto (Britto House). The other two houses are named for <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a> (Xavier House) and <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a> (Loyola House). One of four houses in the Jesuit school, <a href="/wiki/St._Stanislaus_High_School" title="St. Stanislaus High School">St Stanislaus High School</a>. The other three houses named after Francis Xavier (Xavier House), Ignatius of Loyola (Loyola House) and <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_Kostka" title="Stanislaus Kostka">Stanislaus Kostka</a> (Kostka House). Also one of the four houses in St. Xavier's School, Kolkata, is named after Britto. 
</p><p>One of the four houses in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Infant_Jesus_Anglo-Indian_Higher_Secondary_School&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Infant Jesus Anglo-Indian Higher Secondary School (page does not exist)">Infant Jesus Anglo-Indian Higher Secondary School</a> <a href="/wiki/Tangasseri" title="Tangasseri">Tangasseri</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kollam" title="Kollam">Kollam</a> is named after John de Britto (Brittos). The other three houses are named for <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">John Bosco</a> (Boscos),  <a href="/wiki/John_Berchmans" title="John Berchmans">John Berchmans</a> (Berchmans) and <a href="/wiki/Dominic_Savio" title="Dominic Savio">Dominic Savio</a> (Savios). <a href="/w/index.php?title=Campion_Anglo_Indian_Higher_Secondary_school_Tiruchirapalli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Campion Anglo Indian Higher Secondary school Tiruchirapalli (page does not exist)">Campion Anglo Indian Higher Secondary school Tiruchirapalli</a> which is one of the prestigious schools in <a href="/wiki/Tiruchirapalli" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiruchirapalli">Tiruchirapalli</a> district of Tamil Nadu also has a house named after him.
</p><p>St Britto High School in <a href="/wiki/Goa" title="Goa">Goa</a> is named after Britto as he lived there for seven months to complete his theological studies at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Paul%27s_College,_Goa" title="Saint Paul&#39;s College, Goa">St Paul's College</a> in Old <a href="/wiki/Goa" title="Goa">Goa</a>. The school is administered by the Jesuits. There is an Anglo-Indian Boys High School in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Cochin" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Cochin">Kochi Diocese</a>, in the old Portuguese city of <a href="/wiki/Fort_Cochin" class="mw-redirect" title="Fort Cochin">Fort Cochin</a>, named after John de Britto, nearby the Bishop's House, in <a href="/wiki/Kochi" title="Kochi">Kochi</a>. There is a St. John De Britto College of Education at Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu (B.Ed. and M.Ed. college) since Britto worked in the Diocese of Thanjavur as well.
</p><p>Britto is the patron saint (referred as <i>Pathukavul</i>) of <a href="/wiki/Sakthikulangara" title="Sakthikulangara">Sakthikulangara</a> Parish in <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Quilon" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Quilon">Kollam Diocese</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kerala" title="Kerala">Kerala</a>. Every year, Britto's feast day is celebrated in <a href="/wiki/Sakthikulangara" title="Sakthikulangara">Sakthikulangara</a> with a big procession (<i>prathikshanam</i>). The <a href="/wiki/St_John_De_Britto_Anglo-Indian_High_School" class="mw-redirect" title="St John De Britto Anglo-Indian High School">St John De Britto Anglo-Indian High School</a> is named after him.
One of the Jesuit colleges established in Tamil Nadu is named after Britto as <a href="/wiki/Arul_Anandar_College,_Karumathur" title="Arul Anandar College, Karumathur">Arul Anandar College</a> (Arts &amp; Science) which is in Karumathur, Madurai, as well as <a href="/wiki/St._Arul_Anandar_School,_Orur" title="St. Arul Anandar School, Orur">St. Arul Anandar School, Orur</a>, in the town where Britto died. These last two schools were established by the Jesuits to promote education in the rural parts of <a href="/wiki/Tamil_Nadu" title="Tamil Nadu">Tamil Nadu</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_countries">Other countries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_de_Britto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Other countries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, Britto is honoured with several class sections named after him in the Jesuit-run schools:
</p>
<ul><li>Section 11-De Brito in <a href="/wiki/Ateneo_de_Manila_University#Senior_High_School" title="Ateneo de Manila University">Ateneo de Manila Senior High School</a></li>
<li>Four high school class sections in <a href="/wiki/Ateneo_de_Davao_University" title="Ateneo de Davao University">Ateneo de Davao University</a> (1-De Brito; 2-De Brito; 3-De Brito; 4-De Brito)</li>
<li>A Grade 8 level (formerly Grade 9 but changed into a Grade 8 section) class section in <a href="/wiki/Xavier_University_%E2%80%93_Ateneo_de_Cagayan" title="Xavier University – Ateneo de Cagayan">Xavier University – Ateneo de Cagayan</a></li></ul>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Yogyakarta_(city)" class="mw-redirect" title="Yogyakarta (city)">Yogyakarta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Indonesia" title="Indonesia">Indonesia</a>, a Jesuit school for boys is named after him, <a href="/wiki/De_Britto_High_School_Yogyakarta" title="De Britto High School Yogyakarta">SMA Kolese De Britto</a> (De Britto College Senior High School).
</p><p>In Penang, Malaysia, there is a church called Church of St John Britto. The church is part of City Parish in the city of Georgetown, Penang. It was built in 1969. On 3 February 2019, the city parish held a Golden Jubilee celebration.
</p><p>In Mauritius there is a chapel named St Jean de Britto dedicated to Tamil Catholics living in the area.
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> there is a São João de Brito Parish in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Santo_Amaro" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Santo Amaro">Roman Catholic Diocese of Santo Amaro</a>, one of the dioceses in the city of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a>. The parish was founded on 30 December 1952 and remains as one of the churches among the Pastoral Sector of Saint Amaro in the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
