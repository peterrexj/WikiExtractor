<p><b>Wiborada of St. Gall</b> (also <b>Guiborat</b>, <b>Weibrath</b> or <b>Viborata</b>; <a href="/wiki/Alemannic_German" title="Alemannic German">Alemannic</a>: <i>Wiberat</i>) (died 926) was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Swabia" title="Swabia">Swabian</a> nobility in what is present-day <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>. She was an <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchoress</a>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine nun</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wiborada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are two biographies of Wiborada: one by Hartmann, a monk of St. Gall, written between 993 and 1047 (<a href="/wiki/Bibliotheca_Hagiographica_Latina" title="Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina">BHL</a> 8866); and another written between 1072 and 1076 by the monk Herimannus (<a href="/wiki/Bibliotheca_Hagiographica_Latina" title="Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina">BHL</a> 8867).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wiborada was born to a wealthy noble family in Swabia. When they invited the sick and poor into their home, Wiborada proved a capable nurse. Her brother Hatto became a priest. A pilgrimage to Rome influenced Hatto to decide to become a monk at the Abbey of Saint Gall, a decision which Wiborada supported. After the death of their parents, Wiborada joined Hatto and became a Benedictine at the Abbey of Saint Gall. Wiborada became settled at the monastery and Hatto taught her <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> so that she could chant the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Liturgy of the Hours</a>. There, she occupied herself by making Hatto's clothes and helping to <a href="/wiki/Bookbinding" title="Bookbinding">bind many of the books</a> in the monastery library.<sup id="cite_ref-salive_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-salive-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At this time, it appears that Wiborada was charged with some type of serious infraction or wrongdoing, and was subjected to the medieval practice of <a href="/wiki/Trial_by_ordeal" title="Trial by ordeal">ordeal by fire</a> to prove her innocence. Although she was exonerated, the embarrassment probably influenced her next decision: withdrawing from the world and becoming an <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg/200px-CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="199" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg/300px-CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg/400px-CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2204" data-file-height="2193" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:CodSang602_S298_Wiborada_im_Gespr%C3%A4ch_mit_Salomo_III.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Wiborada with Solomon III, Bishop of Constance</div></div></div>
<p>When she petitioned to become an anchoress, <a href="/wiki/Solomon_III_(bishop_of_Constance)" title="Solomon III (bishop of Constance)">Solomon III</a>, Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Konstanz" title="Konstanz">Konstanz</a>, arranged for her to stay in a cell next to the church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a> near the monastery, where she remained for four years before relocating to a cell adjoining the church of <a href="/wiki/Magnus_of_F%C3%BCssen" title="Magnus of Füssen">Magnus of Füssen</a> in 891.<sup id="cite_ref-marymartha_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marymartha-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-patrick_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She became renowned for her austerity, and was said to have a gift of <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecy</a>, both of which drew admirers and hopeful students. One of these, a woman named Rachildis, whom Wiborada had cured of a disease, joined her as an anchoress. A young student at St. Gall, <a href="/wiki/Ulrich_of_Augsburg" title="Ulrich of Augsburg">Ulrich</a>, is said to have visited Wiborada often. She supposedly prophesied his elevation to the <a href="/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Augsburg" title="Prince-Bishopric of Augsburg">episcopate of Augsburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Jestice_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jestice-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg/200px-Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="170" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg/300px-Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg/400px-Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1116" data-file-height="950" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyrdom_of_Wiborada.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Martyrdom of Saint Wiborada</i>, c. 1451.</div></div></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wiborada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 925, she predicted a <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_invasions_of_Europe" title="Hungarian invasions of Europe">Hungarian invasion</a> of her region. Her warning allowed the priests and religious of St. Gall and St. Magnus to hide the books and wine and escape into caves in nearby hills.<sup id="cite_ref-library_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-library-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The most precious manuscripts were transferred to the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Reichenau_Island" title="Reichenau Island">Reichenau Island</a>. However, the main refuge castle for the monks and the abbot was the Waldburg in the Sitterwood.<sup id="cite_ref-Heule_2018_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Heule_2018-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Her abbot, <a href="/wiki/Engilbert_II_of_Saint_Gall" title="Engilbert II of Saint Gall">Engilbert</a>, urged Wiborada to escape to safety, but she refused to leave her cell.<sup id="cite_ref-Jestice_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jestice-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 926 the <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_invasions_of_Europe" title="Hungarian invasions of Europe">Magyar marauders</a> reached St. Gall. They burned down St. Magnus and broke into the roof of Wiborada's cell. Upon finding her kneeling in prayer, they <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cleave" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:cleave">clove</a> her skull with a <i>fokos</i> (<a href="/wiki/Shepherd%27s_axe" title="Shepherd&#39;s axe">shepherd's axe</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Jestice_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jestice-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her companion Rachildis was not killed, and lived another 21 years, during which her disease returned. She spent the rest of her life learning <a href="/wiki/Patience" title="Patience">patience</a> through <a href="/wiki/Suffering" title="Suffering">suffering</a>. Wiborada's refusal to leave her cell and the part she played in saving the lives of the priests and religious of her convent have merited her the title of <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wiborada&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Wiborada was formally canonized by the Holy See, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_II" title="Pope Clement II">Pope Clement II</a> in 1047. Her feast day is 2 May. In Switzerland, Wiborada is considered the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Library" title="Library">libraries</a> and librarians. In art, she is commonly represented holding a book to signify the library she saved, and an axe, which signifies the manner of her martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-library_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-library-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The axe with which she is commonly depicted is in fact <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/anachronistic" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:anachronistic">anachronistic</a>, being a <a href="/wiki/Halberd" title="Halberd">halberd</a>, which did not come into existence until the 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-marymartha_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marymartha-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
