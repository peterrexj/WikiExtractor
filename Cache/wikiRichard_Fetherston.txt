					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">English Roman Catholic priest.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the Australian politician and doctor, see <a href="/wiki/Richard_Fetherston_(politician)" title="Richard Fetherston (politician)">Richard Fetherston (politician)</a>.</div>
<p>
<b>Richard Fetherston</b> (Fetherstone, Featherstone) (died 30 July 1540) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He was <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon_of_Brecon" title="Archdeacon of Brecon">Archdeacon of Brecon</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">Chaplain</a> to <a href="/wiki/Catharine_of_Aragon" class="mw-redirect" title="Catharine of Aragon">Catharine of Aragon</a> and tutor to her daughter, <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Mary Tudor</a>. He was executed in 1540 and <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on 29 December 1886.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_Fetherston&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is called <i>sacrae theologiae Doctor</i> by <a href="/wiki/John_Pits" class="mw-redirect" title="John Pits">John Pits</a> (<i>De illustribus Angliae scriptoribus</i>, 729). He was one of the theologians appointed to defend Queen Catharine's cause in the divorce proceedings before the <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legates</a> <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Wolsey" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Wolsey">Cardinal Wolsey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Campeggio" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Campeggio">Cardinal Campeggio</a>, and is said to have written a treatise <i>Contra divortium Henrici et Catharinae, Liber unus</i>. No copy of this work is known to exist.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He took part in the session of <a href="/wiki/Convocation" title="Convocation">Convocation</a> which began in April 1529, and was one of the few members who refused to sign the Act declaring <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII" title="Henry VIII">Henry VIII</a>'s marriage with Catharine to be illegal <i>ab initio</i>, through the pope's inability to grant a dispensation in such a case. In 1534 he was called upon to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> and, on refusing to do so, was committed to the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> on 13 December 1534. He seems to have remained in prison until 1540.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn,_and_quartered" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanged, drawn, and quartered">hanged, drawn, and quartered</a> at <a href="/wiki/Smithfield,_London" title="Smithfield, London">Smithfield</a> on 30 July 1540, together with the Catholic theologians <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Abel_(martyr)" title="Thomas Abel (martyr)">Thomas Abel</a> and <a href="/wiki/Edward_Powell_(martyr)" title="Edward Powell (martyr)">Edward Powell</a>, like himself councillors to Queen Catharine in the divorce proceedings, and three others, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Barnes_(martyr)" title="Robert Barnes (martyr)">Robert Barnes</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Garret" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Garret">Thomas Garret</a>, and <a href="/wiki/William_Jerome_(martyr)" class="mw-redirect" title="William Jerome (martyr)">William Jerome</a>, condemned for teaching <a href="/wiki/Zwinglianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Zwinglianism">Zwinglianism</a>. All six were drawn through the streets upon three hurdles, a Catholic and a heretic on each hurdle. The Protestants were burned, and the three Catholics executed in the usual manner, their limbs being fixed over the gates of the city and their heads being placed upon poles on London Bridge.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
