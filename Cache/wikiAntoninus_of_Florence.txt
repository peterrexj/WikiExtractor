<p><b>Antoninus of Florence</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">OP</a></span> (1 March 1389&#160;&#8211;&#32;2 May 1459), was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, who ruled as an <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Florence" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Florence">archbishop of Florence</a>. He is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoninus_of_Florence&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <b>Antonio Pierozzi</b> (also called <b>de Forciglioni</b>) on 1 March 1389 in the city of <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, then capital of an independent <a href="/wiki/Florentine_Republic" class="mw-redirect" title="Florentine Republic">Republic</a>, to Niccolò and Tomasina Pierozzi, prominent citizens of the city, Niccolò being a <a href="/wiki/Notary" title="Notary">notary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McMahon_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McMahon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His mother died when he was about five years of age. As a child, he spent time at the church of <a href="/wiki/Orsanmichele" title="Orsanmichele">Orsanmichele</a>.
</p><p>The young Anthony was received into the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> in 1405 at the age of sixteen at the new priory of San Domenico in <a href="/wiki/Fiesole" title="Fiesole">Fiesole</a> and given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <a href="/wiki/John_Dominici" title="John Dominici">John Dominici</a>, founder of the community, becoming its first candidate. With <a href="/wiki/Fra_Angelico" title="Fra Angelico">Fra Angelico</a> and <a href="/wiki/Fra_Bartolommeo" class="mw-redirect" title="Fra Bartolommeo">Fra Bartolommeo</a>, the one to become famous as a painter, the other as a miniaturist, he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Cortona" title="Cortona">Cortona</a> to make his novitiate under Bl. <a href="/wiki/Lorenzo_da_Ripafratta" title="Lorenzo da Ripafratta">Lorenzo da Ripafratta</a>. Upon the completion of his year in the novitiate, he returned to Fiesole, where he remained until 1409, when with his brethren, all faithful adherents of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XII" title="Pope Gregory XII">Pope Gregory XII</a>, he was constrained by the Florentines, who had refused obedience, to take shelter in the Convent of <a href="/wiki/Foligno" title="Foligno">Foligno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McMahon_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McMahon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was tasked with the administration of various houses of his Order at <a href="/wiki/Cortona" title="Cortona">Cortona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, as well as Florence, which he labored zealously to reform.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> These communities became part of a new Dominican <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">Congregation</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, established by John Dominici in order to promote a stricter form of life within the Order, which had been devastated through its division in the <a href="/wiki/Western_Schism" title="Western Schism">Western Schism</a> of the preceding century. In 1430 he became prior of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_sopra_Minerva" title="Santa Maria sopra Minerva">Santa Maria sopra Minerva</a>.
</p><p>From 1433 to 1446 Antoninus served as <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a> of the Congregation. In this office, he was involved in the establishment of the Priory of <a href="/wiki/San_Marco,_Florence" title="San Marco, Florence">St Mark</a> in Florence with friars from Fiesole.<sup id="cite_ref-McMahon_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McMahon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The priory's cells, including one for <a href="/wiki/Cosimo_de%27_Medici" title="Cosimo de&#39; Medici">Cosimo de' Medici</a>, were painted in <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">frescos</a> by Fra Angelico and his assistants.
</p><p>Antoninus was <a href="/wiki/Consecrated" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated">consecrated</a> <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Florence" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Florence">Archbishop of Florence</a> on 13 March 1446, at the Dominican priory in <a href="/wiki/Fiesole" title="Fiesole">Fiesole</a>, on the initiative of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV" title="Pope Eugene IV">Pope Eugene IV</a>, who had come to admire him through his participation in the major church councils of the period. He came to win the esteem and love of his people, especially by his energy and resource in combating the effects of the plague and earthquake in 1448 and 1453.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It was they who began the use of the diminutive form of his name which has come to prevail. Antoninus lived a life of austerity as archbishop, continuing to follow the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">Rule</a>. His relations with the <a href="/wiki/Medici" class="mw-redirect" title="Medici">Medici</a> regime were close but not always harmonious, with his serving several times as an ambassador for the Republic to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> during the 1450s.
</p><p>Antoninus died on 2 May 1459, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_II" title="Pope Pius II">Pope Pius II</a> conducted his funeral. The pope happened to be on his way to the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Mantua" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Mantua">Council of Mantua</a> when he heard of the archbishop's death. The archbishop's wish was that he be buried at the priory which he had founded in the city.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoninus_of_Florence&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Antonino_-_Confessionale,_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Antonino_-_Confessionale%2C_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg/220px-Antonino_-_Confessionale%2C_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="367" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Antonino_-_Confessionale%2C_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg/330px-Antonino_-_Confessionale%2C_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/24/Antonino_-_Confessionale%2C_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg/440px-Antonino_-_Confessionale%2C_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg 2x" data-file-width="693" data-file-height="1155" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Antonino_-_Confessionale,_circa_1488-1490_-_2346265.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Confessionale</i>, circa 1488-1490</div></div></div>
<p>Antoninus had a great reputation for theological learning, and had assisted as a papal theologian at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Florence" title="Council of Florence">Council of Florence</a>. Of his various works, the list of which is given in <a href="/wiki/Qu%C3%A9tif" class="mw-redirect" title="Quétif">Quétif</a>-<a href="/wiki/Jacques_%C3%89chard" title="Jacques Échard">Échard</a>, <i>De Scriptoribus Ordinis Praedicatorum</i>, vol. i.818, the best-known are his <i>Summa theologica moralis</i> (printed in 1477) and the <i>Summa confessionalis, Curam illius habes</i> (printed in 1472).<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Both were printed years after the author's death. The former condenses the material of <a href="/wiki/Moral_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Moral theology">moral theology</a> in four parts drawing heavily from the writings of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a>.  The first presents the anthropological foundations of moral teaching examining the soul and its powers; the second discusses different sins; the third examines social, religious, and ecclesiastical states of life as well as excommunications and censures; and the fourth concerns itself with the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_virtues" title="Cardinal virtues">cardinal virtues</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Gifts_of_the_Holy_Spirit" class="mw-redirect" title="Gifts of the Holy Spirit">gifts of the Holy Spirit</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The latter is one of three guides for <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessors</a> which he wrote, and it was highly regarded by the clergy as an aid for centuries. His writings were a major development in the field of moral theology. For a more up to date list of works and manuscripts, see Thomas Kaeppeli, <i>Scriptores ordinis praedicatorum medii aevi</i>, vol. 1 (Rome: Ad S. Sabinaa, 1970).
</p><p>In 1477 Antoninus' the <i>Chronicon partibus tribus distincta ab initio mundi ad MCCCLX</i> was published; it was intended to be a history of creation from a religious perspective, up to the end of the 14th century. Though uncritical in its account of earlier ages, his accounts of more current events have been useful to historians.
</p><p>Antoninus' writings, some in Italian, reflect a pronounced awareness of the problems of social and economic development. He argued in them that the state had a duty to intervene in mercantile affairs for the <a href="/wiki/Common_good" title="Common good">common good</a>, and the obligation to help the poor and needy. His viewpoint on the vanity of women's dress made concessions to the social status of women of high birth or married to holders of high office.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoninus_of_Florence&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Antoninus was canonized on 31 May 1523 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_VI" title="Pope Adrian VI">Pope Adrian VI</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who himself held ideas of radical and drastic church reform similar to those of Antoninus.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a>, which was not in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>, was inserted in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1683, for celebration on 10 May<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> as a Double, a rank altered in 1960 to that of Third-Class Feast. Since 1969, it is <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">no longer in</a> the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, but the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> indicates that it is still observed, moved to 2 May, the day of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Antoninus is honored as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Moncalvo" title="Moncalvo">Moncalvo</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a>.
</p><p>Saint Antoninus is honored as the patron saint of the Catholic Church &amp; Aglipayan Church of the Municipality of Pura, Tarlac Philippines.
</p><p>His body lies in the Chapel of St. Antoninus in <a href="/wiki/San_Marco,_Florence" title="San Marco, Florence">San Marco, Florence</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
