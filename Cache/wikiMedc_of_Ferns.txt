<p><b>Saint Máedóc of Ferns</b> (<span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="und-Latn-fonipa">/ˈmai.ðoːɡ/</span>; <small><abbr title="Active">fl.</abbr></small>&#160;6th &amp; 7th century), also known as <b>Saint Aidan</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Áedan</i>; <a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Aeddan</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Aidanus</i> and <i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Edanus</i></span></i>), <b>Saint Madoc<sup id="cite_ref-auto_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></b> or <b>Saint Mogue</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Mo Aodh Óg</i>), was an <a href="/wiki/List_of_Irish_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Irish saints">Irish saint</a> who was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ferns" title="Bishop of Ferns">Bishop of Ferns</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Wexford" title="County Wexford">County Wexford</a> and the founder of thirty churches. His birth name was <a href="/wiki/Aed_(god)" title="Aed (god)">Áed</a>, the name of the <a href="/wiki/Irish_mythology" title="Irish mythology">Irish god of the underworld</a>, meaning "<a href="/wiki/Fire" title="Fire">fire</a>". The name <a href="/wiki/Aidan_(name)" class="mw-redirect" title="Aidan (name)">Aidan</a> is a <a href="/wiki/Diminutive" title="Diminutive">diminutive</a> form of <a href="/wiki/Aed_(god)" title="Aed (god)">Aed</a> or <a href="/wiki/Aodh_(given_name)" title="Aodh (given name)">Aodh</a>, and was also a form of the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> name <a href="/wiki/Dominus_(title)" title="Dominus (title)">Dominus</a>. <a href="/wiki/Madog_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Madog (disambiguation)">Máedóc</a> and Mogue are other <a href="/wiki/Pet_name" class="mw-redirect" title="Pet name">pet forms</a> of Aed or Aodh, formed from the Irish affectionate prefix <i>mo-</i> and the diminutive suffix <i>-óg</i>, meaning "young", making for something like "my dear little Aodh".<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1ed%C3%B3c_of_Ferns&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Áed was born c. 558 at Inisbrefny (an island in Templeport Lake), in the area then known as <a href="/wiki/Magh_Sl%C3%A9cht" title="Magh Slécht">Magh Slécht</a>, now the parish of <a href="/wiki/Templeport" title="Templeport">Templeport</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-toke_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Bonedd_y_Seint" class="mw-redirect" title="Bonedd y Seint">Welsh genealogies of the saints</a>, Aeddan is called the son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gildas" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gildas">Gildas</a> or <a href="/wiki/Aneirin" title="Aneirin">Aneurin</a>, sons of <a href="/wiki/Caw_of_Strathclyde" title="Caw of Strathclyde">Caw</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Strathclyde" title="Kingdom of Strathclyde">Strathclyde</a>; Irish sources make him a son of Sedna, a chieftain of <a href="/wiki/Connaught" class="mw-redirect" title="Connaught">Connaught</a>, and his wife, Eithne and a first cousin of St. <a href="/wiki/Dall%C3%A1n_Forgaill" title="Dallán Forgaill">Dallán Forgaill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-plummer_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-plummer-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> These separate origins, his work in Wales, the extraordinary span of Aidan's activity, and the appearance of two dates of death has led some scholars&#8212;such as <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a>&#8212;to propose that the existing stories of Maedoc are a conflation of two separate Aeds, one Welsh and one Irish, who served as Bishop of Ferns a generation apart.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> There is also confusion of these Aeds with <a href="/wiki/Aed_mac_Bricc" class="mw-redirect" title="Aed mac Bricc">Aed mac Bricc</a>, who preceded them and possibly participated in the cursing of <a href="/wiki/Hill_of_Tara" title="Hill of Tara">Tara</a> with <a href="/wiki/Ruadh%C3%A1n_of_Lorrha" title="Ruadhán of Lorrha">Saint Ruadhán</a>.
</p><p>Irish legend says that the "Bell of St. Mogue" was given to the infant on his birth by Saint <a href="/wiki/Caill%C3%ADn" title="Caillín">Caillín</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cavan_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cavan-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> When a boat could not be found to take the infant Aedan across the lake to where Caillín waited to baptize him, Aedan was floated to shore on a slab of stone. The <a href="/wiki/Holy_water" title="Holy water">font</a> at St Mogue's in <a href="/wiki/Bawnboy" title="Bawnboy">Bawnboy</a> is said to be made from part of the stone. As a youth, Aedan was a <a href="/wiki/Hostage" title="Hostage">hostage</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ainmuire_mac_S%C3%A9tnai" title="Ainmuire mac Sétnai">Ainmuire mac Sétnai</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_Conaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenél Conaill">Cenél Conaill</a>, <a href="/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland" title="High King of Ireland">High King of Ireland</a>. Ainmire was so impressed with Aedan that he told him he could stay or go. Aedan said he would go, but only if the other hostages were also released, whereupon Ainmire let them all return home.<sup id="cite_ref-plummer_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-plummer-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He studied at the great school of Saint <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian</a> at <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard Abbey</a>. While at Clonard, Aedan made friends with <a href="/wiki/Laisr%C3%A9n_mac_Nad_Fro%C3%ADch" title="Laisrén mac Nad Froích">Molaise</a>, who would later found the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Devenish_Island" title="Devenish Island">Devenish Island</a> on the <a href="/wiki/River_Erne" title="River Erne">River Erne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cib_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cib-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By this point, many began to come to the young man desiring to become his disciples. Instead of indulging them, he fled from Ireland to <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> to study under <a href="/wiki/St._David" class="mw-redirect" title="St. David">St. David</a>. Welsh legends place his upbringing with David and go into detail concerning attempts on his life by David's <a href="/wiki/Steward_(office)" title="Steward (office)">steward</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Along with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cadoc" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cadoc">Saint Cadoc</a>, he was said to have exterminated an army of <a href="/wiki/Saxon_invasion_of_Wales" class="mw-redirect" title="Saxon invasion of Wales">Saxons</a> or <a href="/wiki/Irish_invasions_of_Wales" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish invasions of Wales">Irishmen</a> by rolling stones upon their camp in a narrow valley. He was listed in the <a href="/wiki/Welsh_triads" class="mw-redirect" title="Welsh triads">Welsh triads</a> as one of David's three most faithful disciples. He returned to Ireland in 570, landing on the coast of <a href="/wiki/Wexford" title="Wexford">Wexford</a> with hives of <a href="/wiki/Western_honey_bee" title="Western honey bee">honey bees</a>, which he had been told were scarce on the island.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He landed as some locals were plundering another group of strangers and his quick response impressed the local chieftain, who granted him lands for religious communities.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He then settled at Brentrocht in Leinster. Aidan is said to have fasted for seven years, during this period he ate only <a href="/wiki/Barley_bread" title="Barley bread">barley bread</a> with water.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aidan seems to have played a role in the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Leinster" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Leinster">king of Leinster</a>'s defeat over the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Uí Néill">Uí Néill</a> <a href="/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland" title="High King of Ireland">High King</a> <a href="/wiki/%C3%81ed_mac_Ainmuirech" title="Áed mac Ainmuirech">Áed mac Ainmuirech</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_Conaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenél Conaill">Cenél Conaill</a> at the Battle of Dún Bolg in 598, either (according to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographers</a>) through the intercession of his prayers<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> or (according to the poets of the <i>Bóroma Laigen</i>) through a failed peace embassy followed by the successful idea to sneak Leinster's soldiers into the enemy camp inside food baskets.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> However the Aidan, Bishop of Glendalough, referred to in the tale is unlikely to have been St. Máedóc as they had different mothers. Maedóc's mother was Eithne while Aidan's mother was Bríg, daughter of Chobtaig m. <a href="/wiki/Crimthann_mac_%C3%89nnai" title="Crimthann mac Énnai">Crimthann mac Énnai</a> m. <a href="/wiki/%C3%89nnae_Cennsalach" title="Énnae Cennsalach">Énnae Cennsalach</a> of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Ceinnselaig" title="Uí Ceinnselaig">Uí Ceinnselaig</a> dynasty from <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The grateful <a href="/wiki/Brandub_mac_Echach" title="Brandub mac Echach">King Brandubh</a> then granted him <a href="/wiki/Ferns,_County_Wexford" title="Ferns, County Wexford">Ferns</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Wexford" title="County Wexford">County Wexford</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-cavan_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cavan-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> where he established a monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The influence of Brandubh also convened a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> whereat Ferns was not merely constituted a see but its bishop Aedan was also given nominal supremacy over the other Leinster bishops as their Chief Bishop (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Ard-Escop</i>). He was noted for his benevolence and hospitality:<sup id="cite_ref-cib_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cib-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> on one occasion, seeing them exhausted by their journey, he permitted beef to be given to a visiting delegation of British bishops during <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> and permitted them to excuse themselves with the claim that the slaughtered cow was merely "milk and vegetables in condensed form" whereas the <a href="/wiki/Whey" title="Whey">whey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Biscuit" title="Biscuit">biscuits</a> the other monks consumed had so many <a href="/wiki/Weevil" title="Weevil">weevils</a> as to occasion "conscientious scruples".<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On another, he was pushed into a lake to see whether he would lose his temper; upon his meekly restoring himself, his tormenter confessed himself and apologized.<sup id="cite_ref-cib_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cib-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aeddan himself died on 31 January 632 on <a href="/wiki/Lough_Melvin" title="Lough Melvin">Lough Melvin</a>'s shore in <a href="/wiki/County_Leitrim" title="County Leitrim">County Leitrim</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> are claimed by <a href="/wiki/Ferns_Cathedral" title="Ferns Cathedral">St. Edan's</a> in Ferns. His stone tomb is inside the cathedral, although his remains are in the original cathedral crypt below. The <a href="/wiki/Breac_Maodh%C3%B3g" title="Breac Maodhóg">Breac Maodhóg</a> (his <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a>) dates from the 9th century and is an example of an early medieval <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a>. It was often used as a sacred object upon which to swear binding oaths. It was acquired by the <a href="/wiki/National_Museum_of_Ireland" title="National Museum of Ireland">National Museum of Ireland</a> in the 1890s.<sup id="cite_ref-cavan_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cavan-7">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1ed%C3%B3c_of_Ferns&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Many miracles are recorded of St. Maedoc during his sojourn in Wales.<sup id="cite_ref-toke_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was said to have broken a jug while fetching <a href="/wiki/Ale" title="Ale">ale</a> for his fellow monks; making the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">sign of the cross</a> over the shards, however, it was repaired and he continued on his errand.<sup id="cite_ref-plummer_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-plummer-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A yoke given to him by David's steward purposefully too small to fit the necks of his oxen miraculously accommodated them and permitted him to bring the necessary materials for <a href="/wiki/Llanddewi_Velfrey" title="Llanddewi Velfrey">Llanddewi Velfrey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Following his return to Ireland, a local begged him for some meal as he was grinding flour and, after receiving some, disguised himself as a blind man to come back and beg for more. Annoyed, the saint cursed him that the generations of his descendants would never lack for a blind member.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Another time, wolves devoured a calf at one of his monasteries; its mother being inconsolable, Aedan blessed the head of his cook and told him to offer it to the heifer, which licked him and thenceforth "loved him like a calf".<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When asked by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fintan_Munnu" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Fintan Munnu">Saint Fintan Munnu</a> to heal monks suffering under an epidemic, Aedan was said to have indulged him: first by curing the monks and then by permitting the sickness to resume when Saint Fintan changed his mind, considering the sickness to be good for their souls.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His hagiographers credited Aidan's curses with Brandubh's defeat of the Uí Néill; they further state that, when Sarán slew his father-in-law, he attempted to accommodate the saint only for Aidan to curse him that his right hand would wither to the stump. When Sarán begged for a <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a>, Aidan directed him to pray for forgiveness at Brandubh's tomb in Ferns; when Sarán did so, a voice from the crypt forgave him. He lost his hand regardless.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Miraculous blessings from Aidan were credited with <a href="/wiki/Brandub_mac_Echach" title="Brandub mac Echach">King Brandubh</a>'s victory at <a href="/w/index.php?title=D%C3%BAn_Bolg&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Dún Bolg (page does not exist)">Dún Bolg</a> in 598; with the selection of his successor <a href="/w/index.php?title=Dachua&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Dachua (page does not exist)">Dachua</a> (Mochua Luachra); and with the success of the Irish architect <a href="/wiki/Gob%C3%A1n_Saor" title="Gobán Saor">Gobán Saor</a>. His heirs to <a href="/wiki/Rossinver" title="Rossinver">Rosinver Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Leitrim" title="County Leitrim">County Leitrim</a> and <a href="/wiki/Drumlane" title="Drumlane">Drumlane Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a> were the <a href="/wiki/Connachta" title="Connachta">Connachta</a> noblemen <a href="/wiki/%C3%93_Fearghail" title="Ó Fearghail">Fearghus Mac Ailill</a> and <a href="/wiki/Farrelly" title="Farrelly">Faircheallaigh Mac Ailill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A1ed%C3%B3c_of_Ferns&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg/220px-Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="229" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg/330px-Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2a/Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg/440px-Wexford_Church_of_the_Assumption_South_Aisle_Window_Harry_Clarke_The_Madonna_with_Sts_Aidan_and_Adrian_Detail_Saint_Aidan_2010_09_29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1332" data-file-height="1386" /></a><figcaption>Stained glass window of elderly Saint Áedan in profile at the Church of the Assumption, Wexford</figcaption></figure>
<p>He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Hy Kinsellagh or Wexford;<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Ferns,_County_Wexford" title="Ferns, County Wexford">Ferns</a>; and <a href="/wiki/Templeport" title="Templeport">Templeport</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a>. His feast is commemorated in <a href="/wiki/Bawnboy" title="Bawnboy">Bawnboy</a> with prayerful visits to his church and to the island where he was born. Mogue is no longer used as a <a href="/wiki/Given_name" title="Given name">Christian name</a> but the name Aidan is popular for boys in the West Cavan area. 
</p><p>The Catholic episcopal seat formerly located at <a href="/wiki/Ferns,_County_Wexford" title="Ferns, County Wexford">Ferns</a> (prior to its destruction) is now at <a href="/wiki/St._Aidan%27s_Cathedral" title="St. Aidan&#39;s Cathedral">St. Aidan's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Enniscorthy" title="Enniscorthy">Enniscorthy</a>, although the bishop resides at <a href="/wiki/Wexford" title="Wexford">Wexford</a>. The Anglican diocese is administered from <a href="/wiki/Kilkenny" title="Kilkenny">Kilkenny</a>, although <a href="/wiki/Ferns_Cathedral" title="Ferns Cathedral">St. Edan's Cathedral</a> in Ferns remains the seat of <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> diocese (itself part of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Cashel_and_Ossory" title="Diocese of Cashel and Ossory">United Dioceses of Cashel and Ossory</a>). Saint Áedan's is located on the site (and partially includes the ruins) of the earlier Catholic cathedral, which was burnt down in 1575 by the O'Byrnes of Wicklow. It was ordered to be rebuilt by Queen Elizabeth I during her reign, but was only half rebuilt.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aedan is credited as the founder of thirty churches and a number of monasteries. The first of these monasteries was on the island of his birth, now the site of 18th-century ruins and burial ground. The clay or mortar from the ruins of the church is said to provide protection against fire or drowning and is kept by many local people in their homes. Other monasteries include <a href="/wiki/Drumlane" title="Drumlane">Drumlane</a> (near <a href="/wiki/Milltown,_County_Cavan" title="Milltown, County Cavan">Milltown</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Cavan" title="County Cavan">County Cavan</a>); at <a href="/wiki/Ferns,_County_Wexford" title="Ferns, County Wexford">Ferns</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Wexford" title="County Wexford">County Wexford</a>; at Dissert-Nairbre in <a href="/wiki/County_Waterford" title="County Waterford">County Waterford</a>; and at <a href="/wiki/Rossinver" title="Rossinver">Rosinver</a> near the site of his death. In Wales he founded Saint Madoc of Ferns church in Haroldston West, Pembrokeshire.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The church of <a href="/wiki/Llawhaden" title="Llawhaden">Llawhaden</a>, also in <a href="/wiki/Pembrokeshire" title="Pembrokeshire">Pembrokeshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, commemorates him near the site of a ford he supposedly discovered <a href="#Miracles">while leading his oxen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_2-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
