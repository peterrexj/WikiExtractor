<p><b>Marija Petković</b>, also known as "The Blessed Mary of Jesus Crucified Petković"; (Croatian: <i>Marija od Propetoga Isusa Petković</i>, Italian <i>Maria Di Gesù Crocifisso</i>), (10 December 1892 -  9 July 1966) was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Catholic</a> Congregation of the Daughters of Mercy.  She was recognized by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> on 8 May 1998, and was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 6 June 2003.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marija_Petkovi%C4%87&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Petković was born in the small town of <a href="/wiki/Blato,_Kor%C4%8Dula" title="Blato, Korčula">Blato</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatian</a> island of <a href="/wiki/Kor%C4%8Dula" title="Korčula">Korčula</a> (at that time part of <a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austria-Hungary</a>), the sixth of eleven children born to Marija Marinović and Antun Petković-Kovač.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She died in Rome, Italy.
</p><p>After elementary school, Petković enrolled in 1904 in Blato's municipal school, which had been recently founded by the <a href="/wiki/Servants_of_Charity" title="Servants of Charity">Servants of Charity</a>, a Catholic order newly arrived from Italy.  After successfully completing the three-year program, she continued her studies at the School of Domestic Science, also directed by the Servants of Charity.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1906 she joined the association of the Daughters of Mary. About this time she revealed to Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Josip_Marceli%C4%87&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Josip Marcelić (page does not exist)">Josip Marcelić</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dubrovnik" title="Dubrovnik">Dubrovnik</a> that she wanted to enter the convent, which marked the beginning of Marija's spiritual direction under the Bishop's care. On 21 November 1906, she made a private vow of chastity to <a href="/wiki/Yahweh" title="Yahweh">the Lord</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1909 to 1919 Petković was president of the Daughters of Mary. Although frail and frequently ill, in addition to her obligations in her parents' home, she provided <a href="/wiki/Catechesis" title="Catechesis">catechesis</a> and instruction in general subjects to the children of the families whose parents worked on her father's estate.  In 1911 her father died, leaving her to help her mother care for the family and provide for the education of the other children. That labor, amid the destruction wreaked in Croatia by World War I, was very influential in Petković's vocational discernment.  She became involved in a number of Catholic organizations, and, in 1915, under the guidance of Bishop Josip, began her first new association, the Society of Catholic Mothers. In 1917 she assumed the responsibility of guiding the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_Franciscans" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order Franciscans">Third Order Franciscans</a>. That same year, she began helping the Servants of Charity in the "soup kitchen" that they directed.<sup id="cite_ref-sydney_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sydney-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1918, in front of the citizens of Blato, Petković made a formal promise to the Bishop to remain in Blato to help and live with the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-va_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-va-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 25 March 1919, Petković and her friend, Marija Telenta, entered the convent of the Servants of Charity. Two months later, however, the Mother Superior died and the other sisters, for political reasons, returned to Italy. Petković, her companion, and two other nuns were all that remained of the convent.<sup id="cite_ref-sydney_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sydney-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bishop Josip watched over and advised the four Sisters, especially Petković, whom he counseled in humility and service. She requested from him the Rule of the Third Order Franciscans, and, in the winter of 1919, opened three institutions in Blato: a day-recovery centre, a child-care facility, and an orphanage.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>On 25 August 1920, in <a href="/wiki/Pri%C5%BEba" title="Prižba">Prižba</a> (on the island of <a href="/wiki/Kor%C4%8Dula" title="Korčula">Korčula</a>), Petković wrote the first Constitutions of the Order of Daughters of Mercy, the Third Independent Order of St. Francis, setting forth their mission of spreading knowledge of divine love and mercy through charitable acts.  Bishop Josip inaugurated the Order on 4 October, the Feast of St. <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>.  On that day, Petković received the name of <i>Marija od Propetoga Isusa</i> (Mary of Jesus Crucified) and was chosen as the Mother Superior of the Order.  When the nuns in Blato had exhausted their own funds for helping poor and hungry children, She went to fertile plains of <a href="/wiki/Slavonia" title="Slavonia">Slavonia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka" title="Bačka">Bačka</a> to solicit help for the orphans and widows, receiving, among other contributions, a donation from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>. In return, Bishop of Bačka <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Administration" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Administration">Apostolic Administration</a>, Ljudevit Lajčo Budanović, asked Petković to found monasteries of her Order in Subotica and surrounding areas, so the locals could get the spiritual gain that nuns of her Order could provide them.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Noticing that Bačka also had problems of numerous poor and abandoned children, in 1923, Petković acted quickly and opened <i>Kolijevka</i> Children's Home in <a href="/wiki/Subotica" title="Subotica">Subotica</a>, Home for sick and neglected children, after which she opened numerous centers for children in <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Slavonia" title="Slavonia">Slavonia</a> particularly), <a href="/wiki/Serbia" title="Serbia">Serbia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Vardar_Macedonia" title="Vardar Macedonia">Vardar Macedonia</a>.
</p><p>From 1920-52, Petković was elected five times as the Superior General of the Daughters of Mercy. She traveled throughout <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, Latin America, and Italy, opening 46 communities. The growing number of Sisters served in various social ministries, such as in nursing homes, <a href="/wiki/Kindergarten" title="Kindergarten">kindergartens</a>, hospitals, nursery schools, parish work, and work in the seminaries.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Beginning in 1936, the Order was prominent in Latin America and South America. Petković lived in <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a> from 1940–52, promoting charity work in many countries in Latin America.<sup id="cite_ref-sydney_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sydney-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In addition to catechesis, she organized instruction in hygiene, economics, homemaking, sewing and tailoring, knitting, nursing, and typing.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> In 1952, she went to Rome to purchase and set up the Congregation's headquarters. While there in 1954 she suffered a stroke which left her permanently paralyzed. In 1961, she concluded that her duty to the Congregation was no longer one of leadership but of sacrifice and suffering, prayer and offering herself to the Lord, and relinquished the office of Mother Superior that she had held for 40 years.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>She continued to follow the events in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and her Congregation, especially those changes brought about by the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, until age 74, when she died. She was buried in the Roman cemetery of <a href="/wiki/Campo_Verano" title="Campo Verano">Campo Verano</a>. Three years later, her remains were translated to the house chapel of the Congregation headquarters, where they remained until 1998 when they were translated to her native Blato and buried in the crypt of the monastery chapel of Christ the King.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marija_Petkovi%C4%87&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 26 August 1988, the <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peruvian</a> <a href="/wiki/Submarine" title="Submarine">submarine</a> <a href="/wiki/BAP_Pacocha_(SS-48)" title="BAP Pacocha (SS-48)">BAP <i>Pacocha</i></a> was rammed and sunk by a Japanese fishing <a href="/wiki/Commercial_trawler" class="mw-redirect" title="Commercial trawler">trawler</a>. <i>Teniente</i> (lieutenant) Roger Cotrina Alvarado, a Peruvian naval officer, distinguished himself during the disaster – praying for Petković's aid, Cotrina displayed superhuman strength in opening a hatch against several thousands of pounds of water. That action prevented the immediate deaths of 22 crewmen, and Cotrina's subsequent actions brought about their rescue.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 28 February 1989, the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_the_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of the Saints">Congregation for the Causes of the Saints</a> began an investigation into the reports of the <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a>.  On 8 May 1998, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed the Congregation's initial report and decreed that Petković's <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtues</a> be publicly declared, formally recognizing her as a "Servant of God", to be entitled "Venerable."
</p><p>The Congregation issued their ruling on Petković's heroic virtues on 5 July 2002, and on the following 20 December issued a ruling recognizing the miraculous nature of the submarine rescue.  On 6 June 2003, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> celebrated her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in <a href="/wiki/Dubrovnik" title="Dubrovnik">Dubrovnik</a>, formally recognizing her as "Blessed."
</p><p>Today, her Congregation has its branches, besides other countries, in Italy, Romania (by <a href="/wiki/Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Catholic">Greek Catholic</a> community), Paraguay, Chile, Peru and Argentina.
</p><p>The Sanctuary of Blessed Marija Petković is in her town of birth, Blato on the Dalmatian island of Korčula.
</p>
