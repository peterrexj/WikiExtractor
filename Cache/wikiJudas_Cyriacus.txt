<p><b>Judas Cyriacus (Cyriacus of Ancona, Cyriacus of Jerusalem, Quiriacus, Quiricus, Kyriakos)</b>; <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Quirico</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Ciriaco</i>), d. ca. AD 360, is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> on 4 May.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Judas_Cyriacus.2C_Bishop_of_Ancona"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Judas_Cyriacus,_Bishop_of_Ancona">Judas Cyriacus, Bishop of Ancona</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judas_Cyriacus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Judas Cyriacus, Bishop of Ancona">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is said to have been the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ancona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ancona">bishop of Ancona</a> who died or was killed during a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. His feast is celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/April_14_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="April 14 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">14 April</a>.
</p><p>(Judas Cyriacus of Ancona is often confused with the legendary Bishop <a href="/wiki/Judah_Kyriakos" title="Judah Kyriakos">Judas Cyriacus</a> of Jerusalem (<i>Saint Cyriacus of Jerusalem</i>), who was killed during a disturbance there, in 133 AD. The 2nd century Bishop Judah Kyriakos of Jerusalem is said to the last in the <a href="/wiki/Desposyni" class="mw-redirect" title="Desposyni">desposynic</a> line for that post, some of his predecessors being descendants from the family of Jesus.  This meant that '2nd century' Judah Kyriakos was the last Christian Bishop of Jerusalem of Jewish origins.)
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Judas_Cyriacus_and_the_True_Cross">Judas Cyriacus and the True Cross</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judas_Cyriacus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Judas Cyriacus and the True Cross">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg/200px-Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="133" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg/300px-Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg/400px-Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1949" data-file-height="1295" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_003.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The martyrdom of Judas Cyriacus.</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_(Venice)_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_%28Venice%29_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg/220px-Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_%28Venice%29_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="476" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_%28Venice%29_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg/330px-Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_%28Venice%29_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_%28Venice%29_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg/440px-Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_%28Venice%29_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2603" data-file-height="5632" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Interior_of_Chiesa_dei_Gesuiti_(Venice)_-_sacristy_-_Judas_Cyriacus_by_Palma_il_Giovane.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Judas Cyriacus by <a href="/wiki/Palma_il_Giovane" title="Palma il Giovane">Palma il Giovane</a></div></div></div>
<p>The local tradition of Ancona has identified this saint with the <a href="/wiki/Jew" class="mw-redirect" title="Jew">Jew</a> named <b>Judas Quiriacus</b> or <b>Kyriakos</b>.
</p><p>According to legend, a Jew Judas Kyriakos aided the Empress <a href="/wiki/Helena_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Helena of Constantinople">Helena</a> in finding the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a>, which had been buried at <a href="/wiki/Golgotha" class="mw-redirect" title="Golgotha">Golgotha</a> after the crucifixion. The oldest extant <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> text of the legend of the discovery of the True Cross by Judas Kyriakos dates from c. 500 AD. Its recent editor and translator says that the manuscript is "of great value for the history of the legend of the <i>inventio crucis</i>".<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Sozomen" title="Sozomen">Sozomen</a> (died c. 450 AD), in his <i>Ecclesiastical History</i> <a rel="nofollow" class="external autonumber" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20080518020255/http://www.ccel.org/fathers2/NPNF2-02/Npnf2-02-20.htm#P3156_1288060">[1]</a>, states that it was said (by whom he does not say) that the location of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Sepulchre" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Sepulchre">Holy Sepulchre</a> was "disclosed by a Hebrew who dwelt in the East, and who derived his information from some documents which had come to him by paternal inheritance" (although Sozomen himself disputes this account) and that a dead person was also revived by the touch of the Cross.  Later, popular versions of this story state that a Jew who assisted Helena was named Jude or Judas, but later converted to Christianity and took the name Kyriakos (kyriakos means "lordly" or "lord-like" in Greek).
</p><p>Among the three accounts about the discovery of the True Cross that circulated throughout the Roman Empire in the 4th century,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Accuracy_dispute#Disputed_statement" title="Wikipedia:Accuracy dispute"><span title="The material near this tag is possibly inaccurate or nonfactual. (December 2018)">dubious</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Judas_Cyriacus#Which_are...?_Which_one_credited_smb._else_than_Helena?" title="Talk:Judas Cyriacus">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup> the two most widely repeated both credited Helena, the aged mother of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine the Great</a>, who travelled to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> at her son's request. To recover it, it was necessary to demolish a temple, perhaps dedicated to Venus, that occupied the site. In one, Judas knew of the location of the Cross; he had been the recipient of that secret knowledge which was handed down the paternal line of his family, and revealed it under torture. As J. W. Drijvers, the editor of the text, has noted,
</p>
<blockquote><p>The Judas Kyriakos legend originated in Greek, but became also known in Latin and Syriac and later on in many vernacular languages. This version relates how Helena discovered the Cross with the help of a Jew Judas, who later converted and received the name Kyriakos. It became the most popular version of the three.</p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_martyrdom_of_Judas_Cyriacus">The martyrdom of Judas Cyriacus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judas_Cyriacus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The martyrdom of Judas Cyriacus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Judas_Cyriacus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Judas_Cyriacus.jpg/200px-Judas_Cyriacus.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="120" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d8/Judas_Cyriacus.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="283" data-file-height="170" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Judas_Cyriacus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The body of Judas Cyriacus in the Cathedral of Ancona.</div></div></div>
<p>After assisting Helena with the finding of the True Cross, Judas Cyriacus was baptized, consecrated as bishop of Jerusalem, and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> during the persecutions of <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>, which would place his death in the 4th century.  Another saint, named <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cyriacus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cyriacus">Saint Cyriacus</a>, died during this century, and there may have been confusion between the two saints.
</p><p>In the legendary <i>Acts</i> of his martyrdom, at first written in Greek and than translated in Latin and Syriac,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> he engaged in dialogue with the emperor Julian and, along with his mother Anna, was described as being subjected to horrible torments.
The <a href="/wiki/Galla_Placidia" title="Galla Placidia">Empress Galla Placidia</a> is said to have presented Ancona with the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of Judas Cyriacus.  However, the saint's head was located at <a href="/wiki/Provins" title="Provins">Provins</a>. <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Champagne" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry I of Champagne">Henry I of Champagne</a> brought it from <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> and built a church in this town to contain it.  This still stands as the Saint Quiriace Collegiate Church, although construction work during the 12th century was never completed due to financial difficulties during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Philippe_le_Bel" class="mw-redirect" title="Philippe le Bel">Philippe le Bel</a>.  A <a href="/wiki/Dome" title="Dome">dome</a> was added in the 17th century, and the old families of Provins who lived in the upper town were called "Children of the Dome." <a rel="nofollow" class="external autonumber" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20060525185521/http://www.provins.net/anglais/_private/cadrehistoriq.htm">[2]</a>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cathedral_of_San_Ciriaco">Cathedral of San Ciriaco</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Judas_Cyriacus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cathedral of San Ciriaco">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Ancona_Cathedral" title="Ancona Cathedral">Ancona Cathedral</a></div>
<p>Monte Guasco, in Ancona, the location of the <a href="/wiki/Duomo" title="Duomo">Duomo</a> is dedicated to Saint Judas Cyriacus.  It is said to occupy the site of a temple of <a href="/wiki/Venus_(god)" class="mw-redirect" title="Venus (god)">Venus</a>, who is mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Catullus" title="Catullus">Catullus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Satires_of_Juvenal" class="mw-redirect" title="Satires of Juvenal">Juvenal</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Tutelary_deity" title="Tutelary deity">tutelary deity</a> of the place.
</p><p>It was consecrated in 1128 and completed in 1189. Some writers suppose that the original church was in the form of a <a href="/wiki/Latin_cross" title="Latin cross">Latin cross</a> and belonged to the 8th century. An early restoration was completed in 1234. It is a fine <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a> building in grey stone, built in the form of a <a href="/wiki/Greek_cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek cross">Greek cross</a>, with a dodecagonal dome over the center slightly altered by Margaritone d'Arezzo in 1270.
</p><p>The body purported to be Cyriacus' lies prostrate and visible in his tomb.
</p>
