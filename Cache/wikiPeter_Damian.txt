<p><b>Peter Damian</b>, OSB (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Petrus Damianus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Pietro</i> or <i><span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Pier Damiani</i></span></i>; <span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1007</span>&#160;&#8211; 21 or 22 February 1072 or 1073)<sup id="cite_ref-howe_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-howe-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Reform" title="Gregorian Reform">reforming</a> <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism" title="Christian monasticism">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> in the circle of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo&#160;IX</a>. <a href="/wiki/Dante" class="mw-redirect" title="Dante">Dante</a> placed him in one of the highest circles of <i><a href="/wiki/Paradiso_(Dante)" title="Paradiso (Dante)">Paradiso</a></i> as a great predecessor of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> and he was declared a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a> on 27 September 1828. His feast day is 21 February.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter was born in <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> around 1007,<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the youngest of a large but poor noble family. Orphaned early, he was at first adopted by an elder brother, who ill-treated and under-fed him while employing him as a swineherd. After some years, another brother, Damianus, who was <a href="/wiki/Archpriest" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a> at Ravenna, had pity on him and took him away to be educated. Adding his brother's name to his own, Peter made such rapid progress in his studies of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon law</a>, first at Ravenna, then at <a href="/wiki/Faenza" title="Faenza">Faenza</a>, and finally at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Parma" title="University of Parma">University of Parma</a>, that, around the age of 25, he was already a famous teacher at <a href="/wiki/Parma" title="Parma">Parma</a> and Ravenna.<sup id="cite_ref-holopainen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holopainen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life">Religious life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Religious life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg/220px-Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="299" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg/330px-Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg/440px-Ercole_de%27_Roberti_007.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2024" data-file-height="2755" /></a><figcaption>Peter Damian (far right), depicted with <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Anne" title="Saint Anne">Anne</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_(Biblical_person)" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth (Biblical person)">Elizabeth</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>About 1035, however, he gave up his secular calling and, avoiding the compromised luxury of <a href="/wiki/Cluny" title="Cluny">Cluniac monasteries</a>, entered the isolated <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> of <a href="/wiki/Fonte_Avellana" title="Fonte Avellana">Fonte Avellana</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Gubbio" title="Gubbio">Gubbio</a>. Both as a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> and as a monk, his fervour was remarkable but led him to such extremes of self-mortification in <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> that his health was affected, and he developed severe insomnia.<sup id="cite_ref-holopainen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holopainen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>On his recovery, he was appointed to lecture to his fellow monks. Then, at the request of Guy of Pomposa (Guido d'Arezzo) and other heads of neighbouring monasteries, for two or three years he lectured to their brethren also, and (about 1042) wrote the life of <a href="/wiki/Romuald" title="Romuald">Romuald</a> for the monks of <a href="/wiki/Pietrapertosa" title="Pietrapertosa">Pietrapertosa</a>. Soon after his return to Fonte Avellana he was appointed <i>economus</i> (manager or housekeeper) of the house by the <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a>, who designated him as his successor. In 1043 he became prior of Fonte Avellana, and remained so until his death in February 1072.<sup id="cite_ref-holopainen_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holopainen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Subject-hermitages were founded at <a href="/wiki/San_Severino_Marche" title="San Severino Marche">San Severino</a>, Gamogna, Acerreta, Murciana, San Salvatore, <a href="/wiki/Scheggia_e_Pascelupo" title="Scheggia e Pascelupo">Sitria</a> and Ocri. A zealot for monastic and clerical reform, he introduced a more-severe discipline, including the practice of <a href="/wiki/Flagellation" title="Flagellation">flagellation</a> ("the <i>disciplina</i>") into the house, which, under his rule, quickly attained celebrity, and became a model for other foundations, even the great <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> of <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>. There was much opposition outside his own circle to such extreme forms of penitence, but Peter's persistent advocacy ensured its acceptance, to such an extent that he was obliged later to moderate the imprudent zeal of some of his own hermits.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another innovation was that of the daily <a href="/wiki/Siesta" title="Siesta">siesta</a>, to make up for the fatigue of the night office. During his tenure of the priorate, a <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloister</a> was built, silver <a href="/wiki/Chalice_(cup)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chalice (cup)">chalices</a> and a silver <a href="/wiki/Processional_cross" title="Processional cross">processional cross</a> were purchased, and many books were added to the library.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reformer">Reformer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Reformer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif/lossy-page1-220px-Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="336" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif/lossy-page1-330px-Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif/lossy-page1-440px-Sancti_Petri_Damiani_Opera_Omnia.tif.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4329" data-file-height="6620" /></a><figcaption><i>Sancti Petri Damiani Opera Omnia</i> (1743)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Although living in the seclusion of the cloister, Peter Damian closely watched the fortunes of the church, and like his friend Hildebrand, the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>, he strove for reforms in a deplorable time. After almost two centuries of political and social upheaval, doctrinal ignorance and the petty venality among the clergy had reached intolerable levels. When the scandalous <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_IX" title="Pope Benedict IX">Benedict IX</a> resigned the pontificate into the hands of the <a href="/wiki/Archpriest" title="Archpriest">archpriest</a> John Gratian (<a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VI" title="Pope Gregory VI">Gregory VI</a>) in 1045, Peter hailed the change with joy and wrote to the new pope, urging him to deal with the scandals of the church in Italy, singling out the wicked bishops of <a href="/wiki/Pesaro" title="Pesaro">Pesaro</a>, of <a href="/wiki/Citt%C3%A0_di_Castello" title="Città di Castello">Città di Castello</a> and of <a href="/wiki/Fano" title="Fano">Fano</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Extending the area of his activities, he entered into communication with the <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Henry III</a>. He was present in Rome when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_II" title="Pope Clement II">Clement II</a> crowned Henry III and his consort <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Poitou" title="Agnes of Poitou">Agnes</a>, and he also attended a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> held at the <a href="/wiki/Lateran" title="Lateran">Lateran</a> in the first days of 1047, in which decrees were passed against <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After this he returned to his hermitage. Damian published a constant stream of open letters on a variety of theological and disciplinary controversies. About 1050, he wrote <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Gomorrhianus" title="Liber Gomorrhianus">Liber Gomorrhianus</a></i> addressed to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo IX</a>, containing a scathing indictment of the practice of sodomy, as threatening the integrity of the clergy. Meanwhile, the question arose as to the validity of the ordinations of simoniacal clerics. Peter Damian wrote (about 1053) a treatise, the <i>Liber Gratissimus</i>, in favour of their validity, a work which, though much combatted at the time, was potent in deciding the question in their favor before the end of the 12th century. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> described him as "one of the most significant figures of the 11th century, ... a lover of solitude and at the same time a fearless man of the Church, committed personally to the task of reform."<sup id="cite_ref-cna_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Peter often condemned <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a>. He claimed that the first grammarian was the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">Devil</a>, who taught <a href="/wiki/Adam_(Bible)" class="mw-redirect" title="Adam (Bible)">Adam</a> to decline <i>deus</i> in the plural. He argued that monks should not have to study philosophy, because <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a> did not choose philosophers as disciples, and so philosophy is not necessary for salvation. But the idea (later attributed to <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a>) that philosophy should serve theology as a servant serves her mistress originated with him.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> However, this apparent animosity may reflect his view that logic is only concerned with the validity of argument, rather than the nature of reality. Similar views are found in <a href="/wiki/Al-Ghazali" title="Al-Ghazali">Al-Ghazali</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wittgenstein" class="mw-redirect" title="Wittgenstein">Wittgenstein</a>.
</p><p>Damian's tract <i>De divina omnipotentia</i> is frequently misunderstood. Damian's purpose is to defend the "doctrine of omnipotence", which he defines as the ability of God to do anything that is good, e.g., God cannot lie. Toivo J. Holopainen identifies <i>De divina omnipotentia</i> as "an interesting document related to the early developments of medieval discussion concerning modalities and divine omnipotence."<sup id="cite_ref-holopainen_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-holopainen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Peter also recognized that God can act outside time, as <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Rimini" title="Gregory of Rimini">Gregory of Rimini</a> later argued.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_envoy_and_Cardinal">Papal envoy and Cardinal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Papal envoy and Cardinal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During his illness the pope died, and Frédéric, <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Monte_Cassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Monte Cassino">abbot of Monte Cassino</a>, was elected pope as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_IX" title="Pope Stephen IX">Stephen IX</a>. In the autumn of 1057, Stephen IX determined to make Damian a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a>. For a long time, Damian resisted the offer, for he was more at ease as an itinerant hermit-preacher than a reformer from within the <a href="/wiki/Curia" title="Curia">Curia</a>, but was finally forced to accept, and was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Bishop_of_Ostia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Bishop of Ostia">Cardinal Bishop of Ostia</a> on 30 November 1057.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In addition he was appointed administrator of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Gubbio" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Gubbio">Diocese of Gubbio</a>. The new cardinal was impressed with the great responsibilities of his office and wrote a stirring letter to his brother-cardinals, exhorting them to shine by their example before all. Four months later Pope Stephen died in Florence, and the church was once more distracted by <a href="/wiki/Schism_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Schism (religion)">schism</a>. Peter was vigorous in his opposition to the <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Benedict_X" title="Antipope Benedict X">Benedict X</a>, but the force was on the side of the intruder and Damian retired temporarily to Fonte Avallana.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Milan">Milan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Milan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"><table class="box-One_source plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-one_source" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/50px-Question_book-new.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="39" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/75px-Question_book-new.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/100px-Question_book-new.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="399" /></a></span></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>relies largely or entirely on a <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_with_a_single_source" title="Wikipedia:Articles with a single source">single source</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Relevant discussion may be found on the <a href="/wiki/Talk:Peter_Damian##" title="Talk:Peter Damian">talk page</a>. <p>Around the end of 1059, Peter was sent as <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legate</a> to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II" title="Pope Nicholas II">Pope Nicholas II</a>. So bad was the state of things at Milan, that <a href="/wiki/Benefice" title="Benefice">benefices</a> (a reward received in exchange for services rendered and as a retainer for future services) were openly bought and sold, and the clergy publicly married the women with whom they lived. The resistance of the clergy of Milan to the reform of <a href="/wiki/Ariald_the_Deacon" class="mw-redirect" title="Ariald the Deacon">Ariald the Deacon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Lucca" title="Anselm of Lucca">Anselm of Lucca</a> rendered a contest so bitter that an appeal was made to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>. 
</p><p>Nicholas II sent Damian and the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lucca" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Lucca">Bishop of Lucca</a> as his legates. The party of the irregular clerics took alarm and raised the cry that Rome had no authority over Milan. Peter boldly confronted the rioters in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> and proved to them the authority of the Holy See with such effect that all parties submitted to his decision.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He exacted first a solemn oath from the archbishop and all his clergy that for the future no preferment should be paid for; then, imposing a <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> on all who had been guilty, he reinstated in their benefices all who undertook to live in celibacy. The prudent decision was attacked by some of the rigorists at Rome but was not reversed. Unfortunately, on the death of Nicholas II, the same disputes broke out, and they were not finally settled till after the martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Arialdo" title="Arialdo">Arialdo</a> in 1066. Meanwhile, Peter was pleading in vain to be released from the cares of his office. Neither Nicholas II nor Hildebrand would consent to spare him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_career">Later career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Later career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1097763485"><table class="box-More_citations_needed plainlinks metadata ambox ambox-content ambox-Refimprove" role="presentation"><tbody><tr><td class="mbox-image"><div class="mbox-image-div"><span typeof="mw:File"><a href="/wiki/File:Question_book-new.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/50px-Question_book-new.svg.png" decoding="async" width="50" height="39" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/75px-Question_book-new.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/99/Question_book-new.svg/100px-Question_book-new.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="512" data-file-height="399" /></a></span></div></td><td class="mbox-text"><div class="mbox-text-span">This section <b>needs additional citations for <a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability">verification</a></b>.<span class="hide-when-compact"> Please help <a href="/wiki/Special:EditPage/Peter_Damian" title="Special:EditPage/Peter Damian">improve this article</a> by <a href="/wiki/Help:Referencing_for_beginners" title="Help:Referencing for beginners">adding citations to reliable sources</a>&#32;in this section. Unsourced material may be challenged and removed.<br /><small><span class="plainlinks"><i>Find sources:</i>&#160;<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?as_eq=wikipedia&amp;q=%22Peter+Damian%22">"Peter Damian"</a>&#160;–&#160;<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?tbm=nws&amp;q=%22Peter+Damian%22+-wikipedia&amp;tbs=ar:1">news</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?&amp;q=%22Peter+Damian%22&amp;tbs=bkt:s&amp;tbm=bks">newspapers</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.google.com/search?tbs=bks:1&amp;q=%22Peter+Damian%22+-wikipedia">books</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Peter+Damian%22">scholar</a>&#160;<b>·</b> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.jstor.org/action/doBasicSearch?Query=%22Peter+Damian%22&amp;acc=on&amp;wc=on">JSTOR</a></span></small></span>  <span class="date-container"><i>(<span class="date">December 2017</span>)</i></span><span class="hide-when-compact"><i> (<small><a href="/wiki/Help:Maintenance_template_removal" title="Help:Maintenance template removal">Learn how and when to remove this template message</a></small>)</i></span></div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>He rendered valuable assistance to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a> in his struggle with the antipope, <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Honorius_II" title="Antipope Honorius II">Honorius II</a>. In July 1061 Pope Nicholas II died and once more a schism ensued. Peter Damian used all his powers to persuade the antipope Cadalous to withdraw, but to no purpose. Finally <a href="/wiki/Anno_II,_Archbishop_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Anno II, Archbishop of Cologne">Anno II, Archbishop of Cologne</a> and acting regent in Germany, summoned a council at <a href="/wiki/Augsburg" title="Augsburg">Augsburg</a> at which a long argument by Peter Damian was read and greatly contributed to the decision in favour of Alexander II.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1063 the pope held a synod at Rome, at which Peter Damian was appointed legate to settle the dispute between the <a href="/wiki/Cluny" title="Cluny">Abbey of Cluny</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_M%C3%A2con" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Mâcon">Bishop of Mâcon</a>. He proceeded to France, summoned a council at <a href="/wiki/Chalon-sur-Sa%C3%B4ne" title="Chalon-sur-Saône">Chalon-sur-Saône</a>, proved the justice of the contentions of Cluny, settled other questions at issue in the church of France, and returned in the autumn to Fonte Avellana.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>While he was in France the antipope Cadalous had again become active in his attempts to gain Rome, and Peter Damian brought upon himself a sharp reproof from Alexander and Hildebrand for twice imprudently appealing to the royal power to judge the case anew. In 1067, the cardinal was sent to Florence to settle the dispute between the bishop and the monks of <a href="/wiki/Vallombrosa" title="Vallombrosa">Vallombrosa</a>, who accused the former of <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a>. His efforts, however, were not successful, largely because he misjudged the case and threw the weight of his authority on the side of the bishop. The matter was not settled until the following year by the pope in person.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Having served the papacy as legate to France and to Florence, he was allowed to resign his bishopric in 1067. After a period of retirement at Fonte Avellana, he proceeded in 1069 as papal legate to Germany, and persuaded the <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">emperor Henry IV</a> to give up his intention of divorcing his wife <a href="/wiki/Bertha_of_Savoy" title="Bertha of Savoy">Bertha</a>. He accomplished this task at a council in <a href="/wiki/Frankfurt" title="Frankfurt">Frankfurt</a> before returning to Fonte-Avellana.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Early in 1072 or 1073<sup id="cite_ref-howe_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-howe-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> he was sent to Ravenna to reconcile its inhabitants to the Holy See, they having been excommunicated for supporting their archbishop in his adhesion to the schism of <a href="/wiki/Cadalous" class="mw-redirect" title="Cadalous">Cadalous</a>. On his return thence he was seized with fever near Faenza. He lay ill for a week at the monastery of Santa Maria degl'Angeli, now Santa Maria Vecchia. On the night preceding the feast of the Chair of St. Peter at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, he ordered the office of the feast to be recited and at the end of the Lauds he died. He was at once buried in the monastery church, lest others should claim his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>During his concluding years, he was not altogether in accord with the political ideas of Hildebrand. He died the year before Hildebrand became pope, as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Gregory VII</a>. "It removed from the scene the one man who could have restrained Gregory", Norman F. Cantor remarked (<i>Civilization of the Middle Ages</i>, p 251).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Damian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Peter Damian is venerated as a saint and was made a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XII" title="Pope Leo XII">Pope Leo XII</a> on 27 September 1828 with a feast day which is now celebrated on 21 February (Ordinary calendar).<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In 1970, his feast was moved there from 23 February where it had been before.
</p><p>His body has been moved six times. Since 1898, Peter Damian has rested in a chapel dedicated to the saint in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Faenza" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Faenza">cathedral of Faenza</a>. No formal <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> ever took place, but his <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> has existed since his death at Faenza, at Fonte-Avellana, at Monte Cassino, and at Cluny.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>The saint is represented in art as a cardinal bearing a knotted rope (the <i>disciplina</i>) in his hand; also sometimes he is depicted as a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrim</a> holding a <a href="/wiki/Papal_Bull" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Bull">papal Bull</a>, to signify his many legations.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>

<p>Peter Damian's voluminous writings, including treatises (67 survive), letters, sermons, prayers, hymns and liturgical texts (though, in a departure from many early medieval monks, no biblical commentaries)<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> reflect the spiritual conditions of Italy: the groundswell of intense personal piety that would overflow in the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a> at the end of the century, and his <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> abounds in denunciatory epithets.
</p><p>His works include: 
</p>
<ul><li>His most famous work is <i>De Divina Omnipotentia</i>, a long letter in which he discusses God's power. The <i>De Divina Omnipotentia</i> purports to be a letter from Peter Damian to Desiderius, <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>. Peter develops a position he had taken in an earlier discussion with Desiderius on the claim of <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">St. Jerome</a> that, although God can do all things, he cannot restore <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virginity</a> to a woman who had lost it. Desiderius had sided with Jerome; Damian had claimed that God could indeed restore lost virginity. In this letter Peter defends his views, an undertaking that takes him into the discussion of the scope of <a href="/wiki/Omnipotence" title="Omnipotence">divine power</a>, the possibility of God's annulling the past, and the problems that arise from using the language of human <a href="/wiki/Temporality" title="Temporality">temporality</a> to describe divine possibilities in an eternal present. The central question of the nature and scope of divine power is related to previous discussions of the question and to the more sophisticated debates of the later Middle Ages. Damian's apparent claims that the law of contradiction does not apply to God and that God is able to annul the past deserve recognition. In these discussions Damian shows himself the equal of any of the dialecticians that he so severely criticizes.</li>
<li>In the short treatise <i>Dominus vobiscum</i> (<i>The Book of "The Lord be with You"</i>) (PL 145:231-252), he questions whether a hermit praying in solitude should use the plural; Damian concludes that the hermit should use the plural, since he is linked to the whole church by faith and fellowship.</li>
<li>His <i>Life of Romauld</i> and his treatise <i>The Eremitical Order</i> demonstrate his continuing commitment to solitude and severe asceticism as the ultimate form of Christian life.</li>
<li>He was especially devoted to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>, and wrote an <i><a href="/wiki/Officium_Beatae_Virginis" class="mw-redirect" title="Officium Beatae Virginis">Officium Beatae Virginis</a>.</i></li>
<li>The treaty about sodomy and insiders of the Catholic Church called <a href="/wiki/Liber_Gomorrhianus" title="Liber Gomorrhianus">Liber Gomorrhianus</a></li>
<li><i>De Institutione monialis</i>, which had the aim of safeguarding Western Christians from the decadent uses of the East. Notable in this work, among other things, Damiani, then Bishop of <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica_(district)" title="Ostia Antica (district)">Ostia</a>, condemned <a href="/wiki/Maria_Argyre" class="mw-redirect" title="Maria Argyre">Maria Argyre</a>'s use of a golden fork to eat. Forks were a new invention at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>Disceptatio synodalis</i>, in defense of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a> against <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Honorius_II" title="Antipope Honorius II">Antipope Honorius II</a></li>
<li><i>De Sancta Simplicitate</i></li>
<li><i>Liber Gratissimus</i>, against <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a></li></ul>
