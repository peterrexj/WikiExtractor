<p><b>Laeti</b> <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/ɛ/: &#39;e&#39; in &#39;dress&#39;">ɛ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/aɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;tide&#39;">aɪ</span></span>/</a></span></span>, the plural form of <b>laetus</b> <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/iː/: &#39;ee&#39; in &#39;fleece&#39;">iː</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span>, was a term used in the late <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a> to denote communities of <i>barbari</i> ("<a href="/wiki/Barbarians" class="mw-redirect" title="Barbarians">barbarians</a>"), i.e. foreigners, or people from outside the Empire, permitted to settle on, and granted land in, imperial territory on condition that they provide recruits for the Roman military.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The term <i>laetus</i> is of uncertain origin. It means "lucky" or "happy" in <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> but may derive from a non-Latin word. It may derive from a <a href="/wiki/Germanic_languages" title="Germanic languages">Germanic</a> <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/l%C4%93taz" class="extiw" title="wikt:Reconstruction:Proto-Germanic/lētaz">word</a> meaning "serf" or "half-free colonist".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  Other authorities suggest the term was of Celtic or Iranian origin.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin">Origin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laeti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Laeti</i> may have been groups of migrants drawn from the tribes that lived beyond the Empire's borders. These had been in constant contact and intermittent warfare with the Empire since its northern borders were stabilized in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Augustus" title="Augustus">Augustus</a> in the early 1st century. In the West, these tribes were primarily <a href="/wiki/Germanic_peoples" title="Germanic peoples">Germans</a>, living beyond the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a>. There is no mention in the sources of <i>laeti</i> in the Eastern section of the Empire.<sup id="cite_ref-Jones_1964_620_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jones_1964_620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Literary sources mention <i>laeti</i> only from the late 3rd and 4th centuries.
</p><p>Although the literary sources mention <i>laeti</i> only from the 4th century onwards, it is likely that their antecedents existed from as early as the 2nd century: the 3rd-century historian <a href="/wiki/Dio_Cassius" class="mw-redirect" title="Dio Cassius">Dio Cassius</a> reports that emperor <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a> (ruled 161–180) granted land in the border regions of <a href="/wiki/Germania" title="Germania">Germania</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pannonia" title="Pannonia">Pannonia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Moesia" title="Moesia">Moesia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dacia" title="Dacia">Dacia</a>, and even in Italy itself, to groups of <a href="/wiki/Marcomanni" title="Marcomanni">Marcomanni</a>, <a href="/wiki/Quadi" title="Quadi">Quadi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Iazyges" title="Iazyges">Iazyges</a> tribespeople captured during the <a href="/wiki/Marcomannic_Wars" title="Marcomannic Wars">Marcomannic Wars</a> (although Marcus Aurelius later expelled those settled in the peninsula after one group mutinied and briefly seized <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>, the base of the <a href="/wiki/Classis_Ravennatis" class="mw-redirect" title="Classis Ravennatis">Adriatic fleet</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> These settlers may have been the original <i>laeti</i>. Indeed, there is evidence that the practice of settling communities of <i>barbari</i> inside the Empire stretches as far back as the founder-emperor <a href="/wiki/Augustus" title="Augustus">Augustus</a> himself (ruled 42 BC - 14 AD): during his time, a number of subgroups of German tribes from the eastern bank of the Rhine were transferred, at their own request, to the Roman-controlled western bank, e.g. the <a href="/wiki/Cugerni" title="Cugerni">Cugerni</a>, a subgroup of the <a href="/wiki/Sugambri" class="mw-redirect" title="Sugambri">Sugambri</a> tribe, and the <a href="/wiki/Ubii" title="Ubii">Ubii</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Tacitus_Germ._XXVIII_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tacitus_Germ._XXVIII-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 69, the emperor <a href="/wiki/Otho" title="Otho">Otho</a> is reported to have settled communities of <a href="/wiki/Mauri_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Mauri people">Mauri</a> from North Africa in the province of <a href="/wiki/Hispania_Baetica" title="Hispania Baetica">Hispania Baetica</a> (modern Andalusia, Spain).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Given the attestation of several auxiliary regiments with the names of these tribes in the 1st and 2nd centuries, it is likely that their admission to the empire was conditional on some kind of military obligations (Tacitus states that the Ubii were given the task of guarding the West bank of the Rhine) i.e. that they were <i>laeti</i> in all but name.<sup id="cite_ref-Tacitus_Germ._XXVIII_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tacitus_Germ._XXVIII-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The name <i>Laeti</i> may have become more widely used after <a href="/wiki/Quintus_Aemilius_Laetus" title="Quintus Aemilius Laetus">Quintus Aemilius Laetus</a> managed the support of the Danubian Legions for <a href="/wiki/Septimius_Severus" title="Septimius Severus">Septimius Severus</a> and eventually took 15 thousand Danubians to the <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Guard" title="Praetorian Guard">Praetorian Guards</a> in Rome.
The <a href="/wiki/Severan_dynasty" title="Severan dynasty">Severan dynasty</a> lasted for 42 years, during which Danubians served as <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Guard" title="Praetorian Guard">Praetorian Guards</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Organisation">Organisation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laeti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Organisation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The precise constitutions which regulated <i>laeti</i> settlements are obscure.<sup id="cite_ref-Jones_1964_620_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jones_1964_620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It is possible that their constitutions were standard, or alternatively that the terms varied with each individual settlement.<sup id="cite_ref-Elton_1996_130_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elton_1996_130-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> There is also doubt about whether the terms governing <i>laeti</i> were distinct from those applying to <i>gentiles</i> ("natives") or <i>dediticii</i> ("surrendered barbarians") or <i>tributarii</i> (peoples obliged to pay tribute).<sup id="cite_ref-Elton_1996_130_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elton_1996_130-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It is possible that these names were used interchangeably, or at least overlapped considerably. On the other hand, they may refer to juridically distinct types of community, with distinct sets of obligations and privileges for each type. Most likely, the terms <i>laeti</i> and <i>gentiles</i> were interchangeable, as they are listed in the same section of the <i>Notitia</i>, and both referred to voluntary settlements.<sup id="cite_ref-Jones_1964_620_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jones_1964_620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In addition, the <i>Notitia</i> often places the two terms together e.g. the <i>praefectus laetorum gentilium Svevorum</i> at Bayeux and the <i>praefectus laetorum gentilium</i> at Reims.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Reproductively self-sufficient groups of <i>laeti</i> (i.e. including women and children) would be granted land (<i>terrae laeticae</i>) to settle in the empire by the imperial government.<sup id="cite_ref-Jones_1964_620_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jones_1964_620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> They appear to have formed distinct military <a href="/wiki/Canton_(administrative_division)" title="Canton (administrative division)">cantons</a>, which probably were outside the normal <a href="/wiki/Roman_province" title="Roman province">provincial administration</a>, since the settlements were under the control of a Roman <i>praefectus laetorum</i> (or <i>praefectus gentilium</i>), who were probably   military officers, as they reported to the <i><a href="/wiki/Magister_militum" title="Magister militum">magister peditum praesentalis</a></i> (commander of the imperial escort army) in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Notitia_Occ._XLII_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Notitia_Occ._XLII-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> This officer was, in the late 4th/early 5th centuries, the effective supreme commander of the Western Roman army.
</p><p>In return for their privileges of admission to the empire and land grants, the <i>laeti</i> settlers were under an obligation to supply recruits to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a>, presumably in greater proportions than ordinary communities were liable to under the regular <a href="/wiki/Conscription" title="Conscription">conscription</a> of the late empire. The treaty granting a <i>laeti</i> community land might specify a once-and-for-all contribution of recruits.<sup id="cite_ref-Jones_1964_620_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jones_1964_620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Or a fixed number of recruits required each year.<sup id="cite_ref-Goldsworthy_2005_208_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Goldsworthy_2005_208-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> A possible parallel is the treaty with Rome of the <a href="/wiki/Batavians" class="mw-redirect" title="Batavians">Batavi</a> tribe of <a href="/wiki/Germania_Inferior" title="Germania Inferior">Germania Inferior</a> in the 1st century. It has been calculated that in the <a href="/wiki/Julio-Claudian" class="mw-redirect" title="Julio-Claudian">Julio-Claudian</a> era, as many as half of all Batavi males reaching military age were enlisted in the Roman <a href="/wiki/Auxilia" title="Auxilia">auxilia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is considerable dispute about whether recruits from <i>laeti</i> settlements formed their own distinct military units or were simply part of the general pool of army recruits.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The traditional view of scholars is that the <i>praefecti laetorum</i> or <i>gentilium</i> mentioned in the <i>Notitia</i> were each in command of a regiment composed of the <i>laeti</i> ascribed to them. Some regiments of <i>laeti</i> certainly existed. The <i>praesentales</i> armies in both East and West contained <i><a href="/wiki/Scholae" title="Scholae">scholae</a></i> (elite cavalry units) of <i>gentiles</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> There is also a mention of a regular regiment called <i>Laeti</i> in the clash between emperors <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Julian_(emperor)" title="Julian (emperor)">Julian</a> in 361; and a regiment called <i>Felices Laetorum</i> in 6th century Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Elton_1996_131_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elton_1996_131-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The units <i>ala I Sarmatarum</i> and <i>numerus Hnaufridi</i> attested in 3rd century Britain may have been formed of <i>laeti</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>But Elton and Goldsworthy argue that <i>laeti</i> were normally drafted into existing military units, and only rarely formed their own.<sup id="cite_ref-Elton_1996_131_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elton_1996_131-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The main support for this view is a decree of 400 AD in the <i><a href="/wiki/Codex_Theodosianus" title="Codex Theodosianus">Codex Theodosianus</a></i> which authorises a <i>magister militum praesentalis</i> to enlist Alamanni and Sarmatian <i>laeti</i>, together with other groups such as the sons of veterans. This probably implies that <i>laeti</i> were seen as part of the general pool of recruits.<sup id="cite_ref-Elton_1996_131_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elton_1996_131-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In this case, the <i>praefecti laetorum/gentilium</i> may have been purely administrative roles, especially charged with ensuring the full military levy from their cantons each year.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notitia_Dignitatum"><i>Notitia Dignitatum</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laeti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Notitia Dignitatum">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Notitia_Dignitatum" title="Notitia Dignitatum">Notitia Dignitatum</a></div>
<p>Much of our information on <i>laeti</i> is contained in the <i><a href="/wiki/Notitia_Dignitatum" title="Notitia Dignitatum">Notitia Dignitatum</a></i>, a document drawn up at the turn of the 4th/5th centuries. The document is a list of official posts in the Roman Empire, both civil and military. It must be treated with caution, as many sections are missing or contain gaps, so the <i>Notitia</i> does not account for all posts and commands in existence at the time of compilation. Furthermore, the lists for the two halves of the Empire are separated by as much as 30 years, corresponding to ca. 395 for the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Empire">Eastern section</a> and ca. 425 for the <a href="/wiki/West_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="West Roman Empire">West</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Therefore, not all posts mentioned were in existence at the same time, and not all posts that <i>were</i> in existence are shown.
</p><p>The surviving <i>Notitia</i> only mentions <i>laeti</i> settlements in Italy and <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a> - and even the two lists of <i>laeti</i> prefects extant<sup id="cite_ref-Notitia_Occ._XLII_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Notitia_Occ._XLII-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> are incomplete. But the <i>Notitia</i> suggests that <i>laeti</i> settlements may have existed in the Danubian provinces also.<sup id="cite_ref-Notitia_Occ._XXXIV_and_XXXV_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Notitia_Occ._XXXIV_and_XXXV-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Furthermore, the lists probably contain errors. The list of <i>praefecti laetorum</i> in Gaul contains prefects for the <a href="/wiki/Lingones" title="Lingones">Lingones</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nervii" title="Nervii">Nervii</a> and <a href="/wiki/Batavi_(Germanic_tribe)" title="Batavi (Germanic tribe)">Batavi</a>: but these tribes had been inside the empire since its inception under <a href="/wiki/Augustus" title="Augustus">Augustus</a>. Thus, their classification as <i>laeti</i> is problematical. Most likely the text is corrupt. However, it has been suggested that these names may relate to Roman  people displaced from their home areas.<sup id="cite_ref-Jones_1964_620_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jones_1964_620-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="List_of_known_laeti_settlements">List of known <i>laeti</i> settlements</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laeti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: List of known laeti settlements">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Title XLII of the Western part contains two lists of <i>laeti</i> prefects, one for the <i>praefecti laetorum</i> in Gaul, and one for the <i>praefecti gentilium Sarmatarum</i> (prefects of Sarmatian <i>gentiles</i> i.e. "natives") in Italy and Gaul, all under the command of the <i>magister peditum praesentalis</i>- the commander of the imperial escort army in Italy (despite his title, which means "master of infantry", this officer commanded cavalry as well as infantry units).<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marcomanni">Marcomanni</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laeti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Marcomanni">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <i>Notitia</i> also mentions a <i>tribunus gentis Marcomannorum</i> under the command of the <i>dux Pannoniae et Norici</i>  and a <i>tribunus gentis per Raetias deputatae</i> (tribune of natives in the <a href="/wiki/Raetia" title="Raetia">Raetian</a> provinces).<sup id="cite_ref-Notitia_Occ._XXXIV_and_XXXV_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Notitia_Occ._XXXIV_and_XXXV-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> These <a href="/wiki/Marcomanni" title="Marcomanni">Marcomanni</a> were probably <i>laeti</i> also and may be the descendants of tribespeople settled in the area in the 2nd century by Marcus Aurelius. Alternatively (or additionally), they may have been descended from Germans settled in Pannonia following <a href="/wiki/Gallienus" title="Gallienus">Gallienus</a>'s treaty with King Attalus of the Marcomanni in AD 258/9.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Notitia</i> thus contains 34 entries concerning <i>laeti</i>. But some entries relate to several settlements, not just one, e.g. the Sarmatian settlements in Apulia and Calabria. Furthermore, more than two pages of entries appear to be missing. The number of settlements may thus have been in the hundreds, in the western half of the empire alone.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Impact">Impact</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laeti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Impact">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Notitia</i> lists of <i>laeti</i> settlements, incomplete as they are, show their considerable proliferation over the fourth century. This, together with the large numbers of military units with barbarian names, gave rise to the "barbarisation" theory of the fall of the Roman empire. This view ultimately originates from <a href="/wiki/Edward_Gibbon" title="Edward Gibbon">Edward Gibbon</a>'s <i>magnum opus</i>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Decline_and_Fall_of_the_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire">Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire</a></i>. According to this view, a critical factor in the disintegration of the western Roman empire in the 5th century was the Romans' ever-increasing reliance on barbarian recruits to man (and lead) their armies, while they themselves became soft and averse to military service. The barbarian recruits had no fundamental loyalty to Rome and repeatedly betrayed Rome's interests. This view does not distinguish between <i>laeti</i>, <a href="/wiki/Foederati" title="Foederati">foederati</a> and mercenaries.
</p><p>This view has remained in history writing since the more than 200 years since Gibbon wrote his narrative. In recent times the views of Gibbon has been generally discounted. According to Goldsworthy, there is no evidence that barbarian officers or men were any less reliable than their Roman counterparts.<sup id="cite_ref-Goldsworthy_2005_208_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Goldsworthy_2005_208-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Instead, the evidence points to the conclusion that <i>laeti</i> were a crucial source of first-rate recruits to late Roman army. Recruitment of Barbarians was not something new and had been present since the days of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Republic" title="Roman Republic">Roman Republic</a>, Julius Caesar and Marc Antony recruited defeated Gallic and German horsemen which served in their campaigns. The practice was taken up by the first emperor <a href="/wiki/Augustus" title="Augustus">Augustus</a> with the establishment of the auxiliaries, incorporating the defeated Barbarians into the Roman army. The Laeti, like the auxiliaries, were set on a path of Romanization.
</p>
