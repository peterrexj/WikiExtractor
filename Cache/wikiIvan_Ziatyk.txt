					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Catholic priest</div>
<p><b>Ivan Ziatyk (Zyatyk)</b> (1899–1952) was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Redemptorists" title="Redemptorists">Redemptorists</a> (Congregation of the Holy Redeemer) a <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a> branch of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and is considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_background">Family background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ivan_Ziatyk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ziatyk was born on December 26th, 1899, the day after Gregorian <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Hamlet_(place)" title="Hamlet (place)">hamlet</a> of Odrekhova near <a href="/wiki/Sanok" title="Sanok">Sanok</a> in southeastern <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>. He was the younger of two sons born to Maria and Stefan Ziatyk, his older sibling being called Mykhailo. The family were <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Rite">Ukrainian Rite</a> Catholics. Stefan Ziatyk died when Ivan was 14 years of age.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ukrainian_seminary">Ukrainian seminary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ivan_Ziatyk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ukrainian seminary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In his late teenage years, Ziatyk decided to follow his calling from God and prepare for the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priesthood">Catholic priesthood</a>. He entered the Ukrainian Catholic <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemyśl</a> where he spent time studying <a href="/wiki/Christian_spirituality" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian spirituality">Christian spirituality</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> together with the history and <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">Liturgy</a> of the Ukrainian Rite Catholic Church. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> and then priesthood in 1923.
In 1925, Ziatyk returned to the seminary where he lectured in <a href="/wiki/Dogmatic_theology" title="Dogmatic theology">dogmatic theology</a> and Holy Scripture, as well as serving as <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> for the next ten years.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_as_a_Redemptorist">Life as a Redemptorist</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ivan_Ziatyk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Life as a Redemptorist">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>For some time Ziatyk had desired to live a more austere life and, in 1935, made the decision to join the Redemptorists. Although he was an ordained priest, he was required to spend a year in the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> which was located near <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a> in western <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>, making his first profession in August 1936.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
During his first year as a Redemptorist, Ziatyk lived in the monastery dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Perpetual_Help" title="Our Lady of Perpetual Help">Our Lady of Perpetual Help</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ivano-Frankivsk" title="Ivano-Frankivsk">Ivano-Frankivsk</a> (then called Stanislaviv) before moving to another monastery in Lviv, where he was both assistant superior and <a href="/wiki/Treasurer" title="Treasurer">treasurer</a>. Then, in 1938, he was appointed to teach dogmatic theology at the newly opened <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in Holosko on the outskirts of present-day Lviv. In 1941, Ziatyk was made superior of the monastery dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Dormition_of_the_Mother_of_God" title="Dormition of the Mother of God">Dormition of the Mother of God</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ternopil" title="Ternopil">Ternopil</a> where he served before taking up the same position at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Zboiska,_Lw%C3%B3w&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Zboiska, Lwów (page does not exist)">Zboiska</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zboiska_(Lw%C3%B3w)" class="extiw" title="pl:Zboiska (Lwów)">pl</a>&#93;</span> in 1944. As well as being superior at Zboiska, he was engaged in the education of teenage boys interested in becoming Redemptorists.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Persecution_and_death">Persecution and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ivan_Ziatyk&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Persecution and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">Second World War</a> the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> regime renewed its oppression of Christian denominations; as Ukraine was part of the U.S.S.R. its people also suffered, but for a unique reason. The Soviets sought to abolish the Ukrainian Catholic Church by merging it with the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ukraine" title="Orthodox Church of Ukraine">Ukrainian Orthodox Church</a>, which was considered easier to control as it was both state-sanctioned and did not acknowledge the spiritual leadership of the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">Bishop of Rome</a>.
All the bishops of the Ukrainian Catholic Church (also known by some as the Greek Catholic Church) found themselves placed under arrest in early 1946. Members of the Redemptorist order were gathered at the monastery in Holosko and placed under virtual arrest for the next two years as their activities were constantly monitored by the <a href="/wiki/Secret_police" title="Secret police">secret police</a>.  The members of the community were also subjected to periodic interrogation. Ziatyk came under particular scrutiny as he had become responsible for the leadership of Ukrainian Catholics. (When Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Slipyj" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseph Slipyj">Joseph Slipyj</a> was arrested, he delegated the <a href="/wiki/Belgians" title="Belgians">Belgian</a> priest Joseph De Vocht to lead the church. After De Vocht was expelled in 1948, Ziatyk took over).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eventually, Ziatyk was arrested in January 5th, 1950. At the end of his show trial, he was found guilty of promoting “...the ideas of the Roman Pope, of spreading the Catholic Faith among the nations of the whole world and of making all Catholics.” and “cooperating with anti-Soviet nationalistic organizations and anti-Soviet propaganda.” and sentenced to ten years hard labour.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ziatyk served time in prison, first at <a href="/wiki/Zolochiv,_Lviv_Oblast" title="Zolochiv, Lviv Oblast">Zolochiv</a>  in western Ukraine and then at Ozernyi prison near <a href="/wiki/Irkutsk" title="Irkutsk">Irkutsk</a> in <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberia</a>. Like many other priests and religious who were imprisoned by the Soviet regime, Ziatyk endured frequent interrogations, various deprivations and torture to persuade him to renounce his faith in <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> or at least abandon his Catholicism and convert to the state-sanctioned Orthodox Church; he refused to comply.
</p><p>In an interview with one of Ziatyk's fellow prisoners, Antolii Medellian, he said, "he [Fr. Ivan Ziatyk] stood and prayed the whole day; for whole day she prayed every moment. He was such a pleasant person to talk to/ You could hear many wise and instructive words from him; this was especially  so in my case, as at that time i was a youngster."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1952, on <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> (the day on which <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> commemorate the death of <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>) Ziatyk was drenched in water and beaten unconscious before being left outside in the <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberian</a> cold. As a result of his injuries, he died a few days later and was buried in “....Cemetery 373 in the Lake Baikal zone, in the district of Tajshet in the region of Irkyts’k”<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 6 April 2001, the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> recognised Ivan Ziatyk as being a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and he was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> on 27 June, the feast of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Perpetual_Help" title="Our Lady of Perpetual Help">Our Lady of Perpetual Help</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patroness" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness">patroness</a> of the Redemptorists.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
