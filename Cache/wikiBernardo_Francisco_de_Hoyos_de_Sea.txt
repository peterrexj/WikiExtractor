<p><b>Bernardo Francisco de Hoyos de Seña</b>, <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">SJ</a> (21 August<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 1711 – 29 November 1735), best known simply as <b>Bernardo de Hoyos</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> Catholic priest, <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a> and member of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-GB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He is best known for his ardent devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a> and for his constant promotion of it until his premature death.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> was held in <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a> on 18 April 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CB_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernardo_Francisco_de_Hoyos_de_Se%C3%B1a&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bernardo Francisco de Hoyos de Seña was born on 21 August 1711 to Don Manuel de Hoyos and Doña Francisca de Seña. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 6 September in his local parish church in the names of "Bernardo Francisco Javier"; he was named in honor of <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> and <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father worked at the town hall at <a href="/wiki/Torrelobat%C3%B3n" title="Torrelobatón">Torrelobatón</a> near <a href="/wiki/Valladolid" title="Valladolid">Valladolid</a>. He received his Confirmation in 1720.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He entered the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> colleges of <a href="/wiki/Medina_del_Campo" title="Medina del Campo">Medina del Campo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Villagarc%C3%ADa_de_Campos" title="Villagarcía de Campos">Villagarcía de Campos</a> and later entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> under the direction of Félix de Vargas on 11 July 1726. He took his simple vows in 1728. He underwent philosophical studies at the College of San Pedro i San Pablo in Medina del Campo. In September 1731 he started began his theological studies at the College of San Ambrosio in Valladolid. He had spiritual experiences and revelations about the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> that led him to spread this devotion and worship. He was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> on 2 January 1735 with a special dispensation as he was not old enough. On 6 January 1735 he celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in the College of San Ignacio.
</p><p>In late April 1733 he received a letter from his friend Augustine Cadaveraz (in <a href="/wiki/Bilbao" title="Bilbao">Bilbao</a>) after he had requested the priest to translate a Latin chapter on <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Christi_(feast)" class="mw-redirect" title="Corpus Christi (feast)">Corpus Christi</a> from Joseph de Gallifet's book "On Devotion to the Sacred Heart of Jesus".<sup id="cite_ref-GB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On 3 May 1733 he took the book from the library of the house and took it to his room where he began to read it; the Sacred Heart both inspired and illuminated him. On 4 May he reported receiving a vision from <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a> who said to him: "I wish for you to spread the devotion to My Sacred Heart throughout all of Spain". Christ returned to him in another vision on 14 May. On 12 June 1733 he consecrated himself to the Sacred Heart using the same formula that <a href="/wiki/Claude_de_la_Colombi%C3%A8re" class="mw-redirect" title="Claude de la Colombière">Claude de la Colombière</a> used.<sup id="cite_ref-GB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He distributed leaflets for the devotion and even wrote a letter to <a href="/wiki/King_Philip_V_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="King Philip V of Spain">King Philip V</a> asking for his support in requesting the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> to approve a special feast for the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-CB_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1726 both <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Gonzaga" title="Aloysius Gonzaga">Aloysius Gonzaga</a> and <a href="/wiki/Stanislaus_Kostka" title="Stanislaus Kostka">Stanislaus Kostka</a> were both canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a>; the two became models of holiness for the Jesuit priest as well as <a href="/wiki/John_Berchmans" title="John Berchmans">John Berchmans</a> who was already on the course for canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Berchmans in particular was a main influence on him.<sup id="cite_ref-CB_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CB-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He fell ill with <a href="/wiki/Typhus" title="Typhus">typhus</a> and he died on 29 November 1735 at the Colegio de San Ignacio; his condition grew worse from 19 November onwards.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His remains were buried in the College of San Ignacio; his remains were later removed afterwards to an unknown place.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernardo_Francisco_de_Hoyos_de_Se%C3%B1a&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:242px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg/240px-Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="180" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg/360px-Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg/480px-Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2816" data-file-height="2112" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Beatificaci%C3%B3n_de_Bernardo_de_Hoyos_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bernardo's beatification in 2010.</div></div></div>
<p>The beatification process started in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Valladolid" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Valladolid">Archdiocese of Valladolid</a> in an informative process that Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Mar%C3%ADa_Cascajares_y_Azara" title="Antonio María Cascajares y Azara">Antonio María Cascajares y Azara</a> inaugurated on 17 October 1895 and later closed in 1899 while his writings received the approval of theologians on 7 June 1902; an apostolic process was also held in Valladolid that Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Cos_y_Macho" title="José Cos y Macho">José Cos y Macho</a> oversaw from 1914 until 1919. The formal introduction to the cause came on 11 February 1914 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> and the late Jesuit priest was made a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>.
</p><p>Historians assessed and approved the cause on 31 May 1961 while the postulation submitted the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in 1990. Theologians assented to the cause later on 27 June 1995 as did the C.C.S. on 12 December 1995. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> named him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 12 January 1996 after confirming that the late Jesuit priest had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. The process for a miracle spanned in Spain from 11 November 1947 to 8 October 1949 while being validated on 1 March 1996; the miracle in question was the cure of Mercedes Cabezas on 22 April 1936 from <a href="/wiki/Typhoid" class="mw-redirect" title="Typhoid">typhoid</a> and a serious tumor in <a href="/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_la_Cuesta" title="San Cristóbal de la Cuesta">San Cristóbal de la Cuesta</a> in <a href="/wiki/Salamanca" title="Salamanca">Salamanca</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A medical board approved this on 13 March 2008 with theologians doing likewise on 7 July 2008 and the C.C.S. soon after on 18 November 2008. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> approved this on 17 January 2009 and delegated Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> to preside over the beatification in Spain on 18 April 2010.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for the cause is the Jesuit priest Anton Witwer.
</p>
