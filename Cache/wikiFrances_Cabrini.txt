<p><b>Frances Xavier Cabrini</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" title="Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">MSC</a></span> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Francesca Saverio Cabrini</i>; July 15, 1850&#160;– December 22, 1917), also called <b>Mother Cabrini</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_Americans" title="Italian Americans">Italian-American</a> Catholic <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sister</a>. She founded the <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" title="Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> that was a major support to her fellow Italian immigrants to the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>. She was the first U.S. citizen to be <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, on July 7, 1946.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born <b>Maria Francesca Cabrini</b> on July 15, 1850, in <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Angelo_Lodigiano" title="Sant&#39;Angelo Lodigiano">Sant'Angelo Lodigiano</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombard</a> <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Lodi" title="Province of Lodi">Province of Lodi</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Austrian_Empire" title="Austrian Empire">Austrian Empire</a>. She was the youngest of the thirteen children of farmers Agostino Cabrini and Stella Oldini.<sup id="cite_ref-SanJose_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SanJose-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Only four of the thirteen survived beyond adolescence.
</p><p>Born two months early, she was small and weak as a child and remained in delicate health throughout her life. During her childhood, she visited an uncle, Don Luigi Oldini of Livagra, a priest who lived beside a swift canal. While there, she made little boats of paper, dropped violets in them, called the flowers "missionaries", and launched them to sail off to India and China. At thirteen, Francesca attended a school run by the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" title="Daughters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">Daughters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus</a>. Five years later she graduated <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cum_laude" class="extiw" title="wiktionary:cum laude">cum laude</a></i>, with a teaching certificate.<sup id="cite_ref-Crawley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crawley-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her parents died in 1870, she applied for admission to the Daughters of the Sacred Heart at Arluno. These sisters were her former teachers, but reluctantly, they told her she was too frail for their life.<sup id="cite_ref-chss_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chss-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She became the headmistress of the House of Providence orphanage in <a href="/wiki/Codogno" title="Codogno">Codogno</a>, where she taught and drew a small community of women to live a religious way of life. Cabrini took <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> in 1877 and added Xavier (<i>Saverio</i>) to her name to honor the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> saint, <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of missionary service. She had planned, like Francis Xavier, to be a missionary in the <a href="/wiki/Far_East" title="Far East">Far East</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_Sisters_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus">Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In November 1880, Cabrini and seven other women who had taken religious vows with her founded the <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_the_Sacred_Heart_of_Jesus" title="Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus">Missionary Sisters of the Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> (MSC).<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> She wrote the Rule and Constitutions of the <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a>, and she continued as its <a href="/wiki/Superior_general" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior general">superior general</a> until her death. The sisters took in orphans and <a href="/wiki/Foundlings" class="mw-redirect" title="Foundlings">foundlings</a>, opened a day school to help pay expenses, started classes in <a href="/wiki/Needlework" title="Needlework">needlework</a> and sold their fine <a href="/wiki/Embroidery" title="Embroidery">embroidery</a> to earn a little more money.<sup id="cite_ref-Crawley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crawley-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The institute established seven homes and a free school and nursery in its first five years. Its good works brought Cabrini to the attention of <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Scalabrini" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni Scalabrini">Giovanni Scalabrini</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Piacenza" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Piacenza">Bishop of Piacenza</a>, and of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mission_to_United_States">Mission to United States</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Mission to United States">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Stephen,_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_(Chesapeake,_Virginia)_-_stained_glass,_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Saint_Stephen%2C_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_%28Chesapeake%2C_Virginia%29_-_stained_glass%2C_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg/220px-Saint_Stephen%2C_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_%28Chesapeake%2C_Virginia%29_-_stained_glass%2C_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="276" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Saint_Stephen%2C_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_%28Chesapeake%2C_Virginia%29_-_stained_glass%2C_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg/330px-Saint_Stephen%2C_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_%28Chesapeake%2C_Virginia%29_-_stained_glass%2C_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Saint_Stephen%2C_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_%28Chesapeake%2C_Virginia%29_-_stained_glass%2C_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg/440px-Saint_Stephen%2C_Martyr_Roman_Catholic_Church_%28Chesapeake%2C_Virginia%29_-_stained_glass%2C_St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2460" data-file-height="3083" /></a><figcaption>Stained glass window in Chesapeake, Virginia, depicting Cabrini</figcaption></figure>
<p>In September 1887, Cabrini went to seek the pope's approval to establish missions in China. Instead, he urged that she go to the United States to help the Italian immigrants who were flooding to that nation, mostly in great poverty. "Not to the East, but to the West" was his advice.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cabrini left for the United States, arriving in <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> on March 31, 1889, along with six other sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In New York she encountered disappointment and difficulties.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Michael_Corrigan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Michael Corrigan">Archbishop Michael Corrigan</a>, who was not immediately supportive, found them housing at the convent of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_New_York" title="Sisters of Charity of New York">Sisters of Charity</a>. She obtained the archbishop's permission to found the Sacred Heart Orphan Asylum in rural <a href="/wiki/West_Park,_New_York" title="West Park, New York">West Park, New York</a>, later renamed <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cabrini_Home" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cabrini Home">Saint Cabrini Home</a>.
</p><p>Cabrini organized <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> and education classes for the Italian immigrants and provided for many orphans' needs. She established schools and orphanages despite tremendous odds. She was as resourceful as she was prayerful, finding people who would donate what she needed in money, time, labor, and support.<sup id="cite_ref-college_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-college-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In New York City, she founded Columbus Hospital, which merged with Italian Hospital to become <a href="/wiki/Cabrini_Medical_Center" title="Cabrini Medical Center">Cabrini Medical Center</a> from 1973<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> until its closure in 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois" class="mw-redirect" title="Chicago, Illinois">Chicago, Illinois</a>, the sisters opened Columbus Hospital in <a href="/wiki/Lincoln_Park,_Chicago" title="Lincoln Park, Chicago">Lincoln Park</a> and Columbus Extension Hospital (later renamed Saint Cabrini Hospital) in the heart of the city's Italian neighborhood on the <a href="/wiki/Near_West_Side,_Chicago" title="Near West Side, Chicago">Near West Side</a>. Both hospitals eventually closed in 2001–2002.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Their foundress's name lives on in Chicago's Cabrini Street.
</p><p>She founded 67 missionary institutions to serve the sick and poor, long before government agencies provided extensive social services – in New York; Chicago and Des Plaines, Illinois; Seattle; New Orleans; Denver and Golden, Colorado; <a href="/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California" class="mw-redirect" title="Los Angeles, California">Los Angeles</a>; <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia,_Pennsylvania" class="mw-redirect" title="Philadelphia, Pennsylvania">Philadelphia</a>; and in countries throughout <a href="/wiki/Latin_America" title="Latin America">Latin America</a> and <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1926, nine years after her death, the Missionary Sisters achieved Cabrini's original goal of becoming missionaries to China.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cabrini was <a href="/wiki/United_States_nationality_law" title="United States nationality law">naturalized</a> as a United States citizen in 1909.<sup id="cite_ref-stella_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stella-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cabrini died of complications from <a href="/wiki/Malaria" title="Malaria">malaria</a> at age 67 in Columbus Hospital in Chicago on December 22, 1917,<sup id="cite_ref-SanJose_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SanJose-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> while preparing Christmas candy for local children.
</p><p>Her body was initially interred at what became <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cabrini_Home" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cabrini Home">Saint Cabrini Home</a>, the orphanage she founded in <a href="/wiki/West_Park,_New_York" title="West Park, New York">West Park</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ulster_County,_New_York" title="Ulster County, New York">Ulster County</a>, <a href="/wiki/New_York_(state)" title="New York (state)">New York</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1933, her body was exhumed and divided as part of the process toward sainthood. At that time, her head was removed and is preserved in the chapel of the congregation's international <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">motherhouse</a> in Rome. Her heart is preserved in Codogno, where she founded her missionary order. An arm bone is at <a href="/wiki/National_Shrine_of_Saint_Frances_Xavier_Cabrini" title="National Shrine of Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini">her national shrine in Chicago</a>. Most of the rest of her body is at <a href="/wiki/St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini_Shrine" title="St. Frances Xavier Cabrini Shrine">her major shrine in New York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-NYT2015_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NYT2015-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cabrini was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on November 13, 1938, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on July 7, 1946, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-college_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-college-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Her beatification <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> involved purportedly restoring the sight of a day-old baby who had been blinded by a 50% <a href="/wiki/Silver_nitrate" title="Silver nitrate">silver nitrate</a> solution instead of the normal 1% solution in the child's eyes. The child, named Peter Smith (1921–2002), would later be present at her beatification and become a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Her canonization miracle involved the purported healing of a terminally ill member of her congregation. When Cabrini was canonized, an estimated 120,000 people filled Chicago's <a href="/wiki/Soldier_Field" title="Soldier Field">Soldier Field</a> for a Mass of thanksgiving.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is December 22, the anniversary of her death, the day ordinarily chosen as a saint's feast day.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Following the reforms in <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Code_of_Rubrics" title="Code of Rubrics">Code of Rubrics</a>, the United States since 1961 has celebrated Cabrini's feast on November 13, the anniversary of her beatification, to avoid conflicting with the greater <a href="/wiki/Feria" title="Feria">ferias</a> of <a href="/wiki/Advent" title="Advent">Advent</a>.
</p><p>In 1950, Pope Pius XII named Frances Xavier Cabrini as the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Immigrant" class="mw-redirect" title="Immigrant">immigrants</a>, recognizing her efforts on their behalf across the Americas in schools, orphanages, hospitals, and prisons.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cabrini is also informally recognized as an effective intercessor for finding a parking space. As one priest explained: "She lived in New York City. She understands traffic."<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrines">Shrines</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Shrines">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Chicago.2C_Illinois_.28National_Shrine.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Chicago,_Illinois_(National_Shrine)">Chicago, Illinois (National Shrine)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Chicago, Illinois (National Shrine)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine,_Lincoln_Park,_Chicago_(10369122804).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine%2C_Lincoln_Park%2C_Chicago_%2810369122804%29.jpg/170px-St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine%2C_Lincoln_Park%2C_Chicago_%2810369122804%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine%2C_Lincoln_Park%2C_Chicago_%2810369122804%29.jpg/255px-St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine%2C_Lincoln_Park%2C_Chicago_%2810369122804%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine%2C_Lincoln_Park%2C_Chicago_%2810369122804%29.jpg/340px-St._Frances_Cabrini_Shrine%2C_Lincoln_Park%2C_Chicago_%2810369122804%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="2560" /></a><figcaption>National Shrine in Chicago</figcaption></figure>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/National_Shrine_of_Saint_Frances_Xavier_Cabrini" title="National Shrine of Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini">National Shrine of Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini</a></div>
<p>After Cabrini's death, her convent room at Columbus Hospital, in Chicago's <a href="/wiki/Lincoln_Park,_Chicago" title="Lincoln Park, Chicago">Lincoln Park</a> neighborhood, became a popular destination for the faithful seeking personal healing and spiritual comfort. Due to the overwhelming number of pilgrims after her canonization in 1946, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Chicago" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Chicago">Archbishop of Chicago</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Stritch" title="Samuel Stritch">Samuel Stritch</a>, commissioned a large <a href="/wiki/National_Shrine_of_Saint_Frances_Xavier_Cabrini" title="National Shrine of Saint Frances Xavier Cabrini">National Shrine</a> in her honor within the hospital complex. He dedicated the shrine in 1955.<sup id="cite_ref-national_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-national-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The hospital and shrine closed in 2002 to be replaced by a high-rise development on North Lakeview Avenue. Still, the shrine and Cabrini's room were preserved and refurbished during the long demolition and construction period. They were solemnly blessed and re-dedicated by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Francis_George" title="Francis George">Francis George</a> on September 30, 2012, and reopened to the public the next day. The shrine is an architectural gem of gold mosaics, Carrara marble, frescoes, and Florentine stained glass, functioning as a stand-alone center for prayer, worship, spiritual care, and pilgrimage.<sup id="cite_ref-national_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-national-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Golden.2C_Colorado"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Golden,_Colorado">Golden, Colorado</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Golden, Colorado">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG/220px-Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG/330px-Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG/440px-Queen_of_Heaven_Orphanage_Summer_Camp.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4514" data-file-height="2539" /></a><figcaption>Stone House in Golden, Colorado</figcaption></figure>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Mother_Cabrini_Shrine" title="Mother Cabrini Shrine">Mother Cabrini Shrine</a></div>
<p>In 1904, Cabrini established <a href="/wiki/Denver" title="Denver">Denver</a>'s Queen of Heaven Orphanage for girls, including many orphans of local Italian miners. In 1910, she purchased a rural property from the town of <a href="/wiki/Golden,_Colorado" title="Golden, Colorado">Golden</a>, on the east slope of <a href="/wiki/Lookout_Mountain_(Colorado)" title="Lookout Mountain (Colorado)">Lookout Mountain</a>, as a summer camp for the girls. A small farming operation was established and maintained by three of the Sisters of the Sacred Heart. The camp dormitory, built of native rock and named the Stone House, was completed in 1914 and later listed on the <a href="/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places" title="National Register of Historic Places">National Register of Historic Places</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-library_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-library-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Where Cabrini had once located an underground spring on the mountainside, a <a href="/wiki/Lourdes_grotto" title="Lourdes grotto">replica</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Lourdes_Grotto" class="mw-redirect" title="Lourdes Grotto">Lourdes Grotto</a> was built in 1929, later replaced by a simpler sandstone structure. After Cabrini's canonization, the campsite officially became a shrine. Extensive additions in 1954 included a long Stairway of Prayer for pilgrims following her footpath up the mountain, marked with the <a href="/wiki/Stations_of_the_Cross" title="Stations of the Cross">Stations of the Cross</a>, leading to a 22-foot (7&#160;m) Statue of Jesus at the highest point of the site.<sup id="cite_ref-golden_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-golden-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Queen of Heaven Orphanage closed in 1967, replaced by a system of foster care. The summer campsite became a year-round facility for retreats and small prayer gatherings. A new convent building, completed in 1970, includes housing for the resident Sisters, overnight accommodations for visitors, a chapel dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a>, and an exhibit of artifacts and clothing once used by Cabrini.<sup id="cite_ref-library_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-library-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The statues and stained-glass windows of the chapel came from <a href="/wiki/Villa_Cabrini_Academy" title="Villa Cabrini Academy">Villa Cabrini Academy</a> in Burbank, California, a former school founded by the Missionary Sisters.<sup id="cite_ref-golden_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-golden-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Upper_Manhattan.2C_New_York"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Upper_Manhattan,_New_York">Upper Manhattan, New York</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Upper Manhattan, New York">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg/220px-Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="105" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg/330px-Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg/440px-Cabrini_Shrine_wall_crop_jeh.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3506" data-file-height="1679" /></a><figcaption>Cabrini Shrine in Manhattan</figcaption></figure>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini_Shrine" title="St. Frances Xavier Cabrini Shrine">St. Frances Xavier Cabrini Shrine</a></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/St._Frances_Xavier_Cabrini_Shrine" title="St. Frances Xavier Cabrini Shrine">St. Frances Xavier Cabrini Shrine</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Hudson_Heights,_Manhattan" title="Hudson Heights, Manhattan">Hudson Heights</a> neighborhood of Upper Manhattan overlooks the <a href="/wiki/Hudson_River" title="Hudson River">Hudson River</a>, the <a href="/wiki/George_Washington_Bridge" title="George Washington Bridge">George Washington Bridge</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/New_Jersey_Palisades" class="mw-redirect" title="New Jersey Palisades">New Jersey Palisades</a>.
</p><p>As Cabrini's cause for sainthood accelerated in 1933, the Missionary Sisters moved her remains from the <a href="/wiki/St._Cabrini_Home" title="St. Cabrini Home">Sacred Heart Orphanage</a> she had founded in rural <a href="/wiki/West_Park,_New_York" title="West Park, New York">West Park, New York</a>, to the chapel of Sacred Heart Villa, a Catholic school she had founded in Manhattan, freshly renamed <a href="/wiki/Mother_Cabrini_High_School" title="Mother Cabrini High School">Mother Cabrini High School</a>. When it became a popular pilgrimage site with her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> in 1938, the Sisters enshrined the major portion of her body in a glass-enclosed coffin under the altar of the school chapel. Her 1946 <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> brought a further sustained level of public interest, so in 1957–1960 a larger shrine was built adjoining the school.
</p><p>When the new shrine was near completion in 1959, her remains were transferred to a large bronze-and-glass <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> casket in the shrine's altar. She still rests in perpetual display for veneration, covered with her religious habit and a sculpted face mask and hands for more-lifelike viewing.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In addition to accommodating the public, the new shrine also served Cabrini High School students as a place for their liturgies and prayer services until the school closed in 2014.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> "Today, the shrine continues as a center of welcome for new immigrants and pilgrims of many nationalities who come to pray and reflect."<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_shrines">Other shrines</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Other shrines">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg/220px-Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg/330px-Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg/440px-Cabrini_statue_in_St_George%27s_Cathedral.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4320" data-file-height="3240" /></a><figcaption>Shrine in St George's Cathedral, Southwark</figcaption></figure>
<p><b><a href="/wiki/Southwark,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Southwark, London">Southwark, London</a>, England:</b> In <a href="/wiki/St_George%27s_Cathedral,_Southwark" title="St George&#39;s Cathedral, Southwark">St George's Cathedral, Southwark</a>, where Cabrini regularly worshipped during her time in London, a shrine was dedicated to her in 2009, designed by brothers Theodore, <a href="/wiki/James_Gillick" title="James Gillick">James</a>, and Gabriel Gillick. The bronze sculpture depicts the saint watching over a group of migrants standing on a pile of suitcases.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><b><a href="/wiki/Burbank,_California" title="Burbank, California">Burbank, California</a>, U.S.:</b> Near the site of <a href="/wiki/Villa_Cabrini_Academy" title="Villa Cabrini Academy">Villa Cabrini Academy</a> (1937–1970), Burbank's Cabrini shrine consists of a chapel founded by Cabrini in 1916, relocated to St. Francis Xavier Church and renovated during 1973–1975, and joined by a library wing in 1993. The Italian Catholic Federation sponsors the shrine.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><b><a href="/wiki/Lewiston,_New_York" title="Lewiston, New York">Lewiston, New York</a>, U.S.:</b> Near <a href="/wiki/Niagara_Falls" title="Niagara Falls">Niagara Falls</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_Our_Lady_of_Fatima" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of the National Shrine of Our Lady of Fatima">Basilica of the National Shrine of Our Lady of Fatima</a> includes a shrine to Cabrini along the Avenues of Saints.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><b><a href="/wiki/Lower_Manhattan" title="Lower Manhattan">Lower Manhattan, New York</a>, U.S.:</b> <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Pompeii_Church_(Manhattan)" title="Our Lady of Pompeii Church (Manhattan)">Our Lady of Pompeii Church</a> was founded in 1892 as a <a href="/wiki/National_parish" title="National parish">national parish</a> to serve the Italian-American immigrants of <a href="/wiki/Greenwich_Village" title="Greenwich Village">Greenwich Village</a>. Cabrini and her Missionary Sisters taught religious education there for a time, and the church now honors her with a shrine, a statue, and a stained-glass window.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><b><a href="/wiki/Peru,_New_York" title="Peru, New York">Peru, New York</a>, U.S.:</b> In 1947, one year after Cabrini's canonization, a shrine was dedicated to her in Peru, New York, near the state's northern border with Canada. The shrine is a stone grotto located on the grounds of St. Patrick's, a mission church built in 1841 for Irish immigrants.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><b><a href="/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania" title="Scranton, Pennsylvania">Scranton, Pennsylvania</a>, U.S.:</b> In 1899–1900, Cabrini helped to found St. Lucy parish and school for Scranton's Italian immigrants. A century later, the church dedicated a shrine in honor of St. Cabrini.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_tributes">Other tributes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Frances_Xavier_Cabrini&amp;action=edit&amp;section=21" title="Edit section: Other tributes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg/220px-2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg/330px-2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg/440px-2014_Fall_foliage_Cabrini_Boulevard_Hudson_Heights.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="2760" /></a><figcaption>Cabrini Boulevard and Cabrini Woods, New York City</figcaption></figure>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St._Cabrini_Home" title="St. Cabrini Home">St. Cabrini Home</a>, West Park, New York, was Cabrini's early orphanage, headquarters, and burial place.</li>
<li>The Cabrini Museum and Spirituality Center occupies her original convent in Codogno, Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>RSA Santa Francesca Cabrini is an assisted living facility in Codogno.<sup id="cite_ref-57" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-57">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The Cabrini Mission Foundation, founded in 1998, is a non-profit organization that raises funds to support worldwide Cabrini programs and institutions focused on health care, education, and social services.<sup id="cite_ref-58" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-58">&#91;57&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The Cabrini Sisters operate Cabrini Eldercare, a pair of non-profit residential facilities in Manhattan and Dobbs Ferry, New York.<sup id="cite_ref-59" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-59">&#91;58&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Cabrini was inducted into the <a href="/wiki/National_Women%27s_Hall_of_Fame" title="National Women&#39;s Hall of Fame">National Women's Hall of Fame</a> in 1996.<sup id="cite_ref-60" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-60">&#91;59&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Cabrini was inducted into the <a href="/wiki/Colorado_Women%27s_Hall_of_Fame" title="Colorado Women&#39;s Hall of Fame">Colorado Women's Hall of Fame</a> in 2022.<sup id="cite_ref-61" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-61">&#91;60&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Colorado replaced its <a href="/wiki/Columbus_Day" title="Columbus Day">Columbus Day</a> state holiday with Cabrini Day starting in 2020.<sup id="cite_ref-62" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-62">&#91;61&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Milan_Central_railway_station" class="mw-redirect" title="Milan Central railway station">Milan Central railway station</a> was dedicated to Cabrini in 2010.<sup id="cite_ref-63" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-63">&#91;62&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Chicago's <a href="/wiki/Cabrini%E2%80%93Green" class="mw-redirect" title="Cabrini–Green">Cabrini–Green</a> housing project, built 1942–1962, was named in honor of her work with Italian immigrants in the location. It has since been mostly torn down.<sup id="cite_ref-64" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-64">&#91;63&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Cabrini_Boulevard" title="Cabrini Boulevard">Cabrini Boulevard</a> and "Cabrini Woods Nature Sanctuary" are adjacent to the Cabrini shrine in Manhattan, New York.<sup id="cite_ref-65" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-65">&#91;64&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>In a 2019 New York City survey, Cabrini was "the leading vote-getter by far" among more than 300 nominees for the "She Built NYC" municipal statue program. Mayor <a href="/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio" title="Bill de Blasio">Bill de Blasio</a> and First Lady <a href="/wiki/Chirlane_McCray" title="Chirlane McCray">Chirlane McCray</a> nevertheless declined a Cabrini statue and were widely criticized, until Governor <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Cuomo" title="Andrew Cuomo">Andrew Cuomo</a> stepped in to commission one with state funds. On Columbus Day 2020, Cabrini's public memorial was unveiled in Manhattan's <a href="/wiki/Battery_Park_City" title="Battery Park City">Battery Park City</a>, looking out at the immigration landmarks of <a href="/wiki/Ellis_Island" title="Ellis Island">Ellis Island</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Statue_of_Liberty" title="Statue of Liberty">Statue of Liberty</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-66" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-66">&#91;65&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-67" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-67">&#91;66&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Mother Cabrini Park in <a href="/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey" title="Newark, New Jersey">Newark, New Jersey</a>, includes a 1958 statue of the saint on the former site of one of her schools.<sup id="cite_ref-68" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-68">&#91;67&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Mother Cabrini Park was created in Brooklyn, New York, in 1992, one hundred years after she established a school on the site.<sup id="cite_ref-69" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-69">&#91;68&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>A 2012 mural on the side of Arriana Condominium in <a href="/wiki/Carroll_Gardens,_Brooklyn" title="Carroll Gardens, Brooklyn">Carroll Gardens, Brooklyn</a>, honors Cabrini and the local Italian community.<sup id="cite_ref-70" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-70">&#91;69&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>'s religious vocation was partly inspired by Cabrini's ministry to his family's Italian immigrant community in Argentina.<sup id="cite_ref-NYT2015_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-NYT2015-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
