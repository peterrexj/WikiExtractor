<a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>: <span lang="hy">Մեսրոպ Մաշտոց</span> <i><span title="Armenian-language text"><i lang="hy-Latn">Mesrop Maštoc'</i></span></i>; <small>Eastern Armenian:</small> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="hy-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Armenian" title="Help:IPA/Armenian">[mɛsˈɾop maʃˈtotsʰ]</a></span>; He is venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenian Apostolic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church" title="Armenian Catholic Church">Armenian Catholic Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> churches.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is best known for inventing the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_alphabet" title="Armenian alphabet">Armenian alphabet</a> <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;405</span> AD, which was a fundamental step in strengthening Armenian national identity.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He is also considered to be the creator of the <a href="/wiki/Caucasian_Albanian_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Caucasian Albanian alphabet">Caucasian Albanian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Georgian_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Georgian alphabet">Georgian</a> alphabets by a number of scholars.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mesrop_Mashtots&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg/150px-Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="213" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg/225px-Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg/300px-Mesrop_Mashtots_by_Francesco_Maggiotto.jpg 2x" data-file-width="504" data-file-height="714" /></a><figcaption>Mesrop creating the Armenian alphabet, by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Maggiotto" title="Francesco Maggiotto">Francesco Maggiotto</a> (1750-1805)</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg/150px-Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="169" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg/225px-Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg/300px-Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo._Mesrop_Mashtots.jpg 2x" data-file-width="479" data-file-height="541" /></a><figcaption>Fresco of Mesrop by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo" title="Giovanni Battista Tiepolo">Giovanni Battista Tiepolo</a> (1696-1770)</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mesrop1776t.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Mesrop1776t.jpg/150px-Mesrop1776t.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="235" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Mesrop1776t.jpg/225px-Mesrop1776t.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/Mesrop1776t.jpg 2x" data-file-width="239" data-file-height="374" /></a><figcaption>Mesrop in a 1776 Armenian manuscript</figcaption></figure>
<p>He was born in a noble family ("from the house of an <i><a href="/wiki/Azat" title="Azat">azat</a></i>" according to <a href="/wiki/Anania_Shirakatsi" title="Anania Shirakatsi">Anania Shirakatsi</a>) in the settlement of Hatsekats in <a href="/wiki/Taron_(historic_Armenia)" title="Taron (historic Armenia)">Taron</a><sup id="cite_ref-koryun_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-koryun-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> (identified as the village of Hatsik in the Mush plain),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and died in <a href="/wiki/Vagharshapat,_Armenia" class="mw-redirect" title="Vagharshapat, Armenia">Vagharshapat</a>. He was the son of a man named Vardan.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Koryun" class="mw-redirect" title="Koryun">Koryun</a>, his pupil and biographer, tells us that Mashtots (in his work he does not mention the name Mesrop) received a good education and was versed in the <a href="/wiki/Medieval_Greek" title="Medieval Greek">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Middle_Persian" title="Middle Persian">Persian</a> languages.<sup id="cite_ref-koryun_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-koryun-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> On account of his piety and learning, Mesrop was appointed secretary to King <a href="/wiki/Khosrov_IV_of_Armenia" title="Khosrov IV of Armenia">Khosrov IV</a>, in charge of writing royal decrees and edicts in Persian and Greek. 
</p><p>Leaving the court, he took the <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders" title="Holy orders">holy orders</a> and withdrew to a monastery with a few companions, leading a life of great austerity for several years.
</p><p>Armenia lost its independence in 387 and was divided between the Byzantine Empire and Persia, which received about four-fifths of its territory. Western Armenia was governed by Byzantine generals, while an Armenian king ruled as Persian vassal over eastern Armenia. The principal events of this period are the invention of the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_alphabet" title="Armenian alphabet">Armenian alphabet</a>, the revision of the liturgy, the creation of an ecclesiastical and national literature, and the revision of hierarchical relations. Three men are prominently associated with this work: Mesrop, <a href="/wiki/Isaac_of_Armenia" title="Isaac of Armenia">Catholicos Isaac</a> (Sahak Part'ev), and King <a href="/wiki/Vramshapuh" title="Vramshapuh">Vramshapuh</a>, who succeeded his brother Khosrov IV in 389. 
</p><p>In 394, with the blessing of Catholicos Sahak Partev, Mesrop set out on a proselytizing mission. With the support of Prince Shampith, he preached the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> in the district of <a href="/wiki/Goghtn" title="Goghtn">Goghtn</a> near the river <a href="/wiki/Aras_(river)" title="Aras (river)">Araxes</a>, converting many.
</p><p>Armenians had no alphabet of their own, and instead used <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>, <a href="/wiki/Persian_language" title="Persian language">Persian</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> scripts, none of which was well suited for representing the many complex sounds of their native tongue. The Holy Scriptures and the liturgy were, to a large extent, unintelligible to the faithful and required the intervention of translators and interpreters.
</p><p>Mesrop was assisted in inventing a national alphabet by Catholicos Sahak and King Vramshapuh. He consulted Daniel, a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Mesopotamia" title="Mesopotamia">Mesopotamia</a>, and Rufinus, a monk of <a href="/wiki/Samosata" class="mw-redirect" title="Samosata">Samosata</a>, on the matter and created an alphabet of thirty-six letters; two more (long O (Օ, օ) and F (Ֆ, ֆ)) were added in the twelfth century.
</p><p>The first sentence in Armenian written down by St. Mesrop after he invented the letters was the opening line of Solomon's <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Proverbs" title="Book of Proverbs">Book of Proverbs</a>:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><span title="Armenian-language text"><span lang="hy"><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D5%B3%D5%A1%D5%B6%D5%A1%D5%B9%D5%A5%D5%AC_%D5%A6%D5%AB%D5%B4%D5%A1%D5%BD%D5%BF%D5%B8%D6%82%D5%A9%D5%AB%D6%82%D5%B6_%D5%A5%D6%82_%D5%A6%D5%AD%D6%80%D5%A1%D5%BF,_%D5%AB%D5%B4%D5%A1%D5%B6%D5%A1%D5%AC_%D5%A6%D5%A2%D5%A1%D5%B6%D5%BD_%D5%B0%D5%A1%D5%B6%D5%B3%D5%A1%D6%80%D5%B8%D5%B5" class="extiw" title="wikt:ճանաչել զիմաստութիւն եւ զխրատ, իմանալ զբանս հանճարոյ">Ճանաչել զիմաստութիւն եւ զխրատ, իմանալ զբանս հանճարոյ</a>:</span></span><br /><i>Čanačʿel zimastutʿiwn ew zxrat, imanal zbans hančaroy.</i><br />«To know wisdom and instruction; to perceive the words of understanding.»</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8202;<cite><i><a href="/wiki/Book_of_Proverbs" title="Book of Proverbs">Book of Proverbs</a></i>, 1:2.</cite></div></blockquote>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Amaras-vank.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Amaras-vank.jpg/200px-Amaras-vank.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Amaras-vank.jpg/300px-Amaras-vank.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Amaras-vank.jpg/400px-Amaras-vank.jpg 2x" data-file-width="542" data-file-height="398" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/Amaras_Monastery" title="Amaras Monastery">Amaras Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Artsakh_(historic_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Artsakh (historic province)">Artsakh</a>, where Mesrop set up the first school that used his script.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>The invention of the alphabet around 405 was the beginning of <a href="/wiki/Armenian_literature" title="Armenian literature">Armenian literature</a> and proved a powerful factor in the building of national spirit. "The result of the work of Isaac and Mesrop", says St. Martin,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> "was to separate for ever the Armenians from the other peoples of the East, to make of them a distinct nation, and to strengthen them in the Christian Faith by forbidding or rendering profane all the foreign alphabetic scripts which were employed for transcribing the books of the heathens and of the followers of <a href="/wiki/Zoroaster" title="Zoroaster">Zoroaster</a>. To Mesrop we owe the preservation of the language and literature of Armenia; but for his work, the people would have been absorbed by the Persians and Syrians, and would have disappeared like so many nations of the East".
</p><p>Medieval Armenian sources also claim that Mashtots invented the <a href="/wiki/Georgian_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Georgian alphabet">Georgian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Caucasian_Albanian_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Caucasian Albanian alphabet">Caucasian Albanian</a> alphabets around the same time. Most scholars link the creation of the Georgian script to the process of <a href="/wiki/Christianization_of_Iberia" title="Christianization of Iberia">Christianization of Iberia</a>, a core Georgian kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Kartli" title="Kartli">Kartli</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The alphabet was therefore most probably created between the conversion of Iberia under King <a href="/wiki/Mirian_III_of_Iberia" title="Mirian III of Iberia">Mirian III</a> (326 or 337) and the <a href="/wiki/Bir_el_Qutt_inscriptions" title="Bir el Qutt inscriptions">Bir el Qutt inscriptions</a> of 430,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> contemporaneously with the Armenian alphabet.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Encouraged by the patriarch and the king, Mesrop founded numerous schools in different parts of the country, in which the youth were taught the new alphabet. He himself taught at the <a href="/wiki/Amaras_Monastery" title="Amaras Monastery">Amaras monastery</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Artsakh_(historical_province)" title="Artsakh (historical province)">Armenian province of Artsakh</a> (located in the contemporary Martuni region of the unrecognized Nagorno-Karabakh Republic).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> However, his activity was not confined to Eastern Armenia. Provided with letters from the Catholicos, he went to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and obtained from emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodosius the Younger">Theodosius the Younger</a> permission to preach and teach in his Armenian possessions. Having returned to Eastern Armenia to report to the patriarch, his first thought was to provide religious literature for his countrymen. He sent some of his numerous disciples to <a href="/wiki/Edessa,_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Edessa, Mesopotamia">Edessa</a>, Constantinople, <a href="/wiki/Athens" title="Athens">Athens</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, and other centers of learning, to study the Greek language and bring back the masterpieces of Greek literature. The most famous of his pupils were John of Egheghiatz, Joseph of Baghin, <a href="/wiki/Yeznik" class="mw-redirect" title="Yeznik">Yeznik</a>, <a href="/wiki/Koriun" title="Koriun">Koriun</a>, <a href="/wiki/Moses_of_Chorene" class="mw-redirect" title="Moses of Chorene">Moses of Chorene</a>, and John Mandakuni.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Dzeragir.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Dzeragir.jpg/150px-Dzeragir.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="225" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/Dzeragir.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="200" data-file-height="300" /></a><figcaption>Verses of Mesrop Mashtots</figcaption></figure>
<p>The first monument of Armenian literature is the version of the Holy Scriptures. Isaac, says Moses of Chorene, made a translation of the Bible from the Syriac text about 411. This work was considered imperfect, for soon afterwards John of Egheghiatz and Joseph of Baghin were sent to Edessa to translate the Scriptures. They journeyed as far as Constantinople and brought back authentic copies of the Greek text with them. With the help of other copies obtained from Alexandria, the Bible was translated again from the Greek according to the text of the <a href="/wiki/Septuagint" title="Septuagint">Septuagint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Hexapla" title="Hexapla">Hexapla</a></i>. This version, now in use in the Armenian Church, was completed about 434.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg/220px-Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="179" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg/330px-Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg/440px-Mesrop_Mashtots%27_grave_stone.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4788" data-file-height="3888" /></a><figcaption>Gravesite of Mesrop Mashtots in the village of <a href="/wiki/Oshakan" title="Oshakan">Oshakan</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>The decrees of the first three ecumenical councils — <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">Nicæa</a>, <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Constantinople" title="First Council of Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, and <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Ephesus" class="mw-redirect" title="First Council of Ephesus">Ephesus</a> — and the national liturgy (so far written in Syriac) were also translated into Armenian, the latter being revised on the liturgy of <a href="/wiki/St._Basil" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Basil">St. Basil</a>, though retaining characteristics of its own. Many works of the Greek Fathers were also translated into Armenian. The loss of the Greek originals has given some of those versions a special importance; thus, the second part of <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a>'s <i>Chronicle,</i> of which only a few fragments exist in Greek, has been preserved entirely in Armenian. In the midst of his literary labors, Mesrop revisited the districts he had evangelized in his earlier years, and, after the death of Isaac in 439, looked after the spiritual administration of the patriarchate. He survived his friend and master by only six months. <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenians</a> read his name in the Canon of the Liturgy and celebrate his memory on 19 February.
</p><p>Saint Mashtots is buried at a chapel in <a href="/wiki/Oshakan" title="Oshakan">Oshakan</a>, a historical village 8&#160;km (5.0 miles) southwest from the town of <a href="/wiki/Ashtarak" title="Ashtarak">Ashtarak</a>.
</p><p>Saint Mesrop is listed officially in the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>; his feast day is February 17.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mesrop_Mashtots&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:2014_Erywa%C5%84,_Matenadaran_(16).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/2014_Erywa%C5%84%2C_Matenadaran_%2816%29.jpg/250px-2014_Erywa%C5%84%2C_Matenadaran_%2816%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="166" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/2014_Erywa%C5%84%2C_Matenadaran_%2816%29.jpg/375px-2014_Erywa%C5%84%2C_Matenadaran_%2816%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/2014_Erywa%C5%84%2C_Matenadaran_%2816%29.jpg/500px-2014_Erywa%C5%84%2C_Matenadaran_%2816%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4467" data-file-height="2975" /></a><figcaption>The statue of Mesrop Mashtots and <a href="/wiki/Koriun" title="Koriun">Koryun</a> (kneeling) in front of <a href="/wiki/Matenadaran" title="Matenadaran">Matenadaran</a>, Yerevan</figcaption></figure>
<p>Virtually every town in Armenia has a street named after Mashtots.  In <a href="/wiki/Yerevan" title="Yerevan">Yerevan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mashtots_Avenue" title="Mashtots Avenue">Mashtots Avenue</a> is one of the most important in the city center, which was previously known as Lenin Avenue. There is a statue of him at the <a href="/wiki/Matenadaran" title="Matenadaran">Matenadaran</a>, one at the church he was buried at in Oshakan village, and one at the monument to the Armenian alphabet found on the skirts of Mt. Aragats, north of <a href="/wiki/Ohanavan" title="Ohanavan">Ohanavan</a> Village.  Stamps have been issued with his image by both the Soviet Union and by post-Soviet Armenia.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Mesrop_Mashtots" title="Order of St. Mesrop Mashtots">Order of St. Mesrop Mashtots</a>, established in 1993, is awarded for significant achievements in economic development of the Republic of Armenia or for accomplishments in science, culture, education or public service, and for activities promoting those fields.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Music">Music</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mesrop_Mashtots&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Music">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mashtots also produced a number of liturgical compositions. Some of the works attributed to him are: «Մեղայ քեզ Տէր» (<i>Meġay k’ez Tēr</i>, “I have sinned against you, Lord”), «Ողորմեա ինձ Աստուած» (<i>Voġormea inj Astuac</i>, “Have mercy on me, God”), «Անկանիմ առաջի քո» (<i>Ankanim aṙaǰi k’o</i>, “I kneel before you”) and «Ողորմեա» (<i>Voġormea</i>, “Miserere”), all of which are hymns of <a href="/wiki/Repentance_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Repentance (Christianity)">repentance</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
