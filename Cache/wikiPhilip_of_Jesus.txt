<p><b>Philip of Jesus</b>, OFM (Spanish: <b>Felipe de Jesús</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/New_Spain" title="New Spain">Novohispanic</a> Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> missionary who became one of the <a href="/wiki/Twenty-six_Martyrs_of_Japan" class="mw-redirect" title="Twenty-six Martyrs of Japan">Twenty-six Martyrs of Japan</a>, the first Mexican saint and patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Mexico_City" title="Mexico City">Mexico City</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Philip was born in <a href="/wiki/Mexico_City" title="Mexico City">Mexico City</a> in 1572. Though unusually frivolous as a boy, he joined the Reformed <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a> of the Province of St. Didacus, founded in Mexico by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Baptista" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter Baptista">Peter Baptista</a>, with whom he suffered <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> later. After some months in the Order, Philip grew tired of religious life, left the Franciscans in 1589, took up a mercantile career, and went to the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, another Spanish colony, where he led a life of pleasure. Later he desired to re-enter the Franciscans and was again admitted at <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a> in 1590.<sup id="cite_ref-Bihl_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bihl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After some years it was determined that he was ready for <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> and sent to Mexico for this, since the <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">episcopal</a> <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> of Manila was vacant at that time, and thus no <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> was available locally to ordain him. He sailed on the <i><a href="/wiki/San_Felipe_incident_(1596)" title="San Felipe incident (1596)">San Felipe</a></i> on 12 July 1596, but a storm drove the vessel upon the coast of Japan. The governor of the province confiscated the ship and imprisoned its crew and passengers, among whom were another Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, Juan de Zamorra, as well as three other friars, two <a href="/wiki/Augustinians" title="Augustinians">Augustinians</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a>. The discovery of soldiers, cannon and ammunition on the ship led to the suspicion that it was intended for the conquest of Japan, and that the missionaries were merely to prepare the way for the soldiers. This was also said, falsely and unwarrantably, by one of the crew, and it enraged the Japanese <a href="/wiki/Taik%C5%8D" class="mw-redirect" title="Taikō">Taikō</a>, <a href="/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi" title="Toyotomi Hideyoshi">Toyotomi Hideyoshi</a>, generally called <a href="/wiki/Taicosama" class="mw-redirect" title="Taicosama">Taicosama</a> by Europeans. In consequence, he commanded on  December 8, 1596, the arrest of the Franciscans in the <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> at <a href="/wiki/Miako" class="mw-redirect" title="Miako">Miako</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Kyoto" title="Kyoto">Kyoto</a>, whither Philip had gone.<sup id="cite_ref-Bihl_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bihl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The friars were all kept prisoners in the friary until December 30, when they were transferred to the city prison. There were six Franciscan friars, seventeen Japanese  Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Third_order" title="Third order">tertiaries</a> and the Japanese <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Paulo_Miki" class="mw-redirect" title="Paulo Miki">Paul Miki</a>, with his two native servants. The ears of the prisoners were <a href="/wiki/Cropping_(punishment)" title="Cropping (punishment)">cropped</a> on January 3, 1597, and they were paraded through the streets of <a href="/wiki/Kyoto" title="Kyoto">Kyoto</a>; on January 21 they were taken to <a href="/wiki/Osaka" title="Osaka">Osaka</a>, and thence to <a href="/wiki/Nagasaki,_Nagasaki" class="mw-redirect" title="Nagasaki, Nagasaki">Nagasaki</a>, which they reached on February 5, 1597. They were taken to a mountain near Nagasaki city, "Mount of the Martyrs", bound upon crosses, after which they were pierced with spears.<sup id="cite_ref-Bihl_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bihl-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bones of Philip were brought to Mexico City in 1598: "Annals of His Time", Chimalpahin.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Beatification_.26_Canonization"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification_&amp;_Canonization">Beatification &amp; Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Beatification &amp; Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Philip was beatified in 1627 by Urban VIII, and, with his companions, canonized 8 June, 1862, by Pius IX. He is the patron saint of the city of Mexico.
</p><p><br />
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Popular_Culture">In Popular Culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philip_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: In Popular Culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1949 a Mexican film <i><a href="/wiki/Philip_of_Jesus_(film)" title="Philip of Jesus (film)">Philip of Jesus</a></i> portrayed his life and death. It was directed by <a href="/wiki/Julio_Bracho" title="Julio Bracho">Julio Bracho</a> with the actor <a href="/wiki/Ernesto_Alonso" title="Ernesto Alonso">Ernesto Alonso</a> playing Philip.
</p>
