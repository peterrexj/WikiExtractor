<p><b>Casimir Jagiellon</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Casimirus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Lithuanian_language" title="Lithuanian language">Lithuanian</a>: <i lang="lt">Kazimieras</i>; <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Kazimierz</i>; 3 October 1458 – 4 March 1484) was a prince of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_(1385%E2%80%931569)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Poland (1385–1569)">Kingdom of Poland</a> and of the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania" title="Grand Duchy of Lithuania">Grand Duchy of Lithuania</a>. Second son of King <a href="/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon" title="Casimir IV Jagiellon">Casimir IV Jagiellon</a>, he was tutored by <a href="/wiki/Johannes_Longinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Johannes Longinus">Johannes Longinus</a>, a Polish <a href="/wiki/Chronicler" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicler">chronicler</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diplomat" title="Diplomat">diplomat</a>. After his elder brother <a href="/wiki/Vladislaus_II_of_Bohemia_and_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladislaus II of Bohemia and Hungary">Vladislaus</a> was elected as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Bohemia">King of Bohemia</a> in 1471, Casimir became the <a href="/wiki/Heir_apparent" title="Heir apparent">heir apparent</a>. At the age of 13, Casimir participated in the failed military campaign to install him as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Hungary" title="King of Hungary">King of Hungary</a>. He became known for his piety, devotion to God, and generosity towards the sick and poor. He became ill (most likely with <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>) and died at the age of 25. He was buried in <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Cathedral" title="Vilnius Cathedral">Vilnius Cathedral</a> and his cult grew. His canonization was initiated by his brother King <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_I_the_Old" title="Sigismund I the Old">Sigismund I the Old</a> in 1514 and the tradition holds that he was canonized in 1521.
</p><p>The age of the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> was not conducive to the cult of saints. Veneration of Casimir saw a resurgence in the 17th century when his feast day was confirmed by the pope in 1602 and the dedicated <a href="/wiki/Chapel_of_Saint_Casimir" title="Chapel of Saint Casimir">Chapel of Saint Casimir</a> was completed in 1636. Casimir became a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lithuania" title="Lithuania">Lithuania</a> and Lithuanian youth. In <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a>, his feast day is marked annually with <a href="/wiki/Kaziuko_mug%C4%97" title="Kaziuko mugė">Kaziuko mugė</a> (a trade fair) held on the Sunday nearest to 4 March, the anniversary of his death. There are more than 50 churches named after Casimir in Lithuania and Poland, including <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Casimir,_Vilnius" title="Church of St. Casimir, Vilnius">Church of St. Casimir, Vilnius</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Kazimierz_Church,_Warsaw" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Kazimierz Church, Warsaw">St. Kazimierz Church, Warsaw</a>, and more than 50 churches in Lithuanian and Polish diaspora communities in America. Women's congregation <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Saint_Casimir" title="Sisters of Saint Casimir">Sisters of Saint Casimir</a> was established in 1908 and remains active in the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_education">Early life and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A member of the <a href="/wiki/Jagiellon_dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Jagiellon dynasty">Jagiellon dynasty</a>, Casimir was born in <a href="/wiki/Wawel_Castle" title="Wawel Castle">Wawel Castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>. Casimir was the third child and the second son of the King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania <a href="/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon" title="Casimir IV Jagiellon">Casimir IV</a> and Queen <a href="/wiki/Elisabeth_of_Austria_(1436%E2%80%931505)" class="mw-redirect" title="Elisabeth of Austria (1436–1505)">Elisabeth Hamsburg of Austria</a>. Elisabeth was a loving mother and took active interest in her children's upbringing.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012302_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012302-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The Queen and the children often accompanied the King in his annual trips to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>From the age of nine, Casimir and his brother <a href="/wiki/Vladislaus_II_of_Bohemia_and_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladislaus II of Bohemia and Hungary">Vladislaus</a> were educated by the Polish priest  Fr. <a href="/wiki/Jan_D%C5%82ugosz" title="Jan Długosz">Jan Długosz</a>. The boys were taught Latin and German, law, history, rhetoric, and classical literature.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012302_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012302-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Długosz was a strict and conservative teacher who emphasized ethics, morality, and religious devotion. According to <a href="/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Orzechowski" title="Stanisław Orzechowski">Stanisław Orzechowski</a> (1513–1566), the princes were subject to <a href="/wiki/Corporal_punishment" title="Corporal punishment">corporal punishment</a> which was approved by their father.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012303_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012303-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Długosz noted Casimir's skills in oratory when he delivered speeches to greet his father returning to Poland in 1469 and Jakub Sienienski, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Kujawy" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Kujawy">Bishop of Kujawy</a>, in 1470.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012303_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012303-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Hungarian_campaign">Hungarian campaign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Hungarian campaign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main articles: <a href="/wiki/Bohemian%E2%80%93Hungarian_War_(1468%E2%80%9378)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bohemian–Hungarian War (1468–78)">Bohemian–Hungarian War (1468–78)</a> and <a href="/wiki/Black_Army_of_Hungary" title="Black Army of Hungary">Black Army of Hungary</a></div>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg/220px-Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="172" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg/330px-Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg/440px-Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg 2x" data-file-width="768" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cynk_D%C5%82ugosz_and_Saint_Casimir.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Długosz and Saint Casimir</i> by <a href="/wiki/Florian_Cynk" title="Florian Cynk">Florian Cynk</a> (circa 1869)</div></div></div>
<p>Prince Casimir's uncle <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_the_Posthumous" title="Ladislaus the Posthumous">Ladislaus the Posthumous</a>, <a href="/wiki/King_of_Hungary" title="King of Hungary">King of Hungary</a> and <a href="/wiki/King_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Bohemia">Bohemia</a>, died in 1457 at the age of 17, without leaving an heir. Casimir's father, King Casimir IV, subsequently advanced his claims to Hungary and Bohemia, but could not enforce them due to the <a href="/wiki/Thirteen_Years%27_War_(1454%E2%80%9366)" class="mw-redirect" title="Thirteen Years&#39; War (1454–66)">Thirteen Years' War (1454–66)</a>. Instead, <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_nobles" class="mw-redirect" title="Hungarian nobles">Hungarian nobles</a> elected <a href="/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus" title="Matthias Corvinus">Matthias Corvinus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bohemian_nobles" class="mw-redirect" title="Bohemian nobles">Bohemian nobles</a> selected <a href="/wiki/George_of_Pod%C4%9Bbrady" title="George of Poděbrady">George of Poděbrady</a> as their kings. George of Poděbrady died in March 1471. In May 1471, <a href="/wiki/Vladislaus_II_of_Bohemia_and_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladislaus II of Bohemia and Hungary">Vladislaus</a>, eldest son of Casimir IV, was elected to the throne of Bohemia. However, a group of Catholic Bohemian nobles supported Matthias Corvinus instead of Vladislaus II. In turn, a group of Hungarian nobles conspired against Matthias Corvinus and invited the Polish king to overthrow him. King Casimir IV decided to install his son, Casimir, in Hungary.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Poland amassed an army of 12,000 men, commanded by <a href="/wiki/Piotr_Dunin" title="Piotr Dunin">Piotr Dunin</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Dziers%C5%82aw_of_Rytwiany&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Dziersław of Rytwiany (page does not exist)">Dziersław of Rytwiany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012304_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012304-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Both King Casimir and Prince Casimir participated in the campaign. In October 1471, the Polish army crossed the Hungarian border and slowly marched towards <a href="/wiki/Buda" title="Buda">Buda</a>. Matthias Corvinus managed to win over the majority of the Hungarian nobles, including the main conspirator Archbishop <a href="/wiki/J%C3%A1nos_Vit%C3%A9z_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="János Vitéz (archbishop)">János Vitéz</a>, and the Polish army did not receive the expected reinforcements. Only Deák, Perény and Rozgonyi families sent troops.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012305_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012305-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Upon hearing that Corvinus' army of 16,000 men camped outside of <a href="/wiki/Pest,_Hungary" title="Pest, Hungary">Pest</a>, the Polish army decided to retreat from <a href="/wiki/Hatvan" title="Hatvan">Hatvan</a> to <a href="/wiki/Nitra" title="Nitra">Nitra</a>. There the soldiers battled food shortages, spreading infectious diseases, and the upcoming winter. The Polish King also lacked funds to pay the mercenaries. As a result, the Polish army decreased by about a third.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012305_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012305-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In December 1471, Prince Casimir, fearing for his safety, was sent to <a href="/wiki/Jihlava" title="Jihlava">Jihlava</a> closer to the Polish border and that further eroded their soldiers' morale. Corvinus took Nitra and a one-year truce was completed in March 1472 in Buda.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012305_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012305-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Prince Casimir returned to <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a> to resume his studies with Długosz.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Długosz remarked that Prince Casimir felt "great sorrow and shame" regarding the failure in Hungary.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Polish propaganda, however, portrayed him as a savior, sent by divine providence, to protect the people from a godless tyrant (i.e. Matthias Corvinus) and marauding pagans (i.e. Muslim <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Turks" title="Ottoman Turks">Ottoman Turks</a>). Prince Casimir was also exposed to the cult of his uncle King <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_III_of_Poland" title="Władysław III of Poland">Władysław III of Poland</a> who died in the 1444 <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Varna" title="Battle of Varna">Battle of Varna</a> against the Ottomans. This led some researchers, including <a href="/wiki/Jacob_Caro" title="Jacob Caro">Jacob Caro</a>, to conclude that the Hungarian campaign pushed Prince Casimir into religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life_and_death">Later life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG/220px-Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="333" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG/330px-Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG/440px-Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2010" data-file-height="3038" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Wilno_-_kaplica_Kazimierza.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Casimir's silver sarcophagus at the <a href="/wiki/Chapel_of_Saint_Casimir" title="Chapel of Saint Casimir">Chapel of Saint Casimir</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Cathedral" title="Vilnius Cathedral">Vilnius Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>As his elder brother, Vladislaus II, ruled Bohemia, Prince Casimir became the heir apparent to the throne of Poland and Lithuania. Italian humanist writer <a href="/wiki/Filippo_Buonaccorsi" title="Filippo Buonaccorsi">Filippo Buonaccorsi</a> (also known as Filip Callimachus) was hired to become Casimir's tutor in political matters, but his Renaissance views had less influence on Casimir than Długosz.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1474, the Italian merchant and traveler <a href="/wiki/Ambrogio_Contarini" title="Ambrogio Contarini">Ambrogio Contarini</a> met with Prince Casimir and was impressed by his wisdom. Prince Casimir completed his formal education at age 16 and spent most of his time with his father.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012306-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1476, Prince Casimir accompanied his father to <a href="/wiki/Royal_Prussia" title="Royal Prussia">Royal Prussia</a> where he tried to resolve the conflict with the <a href="/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Warmia" title="Prince-Bishopric of Warmia">Prince-Bishopric of Warmia</a> (see <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Priests_(Poland)" title="War of the Priests (Poland)">War of the Priests</a>). In 1478 <a href="/wiki/Seimas_of_the_Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania" class="mw-redirect" title="Seimas of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania">Seimas of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania</a> demanded that King Casimir IV leave either Prince Casimir or Prince <a href="/wiki/John_I_Albert" title="John I Albert">John I Albert</a> in Lithuania as a regent. King Casimir IV feared separatist moods and refused, but after settling the conflict in Prussia, moved to <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012307_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012307-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Between 1479 and 1484 his father spent most of his time in Vilnius attending to the affairs of Lithuania. In 1481, <a href="/wiki/Mikhailo_Olelkovich" title="Mikhailo Olelkovich">Mikhailo Olelkovich</a> and his relatives planned to murder King Casimir and Prince Casimir during a hunt at a wedding of <a href="/wiki/Belsky_family_(Gediminid)" title="Belsky family (Gediminid)">Feodor Ivanovich Belsky</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012308_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012308-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The plan was discovered and Prince Casimir, perhaps fearing for his safety, was sent to Poland to act as vice-regent. Around the same time his father tried to arrange his marriage to <a href="/wiki/Kunigunde_of_Austria" title="Kunigunde of Austria">Kunigunde of Austria</a>, daughter of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick III, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick III</a>. It is often claimed that Prince Casimir refused the match, preferring to remain celibate and sensing his approaching death.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012308_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012308-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Maciej_Miechowita" title="Maciej Miechowita">Maciej Miechowita</a>, Prince Casimir developed <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>. In May 1483, Prince Casimir joined his father in Vilnius. There, after the death of <a href="/wiki/Andrzej_Oporowski" class="mw-redirect" title="Andrzej Oporowski">Andrzej Oporowski</a>, Bishop and <a href="/wiki/Vice-Chancellor_of_the_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Vice-Chancellor of the Crown">Vice-Chancellor of the Crown</a>, Prince Casimir took over some of his duties in the chancellery.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012308–309_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012308–309-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> However, his health deteriorated while rumors about his piousness and good deeds spread further. In February 1484, the <a href="/wiki/Sejm_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland" title="Sejm of the Kingdom of Poland">Polish sejm</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lublin" title="Lublin">Lublin</a> was aborted as King Casimir IV rushed back to Lithuania to be with his ill son.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012309_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012309-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Prince Casimir died on 4 March 1484, in <a href="/wiki/Hrodna" class="mw-redirect" title="Hrodna">Grodno</a>. His remains were interred in <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Cathedral" title="Vilnius Cathedral">Vilnius Cathedral</a>, where the dedicated <a href="/wiki/Saint_Casimir%27s_Chapel" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Casimir&#39;s Chapel">Saint Casimir's Chapel</a> was built in 1636.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Pious_life_and_attributed_miracles">Pious life and attributed miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Pious life and attributed miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Kazimierzm.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Kazimierzm.jpg/170px-Kazimierzm.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="286" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Kazimierzm.jpg/255px-Kazimierzm.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/70/Kazimierzm.jpg 2x" data-file-width="294" data-file-height="495" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Kazimierzm.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Dievdirbys" title="Dievdirbys">Lithuanian folk sculpture</a> of Saint Casimir</div></div></div>
<p>Surviving contemporary accounts described Prince Casimir as a young man of exceptional intellect and education, humility and politeness, who strove for justice and fairness.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012309–310_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012309–310-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Early sources do not attest to his piety or devotion to God, but his inclination to religious life increased towards the end of his life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012310_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012310-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Later sources provide some stories of Casimir's religious life. <a href="/wiki/Marcin_Kromer" title="Marcin Kromer">Marcin Kromer</a> (1512–1589) claimed that Casimir refused his physician's advice to have sexual relations with women in hopes to cure his illness.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012310_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012310-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Other accounts claimed that Casimir contracted his lung disease after a particularly hard fast or that he could be found pre-dawn, kneeling by the church gates, waiting for a priest to open them. <a href="/wiki/Zacharias_Ferreri" title="Zacharias Ferreri">Zacharias Ferreri</a> (1479–1524) wrote that Casimir composed a prayer in <a href="/wiki/Hexameter" title="Hexameter">hexameter</a> on Christ's <a href="/wiki/Incarnation_(Christianity)" title="Incarnation (Christianity)">incarnation</a> but this text has not survived.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200433_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200433-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Later, a copy of <i>Omni die dic Mariae</i> (<i>Daily, Daily Sing to Mary</i>) was found in Casimir's coffin.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBurns2007104_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurns2007104-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The hymn became so strongly associated with Casimir that sometimes it known as <i>Hymn of St. Casimir</i> and he is credited as its author.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBurns2007104_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurns2007104-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The lengthy hymn has an intricate <a href="/wiki/Metre_(poetry)" title="Metre (poetry)">meter</a> and rhyme scheme (alternate <a href="/wiki/Acatalectic" class="mw-redirect" title="Acatalectic">acatalectic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catalectic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catalectic">catalectic</a> <a href="/wiki/Trochaic" class="mw-redirect" title="Trochaic">trochaic</a> <a href="/wiki/Dimeter" title="Dimeter">dimeter</a> with <a href="/wiki/Internal_rhyme" title="Internal rhyme">internal rhyme</a> in the first and third verses (aa/b, cc/b)) and was most likely written by <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Cluny" title="Bernard of Cluny">Bernard of Cluny</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEConway2003591_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConway2003591-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of the first miracles attributed to Casimir was his appearance before the Lithuanian army during the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Polotsk" title="Siege of Polotsk">Siege of Polotsk</a> in 1518. Casimir showed where Lithuanian troops could safely cross the <a href="/wiki/Daugava_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Daugava River">Daugava River</a> and relieve the city, besieged by the army of the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow" title="Grand Duchy of Moscow">Grand Duchy of Moscow</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012310_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDuczmal2012310-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Ferreri's hagiography of 1521 mentions many miracles of Casimir are known but describes only one – a Lithuanian victory against the Russians. The description lacks specifics, such as date or location, but most likely refers to the Lithuanian victory in 1519 against Russian troops that raided environs of Vilnius, and not the more popular story of the Siege of Polotsk.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200414–15_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200414–15-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization_and_official_veneration">Canonization and official veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Canonization and official veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg/170px-Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="219" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg/255px-Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg/340px-Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg 2x" data-file-width="419" data-file-height="540" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Casimir_from_Vita_beati_Casimiri.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Casimir on the cover page of his first hagiography</div></div></div>
<p>Casimir's official cult started spreading soon after his death. Already in 1501, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Pope Alexander VI</a>, citing chapel's splendor and Casimir's miracles, granted a special <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgence</a> to those who would pray in the chapel where Casimir was buried from one <a href="/wiki/Vespers" title="Vespers">vespers</a> to another during certain Catholic festivals, and would contribute to the upkeep of the chapel.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003146–147_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003146–147-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> In 1513, <a href="/wiki/Andrzej_Krzycki" title="Andrzej Krzycki">Andrzej Krzycki</a> wrote a poem mentioning numerous wax <a href="/wiki/Votive_offering" title="Votive offering">votive offerings</a> on Casimir's grave.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003148–149_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003148–149-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In 1514, during the <a href="/wiki/Fifth_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Fifth Council of the Lateran">Fifth Council of the Lateran</a>, Casimir's brother <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_I_the_Old" title="Sigismund I the Old">Sigismund I the Old</a> petitioned the pope to canonize Casimir.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200315_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200315-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> After repeated requests, in November 1517, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_X" title="Pope Leo X">Pope Leo X</a> appointed a three-bishop commission and later sent his legate <a href="/wiki/Zacharias_Ferreri" title="Zacharias Ferreri">Zacharias Ferreri</a> to investigate.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200317_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200317-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> He arrived at Vilnius in September 1520 and completed his work in about two months.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas20044_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas20044-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> His findings, the first short <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> of Casimir, was published in 1521 in <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a> as <i>Vita Beati Casimiri Confessoris</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas20044_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas20044-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The canonization was all but certain but Pope Leo X died in December 1521. Research of <a href="/wiki/Zenonas_Ivinskis" title="Zenonas Ivinskis">Zenonas Ivinskis</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paulius_Rabikauskas&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paulius Rabikauskas (page does not exist)">Paulius Rabikauskas</a> showed that there is no documentary proof that he issued a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> canonizing Casimir<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEŽemaitytė2012_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEŽemaitytė2012-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> but many important documents were lost during the <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(1527)" title="Sack of Rome (1527)">Sack of Rome (1527)</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPaltarokas201070_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaltarokas201070-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Protestant Reformation</a> attacked the cult of saints and there were no new canonizations between 1523 and 1588.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAhlgren1998145_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAhlgren1998145-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> However, Casimir was included in the first <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>,</i> published in 1583.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200318_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200318-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The cause of Casimir's cult was taken up by the new Bishop of Vilnius <a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedykt_Woyna&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Benedykt Woyna (page does not exist)">Benedykt Woyna</a> (appointed in 1600). He sent canon Gregorius Swiecicki to Rome with a letter from King <a href="/wiki/Sigismund_III_Vasa" title="Sigismund III Vasa">Sigismund III Vasa</a> requesting to add the feast of Casimir to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Breviary" title="Roman Breviary">Roman Breviary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958b_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958b-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Congregation_of_Rites" title="Sacred Congregation of Rites">Sacred Congregation of Rites</a> refused the request<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958b_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958b-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> but on 7 November 1602 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> issued a <a href="/wiki/Papal_brief" title="Papal brief">papal brief</a> <i>Quae ad sanctorum</i> which authorized his feast <i>sub duplici ritu</i> on 4 March but only in Poland and Lithuania.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003173_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003173-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> The brief also mentioned that Casimir was added to the ranks of saints by Pope Leo X.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003173_26-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003173-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> In the absence of any earlier known papal document explicitly mentioning Casimir as saint, the brief is often cited as Casimir's canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEŽemaitytė2012_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEŽemaitytė2012-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200319_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas200319-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Swiecicki returned to Vilnius with the papal brief and red velvet <a href="/wiki/Labarum" title="Labarum">labarum</a> with the image of Saint Casimir.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958a_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958a-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The city organized a large three-day festival on 10–12 May 1604 to properly accept the papal flag. On the third day, the <a href="/wiki/Cornerstone" title="Cornerstone">cornerstone</a> was laid for the new <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Casimir,_Vilnius" title="Church of St. Casimir, Vilnius">Church of St. Casimir</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958a_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958a-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> The coffin of Casimir was taken out of the crypt and elevated to the altar. Swiecicki testified that when the coffin was opened in August 1604 a wonderful smell filed the cathedral for three days.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003175_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003175-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg/170px-Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="332" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg/255px-Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg/340px-Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg 2x" data-file-width="990" data-file-height="1936" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Schultz_Saint_Casimir.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Casimir by <a href="/wiki/Daniel_Schultz" title="Daniel Schultz">Daniel Schultz</a> (1615–1683)</div></div></div>
<p>In 1607 and 1613, Bishop Woyna declared Casimir patron saint of Lithuania (<i>Patronus principalis Lithuaniae</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The issue of a universal Casimir's feast was not forgotten and in 1620 Bishop <a href="/wiki/Eustachy_Wo%C5%82%C5%82owicz" title="Eustachy Wołłowicz">Eustachy Wołłowicz</a> petitioned <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a> to add Casimir to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Breviary" title="Roman Breviary">Roman Breviary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a>. This time the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Congregation_of_Rites" title="Sacred Congregation of Rites">Sacred Congregation of Rites</a> granted the request in March 1621 and added his feast <i>sub ritu semiduplici</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958b_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1958b-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> In March 1636, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a> allowed the celebration of the feast of Casimir with an <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">octave</a> (<i>duplex cum octava</i>) in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Vilnius" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Vilnius">Diocese of Vilnius</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania" title="Grand Duchy of Lithuania">Grand Duchy of Lithuania</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVilimas2012109_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVilimas2012109-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> That is equivalent of proclaiming Casimir as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Lithuania. On 28 September 1652, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_X" title="Pope Innocent X">Pope Innocent X</a> allowed a fest of <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">transfer of relics</a> of Casimir on a Sunday following the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption of Mary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVaišnora195839_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVaišnora195839-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> On 11 June 1948, when many Lithuanians were <a href="/wiki/Displaced_person" class="mw-redirect" title="Displaced person">displaced war refugees</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> named Casimir the special patron of Lithuanian youth.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPaltarokas2010136_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaltarokas2010136-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Casimir's painting in <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Cathedral" title="Vilnius Cathedral">Vilnius Cathedral</a> is considered to be miraculous. The painting, probably completed around 1520, depicts the saint with two right hands. According to a legend, the painter attempted to redraw the hand in a different place and paint over the old hand, but the old hand miraculously reappeared. More conventional explanations claim that three-handed Casimir was the original intent of the painter to emphasize the exceptional generosity of Casimir ("But when you give to someone in need, don't let your left hand know what your right hand is doing." <a href="/wiki/Matthew_6:3" title="Matthew 6:3">Matthew 6:3</a>) or that the old hand bled through a coat of new paint (similar to a <a href="/wiki/Palimpsest" title="Palimpsest">palimpsest</a>). Around 1636 the painting was covered in gilded silver clothing (<i><a href="/wiki/Riza" title="Riza">riza</a></i>).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Casimir's iconography usually follows the three-handed painting. He is usually depicted as a young man in long red robe lined with <a href="/wiki/Stoat" title="Stoat">stoat</a> fur. Sometimes he wears a red cap of the Grand Duke of Lithuania, but other times, to emphasize his devotion to spiritual life, the cap is placed near Casimir. Almost always he holds a lily, a symbol of virginity, innocence, and purity. He might also hold a cross, a rosary, or a book with words from <i>Omni die dic Mariae</i> (Daily, Daily Sing to Mary). The towns of <a href="/wiki/Kv%C4%97darna" title="Kvėdarna">Kvėdarna</a> and <a href="/wiki/Nemunaitis" title="Nemunaitis">Nemunaitis</a> in Lithuania have Saint Casimir depicted on their coat of arms.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Physical_remains_and_relics">Physical remains and relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Physical remains and relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_(C._Dolci,_XVII).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_%28C._Dolci%2C_XVII%29.jpg/220px-%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_%28C._Dolci%2C_XVII%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="267" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_%28C._Dolci%2C_XVII%29.jpg/330px-%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_%28C._Dolci%2C_XVII%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_%28C._Dolci%2C_XVII%29.jpg/440px-%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_%28C._Dolci%2C_XVII%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1333" data-file-height="1616" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:%C5%9Aviaty_Kazimier._%D0%A1%D1%8C%D0%B2%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D1%96%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80_(C._Dolci,_XVII).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Casimir by <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Dolci" title="Carlo Dolci">Carlo Dolci</a> (1616–1686) in the collection of <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Pitti" title="Palazzo Pitti">Palazzo Pitti</a></div></div></div>
<p>Casimir was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> under the Royal Chapel of <a href="/wiki/Vilnius_Cathedral" title="Vilnius Cathedral">Vilnius Cathedral</a> (present-day Wołłowicz Chapel to the left from the main entrance), constructed by his father in the <a href="/wiki/Gothic_architecture" title="Gothic architecture">Gothic style</a> in 1474. In 1604, the coffin was elevated from the crypt to the altar and in 1636 moved to the dedicated <a href="/wiki/Chapel_of_Saint_Casimir" title="Chapel of Saint Casimir">Chapel of Saint Casimir</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958_34-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The present-day sarcophagus was made in 1747 under the last will of Bishop of Warmia <a href="/w/index.php?title=Christopher_Johan_Szembek&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Christopher Johan Szembek (page does not exist)">Christopher Johan Szembek</a> (1680–1740).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVilnius_Cathedral_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVilnius_Cathedral-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> It is made of linden wood and covered with silver plates; its corners are decorated with gilded eagles.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERėklaitis1958_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERėklaitis1958-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> The sarcophagus was removed from the cathedral on three occasions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958_34-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> In 1655, before the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Vilnius_(1655)" title="Battle of Vilnius (1655)">Battle of Vilnius</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Deluge_(history)" title="Deluge (history)">Deluge</a>, the relics were removed most likely by <a href="/wiki/Jerzy_Bia%C5%82%C5%82ozor" title="Jerzy Białłozor">Jerzy Białłozor</a> and hid by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyprian_Pawe%C5%82_Brzostowski&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cyprian Paweł Brzostowski (page does not exist)">Cyprian Paweł Brzostowski</a> and later by the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Sapieha" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Sapieha">Sapiehas</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Ruzhany_Palace" title="Ruzhany Palace">Ruzhany Palace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958_34-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> They were returned to the devastated chapel in 1663. The relics were removed for a short time in 1702 during the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Vilnius_(1702)" title="Battle of Vilnius (1702)">Battle of Vilnius</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Northern_War" title="Great Northern War">Great Northern War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958_34-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠapoka1958-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> In October 1952, the relics were quietly moved to the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Peter_and_St._Paul,_Vilnius" title="Church of St. Peter and St. Paul, Vilnius">Church of St. Peter and St. Paul</a> by the order of <a href="/wiki/Lithuanian_SSR" class="mw-redirect" title="Lithuanian SSR">Soviet authorities</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPaltarokas2010129_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaltarokas2010129-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> The cathedral was turned into an art gallery. The relics returned to their place in 1989 when the cathedral was reconsecrated.
</p><p>After the rediscovery of the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_Rome" title="Catacombs of Rome">Catacombs of Rome</a> in 1578, the cult of <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> spread throughout Europe (see also <a href="/wiki/Catacomb_saints" title="Catacomb saints">catacomb saints</a>) and the trend did not skip Casimir.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200635_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200635-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> The coffin of Casimir was opened in early 1602 and in August 1604. At the time, canon Gregorius Swiecicki testified that despite humidity the body was intact.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003175–176_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČiurinskas2003175–176-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> But in 1667 there were only bones left; they were inventoried and placed into six cloth bags.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200641–42_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200641–42-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Surviving written records indicate that the coffin was opened in 1664, 1667, 1677, 1690,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1960_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1960-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> 1736, 1838, 1878 (twice),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414_30-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> and 1922.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200635_38-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200635-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> There are several recorded instances when Casimir's relics were gifted to prominent figures and societies: to musicians' <a href="/wiki/Confraternity" title="Confraternity">confraternity</a> at <a href="/wiki/San_Giorgio_Maggiore,_Naples" title="San Giorgio Maggiore, Naples">San Giorgio Maggiore, Naples</a> in early 1650s,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009121_42-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009121-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAbos2003XVI_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbos2003XVI-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200638_44-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200638-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> to King <a href="/wiki/John_III_Sobieski" title="John III Sobieski">John III Sobieski</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cosimo_III_de%27_Medici,_Grand_Duke_of_Tuscany" title="Cosimo III de&#39; Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany">Cosimo III de' Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany</a><sup id="cite_ref-cosimo_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cosimo-45">&#91;n 1&#93;</a></sup> in October 1677,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200638_44-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200638-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> to the <a href="/wiki/Sodality_of_Our_Lady" title="Sodality of Our Lady">Sodality of Our Lady</a> of the Jesuit academy in <a href="/wiki/Mechelen" title="Mechelen">Mechelen</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1960_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1960-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Sovereign_Military_Order_of_Malta" title="Sovereign Military Order of Malta">Order of Malta</a> in October 1690,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELiulevičius1984_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELiulevičius1984-46">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> to Queen <a href="/wiki/Maria_Josepha_of_Austria" title="Maria Josepha of Austria">Maria Josepha of Austria</a> in February 1736,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414_30-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> to Cistercian abbot Sztárek Lajos of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Cik%C3%A1dor_Abbey&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Cikádor Abbey (page does not exist)">Cikádor Abbey</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kloster_Cik%C3%A1dor" class="extiw" title="hu:Kloster Cikádor">hu</a>&#93;</span> in 1860.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200639_47-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200639-47">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBékefi1894102_48-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBékefi1894102-48">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> Many more relics of Casimir can be found in local churches. In particular, in 1838, two teeth and ten unspecified bones were removed from the coffin; the bones were cut into small pieces and distributed among various churches.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200640_49-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200640-49">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup> In 1922, the bones were wrapped in a new cloth and the old cloth was distributed as a relic.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200640–41_50-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200640–41-50">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> It was the last time that a relic of Casimir (one tooth for the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Casimir,_Vilnius" title="Church of St. Casimir, Vilnius">Church of St. Casimir</a>) was taken.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200641_51-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMaslauskaitė200641-51">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Dedications">Dedications</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Casimir&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Dedications">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In his 1970 monograph priest Florijonas Neviera (Florian Niewiero, 1896–1976) counted churches named after Casimir. He found 12 churches in Lithuania (as of 1940), 48 churches and 5 chapels in Poland, 23 Lithuanian and 36 Polish churches in United States (as of 1964), five churches in Canada (<a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Winnipeg" title="Winnipeg">Winnipeg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Toronto" title="Toronto">Toronto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Portneuf,_Quebec" title="Portneuf, Quebec">Portneuf</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Ripon,_Quebec" title="Ripon, Quebec">Ripon</a>), two churches in United Kingdom (<a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> and <a href="/wiki/Manchester" title="Manchester">Manchester</a>), and two churches in Belarus (<a href="/wiki/Vselyub" title="Vselyub">Vselyub</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lepiel" class="mw-redirect" title="Lepiel">Lepiel</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414_30-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> The women's congregation <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Saint_Casimir" title="Sisters of Saint Casimir">Sisters of Saint Casimir</a> was established in 1908 by <a href="/wiki/Maria_Kaupas" title="Maria Kaupas">Maria Kaupas</a> and is active in the United States. In 1945, the <a href="/wiki/College_of_Saint_Casimir" class="mw-redirect" title="College of Saint Casimir">College of Saint Casimir</a> was established in Rome to educate Lithuanian priests who fled west after World War II.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414_30-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEČesnulis201414-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While the devotion to Casimir is most prevalent in Lithuania and Poland and their diaspora communities, his cult can be found in other countries as well. In the 17th century, at least two societies of Saint Casimir were active in <a href="/wiki/Mechelen" title="Mechelen">Mechelen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a> (now Belgium).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1960_41-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERabikauskas1960-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> In the 17th century, Casimir's cult also spread in Italy, particularly <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>; his cult in Rome was more associated with Polish dignitaries and émigrés.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009113_52-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009113-52">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup> Musical performances were organized in Rome in 1675 (words by Sebastiano Lazzarini, music by Francesco Beretta, performed at <a href="/wiki/Santo_Spirito_in_Sassia" title="Santo Spirito in Sassia">Santo Spirito in Sassia</a>) and in 1678 (words by Ottavio Santacroce, music probably by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Giovanni_Bicilli&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Giovanni Bicilli (page does not exist)">Giovanni Bicilli</a>, performed at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Vallicella" title="Santa Maria in Vallicella">Santa Maria in Vallicella</a> on the occasion of the visit by <a href="/wiki/Micha%C5%82_Kazimierz_Radziwi%C5%82%C5%82" title="Michał Kazimierz Radziwiłł">Michał Kazimierz Radziwiłł</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Katarzyna_Sobieska" title="Katarzyna Sobieska">Katarzyna Sobieska</a>), and in Florence in 1706 (words possibly by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Ottoboni_(cardinal)" title="Pietro Ottoboni (cardinal)">Pietro Ottoboni</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009124_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009124-53">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup> music by <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Scarlatti" title="Alessandro Scarlatti">Alessandro Scarlatti</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009113–114_54-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERyszka-Komarnicka2009113–114-54">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup> In Palermo, <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Novelli" title="Pietro Novelli">Pietro Novelli</a> was commissioned painting <i>Coronation of Saint Casimir</i> (l’Incoronazione di s. Casimiro) for the altar of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Chiesa_di_San_Nicola_da_Tolentino_(Palermo)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Chiesa di San Nicola da Tolentino (Palermo) (page does not exist)">Chiesa di San Nicola da Tolentino</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_di_San_Nicola_da_Tolentino_(Palermo)" class="extiw" title="it:Chiesa di San Nicola da Tolentino (Palermo)">it</a>&#93;</span> (now held at the <a href="/wiki/Galleria_Regionale_della_Sicilia" class="mw-redirect" title="Galleria Regionale della Sicilia">Galleria Regionale della Sicilia</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMancuso2013_55-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMancuso2013-55">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The settlements of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Casimir,_Quebec" title="Saint-Casimir, Quebec">Saint-Casimir</a> in Canada (founded 1836) and <a href="/wiki/San_Casimiro" title="San Casimiro">San Casimiro</a> in Venezuela (founded 1785)<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELiulevičius1984_46-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELiulevičius1984-46">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> are named after him. Sculptures of Casimir, among other <a href="/wiki/List_of_royal_saints_and_martyrs" title="List of royal saints and martyrs">canonized royals</a>, can be found in <a href="/wiki/San_Ferdinando,_Livorno" title="San Ferdinando, Livorno">San Ferdinando, Livorno</a>, Italy and <a href="/wiki/Mexico_City_Metropolitan_Cathedral" title="Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral">Metropolitan Cathedral</a>, Mexico City. Stained glass windows with Casimir can be found at the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_Basilica_of_St._Joseph_(San_Jose,_California)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral Basilica of St. Joseph (San Jose, California)">Cathedral Basilica of St. Joseph</a> in <a href="/wiki/San_Jose,_California" title="San Jose, California">San Jose, California</a>, and at the Church of St. Peter in <a href="/wiki/Chevaign%C3%A9" title="Chevaigné">Chevaigné</a>, France.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Since 1846, there is a nursing home in Paris named <i>Maison Saint-Casimir</i>. It was created by the Polish community of France and is run since its opening by Polish nuns <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul</a>.
</p>
