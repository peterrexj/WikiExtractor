<p><b>Fidelis of Sigmaringen</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">OFMCap</a> (1577 - 1622) was a Capuchin <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who was involved in the Catholic <a href="/wiki/Counter-Reformation" title="Counter-Reformation">Counter-Reformation</a>, and was martyred by his opponents at <a href="/wiki/Seewis_im_Pr%C3%A4ttigau" title="Seewis im Prättigau">Seewis im Prättigau</a>, now part of <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>. Fidelis was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1746.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_of_Sigmaringen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born <i>Mark Roy</i> or <i>Rey</i> in 1577,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Sigmaringen" title="Sigmaringen">Sigmaringen</a>, a town in modern-day <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, then under the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen" class="mw-redirect" title="House of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen">Principality of Hohenzollern-Sigmaringen</a>. His father's name was John Rey. He studied law and <a href="/wiki/Philosophy" title="Philosophy">philosophy</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Freiburg" title="University of Freiburg">University of Freiburg</a>.
</p><p>Roy subsequently taught philosophy at this university, ultimately earning the degree of <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Law" title="Doctor of Law">Doctor of Law</a>. During his time as a student he did not drink wine, and wore a <a href="/wiki/Cilice" title="Cilice">hair-shirt</a>. He was known for his modesty, meekness and <a href="/wiki/Chastity" title="Chastity">chastity</a>.
</p><p>In 1604, Roy accompanied, as <a href="/wiki/Preceptor" title="Preceptor">preceptor</a> (teacher-mentor), three young <a href="/wiki/Swabia" title="Swabia">Swabian</a> gentlemen on their travels through the principal parts of Europe. During six years of travel, he attended <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> very frequently. In every town they came to, he visited the hospitals and churches, passed several hours on his knees in the presence of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>, and was generous to the poor, sometimes giving them the very clothes off his back.<sup id="cite_ref-fmedia_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fmedia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon his return, he practiced law as a counselor or advocate, at <a href="/wiki/Colmar" title="Colmar">Colmar</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> France, where he came to be known as the 'poor man's lawyer'. He scrupulously forbore all invectives, detractions, and whatever might affect the reputation of any adversary. Disenchanted with the evils associated with his profession, he determined to join his brother George as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin friars</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fmedia_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fmedia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_as_a_friar">Life as a friar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_of_Sigmaringen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life as a friar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Upon entering the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin order</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">guardian</a> gave him the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> <i>Fidelis</i>, the <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> word for "faithful", alluding to that text from the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Revelation" title="Book of Revelation">Book of Revelation</a> which promises a crown of life to him who shall continue faithful to the end. He finished his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> and studies for the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priesthood</a>, presiding over his first Mass at the Capuchin <a href="/wiki/Friary" class="mw-redirect" title="Friary">friary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Fribourg" title="Fribourg">Fribourg</a> (in present-day Switzerland) on 4 October 1612 (the feast day of St. <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, founder of the order).
</p><p>As soon as Fidelis finished his course of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a>, he was immediately employed in <a href="/wiki/Preaching" class="mw-redirect" title="Preaching">preaching</a> and in hearing <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confessions</a>. He became guardian of the Capuchin friary in Weltkirchen, <a href="/wiki/Feldkirch,_Vorarlberg" title="Feldkirch, Vorarlberg">Feldkirch</a>, (in present-day Austria). During a severe epidemic in a city, Fidelis cared for and cured many sick soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-fmedia_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fmedia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Many residents of the town and neighboring places were reformed by his zealous labors, and several <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinists</a> were converted. The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Doctrine of the Faith">Congregation of the Doctrine of the Faith</a> commissioned Fidelis to preach in the <a href="/wiki/Graub%C3%BCnden" class="mw-redirect" title="Graubünden">Graubünden</a> region of eastern Switzerland. Eight other Capuchin friars were to be his assistants, and they labored in this mission under his direction.
</p><p>The Calvinists of that territory, being incensed at his success in converting their brethren, loudly threatened Fidelis' life, and he prepared himself for <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a>. Ralph de Salis and another Calvinist gentleman were both converted by his missionary efforts. Fidelis and his companions entered into <a href="/wiki/Pr%C3%A4ttigau" title="Prättigau">Prättigau</a>, a small district of Graubünden, in 1622, on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Epiphany" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Epiphany">Feast of the Epiphany</a>, January 6. The effects of his ardent zeal, about which the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Coire" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Coire">Bishop of Coire</a> sent a lengthy and full account to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Evangelization_of_Peoples" title="Congregation for the Evangelization of Peoples">Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith</a>, enraged the Calvinists in that province.
</p><p>On 24 April 1622,<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Fidelis made his confession, celebrated Mass and then preached at <a href="/wiki/Gr%C3%BCsch" title="Grüsch">Grüsch</a>. At the end of his sermon, which he had delivered with more than ordinary zeal, he stood silent all of a sudden, with his eyes fixed upon Heaven, in <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">ecstasy</a>. He foretold his death to several persons in the clearest terms, and began signing his letters, <i>P. Fidelis, prope diem esca vermium</i> ("Father Fidelis, in days ahead to become food for worms"). After the service at Grüsch he and several companions traveled to <a href="/wiki/Seewis" class="mw-redirect" title="Seewis">Seewis</a>. His companions noted that he was particularly cheerful.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_of_Sigmaringen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_(Parma_ca.1752).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_%28Parma_ca.1752%29.jpg/200px-Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_%28Parma_ca.1752%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="292" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_%28Parma_ca.1752%29.jpg/300px-Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_%28Parma_ca.1752%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_%28Parma_ca.1752%29.jpg/400px-Giambattista_Tiepolo_-_I_santi_Fedele_da_Sigmaringen_e_Giuseppe_da_Leonessa_%28Parma_ca.1752%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1714" data-file-height="2500" /></a><figcaption>St. Fidelis of Sigmaringen with St. <a href="/wiki/Joseph_of_Leonessa" title="Joseph of Leonessa">Joseph of Leonessa</a> (<a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Tiepolo" title="Giovanni Battista Tiepolo">Tiepolo</a>, 1752–1758).</figcaption></figure>
<p>On 24 April, in a campaign organized by the <a href="/wiki/Habsburgs" class="mw-redirect" title="Habsburgs">Habsburgs</a>, Fidelis was preaching under protection of some <a href="/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy" title="Habsburg monarchy">Austrian</a> imperial soldiers in the Church at Seewis with the aim of reconverting the people of Seewis to <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholicism</a>. During the sermon, his listeners were called "to arms" by the Calvinist agitators outside. Some of the people went to face the Austrian troops outside the church. Fidelis had been persuaded by the remaining Catholics to immediately flee with the Austrian troops out of Seewis, which he did, but then returned alone to Grüsch. On his way back he was confronted by 20 Calvinist soldiers who demanded unsuccessfully that he renounce the Catholic faith, and when he refused, they subsequently murdered him.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A local account:
</p>
<blockquote><p>From Grüsch he went to preach at Seewis, where, with great energy, he exhorted the Catholics to constancy in the faith. After a Calvinist had discharged his <a href="/wiki/Musket" title="Musket">musket</a> at him in the Church, the Catholics entreated him to leave the place. He answered that death was his gain and his joy, and that he was ready to lay down his life in God's cause. On his road back to Grüsch, he met twenty Calvinist soldiers with a minister at their head. They called him a false prophet, and urged him to embrace their sect. He answered: "I am sent to you to confute, not to embrace your heresy. The Catholic religion is the faith of all ages, I fear not death." One of them beat him down to the ground by a stroke on the head with his backsword. Fidelis rose again on his knees, and stretching forth his arms in the form of a cross, said with a feeble voice "Pardon my enemies, O Lord: blinded by passion they know not what they do. Lord Jesus, have mercy on me. <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Mary</a>, Mother of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">God</a>, succor me!." Another sword stroke clove his skull, and he fell to the ground and lay in a pool of his own blood. The soldiers, not content with this, added many <a href="/wiki/Stabbing" title="Stabbing">stab</a> wounds to his body with their long knives, and hacked-off his left leg, as they said, to punish him for his many journeys into those parts to preach to them.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fidelis_of_Sigmaringen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>It is said that a Catholic woman lay concealed near the place of Fidelis' martyrdom as the saint was slain. After the soldiers had left, she came out to assess the incident and found the martyr's eyes open, fixed on the heavens. He was buried by Catholics the next day. 
</p><p>The rebels were soon after defeated by the imperial troops, an event which the martyr had foretold. The Protestant minister who had participated in Fidelis' martyrdom was converted by this circumstance, made a public abjuration of Calvinism and was received into the Catholic Church.
</p><p>After six months, the martyr's body was found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a>, but his head and left arm were separated from his body. The body parts were then placed into two <a href="/wiki/Reliquaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Reliquaries">reliquaries</a>, one sent to the Cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Chur" title="Chur">Coire</a>, at the behest of the bishop, and laid under the <a href="/wiki/High_altar#Roman_Catholic_churches" class="mw-redirect" title="High altar">High Altar</a>; the other was placed in the Capuchin church at Weltkirchen, <a href="/wiki/Feldkirch,_Vorarlberg" title="Feldkirch, Vorarlberg">Feldkirch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>.
</p><p>Saint Fidelis' <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> in the Catholic Church is celebrated on 24 April.
</p>
