					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>William Arnaud</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (died 28 May 1242) was a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Medieval_inquisition" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval inquisition">inquisitor</a> and martyr.
</p><p>William was a native of <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1234, Pope <a href="/wiki/Gregory_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory IX">Gregory IX</a> named him inquisitor in the dioceses of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Agen" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Agen">Agen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Albi" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Albi">Albi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Carcassonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Carcassonne">Carcassonne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Toulouse">Toulouse</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was also active with Pierre Seilan in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Cahors" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Cahors">diocese of Cahors</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was learned in <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> and gained a reputation for zealousness in his inquisitorial duties.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStarrs2003_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStarrs2003-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He raised enough opposition that he was banished from Toulouse in October 1235 and only allowed back in March 1236 after papal intercession with the count.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarber2014151–152_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarber2014151–152-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Chronicle</i> of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Guillaume_Pelhisson&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guillaume Pelhisson (page does not exist)">Guillaume Pelhisson</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Pelhisson" class="extiw" title="de:Guillaume Pelhisson">de</a>&#93;</span> is an important source for William's inquests, since Pelhisson had access to its now lost records.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETaylor201122–23_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETaylor201122–23-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He appears to have been the most active inquisitor in the region in the 1230s.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarber2014154_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarber2014154-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 28 May 1242, William and eleven others were <a href="/wiki/Avignonet_massacre" title="Avignonet massacre">massacred at Avignonet</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Their murder was arranged by the bailiff, Raymond of Alfaro, perhaps with the tacit agreement of Count <a href="/wiki/Raymond_VII_of_Toulouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Raymond VII of Toulouse">Raymond VII of Toulouse</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins2016766_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins2016766-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The murderers themselves came from the <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Monts%C3%A9gur" title="Château de Montségur">castle of Montségur</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarber2014154_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarber2014154-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> None of the murderers were ever punished.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOldenbourg1961337_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOldenbourg1961337-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Cathars" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathars">Cathars</a> celebrated William's death, even composing songs in <a href="/wiki/Old_Occitan" title="Old Occitan">Occitan</a> about it, according to an inquisitorial deposition from 1244.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The Cathar leader <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre-Roger_de_Mirepoix&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pierre-Roger de Mirepoix (page does not exist)">Pierre-Roger de Mirepoix</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Roger_de_Mirepoix" class="extiw" title="fr:Pierre-Roger de Mirepoix">fr</a>&#93;</span> expressed a desire to drink wine from William's skull, had it not been crushed to pieces.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOldenbourg1961336_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOldenbourg1961336-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The fictionalized but historically based account in the <i><a href="/wiki/Isarn_(inquisitor)" title="Isarn (inquisitor)">Novas de l'heretje</a></i> is probably referring to William under the name "Huc Arnaut":<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELéglu2002125,_127_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELéglu2002125,_127-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1157697682">.mw-parser-output .verse_translation .translated{padding-left:2em!important}@media only screen and (max-width:43.75em){.mw-parser-output .verse_translation.wrap_when_small td{display:block;padding-left:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .verse_translation.wrap_when_small .translated{padding-left:0.5em!important}}</style>
</p>
<table role="presentation" class="verse_translation" style="margin-left:1em !important">

<tbody><tr style="vertical-align:top">
<td><div style="font-style:italic;text-align:left" lang="oc" class="poem">
<p>Per quels Prezicadors no se volon cessar,<br />
Nil savis Huc Arnaut anc no s'i volc palpar,<br />
Per que li fals heretje l'an fag lo cap trencar;<br />
Fraire B. de Caus lo volc de recemblar.
</p>
</div>
</td>
<td class="translated"><div style="font-style:roman;text-align:left" lang="en" class="poem">
<p>These preachers do not wish to stop,<br />
nor did the learned Huc Arnaut ever hold back,<br />
which is why the false heretics have had him decapitated;<br />
Friar Bernard de Caux wants to emulate him.
</p>
</div>
</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Catholics reported miracles following the deaths at Avignonet, but it was not until 6 October 1866 that they were <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_IX" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius IX">Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStarrs2003_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStarrs2003-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> William is listed in the revised <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> of 2004, but not in the <i><a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins2016766_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins2016766-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
