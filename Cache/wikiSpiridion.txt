<p><b>Saint Spyridon, Bishop of Trimythous</b> also sometimes written <b>Saint Spiridon</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἅγιος Σπυρίδων</span></span>; c. 270 – 348) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> honoured in both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">Eastern</a> and <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western</a> <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> traditions.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Spyridon was born in <a href="/wiki/Askeia" class="mw-redirect" title="Askeia">Askeia</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>.  He worked as a <a href="/wiki/Shepherd" title="Shepherd">shepherd</a> and was known for his great piety.  He married and had one daughter, Irene.  Upon the death of his wife, Spyridon entered a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, and their daughter, a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>.
</p><p>Spyridon eventually became <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Trimythous" class="mw-redirect" title="Trimythous">Trimythous</a>, or Tremithous (today called <a href="/wiki/Tremetousia" title="Tremetousia">Tremetousia</a>), in the district of <a href="/wiki/Larnaca" title="Larnaca">Larnaca</a>.  He took part in the <a href="/wiki/First_Ecumenical_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="First Ecumenical Council">First Ecumenical Council</a> of <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">Nicaea</a> (325), where he was instrumental in countering the <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theological</a> arguments of <a href="/wiki/Arius" title="Arius">Arius</a> and his followers.
</p><p>He reportedly converted a <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> <a href="/wiki/Philosopher" title="Philosopher">philosopher</a> to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by using a <a href="/wiki/Potsherd" class="mw-redirect" title="Potsherd">potsherd</a> to illustrate how one single entity (a piece of pottery) could be composed of three unique entities (fire, water and clay); a <a href="/wiki/Metaphor" title="Metaphor">metaphor</a> for the Christian <a href="/wiki/Doctrine" title="Doctrine">doctrine</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a>.
</p><p>As soon as Spyridon finished speaking, the shard is said to have miraculously burst into flame, water dripped on the ground, and only dust remained in his hand (other accounts of this event say that it was a brick he held in his hand).
</p><p>After the council, Saint Spyridon returned to his <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a> in Tremithous. He later fell into disfavor during the <a href="/wiki/Persecution" title="Persecution">persecutions</a> of the emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximinus_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Maximinus II">Maximinus</a>, but died peacefully in old age. 
</p><p>Spyridon was popular in Byzantine literature. A poem, now lost, was dedicated to him by his pupil Triphyllios. It inspired two 7th-century <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vitae</a>, one by Theodore of Paphos (c. 655) and another possibly by <a href="/wiki/Leontios_of_Neapolis" title="Leontios of Neapolis">Leontios of Neapolis</a>. The former was used by <a href="/wiki/Simeon_Metaphrastes" class="mw-redirect" title="Simeon Metaphrastes">Simeon Metaphrastes</a>. <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Georgian_language" title="Georgian language">Georgian</a> hagiographies also survive.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When the <a href="/wiki/Arab" class="mw-redirect" title="Arab">Arabs</a> took Cyprus, Spyridon's body was disinterred and taken to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.  The <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a>, and contained a sprig of <a href="/wiki/Basil" title="Basil">basil</a>, the "royal plant," both of which were taken as a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">sign</a> of divine confirmation of his sanctity.
</p><p>When, in 1453, Constantinople <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople" title="Fall of Constantinople">fell to the Ottomans</a>, Spyridon's relics were removed again; this time, to the island of <a href="/wiki/Corfu#Saint_Spyridon_the_Keeper_of_the_City" title="Corfu">Corfu</a> by a Corfiote <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> called Kalohairetis (Καλοχαιρέτης), where they remain to this day, in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Spyridon_Church" title="Saint Spyridon Church">Saint Spyridon Church</a>.
</p><p>The relics are taken in <a href="/wiki/Procession" title="Procession">procession</a> every <a href="/wiki/Palm_Sunday" title="Palm Sunday">Palm Sunday</a> and on other special occasions, for <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">veneration</a> by the faithful. All Philharmonics of Corfu, including the <a href="/wiki/Philharmonic_Society_of_Corfu" title="Philharmonic Society of Corfu">Philharmonic Society of Corfu</a> take part in these ceremonial events. The relic of his right hand was located in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Vallicella" title="Santa Maria in Vallicella">Santa Maria in Vallicella</a>, to which it was given by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> to Cardinal Cesare of Baronio of the Oratory of Saint Philip Neri. There it remained until 1986 when the right arm of Saint Spyridon was brought back to <a href="/wiki/Kerkyra" class="mw-redirect" title="Kerkyra">Kerkyra</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patron_saint_and_miracles">Patron saint and miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patron saint and miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patron_saint">Patron saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patron saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Spyridon is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pottery" title="Pottery">potters</a> (from the miracle of the potsherd, above) and of the island of <a href="/wiki/Corfu" title="Corfu">Corfu</a> where he is called "Αγιος Σπυρίδων ο πολιούχος", "Saint Spyridon, the Keeper of the City", for the miracle of expelling the <a href="/wiki/Plague_(disease)" title="Plague (disease)">plague</a> (πανούκλη) from the island. He is also the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Piraeus" title="Piraeus">Piraeus</a> where he is celebrated and honored every year on 12 December.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Corfu">Corfu</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Corfu">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Saint_Spyridon_Church" title="Saint Spyridon Church">Saint Spyridon Church</a></div>
<p>It is believed by the faithful that the plague, on its way out of the island, scratched one of the fortification stones of the old citadel (<a href="/wiki/Old_Fortress,_Corfu" title="Old Fortress, Corfu">Palaio Frourio</a>) to indicate its fury for being expelled. This scratch is still shown to visitors.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg/220px-THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="285" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg/330px-THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg/440px-THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1038" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:THE_FIRST_COUNCIL_OF_NICEA.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">Icon</a> showing Saint Spyridon (<i>center, front</i>) silencing <a href="/wiki/Arius" title="Arius">Arius</a> (<i>right, with hands over his mouth</i>) during the <a href="/wiki/First_Ecumenical_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="First Ecumenical Council">First Ecumenical Council</a> of <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">Nicaea</a> in 325.</div></div></div>
<p>St. Spyridon is also believed to have saved the island at the <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Corfu_(1716)" title="Siege of Corfu (1716)">second great siege</a> of Corfu which took place in 1716. At that time the Turkish army and naval force led by the great <a href="/wiki/Sultan" title="Sultan">Sultan</a> <a href="/wiki/Achmet_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Achmet III">Achmet III</a> appeared in <a href="/wiki/Butrinto" class="mw-redirect" title="Butrinto">Butrinto</a> opposite Corfu.
</p><p>On July 8 the Turkish fleet carrying 33,000 men sailed to Corfu from Butrinto and established a beachhead in Ipsos the same day the <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venetian</a> fleet encountered the Turkish fleet off the channel of Corfu and defeated it in the ensuing naval battle. On July 19 the Turkish army reached the hills of the town and laid siege to the city. After repeated failed attempts and heavy fighting, the Turks were forced to raise the siege which had lasted 22 days.
</p><p>There were also rumors spreading among the Turks that some of their soldiers saw St. Spyridon as a monk threatening them with a lit torch and that helped increase their panic. This victory over the Ottomans, therefore, was attributed not only to the leadership of Count <a href="/wiki/Johann_Matthias_von_der_Schulenburg" title="Johann Matthias von der Schulenburg">Schulenburg</a> who commanded the stubborn defense of the island against the Ottomans but also to the miraculous intervention of St. Spyridon.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg/220px-Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="243" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg/330px-Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9d/Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="398" data-file-height="439" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Zemen-monastery-st-spiridon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">Fresco</a> icon of St. Spiridon at <a href="/wiki/Zemen_Monastery" title="Zemen Monastery">Zemen Monastery</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>After the victorious outcome of the battle, Venice honored Schulenburg and the Corfiotes for successfully defending the island. The great composer <a href="/wiki/Vivaldi" class="mw-redirect" title="Vivaldi">Vivaldi</a> was commissioned to write an <a href="/wiki/Opera" title="Opera">opera</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/Juditha_triumphans" title="Juditha triumphans">Juditha triumphans</a></i>, in celebration of the victory.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Recognizing St. Spyridon's role in the defense of the island, Venice legislated the annual "<a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">Litany</a> of St. Spyridon" on August 11 as a commemoration of the event. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated in the East on the Saturday before <a href="/wiki/Great_Lent" title="Great Lent">Great Lent</a> (known as "<a href="/wiki/Maslenitsa" title="Maslenitsa">Cheesefare</a> Saturday") and December 12.  For those <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Churches">Eastern Churches</a> that follow the traditional <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>, December 12 falls on December 25 of the modern <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>.  In the <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">West</a> he is commemorated on December 14.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other">Other</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Spyridon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Other">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>St. Spyridon is also the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Tolstoy_family" title="Tolstoy family">Tolstoy family</a>. Andrei Tolstoy (<a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl.</a> 15th century) chose St. Spyridon as the family's saint and he remains so in both branches. The <a href="/wiki/Grand_Prince" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Prince">Grand Prince</a> of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow" title="Grand Duchy of Moscow">Muscovy</a> <a href="/wiki/Vasily_II_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Vasily II of Russia">Basil II</a> (1425–1462) apparently gave a gold <a href="/wiki/Cross" title="Cross">cross</a> containing <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of the saint to Andrei. This <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> survives and is held by the senior member of the Tolstoy family, now <a href="/wiki/Nikolai_Tolstoy" title="Nikolai Tolstoy">Nikolai Tolstoy</a>.
</p>
