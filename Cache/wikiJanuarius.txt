<p><b>Januarius</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˌ/: secondary stress follows">ˌ</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span><span title="/æ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;bad&#39;">æ</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/./: syllable break">.</span><span title="/j/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;yes&#39;">j</span><span title="/u/: &#39;u&#39; in &#39;influence&#39;">u</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/ɛər/: &#39;are&#39; in &#39;bare&#39;">ɛər</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">JAN</span>-yoo-<span style="font-size:90%">AIR</span>-ee-əs</i></a>;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ianuarius</i>; <a href="/wiki/Neapolitan_language" title="Neapolitan language">Neapolitan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Gennaro</i>), also known as <span class="nowrap"><b>Januarius I of Benevento</b></span>, was <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Benevento" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Benevento">Bishop of Benevento</a> and is a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. While no contemporary sources on his life are preserved, later sources and <a href="/wiki/Christian_legend" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian legend">legends</a> claim that he died during the <a href="/wiki/Great_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Persecution">Great Persecution</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-cathenc_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathenc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which ended with <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>'s retirement in 305.
</p><p>Januarius is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, where the faithful gather three times a year in <a href="/wiki/Naples_Cathedral" title="Naples Cathedral">Naples Cathedral</a> to witness the <a href="/wiki/Liquefaction" title="Liquefaction">liquefaction</a> of what is claimed to be a sample of his blood kept in a sealed glass <a href="/wiki/Ampoule" title="Ampoule">ampoule</a>.
</p><p><span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span><span class="anchor" id="History"></span>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known of the life of Januarius,<sup id="cite_ref-cathenc_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathenc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and what follows is mostly derived from later Christian sources, such as the <i>Acta Bononensia</i> (BHL 4132, not earlier than 6th century) and the <i>Acta Vaticana</i> (BHL 4115, 9th century), and from later folk traditions.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg/220px-Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="355" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg/330px-Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg/440px-Napoli-Ribera-San-Gennaro.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1784" data-file-height="2880" /></a><figcaption>Ribera, <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Januarius_Emerges_Unscathed_from_the_Furnace" title="Saint Januarius Emerges Unscathed from the Furnace">Saint Januarius Emerges Unscathed from the Furnace</a></i>, Naples Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to various <a href="/wiki/Hagiographies" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographies">hagiographies</a>, Januarius was born in <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a> to a rich patrician family that traced its descent to the <a href="/wiki/Caudini" title="Caudini">Caudini</a> tribe of the <a href="/wiki/Samnites" title="Samnites">Samnites</a>. At a young age of 15, he became local priest of his parish in Benevento, which at the time was relatively <a href="/wiki/Pagan" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagan">pagan</a>. When Januarius was 20, he became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Naples">Bishop of Naples</a> and befriended <a href="/wiki/Juliana_of_Nicomedia" title="Juliana of Nicomedia">Juliana of Nicomedia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sossius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Sossius">Saint Sossius</a> whom he met during his priestly studies. During the <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1154941027">.mw-parser-output .frac{white-space:nowrap}.mw-parser-output .frac .num,.mw-parser-output .frac .den{font-size:80%;line-height:0;vertical-align:super}.mw-parser-output .frac .den{vertical-align:sub}.mw-parser-output .sr-only{border:0;clip:rect(0,0,0,0);clip-path:polygon(0px 0px,0px 0px,0px 0px);height:1px;margin:-1px;overflow:hidden;padding:0;position:absolute;width:1px}</style><span class="frac" role="math">1<span class="sr-only">+</span><span class="num">1</span>&#8260;<span class="den">2</span></span>-year-long persecution of Christians by Emperor Diocletian, he hid his fellow Christians and prevented them from being caught. Unfortunately, while visiting Sossius in jail, he too was arrested. He and his colleagues were condemned to be thrown to wild bears in the <a href="/wiki/Flavian_Amphitheater_(Pozzuoli)" title="Flavian Amphitheater (Pozzuoli)">Flavian Amphitheater</a> at <a href="/wiki/Pozzuoli" title="Pozzuoli">Pozzuoli</a>, but the sentence was changed due to fear of public disturbances, and they were instead beheaded at the <a href="/wiki/Solfatara_(volcano)" title="Solfatara (volcano)">Solfatara</a> crater near Pozzuoli.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;n 1&#93;</a></sup> Other legends state either that the wild beasts refused to eat them, or that he was thrown into a furnace but came out unscathed.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The earliest extant mention of him is contained in a 432 letter by Uranius, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Nola" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Nola">bishop of Nola</a>, on the death of his mentor Saint <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_Nola" title="Paulinus of Nola">Paulinus of Nola</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-yuri_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-yuri-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> where it is stated that the ghosts of Januarius and <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Saint Martin</a> appeared to Paulinus three days before the latter's death in 431. About Januarius, the account says only that he was "bishop as well as martyr, an illustrious member of the Neapolitan church".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;n 2&#93;</a></sup>  The Acta Bononensia says that "At Pozzuoli in Campania [is honored the memory] of the holy martyrs Januarius, Bishop of Beneventum, Festus his <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>, and Desiderius <a href="/wiki/Lector" title="Lector">lector</a>, together with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sossius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Sossius">Sossius</a> deacon of the church of <a href="/wiki/Misenum" class="mw-redirect" title="Misenum">Misenum</a>, <a href="/wiki/Proculus_of_Pozzuoli" title="Proculus of Pozzuoli">Proculus</a>, deacon of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Pozzuoli" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Pozzuoli">Pozzuoli</a>, Eutyches, and Acutius, who after chains and imprisonment were beheaded under the emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-cathenc_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathenc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Celebrations">Celebrations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Celebrations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_San_Gennaro" title="Feast of San Gennaro">Feast of San Gennaro</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg/220px-Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="168" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg/330px-Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg/440px-Spadaro_eruzione_vesuvio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2260" data-file-height="1730" /></a><figcaption>San Gennaro procession in Naples, 1631</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_San_Gennaro" title="Feast of San Gennaro">Feast of San Gennaro</a> is celebrated on 19 September in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> of the Catholic Church.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;n 3&#93;</a></sup> In the Eastern Church, it is celebrated on 21 April.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  The city of <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> has more than fifty official <a href="/wiki/Patron_saints_of_Naples" title="Patron saints of Naples">patron saints</a>, although its principal patron is Saint Januarius.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>, the Feast of San Gennaro is also a highlight of the year for <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Little_Italy,_Manhattan" title="Little Italy, Manhattan">Little Italy</a>, with the saint's <a href="/wiki/Polychrome" title="Polychrome">polychrome</a> statue carried through the middle of a <a href="/wiki/Street_fair" title="Street fair">street fair</a> stretching for blocks.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Januarius.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Januarius.jpg/200px-Januarius.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="286" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Januarius.jpg/300px-Januarius.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Januarius.jpg/400px-Januarius.jpg 2x" data-file-width="552" data-file-height="788" /></a><figcaption><i>Martyrdom of Saint Januarius</i> by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Girolamo_Pesce&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Girolamo Pesce (page does not exist)">Girolamo Pesce</a></figcaption></figure>

<p>According to an early <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;n 4&#93;</a></sup> Januarius's <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were transferred by order of <a href="/wiki/Severus_of_Naples" title="Severus of Naples">Saint Severus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Naples">Bishop of Naples</a>, to the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_San_Gennaro" title="Catacombs of San Gennaro">Neapolitan catacombs</a> "<a href="/wiki/Extra_moenia" title="Extra moenia">outside the walls</a>" (<i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">extra moenia</i></span></i>).<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;n 5&#93;</a></sup> In the early ninth century the body was moved to <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Beneventum</a> by <a href="/wiki/Sico_of_Benevento" title="Sico of Benevento">Sico</a>, <a href="/wiki/Princes_of_Benevento" class="mw-redirect" title="Princes of Benevento">prince</a> of <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Benevento" class="mw-redirect" title="Principality of Benevento">Benevento</a>, with the head remaining in Naples. Subsequently, during the turmoil at the time of <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick Barbarossa</a>, his body was moved again, this time to the <a href="/wiki/Territorial_Abbey_of_Montevergine" title="Territorial Abbey of Montevergine">Territorial Abbey of Montevergine</a> where it was rediscovered in 1480.
</p><p>
At the instigation of Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Oliviero_Carafa" title="Oliviero Carafa">Oliviero Carafa</a>, his body was finally transferred in 1497 to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, where he is the city's <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>. Carafa commissioned a richly decorated <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>, the <i>Succorpo</i>, beneath the <a href="/wiki/Naples_Cathedral" title="Naples Cathedral">cathedral</a> to house the reunited body and head properly. The <i>Succorpo</i> was finished in 1506 and is considered one of the prominent monuments of the <a href="/wiki/High_Renaissance" title="High Renaissance">High Renaissance</a> in the city.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></p><ul class="gallery mw-gallery-traditional">

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Blood">Blood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Blood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Januarius is famous for the annual liquefaction of his <a href="/wiki/Blood" title="Blood">blood</a>, which, according to <a href="/wiki/Christian_legend" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian legend">legend</a>, was saved by a woman called Eusebia just after the saint's death. A chronicle of Naples written in 1382 describes the cult of Saint Januarius in detail, but mentions neither the relic nor the miracle.<sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-altamura_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-altamura-20">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The first certain date is 1389, when it was found to have melted.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Then, over the following two and a half centuries official reports began to appear declaring that the blood spontaneously melted, at first once a year, then twice, and finally three times a year. While the report of the very first incidence of liquefaction did not make any explicit reference to the skull of the saint, soon afterwards assertions began to appear that this relic was activating the melting process, as if the blood, recognizing a part of the body to which it belonged, "were impatient while waiting for its resurrection".<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> This explanation was definitively abandoned only in the eighteenth century.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thousands of people assemble to witness this event in Naples Cathedral three times a year: on September 19 (Saint Januarius's Day, commemorating his martyrdom), on December 16 (celebrating his patronage of Naples and its archdiocese), and on the Saturday before the first Sunday of May (commemorating the reunification of his relics).<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The blood is also said to spontaneously liquefy at certain other times, such as <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">papal</a> visits. It supposedly liquefied in the presence of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> in 1848, but not that of <a href="/wiki/John_Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="John Paul II">John Paul II</a> in 1979 or <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XVI">Benedict XVI</a> in 2007.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> On March 21, 2015, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> the dried blood during a visit to <a href="/wiki/Naples_Cathedral" title="Naples Cathedral">Naples Cathedral</a>, saying the <a href="/wiki/Lord%27s_Prayer" title="Lord&#39;s Prayer">Lord's Prayer</a> over it and kissing it. <a href="/wiki/Crescenzio_Sepe" title="Crescenzio Sepe">Archbishop Sepe</a> then declared that "The blood has half liquefied, which shows that Saint Januarius loves our pope and Naples."<sup id="cite_ref-Benge_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benge-27">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Ritual_liquefaction">Ritual liquefaction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Ritual liquefaction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Die_Gartenlaube_(1860)_b_524.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Die_Gartenlaube_%281860%29_b_524.jpg/100px-Die_Gartenlaube_%281860%29_b_524.jpg" decoding="async" width="100" height="239" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Die_Gartenlaube_%281860%29_b_524.jpg/150px-Die_Gartenlaube_%281860%29_b_524.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Die_Gartenlaube_%281860%29_b_524.jpg/200px-Die_Gartenlaube_%281860%29_b_524.jpg 2x" data-file-width="830" data-file-height="1984" /></a><figcaption>Drawing of the reliquary containing the two ampoules said to hold Januarius' blood, c. 1860</figcaption></figure>
<p>The blood is stored in two hermetically sealed small <a href="/wiki/Ampoule" title="Ampoule">ampoules</a>, held since the 17th century in a <a href="/wiki/Silver" title="Silver">silver</a> <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> between two round glass plates about 12&#160;cm wide. The smaller, cylindrical ampoule contains only a few reddish spots on its walls, the bulk having allegedly been removed and taken to <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> by <a href="/wiki/Charles_III_of_Spain" title="Charles III of Spain">Charles III</a>. The larger, almond-shaped ampoule, with a capacity of about 60 <a href="/wiki/Milliliter" class="mw-redirect" title="Milliliter">ml</a>, is about 60% filled with a dark reddish substance.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Separate reliquaries hold bone fragments believed to belong to Saint Januarius.
</p><p>For most of the time, the ampoules are kept in a bank vault, whose keys are held by a commission of local notables, including the <a href="/wiki/Mayor" title="Mayor">mayor</a> of Naples; the bones are kept in a <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> under the main altar of Naples Cathedral. On feast days, all these relics are taken in procession from the cathedral to the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Chiara_(Naples)" class="mw-redirect" title="Santa Chiara (Naples)">Monastery of Santa Chiara</a>, where the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> holds up the reliquary and tilts it to show that the contents are solid, then places it on the high altar next to the saint's other relics. After intense prayers by the faithful, including the so-called "relatives of Saint Januarius" (<i>parenti di San Gennaro</i>), the content of the larger vial typically appears to liquify. The archbishop then holds up the vial and tilts it again to demonstrate that liquefaction has taken place. The announcement of the liquifaction is greeted with at the 13th-century <a href="/wiki/Castel_Nuovo" title="Castel Nuovo">Castel Nuovo</a>. The ampoules remain exposed on the altar for eight days, while the priests move or turn them periodically to show that the contents remain liquid.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Sir <a href="/wiki/Francis_Ronalds" title="Francis Ronalds">Francis Ronalds</a> gives a detailed description of the May 1819 ritual in his travel journal.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The liquifaction sometimes takes place almost immediately, but can take hours or even days. Records kept at the Duomo tell that on rare occasions the contents fail to liquify, are found already liquified when the ampoules are taken from the safe,<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> or liquify outside the usual dates.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_28-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Scientific_studies">Scientific studies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Scientific studies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>While the Catholic Church has always supported the celebrations, it has never formulated an official statement on the phenomenon and maintains a neutral stance about scientific investigations.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_28-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-28">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> It does not permit the vials to be opened, for fear that doing so may cause irreparable damage. This makes close analysis impossible. Nevertheless, a spectroscopic analysis performed in 1902 by Gennaro Sperindeo claimed that the spectrum was consistent with <a href="/wiki/Hemoglobin" title="Hemoglobin">hemoglobin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> A later analysis, with similar conclusions, was carried out by a team in 1989.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources" title="Wikipedia:Reliable sources"><span title="The material near this tag may rely on an unreliable source. (October 2015)">unreliable source?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> However, the reliability of these observations has been questioned.<sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>  While clotted blood can be liquefied by mechanical stirring, the resulting suspension cannot solidify again.<sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Measurements made in 1900 and 1904 claimed that the ampoules' weight increased by up to 28&#160;grams during liquefaction. However, later measurements with a precision balance, performed over five years, failed to detect any variation.<sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Various suggestions for the content's composition have been advanced, such as a material that is <a href="/wiki/Photosensitive" class="mw-redirect" title="Photosensitive">photosensitive</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hygroscopic" class="mw-redirect" title="Hygroscopic">hygroscopic</a>, or has a low melting point.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>  However, these explanations run into technical difficulties, such as the variability of the phenomenon and its lack of correlation to ambient temperature.<sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A recent hypothesis by Garlaschelli &amp; al. is that the vial contains a <a href="/wiki/Thixotropy" title="Thixotropy">thixotropic</a> <a href="/wiki/Gel" title="Gel">gel</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-cicap_19-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cicap-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-csi_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-csi-36">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> In such a substance viscosity increases if left unstirred and decreases if stirred or moved.  Researchers have proposed specifically a suspension of <a href="/wiki/Iron(III)_oxide-hydroxide" title="Iron(III) oxide-hydroxide">hydrated iron oxide</a>, FeO(OH), which reproduces the color and behavior of the 'blood' in the ampoule.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>  The suspension can be prepared from simple chemicals that would have been easily available locally since antiquity.<sup id="cite_ref-jse_38-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jse-38">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources" title="Wikipedia:Reliable sources"><span title="The material near this tag may rely on an unreliable source. (February 2020)">unreliable source?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup><sup id="cite_ref-times_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-times-39">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2010, Giuseppe Geraci, a professor in the Department of Molecular Biology at Naples's <a href="/wiki/Universita_Federico_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Universita Federico II">Frederick II University</a>, conducted an experiment on a vial containing old blood—a relic dating back to the 18th century from the <i>Eremo di Camaldoli</i> near <a href="/wiki/Arezzo" title="Arezzo">Arezzo</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>—having the same characteristics of the blood of St. Januarius.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> Prof. Geraci showed that the Camaldoli relic also contains blood that can change its solid-liquid phase by shaking.<sup id="cite_ref-Piedimonte_41-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Piedimonte-41">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> He further reproduced the phenomenon with his own blood stored in the same conditions as the Camaldoli relic. He stated that, "There is no univocal scientific fact that explains why these changes take place. It is not enough to attribute to the movement the ability to dissolve the blood, the liquid contained in the Treasure case changes state for reasons still to be identified."
<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> He ultimately argued that "there's blood, no miracle".<sup id="cite_ref-Piedimonte_41-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Piedimonte-41">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Similar_rites">Similar rites</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Similar rites">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Although Naples became known as "City of Blood" (<i><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">urbs sanguinum</i></span></i>),<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> legends of blood liquefaction are not a unique phenomenon. Other examples include vials of the blood of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patricia" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Patricia">Saint Patricia</a> and Saint <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a> in the monastery of <a href="/wiki/San_Gregorio_Armeno" title="San Gregorio Armeno">San Gregorio Armeno</a>, and of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Pantaleon" title="Saint Pantaleon">Saint Pantaleon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ravello" title="Ravello">Ravello</a>. In all, the church has recognized claims of miraculous liquefying blood for seven<sup id="cite_ref-randi_43-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-randi-43">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> or about twenty<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> saints from <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a> and virtually nowhere else.<sup id="cite_ref-lanky_45-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanky-45">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> The blood cults of the other saints have been discontinued since the 16th century, which noted skeptic <a href="/wiki/James_Randi" title="James Randi">James Randi</a> takes as evidence that local artisans or <a href="/wiki/Alchemist" class="mw-redirect" title="Alchemist">alchemists</a> had a secret recipe for manufacturing this type of relic.<sup id="cite_ref-randi_43-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-randi-43">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> A team of three Italian chemists<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span title="The material near this tag possibly uses too-vague attribution or weasel words. (October 2015)">who?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> managed to create a liquid that reproduces all the characteristics and behavior of the liquid in the vial, using only local materials and techniques that were known to medieval workers.<sup id="cite_ref-randi_43-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-randi-43">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-nickell_46-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nickell-46">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> Jordan Lancaster leaves open the possibility that the practice was a Christian survival of a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Roman_religion" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Roman religion">pagan ritual</a> intended to protect the locals from unexpected eruptions from <a href="/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius" title="Mount Vesuvius">Mount Vesuvius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-lanky_45-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanky-45">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Museum_of_the_Treasure_of_St._Januarius">Museum of the Treasure of St. Januarius</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Januarius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Museum of the Treasure of St. Januarius">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Museum_of_the_Treasure_of_St_Januarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Museum of the Treasure of St Januarius">Museum of the Treasure of St Januarius</a></div>
<p>The Treasure of St Januarius is a collection of magnificent works and donations collected in seven centuries from popes, kings, emperors, famous and ordinary people. According to studies done by a pool of experts who have analyzed all the pieces in the collection, the Treasure of St Januarius is of higher value than the crown of <a href="/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Elizabeth II">Queen Elizabeth II</a> of the <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Tsar_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Tsar of Russia">Tsar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia" title="Tsardom of Russia">Russia</a>. The Treasure is a unique collection of art masterpieces, kept untouched thanks to the Deputation of the Chapel of St Januarius, an ancient secular institution founded in 1527 by a vote of the city of Naples, still existing.
</p><p>Today, the Treasure is exhibited in the <a href="/wiki/Museum_of_the_Treasure_of_St_Januarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Museum of the Treasure of St Januarius">Museum of the Treasure of St Januarius</a>, whose entrance is located on the right side of the Dome of Naples, under the arcades. By visiting the museum, the Chapel of San Gennaro is accessible even when the cathedral is closed.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
