<p><b>Saint Romedius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Romedio</i>). Though the son of a nobleman, as a young man he withdrew to a rock cave in order to meditate. After the death of his parents, he gave away all of his possessions and established himself in the <a href="/wiki/Val_di_Non" class="mw-redirect" title="Val di Non">Val di Non</a> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Nonstal</i>) in <a href="/wiki/Trentino" title="Trentino">Trentino</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romedius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Romedius was the son of a noble family, born in the area of a Roman military station, near <a href="/wiki/Innsbruck" title="Innsbruck">Innsbruck</a>, in what is now <a href="/w/index.php?title=Thaur,_Austria&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Thaur, Austria (page does not exist)">Thaur, Austria</a>. After a pilgrimage to Rome, Romedius gave all his possessions to the Church, withdrawing into a hermitage in some grottoes in the <a href="/wiki/Val_di_Non" class="mw-redirect" title="Val di Non">Val di Non</a>. he was accompanied by two companions, Abraham and David. Although the legend says that Romedius died c. 4th century, some think that he was born in the first decades of the 11th century.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, a chapel was already built on the site of his tomb as early as the 8th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romedius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Romedius is often depicted alongside or astride a <a href="/wiki/Bear" title="Bear">bear</a>. According to his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Verifiability" title="Wikipedia:Verifiability"><span title="The material near this tag failed verification of its source citation(s). (January 2023)">failed verification</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> he wanted to visit the friend of his youth, <a href="/wiki/St._Vigilius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Vigilius">St. Vigilius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trento" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trento">Bishop of Trento</a> (who died in 405), but his horse was torn to pieces by a wild bear. Romedius, however, had the bear bridled by his disciple David (Davide). The bear became docile and carried Romedius on its back to <a href="/wiki/Trento" title="Trento">Trento</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A similar tale is told of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Psalmodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Psalmodius">Saint Psalmodius</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and of <a href="/wiki/Lucanus_of_Sabiona" title="Lucanus of Sabiona">Lucanus of Sabiona</a>. The tamed bear is a motif also of <a href="/wiki/Corbinian" title="Corbinian">Saint Corbinian</a>, bishop of Freising.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romedius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:05SanRomedio.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/05SanRomedio.jpg/170px-05SanRomedio.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/05SanRomedio.jpg/255px-05SanRomedio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/05SanRomedio.jpg/340px-05SanRomedio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="450" data-file-height="600" /></a><figcaption>Sanctuary of San Romedio, <a href="/wiki/Sanzeno" title="Sanzeno">Sanzeno</a>, Italy</figcaption></figure>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_San_Romedio" title="Sanctuary of San Romedio">Sanctuary of San Romedio</a></div>
<p>Upon Romedius' death, his body was laid to rest in a small tomb above his cave in the mountains, a site that was soon visited by pilgrims. The <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_San_Romedio" title="Sanctuary of San Romedio">Sanctuary of San Romedio</a> grew from the little church that was built to venerate him to a popular pilgrimage shrine. The <i>Santuario di San Romedio</i> is across the lake from <a href="/wiki/Cles" title="Cles">Cles</a> at the head of the Val di Non, above the village of <a href="/wiki/Sanzeno" title="Sanzeno">Sanzeno</a>. The sanctuary where Romedio lived with his bear companion is a complex of several churches, from the Romanesque period to the 20th century beyond a gateway in the forested slopes. Votive offerings of crutches line the walls of the narrow stone stairwell up to the highest chapel, said to mark the site of the saint's retreat.  
</p><p>His local cult, which consolidated itself in the course of the 11th century, was officially recognized in the twelfth by the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trento" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trento">Bishop of Trento</a>. In 1795, permission was given to read masses in his name in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Brixen" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Brixen">diocese of Brixen</a>, which at that time included the Northern <a href="/wiki/County_of_Tyrol" title="County of Tyrol">Tyrol</a>. His cult remains popular in <a href="/wiki/Trentino" title="Trentino">Trentino</a>, <a href="/wiki/South_Tyrol" title="South Tyrol">South Tyrol</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Austrian_Tyrol" class="mw-redirect" title="Austrian Tyrol">Austrian Tyrol</a>.
</p>
<h2><span id="Romedius.27_Bear"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Romedius'_Bear">Romedius' Bear</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Romedius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Romedius&#039; Bear">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In remembrance of this legend, in 1958 Italian Senator G. G. Gallarati Scotti, honorary member of the committee for the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund" class="mw-redirect" title="World Wildlife Fund">World Wildlife Fund</a> in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, purchased Charlie, a bear intended to be killed, and donated it to the Sanctuary of San Romedio, in the Valle di Non.
</p><p>Today, the Province of Trentino protects the last <a href="/wiki/Brown_bear" title="Brown bear">brown bears</a> of the Alps in the Adamello-Brenta National Park, and, near the Sanctuary, takes care of young bears born in captivity in Trentino.  
</p><p>In the work known as <i><a href="/wiki/Illustrissimi" title="Illustrissimi">Illustrissimi</a></i>, a collection of letters written by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_I" title="Pope John Paul I">Pope John Paul I</a> when he was Patriarch of <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, Romedius' bear is one of the "recipients" of the letters.
</p>
