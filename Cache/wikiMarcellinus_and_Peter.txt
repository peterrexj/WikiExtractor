<p><b>Saints Marcellinus and Peter</b> (sometimes called <i>Petrus Exorcista</i> - Peter the <a href="/wiki/Exorcist" title="Exorcist">Exorcist</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1997_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1997-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Marcellino e Pietro</i>) are venerated within the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> as <a href="/wiki/Martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrs">martyrs</a> who were beheaded. Hagiographies place them in 4th century <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. They are generally represented as men in middle age, with <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsures</a> and palms of martyrdom; sometimes they hold a crown each.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_and_Peter&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known about the actual lives of these two men. Later hagiography suggests that Marcellinus, a priest, and Peter, an <a href="/wiki/Exorcist" title="Exorcist">exorcist</a>, died in the year 304, during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a> claimed that he heard the story of these two martyrs from their executioner who went on to become a Christian.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Damasus states that they were killed at an out-of-the-way spot by the magistrate Severus or Serenus,<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1997_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1997-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> so that other Christians would not have a chance to bury and venerate their bodies.  The two saints happily cleared the spot chosen for their death: a thicket overgrown with thorns, brambles, and briers three miles from Rome.  They were <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> and buried in that spot.
</p><p>Two women, Lucilla and Firmina, assisted by divine revelation, found the bodies, however, and had them properly buried.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  They buried their bodies near the body of <a href="/wiki/St._Tiburtius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Tiburtius">St. Tiburtius</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Labicana" title="Via Labicana">Via Labicana</a> in what became known as the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_Marcellinus_and_Peter" title="Catacombs of Marcellinus and Peter">Catacombs of Marcellinus and Peter</a>. <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a> writes that "it was thought at one time that forty-four other martyrs died with Marcellinus and Peter, but this is due to a misreading of the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Hieronymianum</a></i>."<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1997_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1997-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around the 6th century, a <i><a href="/wiki/Passio" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio">passio</a></i> connected the martyrdom of Marcellinus and Peter with that of the jailer Artemius, who was converted to Christianity by Marcellinus.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  Artemius' wife Secunda (or Candida) and daughter Paulina were also converted.  Artemius was beheaded; Secunda and Paulina were <a href="/wiki/Buried_alive" class="mw-redirect" title="Buried alive">buried alive</a> under a pile of stones.  The <i>passio</i> states that they were killed at the 12th <a href="/wiki/Roman_milestone" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman milestone">milestone</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Aurelia" title="Via Aurelia">Via Aurelia</a> in a place called <i>Silva Candida</i> ("Whitewood", also called <i>Silva Nigra</i>, "Blackwood", or at <a href="/wiki/Lorium" title="Lorium">Lorium</a>). Their executioner, Dorotheus, was said to have been converted by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_I" title="Pope Julius I">Pope Julius I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_and_Peter&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:ChristPeterPaul.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/ChristPeterPaul.jpg/220px-ChristPeterPaul.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="231" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/43/ChristPeterPaul.jpg/330px-ChristPeterPaul.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/43/ChristPeterPaul.jpg 2x" data-file-width="400" data-file-height="420" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:ChristPeterPaul.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Wall painting (4th century) from the catacomb of Marcellinus and Peter on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Labicana" title="Via Labicana">Via Labicana</a>, showing Christ between <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Paul</a>, and below them the martyrs <a href="/wiki/Gorgonius" title="Gorgonius">Gorgonius</a>, Peter, Marcellinus, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Tiburtius" title="Saint Tiburtius">Tiburtius</a></div></div></div>
<p>Pope Damasus, who opened their catacombs,<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1997_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1997-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> also remarks that he wrote a Latin <a href="/wiki/Epitaph" title="Epitaph">epitaph</a> with the details of their death with which he adorned their tomb.
</p><p>The martyrs were venerated by Christians in the centuries after their martyrdom. Their sepulcher is mentioned in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i>, which includes the information that Marcellinus was a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and that Peter was an <a href="/wiki/Exorcist" title="Exorcist">exorcist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  In the <i>Martyrologium</i>, their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is given as 2 June and their <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> is described as being located <i>ad duas lauros</i> ("at the two laurel trees") at the third mile of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Labicana" title="Via Labicana">Via Labicana</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  From the 7th century onwards, their sepulcher became a site of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>, and their feast day is recorded in local liturgies and hagiographies.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  According to the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i>, <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine the Great</a> built a basilica in their honor, since a structure built by Damasus had been destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The names of Sts. Marcellinus and Peter appeared in the <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite" title="Ambrosian Rite">Ambrosian</a> liturgy.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their names are mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Canon" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Canon">Roman Canon</a>. In 1253 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a> <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> their relics to an ancient church (its presence was first mentioned in 595 AD) near the present-day Via Merulana that was named after them: <a href="/wiki/Santi_Marcellino_e_Pietro_al_Laterano" title="Santi Marcellino e Pietro al Laterano">Santi Marcellino e Pietro</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG/170px-Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG/255px-Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG/340px-Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2304" data-file-height="3072" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sanctus_Petrus_Martyr_Germany_Seligenstadt_2007.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Peter at Seligenstadt</div></div></div>
<p>Relics associated with Marcellinus and Peter were translated to <a href="/wiki/Seligenstadt" title="Seligenstadt">Seligenstadt</a> in <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a> in the 9th century.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  They were sent by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IV" title="Pope Gregory IV">Pope Gregory IV</a> to <a href="/wiki/Einhard" title="Einhard">Einhard</a>, secretary to <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>. Einhard translated the relics to <a href="/wiki/Strasbourg" title="Strasbourg">Strasburg</a>, and then to <a href="/w/index.php?title=Michlenstad&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Michlenstad (page does not exist)">Michlenstad</a> (Michelstadt); and afterwards to Malinheim or Mulinheim (later called Seligenstadt).  In 829, Einhard built a church in honor of Marcellinus and Peter, and became its first abbot. <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_of_Gembloux" title="Sigebert of Gembloux">Sigebert</a>, <a href="/wiki/Aimoin" title="Aimoin">Aimoin</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Rabanus_Maurus" title="Rabanus Maurus">Rabanus Maurus</a> all mention this translation.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A slightly different account states that Einhard had built a basilica at Michelstadt in 827 and then sent a servant, Ratleic, to Rome with an end to find relics for the new building. Once in Rome, Ratleic, with the help of a Roman deacon with a reputation as a relics-swindler and thief<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> named Deusdona, robbed a <a href="/wiki/Catacomb" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb">catacomb</a> of the bones of Marcellinus and Peter and had them translated to Michelstadt. Once there, the relics made it known they were unhappy with their new tomb and thus had to be moved again to Mulinheim (now Seligenstadt). Once established there, they proved to be miracle workers.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Agostino Amore believes that the entire translation was fraudulent, due to Deusdona's reputation.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Cremona_Cathedral" title="Cremona Cathedral">Cremona Cathedral</a> in Italy also claims to have relics, inside a sarcophagus in one of its <a href="/wiki/Transepts" class="mw-redirect" title="Transepts">transepts</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1997_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1997-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marcellinus_and_Peter&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>They are generally represented as men in middle age, with <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsures</a> and palms of martyrdom;<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1997_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1997-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> sometimes they hold a crown each.  In the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs" title="Catacombs">catacombs</a> named after them, a <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a> dating from the 4th or 5th centuries, represents them without <a href="/wiki/Aureola" title="Aureola">aureolae</a>, with short beards, next to the <a href="/wiki/Lamb_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Lamb of Christ">Lamb of Christ</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  In another fresco from the 5th or 6th centuries, in the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_Pontian" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacombs of Pontian">catacombs of Pontian</a>, they are beardless and depicted alongside <a href="/wiki/Saint_Pollio" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Pollio">Saint Pollio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a church dedicated to them at <a href="/wiki/Imbersago" title="Imbersago">Imbersago</a>.
</p>
