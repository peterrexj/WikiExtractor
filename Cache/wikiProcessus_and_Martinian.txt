<p><b>Martinian</b> and <b>Processus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Martiniano</i> and <i>Processo</i>) were <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">ancient Rome</a>.
Neither the years they lived nor the circumstances of their deaths are known. They are currently buried in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> in Rome.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Burial">Burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martinian_and_Processus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>All that is known about Martinian and Processus is that they were originally buried in an <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Age" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Age">apostolic era</a> cemetery along the <a href="/wiki/Via_Aurelia" title="Via Aurelia">Via Aurelia</a> on 2 July.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a><sup id="cite_ref-Martyrologium_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martyrologium-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> state they were buried in some unknown year in the Cemetery of Damasus at the road's second milestone. The <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i>  gives their names under July 2.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Berne manuscript of the <i>Martyrology</i> also states that their <a href="/wiki/Grave_(burial)" class="mw-redirect" title="Grave (burial)">burial-place</a> was at the second <a href="/wiki/Milestone" title="Milestone">milestone</a> of the Via Aurelia, or at the catacombs of St. Agatha on the Via Aurelia. The old catalogues of the burial places of the Roman martyrs likewise mention the graves of both saints on this road.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martinian_and_Processus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Martinian and Processus were <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_Guard" title="Praetorian Guard">Praetorian Guard</a>  soldiers assigned as the <a href="/wiki/Prison_officer" title="Prison officer">warders</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">Saint Paul</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Mamertine_Prison" title="Mamertine Prison">Mamertine Prison</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The apostles converted their jailers after a spring flowed miraculously in the prison. Peter then baptized them in the miraculous waters. By order of the emperor <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>, the guards were then arrested, tortured, and beheaded. After their martyrdom with Paul, a sympathizer called <a href="/wiki/Pomponia_Graecina" title="Pomponia Graecina">Lucina</a> buried them in her own cemetery.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martinian_and_Processus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Martinian and Processus were publicly venerated in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> from the fourth or perhaps the third century. In the fourth century, a <a href="/wiki/Church_(building)" title="Church (building)">church</a> was built over their tomb. At this church, <a href="/wiki/St._Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Gregory the Great">Saint Gregory the Great</a> preached a <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homily</a> on their feast day <i>"in which he referred to the presence of their bodies, to the cures of the sick, to the harassment of perjurers, and the cure of demoniacs there."</i><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> This church no longer exists. <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> mentions Martinian and Processus, and their feast is thus known to have been celebrated in early medieval <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_I" title="Pope Paschal I">Pope Paschal I</a> (817–824) <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> the bones of the two martyrs to a chapel in the old <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Peter">Basilica of St. Peter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-aleteia-burger_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aleteia-burger-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> They still rest under the altar dedicated to them in the right (south) transept of the present <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>. In 1605, their relics were placed in a <a href="/wiki/Porphyry_(geology)" title="Porphyry (geology)">porphyry</a> <a href="/wiki/Urn" title="Urn">urn</a> under the <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> at St. Peter's, which is flanked by two antique yellow <a href="/wiki/Columns" class="mw-redirect" title="Columns">columns</a>. The hemisphere has three <a href="/wiki/Roundel" title="Roundel">roundels</a> with scenes from the life of <a href="/wiki/St._Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Paul">Paul the Apostle</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-aleteia-burger_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aleteia-burger-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> added a feast of the <a href="/wiki/First_Martyrs_of_the_Church_of_Rome" title="First Martyrs of the Church of Rome">First Martyrs of the Church of Rome</a> on June 30, immediately after that of <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">Saints Peter and Paul</a>. It also removed the individual feast of these and other early martyrs from the general calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a>. Nonetheless, Martinian and Processus remain in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, the official list of saints recognized by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Martyrologium_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martyrologium-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Thus, they may still be celebrated with their own Mass on their traditional feast day, 2 July, according to the rules in the present <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> unless in some locality an obligatory celebration is assigned to that day.  Pre-1969 calendars grant these saints only a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(liturgy)" title="Commemoration (liturgy)">Commemoration</a> within the Mass of the <a href="/wiki/Visitation_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Visitation (Christian)">Visitation of Our Lady</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
