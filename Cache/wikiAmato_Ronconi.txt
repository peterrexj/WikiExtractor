<p><b>Amato Ronconi</b> (1226 – 8 May 1292) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> who became a professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Secular_Franciscan_Order" title="Secular Franciscan Order">Secular Franciscan Order</a>. Ronconi lived a life of penitence and dedicated all his works to the poor through the construction of chapels and hospitals – one such hospital still exists in his home of <a href="/wiki/Rimini" title="Rimini">Rimini</a>. His good deeds never went unnoticed for the townspeople hailed Ronconi as a saint in their midst even in the face of a slanderous accusation a jealous sister-in-law spread.<sup id="cite_ref-RCS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RCS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The confirmation of his popular local "cultus" – or enduring veneration – allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> to celebrate his beatification on 17 April 1776 while <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> canonized Ronconi centuries later on 23 November 2014 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amato_Ronconi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Amato Ronconi was born in 1225 in <a href="/wiki/Saludecio" title="Saludecio">Saludecio</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Rimini" title="Province of Rimini">Province of Rimini</a> to a noble and wealthy household. He was orphaned in childhood and cared for by his older brother Giacomo and he spent his childhood and adolescence growing up in his brother's home.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As the younger son of a landowner, he had no fortune, so he worked as a farmhand. He had at least one sister named Chiara.
</p><p>Ronconi's sister-in-law urged him to find a woman to wed but Ronconi had decided to serve the Lord as a hermit. His sister-in-law Lansberga came to dislike him as a result of this for she wanted him to wed her little sister so as to keep her estate intact among familial relations and not amongst outsiders.<sup id="cite_ref-RCS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RCS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She first asked Giacomo to have Ronconi change his mind but to keep the peace her husband gave his brother a house with a bit of land as part of his inheritance. He lived there with his sister, working the land. It was located on the road from Rimini to Rome. Ronconi turned it into a hospice for pilgrims which further infuriated his sister-in-law. Lansberga wanted revenge and so began a slanderous rumor – knowing how close he was with his sister Chiara – that both brother and sister were engaging in incestuous relations which was proven false after an investigation.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg/220px-Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="391" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg/330px-Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg/440px-Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2988" data-file-height="5312" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santuario_di_Santo_Amato_Ronconi.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Santuario di Santo Amato Ronconi</div></div></div>
<p>Feeling a call to serve <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> he devoted his life to catering to the needs of the poor and assisting pilgrims and in doing so gave up all his possessions which he passed on to the poor people of the area. He worked the fields in order to support the hospice and the poor. He joined the <a href="/wiki/Secular_Franciscan_Order" title="Secular Franciscan Order">Secular Franciscan Order</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and adopted a life of a hermit. He later emerged from his hermitage and constructed various chapels and shelters for pilgrims; some of those places still exist at present including a hospital in Rimini.<sup id="cite_ref-congregation_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-congregation-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The hospice is now the <i>Casa di Riposo Pia Beato Amato Ronconi</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Ronconi made a pilgrimage to Rimini to the tomb of <a href="/wiki/Gaudentius_of_Rimini" title="Gaudentius of Rimini">Gaudentius of Rimini</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Monte_Titano" title="Monte Titano">Monte Titano</a> to the cave of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Marinus" title="Saint Marinus">Marinus</a>, and four times to <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> to visit the tomb of <a href="/wiki/James,_son_of_Zebedee" class="mw-redirect" title="James, son of Zebedee">James the Apostle</a> at <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He attempted a fifth pilgrimage to Santiago de Compostela but never made it for an angel reportedly appeared to him and told him he would soon die so had to return to his home; he did so and stopped at the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of San Giuliano in Rimini where he gave them the remainder of his possessions on 10 January 1292.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ronconi died in 1292. His remains are in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Biagio,_Saludecio" title="San Biagio, Saludecio">San Biagio</a> in his home town of Saledecio after being transferred in May 1330 after his original resting place was razed due to fire.<sup id="cite_ref-RCS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RCS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amato_Ronconi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> beatified Ronconi by the confirmation of his cult on 17 April 1776.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1997, the parish of San Biagio in Saludécio and La Pia Unione del Beato Amato revived the process of canonization of Amatus. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> canonized Ronconi on 23 November 2014 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-blesseds6_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blesseds6-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
