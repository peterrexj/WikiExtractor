<p><b>Beatrice of Silva</b> (<a href="/wiki/Campo_Maior,_Portugal" title="Campo Maior, Portugal">Campo Maior, Portugal</a> ca. 1424 – <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Castile" title="Crown of Castile">Castile</a>, 16 August 1492), born <b>Beatriz de Menezes da Silva</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_people" title="Portuguese people">Portuguese</a> <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">noblewoman</a> who became the foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism#Roman_Catholicism" title="Christian monasticism">monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Conceptionists" title="Conceptionists">Order of the Immaculate Conception</a>. <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_of_Portugal" title="Amadeus of Portugal">Amadeus of Portugal</a>'s younger sister, she is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Beatrice_of_Silva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Beatrice was one of the eleven children of <a href="https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_Gomes_da_Silva,_1.%C2%BA_alcaide_de_Campo_Maior" class="extiw" title="pt:Rui Gomes da Silva, 1.º alcaide de Campo Maior">Rui Gomes da Silva</a>, the governor of <a href="/wiki/Campo_Maior,_Portugal" title="Campo Maior, Portugal">Campo Maior, Portugal</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and of Isabel de Menezes, an illegitimate daughter of <a href="/wiki/Dom_(title)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dom (title)">Dom</a> <a href="/wiki/Pedro_de_Menezes,_1st_Count_of_Vila_Real" class="mw-redirect" title="Pedro de Menezes, 1st Count of Vila Real">Pedro de Menezes, 1st Count of Vila Real</a> and 2nd <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Viana_do_Alentejo" title="Count of Viana do Alentejo">Count of Viana do Alentejo</a>, in whose army her father was serving at the time of her birth. One of her brothers was <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_of_Portugal" title="Amadeus of Portugal">Amadeus of Portugal</a>, a noted reformer of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>. She was long thought to have been born in the Portuguese enclave of <a href="/wiki/Ceuta" title="Ceuta">Ceuta</a> in <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>, where her father was serving as a military commander at that time. Modern research has determined that she was, in fact, born in the family home at Campo Maior.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:162px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg/160px-Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="239" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg/240px-Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg/320px-Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg 2x" data-file-width="397" data-file-height="593" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_a_Imaculada_Concei%C3%A7%C3%A3o.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Beatrice of Silva experienced an <a href="/wiki/Marian_apparition" title="Marian apparition">apparition of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a></div></div></div>
<p>Beatrice was raised in the castle of <a href="/wiki/Infante" title="Infante">Infante</a> <a href="/wiki/John,_Lord_of_Reguengos_de_Monsaraz" class="mw-redirect" title="John, Lord of Reguengos de Monsaraz">John, Lord of Reguengos de Monsaraz</a>. In 1447 Beatrice accompanied his daughter, Princess <a href="/wiki/Isabella_of_Portugal,_Queen_of_Castile" title="Isabella of Portugal, Queen of Castile">Isabel of Portugal</a>, to Castile as her <a href="/wiki/Lady-in-waiting" title="Lady-in-waiting">lady-in-waiting</a> when Isabel left to marry King <a href="/wiki/John_II_of_Castile" title="John II of Castile">John II of Castile</a> and became <a href="/wiki/List_of_Castilian_monarchs" title="List of Castilian monarchs">Queen of Castile and León</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Beatrice was her good and close friend, (and later was to receive her support when she founded the <a href="/wiki/Conceptionists" title="Conceptionists">Conceptionists</a>). Soon, however, her great beauty began to arouse the irrational jealousy of the Queen, who had her imprisoned in a tiny cell. During this incarceration, Beatrice experienced an <a href="/wiki/Marian_apparition" title="Marian apparition">apparition of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, in which she was instructed to found a new <a href="/wiki/Religious_order_(Catholic)" title="Religious order (Catholic)">religious order</a> in Marys's honor.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:162px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg/160px-Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="240" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg/240px-Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg/320px-Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg 2x" data-file-width="677" data-file-height="1016" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Santa_Beatriz_da_Silva_e_as_Vener%C3%A1veis_Concepcionistas.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Beatrice was the foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Conceptionists" title="Conceptionists">Order of the Immaculate Conception</a></div></div></div>
<p>Beatrice finally escaped her imprisonment with difficulty and took refuge in the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_order_(religious)" title="Second order (religious)">Second Order</a> monastery of <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> in Toledo. Here she led a life of holiness for thirty-seven years, without becoming a member of that order.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1484 Beatrice, with some companions, took possession of a palace in Toledo set apart for them by Queen <a href="/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile" title="Isabella I of Castile">Isabella I of Castile</a> for the new community under the name Monastery of the Holy Faith, which was to be dedicated to honoring the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a> of Mary.
</p><p>In 1489, by permission of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_VIII" title="Pope Innocent VIII">Pope Innocent VIII</a>, the nuns adopted the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastic_rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic rule">Rule</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> bound themselves to the daily recitation of the <a href="/wiki/Canonical_hours" title="Canonical hours">office</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>, and were placed under obedience to the <a href="/wiki/Ordinary_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordinary (Catholic Church)">ordinary</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese">archdiocese</a>. The foundress determined on the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a>, which is white, with a white <a href="/wiki/Scapular" title="Scapular">scapular</a> and blue <a href="/wiki/Mantle_(clothing)" title="Mantle (clothing)">mantle</a>, with a medallion of Mary under her title of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Beatrice died in the monastery she had founded on 16 August 1492.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Her remains are still venerated in the chapel of that monastery.
</p><p>Beatrice da Silva is the patron saint of prisoners.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Beatrice_of_Silva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:162px;"><a href="/wiki/File:TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg/160px-TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg" decoding="async" width="160" height="230" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg/240px-TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg/320px-TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1207" data-file-height="1734" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:TumbaBeatrizToledo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb of Saint Beatrice da Silva in <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo, Spain</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1501 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Pope Alexander VI</a> united the nuns of the Monastery of the Holy Faith, which Beatrice had founded, with the neighboring <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictine</a> Monastery of San Pedro de las Duenas, and put them all under the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Clare">Rule of St. Clare</a>. Through this, the order became connected with the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_II" title="Pope Julius II">Pope Julius II</a> gave the new order a <a href="/wiki/Monastic_rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic rule">rule of life</a> of its own in 1511, and in 1516 special <a href="/wiki/Constitutions" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitutions">constitutions</a> were drawn up for the new order by the Franciscan Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Francisco_de_Qui%C3%B1ones" title="Francisco de Quiñones">Francisco de Quiñones</a>, who resolved some ongoing tensions between the nuns of Santa Fe and the former Benedictine nuns who had been fused into the order, establishing the community as the Monastery of the Immaculate Conception.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A second monastery was founded in 1507 at Torrigo, from which, in turn, were established seven others. The order soon spread through <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, and their colonies in <a href="/wiki/South_America" title="South America">South America</a>—as early as 1540, as well as in Italy, and France.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> At its height, there were some 200 monasteries of the order throughout the world.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Beatrice_of_Silva&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Beatrice de Menezes da Silva was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1926 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> and later <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> in 1976.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated by both by the Conceptionist nuns and the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan Order</a> and in Spain on 1 September, but in 2012 was transferred to 17 August for Portugal.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
