<p><b>Francis Borgia</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">SJ</a></span> (<a href="/wiki/Valencian_language" title="Valencian language">Valencian</a>: <i lang="ca-valencia">Francesc de Borja</i>; <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Francisco de Borja</i>; 28 October 1510 – 30 September 1572) was a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> priest. The great-grandson of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Pope Alexander&#160;VI</a>, he was <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Gand%C3%ADa" title="Duke of Gandía">Duke of Gandía</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Grandee_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Grandee of Spain">grandee of Spain</a>. After the death of his wife, Borgia renounced his titles and became a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Priesthood in the Catholic Church">priest</a> in the Society of Jesus, later serving as its third <a href="/wiki/Superior_General_of_the_Society_of_Jesus" title="Superior General of the Society of Jesus">superior general</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 20 June 1670 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Borgia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in the Duchy of Gandía in <a href="/wiki/Valencian_Community" title="Valencian Community">the Kingdom of Valencia</a> (part of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon" title="Kingdom of Aragon">Aragon</a>), on 28 October 1510. His father was Juan Borgia, 3rd <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Gand%C3%ADa" title="Duke of Gandía">Duke of Gandía</a>, the son of <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Borgia,_2nd_Duke_of_Gand%C3%ADa" title="Giovanni Borgia, 2nd Duke of Gandía">Giovanni Borgia</a>, the son of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Pope Alexander VI</a> (Rodrigo Borgia). His mother was Juana, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Alonso_de_Arag%C3%B3n" title="Alonso de Aragón">Alonso de Aragón</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Zaragoza" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Zaragoza">Archbishop of Zaragoza</a>, who, in turn, was the illegitimate son of King <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_II_of_Aragon" title="Ferdinand II of Aragon">Ferdinand II of Aragon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His brother, <a href="/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_de_Borja_y_Castro" title="Tomás de Borja y Castro">Tomás de Borja y Castro</a>, also entered the Church, becoming <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_M%C3%A1laga" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Málaga">Bishop of Málaga</a>, and later Archbishop of Zaragoza.
</p><p>As a child he was very pious and wished to become a monk, but his family sent him instead to serve in the court of <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor</a> (who was also <a href="/wiki/King_Charles_I_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="King Charles I of Spain">King Charles I of Spain</a>), where he was welcomed as a kinsman.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He excelled there, accompanying the Emperor on several campaigns.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Adult_life_and_career">Adult life and career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Borgia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Adult life and career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> in the month of September 1529, he married a Portuguese noblewoman, <a href="/wiki/Leonor_de_Castro_Mello_y_Meneses" title="Leonor de Castro Mello y Meneses">Leonor de Castro Mello y Meneses</a>. They had eight children: Carlos in 1530, Isabel in 1532, Juan in 1533, Álvaro circa 1535, Juana also circa 1535, Fernando in 1537, Dorotea in 1538, and Alfonso in 1539. Charles V appointed him Marquess of Lombay, master of the hounds, and equerry to the empress.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1539, he convoyed the corpse of <a href="/wiki/Isabella_of_Portugal_(1503-1539)" class="mw-redirect" title="Isabella of Portugal (1503-1539)">Isabella of Portugal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a>'s mother, to her burial place in <a href="/wiki/Granada" title="Granada">Granada</a>. In that same year (1539), he became <a href="/wiki/Viceroy_of_Catalonia" class="mw-redirect" title="Viceroy of Catalonia">Viceroy of Catalonia</a>, replacing <a href="/wiki/Fadrique_de_Portugal_y_Noro%C3%B1a" class="mw-redirect" title="Fadrique de Portugal y Noroña">Fadrique de Portugal y Noroña</a>, though he was only 29.
</p><p>In these earlier years of his life he enjoyed composing musical ecclesiastical pieces.  In these pieces he displayed a remarkable contrapuntal style, justifying the assertion that, "In the sixteenth century and prior to Palestrina, Borgia was one of the chief restorers of sacred music."<sup id="cite_ref-suau_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1543 upon the death of his father, the 3rd Duke, Francis became the 4th <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Gand%C3%ADa" title="Duke of Gandía">Duke of Gandía</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His diplomatic abilities came into question after his failed attempt at arranging a marriage between Prince Philip of Spain and the Princess of Portugal, thus ending a hope of bringing these two countries together, and resulting in his retirement as duke, handing his title to his son, Carlos.<sup id="cite_ref-Wisconsin_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wisconsin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> By then 33 years old, he had retired to his native place and devoted himself to religious activities.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Jesuit_priest">Jesuit priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Borgia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Jesuit priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:228px;"><a href="/wiki/File:L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg/226px-L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg" decoding="async" width="226" height="333" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg/339px-L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg/452px-L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2329" data-file-height="3432" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:L%27Illumination_de_Saint_Fran%C3%A7ois_Borgia-Pietro_Muttoni_della_Vecchia_mg_8227.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Francis Borgia at Isabella of Portugal's coffin</i> by <a href="/wiki/Pietro_della_Vecchia" title="Pietro della Vecchia">Pietro della Vecchia</a></div></div></div>
<p>In 1546 his wife Eleanor died, and Francis then decided to enter the newly formed <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, after making adequate provisions for his children. He put his affairs in order circa 1551, renounced his titles in favour of his eldest son Carlos de Borja-Aragon y de Castro-Melo, and became a Jesuit priest. He helped in the establishment of what is now the Gregorian University in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Upon Francis’ return from a journey to Peru, Pope Julius III made known his intention to make him a cardinal.<sup id="cite_ref-Wisconsin_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wisconsin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> To prevent this, Borgia decided, in agreement with St. <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius</a>, to leave the city secretly and go to the <a href="/wiki/Basque_Country_(greater_region)" title="Basque Country (greater region)">Basque Country</a>, where it was thought he would be safe from the papal desires.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He felt incompletion  to spend time in seclusion and prayer, but his administrative talents also made him a natural for other tasks. In time his friends persuaded him to accept the leadership role that nature and circumstances had destined him for: in 1554, he became the Jesuit commissary-general in Spain,<sup id="cite_ref-chi_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chi-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> where he founded a dozen colleges.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After only two years, St. Francis was crowned for missions in the East and West Indies.<sup id="cite_ref-Wisconsin_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wisconsin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1565, he was elected the third "Father General" or <a href="/wiki/Superior_General_of_the_Society_of_Jesus" title="Superior General of the Society of Jesus">Superior General of the Society of Jesus</a>, after the death in January 1565 of <a href="/wiki/Diego_Laynez" title="Diego Laynez">Diego Laynez</a>, (<a href="/wiki/Almaz%C3%A1n" title="Almazán">Almazán</a>, Spain, 1512 – January 1565).
</p><p>His successes during the period 1565–1572 made historians to describe Francis as the greatest General after <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Saint Ignatius</a>. He founded the <i>Collegium Romanum</i>, which was to become the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian University">Gregorian University</a>, advised kings and popes, and closely supervised all the affairs of the mushrooming order. Yet, despite being the supreme, Francis led a humble life and was acclaimed in his own lifetime as a saint.
</p><p>Francis Borgia died in 1572.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Francis_Borgia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg/220px-St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg/330px-St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg/440px-St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg 2x" data-file-width="721" data-file-height="1016" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Francis_Borgia_Helping_a_Dying_Impenitent_by_Goya.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Francis Borgia Helping a Dying Impenitent</i>, painted by <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Goya" title="Francisco Goya">Francisco Goya</a></div></div></div>
<p>Francis Borgia died on 30 September 1572, in Rome. His mortal remains were repatriated to Spain in 1617 and kept from 1627 at the Jesuit professed house in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> that was newly built for that purpose north of <a href="/wiki/Plaza_Mayor,_Madrid" title="Plaza Mayor, Madrid">Plaza Mayor</a> on a donation from <a href="/wiki/Francisco_G%C3%B3mez_de_Sandoval_y_Rojas,_1st_Duke_of_Lerma" class="mw-redirect" title="Francisco Gómez de Sandoval y Rojas, 1st Duke of Lerma">Francisco Gómez de Sandoval y Rojas, 1st Duke of Lerma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> On 30 July 1901, the silver urn contained the relics was transferred to the church of the Sacred Church and San Francis Borgia on calle de la Flor Baja, part of a new Jesuit residence (later professed house from 1911) established thanks to a donation from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Manuel_%C3%81lvarez_de_Toledo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Manuel Álvarez de Toledo (page does not exist)">Manuel Álvarez de Toledo</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_%C3%81lvarez_de_Toledo" class="extiw" title="es:Manuel Álvarez de Toledo">es</a>&#93;</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> After that church was destroyed by arson in 1931, some of his ashes were recovered and eventually reinterred in the new Jesuit complex on <a href="/wiki/Calle_de_Serrano" title="Calle de Serrano">calle de Serrano</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Francis Borgia was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> on 23 November 1624, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> nearly 35 years later on 20 June 1670,<sup id="cite_ref-chi_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chi-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_X" title="Pope Clement X">Pope Clement X</a>. His liturgical feast was inserted into the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1688 for celebration on 10 October,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> the date then free from other celebrations that was closest to that of his death.
</p><p>Parishes are dedicated to St. Francis Borgia in Chicago, Illinois,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sturgis,_Kentucky" title="Sturgis, Kentucky">Sturgis, Kentucky</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Washington,_Missouri" title="Washington, Missouri">Washington, Missouri</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Blair,_Nebraska" title="Blair, Nebraska">Blair, Nebraska</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Cedarburg,_Wisconsin" title="Cedarburg, Wisconsin">Cedarburg, Wisconsin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Also in the village of <a href="/wiki/Isio" class="mw-redirect" title="Isio">Isio</a> in the town of <a href="/wiki/Cauayan,_Negros_Occidental" title="Cauayan, Negros Occidental">Cauayan, Negros Occidental</a>, Philippines. The Jesuit-founded city of <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Borja" title="São Borja">São Borja</a>, in southern <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>, is named after him.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Francis_Borgia_Regional_High_School" title="St. Francis Borgia Regional High School">St. Francis Borgia Regional High School</a> is located in <a href="/wiki/Washington,_Missouri" title="Washington, Missouri">Washington, Missouri</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
