<p><b>Maria Cristina of the Immaculate Conception Brando</b> (1 May 1856 – 20 January 1906), born Adelaida Brando,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and the founder of the Congregation of the Sisters, Expiatory Victims of Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament, an international teaching institute. She was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 April 2003, and canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> on 17 May 2015.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Cristina_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_Brando&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adelaida Brando was born on 1 May 1856<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, Italy, the youngest of four sisters, to Christian and wealthy parents Giovanni Giuseppe and Maria Concetta Marrazzo, who died several days after Brando's birth.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-villa_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villa-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Brando received a "fruiful and sound"<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> religious education and showed inclinations towards the religious life, holiness, prayer, and <a href="/wiki/Celibacy" title="Celibacy">celibacy</a> early in her life.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As a child, she would say, "I must be a saint, I want to be a saint".<sup id="cite_ref-villa_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villa-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When she was about 12 years old, she professed a vow of perpetual chastity before an image of the Christ child. She tried to enter the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Sacramentines" title="Sacramentines">Sacramentine</a> nuns in Naples, but her father prevented her. He gave his permission for her to join the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clare</a> nuns at their convent in <a href="/wiki/Fiorentino" title="Fiorentino">Fiorentine</a>, where her oldest sister was a nun, but Brando became ill and had to return to her family for medical care.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-villa_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villa-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After she recovered, she was able to enter the Sacramentine's convent; in 1876, she became a nun, taking on the name Maria Cristina of the Immaculate Conception. She became ill again and had to return home.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1878, Brando founded the Congregation of the Sisters, Expiatory Victims of Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament while renting a room with the <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Teresiane</a> Sisters of <a href="/wiki/Torre_del_Greco" title="Torre del Greco">Torre del Greco</a>. The new congregation grew quickly, despite economic limitations and Brando's health difficulties. The community settled in <a href="/wiki/Casoria" title="Casoria">Casoria</a>, three miles northeast of Naples, grew with new houses and members, and "demonstrated great devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Eucharist in the Catholic Church">Eucharist</a> and diligent care for the education of young boys and girls".<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The congregation founded collegiate schools for girls, orphanages, and boarding and day schools. On 20 July 1903, the congregation received approval from the pope, with Brando as their <a href="/wiki/Superior_General_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General (Christianity)">superior general</a>, and on 2 November, Brando and many other nuns professed perpetual vows. According to her biography published on the <i><a href="/wiki/Vatican_News" title="Vatican News">Vatican News</a></i> website, "She lived her consecration with generosity, with perseverance and with spiritual joy" and "held the office of superior general with humility, prudence and amiability, giving her sisters continual examples of fidelity to God and to one's vocation and of zeal for the growth of the kingdom of God".<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brando built a cell near the church of the convent where she lived,  which she called the <i>grotticella</i> ("the little <a href="/wiki/Grotto" title="Grotto">grotto</a>"), in order to "be nearer in spirit and in body to the tabernacle".<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She slept there in a chair every night, in order to "accompany Jesus in the Eucharist, while awake and while resting".<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She celebrated the <a href="/wiki/Sacraments_of_the_Catholic_Church" title="Sacraments of the Catholic Church">sacraments</a> regularly and practiced devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Incarnation_(Christianity)" title="Incarnation (Christianity)">Incarnation</a>, to the <a href="/wiki/Passion_of_Jesus" title="Passion of Jesus">Passion and death of Christ</a>, and to the Eucharist.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Brando died on 20 January 1906.<sup id="cite_ref-vatican_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vatican-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Cristina_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_Brando&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brando was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 April 2003, along with five others: <a href="/wiki/James_Alberione" title="James Alberione">James Alberione</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Priesthood in the Catholic Church">priest</a> and founder of the <a href="/wiki/Pauline_Family" title="Pauline Family">Pauline Family</a>; <a href="/wiki/Marco_d%27Aviano" title="Marco d&#39;Aviano">Marco d'Aviano</a>, a priest and member of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</a>; <a href="/wiki/Eugenia_Ravasio" title="Eugenia Ravasio">Eugenia Ravasco</a>, <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virgin</a> and founder of the Congregations of the Daughters of the Sacred Hearts of Jesus and Mary; <a href="/wiki/Maria_Domenica_Mantovani" title="Maria Domenica Mantovani">Maria Domenica Mantovani</a>, virgin and co-founder of the Institute of the Little Sisters of the Holy Family; and <a href="/wiki/Giulia_Salzano" title="Giulia Salzano">Giulia Salzano</a>, virgin and founder of the Congregation of the Catechist Sisters of the Sacred Heart.<sup id="cite_ref-johnpaulII_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-johnpaulII-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The pope stated about Brando during his homily for the beatification ceremony: "All that God worked through Maria Christina Brando is astonishing. Her Eucharistic and expiatory spirituality is expressed in two lines, like 'two branches that stem from the same trunk': love of God and love of neighbour. Her desire to take part in Christ's passion, as it were, 'overflowed' into educational works, for the purpose of making people aware of their dignity and open to the Lord's merciful love".<sup id="cite_ref-johnpaulII_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-johnpaulII-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> on 17 May 2015, along with three other 19th century nuns and educators: <a href="/wiki/Marie-Alphonsine_Danil_Ghattas" title="Marie-Alphonsine Danil Ghattas">Marie-Alphonsine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mariam_Baouardy" title="Mariam Baouardy">Mary of Jesus Crucified</a> of Palestine, and <a href="/wiki/%C3%89milie_de_Villeneuve" title="Émilie de Villeneuve">Émilie de Villeneuve</a> from France.<sup id="cite_ref-homily_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-homily-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> During the ceremony, Francis said this about Brando: "She was completely given over to ardent love for the Lord. From prayer and her intimate encounter with the risen Jesus present in the Eucharist, she received strength to endure suffering and to give herself, as bread which is broken, to many people who had wandered far from God and yet hungered for authentic love".<sup id="cite_ref-homily_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-homily-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
