<p><b>Stephen Harding</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Étienne Harding</i>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1060&#160;&#8211;&#32;28 March 1134) was an English-born <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, who was one of the founders of the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian Order">Cistercian Order</a>. He is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_Harding&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There is little archival evidence, yet all sources agree that Stephen was English and spent some time at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Sherborne_Abbey" title="Sherborne Abbey">Sherborne</a> as a young man. The main source about his youth, <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>, states that Stephen then went to France and became friends with another Englishman, called Peter. The two made a pilgrimage to Rome, took new religious names and then went to <a href="/wiki/Molesme_Abbey" title="Molesme Abbey">Molesme Abbey</a> upon returning to <a href="/wiki/Burgundy" title="Burgundy">Burgundy</a>, around 1085.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On their return from Rome, they also experienced the monastic traditions of the <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">Camaldolese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vallombrosians" title="Vallombrosians">Vallombrosians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1098, Stephen went to the new monastery (soon to be known as <a href="/wiki/C%C3%AEteaux_Abbey" title="Cîteaux Abbey">Cîteaux</a>) together with <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Molesme" title="Robert of Molesme">Robert</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alberic_of_C%C3%AEteaux" title="Alberic of Cîteaux">Alberic</a>, was elected abbot in 1108, and died in 1134.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg/220px-Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="181" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg/330px-Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg/440px-Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3484" data-file-height="2872" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sarlat_Kathedrale_-_Fenster_1a_Bernhard.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bernard and Stephen</div></div></div>
<p>Stephen was the third abbot of Cîteaux. Under his administration, very few novices were joining the community and the monks were suffering from hunger and sickness. In 1112, <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> entered the community, bringing with him thirty companions.
</p><p>Between 1112 and 1119, a dozen new Cistercian houses were founded to accommodate those joining the young order. Harding's organizational skills were exceptional; he instituted the system of <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">general chapters</a> and regular visitations. In 1119, he received official approbation for the <i><a href="/wiki/Carta_Caritatis" title="Carta Caritatis">Carta Caritatis</a></i> (Charter of Charity), an important document for the Cistercian Order, establishing its unifying principles.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Many of his policies and decisions were influenced from his time with the Vallombrosians.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stephen Harding served as abbot of Cîteaux for twenty-five years. While no single person is considered the founder of the Cistercian Order, the shape of Cistercian thought and its rapid growth in the 12th century were certainly due in some part to Harding's leadership.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was the abbot who accepted Bernard of Clairvaux. Insisting on simplicity in all aspects of monastic life, Stephen encouraged the severity of Cistercian architecture and the simple beauty of the Order's liturgy and <a href="/wiki/Cistercian_Hymnal" title="Cistercian Hymnal">music</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ss_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ss-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was an accomplished scribe; his highest achievement<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> is considered to be the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Harding_Bible&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Harding Bible (page does not exist)">Harding Bible</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1133, he resigned as abbot because of poor eyesight.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He died on 28 March 1134.<sup id="cite_ref-huddleston_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huddleston-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Veneration for Stephen began in the modern era. His feast was celebrated on 28 March until 1683 and then moved to 17 April, where it remained until the liturgical reforms following the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, when it was moved back to 28 March.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In a joint commemoration with <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Molesme" title="Robert of Molesme">Robert of Molesme</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alberic_of_C%C3%AEteaux" title="Alberic of Cîteaux">Alberic</a>, the first two abbots of Cîteaux, the Cistercians and <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictines</a> today celebrate Stephen Harding's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on 26 January.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
