<p><b>Robert Johnson</b>, a <a href="/wiki/Shropshire" title="Shropshire">Shropshire</a> native, was a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and martyr during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Elizabeth I</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_Johnson_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert Johnson had grown up in one of the four parishes of <a href="/wiki/Claverley" title="Claverley">Claverley</a>, Hales, Owen or <a href="/wiki/Worfield" title="Worfield">Worfield</a> in what was then the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Worcester" title="Anglican Diocese of Worcester">Anglican Diocese of Worcester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-shrewsbury_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrewsbury-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He joined the <a href="/wiki/German_College" class="mw-redirect" title="German College">German College</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 1 October 1571.  In his youth he was a servant in a gentleman's household.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained a priest in <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a> from the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College, Douai</a>. After a pilgrimage to Rome in 1579 he returned to England in 1580, was arrested on 12 July and put in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower</a> on 5 December.  Johnson was <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">racked</a> on 16 December and put in a dungeon until his trial on 14 November 1581.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Johnson was one of 19 priests who stood trial with <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">St Edmund Campion</a> in <a href="/wiki/Westminster_Hall" class="mw-redirect" title="Westminster Hall">Westminster Hall</a> in the late autumn of 1581. They were charged with treason under an Act of 1351 that did not pertain to religion but to a fictitious conspiracy against the Queen known as the "Plot of Rome and Rheims". The purpose was to send out the message that the priests were not condemned for their faith but for conspiring against the Queen, an accusation which they adamantly denied.<sup id="cite_ref-shrewsbury_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrewsbury-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was subsequently condemned on 20 November and executed along with <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Ford_(martyr)" title="Thomas Ford (martyr)">Thomas Ford</a> and <a href="/wiki/John_Shert" title="John Shert">John Shert</a> on 28 May 1582. Johnson was the last to die, after being forced to watch the quartering of Shert.<sup id="cite_ref-shrewsbury_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrewsbury-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Johnson began to pray in Latin and was bidden by a minister to "Pray as Christ taught." Johnson answered, "What! Do you think Christ taught in English?" <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> All three were beatified in 1889.
</p>
