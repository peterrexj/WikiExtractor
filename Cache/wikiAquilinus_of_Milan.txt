<p><b>Saint Aquilinus of Milan</b> (died 1015<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>), also known as <b>Aquilinus of Cologne</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sant'Aquilino</i>), is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>He should not be confused with another Aquilinus, who was killed during the reign of the Arian <a href="/wiki/Vandals" title="Vandals">Vandal</a> king <a href="/wiki/Hunneric" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunneric">Hunneric</a> in 484.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicforum.com_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicforum.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  This 5th century Aquilinus was killed with Eugene, Geminus, Marcian, Quintus, Theodotus, and Tryphon. <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> writes about them.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicforum.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicforum.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  He should also not be confused with an early bishop of Cologne named Aquilinus.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aquilinus_of_Milan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg" title="Würzburg">Würzburg</a> to a noble family, he studied <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>, where he became a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  He was offered the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Cologne">bishopric of Cologne</a>, but he refused, preferring to become a wandering preacher.
</p><p>He traveled to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, where he miraculously cured some people of the <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a>.  As a result, he was offered the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Paris">bishopric of Paris</a>, but this he also refused.  He traveled to <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, where he preached against <a href="/wiki/Cathars" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathars">Cathars</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manichaeans" class="mw-redirect" title="Manichaeans">Manichaeans</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arians</a> there.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati.it_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati.it-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He then traveled to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, where, according to local tradition, he was stabbed by a member of one of these sects,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> along with his companion Constantius (Costanzo).  His body was thrown into a drain, near the <a href="/wiki/Porta_Ticinese" title="Porta Ticinese">Porta Ticinese</a>.  His body was found and then buried in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Lorenzo,_Milan" title="Basilica of San Lorenzo, Milan">Basilica of San Lorenzo, Milan</a>.  The <i>Cappella di Sant'Aquilino</i> is dedicated to him.
</p>
