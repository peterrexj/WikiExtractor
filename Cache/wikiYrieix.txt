<p><b>Aredius</b> (c. 510&#8211;591), also known as <b>Yrieix</b> and <b>Saint Aredius</b>, was Abbot of <a href="/wiki/Limoges" title="Limoges">Limoges</a> and chancellor to <a href="/wiki/Theudebert_I" title="Theudebert I">Theudebert I</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> in the 6th century. He founded the monastery of Attanum, and the various <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Commune_in_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Commune in France">communes</a> called <a href="/wiki/St._Yrieix_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="St. Yrieix (disambiguation)">St. Yrieix</a> are named after him. He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aredius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Aredius was from a prominent Gallo-Roman family of Limoges. He was the son of a noble landowner, Jucundus, and his wife, Pelagia of Limoges. As a young boy he received his education from the abbot Sebastian of the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Vigeois" title="Vigeois">Vigeois</a>. As a young man, he was sent to the court of the Frankish king <a href="/wiki/Theodebert_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodebert I">Theodebert I</a> of Austrasia (534-48) at Trier. By 540 was appointed chancellor.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Nicetius" title="Nicetius">Nicetius</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trier">bishop of Trier</a> persuaded Aredius to leave the dissolute life at court. According to <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, one day, while the clerics sang psalms in the church, a dazzling white dove, after flying around Aredius, landed on his head, as if to show that he was already filled with the Holy Spirit. As he was a little shy, he waved it away, and it flittered a little before landing on its shoulder, and followed him all the way to the bishop's house.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Upon the death of his father, Aredius returned to the Limousin to care for his mother. Entrusting to her the management of his estates, he lived for a time as a hermit in a cave. He used his inheritance to found in the 564/572 monastery of Atane (Attane) on land from his villa Attanum on the rivers Loue and Couchou in Limousin (Haute-Vienne).  He became an abbot in the monastery, and the first monks were members of his own household. Gregory of Tours says that the house followed the rule of <a href="/wiki/John_Cassian" title="John Cassian">Cassian</a> and later incorporated some aspects of the rule of <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Saint Basil</a>. Later, other monks joined them. This later became the site of the city of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Yrieix-le-D%C3%A9jalat" title="Saint-Yrieix-le-Déjalat">Saint Yrieix</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aredius divided his time between agricultural labor and study. He was known for his evangelical journeys throughout Gaul.  He founded monasteries in Vigeois and Excideuil in Périgord and went on pilgrimages, always on foot. Every year he made a pilgrimage to Tours to celebrate the feast of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Saint Martin</a>. He would also travel annually to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Cross_Abbey_(Poitiers)" title="Holy Cross Abbey (Poitiers)">Holy Cross Abbey</a> in Poitiers to visit <a href="/wiki/Radegund" title="Radegund">Queen Radegund</a>. He supported the cult of <a href="/wiki/Medardus" title="Medardus">Medard of Soissons</a> and probably built the church in his honor at Excideuil. Aredius built several churches in honor of saints whose relics he had collected.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Miracle stories began to be associated with him. People in the area believed him to have the gift of healing. Gregory says they crowded to Aredius "like bees to a hive".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On more than one occasion, he intervened with the Merovingian princes on behalf of the people regarding oppressive taxes.
</p><p>He was a friend of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>, and bequeathed some of his wealth to the church at Tours.<sup id="cite_ref-Fletcher_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fletcher-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The town of Saint-Yrieix-La-Perche has requested that the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art" title="Metropolitan Museum of Art">Metropolitan Museum of Art</a> in New York return a reliquary of Aredius, which the town maintains was illegally purchased in 1906.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
