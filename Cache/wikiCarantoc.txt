<p><b>Saint Carantoc</b> (<a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Carannog</i>; <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Cairnech</i>; <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>: <i lang="br">Karanteg</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Carantocus</i>), also <a href="/wiki/Anglicization_of_names" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglicization of names">anglicized</a> as <b>Carantock</b>, <b>Carannog</b> and by other spellings, was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, <a href="/wiki/Confessor_of_the_Faith" title="Confessor of the Faith">confessor</a>, and <a href="/wiki/List_of_Welsh_saints" title="List of Welsh saints">saint</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and the <a href="/wiki/West_Country" title="West Country">West Country</a>. He is credited with founding <a href="/wiki/Llangrannog" title="Llangrannog">Llangrannog</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a><sup id="cite_ref-Cambro_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cambro-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/St_Carantoc%27s_Church,_Crantock" title="St Carantoc&#39;s Church, Crantock">St Carantoc's Church, Crantock</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cambro_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cambro-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His name is listed amongst the <a href="/wiki/Cornish_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Cornish Saints">Cornish Saints</a>. Carantoc's is one of five insular saints' lives and two Breton ones that mention <a href="/wiki/Historical_basis_for_King_Arthur" class="mw-redirect" title="Historical basis for King Arthur">Arthur</a> in contexts that may be independent of <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth" title="Geoffrey of Monmouth">Geoffrey of Monmouth</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Regum_Britanniae" title="Historia Regum Britanniae">Historia Regum Britanniae</a></i>. He is venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_legend">Life and legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carantoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cadoc's early <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> takes the form of a short homily.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Many details of his life are obscure or contradictory. <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a> is given as his birthplace, <i>sua proprio regio.</i> He was the son of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Corun_ap_Ceredig&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Corun ap Ceredig (page does not exist)">Corwn</a>, grandson of King <a href="/wiki/Ceredig" title="Ceredig">Ceredig</a> and Queen <a href="/wiki/Meleri" title="Meleri">Meleri</a> of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ceredigion" title="Kingdom of Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a>. To escape being elected king, he fled to Llangrannog. The shavings he produced for lighting a fire there were carried away as soon as they were made by a dove: where the bird alighted, Carantoc built the present church (This story is sometimes ascribed to <a href="/wiki/Crantock" title="Crantock">Crantock</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cornwall" title="Cornwall">Cornwall</a>, where the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England_parish_church" title="Church of England parish church">parish church</a> is dedicated to Saint Carantoc but according to the early <i>Life</i> in the Léon Breviary,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> which concentrates on Carantoc's early life, set in Ireland, occurred in Wales).
</p><p>He probably moved to Cornwall before preaching for some time in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, around <a href="/w/index.php?title=Dulane&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Dulane (page does not exist)">Dulane</a> in <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a> and Inis-Baithen in <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>. It is also sometimes said that he spent time in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> where there is a town also named <a href="/wiki/Carantec" title="Carantec">Carantec</a>: stories set in Brittany replicate those from Britain.
</p><p>Carantoc's place of death and burial is disputed between Inis Baithen and Dulane. His feast day is 16 May.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carantoc_and_Arthur">Carantoc and Arthur</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carantoc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Carantoc and Arthur">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the most famous incident of Carantoc's life, the saint, having returned to Wales, crossed the <a href="/wiki/Bristol_Channel" title="Bristol Channel">Bristol Channel</a>, looking for his portable altar. He arrived on the banks of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=River_Willett&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="River Willett (page does not exist)">River Willett</a> and came into conflict with both King <a href="/wiki/Cador" title="Cador">Cado</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a> and <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">King Arthur</a> at <a href="/wiki/Dunster" title="Dunster">Dunster</a> in <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somerset</a>.
</p><p>Carantoc was eventually obliged to defeat a ferocious <a href="/wiki/Dragon" title="Dragon">dragon</a> in order to retrieve his altar and, in return, was given land at nearby <a href="/wiki/Carhampton" title="Carhampton">Carhampton</a> to found a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>.
</p>
