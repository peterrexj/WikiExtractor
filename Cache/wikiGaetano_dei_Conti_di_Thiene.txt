<p><b>Gaetano dei Conti di Thiene</b> (6 October 1480 – 7 August 1547), known as <b>Saint Cajetan</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;k&#39; in &#39;kind&#39;">k</span><span title="/æ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;bad&#39;">æ</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/ən/: &#39;on&#39; in &#39;button&#39;">ən</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">KAJ</span>-ə-tən</i></a>), was an Italian Catholic priest and religious reformer, co-founder of the <a href="/wiki/Theatines" title="Theatines">Theatines</a>. He is recognised as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, and his feast day is 7 August.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cajetan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cajetan was born in October 1480, the son of Gaspar, lord of <a href="/wiki/Thiene" title="Thiene">Thiene</a>, and Mary Porta, persons of the first rank among the nobility of the territory of Vicenza, in <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a><sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Region.
</p><p>His father died when he was two years of age. Quiet and retiring by nature,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he was predisposed to piety by his mother. Cajetan studied <a href="/wiki/Law" title="Law">law</a> in <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>, receiving his degree as <i><a href="/wiki/Doctor_utriusque_juris" class="mw-redirect" title="Doctor utriusque juris">doctor utriusque juris</a></i> (i.e., in civil and canon law) at age 24. In 1506 he worked as a diplomat for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_II" title="Pope Julius II">Pope Julius II</a>, with whom he helped reconcile the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Republic of Venice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-keating_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keating-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> But he was not ordained a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> until the year 1516.
</p><p>With the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Julius_II" title="Pope Julius II">Pope Julius II</a> in 1513, Cajetan withdrew from the papal court.<sup id="cite_ref-keating_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keating-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Recalled to Vicenza by the death of his mother, he founded in 1522 a <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">hospital</a> for incurables there.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> By 1523 he had established a hospital in Venice, as well. His interests were as much or more devoted to spiritual healing than the physical kind, and he joined a confraternity in Rome called the "<a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_Divine_Love" title="Oratory of Divine Love">Oratory of Divine Love</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He intended to form a group that would combine the spirit of <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monasticism</a> with the exercises of the active ministry.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Theatines">Theatines</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cajetan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Theatines">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A new congregation was canonically erected by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a> in the year 1524. One of his four companions was Giovanni Pietro Carafa, the Bishop of Chieti, elected first superior of the order, who later became pope as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_IV" title="Pope Paul IV">Paul IV</a>. From the name of the city of <a href="/wiki/Chieti" title="Chieti">Chieti</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Theate</i>), arose the name by which the order is known, the "<a href="/wiki/Theatines" title="Theatines">Theatines</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The order grew at a fairly slow pace: there were only twelve Theatines during the <a href="/wiki/Sack_of_Rome_(1527)" title="Sack of Rome (1527)">sack of Rome in 1527</a>, during which Cajetan was tortured by the Spanish soldiers of <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V</a> who had mutinied.<sup id="cite_ref-Mullett_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mullett-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Theatines managed to escape to Venice.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There Cajetan met <a href="/wiki/Jerome_Emiliani" class="mw-redirect" title="Jerome Emiliani">Jerome Emiliani</a>, whom he assisted in the establishment of his <a href="/wiki/Somaschi_Fathers" title="Somaschi Fathers">Congregation of Clerks Regular</a>. In 1533 he founded a house in Naples. The year 1540 found him in Venice again and from there he extended his work to <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-keating_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keating-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He founded a bank to help the poor and offer an alternative to usurers (who charged high interest rates).<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It later became the <a href="/wiki/Banco_di_Napoli" title="Banco di Napoli">Banco di Napoli</a>.
</p><p>Cajetan died in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> on 7 August 1547.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His remains are in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Paolo_Maggiore" title="San Paolo Maggiore">San Paolo Maggiore</a> in Naples;<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> outside the church is Piazza San Gaetano, with a statue.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cajetan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 8 October 1629 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a>. On 12 April 1671 Cajetan was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Saint Cajetan's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 7 August.
</p><p>Jesuit missionary <a href="/wiki/Eusebio_Kino" title="Eusebio Kino">Eusebio Kino</a> in 1691 established the mission San Cayetano de Tumacácori in honor of Cajetan. It is now <a href="/wiki/Tumacacori_National_Historical_Park" class="mw-redirect" title="Tumacacori National Historical Park">Tumacacori National Historical Park</a> in <a href="/wiki/Arizona" title="Arizona">Arizona</a>.
</p><p>He is known as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Argentina, the unemployed,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> gamblers, document controllers, and good fortune. He was a spiritual aspirant in the mould of St. Francis of Assisi who gave up his riches for leading a life of Christian piety. Fully committed and dedicated as one who practiced total renunciation of material riches would be, he was unwavering in his pursuit of the spiritual goals he had set for completion and never allowed himself to be distracted from realizing them. To the end, he was immersed in his work; and, after being made the patron saint of the unemployed, he meets, fully and favourably, the hopes and aspirations of those searching for work.
</p>
