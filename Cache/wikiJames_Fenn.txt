<p><b>James Fenn</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1540 – 12 February 1584) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest and martyr who was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 15 December 1929, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>. James Fenn was the brother of the Roman Catholic priest and writer <a href="/wiki/John_Fenn_(priest)" title="John Fenn (priest)">John Fenn</a> and of Robert Fenn. All three brothers were <a href="/wiki/Chorister" class="mw-redirect" title="Chorister">choristers</a> and scholars. 
</p><p>Before becoming a priest at around the age of 40, Fenn married and fathered a son and daughter and became a widower. He was executed for his loyalty to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> which was contrary to the demand to recognize supremacy of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Fenn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born about 1540 in <a href="/wiki/Montacute" title="Montacute">Montacute</a>, <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somersetshire</a>, Fenn became a <a href="/wiki/Chorister" class="mw-redirect" title="Chorister">chorister</a> in 1544 at the <a href="/wiki/New_College,_Oxford" title="New College, Oxford">New College, Oxford</a>, where his singing won him a place at <a href="/wiki/Corpus_Christi_College,_Oxford" title="Corpus Christi College, Oxford">Corpus Christi College</a>. Although eligible for a <a href="/wiki/Bachelor%27s_degree" title="Bachelor&#39;s degree">Bachelor's degree</a> in November 1558, he refused to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> that would swear allegiance to the monarch <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a> as <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Governor_of_the_Church_of_England" title="Supreme Governor of the Church of England">Supreme Governor</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>. As insistence on the oath at that time would have affected the status of the majority of the Fellows and most of the Heads of Colleges, the requirement was temporarily suspended by the <a href="/wiki/Privy_council" title="Privy council">Privy council</a> for candidates of degrees at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">University of Oxford</a>. Fenn took his degree on 22 November 1559, but he was expelled and his degree was revoked a year later in November 1560.<sup id="cite_ref-jbw_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jbw-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He remained a while longer at the university and became a tutor to students at <a href="/wiki/Gloucester_College,_Oxford" title="Gloucester College, Oxford">Gloucester Hall</a>, but deciding that it was no longer safe for him there withdrew to Somersetshire.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There he became a tutor, married, and had children. While visiting his father in Wells, Bishop <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Berkeley" title="Gilbert Berkeley">Gilbert Berkeley</a> had him arrested for again refusing to take the oath, but released him when it was pointed out that there had been no occasion for exacting it.<sup id="cite_ref-jbw_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jbw-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father had given him the house in Montacute and he moved there with his family. The local vicar wanted him charged under the <a href="/wiki/Act_of_Uniformity_1558" title="Act of Uniformity 1558">Act of Uniformity</a> for not attending services. On advice of a relative, he went into hiding for two months, during which his wife died. Making provision for his children, he left the area and took a job as agent for Sir Nicholas Poynta of Gloucestershire.<sup id="cite_ref-jbw_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jbw-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While there he met a priest, probably <a href="/wiki/John_Colleton_(priest)" title="John Colleton (priest)">John Colleton</a>, who suggested Fenn consider the priesthood. Fenn left for Rheims in 1579 and was ordained at <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Ch%C3%A2lons" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Châlons">Châlons</a> in April 1580. He returned to <a href="/wiki/Somerset" title="Somerset">Somersetshire</a> the following month, where he worked unmolested for over a year. In July 1581, <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> and Father Colloton were arrested at <a href="/wiki/Lyford,_Oxfordshire" title="Lyford, Oxfordshire">Lyford Grange</a>, along with some prominent people of Somersetshire. In the general excitement this caused, Fenn was arrested as a suspected <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusant</a> and eventually wound up at the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>. Given relative freedom, Fenn ministered to the other prisoners for about two years before he was recognized, possibly by the spy Thomas Dodwell.<sup id="cite_ref-Fenn_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fenn-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 7 February 1584, Fenn and <a href="/wiki/George_Haydock" title="George Haydock">George Haydock</a> (whom Fenn had only met the day before in the dock) were tried and convicted of conspiracy and sedition, among other charges. Fenn was executed at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a> on 12 February 1584, along with George Haydock, John Munden, John Nutter and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Hemerford" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Hemerford">Thomas Hemerford</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Noticing his daughter Frances in the crowd, he attempted to bless her as well as his pinioned hands would allow. His head was mounted on London Bridge and his quarters displayed above the four main gates of London.<sup id="cite_ref-Fenn_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fenn-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=James_Fenn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>James Fenn was beatified on 15 December 1929 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>. He is commemorated on 12 February.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
