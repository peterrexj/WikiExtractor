<p><b>Robert of Arbrissel</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1045 – 1116) was an <a href="/wiki/Itinerant_preacher" title="Itinerant preacher">itinerant preacher</a>, and founder of <a href="/wiki/Fontevraud_Abbey" title="Fontevraud Abbey">Fontevraud Abbey</a>. He was born at Arbrissel (near <a href="/wiki/Retiers" title="Retiers">Retiers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>) and died at <a href="/wiki/Orsan_Priory" title="Orsan Priory">Orsan Priory</a> in the present <a href="/wiki/Cher_(department)" title="Cher (department)">department of Cher</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert's life is primarily known from two biographies (<i>vitae</i>) as well as from a number of letters by other admirers and critics. The first <i>vita</i> was written by <a href="/wiki/Baudri_of_Dol" class="mw-redirect" title="Baudri of Dol">Baudri of Dol</a>, bishop of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Dol-en-Bretagne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Dol-en-Bretagne">Dol-en-Bretagne</a> (formerly abbot of the <a href="/wiki/Bourgueil_Abbey" title="Bourgueil Abbey">monastery of Saint-Pierre of Bourgueil</a>), shortly after Robert's death in 1116 and was likely commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Petronille_de_Chemill%C3%A9" title="Petronille de Chemillé">Petronilla</a>. The second <i>vita</i>, the <i>Vita Altera</i>, was written by a certain Andreas, possibly Robert's chaplain and the head of Fontevraud's male <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> St. Jean and is centered more on the final six months of Robert's life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202138_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202138-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> While it has been suggested that Andreas was commissioned by Petronille as well,<sup id="cite_ref-Dalarun_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalarun-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> others have suggested that Andreas wrote on his behalf for the community.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKerr199922_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKerr199922-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As both authors knew Robert personally, it is likely that the events described in these vitae are more historic than typically can be assumed in this type of medieval genre.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Early_life.2C_studies_in_Paris_and_return_to_Brittany"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life,_studies_in_Paris_and_return_to_Brittany">Early life, studies in Paris and return to Brittany</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early life, studies in Paris and return to Brittany">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Robert was born around 1045 at Arbrissel in Brittany, the son of Domalioch and Orguende.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202139_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202139-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His father was a parish priest. Married priests were not entirely uncommon prior to the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Reform" title="Gregorian Reform">Gregorian reform</a>. He probably succeeded his father as priest to the parish. Seeking to improve his education, he went to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> where he spent some years in study,<sup id="cite_ref-Dalarun_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalarun-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> perhaps under <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Laon" title="Anselm of Laon">Anselm of Laon</a> and later displayed considerable theological knowledge. The date and place of his ordination are unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Sometime prior to 1076, Robert returned to his parish and supported the election of the noblemen Sylvester de La Guerche as <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Rennes,_Dol_and_Saint-Malo" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rennes, Dol and Saint-Malo">Bishop of Rennes</a>. The election was a <a href="/wiki/Simoniac" class="mw-redirect" title="Simoniac">simoniac</a> one, meaning that Sylvester, "who has been described as sword-bearing illiterate who had never bee ordained a priest",<sup id="cite_ref-Dalarun_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalarun-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> bought himself into the office. Sylvester proved himself as an unfit shepherd in a time of growing spirituality and reformative zeal and thus encountered strong opposition from the reform movement. In 1078, a reform council including a papal legate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Gregory VII</a> deposed Sylvester, chasing him out of office as well as his supporters, including Robert. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202139_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202139-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:France_Nord_Ouest_1050.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/France_Nord_Ouest_1050.jpg/250px-France_Nord_Ouest_1050.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="251" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/France_Nord_Ouest_1050.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="300" data-file-height="301" /></a><figcaption>North-Western France in the mid-11th century</figcaption></figure>
<p>Robert resumed his studies in Paris until, dedicating himself to the study of religion but also likely dialectic and rhetoric as his later charismatic preaching could suggest.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202139_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202139-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He spent around a decade in Paris in which he likely embraced the ideals of the church reformers as well as his ideals of eremitic <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>.  
</p><p>In 1089 Robert was recalled by Bishop Sylvester who in the meantime had also taken up the ideals of the Gregorian reform who were becoming popular in all of France.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202140_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202140-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> For the coming four years, Robert would serve as Sylvester's archpriest, effectively running the diocese of Rennes and implementing the ideals of religious life he had lived and studied in Paris. Bishop Sylvester attempted, with Robert's assistance, to introduce reforms to celibacy and liberate churches from the influence of nobles but this provoked antagonism in Brittany, especially amongst the local clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202140_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202140-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Upon the death of Sylvester around 1093, Robert fled to <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a>. Here he resumed his studies at the local <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_school" title="Cathedral school">cathedral school</a> and made acquaintance with its schoolmaster <a href="/wiki/Marbodius_of_Rennes" title="Marbodius of Rennes">Marbodius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202140_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202140-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In addition to his studies, he further turned more intensely to asceticism.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:La_Ro%C3%AB_(53)_Abbaye_05.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/La_Ro%C3%AB_%2853%29_Abbaye_05.jpg/250px-La_Ro%C3%AB_%2853%29_Abbaye_05.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/La_Ro%C3%AB_%2853%29_Abbaye_05.jpg/375px-La_Ro%C3%AB_%2853%29_Abbaye_05.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/La_Ro%C3%AB_%2853%29_Abbaye_05.jpg/500px-La_Ro%C3%AB_%2853%29_Abbaye_05.jpg 2x" data-file-width="794" data-file-height="596" /></a><figcaption>Abbey of La Roë</figcaption></figure>
<h3><span id="Life_as_hermit.2C_itinerant_preacher_and_foundation_of_La_Ro.C3.AB"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_as_hermit,_itinerant_preacher_and_foundation_of_La_Roë">Life as hermit, itinerant preacher and foundation of La Roë</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Life as hermit, itinerant preacher and foundation of La Roë">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1095 he became a hermit in the forest of <a href="/wiki/Craon,_Mayenne" title="Craon, Mayenne">Craon</a> (south-west of <a href="/wiki/Laval,_Mayenne" title="Laval, Mayenne">Laval</a>), living a life of severe penance in the company of <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Thiron" title="Bernard of Thiron">Bernard of Thiron</a>, afterwards founder of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Tiron" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of Tiron">Congregation of Tiron</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vitalis_of_Savigny" title="Vitalis of Savigny">Vitalis</a>, founder of <a href="/wiki/Savigny_Abbey" title="Savigny Abbey">Savigny Abbey</a>, and others of considerable note. His piety, eloquence, and asceticism attracted many followers, and his fame reached <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a> during his travels in France in 1095/96. While in Anger in February 1096, Urban II summoned Robert to him and asked him to preach at the dedication of the church of Saint-Nicholas as a test.<sup id="cite_ref-Dalarun_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dalarun-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Robert passed and was given a licentia praedicandi, a license to preach and initiated Robert's life as a licensed <a href="/wiki/Itinerant_preacher" title="Itinerant preacher">itinerant preacher</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202141_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202141-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Webster_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> These itinerant preachers, although often on the borders to heresy, helped to promote the ideas of the reform movement as well as knowledge of Church teachings in the population and spirituality became a mass phenomenon for the first time since the <a href="/wiki/Early_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Church">early Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202142_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202142-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before Robert could follow Urban's call of preaching, he needed to settle down the followers he had gathered in Craon. This led to the foundation of the monastery of <a href="/wiki/La_Ro%C3%AB" title="La Roë">La Roë</a> on land donated by Renaud I de Craon. The foundation was confirmed by Pope Urban II along with a large number of local bishops and clerics as a community of <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canons regular</a>. Robert stayed during the construction time of the cabins for the canons and the church which was consecrated in April 1097. 
<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202143_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202143-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Nothing is mentioned by Baudri of Dol about the composition of Robert's first group of followers, but it is likely that women also formed part of this group. They were, however, not part of La Roë but might have been settled in other female congregations in the region.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202143_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202143-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Robert's involvement seems not have gone beyond 1098 when he is last mentioned in a charter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202143_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202143-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Robert found a patron in <a href="/wiki/Hildebert" title="Hildebert">Hildebert</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Mans">Bishop of Le Mans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundation_of_Fontevraud">Foundation of Fontevraud</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Foundation of Fontevraud">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Free now to engage in the new stage of life as itinerant preacher, Robert took the roads and sis eloquence, heightened by his strikingly ascetic appearance, drew crowds everywhere.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202145_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202145-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Those who desired to embrace the monastic state under his leadership he sent to La Roë, but the Canons objected to the number and diversity of the postulants. His occasional anticlerical preaching spared no one, leading to criticism of his former schoolmaster Marbod, now bishop of Rennes, who accused him of mentioning the crimes of "even those in high offices".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202145_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202145-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was further accused by Marbod and Abbot <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Vend%C3%B4me" title="Geoffrey of Vendôme">Geoffrey of Vendôme</a> of sleeping in the same room as some of his female followers.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
It is more likely that Robert was mimicking the practice of <i>syneisaktism</i>, an early church practice in which male and female religious would live together in a form of chaste marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-Elliott_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Elliott-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>These accusations led to Robert being summoned to the council of <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a> in November 1100 which two legates of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a> had convened earlier that same year. Fortunately for Robert there were many clerics friendly to his asceticism, among them <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Poitiers" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Poitiers">Bishop Peter II of Poitiers</a>, Robert's most powerful supporter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202146_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202146-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The demands of the council were therefore that the unregulated life of Robert's mixed gender group became regulated and that, most importantly, they built separate living quarters for men and women. Furthermore, the council also settled the place of settlement which was to be in the dioceses of Poitiers in the parish of Roiffe whose church Peter II personally owned.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202146–47_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202146–47-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Robert also took part in the council's decision in excommunicating <a href="/wiki/Philip_I_of_France" title="Philip I of France">Philip I of France</a> on account of his lawless union with <a href="/wiki/Bertrade_de_Montfort" class="mw-redirect" title="Bertrade de Montfort">Bertrade de Montfort</a>, who later would later become a nun in Fontevraud.<sup id="cite_ref-Boyle_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boyle-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Fontevraud3.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Fontevraud3.jpg/250px-Fontevraud3.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="167" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Fontevraud3.jpg/375px-Fontevraud3.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/Fontevraud3.jpg/500px-Fontevraud3.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="400" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Fontevraud_Abbey" title="Fontevraud Abbey">Fontevraud Abbey</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>This led to the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Fontevraud_Abbey" title="Fontevraud Abbey">Fontevraud</a>. He appointed <a href="/wiki/Hersende_of_Champagne" title="Hersende of Champagne">Hersende of Champagne</a>, kinswoman to the Duke of Brittany as abbess, and Petronilla, baroness of Chemille, as coadjutress. Fontrevault followed the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Rule of St. Benedict</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The place was well chosen as it provided the protection from ecclesiastic critics of Robert but also by being between the castles of Chinon, Saumur and Loudon from the near-anarchic and warlike political conditions that haunted Anjou in that time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202147_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202147-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Robert stayed during the construction of the abbey and afterwards took to the roads again, however, this time he kept the contact and placed all subsequent foundation under the authority of Fontevraud .<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202149_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202149-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Robert's legend has long alluded to the presence of converted prostitutes and there is indeed considerable contemporary evidence for this assertion. <a href="/wiki/Baldric_of_Dol" title="Baldric of Dol">Baldric of Dol</a> writes of the presence amongst Robert's disciples of <a href="/wiki/Meretrices" class="mw-redirect" title="Meretrices">meretrices</a> – a Latin word usually used at the time to refer to prostitutes, or at the very least, morally loose women. The almost-certainty of prostitutes being amongst Robert's followers is confirmed by a text discovered at the <a href="/wiki/Vaux-de-Cernay_Abbey" title="Vaux-de-Cernay Abbey">monastery of Vaux-de-Cernay</a>. In the text, Robert visits a brothel in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> and speaks of sin to the prostitutes there; enraptured, they walk away into the wilderness with him. Robert aimed to “attract adulterers and prostitutes to the medicine of repentance”, the text avers. The story it relates may not be entirely true in the matters of its facts, but it relates the essential truth that Robert had prostitute followers – by virtue of showing that such a story was in common currency at the time. Robert also dedicated one of the houses at his abbey of Fontevraud to <a href="/wiki/Mary_Magdalene" title="Mary Magdalene">Mary Magdalene</a>.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life_and_death">Later life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Later life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Robert continued his missionary journeys over the whole of Western France till the end of his life, but little is known of this period. <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Paschal II</a> approved the Fontevraud Order in 1106 and the order would grow to 5,000 during Robert's lifetime.<sup id="cite_ref-Boyle_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boyle-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> In 1110 he attended the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Council_of_Nantes&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Council of Nantes (page does not exist)">Council of Nantes</a>. Knowledge of his approaching death caused him to take steps to ensure the permanence of his foundation at Fontevraud. He imposed a vow of stability on his monks and summoned a Chapter (September 1116) to settle the form of government. From <a href="/wiki/Priory_of_Haute-Bruy%C3%A8re" title="Priory of Haute-Bruyère">Haute-Bruyère</a>, a priory founded by the penitent Bertrade, he went to <a href="/wiki/Orsan_Priory" title="Orsan Priory">Orsan</a>, another priory of Fontevraud, where he died. His panegyric was given by his personal friend Leger, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Bourges" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Bourges">archbishop of Bourges</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKerr199920_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKerr199920-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The accusation made against Robert by <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Vend%C3%B4me" title="Geoffrey of Vendôme">Geoffrey of Vendôme</a> of extreme indiscretion in his choice of exceptional ascetic practices (see P.L., CLVII, 182) was the source of much controversy during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. Other evidence of eccentric actions on Robert's part and scandals among his mixed followers may have helped to give rise to these rumors. The Fontevrists did everything in their power to discredit the attacks on their founder.
</p><p>The accusatory letters of bishop <a href="/wiki/Marbodius_of_Rennes" title="Marbodius of Rennes">Marbodius of Rennes</a> and Geoffrey of Vendôme were without sufficient cause declared to be forgeries and the MS. Letter of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Saumur&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter of Saumur (page does not exist)">Peter of Saumur</a> was made away with, probably at the instigation of Jeanne Baptiste de Bourbon, Abbess of Fontevraud. This natural daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France" title="Henry IV of France">Henry IV of France</a> applied to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_X" title="Pope Innocent X">Pope Innocent X</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> of Robert, her request being supported by <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIV" title="Louis XIV">Louis XIV</a> and <a href="/wiki/Henrietta_Anne_Stuart" class="mw-redirect" title="Henrietta Anne Stuart">Henrietta of England</a>. In the event, Robert was never <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>, but he was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> and so is venerated by the Catholic Church as "Blessed" and recorded as such in the current edition of the Roman Martyrology. In places where this has been authorized, he may be celebrated on February 25. 
</p><p>The original recension of the Rule of Fontevraud no longer exists; the only surviving writing of Robert is his letter of exhortation to <a href="/wiki/Ermengarde_of_Anjou_(d._1146)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ermengarde of Anjou (d. 1146)">Ermengarde of Brittany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Arbrissel&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert of Arbrissel undoubtedly prepared the wave of convent foundations in the twelfth and thirteenth century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202138_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202138-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Many noble women would become nuns at Fontevraud Abbey and several members of <a href="/wiki/Angevin_Empire" title="Angevin Empire">Angevin royalty</a> are interred in the abbey.
</p><p>Robert's life and his foundation of an abbey where male monks where subject to a female abbess has created repeated interest in his life over the centuries by historians and cultural scholars. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKerr199923–26_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKerr199923–26-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202137–38_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202137–38-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> He has been portrayed as proto-feminist, religious radical, ascetic or even an early fighter for class struggles on a mission to change the order of the world. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller202138_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller202138-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
