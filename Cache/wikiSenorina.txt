<p><b>Saint Senhorinha of Basto</b>, also <b>Senorina</b> (<a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">Santa Senhorinha de Basto</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 942 – 982) was a <a href="/wiki/County_of_Portugal" title="County of Portugal">Portuguese</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> in what is today northern <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>. She is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and was related to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Rudesind" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Rudesind">Saint Rudesind</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mondo%C3%B1edo" title="Mondoñedo">Mondoñedo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Senhorinha_of_Basto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Senhorinha of <a href="/wiki/Cabeceiras_de_Basto" title="Cabeceiras de Basto">Basto</a> is thought to have been born into the noble <a href="/wiki/Sousa_family" title="Sousa family">Sousa family</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> as either Domitilla or Genoveva.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After being raised by her aunt, <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> <b>Godinha</b>, abbess of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> convent of St. John of <a href="/wiki/Vieira_do_Minho" title="Vieira do Minho">Vieira</a>, Senhorinha also joined the Benedictines and succeeded her aunt as abbess at Vieira.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:2_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Later, she moved the convent of Vieira to <a href="/wiki/Cabeceiras_de_Basto" title="Cabeceiras de Basto">Basto</a> near <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a>, Portugal.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Senhorinha_of_Basto&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Senhorinha of Basto was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Paio_Mendes" title="Paio Mendes">Paio Mendes</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Braga" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Braga">Archbishop of Braga</a>, in 1130, at a time when bishops had the authority to canonize faithful people in their dioceses.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is April 22,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:4_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> which is recorded in Portuguese liturgical calendars beginning in the 13th century.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her shrine in <a href="/wiki/Cabeceiras_de_Basto" title="Cabeceiras de Basto">Basto</a> was a popular pilgrimage destination in the region in the 12th and 13th centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Sancho_I_of_Portugal" title="Sancho I of Portugal">King Sancho I of Portugal</a> was one famous devotee who made the pilgrimage to cure his son and heir, <a href="/wiki/Afonso_II_of_Portugal" title="Afonso II of Portugal">Afonso II</a>, who was healed and succeeded Sancho I as king of Portugal.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The church in <a href="/wiki/Cabeceiras_de_Basto" title="Cabeceiras de Basto">Basto</a> has been named in her honor since at least 1220, and water from a nearby fountain named after her is believed to have healing properties.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Her tomb is housed in the church.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In art, she is typically depicted with a water jug or vessel at her side and sometimes a frog, in reference to one of the miracles she is said to have performed during her life.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
