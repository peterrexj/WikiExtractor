					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">7th-century Irish abbess</div>
<p>
<b>Saint Ciera</b> of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a> (alternately Chera, Chier, Ciara, Cyra, <b>Keira</b>, <b>Keara</b>, <b>Kiara</b>, Kiera, Ceara, Cier, Ciar) was an <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> in the 7th century who died in 679. Her history is probably commingled with another Cera (alternately Ciar, Ciara) who lived in the 6th century.  However, some authors maintain that monastic mistakes account for references to Cera in the 6th century or that a single Cera had an exceptionally long life span.<sup id="cite_ref-Dunbar_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dunbar-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There are two stories connected with the saint(s).  In the first story, Cera's prayers saved an Irish town from a foul-smelling fire. When a noxious blaze broke out in "Muscraig, in Momonia," St. <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan</a> instructed the inhabitants to seek Cera's prayers. They followed his instructions, Cera prayed in response to their supplications, and the fire disappeared.<sup id="cite_ref-Dunbar_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dunbar-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Smith_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Since St. Brendan died in 577, this story likely refers to an earlier Cera. "Muscraig, in Momonia" may refer to <a href="/wiki/Muskerry" title="Muskerry">Muskerry</a>, an area outside of <a href="/wiki/Cork_(city)" title="Cork (city)">Cork</a>.  "Momonia" refers to southern Ireland in at least one ancient map.<sup id="cite_ref-IA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The other story relates how St. Cera established a nunnery called Teych-Telle around the year 625.  Cera was the daughter of Duibhre (or Dubreus) reportedly in the bloodline of the kings of Connor (or Conaire). She, along with 5 other virgins asked <a href="/wiki/Saint_Fintan_Munnu" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Fintan Munnu">Saint Fintan Munnu</a> for a place to serve God.  He and his monks gave the women their abbey in Heli (or Hele).  Heli may have been in <a href="/wiki/County_Westmeath" title="County Westmeath">County Westmeath</a>.  He blessed Cera, and instructed her to name the place after St. Telle who had given birth to four children, Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John in the plain of Miodhluachra that day.<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Cera eventually returned to her own province and founded another monastery,  Killchree, which she governed until her death in 679.<sup id="cite_ref-Smith_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  The later <a href="/wiki/Kilcrea_Friary" title="Kilcrea Friary">Franciscan Kilcrea Friary</a> stands about a mile west of where her monastery stood, and claims to have taken its name, Kilcrea, in her honor: "Kilcrea (Cill Chre) means the Cell of Cere, Ciara, Cera or Cyra."<sup id="cite_ref-IN_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IN-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Remembrance">Remembrance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Cera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Remembrance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Cera's feast day is March 15, and a festival on July 2 also commemorated her. Both dates are reported to have been the day of her death. Statements also show December 15.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2010)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
