<p><b>Basil</b> or <b>Vasiľ</b> <b>Hopko</b> (21 April 1904, <a href="/wiki/Hrabsk%C3%A9" title="Hrabské">Hrabské</a> &#8212; 23 July 1976) was an <a href="/wiki/Eparch" class="mw-redirect" title="Eparch">eparch</a> (<a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>) of the <a href="/wiki/Slovak_Greek_Catholic_Church" title="Slovak Greek Catholic Church">Slovak Greek Catholic Church</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 14 September 2003 for his <a href="/wiki/Martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrdom">martyrdom</a> under <a href="/wiki/Communist" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist">Communist</a> occupation.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Basil_Hopko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Basil Hopko was born in the <a href="/wiki/Rusyns" title="Rusyns">Rusyn</a> village of <a href="/wiki/Hrabsk%C3%A9" title="Hrabské">Hrabské</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A1ros_County" title="Sáros County">Sáros County</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Kingdom of Hungary</a> (present-day eastern <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a>). His parents, Basil and Anna née  Petrenko, were landless <a href="/wiki/Peasants" class="mw-redirect" title="Peasants">peasants</a>. While Hopko was still an infant, his father was struck by lightning and died. His mother left him in care of her father, while she emigrated to the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a> in search of work. When Hopko was 7 he was sent to live with his uncle Demeter Petrenko, a Greek Catholic priest.
</p><p>He attended the Evangelical <a href="/wiki/Gymnasium_(school)" title="Gymnasium (school)">gymnasium</a> in <a href="/wiki/Pre%C5%A1ov" title="Prešov">Prešov</a>, then <a href="/wiki/Czechoslovakia" title="Czechoslovakia">Czechoslovakia</a>, graduating with honors in 1923. Hopko studied at the Eparchial Seminary in Prešov. He had dreams of joining his mother in America, and of pursuing his priestly vocation there, but the cost of recurring health problems left him unable to afford to travel. He later wrote that when he finally decided to stay and to serve in his homeland, he was suddenly cured, and realized he had been given a sign about his calling. He was ordained a Greek Catholic priest on 3 February 1929.
</p><p>He served as a <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a> (1929&#8211;1936) at the Greek Catholic parish in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>, the Czechoslovak capital, where he was known for his focus on the poor, the unemployed, and students. His mother returned from America after 22 years and rejoined her son in Prague, becoming his housekeeper at the parish rectory.
</p><p>In 1936 he returned to teach in Prešov's Eparchial Seminary, and was awarded the title of <a href="/wiki/Monsignor" title="Monsignor">monsignor</a>. He had already begun graduate studies at <a href="/wiki/Charles_University" title="Charles University">Charles University</a> while in Prague, and he completed his Doctor of Theology in 1940 at <a href="/wiki/Comenius_University" title="Comenius University">Comenius University</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bratislava" title="Bratislava">Bratislava</a>. In Prešov he headed the <a href="/wiki/Eparchy" title="Eparchy">eparchy</a>'s publishing division, where he edited a monthly periodical.
</p><p>After World War II, a growing Soviet Communist influence caused Bishop <a href="/wiki/Pavol_Peter_Gojdi%C4%8D" class="mw-redirect" title="Pavol Peter Gojdič">Pavol Peter Gojdič</a> of Prešov to ask the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> for an <a href="/wiki/Auxiliary_Bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Auxiliary Bishop">Auxiliary Bishop</a> to help defend the Greek Catholic Church. Hopko was appointed to the post on 11 May 1947. The Communist take-over of Czechoslovakia wrought havoc on the Greek Catholic Church. In 1950 it was officially abolished by the state as a religious organization, and its assets were turned over to the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>. Bishop <a href="/wiki/Pavel_Peter_Gojdi%C4%8D" title="Pavel Peter Gojdič">Gojdič</a> was arrested and was imprisoned for life. Bishop Hopko was arrested on 28 April 1950 and kept on starvation rations and tortured for weeks. Eventually he was tried and sentenced to 15 years for the "subversive activity" of staying loyal to Rome. He was repeatedly transferred from prison to prison. His health, physical and emotional, failed, and in 1964 he was transferred to a home for the aged. He never recovered his health.
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Prague_Spring" title="Prague Spring">Prague Spring</a> the Czechoslovak government legally cleared Hopko on 13 June 1968 and the Prešov Eparchy was restored. However, activists insisted that a Slovak bishop be appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> named the <a href="/wiki/Slovaks" title="Slovaks">Slovak</a> priest <a href="/wiki/J%C3%A1n_Hirka" title="Ján Hirka">Ján Hirka</a> as Bishop Hopko's successor.
</p><p>Bishop Hopko died in Prešov at age 72 on 23 July 1976.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 14 September 2003 Pope John Paul II beatified him at a ceremony in Bratislava, Slovakia.
</p>
