<p><b>Raphael of St. Joseph Kalinowski</b> (<a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Józef Kalinowski</i>, <a href="/wiki/Lithuanian_language" title="Lithuanian language">Lithuanian</a>: <i lang="lt">Rapolas Kalinauskas</i>) (1 September 1835 – 15 November 1907) was a Polish <a href="/wiki/Discalced_Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Discalced Carmelite">Discalced Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> inside the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian</a> <a href="/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland" title="Partitions of Poland">partition of Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a>, in the city of <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Вильна</span>). He was a teacher, engineer, prisoner of war, royal tutor, and priest, who founded many Carmelite monasteries around Poland after their suppression by the Russians.
</p><p>Kalinowski was canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1991, the first man to be so recognized in the Order of <a href="/wiki/Discalced_Carmelites" title="Discalced Carmelites">Discalced Carmelites</a> since <a href="/wiki/John_of_the_Cross" title="John of the Cross">John of the Cross</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood">Childhood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_Kalinowski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Childhood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<p>Raphael was born <b>Józef Kalinowski</b> to a noble "<a href="/wiki/Szlachta" title="Szlachta">szlachta</a>" family in the city of  <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a> (Vilna). At the time he was born, the area was known as a <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian</a> <a href="/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland" title="Partitions of Poland">partition</a>, though it had formerly been part of the <a href="/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth" title="Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth">Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth</a>.  He was the second son of Andrew Kalinowski (1805–1878), an assistant superintendent professor of mathematics at the local <a href="/wiki/Institute_for_Nobles" title="Institute for Nobles">Institute for Nobles</a> (<i>Instytut Szlachecki</i>). His mother, Josephine Połońska, also a noblewoman, <a href="/wiki/Leliwa_coat_of_arms" title="Leliwa coat of arms">Leliwa coat of arms</a> died a few months after he was born, leaving him and his older brother Victor without a mother.  His father then married Josephine's sister (a practice that was not uncommon in that time), Sophie Połońska, and had three more children: Charles, Emily, and Gabriel.  After Sophie died in 1845, Andrew married again, this time to the 17-year-old Sophie Puttkamer, daughter of Maryla Wereszczak (famous at the time for being written about by <a href="/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz" title="Adam Mickiewicz">Adam Mickiewicz</a>), who became mother to all of Andrew's existing children and had four more of her own: Mary, Alexander, Monica, and George.
</p><p>From the age of 8, Kalinowski attended the Institute for Nobles at Vilna, and graduated with <a href="/wiki/Honors_student" title="Honors student">honors</a> in 1850.<sup id="cite_ref-Sokol-173_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sokol-173-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He next attended the School of Agriculture (<i>Instytut Agronomiczny</i>) at Hory-Horki, near <a href="/wiki/Orsha" title="Orsha">Orsha</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_career">Military career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_Kalinowski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Military career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Russians strictly limited opportunities for further education, so in 1853 he enlisted in the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army" title="Imperial Russian Army">Imperial Russian Army</a> and entered the Nicholayev Engineering Academy (<i>Mikołajewska Szkoła Inżynierii</i>).  The Army promoted him to Second Lieutenant in 1856.  In 1857 he worked as an associate professor of mathematics, and from 1858 to 1860, he worked as an engineer who helped design the <a href="/wiki/Odessa" class="mw-redirect" title="Odessa">Odessa</a>-<a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a>-<a href="/wiki/Kursk" title="Kursk">Kursk</a> railway.
</p><p>In 1862 the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army" title="Imperial Russian Army">Imperial Russian Army</a> promoted him to captain and stationed him in <a href="/wiki/Brest,_Belarus" title="Brest, Belarus">Brest, Belarus</a>, but he still sympathized with the Poles.  He consequently resigned from the Imperial Russian Army in 1863 to serve as minister of war for the <a href="/wiki/January_Uprising" title="January Uprising">January Uprising</a>, a Polish insurrection, in the <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius</a> region.  He determined never to sentence anyone to death nor to execute any prisoner.
When the Poles rose against the Russians in 1863, Raphael joined them and was soon taken prisoner. Very few survived the forced
march to slave labour in Siberia, but Raphael was sustained by his faith and became a spiritual leader to the prisoners.
He was released ten years later.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Siberia">Siberia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_Kalinowski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Siberia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 24 March 1864, Russian authorities arrested Kalinowski and in June condemned him to death by firing squad.  His family intervened, and the Russians, fearing that their Polish subjects would revere him as a political martyr, commuted the sentence to 10 years in <a href="/wiki/Katorga" title="Katorga">katorga</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Siberian" class="mw-redirect" title="Siberian">Siberian</a> labor camp system.  They forced him to trek overland to the <a href="/wiki/Salt_mine" class="mw-redirect" title="Salt mine">salt mines</a> of <a href="/wiki/Usolye-Sibirskoye" title="Usolye-Sibirskoye">Usolye-Sibirskoye</a> near <a href="/wiki/Irkutsk" title="Irkutsk">Irkutsk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Siberia" title="Siberia">Siberia</a>, a journey that took nine months.<sup id="cite_ref-Sokol-173_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sokol-173-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Three years after arriving in Usolye, Kalinowski moved to Irkutsk. In 1871/1872 he did <a href="/wiki/Meteorology" title="Meteorology">meteorological</a> research for the Siberian subdivision of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Geographical_Society" title="Russian Geographical Society">Russian Geographical Society</a>.  He also participated in research expedition of <a href="/wiki/Benedykt_Dybowski" title="Benedykt Dybowski">Benedykt Dybowski</a> to <a href="/wiki/Kultuk,_Slyudyansky_District,_Irkutsk_Oblast" title="Kultuk, Slyudyansky District, Irkutsk Oblast">Kultuk</a>, on the shore of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Baikal" title="Lake Baikal">Lake Baikal</a>.  Authorities released him from Siberia in 1873 but exiled him from Lithuania; he then moved to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, France.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Royal_tutor">Royal tutor</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_Kalinowski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Royal tutor">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Kalinowski returned to <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a> in 1874, where he became a tutor to 16-year-old Prince <a href="/wiki/August_Czartoryski" title="August Czartoryski">August Czartoryski</a>. The prince was diagnosed with <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> in 1876, and Kalinowski accompanied him to various health destinations in France, <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, Italy, and <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Sokol-173_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sokol-173-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Kalinowski was a major influence on the young man (known as "Gucio"), who later became a priest and was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by Pope John Paul II in 2004.
</p><p>Later Kalinowski decided to travel to the city of <a href="/wiki/Brest,_Belarus" title="Brest, Belarus">Brest</a> where he began a Sunday school at the fortress in Brest-Litovsk where he was a captain, he became increasingly aware of the state persecution of the church, and of his native Poles.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Carmelite_friar_and_priest">Carmelite friar and priest</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_Kalinowski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Carmelite friar and priest">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1877 Kalinowski was admitted to the Carmelite <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> in <a href="/wiki/Linz" title="Linz">Linz</a>, and where he was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> of <b>Brother Raphael of St. Joseph, O.C.D.</b> The name "of St. Joseph" had nothing to do with his birthname—it was common for many Carmelites to list their name as "of St. Joseph", after the "Convent of St. Joseph" founded by <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_Avila" class="mw-redirect" title="Teresa of Avila">Teresa of Avila</a>, co-founder of the Discalced Carmelite Reform.
</p><p>Kalinowski was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> as a priest at Czerna in 1882 by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Albin_Dunajewski" title="Albin Dunajewski">Albin Dunajewski</a>, and in 1883 he became prior of the monastery at Czerna.
</p><p>Kalinowski founded multiple <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a> organizations around Poland and Ukraine, most prominent of which was a monastery in <a href="/wiki/Wadowice" title="Wadowice">Wadowice</a>, Poland, where he was also prior.  He founded a monasteries of Discalced Carmelite nuns in <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemyśl</a> in 1884, and <a href="/wiki/Lvov" class="mw-redirect" title="Lvov">Lvov</a> in 1888.
</p><p>From 1892 to 1907 Kalinowski worked to document the life and work of Mother Theresa Marchocka, a 17th-century Discalced Carmelite nun, to assist with her beatification.
</p><p>Kalinowski died in <a href="/wiki/Wadowice" title="Wadowice">Wadowice</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> in 1907.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Fourteen years later, Karol Wojtyła, later known as <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, was born in the same town.
</p><p>Kalinowski was a noted spiritual director of both Catholic and <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian Orthodox">Russian Orthodox</a> faithful.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raphael_Kalinowski&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites,_Poland.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites%2C_Poland.jpg/220px-Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites%2C_Poland.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites%2C_Poland.jpg/330px-Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites%2C_Poland.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites%2C_Poland.jpg/440px-Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites%2C_Poland.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2122" data-file-height="1415" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Czerna_Monastery_of_Discalced_Carmelites,_Poland.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Monastery of the Discalced Carmelites in <a href="/wiki/Czerna,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship" title="Czerna, Lesser Poland Voivodeship">Czerna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a></div></div></div>
<p>Kalinowski's remains were originally kept in the monastery cemetery, but this caused difficulties because of the large number of pilgrims who came visiting.  So many of them took handfuls of dirt from the grave that the nuns had to keep replacing the earth and plants at the cemetery.  His body was later moved to a tomb, but the pilgrims went there instead, often scratching with their hands at the plaster, just to have some relic to keep with them.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  His remains were then moved to a chapel in <a href="/wiki/Czerna,_Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship" title="Czerna, Lesser Poland Voivodeship">Czerna</a>, where they remain.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> Kalinowski in 1983 in <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, in front of a crowd of over two million people. On 17 November 1991, he was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> when, in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>, Pope John Paul II declared his boyhood hero a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Rafał was the first friar in the Order of the Discalced Carmelites to have been canonized since co-founder <a href="/wiki/John_of_the_Cross" title="John of the Cross">John of the Cross</a> (1542–1591) became a saint in 1726.
</p><p>Kalinowski's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 19 November in the Discalced Carmelites order and on 20 November by the Catholic Church in Poland.
</p><p>Catholics revere him as a patron saint of soldiers and officers of Poland<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
and also of <a href="/wiki/Sybirak" title="Sybirak">Polish exiles in Siberia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
