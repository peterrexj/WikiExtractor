<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a>, the Irish missionary monk who founded monasteries in France and Italy.</div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other uses, see <a href="/wiki/Columba_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Columba (disambiguation)">Columba (disambiguation)</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Columba_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Saint Columba (disambiguation)">Saint Columba (disambiguation)</a>.</div>

<p><b>Columba</b> or <b>Colmcille</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (7 December 521 – 9 June 597 AD) was an <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland" title="Gaelic Ireland">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and missionary evangelist credited with spreading Christianity in what is today <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> at the start of the <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Scottish_mission" title="Hiberno-Scottish mission">Hiberno-Scottish mission</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He founded the important <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">abbey</a> on <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>, which became a dominant religious and political institution in the region for centuries. He is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Derry" title="Derry">Derry</a>. He was highly regarded by both the <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Gaels</a> of <a href="/wiki/D%C3%A1l_Riata" title="Dál Riata">Dál Riata</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Picts</a>, and is remembered today as a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and one of the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Gratton-Flood1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gratton-Flood1-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Columba studied under some of Ireland's most prominent church figures and founded several monasteries in the country. Around 563 AD he and his twelve companions crossed to Dunaverty near <a href="/wiki/Southend,_Argyll" title="Southend, Argyll">Southend, Argyll</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Kintyre" title="Kintyre">Kintyre</a> before settling in Iona in Scotland, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a> kingdom of Dál Riata, where they founded a new abbey as a base for spreading <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Celtic Christianity</a> among the pagan <a href="/wiki/Fortriu" title="Fortriu">Northern Pictish kingdoms</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECharles-Edwards2000303_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECharles-Edwards2000303-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWagnerKonstam201214_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWagnerKonstam201214-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He remained active in Irish politics, though he spent most of the remainder of his life in Scotland. Three surviving early medieval Latin <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymns</a> may be attributed to him.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_in_Ireland">Early life in Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life in Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Columba was born to Fedlimid and Eithne of the <a href="/wiki/Cenel_Conaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenel Conaill">Cenel Conaill</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gartan" title="Gartan">Gartan</a>, a district beside Lough Gartan, in <a href="/wiki/T%C3%ADr_Chonaill" class="mw-redirect" title="Tír Chonaill">Tír Chonaill</a> (mainly modern <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">County Donegal</a>) in the north of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. On his father's side, he is claimed as being the great-great-grandson of <a href="/wiki/Niall_of_the_Nine_Hostages" title="Niall of the Nine Hostages">Niall of the Nine Hostages</a>, a pseudo-historical <a href="/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland" title="High King of Ireland">Irish high king</a> of the 5th century. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptised</a> in Temple-Douglas, in the County Donegal parish of <a href="/wiki/Conwal_and_Leck" title="Conwal and Leck">Conwal</a> (midway between Gartan and <a href="/wiki/Letterkenny" title="Letterkenny">Letterkenny</a>), by his teacher and foster-uncle <a href="/wiki/Cruithnech%C3%A1n" title="Cruithnechán">Cruithnechán</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEReeves185082_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEReeves185082-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMunn1985_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMunn1985-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Ordnance1986p_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ordnance1986p-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Columba lived in the small village of <a href="/wiki/Glencolmcille" title="Glencolmcille">Glencolmcille</a> for roughly 5 years, which was named after him. It is not known for sure if his name at birth was Colmcille or if he adopted this name later in life; <a href="/wiki/Adomn%C3%A1n" title="Adomnán">Adomnán</a> (Eunan) of Iona thought it was his birth name but other Irish sources have claimed his name at birth was Crimthann (meaning 'fox').<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAdomnan_of_Iona1995_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdomnan_of_Iona1995-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In the Irish language his name means 'dove', which is the same name as the Prophet Jonah (Jonah in Hebrew is also 'dove'), which Adomnán of Iona as well as other early Irish writers were aware of, although it is not clear if he was deliberately named after Jonah or not. <i>Columba</i> is also <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> for dove. (See also the bird genus <i><a href="/wiki/Columba_(bird)" title="Columba (bird)">Columba</a></i>.)
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Columba%27s_church,_Gartan,_Donegal.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/St_Columba%27s_church%2C_Gartan%2C_Donegal.jpg/220px-St_Columba%27s_church%2C_Gartan%2C_Donegal.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="221" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/St_Columba%27s_church%2C_Gartan%2C_Donegal.jpg/330px-St_Columba%27s_church%2C_Gartan%2C_Donegal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/St_Columba%27s_church%2C_Gartan%2C_Donegal.jpg/440px-St_Columba%27s_church%2C_Gartan%2C_Donegal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="636" data-file-height="640" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Columba%27s_church,_Gartan,_Donegal.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The remains of St. Columba's Church, <a href="/wiki/Gartan" title="Gartan">Gartan</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Donegal" title="County Donegal">County Donegal</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>When sufficiently advanced in letters he entered the monastic school of Movilla, at <a href="/wiki/Newtownards" title="Newtownards">Newtownards</a>, under <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Movilla" title="Finnian of Movilla">Finnian of Movilla</a> who had studied at <a href="/wiki/Ninian" title="Ninian">Ninian</a>'s "Magnum Monasterium" on the shores of <a href="/wiki/Galloway" title="Galloway">Galloway</a>. He was about twenty, and a deacon when, having completed his training at Movilla, he travelled southwards into <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, where he became a pupil of an aged bard named Gemman. On leaving him, Columba entered the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard</a>, governed at that time by <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian</a>, noted for sanctity and learning. Here he imbibed the traditions of the Welsh Church, for Finnian had been trained in the schools of <a href="/wiki/Saint_David" title="Saint David">Saint David</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Edmonds_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Edmonds-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In early Christian Ireland, the <a href="/wiki/Druid" title="Druid">druidic</a> tradition collapsed due to the spread of the new Christian faith.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The study of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> learning and <a href="/wiki/Christian_theology" title="Christian theology">Christian theology</a> in <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a> flourished. Columba became a pupil at the monastic school at <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard Abbey</a>, situated on the <a href="/wiki/River_Boyne" title="River Boyne">River Boyne</a> in modern <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a>. During the sixth century, some of the most significant names in the history of Celtic Christianity studied at the Clonard monastery. The average number of scholars under instruction at Clonard was said to be 300.<sup id="cite_ref-Gratton-Flood1_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gratton-Flood1-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Columba was one of twelve students of <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Clonard" title="Finnian of Clonard">Finnian of Clonard</a> who became known as the <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a>. He became a monk and eventually was <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Presbyter" title="Presbyter">priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another preceptor of Columba was <a href="/wiki/Mobh%C3%AD_Cl%C3%A1rainech" title="Mobhí Clárainech">Mobhí Clárainech</a>, whose monastery at <a href="/wiki/Glasnevin" title="Glasnevin">Glasnevin</a> was frequented by such famous men as <a href="/wiki/Cainnech_of_Aghaboe" title="Cainnech of Aghaboe">Cainnech of Aghaboe</a>, <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">Comgall</a>, and <a href="/wiki/St_Ciar%C3%A1n" class="mw-redirect" title="St Ciarán">Ciarán</a>. A pestilence which devastated Ireland in 544 caused the dispersion of Mobhi's disciples, and Columba returned to <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a>, the land of his kindred. He was a striking figure of great stature and powerful build, with a loud, melodious voice which could be heard from one hilltop to another.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The following years were marked by the foundation of several important monasteries: <a href="/wiki/Derry" title="Derry">Derry</a>, at the southern edge of <a href="/wiki/Inishowen" title="Inishowen">Inishowen</a>; Durrow, County Offaly; Kells, County Meath; and <a href="/wiki/Swords,_Dublin" title="Swords, Dublin">Swords</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESidwell199570_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESidwell199570-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> While at Derry it is said that he planned a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, but did not proceed farther than <a href="/wiki/Tours,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Tours, France">Tours</a>. Thence he brought a copy of those gospels that had lain on the bosom of <a href="/wiki/St._Martin_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Martin of Tours">St Martin</a> for the space of 100 years. This relic was deposited in Derry.<sup id="cite_ref-Edmonds_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Edmonds-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> St Colmcille is also believed to have established a Church on <a href="/wiki/Inishkea_Islands" title="Inishkea Islands">Inishkea North</a>, County Mayo which is named St Colmcille’s Church.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some traditions assert that sometime around 560 Columba became involved in a quarrel with <a href="/wiki/Finnian_of_Moville" class="mw-redirect" title="Finnian of Moville">Finnian of Moville</a> of <a href="/wiki/Movilla_Abbey" title="Movilla Abbey">Movilla Abbey</a> over a <a href="/wiki/Psalter" title="Psalter">psalter</a>. Columba copied the manuscript at the <a href="/wiki/Scriptorium" title="Scriptorium">scriptorium</a> under Finnian, intending to keep the copy. Finnian disputed his right to keep it. There is a suggestion that this conflict resulted in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_C%C3%BAl_Dreimhne" title="Battle of Cúl Dreimhne">Battle of Cúl Dreimhne</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cairbre_Drom_Cliabh" title="Cairbre Drom Cliabh">Cairbre Drom Cliabh</a> (now in <a href="/wiki/County_Sligo" title="County Sligo">County Sligo</a>) in 561, during which many men were killed. Richard Sharpe, translator of Adomnán's Life of St. Columba (referenced in the bibliography below) makes a stern caution at this point against accepting the many references that link the battle and Columba's leaving of Ireland, even though there is evidence in the annals that Columba supported his own king against the high king.  Political conflicts that had existed for some time resulted in the clan Neill's battle against King Diarmait at Cooldrevny in 561. An issue, for example, was the king's violation of the right of sanctuary belonging to Columba's person as a monk on the occasion of the murder of Prince Curnan, Columba's kinsman.<sup id="cite_ref-Edmonds_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Edmonds-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Prince Curnan of Connacht, who had fatally injured a rival in a hurling match and had taken refuge with Columba, was dragged from his protector's arms and slain by Diarmaid's men, in defiance of the rights of sanctuary.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> of <a href="/wiki/Clerics" class="mw-redirect" title="Clerics">clerics</a> and scholars threatened to <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunicate</a> him for these deaths, but <a href="/wiki/Brendan_of_Birr" title="Brendan of Birr">Brendan of Birr</a> spoke on his behalf. Eventually, the process was deemed a miscarriage of justice. Columba's own conscience was uneasy, and on the advice of an aged hermit, Molaise, he resolved to expiate his sense of offence by departing Ireland. The term "exile" is used in some references. This, too, can be disputed, for the term "pilgrimage" is used more frequently in the literature about him. A marker at Stroove Beach on the <a href="/wiki/Inishowen_Peninsula" class="mw-redirect" title="Inishowen Peninsula">Inishowen Peninsula</a> commemorates the place where Columba set sail for Scotland. He left Ireland, but through the following years, he returned several times in relationships with the communities he had founded there.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> 
</p><p>Columba's copy of the <a href="/wiki/Psalter" title="Psalter">psalter</a> has been traditionally associated with the <a href="/wiki/Cathach_of_St._Columba" title="Cathach of St. Columba">Cathach of St. Columba</a>. In 574/575, during his return for the Synod of Drum Ceat he founded the <a href="/wiki/Drumcliff_monastery,_County_Sligo" title="Drumcliff monastery, County Sligo">monastery of Drumcliff</a> in Cairbre, now County Sligo, near the battlefield.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Scotland">Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 563, he travelled to <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a> with twelve companions (said to include <a href="/wiki/Odran_of_Iona" class="mw-redirect" title="Odran of Iona">Odran of Iona</a>) in a wicker <a href="/wiki/Currach" title="Currach">currach</a> covered with leather. According to legend he first landed on the <a href="/wiki/Mull_of_Kintyre" title="Mull of Kintyre">Kintyre</a> Peninsula, near <a href="/wiki/Southend,_Kintyre" class="mw-redirect" title="Southend, Kintyre">Southend</a>. However, being still in sight of his native land, he moved farther north up the west coast of Scotland. The island of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> was made over to him by his kinsman <a href="/wiki/Conall_mac_Comgaill" title="Conall mac Comgaill">Conall mac Comgaill</a> <a href="/wiki/List_of_kings_of_D%C3%A1l_Riata" title="List of kings of Dál Riata">King of Dál Riata</a>,  who perhaps had invited him to come to Scotland in the first place.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> However, there is a sense in which he was not leaving his native people, as the Ulster <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Gaels</a> had been colonising the west coast of Scotland for the previous couple of centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFletcher198923–24_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFletcher198923–24-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Aside from the services he provided guiding the only centre of <a href="/wiki/Literacy" title="Literacy">literacy</a> in the region, his reputation as a holy man led to his role as a diplomat among the tribes.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>There are also many stories of miracles which he performed during his work to <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">convert</a> the Picts, the most famous being his encounter with an unidentified animal that some have equated with the <a href="/wiki/Loch_Ness_Monster" title="Loch Ness Monster">Loch Ness Monster</a> in 565. It is said that he banished a ferocious "water beast" to the depths of the <a href="/wiki/River_Ness" title="River Ness">River Ness</a> after it had killed a Pict and then tried to attack Columba's disciple, Lugne (see Vita Columbae Book 2 below). He visited the <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> King <a href="/wiki/Bridei_I_of_the_Picts" class="mw-redirect" title="Bridei I of the Picts">Bridei</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Fortriu" title="Fortriu">Fortriu</a>, at his base in <a href="/wiki/Inverness" title="Inverness">Inverness</a>, winning Bridei's respect, although not his conversion.  He subsequently played a major role in the politics of the country.  
</p><p>He was also very energetic in his work as a missionary, and, in addition to founding several churches in the <a href="/wiki/Hebrides" title="Hebrides">Hebrides</a>, he worked to turn his monastery at Iona into a school for missionaries.  He was a renowned <a href="/wiki/Man_of_letters" class="mw-redirect" title="Man of letters">man of letters</a>, having written several <a href="/wiki/Hymns" class="mw-redirect" title="Hymns">hymns</a> and being credited with having <a href="/wiki/Calligraphy" title="Calligraphy">transcribed</a> 300 books.  One of the few, if not the only, times he left Scotland was towards the end of his life, when he returned to Ireland to found the <a href="/wiki/Durrow_Abbey" title="Durrow Abbey">monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Durrow,_County_Offaly" title="Durrow, County Offaly">Durrow</a>.
</p><p>According to traditional sources, Columba died in Iona on Sunday, 9 June 597, and was buried by his <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> he created. However, Dr. Daniel P. Mc Carthy disputes this and assigns a date of 593 to Columba's death.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The Annals record the first raid made upon Iona in 795, with further raids occurring in 802, 806, and 825.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Columba's relics were finally removed in 849 and divided between Scotland and Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ireland">Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Ireland, the saint is commonly known as Colmcille.
</p><p>Colmcille is one of the three patron saints of Ireland, after <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Patrick</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brigid_of_Kildare" title="Brigid of Kildare">Brigid of Kildare</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-IP_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IP-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Colmcille is the patron saint of the city of <a href="/wiki/Derry" title="Derry">Derry</a>, where he founded a monastic settlement in c. 540.<sup id="cite_ref-IP_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IP-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> The name of the city in <a href="/wiki/Irish_Gaelic" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Gaelic">Irish</a> is <i>Doire Cholm Cille</i> and is derived from the native oak trees in the area and the city's association with Colmcille. The Catholic Church of Saint Colmcille's Long Tower, and the Church of Ireland St Augustine's Church both claim to stand at the spot of this original settlement. The Church of Ireland Cathedral, <a href="/wiki/St_Columb%27s_Cathedral" title="St Columb&#39;s Cathedral">St Columb's Cathedral</a>, and the largest park in the city, <a href="/wiki/St._Columb%27s_Park" title="St. Columb&#39;s Park">St. Columb's Park</a>, are named in his honour.  The Catholic Boys' Grammar School, <a href="/wiki/St_Columb%27s_College" title="St Columb&#39;s College">St Columb's College</a>, has him as Patron and namesake.
</p><p>St. Columba's National School in Drumcondra is a girls’ school named after the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Colmcille's Primary School<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> and St. Colmcille's Community School<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> are two schools in <a href="/wiki/Knocklyon" title="Knocklyon">Knocklyon</a>, Dublin, named after him, with the former having an annual day dedicated to the saint on 9 June.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The town of <a href="/wiki/Swords,_Dublin" title="Swords, Dublin">Swords, Dublin</a> was reputedly founded by Colmcille in 560 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESidwell199570_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESidwell199570-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> St Colmcille's Boys’ National School and St. Colmcille's Girls’ National School, both located in the town of Swords, are also named after the Saint as is one of the local gaelic teams, Naomh Colmcille.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Columba Press, a religious and spiritual book company based in Dublin, is named after Colmcille.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Aer_Lingus" title="Aer Lingus">Aer Lingus</a>, Ireland's national <a href="/wiki/Flag_carrier" title="Flag carrier">flag carrier</a> has named one of its <a href="/wiki/Airbus_A330" title="Airbus A330">Airbus A330</a> aircraft in commemoration of the saint (reg: EI-DUO).<sup id="cite_ref-IP_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IP-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Scotland_2">Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Columba is credited as being a leading figure in the revitalisation of <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monasticism</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Clan_MacCallum" class="mw-redirect" title="Clan MacCallum">Clan Malcolm/Clan McCallum</a> claims its name from Columba and was reputedly founded by the descendants of his original followers.<sup id="cite_ref-IP_25-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-IP-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> It is also said that <a href="/wiki/Clan_Robertson" title="Clan Robertson">Clan Robertson</a> Clan Donnachaidh / Duncan are heirs of Columba. <a href="/wiki/Clan_MacKinnon" class="mw-redirect" title="Clan MacKinnon">Clan MacKinnon</a> may also have some claim to being spiritual descendants of St Columba as after he founded his monastery on Isle <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>, the MacKinnons were the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbots</a> of the Church for centuries. Sir <a href="/wiki/Iain_Moncreiffe_of_that_Ilk" class="mw-redirect" title="Iain Moncreiffe of that Ilk">Iain Moncreiffe of that Ilk</a> speculated that Clan MacKinnon belonged to the kindred of Saint Columba, noting the MacKinnon <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_Arms" class="mw-redirect" title="Coat of Arms">Arms</a> bore the hand of the saint holding the <a href="/wiki/Cross" title="Cross">Cross</a>, and the several Mackinnon <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbots</a> of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The cathedral of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Argyll_and_the_Isles" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Argyll and the Isles">Catholic Diocese of Argyll and the Isles</a> is placed under the patronage of Saint Columba,<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> as are numerous <a href="/wiki/Catholic_school" title="Catholic school">Catholic schools</a> and parishes throughout the nation. The <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Episcopal_Church" title="Scottish Episcopal Church">Scottish Episcopal Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Scotland" title="Church of Scotland">Church of Scotland</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
and the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_of_England" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church of England">Evangelical Lutheran Church of England</a><sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> also have parishes dedicated to him. The village of <a href="/wiki/Kilmacolm" title="Kilmacolm">Kilmacolm</a> in <a href="/wiki/Renfrewshire_(historic)" title="Renfrewshire (historic)">Renfrewshire</a> is also derived from Colmcille's name.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St Columba's Hospice, a prominent hospice in <a href="/wiki/Edinburgh" title="Edinburgh">Edinburgh</a>, is named after the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Poetry">Poetry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Poetry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Columba currently has two poems attributed to him: "Adiutor Laborantium" and "<a href="/wiki/Altus_Prosator" class="mw-redirect" title="Altus Prosator">Altus Prosator</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-ailb_Help_39-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ailb_Help-39">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Both poems are examples of <a href="/wiki/Abecedarian_hymn" title="Abecedarian hymn">Abecedarian hymns</a> in Latin written while Columba was at the <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Iona Abbey</a>.
</p><p>The shorter of the two poems, "Adiutor Laborantium" consists of twenty-seven lines of eight syllables each, with each line following the format of an <a href="/wiki/Abecedarian_hymn" title="Abecedarian hymn">Abecedarian hymn</a> using the <a href="/wiki/Classical_Latin_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Classical Latin alphabet">Classical Latin alphabet</a> save for lines 10–11 and 25–27. The content of the poem addresses God as a helper, ruler, guard, defender and lifter for those who are good and an enemy of sinners whom he will punish.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEClancyGilbert199573_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEClancyGilbert199573-40">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"<a href="/wiki/Altus_Prosator" class="mw-redirect" title="Altus Prosator">Altus Prosator</a>" consists of twenty-three stanzas sixteen syllables long, with the first containing seven lines and six lines in each subsequent stanza. It uses the same format and alphabet as "Adiutor Laborantium" except with each stanza starting with a different letter rather than each line. The poem tells a story over three parts split into the beginning of time, history of Creation, and the Apocalypse or end of time.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other">Other</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Other">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>As of 2011, Canadians who are of <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Canadian" class="mw-redirect" title="Scottish Canadian">Scottish</a> ancestry are the third largest <a href="/wiki/Ethnic_group" title="Ethnic group">ethnic group</a> in the country and thus Columba's name is to be found attached to Catholic, <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Canada" title="Anglican Church of Canada">Anglican</a> and <a href="/wiki/Presbyterian" class="mw-redirect" title="Presbyterian">Presbyterian</a> parishes. This is particularly the case in eastern Canada, apart from French-speaking <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Throughout the US there are numerous parishes within the <a href="/wiki/Latin_liturgical_rites" title="Latin liturgical rites">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">Episcopalian</a> denominations dedicated to Columba.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Within the <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> tradition the <a href="/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_(USA)" title="Presbyterian Church (USA)">Presbyterian Church</a> (which has its roots in Scottish Presbyterianism) also has parishes named in honour of Columba.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Columba is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Youngstown" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Youngstown">Roman Catholic Diocese of Youngstown</a>, Ohio. The Cathedral there is named for him.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Iona_College_(New_York)" class="mw-redirect" title="Iona College (New York)">Iona University</a>, a small <a href="/wiki/List_of_Roman_Catholic_universities_and_colleges_in_the_United_States" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Roman Catholic universities and colleges in the United States">Catholic liberal arts college</a> whose main campus is located in <a href="/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York" title="New Rochelle, New York">New Rochelle, New York</a>, is named after the island on which Columba established his first monastery in Scotland,<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> as is <a href="/wiki/Iona_College_(Windsor,_Ontario)" title="Iona College (Windsor, Ontario)">Iona College</a> in <a href="/wiki/Windsor,_Ontario" title="Windsor, Ontario">Windsor, Ontario</a>, <a href="/wiki/Iona_Presentation_College,_Perth" class="mw-redirect" title="Iona Presentation College, Perth">Iona Presentation College, Perth</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> and Iona College Geelong in <a href="/wiki/Charlemont,_Victoria" title="Charlemont, Victoria">Charlemont, Victoria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are at least four <a href="/wiki/Pipe_band" title="Pipe band">pipe bands</a> named for him; one each from <a href="/wiki/Tullamore" title="Tullamore">Tullamore</a>, Ireland,<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> from <a href="/wiki/Derry" title="Derry">Derry</a>, Northern Ireland,<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> from <a href="/wiki/Kearny,_New_Jersey" title="Kearny, New Jersey">Kearny, New Jersey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> and from <a href="/wiki/Cape_Cod,_Massachusetts" class="mw-redirect" title="Cape Cod, Massachusetts">Cape Cod, Massachusetts</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Columba%27s_School,_Delhi" title="St. Columba&#39;s School, Delhi">St. Columba's School</a>, one of the most prominent English-Medium schools in <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>, run by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Christian_Brothers" title="Congregation of Christian Brothers">Irish Christian Brothers</a>, is also named after the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Munich <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_Athletic_Association" title="Gaelic Athletic Association">GAA</a> is named <a href="/wiki/M%C3%BCnchen" class="mw-redirect" title="München">München</a> Colmcilles.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Columba's Feast Day, 9 June, has been designated as International Celtic Art Day. The <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Kells" title="Book of Kells">Book of Kells</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Durrow" title="Book of Durrow">Book of Durrow</a>, great medieval masterpieces of Celtic art, are associated with Columba.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benjamin Britten composed <i><a href="/wiki/A_Hymn_of_St_Columba" title="A Hymn of St Columba">A Hymn of St Columba</a></i> for choir and organ in 1962, setting a poem by the saint, on the occasion of the 1,400th anniversary of his voyage to Iona.<sup id="cite_ref-Spicer_53-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Spicer-53">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Columba is honored in the Anglican communion as well, including by the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and it's American equivalent, the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/June_9" title="June 9">9 June</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-54" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-54">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-55" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-55">&#91;54&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The main source of information about Columba's life is the <i><a href="/wiki/Life_of_Columba" title="Life of Columba">Life of Columba</a></i> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Vita Columbae</i>), a hagiography written by <a href="/wiki/Adomn%C3%A1n" title="Adomnán">Adomnán</a>, one of Columba's successors at Iona, in the style of "saint's lives" narratives that had become widespread throughout medieval Europe. Both the <i>Life of Columba</i> and <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> (672/673–735) record Columba's visit to <a href="/wiki/Bridei_I_of_the_Picts" class="mw-redirect" title="Bridei I of the Picts">Bridei</a>. Whereas Adomnán just tells us that Columba visited Bridei, Bede relates a later, perhaps Pictish tradition, whereby Columba actually converts the Pictish king. Another early source is a poem in praise of Columba, most probably commissioned by Columba's kinsman, the King of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Uí Néill">Uí Néill</a> clan. It was almost certainly written within three or four years of Columba's death and is the earliest vernacular poem in European history. It consists of 25 <a href="/wiki/Stanza" title="Stanza">stanzas</a> of four verses of seven syllables each, called the <a href="/wiki/Amra" title="Amra">Amra</a> Coluim Chille.
</p><p>Through the reputation of its venerable founder and its position as a major European centre of learning, Columba's Iona became a place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. Columba is historically revered as a warrior saint and was often invoked for victory in battle.
Some of his relics were removed in 849 and divided between <a href="/wiki/Alba" title="Alba">Alba</a> and Ireland. Relics of Columba were carried before Scottish armies in the reliquary made at Iona in the mid-8th century, called the Brecbennoch. Legend has it that the Brecbennoch was carried to the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Bannockburn" title="Battle of Bannockburn">Battle of Bannockburn</a> (24 June 1314) by the vastly outnumbered Scots army and the intercession of Columba helped them to victory. Since the 19th century the "Brecbennoch of St. Columba" has been identified with the <a href="/wiki/Monymusk_Reliquary" title="Monymusk Reliquary">Monymusk Reliquary</a>, although this is now doubted by scholars.<sup id="cite_ref-56" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-56">&#91;55&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Antiphoner" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiphoner">Antiphoner</a> of <a href="/wiki/Inchcolm_Abbey" title="Inchcolm Abbey">Inchcolm Abbey</a>, the "Iona of the East" (situated on an island in the <a href="/wiki/Firth_of_Forth" title="Firth of Forth">Firth of Forth</a>), a 14th-century prayer begins <i>O Columba spes Scotorum...</i> "O Columba, hope of the Scots".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGillespie2016133_57-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGillespie2016133-57">&#91;56&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
