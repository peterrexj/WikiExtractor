<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; <p>Saint <b>Abraham</b> (<a href="/wiki/Cyrrhus,_Syria" class="mw-redirect" title="Cyrrhus, Syria">Cyrrhus, Syria</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 350–<a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, 422) (also known as <b>Abraames</b>, <b>Abraham of Charres</b> and <b>Abraham the Apostle of Lebanon</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Greater_Syria" class="mw-redirect" title="Greater Syria">Syrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Harran" title="Harran">Harran</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abraham_of_Cyrrhus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abraham was born and educated at <a href="/wiki/Harran" title="Harran">Carrhae</a> (modern Harran) in Syria, and preached the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> in the valley of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Lebanon" title="Mount Lebanon">Mount Lebanon</a>, where he lived as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>. His life was described by <a href="/wiki/Theodoret" title="Theodoret">Theodoret of Cyr</a> (393-466 A.D.), the Bishop of Cyrrhus, who named him among the other thirty holy men and women in his book "Historia Religiosa" (Religious History).
</p><p>He spent the first part of his life in the desert of <a href="/wiki/Qinnasrin" title="Qinnasrin">Chalcis</a> where he lived an ascetic life, he tried his body by fasting and still standing and was so exhausted that could not move.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> But then he left for Lebanon as a merchant and helped the inhabitants of the village where he stayed to pay the taxes with the help of his friends. The name of the village is not known but it is believed to be <i><a href="/wiki/Aqoura" title="Aqoura">Aqura</a>- <a href="/wiki/Afqa" title="Afqa">Afka</a></i>. "It was probably located in <i>Aqura</i> near the <a href="/wiki/Abraham_River" title="Abraham River">river Adonis</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was asked by the villagers to become their tutor and he accepted providing they would build the Christian church. He stayed in this village for three years as a priest and then returned to his ascetic life as a hermit.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was later elected bishop of Harran in Mesopotamia (Carrhae), where he worked vigorously to reduce the existing abuses. He died in Constantinople in 422 after going there to consult with <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a>, although some argue that it may have instead occurred in 390 under Theodosius II's predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_I" title="Theodosius I">Theodosius I</a>. His body was transferred back to Harran, to the city of Antioch where he was buried. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 14 February.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>,
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>He was a holy solitary, who, going to preach to an idolatrous village on <a href="/wiki/Mount_Lebanon" title="Mount Lebanon">Mount Libanus</a>, overcame the persecutions of the heathens by meekness and patience. When he had narrowly escaped death from their hands, he borrowed money, wherewith to satisfy the demands of the collectors of the public taxes, for their failure in which respect they were to be cast into prison; and by this charity, he gained them all to Christ. After instructing them for three years, he left them in the care of a holy priest and returned to his desert.
</p><p>He was some time after the ordained bishop of <a href="/wiki/Carrhae" class="mw-redirect" title="Carrhae">Carres</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Mesopotamia" title="Mesopotamia">Mesopotamia</a>, which country he cleared of idolatry, dissensions, and other vices. He joined the recollection and penance of a monk with the labors of his functions, and died at <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, in 422, having been sent for to court by <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodosius the Younger">Theodosius the Younger</a>, and they're treated with the greatest honor on account of his sanctity. That emperor kept one of his mean garments, and wore it himself on certain days, out of respect.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1821154_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1821154-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
