<p><b>Saint Christopher</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἅγιος Χριστόφορος</span>, <i>Hágios Christóphoros</i>) is <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> by several <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> denominations as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> killed in the reign of the 3rd-century <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> (reigned 249–251) or alternatively under the emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximinus_Daia" class="mw-redirect" title="Maximinus Daia">Maximinus Daia</a> (reigned 308–313). There appears to be confusion due to the similarity in names "Decius" and "Daia".<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Churches and monasteries were named after him by the 7th century.
</p><p>His most famous legend tells that he carried a child, who was unknown to him, across a river before the child revealed himself as Christ. Therefore, he is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of travelers, and small images of him are often worn around the neck, on a bracelet, carried in a pocket, or placed in vehicles by Christians.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Probably the most important source of the <a href="/wiki/Historicity" title="Historicity">historicity</a> of Christophorus is a stone inscription published by <a href="/wiki/Louis_Duchesne" title="Louis Duchesne">Louis Duchesne</a> in 1878.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The copy of the stone inscription and the first publication took place on 7 April 1877 by Matthieu Paranikas in the <i>Anatolia</i> magazine in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.
The stone of the size of 2 x 1 m was found in the ruins of a church in the ancient <a href="/wiki/Chalcedon" title="Chalcedon">Chalcedon</a>.
The inscription bears witness to the laying of the foundation stone, the construction and the consecration of a church in the name of ‘Saint Christopher’s Martyrdom.’
The inscription also bears witness to the chronological dates from the laying of the foundation stone to the consecration of the church; the construction of this Christophorus church dates back exactly to the time of the 4th Ecumenical Council, the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>.
The inscription also mentions the names of the state ministers of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> and those church ministers who were involved in the laying of the foundation stone, the construction or the consecration of the church.
The inscription reads as follows:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>With God was laid the cornerstone of the martyrdom of Saint Christopher in the third <a href="/wiki/Indiction" title="Indiction">indiction</a> in the month of May under the Consulate of the illustrious Protogenes and Asturius under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a> and Bishop Eulalios of Chalcedon. But it was built by the venerable chamberlain Euphemidus, and the consecration took place at the end of the fifth indiction in the month of September, on the 22nd., under the consulate of the illustrious Sporacius and Herculanus.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_(33278278728).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_%2833278278728%29.jpg/220px-2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_%2833278278728%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_%2833278278728%29.jpg/330px-2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_%2833278278728%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_%2833278278728%29.jpg/440px-2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_%2833278278728%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="6000" data-file-height="4000" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:2019-01-27_Augsburg_084_Augsburger_Dom_(33278278728).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Fresco Saint Christophorus carries the Jesus child, <i>Hoher Dom Mariä Heimsuchung,</i> <a href="/wiki/Augsburg_Cathedral" title="Augsburg Cathedral">Augsburg Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>The German archaeologist Carl Maria Kaufmann writes:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The construction of this church, erected in honour of Saint Christopher, lasted from May 450 to Sept 22nd 452, where the consecration and dedication took place. The names of the mentioned personalities, the consuls, of Bishop Eulalius, are known from the history of the Fourth Ecumenical Council, which met during the construction period on the same ground to which our inscription belongs (Chalcedon, 451). Theodosius II died two months after construction began. The church inscriptions commemorate the cubicularius Euphemius, often the founder or builder as the architect or construction leader.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Not far from the Church of St Christopher, which was under construction at the time, was the Basilica of St <a href="/wiki/Euphemia" title="Euphemia">Euphemia</a>, in which the Council took place; the consuls Protogenes and Sporacius, mentioned in the stone inscription, are mentioned in the Council Acts.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This inscription attests to the veneration of Christophorus in the 5th century in Chalcedony and, consequently, the existence of Christophorus, who probably in the period of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Persecution" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Persecution">Great Persecution</a> in the 4th century suffered the martyrdom.
</p><p>Then for the year 553 a bishop of Arkadiopolis in <a href="/wiki/Lydia" title="Lydia">Lydia</a> is testified, who had taken the name Christophorus. A <a href="/wiki/Nunnery" class="mw-redirect" title="Nunnery">nunnery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Galatia" title="Galatia">Galatia</a> was consecrated to Saint Christopher around the year 600.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Epic">Epic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Epic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Epics about the life and death of Saint Christopher first appeared in Greece in the 6th century and had spread to France by the 9th century. The 11th-century bishop and poet <a href="/wiki/Walter_of_Speyer" title="Walter of Speyer">Walter of Speyer</a> gave one version, but the most popular variations originated from the 13th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the legendary account of his life Christopher was initially called <b><span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la">Reprobus</i></span></b>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was a <a href="/wiki/Canaan" title="Canaan">Canaanite</a>, 5 cubits (7.5 feet (2.3&#160;m)) tall<sup id="cite_ref-Molteni2019_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Molteni2019-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and with a fearsome face. While serving the king of Canaan, he took it into his head to go and serve "the greatest king there was". He went to the king who was reputed to be the greatest, but one day he saw the king <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">cross himself</a> at the mention of the devil. On thus learning that the king feared the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">devil</a>, he departed to look for the devil. He came across a band of marauders, one of whom declared himself to be the devil, so Christopher decided to serve him. But when he saw his new master avoid a wayside cross and found out that the devil feared Christ, he left him and enquired from people where to find Christ. He met a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> who instructed him in the Christian faith. Christopher asked him how he could serve Christ. When the hermit suggested fasting and prayer, Christopher replied that he was unable to perform that service. The hermit then suggested that because of his size and strength Christopher could serve Christ by assisting people to cross a dangerous river, where they were perishing in the attempt. The hermit promised that this service would be pleasing to Christ.
</p><p>After Christopher had performed this service for some time, a little child asked him to take him across the river. During the crossing, the river became swollen and the child seemed as heavy as lead, so much that Christopher could scarcely carry him and found himself in great difficulty. When he finally reached the other side, he said to the child: "You have put me in the greatest danger. I do not think the whole world could have been as heavy on my shoulders as you were." The child replied: "You had on your shoulders not only the whole world but Him who made it. I am Christ your king, whom you are serving by this work." The child then vanished.<sup id="cite_ref-Crawley_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crawley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Christopher later visited <a href="/wiki/Lycia" title="Lycia">Lycia</a> and there comforted the Christians who were being martyred. Brought before the local king, he refused to sacrifice to the pagan gods. The king tried to win him by riches and by sending two beautiful women to tempt him. Christopher converted the women to Christianity, as he had already converted thousands in the city. The king ordered him to be killed. Various attempts failed, but finally Christopher was beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-Crawley_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crawley-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Greek name <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language romanization"><i lang="grc-Latn">Christophoros</i></span> means "Christ bearer".
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_patronage">Veneration and patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration and patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Eastern_Orthodox_liturgy">Eastern Orthodox liturgy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Eastern Orthodox liturgy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> venerates Christopher of Lycea (or <a href="/wiki/Lycia" title="Lycia">Lycia</a>) with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_Day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast Day">Feast Day</a> on May 9. The liturgical reading and hymns refer to his imprisonment by <a href="/wiki/Decius_(emperor)" class="mw-redirect" title="Decius (emperor)">Decius</a> who tempts Christopher with <a href="/wiki/Harlots" class="mw-redirect" title="Harlots">harlots</a> before ordering his beheading.<sup id="cite_ref-GOAA_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GOAA-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Kontakion" title="Kontakion">Kontakion in the Fourth Tone</a> (hymn) reads:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Thou who wast terrifying both in strength and in countenance, for thy Creator's sake thou didst surrender thyself willingly to them that sought thee; for thou didst persuade both them and the women that sought to arouse in thee the fire of lust, and they followed thee in the path of martyrdom. And in torments thou didst prove to be courageous. Wherefore, we have gained thee as our great protector, O great Christopher.<sup id="cite_ref-GOAA_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GOAA-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Roman_Catholic_liturgy">Roman Catholic liturgy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Roman Catholic liturgy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> remembers him on 25 July.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> commemorated him on the same day only in private <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Masses</a>. By <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1954" title="General Roman Calendar of 1954">1954</a> his commemoration had been extended to all Masses, but it was dropped in <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">1970</a> as part of the general reorganization of the calendar of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman rite">Roman rite</a> as mandated by the <a href="/wiki/Motu_proprio" title="Motu proprio">motu proprio</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">Mysterii Paschalis</a></i>. His commemoration was described to be not of Roman tradition, in view of the relatively late date (about 1550) and limited manner in which it was accepted into the Roman calendar,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> but his feast continues to be observed locally.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Museum of Sacred Art at Saint Justine's Church (Sveta Justina) in <a href="/wiki/Rab" title="Rab">Rab</a>, <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a> claims a gold-plated <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> holds the skull of St. Christopher. According to church tradition, a bishop showed the relics from the city wall in 1075 in order to end a siege of the city by an <a href="/wiki/Italo-Norman" class="mw-redirect" title="Italo-Norman">Italo-Norman</a> army.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:112px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Christopher_Medal.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Saint_Christopher_Medal.png/110px-Saint_Christopher_Medal.png" decoding="async" width="110" height="125" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Saint_Christopher_Medal.png/165px-Saint_Christopher_Medal.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Saint_Christopher_Medal.png/220px-Saint_Christopher_Medal.png 2x" data-file-width="226" data-file-height="257" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Christopher_Medal.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A bronze St. Christopher medallion</div></div></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Medals">Medals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Medals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Devotional_medal" title="Devotional medal">Devotional medals</a> with St. Christopher's name and image are commonly worn as <a href="/wiki/Pendant" title="Pendant">pendants</a>, especially by travelers, to show devotion and as a request for his blessing. Miniature statues are frequently displayed in <a href="/wiki/Automobile" class="mw-redirect" title="Automobile">automobiles</a>. In French a widespread phrase for such medals is "Regarde St Christophe et va-t-en rassuré" ("Look at St Christopher and go on reassured", sometimes translated as "Behold St Christopher and go your way in safety"); Saint Christopher medals and <a href="/wiki/Holy_card" title="Holy card">holy cards</a> in Spanish have the phrase "Si en San Cristóbal confías, de accidente no morirás" ("If you trust St. Christopher, you won't die in an accident").<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="General_patronage">General patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: General patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>St. Christopher is a widely popular saint, especially revered by athletes, <a href="/wiki/Sailor" title="Sailor">mariners</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ferry" title="Ferry">ferrymen</a>, and travelers.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He is revered as one of the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>. He holds patronage of things related to travel and travelers—against <a href="/wiki/Lightning" title="Lightning">lightning</a> and <a href="/wiki/Infectious_disease" class="mw-redirect" title="Infectious disease">pestilence</a>—and patronage for <a href="/wiki/Archery" title="Archery">archers</a>; <a href="/wiki/Bachelor" title="Bachelor">bachelors</a>; boatmen; soldiers; <a href="/wiki/Bookbinder" class="mw-redirect" title="Bookbinder">bookbinders</a>; <a href="/wiki/Epilepsy" title="Epilepsy">epilepsy</a>; floods; fruit dealers; <a href="/wiki/Fulling" title="Fulling">fullers</a>; <a href="/wiki/Gardener" title="Gardener">gardeners</a>; a holy death; mariners; market carriers; motorists and drivers; sailors; storms; surfers;<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Toothache" title="Toothache">toothache</a>; mountaineering; and transportation workers.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Christopher_icon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/Christopher_icon.jpg/170px-Christopher_icon.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="418" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/eb/Christopher_icon.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="187" data-file-height="460" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Christopher_icon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>In Eastern icons, Saint Christopher is sometimes represented with the <a href="/wiki/Cynocephaly" title="Cynocephaly">head of a dog</a>.</div></div></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage_of_places">Patronage of places</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Patronage of places">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Christopher is the patron saint of many places, including: <a href="/wiki/Baden" title="Baden">Baden, Germany</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Barga,_Tuscany" title="Barga, Tuscany">Barga, Italy</a>; <a href="/wiki/Braunschweig" title="Braunschweig">Brunswick, Germany</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Mecklenburg" title="Mecklenburg">Mecklenburg, Germany</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_9-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Rab" title="Rab">Rab, Croatia</a>; <a href="/wiki/Roermond" title="Roermond">Roermond, the Netherlands</a>; Saint Christopher's Island (<a href="/wiki/Saint_Kitts" title="Saint Kitts">Saint Kitts</a>); <a href="/wiki/Toses" title="Toses">Toses, Catalonia, Spain</a>; <a href="/wiki/Mondim_de_Basto" title="Mondim de Basto">Mondim de Basto, Portugal</a>; <a href="/wiki/Agrinio" title="Agrinio">Agrinio, Greece</a>; <a href="/wiki/Vilnius" title="Vilnius">Vilnius, Lithuania</a>; <a href="/wiki/Riga" title="Riga">Riga, Latvia</a>; <a href="/wiki/Havana" title="Havana">Havana, Cuba</a>; <a href="/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal,_Dominican_Republic" title="San Cristóbal, Dominican Republic">San Cristóbal, Dominican Republic</a>; <a href="/wiki/Paete" title="Paete">Paete, Laguna, Philippines</a>; and <a href="/wiki/Tivim" title="Tivim">Tivim, Goa, India</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Depictions_in_art">Depictions in art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Depictions in art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Because St. Christopher offered protection to travelers and against sudden death, many churches placed images or statues of him, usually opposite the south door, so he could be easily seen.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> He is usually depicted as a giant, with a child on his shoulder and a staff in one hand.<sup id="cite_ref-magill_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-magill-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> In England, there are more wall paintings of St. Christopher than of any other saint;<sup id="cite_ref-butler_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> in 1904, Mrs. Collier, writing for the <a href="/wiki/British_Archaeological_Association" title="British Archaeological Association">British Archaeological Association</a>, reported 183 paintings, statues, and other representations of the saint, outnumbering all others except for the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-collier_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collier-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, certain icons covertly identify Saint Christopher with the head of a dog. Such images may carry echoes of the Egyptian dog-headed god, <a href="/wiki/Anubis" title="Anubis">Anubis</a>. Christopher pictured with a dog's head is not generally supported by the Orthodox Church, as the icon was proscribed in the 18th century by Moscow.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The roots of that iconography lie in a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographic narrative</a> set during the reign of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a>, which tell of a man named <i>Reprebus</i>, <i>Rebrebus</i> or <i>Reprobus</i> (the "reprobate" or "scoundrel") being captured by Roman forces fighting against tribes dwelling to the west of <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cyrenaica" title="Cyrenaica">Cyrenaica</a> and forced to join the Roman <i>numerus Marmaritarum</i> or "Unit of the Marmaritae", which suggests an otherwise-unidentified "Marmaritae" (perhaps the same as the Marmaricae <a href="/wiki/Berbers" title="Berbers">Berber</a> tribe of <a href="/wiki/Cyrenaica" title="Cyrenaica">Cyrenaica</a>).  He was reported to be of enormous size, with the head of a dog instead of a man, both apparently being typical of the Marmaritae. He and the unit were later transferred to <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Syrian Antioch</a>, where bishop Peter of Attalia baptised him and where he was martyred in 308.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> It has also been speculated that this Byzantine depiction of St. Christopher as dog-headed may have resulted from a misreading of the Latin term <i>Cananeus</i> (<a href="/wiki/Canaan" title="Canaan">Canaanite</a>) as <i>caninus</i>, that is, "canine".<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Roman writer <a href="/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder" title="Pliny the Elder">Pliny the Elder</a> also reported that the “Cynamolgi (or Cynocephali), of ‘Ethiopia’ were men with the heads of dogs.” Pliny’s work was a well respected compendium of Roman science - <a href="/wiki/Natural_History_(Pliny)" title="Natural History (Pliny)">The Natural Historica</a>-  during the first century A.D. In it, there is reported accepted “knowledge” about people from the area of Western Egypt (<a href="/wiki/Cyrenaica" title="Cyrenaica">Cyrenaica</a>). Pliny notes that these “dog-headed men” resided in “Ethiopia” -a name used to encapsulate areas of Africa West and South of Alexandrian Egypt by contemporary Romans. <sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> It’s more likely that the iconography roots lie in a narrative of a “Rebrebus/Rebrebus/ or Reprobus”  captured out of “West Egypt” (a <a href="/wiki/Cynocephaly" title="Cynocephaly">Cynocephali</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cyrenaica" title="Cyrenaica">Cyrenaica</a>) and matching the current cultural belief that men (tall, strong, reprobates) from that area simply had dog heads.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the medieval Irish Passion of St. Christopher, "This Christopher was one of the Dog-heads, a race that had the heads of dogs and ate human flesh."<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> It was commonly accepted at the time that there were several types of races, the Cynocephalus, or dog-headed people, being one of many believed to populate the world.
</p><p>The German bishop and poet <a href="/wiki/Walter_of_Speyer" title="Walter of Speyer">Walter of Speyer</a> portrayed St. Christopher as a giant of a cynocephalic species in the land of the Chananeans who ate human flesh and barked. Eventually, Christopher met the Christ child, regretted his former behavior, and received baptism. He, too, was rewarded with a human appearance, whereupon he devoted his life to Christian service and became an <a href="/wiki/Athlete_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Athlete of God">athlete of God</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Soldier_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Soldier saint">soldier saints</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Christopher.27s_.22gigantic_tooth.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Christopher's_&quot;gigantic_tooth&quot;">St. Christopher's "gigantic tooth"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: St. Christopher&#039;s &quot;gigantic tooth&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the Late Middle Ages, a claimed large tooth of St. Christopher was delivered to the church in <a href="/wiki/Vercelli" title="Vercelli">Vercelli</a>. Pilgrims came from all over Europe to look at this relic until the end of 18th century when a naturalist determined it was a tooth of <a href="/wiki/Hippopotamus" title="Hippopotamus">hippopotamus</a>. Since then, the tooth has been removed from the altar and forbidden to be venerated.<sup id="cite_ref-Molteni2019_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Molteni2019-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<ul><li><i>See <a href="/wiki/Saint_Christopher_in_popular_culture" title="Saint Christopher in popular culture">Saint Christopher in popular culture</a></i></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Honours">Honours</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Christopher&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Honours">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Numerous places are named for the saint, including <a href="/wiki/Saint_Christopher_Island" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Christopher Island">Saint Christopher Island</a>, the official name of the Caribbean island of Saint Kitts, and <a href="/wiki/St._Christopher_Island" title="St. Christopher Island">St. Christopher Island</a> in <a href="/wiki/Antarctica" title="Antarctica">Antarctica</a>.  Many places are named after the saint in other languages, including <a href="/wiki/Saint-Christophe_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint-Christophe (disambiguation)">Saint-Christophe</a>, a common place name, particularly in France; <a href="/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="San Cristóbal (disambiguation)">San Cristóbal</a>; and <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Crist%C3%B3v%C3%A3o_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="São Cristóvão (disambiguation)">São Cristóvão</a>.
</p>
