</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:132px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg/130px-Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg" decoding="async" width="130" height="329" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg/195px-Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg/260px-Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="711" data-file-height="1800" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gunther_of_Bohemia.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gunther of Bohemia – tombstone effigy on the wall of main church of Břevnov Monastery (now in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a>)</div></div></div>
<p><b>Gunther</b> (<a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Vintíř</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Günther</i>; <a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Günter</i>; c. 955&#8211;1045) was a <a href="/wiki/German_people" class="mw-redirect" title="German people">German</a> (<a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavarian</a>) Catholic <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diplomat" title="Diplomat">diplomat</a>, who had quite important role in early history of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary" title="Kingdom of Hungary">Hungary</a> and especially <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Bohemia" title="Duchy of Bohemia">Bohemia</a>, thanks to his good personal relationships to the rulers of those countries. In Czech, German and Hungarian settings is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> although he never had been officially <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gunther_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gunther was born in around 955 to a noble family, related to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperors" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Emperors">imperial</a> <a href="/wiki/Ottonian_dynasty" title="Ottonian dynasty">Ottonian dynasty</a> as well as with the Hungarian <a href="/wiki/Arpads" class="mw-redirect" title="Arpads">Arpads</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-niederalteich_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-niederalteich-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was a <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Thuringia" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Thuringia">Duke of Thuringia</a>, and is numbered among the ancestors of the princely house of <a href="/wiki/House_of_Schwarzburg" title="House of Schwarzburg">Schwarzburg</a>. He spent the early part of his life at court in the midst of worldly pleasures and ambitious intrigues.<sup id="cite_ref-besse_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-besse-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was converted in 1005 at the age of fifty by <a href="/wiki/Gotthard_of_Hildesheim" title="Gotthard of Hildesheim">Gotthard of Hildesheim</a>, Abbot of <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld" class="mw-redirect" title="Hersfeld">Hersfeld</a>, later <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hildesheim" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Hildesheim">Bishop of Hildesheim</a>, and resolved to embrace the monastic life in order to do penance for his past faults. With the consent of his heirs, he bequeathed all his goods to the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Hersfeld" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Hersfeld">Abbey of Hersfeld</a>, reserving the right to richly endow and maintain the monastery of <a href="/wiki/G%C3%B6llingen" title="Göllingen">Göllingen</a>, the ownership of which he persisted in retaining despite all the efforts of Gotthard to prevent him. In 1006, the novice made a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and in the following year made his vows as lay brother in the <a href="/wiki/Niederaltaich_Abbey" title="Niederaltaich Abbey">monastery of Niederaltaich</a> before the holy Abbot Gotthard.<sup id="cite_ref-besse_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-besse-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Gunther helped to bring civilization to the region by clearing the forests and planting fields.<sup id="cite_ref-niederalteich_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-niederalteich-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon afterwards, Gunther urgently entreated to be allowed to govern his monastery of Göllingen, and Gotthard's remonstrances could not turn him aside from his purpose. Shortly after his elevation to the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbacy</a>, the former lay brother fell ill, and as he could not agree with his <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a>, the affairs of the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> were soon in a perilous condition. By his charitable counsels mingled with severe reprimands, Gotthard succeeded in dispelling the ambitious views of Gunther, who returned once more to his humble condition at Niederaltaich, and led an edifying life.<sup id="cite_ref-besse_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-besse-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1008, he withdrew to a wild, steep place near <a href="/wiki/Lalling" title="Lalling">Lalling</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Bavarian_Forest" title="Bavarian Forest">Bavarian Forest</a>, to live there as a hermit. In 1011, he penetrated farther north in the forest with several companions and settled at <a href="/wiki/Rinchnach" title="Rinchnach">Rinchnach</a>, where he built cells and a church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_the_Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John the Baptist">St. John Baptist</a>. Here he lived for thirty-four years a life of the greatest poverty and <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification</a>. The very water was measured out to the brothers, guests alone being free to use at as they would.
</p><p>It is said that Gunther received the gift of infused knowledge and became a powerful preacher though deficient in ordinary ecclesiastical learning: he could probably neither read nor write.<sup id="cite_ref-hungarian_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hungarian-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Wolfher, his biographer, relates that he knew him intimately, and often heard his admirable sermons on his patron, St John the Baptist—<a href="/wiki/Sermons" class="mw-redirect" title="Sermons">sermons</a> which drew tears from all who heard them.
</p><p>Gunther paid many visits to his relative, Hungarian king, obtained from him large sums for the poor, and urged him to build a number of <a href="/wiki/Church_(building)" title="Church (building)">churches</a> and monasteries. <a href="/wiki/Jean_Mabillon" title="Jean Mabillon">Mabillon</a> has reproduced the deed of donation made by King <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen_of_Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Stephen of Hungary">Stephen</a> on 6 June 1009. In 1029 <a href="/wiki/Conrad_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Conrad II, Holy Roman Emperor">Conrad II</a> richly endowed the monastery of Rinchnach, and in 1040 <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry III">Emperor Henry III</a> affiliated it with <a href="/wiki/Niederaltaich_Abbey" title="Niederaltaich Abbey">Niederaltaich Abbey</a>.
</p><p>In his old age, Gunther was highly respected by the rulers of <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a> (Germany), Bohemia, Hungary and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_(1025%E2%80%931385)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Poland (1025–1385)">Poland</a>. This allowed him to successfully negotiate between the Bohemian and German factions in the conflict
Duke <a href="/wiki/Bretislaus_I_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bretislaus I of Bohemia">Bretislaus I of Bohemia</a> and the German king <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry III</a> in 1040. Gunther was likely present at the German defeat in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_at_Br%C5%AFdek" title="Battle at Brůdek">Battle at Brůdek</a> on 22/23 August 1040, and he was the leader of the delegation negotiating with Bretislaus <a href="/wiki/Battle_at_Chlumec_(1040)" title="Battle at Chlumec (1040)">at Chlumec</a> in the aftermath of the battle.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gunther died at about the age of ninety in the hermitage on Březník mountain (in today's <a href="/wiki/Pr%C3%A1%C5%A1ily" title="Prášily">Prášily</a> municipality, near <a href="/wiki/Hartmanice_(Klatovy_District)" title="Hartmanice (Klatovy District)">Hartmanice</a>, southwestern Bohemia) on 9 October 1045, in the arms of Duke Bretislaus and of <a href="/w/index.php?title=%C5%A0eb%C3%AD%C5%99&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Šebíř (page does not exist)">Šebíř</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Prague">Bishop of Prague</a>. He was buried in <a href="/wiki/B%C5%99evnov_Monastery" title="Břevnov Monastery">Břevnov Monastery</a> but his remains were destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Hussites" title="Hussites">Hussites</a> in 1420.<sup id="cite_ref-hungarian_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hungarian-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gunther_of_Bohemia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_(Hartmanice)_-_okres_Klatovy_(024).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_%28Hartmanice%29_-_okres_Klatovy_%28024%29.jpg/220px-Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_%28Hartmanice%29_-_okres_Klatovy_%28024%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_%28Hartmanice%29_-_okres_Klatovy_%28024%29.jpg/330px-Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_%28Hartmanice%29_-_okres_Klatovy_%28024%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_%28Hartmanice%29_-_okres_Klatovy_%28024%29.jpg/440px-Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_%28Hartmanice%29_-_okres_Klatovy_%28024%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5184" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Dobr%C3%A1_Voda_(Hartmanice)_-_okres_Klatovy_(024).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Church of St. Gunther in Hartmanice-Dobrá Voda</div></div></div>
<p>St. Gunther's Way (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Gunthersteig</i>; <a href="/wiki/Czech_language" title="Czech language">Czech</a>: <i lang="cs">Vintířova stezka</i>) is a walking holiday route that runs from <a href="/wiki/Niederalteich_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Niederalteich Abbey">Niederalteich Abbey</a> on the Danube via Rinchnach and the <a href="/wiki/Guntherstein" title="Guntherstein">Guntherstein</a> into Bohemia.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Departing from the Niederalteich Monastery on the Danube, the path goes deep into the Bavarian Forest and across the <a href="/wiki/Czech_Republic" title="Czech Republic">Czech</a> border to hamlet Dobrá Voda (now part of town Hartmanice).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In that hamlet stands the only church in the world dedicated to this person, the Church of Saint Gunther, built in the years of 1706–34 and carefully renovated to 1995, on the occasion of 950 years since the saint's death. During the reconstruction, interior of the church was newly created by Czech artist Vladimíra Tesařová in a unique way: altar, sacrificial table, <a href="/wiki/Lectern" title="Lectern">lectern</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nativity_scene" title="Nativity scene">crèche</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stations_of_the_Cross" title="Stations of the Cross">Stations of the Cross</a> and the statue of Gunther himself are all cast from <a href="/wiki/Glass" title="Glass">glass</a>.
</p><p>Also in Hungary can be found a reminder of Gunther: one of the two main churches in the town <a href="/wiki/M%C3%B3r" title="Mór">Mór</a>, the Church of Holy Trinity, has a <a href="/wiki/Baroque_Art" class="mw-redirect" title="Baroque Art">Baroque</a> Gunther's statue (from 1744) in a niche on the right side of its portal facade.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
