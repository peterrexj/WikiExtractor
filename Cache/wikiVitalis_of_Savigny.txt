<p><b>Vitalis of Savigny</b> (ca. 1060 – 16 September 1122) was the <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> founder of <a href="/wiki/Savigny_Abbey" title="Savigny Abbey">Savigny Abbey</a> and of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Savigny" title="Congregation of Savigny">Congregation of Savigny</a> (1112).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Savigny&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_work_as_chaplan">Early life and work as chaplan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Savigny&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life and work as chaplan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tierceville" title="Tierceville">Tierceville</a> near <a href="/wiki/Bayeux" title="Bayeux">Bayeux</a> about 1060–65 in a family not belonging to the nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201448_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201448-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents were Regefredus (Rainfredus) and Rohardis (Rohes) and he had at least one sister, Adeline, and a brother called Osbert who later also became a monk. We know nothing of his early years; it is possible that he first went to the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Grestain" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Grestain">monastery of Grestain</a>, the family monastery of the local noble family of Conteville, before possibly studying in Bayeux or Liège.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201411_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201411-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After ordination he pursued advanced studies before becoming <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> to Duke <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror</a>'s brother, <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Mortain" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert of Mortain">Robert of Mortain</a> (died 1090).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Vita</i> (biography) of Vitalis tells that Robert was beating his wife, but Vitalis intervened and threatened to end the marriage if Robert did not repent. In another entry, Vitalis leaves Robert's service abruptly, and after being escorted back to him, Robert begs for Vitalis' pardon for his actions.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Vitalis gained the respect and confidence of Robert, who bestowed upon him a <a href="/wiki/Canonry" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonry">canonry</a> in the collegiate church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Evroul" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Evroul">Saint Evroul</a> at <a href="/wiki/Mortain" title="Mortain">Mortain</a>, which he had founded in 1082.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital,_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital%2C_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg/250px-Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital%2C_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="572" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital%2C_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg/375px-Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital%2C_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital%2C_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg/500px-Rouleau_fun%C3%A9raire_du_bienheureux_Vital%2C_abb%C3%A9_de_Savigny._-_Archives_Nationales_-_AE-II-138.jpg 2x" data-file-width="867" data-file-height="1982" /></a><figcaption>Mortuary roll of Vitalis of Savigny</figcaption></figure>
<h3><span id="Hermit.2C_itinerant_preacher_and_abbot_of_Savigny"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Hermit,_itinerant_preacher_and_abbot_of_Savigny">Hermit, itinerant preacher and abbot of Savigny</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Savigny&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Hermit, itinerant preacher and abbot of Savigny">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Vitalis felt a desire for a more perfect state of life. He gave up his canonry in 1095, settled at <a href="/wiki/Dompierre,_Orne" title="Dompierre, Orne">Dompierre</a>, 19 miles east of Mortain, and became one of the leaders of the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> colony of the forest of <a href="/wiki/Craon,_Mayenne" title="Craon, Mayenne">Craon</a> together with <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Thiron" title="Bernard of Thiron">Bernard of Thiron</a> and <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Arbrissel" title="Robert of Arbrissel">Robert of Arbrissel</a>. Here for seventeen years he lived an ascetic life, and was called Vital le Vieux ("Vitalis the Old") taken from his father's name. At the same time he concerned himself, like his mentor Robert of Arbrissel, with the salvation of the surrounding population, giving practical help to the outcasts who gathered round him.<sup id="cite_ref-Webster_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webster-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It is further known that he helped prostitutes into lawful marriages and that he showed special compassion to prisoners.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201449_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201449-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was also an itinerant preacher, remarkable for zeal, insensible to fatigue, and fearlessly outspoken; he is said to have attempted to reconcile <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry I of England</a> with his brother, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Curthose" title="Robert Curthose">Robert Curthose</a>. He seems to have visited England and a considerable part of western France, but Normandy was the chief scene of his labours. Between 1105 and 1120 he founded a monastery of nuns, <a href="/wiki/Abbaye_Blanche" title="Abbaye Blanche">Abbaye Blanche</a>, at Mortain, with his sister Adeline—later canonized—as abbess.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Between 1112 and 1122 Vitalis was abbot of the newly founded <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Savigny" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Savigny">abbey of Savigny</a> whose protection was guaranteed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Callixtus_II" title="Pope Callixtus II">Pope Calixtus II</a> in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a> in September 1119.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShopkow202129_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShopkow202129-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Vitalis died at Savigny, on 16 September 1122. At the time of his death, he was abbot of 140 religious, both men and women and some members likely from aristocratic families.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201415_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201415-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Although Vitalis was recognised as a saint some time after his death by the local population, a request for formal canonisation in 1244 had no success and thus Vitalis was canonised only in 1738 by the Cistercian General Chapter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShopkow202130_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShopkow202130-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Vitalis_of_Savigny&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Much about Vitalis is known from the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a> written 50 years after his death by <a href="/wiki/Stephen_de_Foug%C3%A8res" title="Stephen de Fougères">Stephen de Fougères</a> who had been, like Vitalis himself, a canon at the Church of Saint-Evroult in <a href="/wiki/Mortain" title="Mortain">Mortain</a> before becoming <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rennes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rennes">bishop of Rennes</a>. Stephen wrote in his prologue to this <i>vita</i> that his sources include vernacular writing, recollection of trustworthy men as well as Vitalis' <a href="/wiki/Mortuary_roll" title="Mortuary roll">mortuary roll</a>, particularly the cover letter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201443–44_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeissO&#39;BrienPepin201443–44-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> This mortuary roll has been preserved more or less intact (although the cover letter has been lost), and is more than 9 meters long (see picture on the right).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShopkow202130_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShopkow202130-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Other sources include <a href="/wiki/Orderic_Vitalis" title="Orderic Vitalis">Orderic Vitalis</a>, the biography of <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Thiron" title="Bernard of Thiron">Bernard of Tiron</a> (written around 1147) as well as a treatise written by <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Torigni" title="Robert of Torigni">Robert of Torigni</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEShopkow202115–17_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEShopkow202115–17-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
