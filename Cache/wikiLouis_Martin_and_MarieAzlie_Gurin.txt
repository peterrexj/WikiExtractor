<p><b>Louis Martin</b> (22 August 1823 – 29 July 1894) and <b>Azélie-Marie ("Zélie") Guérin Martin</b> (23 December 1831 – 28 August 1877) were a French Roman Catholic couple and the parents of five nuns, including <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_of_Lisieux" title="Thérèse of Lisieux">Thérèse of Lisieux</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as a saint of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in 1925 and <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9onie_Martin" title="Léonie Martin">Léonie Martin</a> declared "<a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>" in 2015. In 2015, the couple were also canonized as saints, becoming the first spouses in the church's history to be canonized as a couple.<sup id="cite_ref-osvziegler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-osvziegler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_Martin_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Louis_Martin">Louis Martin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_Martin_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Louis Martin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Louis Joseph Aloys Stanislaus Martin was the third of five children of Pierre-François Martin and Marie-Anne-Fanny Boureau.<sup id="cite_ref-biocarm_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biocarm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> All his siblings died before reaching age 30.
</p><p>Although Louis intended to become a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, wishing to enter the <a href="/wiki/Augustinian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustinian Order">Augustinian</a> <a href="/wiki/Great_St_Bernard_Hospice" title="Great St Bernard Hospice">Great St. Bernard Monastery</a>, he was rejected because he did not succeed at learning Latin.<sup id="cite_ref-biocarm_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biocarm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Later he decided to become a <a href="/wiki/Watchmaker" title="Watchmaker">watchmaker</a> and studied his craft in <a href="/wiki/Rennes" title="Rennes">Rennes</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Strasbourg" title="Strasbourg">Strasbourg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-biocarm_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biocarm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-louisreliable_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-louisreliable-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Az.C3.A9lie-Marie_Gu.C3.A9rin"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Azélie-Marie_Guérin">Azélie-Marie Guérin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_Martin_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Azélie-Marie Guérin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>Azélie-Marie Guérin was born in Gandelain, near <a href="/wiki/Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon" title="Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon">Saint-Denis-sur-Sarthon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Orne" title="Orne">Orne</a>, France. She was the second daughter of Isidore Guérin and Louise-Jeanne Macé.<sup id="cite_ref-zeliemodern_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zeliemodern-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She had an older sister, Marie-Louise, who became a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Visitation_of_Holy_Mary" title="Order of the Visitation of Holy Mary">Visitandine</a> nun, and a younger brother, Isidore, who was a <a href="/wiki/Pharmacist" title="Pharmacist">pharmacist</a>. Her maternal family was from the Madré, in the neighbouring department of Mayenne, where her grandfather, Louis Macé, was baptised on 16 March 1778.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Zélie wanted to become a nun, but was turned away by the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Sisters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul</a> due to respiratory difficulties and recurrent headaches. She then prayed for God to give her many children and that they would be consecrated to God. She later decided to become a lacemaker, manufacturing <a href="/wiki/Alen%C3%A7on_lace" title="Alençon lace">Alençon lace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-zeliemodern_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zeliemodern-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She fell in love with the watchmaker Louis Martin in 1858 and married him, only three months later, on 13 July 1858, at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Notre-Dame_d%27Alen%C3%A7on" title="Basilica of Notre-Dame d&#39;Alençon">Basilica of Notre-Dame</a> in <a href="/wiki/Alen%C3%A7on" title="Alençon">Alençon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-zeliemodern_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zeliemodern-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Zélie's business became so successful that, in 1870, Louis sold his watchmaking business to go into partnership with her.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_and_family">Marriage and family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_Martin_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Marriage and family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although the couple lived celibately for ten months after their wedding, they decided to <a href="/wiki/Consummate" class="mw-redirect" title="Consummate">consummate</a> their marriage after a spiritual director encouraged them to do so.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They would later have nine children, though only five daughters would survive infancy:
</p>
<ol><li><i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Marie_of_the_Sacred_Heart&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Marie of the Sacred Heart (page does not exist)">Marie Louise</a></i> (22 February 1860 – 19 January 1940), as a nun, <i>Sister Marie of the Sacred Heart</i>, Carmelite at Lisieux;</li>
<li><i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Jesus_(religious)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Agnes of Jesus (religious) (page does not exist)">Marie Pauline</a></i> (7 September 1861 – 28 July 1951), as a nun, <i>Mother Agnès of Jesus</i>, Carmelite at Lisieux;</li>
<li><i><a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9onie_Martin" title="Léonie Martin">Marie Léonie</a></i> (3 June 1863 – 16 June 1941), as a nun, <i>Sister Françoise-Thérèse</i>, Visitandine at Caen; candidate for sainthood since January 2015;</li>
<li><i>Marie Hélène</i> (3 October 1864 – 22 February 1870);</li>
<li><i>Joseph Louis</i> (20 September 1866 – 14 February 1867);</li>
<li><i>Joseph Jean-Baptiste</i> (19 December 1867 – 24 August 1868);</li>
<li><i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Genevieve_of_the_Holy_Face&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Genevieve of the Holy Face (page does not exist)">Marie Céline</a></i> (28 April 1869 – 25 February 1959), as a nun, <i>Sister Geneviève of the most Holy Face</i>, Carmelite at Lisieux;</li>
<li><i>Marie Mélanie-Thérèse</i> (16 August 1870 – 8 October 1870);</li>
<li><i><a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_de_Lisieux" class="mw-redirect" title="Thérèse de Lisieux">Marie Françoise-Thérèse</a></i> (2 January 1873 – 30 September 1897), as a nun, <i>Sister Thérèse of the Child Jesus and of the Holy Face</i>, Carmelite at Lisieux, canonised in 1925.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li></ol>
<p>As a father, Louis loved nature with a deep sentimental enthusiasm. It was from him that Thérèse inherited her passion for flowers and meadows, and for her native landscape, clouds, thunderstorms, the sea and the stars. He made pilgrimages to <a href="/wiki/Chartres" title="Chartres">Chartres</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lourdes" title="Lourdes">Lourdes</a>, went to Germany and Austria, travelled twice to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and even to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, and planned, but did not live to carry out, a pilgrimage to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-gorres41_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gorres41-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Along with this desire for adventure was an impulse towards withdrawal; in Lisieux Louis arranged a little den for himself high up in the attic, a true monastic cell for praying, reading and meditation. Even his daughters were allowed to enter it only if they wished spiritual converse and self-examination. As in a monastery, he divided the day into worship, garden work and relaxation. As a jeweller and watchmaker, Louis loved the precious things with which he dealt. To his daughters he gave touching and naïve pet names: Marie was his "diamond", Pauline his "noble pearl", Céline "the bold and fearless one", and "the guardian angel". Thérèse was his "little queen ... to whom all treasures belonged".<sup id="cite_ref-gorres42_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gorres42-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_Martin_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Zélie died of <a href="/wiki/Breast_cancer" title="Breast cancer">breast cancer</a> on 28 August 1877 in Alençon, aged 45, leaving her husband and daughters.<sup id="cite_ref-zeliemodern_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zeliemodern-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her funeral was held in the basilica where she had married Louis.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> A few weeks later, Louis sold her lacemaking business and their house along Rue St. Blaise,<sup id="cite_ref-home_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-home-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and moved to <a href="/wiki/Lisieux" title="Lisieux">Lisieux</a>, <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, where Zélie's brother Isidore Guérin, a pharmacist, lived with his wife and two daughters.
</p><p>After Zélie's death, Marie (Louise), Pauline, Thérèse (Françoise-Thérèse), and Céline all became Carmelite nuns, along with a cousin, Marie Guérin. Léonie became a Visitandine nun at <a href="/wiki/Caen" title="Caen">Caen</a> after leaving the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1889 Louis suffered two paralyzing strokes followed by <a href="/wiki/Arteriosclerosis" title="Arteriosclerosis">cerebral arteriosclerosis</a>, and was hospitalised for three years at the Bon Sauveur Asylum in Caen. In 1892 he returned to Lisieux, where two of his daughters looked after him until his death on 29 July 1894 at Chateau La Musse near <a href="/wiki/%C3%89vreux" title="Évreux">Évreux</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Louis_Martin_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Patrick_Church_(Columbus,_Ohio)_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin,_detail.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Saint_Patrick_Church_%28Columbus%2C_Ohio%29_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin%2C_detail.jpg/200px-Saint_Patrick_Church_%28Columbus%2C_Ohio%29_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin%2C_detail.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="301" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Saint_Patrick_Church_%28Columbus%2C_Ohio%29_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin%2C_detail.jpg/300px-Saint_Patrick_Church_%28Columbus%2C_Ohio%29_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin%2C_detail.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4d/Saint_Patrick_Church_%28Columbus%2C_Ohio%29_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin%2C_detail.jpg/400px-Saint_Patrick_Church_%28Columbus%2C_Ohio%29_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin%2C_detail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2848" data-file-height="4288" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Patrick_Church_(Columbus,_Ohio)_-_relics_of_Louis_%26_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Gu%C3%A9rin_Martin,_detail.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relics of Louis and Marie-Azélie Guérin Martin at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick_Church_(Columbus,_Ohio)" title="Saint Patrick Church (Columbus, Ohio)">Saint Patrick Church (Columbus, Ohio)</a></div></div></div>
<p>Louis and Zélie were declared "<a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a>" on 26 March 1994 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>. They were <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 19 October 2008 by <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Saraiva_Martins" title="José Saraiva Martins">José Saraiva Cardinal Martins</a>, the legate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se,_Lisieux" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. Thérèse, Lisieux">Basilica of Saint Thérèse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lisieux" title="Lisieux">Lisieux</a>.
</p><p>
A few months earlier, the Catholic Church had recognized the miracle  of Pietro Schilirò, an Italian child cured of lung trouble at their intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-firstmiracle_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-firstmiracle-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> For Louis and Zélie to be canonized, the Church needed to find that God worked a second miracle at their intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></p><div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg/180px-Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg/270px-Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg/360px-Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1835" data-file-height="1915" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Louis_and_Marie-Az%C3%A9lie_Martin.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Canonization tapestry in 2015.</div></div></div>
<p>On 7 January 2013, <a href="/wiki/Carlos_Osoro_Sierra" title="Carlos Osoro Sierra">Carlos Osoro Sierra</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Valencia_in_Spain" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Valencia in Spain">Archbishop of Valencia</a>, presided at the opening of the canonical process to inquire into the healing in 2008 of a girl named Carmen, who was born in Valencia four days before Louis and Zélie were beatified.<sup id="cite_ref-secondmiracle_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-secondmiracle-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-carmen_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-carmen-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Eight doctors testified that there was no scientific explanation for her cure.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The diocesan tribunal held its closing session on 21 May 2013, and the file was sent to Rome for review by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-miracle_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-miracle-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-canonization_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canonization-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 3 March 2015 <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Cardinal Amato</a> announced informally that Louis and Zélie Martin would be declared saints during the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Bishops_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Bishops (Catholic)">Synod of Bishops</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The Congregation accepted and promulgated the miracle on 18 March 2015.<sup id="cite_ref-popefrancisrec_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-popefrancisrec-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 26 June 2015, <i>Miracle of Life in Valencia</i>, a film produced by the Archdiocese of Valencia about the canonization miracle was released online in English.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> The next day, at a consistory of cardinals in Rome, Pope Francis approved the decree for the canonization of Louis and Zélie Martin and announced that the ceremony would take place in October in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-popefrancisrec_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-popefrancisrec-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 18 October 2015, Louis and Azélie-Marie Martin were canonized as saints by Pope Francis.<sup id="cite_ref-canonizationmass_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-canonizationmass-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In December 2003, <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady%27s_Hospice" title="Our Lady&#39;s Hospice">Our Lady's Hospice &amp; Care Services</a> of <a href="/wiki/Harold%27s_Cross" title="Harold&#39;s Cross">Harold's Cross</a>, Dublin opened a satellite unit for specialist palliative care in Blackrock, Co. Dublin, provided through the generosity of the Louis and Zelie Martin Foundation. The Foundation was established in 1997 by developer Noel Smyth in memory of his nephew Luke, and named in honor of the Martins.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
