<p><b>Gilbert of Sempringham</b> (c. 1085 – 4 February 1189)<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Gilbertine_Order" title="Gilbertine Order">Gilbertine Order</a>, was the only Medieval <a href="/wiki/Englishman" class="mw-redirect" title="Englishman">Englishman</a> to found a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">conventual</a> order, mainly because the Abbot of <a href="/wiki/C%C3%AEteaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Cîteaux">Cîteaux</a> declined his request to assist him in organising a group of women who wanted to live as <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>, living with <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brothers</a> and sisters, in 1148.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He founded a <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a> and nuns in spite of such a foundation being contrary to canonical practice.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gilbert_of_Sempringham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gilbert was born at <a href="/wiki/Sempringham" title="Sempringham">Sempringham</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Bourne,_Lincolnshire" title="Bourne, Lincolnshire">Bourne</a> in Lincolnshire, the son of Jocelin, an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Normans" title="Anglo-Normans">Anglo-Norman</a> <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_the_manor" title="Lord of the manor">lord of the manor</a>, and an unnamed Anglo-Saxon mother. He had a brother, Roger, and a sister, Agnes.
</p><p>Unusually for that period, his father actively prevented his son from becoming a knight, instead sending him to  France, probably the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a> but possibly under <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Laon" title="Anselm of Laon">Anselm of Laon</a>, to study theology. Some physical deformity may have made him unfit for military service, making an ecclesiastical career the best option. When he returned in 1120 he became a clerk in the household of <a href="/wiki/Robert_Bloet" title="Robert Bloet">Robert Bloet</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lincoln" title="Bishop of Lincoln">Bishop of Lincoln</a>, started a school for boys and girls (the existing primary school at <a href="/wiki/Pointon" title="Pointon">Pointon</a> is still named after him) and was ordained by Robert's successor, <a href="/wiki/Alexander_of_Lincoln" title="Alexander of Lincoln">Alexander</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Offered the <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon_of_Lincoln" title="Archdeacon of Lincoln">archdeaconry of Lincoln</a>, he refused, saying that he knew no surer way to perdition.
</p><p>In the period 1115<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>-1123<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> he was given both the vacant churches of Sempringham and <a href="/wiki/West_Torrington" title="West Torrington">West Torrington</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Wragby" title="Wragby">Wragby</a>, by his father, Jocelin. In 1129 he became the Vicar of both St Andrew's, <a href="/wiki/Sempringham" title="Sempringham">Sempringham</a> and St Mary's, <a href="/wiki/West_Torrington" title="West Torrington">West Torrington</a> having been instituted by Robert Bloet, Bishop of Lincoln.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"Gilbert was a lover of truth and justice, chastity and sobriety, and a diligent cultivator of the other virtues: wherefore he was revered and praised by all and obtained their favour and regard. Even Jocelin now rejoiced in the goodness of his son, he began to cherish him with fatherly affection, and ministered to his needs out of his own riches. Gilbert would be in his late twenties when his father presented him to the vacant churches of Sempringham and West Torrington, which he had built on his own demesne 'in the custom of his country' "<sup id="cite_ref-:1_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Gilbertines">The Gilbertines</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gilbert_of_Sempringham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Gilbertines">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg/220px-St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg/330px-St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg/440px-St.Gilbert%27s_well_-_geograph.org.uk_-_528617.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="427" /></a><figcaption>St Gilbert's well at Sempringham</figcaption></figure>
<p>When his father died in 1130, Gilbert became <a href="/wiki/Lord_of_the_manor" title="Lord of the manor">lord of the manor</a> of Sempringham and West Torrington. In 1131 he founded the <a href="/wiki/Gilbertine_Order" title="Gilbertine Order">Gilbertine Order</a>, and with the help of Alexander constructed at Sempringham a dwelling and cloister for nuns, at the north of the church of St Andrew.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Eventually he had a chain of twenty-six convents, <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mission_(station)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (station)">missions</a>. A custom developed in the houses of the order called "the plate of the Lord Jesus", whereby the best portions of the dinner were put on a special plate and shared with the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In 1148 he approached the <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a> for help. They refused because he included women in his order. The male part of the order consisted of <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons Regular">Canons Regular</a>.
</p><p>In 1165 Gilbert was charged with having aided <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a> when Thomas fled from King <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II</a> after the council of Northampton, but he was eventually found innocent.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1174 some of his lay brothers revolted, but he received the backing of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_III" title="Pope Alexander III">Pope Alexander III</a>. Gilbert resigned his office late in life because of blindness and died at Sempringham in about 1190, at the claimed age of 106.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The only religious order of English origin founded during the Middle Ages, it thrived until the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Dissolution of the Monasteries">Dissolution of the Monasteries</a> under <a href="/wiki/King_Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="King Henry VIII of England">King Henry VIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gilbert_of_Sempringham&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School,_Pointon,_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School%2C_Pointon%2C_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg/220px-St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School%2C_Pointon%2C_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School%2C_Pointon%2C_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg/330px-St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School%2C_Pointon%2C_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School%2C_Pointon%2C_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg/440px-St_Gilbert_of_Sempringham_C_of_E_Primary_School%2C_Pointon%2C_Lincs_-_geograph.org.uk_-_215132.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>St Gilbert of Sempringham C of E Primary School, <a href="/wiki/Pointon" title="Pointon">Pointon</a>, Lincs</figcaption></figure>
<p>Gilbert was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> in 1202 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a>. His liturgical <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is on <a href="/wiki/February_4" title="February 4">4 February</a>, <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">commemorating</a> his death, as it remains also in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> (<a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> According to the order of <a href="/wiki/Hubert_Walter" title="Hubert Walter">Hubert Walter</a>, the bishops of England celebrated his feast, and his name was added to the wall of the church of the <a href="/wiki/Four_Crowned_Martyrs" title="Four Crowned Martyrs">Four Crowned Martyrs</a>. His Order did not outlast the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>, however; and despite being influenced by <a href="/wiki/Continental_Europe" title="Continental Europe">Continental</a> models, it did not maintain a foothold in Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> There are, however, at least three primary schools in England named after him: in <a href="/wiki/Pointon" title="Pointon">Pointon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Stamford,_Lincolnshire" title="Stamford, Lincolnshire">Stamford, Lincolnshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winton,_Greater_Manchester" title="Winton, Greater Manchester">Winton, Greater Manchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Schools_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Schools-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
