<p><b>María Antonia Bandrés Elósegui</b> (6 March 1898 – 27 April 1919) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Jesus_(Spain)" title="Daughters of Jesus (Spain)">Daughters of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She lived a brief life but was noted for her ardent faith and her Marian devotion while also being known for the effect she had on the faithful as well as agnostics whom she came into contact with.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The beatification process for the late Elósegui opened in 1982 – she was then titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> – and she later became <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 1995. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified her in mid-1996.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mar%C3%ADa_Antonia_Bandr%C3%A9s_El%C3%B3segui&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>María Antonia Bandrés Elósegui was born in <a href="/wiki/Tolosa,_Spain" title="Tolosa, Spain">Tolosa</a> – in the <a href="/wiki/Restoration_(Spain)" title="Restoration (Spain)">Kingdom of Spain</a> – as the second of fifteen children born to Ramon Bandrés and Teresa Elósegui.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her paternal uncle and godfather was Antonio Bandrés. Her siblings and parents often referred to her as "Antonita".<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bandrés Elósegui attended a school that the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Jesus_(Spain)" title="Daughters of Jesus (Spain)">Daughters of Spain</a> managed in her town and she later entered the religious life in that order in <a href="/wiki/Salamanca" title="Salamanca">Salamanca</a> on 8 December 1915 – the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Immaculate_Conception" title="Feast of the Immaculate Conception">Feast of the Immaculate Conception</a> – during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>. In 1913 while making the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">Spiritual Exercises of St. Ignatius</a> she met the Daughter's founder <a href="/wiki/Candida_Maria_of_Jesus" title="Candida Maria of Jesus">Candida Maria of Jesus</a> who said to her: "You will be a Daughter of Jesus".<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She made her solemn religious profession on 31 May 1918 and then fell ill that June, marking the start of a long illness and then death ten months later.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her confessor around this time was Ilario Oscoz.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her doctor – Filiberto Villalobos – testified that he was moved as he cared for the <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> due to her ardent spirit and her commitment to the faith. Her death impacted the agnostic doctor as well as Miguel de Unamuno and Indalecio Prieto who left either oral or written testimonies about the impact her death had on them.<sup id="cite_ref-EWTN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EWTN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She died on 27 April 1919 while singing a Marian song.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mar%C3%ADa_Antonia_Bandr%C3%A9s_El%C3%B3segui&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process commenced with the inauguration of an informative process in Salamanca that opened on 23 January 1962. This concluded the following year after her writings were checked by theologians who certified their orthodoxy, on 10 July 1970, leading to the formal introduction of her cause on 11 May 1982, which entitled her to the title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> validated these two previous processes in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 27 January 1983 and received the official <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier in 1989 for further evaluation.
</p><p>Theologians assented to the contents of the dossier on 28 June 1994 while the cardinal and bishop members of the C.C.S.did so on 10 January 1995. She became titled as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 6 April 1995 after <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> confirmed the fact that she had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The miracle required for her beatification was investigated in Spain and received full validation on 12 May 1995 from the C.C.S. before a medical team examined the miracle in question and voiced their approval of it on 11 January 1996; theologians also assented on 29 March 1996 and the C.C.S. as well on 16 April 1996. The pope issued his final approval of this on 30 April 1996 and later beatified her in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 12 May 1996.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for this cause is Ana Maria Cinco.
</p>
