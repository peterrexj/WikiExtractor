					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Ukrainian Greek Catholic priest and martyr</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Roman Lysko</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><span style="display:none">(<span class="bday">1914-08-14</span>)</span>14 August 1914<br /><a href="/wiki/Horodok,_Lviv_Oblast" title="Horodok, Lviv Oblast">Horodok</a> (Gródek/ Galicia/), <a href="/wiki/Lviv_Oblast" title="Lviv Oblast">Lviv Oblast</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia_and_Lodomeria" title="Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria">Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria</a><br /> <a href="/wiki/Austro-Hungary" class="mw-redirect" title="Austro-Hungary">Austro-Hungary</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">14 October 1949<span style="display:none">(1949-10-14)</span> (aged&#160;35)<br /><a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian SSR">Ukrainian SSR</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">27 June 2001, Ukraine by Pope John Paul II</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Roman Lysko</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Роман Лиско</span>; 14 August 1914 – 14 October 1949) was a <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic">Ukrainian Greek Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p><p>Lysko was born on 14 August 1914 in <a href="/wiki/Horodok,_Lviv_Oblast" title="Horodok, Lviv Oblast">Horodok, Lviv Oblast</a>. He studied theology and graduated from the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Catholic_University" title="Ukrainian Catholic University">Lviv Theological Academy</a>. On 28 August 1941 he was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> by <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">Metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Andrey_Sheptytsky" title="Andrey Sheptytsky">Andrey Sheptytsky</a>.  He was pastor of the Archeparchy of Lviv for Ukrainians.
</p><p>He was assigned administrator for the parish in the village of Kotliw, Oliyiv county. In 1944 Lysko was assigned to a parish in the village of Belzets, Zolochiv county. He was also a member of the underground Ukrainian youth organization Plast in his 30s and leader of the Plast group "Fox" (Lys).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lysko was also active in working with youth, together with his wife (ordaining married priests is a common practice in the Eastern Churches, since the beginning of Christianity).
</p><p>He refused to sign statement of conversion to Orthodoxy.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>On 9 September 1949, he was arrested by the <a href="/wiki/NKVD" title="NKVD">NKVD</a>. He was put into prison in Lviv.  The people of the city reported hearing him loudly sing <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a> after he was tortured.  His torturers reportedly thought he had gone insane.  He died from starvation after being <a href="/wiki/Immurement" title="Immurement">immured</a> in the prison walls.  The official date of his death was 14 October 1949. For years his family attempted to find out his fate until they were told in 1956 that he died of heart paralysis,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but some witnesses reported seeing him in prison after this date, with accounts that they had heard him singing the psalms.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It was even reported that he was sealed up in a wall, still alive, where he gave his life as martyr.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>A plaque on that building on Lonsky Avenue reads that "here, within the walls of this building, entombed alive, lies Father Roman Lysko, who gave up his life for his faith."
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 June 2001.
</p>
<h2><span id="Niece.27s_testimony"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Niece's_testimony">Niece's testimony</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Roman_Lysko&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Niece&#039;s testimony">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>"He was imprisoned on Lontskyi Street. His mother brought him some packages. Sometimes his grandmother came from Zhulychi to visit him. At first the packages were accepted. The prisoner always had the right to thank the giver with the same card with which the package was sent. These cards were always sent back; even the bags in which they usually put the packages were sent back. And there were always those cards, on which he wrote, ""Thank you. Many kisses', and signed it. After the murder of Halan [a communist agitator], they refused to accept packages. But after 6 months, when they started to accept packages again, then the relatives found a card with 'thanks' and a signature written, but in a stranger's hand. It was a completely different handwriting." – From an interview with his niece, Lidia Kupchyk.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  
</p>
