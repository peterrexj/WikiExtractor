<p><b>Benedict Joseph Labre</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Benoît-Joseph Labre</i>, 25 March 1748 – 16 April 1783) was a French <a href="/wiki/Mendicant" title="Mendicant">mendicant</a>, <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Franciscan tertiary</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. Labre was from a well to do family near Arras, France. After attempting a monastic lifestyle, he opted instead for the life of a pilgrim. He traveled to most of the major shrines of Europe, subsisting by begging. Labre is patron saint of the homeless.<sup id="cite_ref-jcu_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jcu-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Joseph_Labre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Labre was born in 1748 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Amettes" title="Amettes">Amettes</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Arras,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Arras, France">Arras</a>, in the former <a href="/wiki/Provinces_of_France" title="Provinces of France">Province</a> of <a href="/wiki/Artois" title="Artois">Artois</a> in the north of France. He was the eldest of fifteen children of a prosperous shopkeeper, Jean-Baptiste Labre, and his wife, Anne Grandsire.<sup id="cite_ref-delany_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-delany-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Labre had an uncle, a <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a>, living some distance from his family home; this uncle gladly received him, and undertook his early education for the priesthood. At the age of sixteen, he approached his uncle about becoming a <a href="/wiki/Trappist" class="mw-redirect" title="Trappist">Trappist</a> monk, but his parents told him he would have to wait until he grew older. When Benedict was about eighteen, an epidemic struck the city, and uncle and nephew busied themselves in the service of the sick. While the uncle took care of the souls and bodies of the people, Benedict went to and fro caring for the cattle. Among the last victims of the epidemic was Labre's uncle.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg/170px-Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="222" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg/255px-Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg/340px-Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1132" data-file-height="1479" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cavallucci_-_San_Benedetto_Giuseppe_Labre.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Benedict Joseph Labre depicted by <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Cavallucci" title="Antonio Cavallucci">Antonio Cavallucci</a> (1752–1795)</div></div></div>
<p>Labre set off for <a href="/wiki/La_Trappe_Abbey" title="La Trappe Abbey">La Trappe Abbey</a> to apply to the Order, but did not come up to their requirements. He was under age, he was too delicate; he had no special recommendations.<sup id="cite_ref-goodier_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goodier-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He later attempted to join the <a href="/wiki/Carthusians" title="Carthusians">Carthusians</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a>, but each order rejected him as unsuitable for communal life. He was, for about six weeks, a postulant with the Carthusians at Neuville. In November 1769 he obtained admission to the Cistercian Abbey of Sept-Fonts. After a short stay at Sept-Fonts his health gave way, and it was decided that his vocation lay elsewhere.<sup id="cite_ref-delany_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-delany-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Labre, according to Catholic tradition, experienced a desire, which he considered was given to him by God and inspired by the example of <a href="/wiki/Alexius_of_Rome" title="Alexius of Rome">Alexius of Rome</a> and that of the Franciscan tertiary pilgrim, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Roch" title="Saint Roch">Saint Roch</a>, to "abandon his country, his parents, and whatever is flattering in the world to lead a new sort of life, a life most painful, most penitential, not in a wilderness nor in a cloister, but in the midst of the world, devoutly visiting as a pilgrim the famous places of Christian devotion."<sup id="cite_ref-delany_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-delany-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Labre joined the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> and settled on a life of poverty and <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>. He first traveled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on foot, subsisting on what he could get by begging. He then traveled to most of the major shrines of <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>, often several times each.  He visited the various <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrines</a> in <a href="/wiki/Loreto_(AN)" class="mw-redirect" title="Loreto (AN)">Loreto</a>, <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bari" title="Bari">Bari</a> in Italy, <a href="/wiki/Einsiedeln,_Switzerland" class="mw-redirect" title="Einsiedeln, Switzerland">Einsiedeln</a> in <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Paray-le-Monial" title="Paray-le-Monial">Paray-le-Monial</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>. During these trips he would always travel on foot, sleeping in the open or in a corner of a room, with his clothes muddy and ragged. On one occasion he stopped at the farmhouse of Matthieu and Marie Vianney, who would later become the parents of <a href="/wiki/Cur%C3%A9_d%27Ars" class="mw-redirect" title="Curé d&#39;Ars">Curé d'Ars</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He lived on what little he was given, and often shared the little he did receive with others. He is reported to have talked rarely, prayed often, and accepted quietly the abuse he received.
</p><p>In so doing, Labre was following in the role of the mendicant, the "<a href="/wiki/Fool-for-Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Fool-for-Christ">Fool-for-Christ</a>," found more often in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Church</a>. He spent many hours in adoration of the Blessed Sacrament.<sup id="cite_ref-jcu_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jcu-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He would often swoon when contemplating the <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_thorns" title="Crown of thorns">crown of thorns</a>, in particular, and, during these states, it is said he would levitate or <a href="/wiki/Bilocation" title="Bilocation">bilocate</a>. He was also said to have cured some of the other homeless he met and to have multiplied bread for them.  In the last years of his life (his thirties), he lived in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, for a time living in the ruins of the <a href="/wiki/Colosseum" title="Colosseum">Colosseum</a>, and would leave only to make a yearly pilgrimage to the shrine of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Loreto" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of Loreto">Our Lady of Loreto</a>. He was a familiar figure in the city and known as the "saint of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Hours" class="mw-redirect" title="Forty Hours">Forty Hours</a>" (or <i>Quarant' Ore</i>) for his dedication to <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharistic</a> adoration.
</p><p>The day before he died, Labre collapsed on the steps of the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_ai_Monti" title="Santa Maria ai Monti">Santa Maria ai Monti</a>, blocks from the <a href="/wiki/Colosseum" title="Colosseum">Colosseum</a>, and despite his protestations was charitably taken to a house behind the church at Via dei Serpenti 2. He died there of malnutrition and exhaustion on 16 April 1783,<sup id="cite_ref-jcu_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jcu-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> during <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week" title="Holy Week">Holy Week</a>, and was buried in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_ai_Monti" title="Santa Maria ai Monti">Santa Maria ai Monti</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_Joseph_Labre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Labre's <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>, Marconi, wrote his biography and attributed 136 separate cures to his intercession within three months of his death. Those miracles were instrumental in the conversion of the Reverend <a href="/wiki/John_Thayer_(priest)" title="John Thayer (priest)">John Thayer</a>, the first American Protestant clergyman to convert to Catholicism, who was resident in Rome at the time of St. Benedict's death.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> A cult grew up around him very soon after his death; he was declared <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Blessed Pius IX</a> in 1860, and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1881.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Benedict is patron saint of the homeless.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is observed on April 16.
</p>
