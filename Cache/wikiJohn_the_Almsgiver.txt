<p><b>John the Merciful</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἰωάννης ὁ Ἐλεήμων</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Iōannēs ho Eleēmōn</i></span>), also known as <b>St John the Almsgiver</b>, <b>John the Almoner</b>, <b>John V of Alexandria</b>, <b>John Eleymon</b>, and <b>Johannes Eleemon</b>, was the <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Alexandria_and_all_Africa" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Alexandria and all Africa">Chalcedonian</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch of Alexandria">Patriarch of Alexandria</a> in the early 7th century (from 606 to 616) and a <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">Christian saint</a>. He is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Casarano" title="Casarano">Casarano</a>, Italy and of <a href="/wiki/Limassol" title="Limassol">Limassol</a>, Cyprus.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_the_Merciful&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born at <a href="/wiki/Amathus" title="Amathus">Amathus</a> as the son of Epiphanius, governor of <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>, and was of noble descent. In early life he was married and had children, but his wife and children soon died, after which he entered religious life.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patriarch_of_Alexandria">Patriarch of Alexandria</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_the_Merciful&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Patriarch of Alexandria">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:John_the_Merciful.PNG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/John_the_Merciful.PNG/220px-John_the_Merciful.PNG" decoding="async" width="220" height="519" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/ba/John_the_Merciful.PNG 1.5x" data-file-width="266" data-file-height="628" /></a><figcaption>John the Merciful, second half of the 15th century, <a href="/wiki/Warsaw" title="Warsaw">Warsaw</a> National Museum</figcaption></figure>
<p>On the death of the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch" title="Patriarch">Patriarch</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Theodore_I_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch Theodore I of Alexandria">Theodore</a>, the Alexandrians besought <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Phocas" title="Phocas">Phocas</a> to appoint John his successor, which was accordingly done. One of the first steps he took was to make a list of several thousand needy persons, whom he took under his especial care. He always referred to the poor as his "lords and masters", because of their mighty influence at the Court of the Most High. He assisted people of every class who were in need. 
</p><p>He was a reformer who attacked <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a>, and fought heresy by means of improvements in religious education. He also reorganized the system of weights and measures for the sake of the poor, and put a stop to corruption among the officials.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He increased the number of churches in <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> from seven to seventy.
</p><p>The ministry of <a href="/wiki/Vitalis_of_Gaza" title="Vitalis of Gaza">Vitalis of Gaza</a>, a monk who worked among the prostitutes of the city, was a noteworthy episode of John's reign. The patriarch was considered to have behaved with wisdom for not punishing this monk who was notorious for visiting the seedy part of town, and his judgment was vindicated only after the death of Vitalis when the story of the monk's mission of mercy became known.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Anecdotes_about_almsgiving">Anecdotes about almsgiving</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_the_Merciful&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Anecdotes about almsgiving">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In his youth John had had a vision of a beautiful maiden with a garland of olives on her head, who said that she was Compassion, the eldest daughter of the Great King. This had evidently made a deep impression on John's mind, and, now that he had the opportunity of exercising benevolence on a large scale, he soon became widely known all over the East for his liberality towards the poor.
</p><p>A shipwrecked merchant was thus helped three times, on the first two occasions apparently without doing him much good; the third time however, John fitted him out with a ship and a cargo of wheat, and by favourable winds he was taken as far as <a href="/wiki/Britain_in_the_Middle_Ages" title="Britain in the Middle Ages">Britain</a>, where, as there was a shortage of wheat, he obtained his own price.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Another person, who was not really in need, applied for alms and was detected by the officers of the palace; but John merely said "Give unto him; he may be Our Lord in disguise." He visited the hospitals three times every week, and he freed a great many slaves. John is said to have devoted the entire revenues of his see to the alleviation of those in need. A rich man presented him with a magnificent bed covering; he accepted it for one night, but then sold it, and disposed of the money in alms. The rich man "bought in" the article, and again presented it to John, with the same result. This was repeated several times; but John drily remarked: "We will see who tires first."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Another instance of his piety was that he caused his own grave to be dug, but only partly so, and appointed a servant to come before him on all state occasions and say "My Lord, your tomb is unfinished; pray give orders for its completion, for you know not the hour when death may seize you." When the <a href="/wiki/Sassanid_dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Sassanid dynasty">Sassanids</a> sacked <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> in 614, John sent large supplies of food, wine, and money to the fleeing <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a>. But eventually the Persians <a href="/wiki/Sassanid_conquest_of_Egypt" class="mw-redirect" title="Sassanid conquest of Egypt">occupied Alexandria</a>, and John himself in his old age was forced to flee to his native country, where he died.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_veneration">Death and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_the_Merciful&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John died in <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a> somewhere between 616 and 620.
</p><p>From Cyprus his body was moved to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, then in 1249 to <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>, where there is a church dedicated to him, the <a href="/wiki/Chiesa_di_San_Giovanni_Elemosinario" class="mw-redirect" title="Chiesa di San Giovanni Elemosinario">Chiesa di San Giovanni Elemosinario</a>, although his relics are preserved in another church, <a href="/wiki/San_Giovanni_in_Bragora" title="San Giovanni in Bragora">San Giovanni in Bragora</a>, in a separate chapel.
</p><p>Another relic of him was sent by Sultan <a href="/wiki/Bayezid_II" title="Bayezid II">Bayezid II</a> in 1489 to King <a href="/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus" title="Matthias Corvinus">Matthias Corvinus</a> of Hungary. It was placed in the private <a href="/wiki/Palace_Chapel_(Buda_Castle)" title="Palace Chapel (Buda Castle)">Royal Chapel</a> in <a href="/wiki/Buda_Castle" title="Buda Castle">Buda Castle</a>, which was dedicated to him. Now his body lies in the <i>St. John the Merciful Chapel</i> in <a href="/wiki/St._Martin%27s_Cathedral,_Bratislava" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Martin&#39;s Cathedral, Bratislava">St. Martin's Cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bratislava" title="Bratislava">Bratislava</a>, <a href="/wiki/Slovakia" title="Slovakia">Slovakia</a>. 
</p><p>A church in <a href="/wiki/Cospicua" title="Cospicua">Cospicua</a>, Malta, is dedicated to him, and one of the bastions of the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Margherita_Lines" title="Santa Margherita Lines">Santa Margherita Lines</a> in the same city is also named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
