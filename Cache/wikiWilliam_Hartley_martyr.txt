					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>William Hartley</b> (born at <a href="/wiki/Great_Wilne" title="Great Wilne">Wyn</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Derbyshire" title="Derbyshire">Derbyshire</a>, England, of a yeoman family about 1557; executed 5 October 1588) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Hartley_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At eighteen he matriculated at <a href="/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Oxford" title="St John&#39;s College, Oxford">St John's College, Oxford</a>, where he became a <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a>. Being removed by the vice-chancellor, <a href="/wiki/Tobias_Matthew" title="Tobias Matthew">Tobias Matthew</a>, in 1579 n suspicion of Catholic tendencies, he went to <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a> in August, was ordained at <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2lons-en-Champagne" title="Châlons-en-Champagne">Châlons</a> on 24 February 1580, and returned to England in June of that year.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hartley helped <a href="/wiki/Robert_Parsons_(Jesuit)" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Parsons (Jesuit)">Robert Parsons</a> and <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Campion" title="Edmund Campion">Edmund Campion</a> in printing and distributing their books in England. On 4 August 1581 (Pollen gives 13 August) a search of <a href="/wiki/Stonor_Park" title="Stonor Park">Stonor Park</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a> found the press on which Campion's <i>Decem Rationes</i> had been printed.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Hartley, along with members of the Stonor family, printers and some servants, were arrested at Stonor Park. Hartley was sent to <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea_Prison" class="mw-redirect" title="Marshalsea Prison">Marshalsea Prison</a>, London. Here he was detected saying Mass in a cell before <a href="/wiki/William_Vaux,_3rd_Baron_Vaux_of_Harrowden" title="William Vaux, 3rd Baron Vaux of Harrowden">Lord Vaux</a>, and for this he was laid in irons on 5 December 1583. He was indicted for conspiracy, despite the fact that he had already been imprisoned in the Marshalsea at the time the alleged conspiracy took place.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In January 1585, he was sent into exile and put on board a ship at Tower Wharf bound for Normandy. He then spent some little time at Reims, recovering his health, and made a pilgrimage to Rome on 15 April 1586, before returning to the English mission.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In September 1588, he was arrested in <a href="/wiki/Holborn" title="Holborn">Holborn</a>, London, and, as his friend Father Warford said, incurred the suspicion of having apostatized.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In the aftermath of the Armada, Hartley was executed at <a href="/wiki/Shoreditch" title="Shoreditch">Shoreditch</a> on 5 October 1588.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Companions">Companions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Hartley_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Companions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Less is known of Hartley's companions.
</p>
<ul><li><b><a href="/wiki/John_Hewitt_(priest)" title="John Hewitt (priest)">John Hewitt</a></b> was son of a draper at York and a student at <a href="/wiki/Caius_College,_Cambridge" class="mw-redirect" title="Caius College, Cambridge">Caius College, Cambridge</a>. He had once been in York prison, but was arrested in Grey's Inn Lane, London, 10 March 1587, going under the name of Weldon, and died under that name; this had led several early martyrologists into the mistake of making him into two martyrs, Hewett dying at York, and Weldon at London.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><b>Robert Sutton</b> was a tutor or schoolmaster, born at <a href="/wiki/Kegworth" title="Kegworth">Kegworth</a> in Leicestershire, who had practiced his profession in Paternoster Row, London. Sometime prior to June 1586, he met there a Mr. Blythe (aka Oliver Heywoode), an old priest working in the area of <a href="/wiki/Newgate" title="Newgate">Newgate</a>, and converted to Catholicism. He was soon arrested and incarcerated. He was executed at Clerkenwell 5 October 1588.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><b>John Harrison</b>, alias Symons, had carried letters from one priest to another. As he had before "been slandered to be a spy", his fame suffered some obscurity. It is also hardly doubtful that his name, Harrison, was confounded with that of either Matthias or <a href="/wiki/James_Harrison_(priest)" title="James Harrison (priest)">James Harrison</a>, priests, who suffered martyrdom in 1599 or 1602 respectively. This perhaps explains why his name has fallen out of the process of the English martyrs, and in its place was inserted that of Richard Williams, a "Queen Mary priest" who really suffered four years later.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="John_Robinson">John Robinson</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Hartley_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: John Robinson">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John Robinson was born in the <a href="/wiki/North_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="North Riding of Yorkshire">North Riding of Yorkshire</a>. He married and had a son named Francis, born in 1569. Upon the death of his wife, he entered the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College at Rheims</a>. He returned to England briefly in mid-August 1584 to visit his son, who was the fifteen. The following April, Harrison was ordained and went on the English mission in June. Upon arriving, he immediately took ship for the North. His ship, likely bound for Newcastle, put in at <a href="/wiki/Yarmouth_Roads" title="Yarmouth Roads">Yarmouth Roads</a>, where he was arrested  and sent to <a href="/wiki/The_Clink" title="The Clink">The Clink</a>, where he remained for three years. The persecution that broke out after the failure of the Armada brought him to the scaffold. The place set for his execution was <a href="/wiki/Ipswich" title="Ipswich">Ipswich</a>; Harrison chose to walk. He was hanged, drawn, and quartered on 1 October 1588. His son was ordained five years later.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
