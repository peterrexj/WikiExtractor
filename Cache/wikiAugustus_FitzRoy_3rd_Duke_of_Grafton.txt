<p><b>Augustus Henry FitzRoy, 3rd Duke of Grafton</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a></span> (28 September 1735&#160;&#8211;&#32;14 March 1811), styled <b>Earl of Euston</b> between 1747 and 1757, was a <a href="/wiki/British_Whig" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig">British Whig</a> statesman of the <a href="/wiki/Georgian_era" title="Georgian era">Georgian era</a>. He is one of a handful of <a href="/wiki/Duke" title="Duke">dukes</a> who have served as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">prime minister</a>.
</p><p>He became prime minister in 1768 at the age of 33, leading the supporters of <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt</a>, and was the youngest person to hold the office until the appointment of <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt the Younger</a> 15 years later. However, he struggled to demonstrate an ability to counter increasing challenges to Britain's global dominance following the nation's <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years%27_War" title="Great Britain in the Seven Years&#39; War">victory in the Seven Years' War</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Crisis" title="Corsican Crisis">widely attacked</a> for allowing France to annex <a href="/wiki/Corsica" title="Corsica">Corsica</a>, and stepped down in 1770, handing over power to <a href="/wiki/Lord_North" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord North">Lord North</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background_and_education">Background and education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustus_FitzRoy,_3rd_Duke_of_Grafton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background and education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was a son of <a href="/wiki/Lord_Augustus_FitzRoy" title="Lord Augustus FitzRoy">Lord Augustus FitzRoy</a>, a captain in the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Navy" title="Royal Navy">Royal Navy</a>,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and Elizabeth Cosby, the daughter of Colonel <a href="/wiki/William_Cosby" title="William Cosby">William Cosby</a>, who served as a colonial <a href="/wiki/Governor_of_New_York" title="Governor of New York">Governor of New York</a>. His father was the third son of the <a href="/wiki/Charles_FitzRoy,_2nd_Duke_of_Grafton" title="Charles FitzRoy, 2nd Duke of Grafton">2nd Duke of Grafton</a> and Lady Henrietta Somerset, which made FitzRoy a great-grandson of both the <a href="/wiki/Henry_FitzRoy,_1st_Duke_of_Grafton" title="Henry FitzRoy, 1st Duke of Grafton">1st Duke of Grafton</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Somerset,_Marquess_of_Worcester" title="Charles Somerset, Marquess of Worcester">Marquess of Worcester</a>. He was notably a fourth-generation descendant of <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_England" title="Charles II of England">King Charles II</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Barbara_Palmer,_1st_Duchess_of_Cleveland" title="Barbara Palmer, 1st Duchess of Cleveland">1st Duchess of Cleveland</a>; the surname FitzRoy stems from this illegitimacy. His younger brother was the <a href="/wiki/Charles_FitzRoy,_1st_Baron_Southampton" title="Charles FitzRoy, 1st Baron Southampton">1st Baron Southampton</a>. Since the death of his uncle in 1747, he was styled <b>Earl of Euston</b> as his grandfather's heir apparent.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg/170px-Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="119" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg/255px-Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg/340px-Hackney_School_Reeve.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="419" /></a><figcaption>Euston was educated at <a href="/wiki/Newcome%27s_School" title="Newcome&#39;s School">Newcome's School</a> (pictured)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Lord Euston was educated at <a href="/wiki/Newcome%27s_School" title="Newcome&#39;s School">Newcome's School</a> in Hackney and at <a href="/wiki/Westminster_School" title="Westminster School">Westminster School</a>, made the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Tour" title="Grand Tour">Grand Tour</a>, and obtained a degree at <a href="/wiki/Peterhouse,_Cambridge" title="Peterhouse, Cambridge">Peterhouse</a>, <a href="/wiki/University_of_Cambridge" title="University of Cambridge">University of Cambridge</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_career">Political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustus_FitzRoy,_3rd_Duke_of_Grafton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1756, he entered <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain" title="Parliament of Great Britain">Parliament</a> as <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_(United_Kingdom)" title="Member of Parliament (United Kingdom)">MP</a> for <a href="/wiki/Boroughbridge_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Boroughbridge (UK Parliament constituency)">Boroughbridge</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Pocket_borough" class="mw-redirect" title="Pocket borough">pocket borough</a>; several months later, he switched constituencies to <a href="/wiki/Bury_St_Edmunds_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Bury St Edmunds (UK Parliament constituency)">Bury St Edmunds</a>, which was controlled by his family. However, a year later, his grandfather died, and he succeeded as the 3rd Duke of Grafton, which elevated him to the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>.
</p><p>He first became known in politics as an opponent of <a href="/wiki/Lord_Bute" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Bute">Lord Bute</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> a favourite of <a href="/wiki/King_George_III" class="mw-redirect" title="King George III">King George III</a>. Grafton aligned himself with the <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pelham-Holles,_1st_Duke_of_Newcastle" title="Thomas Pelham-Holles, 1st Duke of Newcastle">Duke of Newcastle</a> against Lord Bute, whose term as prime minister was short-lived largely because it was felt that the peace terms to which he had agreed at the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1763)" title="Treaty of Paris (1763)">Treaty of Paris</a> were not a sufficient return for <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain_in_the_Seven_Years%27_War" title="Great Britain in the Seven Years&#39; War">Britain's performance in the Seven Years' War</a>.
</p><p>In 1765, Grafton was appointed a <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of Great Britain">Privy Counsellor</a>; then, following discussions with <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham" title="William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham">William Pitt the Elder</a>, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Northern_Department" title="Secretary of State for the Northern Department">Northern Secretary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Charles_Watson-Wentworth,_2nd_Marquess_of_Rockingham" title="Charles Watson-Wentworth, 2nd Marquess of Rockingham">Lord Rockingham's</a> first government. However, he retired the following year, and Pitt (by then Lord Chatham) formed a ministry in which Grafton was <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury" title="First Lord of the Treasury">First Lord of the Treasury</a> but not the prime minister.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On September 20, 1769, he was appointed a Knight of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter" title="Order of the Garter">Order of the Garter</a>.
</p>

<p>Chatham's illness, at the end of 1767, resulted in Grafton becoming the government's effective leader (he is credited with entering the office of prime minister in 1768), but political differences, the impact of the <a href="/wiki/Corsican_Crisis" title="Corsican Crisis">Corsican Crisis</a> and the attacks of "<a href="/wiki/Junius_(writer)" title="Junius (writer)">Junius</a>" led to his resignation in January 1770. Also, in 1768, Grafton became Chancellor of Cambridge University.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He became <a href="/wiki/Lord_Privy_Seal" title="Lord Privy Seal">Lord Privy Seal</a> in <a href="/wiki/Frederick_North,_Lord_North" title="Frederick North, Lord North">Lord North's</a> ministry (1771) but resigned in 1775, being in favour of conciliatory action towards the <a href="/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies" title="Thirteen Colonies">American colonists</a>. In the second Rockingham ministry of 1782, he was again Lord Privy Seal and continued in the post in the following <a href="/wiki/Shelburne_ministry" title="Shelburne ministry">Shelburne ministry</a> until March 1783.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_interests">Religious interests</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustus_FitzRoy,_3rd_Duke_of_Grafton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Religious interests">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In later years, he was a prominent <a href="/wiki/Unitarianism" title="Unitarianism">Unitarian</a>, being one of the early members of the inaugural <a href="/wiki/Essex_Street_Chapel" title="Essex Street Chapel">Essex Street Chapel</a> under Rev. <a href="/wiki/Theophilus_Lindsey" title="Theophilus Lindsey">Theophilus Lindsey</a> when it was founded in 1774. Grafton had associated with a number of liberal Anglican theologians when at Cambridge, and devoted much time to theological study and writing after leaving office as prime minister. In 1773, in the House of Lords, he supported a bill to release Anglican clergy from subscribing to all the <a href="/wiki/Thirty-nine_Articles" title="Thirty-nine Articles">Thirty-nine Articles</a>. He became a supporter of moral reform among the wealthy and of changes to the church. He was the author of:
</p>
<ul><li><i>Hints Submitted to the Serious Attention of the Clergy, Nobility and Gentry, by a Layman</i> (1789).</li>
<li><i>Serious Reflections of a Rational Christian from 1788–1797</i>.</li></ul>
<p>He was a sponsor of Richard Watson's <i>Consideration of the Expediency of Revising the Liturgy and Articles of the Church of England</i> (published in 1790), and he funded the printing of 700 copies of Griesbach's edition of the Greek <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a> in 1796.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDurrant2004928_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDurrant2004928-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Horseracing">Horseracing</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustus_FitzRoy,_3rd_Duke_of_Grafton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Horseracing">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Duke also had <a href="/wiki/Horse_racing" title="Horse racing">horse racing</a> interests. His <a href="/wiki/Horse_racing_colours_in_Great_Britain" title="Horse racing colours in Great Britain">racing colours</a> were sky blue, with a black cap.<sup id="cite_ref-RC1801_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-RC1801-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Augustus_FitzRoy,_3rd_Duke_of_Grafton&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Grafton_County,_New_Hampshire" title="Grafton County, New Hampshire">Grafton County, New Hampshire</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> in the United States, is named in his honour, as is the city of <a href="/wiki/Grafton,_New_South_Wales" title="Grafton, New South Wales">Grafton</a>, New South Wales, Australia, the town of <a href="/wiki/Grafton,_New_York" title="Grafton, New York">Grafton, New York</a>, the unincorporated community of <a href="/wiki/Grafton,_Virginia" title="Grafton, Virginia">Grafton, Virginia</a>, and possibly the township (since 1856 a city) of <a href="/wiki/Grafton,_West_Virginia" title="Grafton, West Virginia">Grafton, West Virginia</a>. The Grafton Centre Shopping Mall in Cambridge is also named after him and indeed lies on Fitzroy Street. <a href="/wiki/Cape_Grafton" title="Cape Grafton">Cape Grafton</a> in <a href="/wiki/Far_North_Queensland" title="Far North Queensland">Far North Queensland</a> was named after him by Lieutenant <a href="/wiki/James_Cook" title="James Cook">James Cook</a> during his <a href="/wiki/First_voyage_of_James_Cook" title="First voyage of James Cook">first voyage of discovery</a>.
</p><p>Grafton had the longest post-premiership of any prime minister in British history, <a href="/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_the_United_Kingdom_by_age" title="List of prime ministers of the United Kingdom by age">totalling 41&#160;years and 45&#160;days</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>On 29 January 1756, he married <a href="/wiki/Anne_FitzPatrick" class="mw-redirect" title="Anne FitzPatrick">The Hon. Anne Liddell</a>, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_Liddell,_1st_Baron_Ravensworth" title="Henry Liddell, 1st Baron Ravensworth">Henry Liddell, 1st Baron Ravensworth</a> (1708&#8211;1784), at Lord Ravensworth's house in St James's Square, by licence. The marriage was witnessed by Lord Ravensworth and <a href="/wiki/Francis_Seymour-Conway,_1st_Marquess_of_Hertford" title="Francis Seymour-Conway, 1st Marquess of Hertford">Francis Seymour-Conway, 1st Earl of Hertford</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Augustus and Anne had three children:
</p>
<ul><li>Lady Georgiana FitzRoy<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> (8 May 1757&#160;&#8211;&#32;18 January 1799),<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> who married <a href="/wiki/John_Smyth_(1748%E2%80%931811)" title="John Smyth (1748–1811)">John Smyth</a> (12 February 1748&#160;&#8211;&#32;12 February 1811) on 4 June 1778.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/George_FitzRoy,_4th_Duke_of_Grafton" title="George FitzRoy, 4th Duke of Grafton">George Henry FitzRoy, 4th Duke of Grafton</a> (1760–1844)</li>
<li>General <a href="/wiki/Lord_Charles_FitzRoy_(British_Army_officer)" title="Lord Charles FitzRoy (British Army officer)">Lord Charles FitzRoy</a> (14 July 1764&#160;&#8211;&#32;20 December 1829), who married, firstly, Frances Mundy (1773&#160;&#8211;&#32;9 August 1797) on 20 June 1795, and had one son. He married, secondly, Lady Frances Stewart (24 June 1777&#160;&#8211;&#32;9 February 1810) on 10 March 1799 and had three children. His sons <a href="/wiki/Charles_Augustus_FitzRoy" title="Charles Augustus FitzRoy">Sir Charles FitzRoy</a> (1796–1858), governor of <a href="/wiki/New_South_Wales" title="New South Wales">New South Wales</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Robert_FitzRoy" title="Robert FitzRoy">Robert FitzRoy</a>, the hydrographer, were notable for their achievements.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>In 1764, the Duke had a very public affair with the courtesan <a href="/wiki/Anne_Parsons" title="Anne Parsons">Nancy Parsons</a><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> whom he kept at his townhouse and took to the opera, where they allegedly were found <i><a href="/wiki/In_flagrante_delicto" title="In flagrante delicto">in flagrante delicto</a></i>. This brazen lack of convention offended society's standards.  After the Duchess had become pregnant by her own lover, the <a href="/wiki/John_FitzPatrick,_2nd_Earl_of_Upper_Ossory" title="John FitzPatrick, 2nd Earl of Upper Ossory">Earl of Upper Ossory</a>, she and the Duke were divorced by Act of Parliament, passed 23 March 1769.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDurrant2004_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDurrant2004-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (September 2017)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Three months later, on 24 June 1769, the Duke married <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Wrottesley" title="Elizabeth Wrottesley">Elizabeth Wrottesley</a> (1 November 1745&#160;&#8211;&#32;25 May 1822), daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Sir_Richard_Wrottesley,_7th_Baronet" title="Sir Richard Wrottesley, 7th Baronet">Reverend Sir Richard Wrottesley</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_Worcester" title="Dean of Worcester">Dean of Worcester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> They had the following children:
</p>
<ul><li>Lord Henry FitzRoy (9 April 1770&#160;&#8211;&#32;7 June 1828), clergyman; he married Caroline Pigot (died 1 January 1835) on 10 September 1800 and had five children. Ancestor of <a href="/wiki/Daisy_Greville,_Countess_of_Warwick" title="Daisy Greville, Countess of Warwick">Daisy Greville, Countess of Warwick</a>.</li>
<li>Lord Frederick FitzRoy (born 16 September 1774; died young).</li>
<li>Lady Augusta FitzRoy (1779&#160;&#8211;&#32;29 June 1839), who married Rev. George F. Tavel (died 1829) on 19 November 1811.</li>
<li>Lady Frances FitzRoy (1 June 1780&#160;&#8211;&#32;7 January 1866), who married the <a href="/wiki/Francis_Spencer,_1st_Baron_Churchill" title="Francis Spencer, 1st Baron Churchill">1st Baron Churchill</a> on 25 November 1800.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Admiral" title="Admiral">Admiral</a> <a href="/wiki/Lord_William_FitzRoy" title="Lord William FitzRoy">Lord William FitzRoy</a> (1 June 1782&#160;&#8211;&#32;13 May 1857), who married Georgiana Raikes (died 2 December 1861) in 1816 and had two children.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Lord_John_FitzRoy" title="Lord John FitzRoy">Lord John Edward FitzRoy</a> (24 September 1785&#160;&#8211;&#32;28 December 1856), MP, died unmarried.</li>
<li>Lady Charlotte FitzRoy (died 23 June 1857).</li>
<li>Lady Elizabeth FitzRoy (died 13 March 1839), who married her cousin <a href="/w/index.php?title=William_FitzRoy_(1773%E2%80%931837)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="William FitzRoy (1773–1837) (page does not exist)">Lt. Gen. The Hon. William FitzRoy</a> (1773–1837), son of the <a href="/wiki/Charles_FitzRoy,_1st_Baron_Southampton" title="Charles FitzRoy, 1st Baron Southampton">1st Baron Southampton</a>, on 4 July 1811.</li>
<li>Lady Isabella FitzRoy (died 10 December 1866), who married Barrington Pope Blachford (3 December 1783&#160;&#8211;&#32;14 May 1816) on 11 August 1812.</li></ul>
<p>Grafton is thus the first British prime minister before <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Eden" title="Anthony Eden">Anthony Eden</a><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> (and one of only three) to have been divorced, and the second, after <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a>, to marry while in office.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Grafton would be the only prime minister to divorce and remarry while in office until <a href="/wiki/Boris_Johnson" title="Boris Johnson">Boris Johnson</a> in 2021.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
FitzRoy died on 14 March 1811.
</p>
