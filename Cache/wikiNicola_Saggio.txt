<p><b>Nicola Saggio</b> was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> professed oblate of the <a href="/wiki/Minim_(religious_order)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minim (religious order)">Order of Minims</a>.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 17 September 1786 and was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> as a saint of the Roman Catholic Church on 23 November 2014.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicola_Saggio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nicola Saggio was born on 6 January 1650 in <a href="/wiki/Longobardi,_Calabria" title="Longobardi, Calabria">Longobardi</a>, a small town on the <a href="/wiki/Tyrrhenian_Sea" title="Tyrrhenian Sea">Tyrrhenian</a> coast. His parents were Fulvio Saggio, a farmer, and Aurelia Saggio née Pizzini. He was the first of five children while the twins Antonio and Domenica followed and then Nicola and Muzio. He was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> in the names of "Giovanni Battista Clemente"<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> on 10 January 1650.
</p><p>His parents raised him with high moral and spiritual values and instilled in him a Christian education.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He visited the convent of the <a href="/wiki/Minim_(religious_order)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minim (religious order)">Minims</a> which made him desire the religious life as his path in life.
</p><p>He was famous as a <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechist</a> in Longobardi and in Roman circles. A further change in his spiritual life was recorded in 1683 after a pilgrimage on foot to <a href="/wiki/Loreto,_Marche" title="Loreto, Marche">Loreto</a> to ask <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> – through the intercession of the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> – for the liberation of <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Turks</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Nicola_Saggio&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg/170px-Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="96" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg/255px-Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg/340px-Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg 2x" data-file-width="960" data-file-height="540" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fonte_battesimale_beato_Nicola.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Baptismal_font" title="Baptismal font">baptismal font</a> where St. Nicola Saggio was baptized.</div></div></div>
<p>On 17 March 1771 he was made <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> once <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIV" title="Pope Clement XIV">Pope Clement XIV</a> confirmed his life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> presided over the rite of beatification for the late oblate on 17 September 1786 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a> after approving two miracles attributed to his intercession on 2 April 1786. After the beatification he was made the patron of Longobardi (his village).
</p><p>The third and final miracle for the canonization occurred in 1938 for a mason of Longobardi who fell from a scaffold without sustaining injuries. The diocesan investigation took place between 24 May 2008 and 15 June 2009; the process received the validation of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> on 11 March 2011 while the consulting medical board approved the miracle on 13 December 2012. Theologians voiced approval on 28 November 2013 while the C.C.S. also assented on 4 March 2014. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> canonized him as a saint on 23 November 2014<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> after approving the miracle on 3 April 2014.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> at the time of the canonization was the Rev. Ottavio Laino.
</p>
