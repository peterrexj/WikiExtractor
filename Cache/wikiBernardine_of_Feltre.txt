<p><b>Bernardine of Feltre</b> (sometimes <b>Bernardinus of Feltre</b>) was a Friar Minor and missionary, b. at <a href="/wiki/Feltre" title="Feltre">Feltre</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, in 1439 and d. at <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, 28 September 1494. He is remembered in connection with the <a href="/wiki/Monti_di_piet%C3%A0" class="mw-redirect" title="Monti di pietà">monti di pietà</a> of which he was the reorganizer and, in a certain sense, the founder, together with the <a href="/wiki/Michele_Carcano" title="Michele Carcano">Michele Carcano</a>. The feast of Blessed Bernardino is kept in the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> on 28 September.
</p><p>He was beatified by Pope Innocent X on 13 April 1654 via the confirmation of his cult.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernardine_of_Feltre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born Martin Tomitani, he belonged to the noble family of Tomitano and was the eldest of nine children. In 1456, while a law student in Padua, he heard <a href="/wiki/James_of_the_Marches" title="James of the Marches">James of the Marches</a> preach the <a href="/wiki/Lenten" class="mw-redirect" title="Lenten">Lenten</a> course and was inspired to enter the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan order">Franciscan order</a>, taking the name Bernardino, after <a href="/wiki/Bernardino_of_Siena" title="Bernardino of Siena">Bernardino of Siena</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabbatelli_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabbatelli-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In May that year he joined the "Observantine" Franciscans, an austere branch of the Franciscan friars. He completed successfully his studies at <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a> and was ordained priest in 1463.<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was small, shy, and stammered but his superiors assigned him to preach home-missions. Cured of an impediment in his speech, Bernardine began his apostolate up and down the Italian peninsula. Every city of note and every province from <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> in the north to <a href="/wiki/Sardinia" title="Sardinia">Sardinia</a> and the provinces of the south became successively the scene of his missionary labours.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was an extremely popular preacher because he spoke simply and powerfully against the vanity, ambition and greed rife at the time. The crowds that flocked to hear him were too large for the local churches, so he addressed them in the city squares and the fields. Like many other missioners of his century, he had made a vast outdoor bonfire called "burning the Devil's stronghold". The crowds were asked to throw into the fire all objects of vanity and sin such as playing cards, dice, pornographic books and pictures, jewelry, wigs, superstitious charms, cosmetics, and so forth.
</p><p>Bernardine was able to reconcile warring communities. He also sought civic legislation to correct public injustices such as usury, the charging of excessive interest for loans, which was especially onerous on the poor. In 1484, Bernardine established the charitable credit organization, <a href="/wiki/Mont-de-pi%C3%A9t%C3%A9s" class="mw-redirect" title="Mont-de-piétés">mont-de-piétés</a> run by a joint committee of clergy and laymen. The institution was founded as an alternative to the high interest loans of the money lenders and Lombard traveling bankers of the Middle Ages.<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His fund raising drives were generally preceded with a procession featuring an image of either the <a href="/wiki/Man_of_Sorrows" title="Man of Sorrows">Man of Sorrows</a> or <a href="/wiki/Piet%C3%A0" title="Pietà">Pietà</a> to encourage charitable donations.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His insistence on charging a low interest to protect the institution's permanency raised a controversy among the theologians who thought it promoted the continuance of usury. (In 1515, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_X" title="Pope Leo X">Pope Leo X</a> declared the institution meritorious and it spread rapidly throughout France, Italy, and Spain.)
</p><p>In 1491, Bernardine was expelled from Milan by <a href="/wiki/Ludovico_Sforza" title="Ludovico Sforza">Ludovico Sforza</a> for contesting with the Duke's astrologer.<sup id="cite_ref-Sabbatelli_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sabbatelli-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Antisemitism">Antisemitism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernardine_of_Feltre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Antisemitism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>With the practical notion of establishing <a href="/wiki/Mount_of_piety" title="Mount of piety">monts-de-piété</a>, he called for the expulsion of Jews all over Italy and <a href="/wiki/County_of_Tyrol" title="County of Tyrol">Tyrol</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-lazare_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lazare-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> However, no secular or ecclesiastical authorities specifically took up Bernardine's call. This led Bernardine to accuse the nobility of having sold out to <a href="/wiki/Jehiel_of_Pisa" class="mw-redirect" title="Jehiel of Pisa">Jehiel of Pisa</a>, one of the wealthiest Jews in Italy. However, one effect of his preaching against usury was the outbreak of <a href="/wiki/Antisemitism" title="Antisemitism">antisemitism</a> in <a href="/wiki/Trento" title="Trento">Trent</a> based around the <a href="/wiki/Blood_libel" title="Blood libel">blood libel</a> conspiracy against Jewish residents after the death of <a href="/wiki/Simon_of_Trent" title="Simon of Trent">Simon of Trent</a>. This led to the torture and execution of seventeen Jews and the expulsion of the remainder from that city, with no return for the next 300 years.<sup id="cite_ref-lazare_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lazare-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernardine_of_Feltre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bernardine is generally represented in <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> as carrying in his hand a <a href="/wiki/Monti_di_piet%C3%A0" class="mw-redirect" title="Monti di pietà">monti di pietà</a>, that is, a little green hill composed of three mounds and on the top either a cross<sup id="cite_ref-ncd_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ncd-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> or a standard with the inscription <i>Curam illius habe</i> (a snippet from the <a href="/wiki/Vulgate" title="Vulgate">Vulgate</a> translation of the Gospel of Luke's <a href="/wiki/Parable_of_the_Good_Samaritan" title="Parable of the Good Samaritan">Parable of the Good Samaritan</a>).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernardine_of_Feltre&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The authorship of the well-known <a href="/wiki/Anima_Christi" title="Anima Christi">Anima Christi</a> has as often as not been ascribed to Bernardine of Feltre. The fact, however, that the Anima Christi was composed sometime before 1439 disproves any claim that he might have of being its author,<sup id="cite_ref-Donovan_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Donovan-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> though much like <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a>, Bernardine made frequent use of it and recommended it to his brethren.
</p>
