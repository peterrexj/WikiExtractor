<p><b>Dominic Collins</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Doiminic Ó Coileáin</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 1566 – 31 October 1602) was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>, an ex-soldier, who died for his <a href="/wiki/Catholic_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic faith">Catholic faith</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> as a martyr by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 September 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Collins was born in 1566 of a prominent merchant family in <a href="/wiki/Youghal" title="Youghal">Youghal</a>, County Cork, in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ireland" title="Kingdom of Ireland">Kingdom of Ireland</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His father and brother both served as mayors of the town.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 1586 he went to <a href="/wiki/Nantes" title="Nantes">Nantes</a>, France, where he worked for three years as a servant in various hostelries in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, to acquire a horse and to join the cavalry. In 1589 he joined the forces of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_League_(French)" title="Catholic League (French)">Catholic League</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Philippe_Emmanuel,_Duke_of_Merc%C5%93ur" title="Philippe Emmanuel, Duke of Mercœur">Philippe Emmanuel, Duke of Mercœur</a>, at war against the <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Huguenots</a> of Brittany. He was soon promoted to the rank of captain (under the name of <i>La Branche</i>), and was appointed military governor of territory taken from the Huguenots.<sup id="cite_ref-ls_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ls-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Collins went to Spain with a letter of recommendation for King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II</a>, whose service he entered. He transferred to the Spanish army and was stationed in the garrison at <a href="/wiki/A_Coru%C3%B1a" title="A Coruña">La Coruña</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ci_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ci-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> After nine years of military service, he decided to leave the army, and was granted a pension of 25 <i><a href="/wiki/Spanish_escudo" title="Spanish escudo">escudos</a></i> a month by King Philip.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> of 1598, Collins met an Irish Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, Thomas White from Clonmel, who had founded the <a href="/wiki/Irish_College" title="Irish College">Irish College</a> at <a href="/wiki/Salamanca" title="Salamanca">Salamanca</a>. Collins expressed a desire to become a Jesuit and White introduced him to the superiors of the college.<sup id="cite_ref-ci_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ci-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Although he was now 32 years old, the Jesuit provincial thought it was wise to delay his entrance, perhaps to test the strength of his vocation. There were doubts too about his being sufficiently educated to become a priest but he was willing to be a Jesuit lay brother. He entered the Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a> in December 1598. When the Jesuit College was struck by a plague, Dominic tended the victims, nursing some of them back to health and comforting the others in their last hours.<sup id="cite_ref-ci_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ci-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A report from that time describes him as a man of sound judgement and great physical strength; mature, prudent and sociable, though inclined to be hot-tempered and obstinate. He was allowed to <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">profess</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> in the Society in February 1601.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon after his profession, an expedition was organised by King <a href="/wiki/Philip_III_of_Spain" title="Philip III of Spain">Philip III of Spain</a> to assist <a href="/wiki/Hugh_O%27Neill,_Earl_of_Tyrone" title="Hugh O&#39;Neill, Earl of Tyrone">Hugh O'Neill</a> and <a href="/wiki/Red_Hugh_O%27Donnell" class="mw-redirect" title="Red Hugh O&#39;Donnell">Red Hugh O'Donnell</a> in their attempt at revolt against English rule. James Archer, an Irish Jesuit priest assigned by the king as chaplain to army requested that Collins be assigned as his companion and assistant, due to his extensive military background.<sup id="cite_ref-ls_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ls-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The fleet set sail on 3 September 1601 in two squadrons. The smaller part of the fleet, to which Collins was assigned, ran into bad weather and was delayed, eventually reaching <a href="/wiki/Castlehaven" title="Castlehaven">Castlehaven</a> in southwestern Cork on 1 December, the main squadron having reached <a href="/wiki/Kinsale" title="Kinsale">Kinsale</a> more than two months earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyr_memorial,_Youghal.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg/220px-Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg/330px-Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg/440px-Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyr_memorial,_Youghal.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Site of Collins' martyrdom</div></div></div>
<p>After the disastrous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Kinsale" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Kinsale">Battle of Kinsale</a> (24 December 1601), Archer returned to Spain to seek help. Collins remained behind at <a href="/wiki/Dunboy_Castle" title="Dunboy Castle">Dunboy Castle</a> to attend to the spiritual needs of 143 Irish soldiers who were besieged by 4,000 English troops. <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Dunboy" title="Siege of Dunboy">After an eleven-day siege</a>, Dunboy fell into the hands of the English on 18 June 1602.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> While the others were immediately hanged, the Jesuit and two other survivors were held prisoner. They were then taken to Cork where they were tortured, and the two surviving Irish soldiers soon executed.
</p><p>Relishing the prospect of having a Jesuit abjure his faith in his very hometown, the English took Collins from <a href="/wiki/Shandon_Castle,_Cork" title="Shandon Castle, Cork">Shandon Castle</a> in Cork to Youghal, a distance of 173&#160;km.<sup id="cite_ref-collins1943_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collins1943-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> There, he addressed the crowd in Spanish, Irish and English, cheerfully telling them that he had come to Ireland to defend the Catholic faith, for which he was happy to die. So moved was the crowd that the hangman fled and a passing fisherman was forced to do carry out the execution. He was <a href="/wiki/Hanged" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanged">hanged</a> on 31 October 1602, condemned to death Without any formal trial.<sup id="cite_ref-page24_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-page24-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Collins was beatified – along with <a href="/wiki/Francis_Taylor_(martyr)" title="Francis Taylor (martyr)">Francis Taylor</a> and 13 other Irish martyrs – on 27 September 1992 by Pope John-Paul II.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">Liturgically</a> his feast is celebrated on 20 June, or 30 October (in the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>). Today a Jesuit residence in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> is named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
