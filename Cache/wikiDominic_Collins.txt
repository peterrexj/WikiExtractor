<p><b>Dominic Collins</b> (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: <i lang="ga">Doiminic Ó Coileáin</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 1566 – 31 October 1602) was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>, an ex-soldier, who died for his <a href="/wiki/Catholic_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic faith">Catholic faith</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> as a martyr by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 September 1992.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Dominic Collins was born in 1566 into a prominent <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Norman" class="mw-redirect" title="Hiberno-Norman">Hiberno-Norman</a> merchant family in <a href="/wiki/Youghal" title="Youghal">Youghal</a>, County Cork, in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ireland" title="Kingdom of Ireland">Kingdom of Ireland</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> John Collins, his father, and one of his brothers both served as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Mayor" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Mayor">Lord Mayors</a> of the town.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His mother, Felicity O'Dril, or O'Dula, was descended from a family of <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Gaels</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1586, Collins sailed to France, landed at <a href="/wiki/Les_Sables_d%27Olonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Les Sables d&#39;Olonne">Les Sables d'Olonne</a>, and travelled overland to <a href="/wiki/Nantes" title="Nantes">Nantes</a>, where he worked for three years as a inn servant. His years as an inn servant enabled Collins to acquire both the money for a horse and a working knowledge of both the <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> and <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton languages</a>. In 1586, Collins joined a <a href="/wiki/Cavalry" title="Cavalry">cavalry</a> unit during the <a href="/wiki/Brittany_Campaign" title="Brittany Campaign">Brittany Campaign</a> of the <a href="/wiki/French_Wars_of_Religion" title="French Wars of Religion">French Wars of Religion</a>; namely, the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_League_(French)" title="Catholic League (French)">Catholic League</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Philippe_Emmanuel,_Duke_of_Merc%C5%93ur" title="Philippe Emmanuel, Duke of Mercœur">Philippe Emmanuel, Duke of Mercœur</a>. As such, Collins was at war against both <a href="/wiki/Breton_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton people">Breton</a> <a href="/wiki/Huguenots" title="Huguenots">Huguenots</a> and the English and German troops who were sent to assist them. Collins' other enemy was the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Bourbon" title="House of Bourbon">House of Bourbon</a>, as the Duke de Mercoeur also sought, with both Spanish and Vatican backing, to restore the <a href="/wiki/Breton_nationalism" title="Breton nationalism">political independence</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brittany" title="Duchy of Brittany">Duchy of Brittany</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_France" title="Kingdom of France">Kingdom of France</a>. Collins was known to be an effective soldier and was promoted to the rank of Captain (and was known to the French under the <a href="/wiki/Nom_de_guerre" class="mw-redirect" title="Nom de guerre">nom de guerre</a> of <i>Capitaine de La Branche</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After personally retaking the district and chateau of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Lapena&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Lapena (page does not exist)">Lapena</a> from the Huguenots, Collins was appointed <a href="/wiki/Military_governor" class="mw-redirect" title="Military governor">military governor</a> of the territory by the Duke de Mercoeur.<sup id="cite_ref-ls_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ls-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Henri_IV_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Henri IV of France">Henri of Navarre</a>, the French throne's still <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinist</a> <a href="/wiki/Heir_presumptive" title="Heir presumptive">heir presumptive</a>, offered Collins a <a href="/wiki/Bribery" title="Bribery">bribe</a> of 2,000 ducats to surrender the chateau and surrounding district, but was refused. Only when Collins realized that both the Catholic League and the <a href="/wiki/Breton_nationalist" class="mw-redirect" title="Breton nationalist">Breton nationalist</a> armies of the Duke de Mercoeur were collapsing, did he surrender Lapena to Spanish General Don <a href="/wiki/Juan_del_%C3%81guila" title="Juan del Águila">Juan del Águila</a>, who dispatched Collins to <a href="/wiki/Hapsburg_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Hapsburg Spain">Hapsburg Spain</a> via <a href="/wiki/San_Sebastian" class="mw-redirect" title="San Sebastian">San Sebastian</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Basque_Country" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish Basque Country">Basque Country</a>, with a letter of recommendation to <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">King Philip II</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> by whom Collins was granted a pension of 25 <i><a href="/wiki/Spanish_escudo" title="Spanish escudo">escudos</a></i>,<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which was received only, "for a twelvemonth or thereabouts."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Collins then transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Royal_Navy" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish Royal Navy">Spanish Royal Navy</a> and was stationed at the port of <a href="/wiki/A_Coru%C3%B1a" title="A Coruña">La Coruña</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Galicia" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish Galicia">Galicia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ci_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ci-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Jesuit_vocation">Jesuit vocation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Jesuit vocation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> of 1598, Collins met Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> Fr. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_White_(Jesuit)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Thomas White (Jesuit) (page does not exist)">Thomas White</a>, a native of <a href="/wiki/Clonmel" title="Clonmel">Clonmel</a>, who had arrived at A Coruña to hear the confessions of his fellow Irishmen serving there with the Spanish Navy. Collins was convinced that their meeting was providential and Fr. White, who had previously founded the <a href="/wiki/Irish_College_at_Salamanca" title="Irish College at Salamanca">Irish College at Salamanca</a>, has left behind a biography of Dominic Collins, which relates in considerable detail the story of the former Captain's call to the religious life. Collins had so firm a desire to become a Jesuit, that Father White introduced him to Fr. <a href="/w/index.php?title=Alfonso_Ferrer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Alfonso Ferrer (page does not exist)">Alfonso Ferrer</a>, the Jesuit Provincial of <a href="/wiki/Castile_(historical_region)" title="Castile (historical region)">Castile</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ci_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ci-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although he was now 32 years old, the Provincial thought it was wise to delay his <a href="/wiki/Jesuit_formation" title="Jesuit formation">Jesuit formation</a>, perhaps to test the strength of his vocation. There were doubts too about whether Collins was well-educated enough to become a priest, "but the Provincial finally relented in the face of Collins' repeated requests, as he was received as a brother-novice on 8 December 1598". He began his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> in the Jesuit college at <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a>, where the annals describe Collins as, "an Irishman of distinguished parentage, comely appearance and stature", who had just, "passed thirty-two years of age", and, "had been a Captain of Duke Philip's cavalry in Brittany."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the Jesuit College was struck by a plague, Dominic tended the victims, nursing some of them back to health and comforting the others in their last hours.<sup id="cite_ref-ci_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ci-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> A report from that time describes him as a man of sound judgement and great physical strength; mature, prudent and sociable, though inclined to be hot-tempered and obstinate. He was allowed to <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">profess</a> his <a href="/wiki/Solemn_vow" title="Solemn vow">Solemn vows</a> in February 1601.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Nine_Years_War">The Nine Years War</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The Nine Years War">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Soon after his profession, a Spanish expedition was organised by <a href="/wiki/Philip_III_of_Spain" title="Philip III of Spain">King Philip III</a> to assist the coalition of <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clans</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Hugh_O%27Neill,_Earl_of_Tyrone" title="Hugh O&#39;Neill, Earl of Tyrone">Aodh Mór Ó Néill</a> and <a href="/wiki/Red_Hugh_O%27Donnell" class="mw-redirect" title="Red Hugh O&#39;Donnell">Red Hugh O'Donnell</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Nine_Years_War_(Ireland)" class="mw-redirect" title="Nine Years War (Ireland)">Nine Years War</a> against <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a>. Fr. <a href="/wiki/James_Archer_(Jesuit)" title="James Archer (Jesuit)">James Archer</a>, an Irish Jesuit priest assigned by the king as <a href="/wiki/Military_chaplain" title="Military chaplain">military chaplain</a> to the Spanish Army, requested that Collins be assigned as his companion and assistant, due to his extensive military background.<sup id="cite_ref-ls_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ls-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The fleet set sail on 3 September 1601 in two squadrons. The smaller part of the fleet, to which Collins was assigned, ran into bad weather and was delayed, eventually reaching <a href="/wiki/Castlehaven" title="Castlehaven">Castlehaven</a> in southwestern Cork on 1 December, the main squadron having reached <a href="/wiki/Kinsale" title="Kinsale">Kinsale</a> more than two months earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-timoney_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-timoney-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In a letter to <a href="/wiki/Robert_Cecil,_1st_Earl_of_Salisbury" title="Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury">Robert Cecil</a>, Sir <a href="/wiki/George_Carew,_1st_Earl_of_Totnes" title="George Carew, 1st Earl of Totnes">George Carew</a>, "could not forbear", according to Irish historian Proinsias Ó Fionnagáin, "voicing his almost superstitious fear of Archer". Carew commented, "Archer the priest conjures the foul weather, which I do partly believe, for the old men have never seen the like in May. If he remains in Dunboy I hope to conjure his head in a halter. He hath a fellow devil with him, one Dominic Collins, a <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, who in his youth was a scholar and brother to him that was last year mayor of Youghal. Every week that traitorly priest administers the sacrament to them; yet I hope to sow such sedition amongst them that they will break."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the disastrous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Kinsale" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Kinsale">Battle of Kinsale</a> (24 December 1601),
Fr. Archer returned to Spain to seek further reinforcements, while <a href="/wiki/Chief_of_the_Name" title="Chief of the Name">Irish clan chief</a> <a href="/wiki/Donal_Cam_O%27Sullivan_Beare" title="Donal Cam O&#39;Sullivan Beare">Donal Cam O'Sullivan Beare</a> refused to give up his fight. Collins remained behind at <a href="/wiki/Dunboy_Castle" title="Dunboy Castle">Dunboy Castle</a> to attend to the spiritual needs of 143 Irish soldiers who were besieged by 4,000 English troops. After an <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Dunboy" title="Siege of Dunboy">eleven-day siege</a>, Dunboy fell into the hands of the English on 18 June 1602.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> While all other prisoners were immediately hanged en masse, the Jesuit and two other survivors were held prisoner. They were then taken as State prisoners to <a href="/wiki/Shandon_Castle,_Cork" title="Shandon Castle, Cork">Shandon Castle</a>, on the modern location of <a href="/wiki/Firkin_Crane" title="Firkin Crane">Firkin Crane</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Cork_City" class="mw-redirect" title="Cork City">Cork City</a>. There, they were tortured and the two surviving Irish soldiers were executed.
</p><p>Dominic Collins, however, was far more valuable to the Queen's officials alive rather than dead. More than four months were accordingly spent deciding his fate. Determined to have a Jesuit abjure the Catholic Faith in his very hometown, the Queen's officials removed Collins from Shandon Castle and brought him back to Youghal, a distance of 173&#160;km.<sup id="cite_ref-collins1943_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-collins1943-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> There, the <a href="/wiki/Lord_Deputy_of_Ireland" title="Lord Deputy of Ireland">Lord Deputy of Ireland</a>, Sir <a href="/wiki/Charles_Blount,_8th_Baron_Mountjoy" title="Charles Blount, 8th Baron Mountjoy">Charles Blount, Lord Mountjoy</a>, offered Collins a full pardon for <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a>, the favour of the Queen, and promotion to a high position within either the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Royal_Army" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish Royal Army">Irish Royal Army</a> or the <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Ireland" title="Church of Ireland">Church of Ireland</a> in return for taking the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> and accepting the <a href="/wiki/Caesaropapism" title="Caesaropapism">subservience of the Church to the State</a>. To Lord Mountjoy's fury, however, theological arguments by <a href="/wiki/Anglican_clergy" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican clergy">Anglican clergy</a> and even visits from Collins' relatives and their pleas with him to conform to <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglicanism</a> and spare them from the shame of having a family member face a traitors death, did nothing to shake the prisoner's resolve.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom_and_burial">Martyrdom and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Martyr_memorial,_Youghal.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg/220px-Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg/330px-Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg/440px-Martyr_memorial%2C_Youghal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4608" data-file-height="3456" /></a><figcaption>Site of Collins' martyrdom</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pacata_Hibernia&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pacata Hibernia (page does not exist)">Pacata Hibernia</a></i>, a detailed account of Sir George Carew's term as <a href="/wiki/President_of_Munster" class="mw-redirect" title="President of Munster">President of Munster</a>, which was compiled under his close supervision soon after the events it describes and published posthumously in 1633,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> "the Fryer, in whom no penitence appeared for his detestable treasons, nor yet would endeavour to merit his life, either by discovering the Rebells' intention, (which was in his power) or by doing <a href="/wiki/Espionage" title="Espionage">some service</a> that might deserve favour, was hanged at Youghall, the Towne wherein he was born."<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The accounts by Bishop <a href="/wiki/David_Rothe" title="David Rothe">David Rothe</a> and John Mullin confirm that, after being tried and condemned to death by <a href="/wiki/Military_tribunal" class="mw-redirect" title="Military tribunal">military tribunal</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Dominic Collins was publicly <a href="/wiki/Hanged" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanged">hanged</a> at Youghal on 31 October 1602.<sup id="cite_ref-page24_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-page24-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>According to D. P. Conyngham, "At dawn, they led him out to execution, with his hands tied behind his back and a <a href="/wiki/Halter" title="Halter">halter</a> around his neck. He walked calmly along, with his eyes raised to heaven and his mind fixed on God, reflecting on Christ bearing his Cross. When he arrived at the foot of the gallows, he fell on his knees and kissed it, commending his passage to God; then, following the example of the martyrs, he prayed for his enemies, for the Queen, and for his country, and with alacrity and a cheerful countenance ascended the ladder."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Conyngham, "Turning round on the topmost step, from thence, as from a pulpit", Collins addressed the crowd in Spanish, Irish and English. He urged local Catholics, "to preserve the Faith undaunted unto death, and disregard alike the threats and promises of the heretics."<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>So moved was the crowd that the hangman fled and a passing fisherman was forced to do carry out the execution. Collins's last words before being turned off of the ladder and hanged were, "Look up to heaven and, worthy descendants of your ancestors, who ever constantly professed it, hold fast to the faith for which I am this day to die!"<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to D. P. Conyngham, Dominic Collins was secretly buried two days later by local Catholics inside a nearby chapel and under the cover of night. He was the last of the <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a> to die for his faith during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following the <a href="/wiki/Sollicitudo_omnium_ecclesiarum" title="Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum">reversal</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Suppression_of_the_Society_of_Jesus" title="Suppression of the Society of Jesus">Suppression</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> in 1814 and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Emancipation" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Emancipation">Catholic Emancipation</a> in the <a href="/wiki/British_Isles" title="British Isles">British Isles</a> in 1829, an early 17th-century portrait of Brother Dominic Collins was brought from the <a href="/wiki/Irish_College_at_Salamanca" title="Irish College at Salamanca">Irish College at Salamanca</a> to <a href="/wiki/St_Patrick%27s_College,_Maynooth" title="St Patrick&#39;s College, Maynooth">St Patrick's College</a> at <a href="/wiki/Maynooth" title="Maynooth">Maynooth</a>, where it still remains.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Collins was beatified – along with <a href="/wiki/Francis_Taylor_(martyr)" title="Francis Taylor (martyr)">Francis Taylor</a> and 13 other <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish Catholic Martyrs</a> – on 27 September 1992 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John-Paul_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope John-Paul II">Pope John-Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">Liturgically</a> his feast is celebrated on 20 June, or 30 October (in the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>). Today a Jesuit residence in <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a> is named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Folklore">Folklore</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Dominic_Collins&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Folklore">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>According to historian and <a href="/wiki/Folklorist" class="mw-redirect" title="Folklorist">folklorist</a> Tony Nugent, Fr. James Archer and Brother Dominic Collins are associated in local <a href="/wiki/Irish_folklore" title="Irish folklore">Irish folklore</a> with a rock located in a mountain pass between <a href="/wiki/Kenmare" title="Kenmare">Kenmare</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Kerry" title="County Kerry">County Kerry</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bantry" title="Bantry">Bantry</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Cork" title="County Cork">County Cork</a>. The rock, which is known in <a href="/wiki/Munster_Irish" title="Munster Irish">Munster Irish</a> as <i>Léim an tSagairt</i> ("The Priest's Leap"), is alleged to be where Fr. Archer, Brother Dominic, or in some accounts both, leapt an enormous distance on horseback to escape pursuit by a posse of Elizabethan soldiers. A metal cross now marks the site.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
