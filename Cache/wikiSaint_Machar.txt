<p><b>Machar</b> was a 6th-century <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a> active in Scotland.
</p><p>A Bishop of Irish origin, Machar is said to have been a former nobleman, baptized by St Colman.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He came to Iona with Columba and preached in Mull and later ministered to the Picts around Aberdeen.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> For this reason he was described anachronistically as the first Bishop of the see of Aberdeen. His legend, however, in the Aberdeen breviary makes him "Archbishop of Tours", appointed by Gregory the Great for the last few years of his life. This story deserves no credence. Water from his well was used for baptism in Aberdeen Cathedral. A few dedications survive from this area.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Much of what is claimed to be known about St Machar derives from the <a href="/wiki/Aberdeen_Breviary" title="Aberdeen Breviary">Aberdeen Breviary</a>, a work compiled in the late fifteenth to early sixteenth centuries, long after the traditional date of Machar's life. It is therefore hard to assess its reliability.
</p><p>One recent theory is that St Machar and <a href="/wiki/St_Mungo" class="mw-redirect" title="St Mungo">St Mungo</a> were the same person, on the grounds of a possible link between their names (Colm Ó Baoill, <i>St Machar - some linguistic light?</i>, Innes Review XLIV, p.&#160;1-13).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commemoration">Commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Machar&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/St_Machar%27s_Cathedral" title="St Machar&#39;s Cathedral">St Machar's Cathedral</a> in Aberdeen is named in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Cathedral's font by <a href="/wiki/Hew_Lorimer" title="Hew Lorimer">Hew Lorimer</a> depicts the saint baptising converts.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Machar oil field in the <a href="/wiki/North_Sea_oil" title="North Sea oil">North Sea</a> is named after the saint.
</p><p>There is also a <a href="/wiki/St._Machar_Academy" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Machar Academy">St Machar Academy</a> in Aberdeen, situated on St Machar Drive.
</p><p>His feast day is 12 November.<sup id="cite_ref-Oxford_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Oxford-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St Machar's, Ranfurly church in <a href="/wiki/Bridge_of_Weir" title="Bridge of Weir">Bridge of Weir</a> is named in his honour after a minister from St Machar's cathedral became the minister.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
