<p><b>William Spenser</b> (died 24 September 1589) was an English <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> priest. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1987.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Spenser&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Spenser was born at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ghisburn&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ghisburn (page does not exist)">Ghisburn</a>, Yorkshire.  His maternal uncle, William Horn, who signed for the Rectory of <a href="/wiki/Cornwell,_Oxfordshire" title="Cornwell, Oxfordshire">Cornwell, Oxfordshire</a>, in 1559, sent him in 1573 to <a href="/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford" title="Trinity College, Oxford">Trinity College, Oxford</a>, where he became Fellow in 1579 and M.A. in 1580. There, convinced of the truth of Catholicism, he used his position to influence his pupils in that direction; but he delayed his conversion till 1582, when, with four other Trinity men (<a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Appletree&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John Appletree (page does not exist)">John Appletree</a>, B.A., already a priest; <a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Warford&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="William Warford (page does not exist)">William Warford</a>, M.A. and Fellow, afterwards a Jesuit; <a href="/w/index.php?title=Anthony_Shirley_(priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Anthony Shirley (priest) (page does not exist)">Anthony Shirley</a>, M.A. and Fellow, afterwards a priest; and <a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Fixer&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John Fixer (page does not exist)">John Fixer</a>, B.A., afterwards a priest), he embarked from the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Wight" title="Isle of Wight">Isle of Wight</a>.
</p><p>They landed near <a href="/wiki/Cherbourg" title="Cherbourg">Cherbourg</a>, arriving at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, 2 November. Received into the Catholic Church five days later, he was ordained <a href="/wiki/Sub-deacon" class="mw-redirect" title="Sub-deacon">sub-deacon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> at <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a> by the bishop, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Valentine_Douglas&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Valentine Douglas (page does not exist)">Valentine Douglas</a>, 7 April 1583, and priest at Reims by the Cardinal Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Louis_de_Guise" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis de Guise">Louis de Guise</a>, 24 September.
</p><p>He was sent on the English mission 29 August 1584. He effected the reconciliation of his parents and his uncle (the latter was living as a Catholic priest in 1593), and afterwards voluntarily immured himself in <a href="/wiki/York_Castle" title="York Castle">York Castle</a> to help the prisoners there.
</p><p>He was condemned under 27 Elizabeth, c. 2, for being a priest, and executed at <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>. With him was executed a layman, <b>Robert Hardesty</b>, who had given him shelter.
</p>
