<p>Saint <b>Poppo</b> (<a href="/wiki/Deinze" title="Deinze">Deinze</a>, 977 – <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes" title="Marchiennes">Marchiennes</a>, 25 January 1048) was a knight of <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">noble</a> descent who turned to a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastic</a> life after experiencing a spiritual conversion. He became one of the best known <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbots</a> of <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Stavelot" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Stavelot">Stavelot</a> and was one of the first recorded <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flemish</a> <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. 
<a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">Liturgically</a>, he is commemorated on the 25th of January.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Poppo_of_Stavelot&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Vita Popponis</i>, the biography of Poppo, was written shortly after his death by the monk Onulf and the abbot Everhelm of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Hautmont&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Hautmont (page does not exist)">Abbey of Hautmont</a>. According to this source, Poppo belonged to a noble family of Flanders; his parents being Tizekinus and Adalwif. Around the year 1000, he made a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> with two companions. Soon after this he also went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. He was about to marry a lady of noble family, when a flame suddenly burst out of the sky late at night and kept his lance radiating. Poppo believed this to be an illumination of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit" title="Holy Spirit">Holy Spirit</a>, and soon after, he decided to enter the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_of_Saint-Thierry&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey of Saint-Thierry (page does not exist)">monastery of Saint Thierry</a> at <a href="/wiki/Rheims" class="mw-redirect" title="Rheims">Rheims</a> in 1005.
</p><p>Around 1008, Abbot <a href="/w/index.php?title=Richard_of_Saint_Vannes&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Richard of Saint Vannes (page does not exist)">Richard of Saint Vannes</a> at <a href="/wiki/Verdun" title="Verdun">Verdun</a>, a reformer of monasteries, took Poppo to his monastery. Richard made Poppo prior of <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Vaast" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Vaast">St. Vaast</a> in <a href="/wiki/Arras" title="Arras">Arras</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Cambrai">Diocese of Cambrai</a>, at around 1013. This job consisted of reclaiming the monastery's lands from <a href="/wiki/Vassal" title="Vassal">vassals</a> and securing the possession of the monastery by deeds. Sometime before 1016, he was appointed to the same position at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Vasloges&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Vasloges (page does not exist)">Vasloges</a> (<i>Beloacum</i>, Beaulieu) in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Verdun" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Verdun">Diocese of Verdun</a>.
</p><p>In 1020, the German emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a> appointed Poppo Abbot of the <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Stavelot-Malmedy" class="mw-redirect" title="Principality of Stavelot-Malmedy">Abbeys of Stavelot and Malmedy</a> (in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Lower Lorraine">Lower Lorraine</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>). In 1023, Poppo also received the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Maximin" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Maximin">Abbey of St. Maximin</a> at <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blumenthal_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blumenthal-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Poppo became even more important during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Conrad_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Conrad II, Holy Roman Emperor">Conrad II</a>. From St. Maximin, the Cluniac reform now found its way into the German monasteries. The emperor placed several imperial monasteries under Poppo's control or supervision, such as <a href="/wiki/Limburg_Abbey" title="Limburg Abbey">Limburg an der Hardt</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Echternach" title="Abbey of Echternach">Echternach</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Gislen&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Gislen (page does not exist)">St. Gislen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wissembourg" title="Wissembourg">Weissenburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_St._Gall" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of St. Gall">St. Gall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld_Abbey" title="Hersfeld Abbey">Hersfeld</a>, <a href="/wiki/Waulsort" title="Waulsort">Waulsort</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hautmont" title="Hautmont">Hautmont</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hasti%C3%A8res&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hastières (page does not exist)">Hastières</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cambridge_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cambridge-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Soon after Poppo transferred these positions to his pupils and family members, the bishops and laymen who had founded these monasteries placed a series of other monasteries under his care, including <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Laurence_at_Li%C3%A8ge&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Laurence at Liège (page does not exist)">St. Laurence at Liège</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Vincent_at_Metz&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Vincent at Metz (page does not exist)">St. Vincent at Metz</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Eucharius_at_Trier&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Eucharius at Trier (page does not exist)">St. Eucharius at Trier</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hohorst" class="mw-redirect" title="Hohorst">Hohorst</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brauweiler_Abbey" title="Brauweiler Abbey">Brauweiler</a>, St. Vaast and <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes" title="Marchiennes">Marchiennes</a>. However, the reform of <a href="/wiki/Richard_of_Verdun" title="Richard of Verdun">Richard of Saint-Vanne</a> had no permanent success in the German Empire.
</p><p>Poppo practiced the most severe <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a>. He had no interest in literary affairs, lacked management capacities, and was not prominent in politics. During the reign of <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry III</a> he lost influence. Poppo died while staying at the Abbey of Marchiennes and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Stavelot" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Stavelot">Abbey of Stavelot</a>.
</p>
