<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the village with the same name, see <a href="/wiki/St_Breock" title="St Breock">St Breock</a>.</div>

<p><b>Saint Brioc</b> (<a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>: <i>Brieg</i>; <a href="/wiki/Welsh_language" title="Welsh language">Welsh</a>: <i lang="cy">Briog</i>; <a href="/wiki/Cornish_language" title="Cornish language">Cornish</a>: <i lang="kw">Breock</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Brieuc</i>; died c. 502) was a 5th-century <a href="/wiki/Welsh_people" title="Welsh people">Welsh</a> holy man who became the first <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Brieuc" title="Saint-Brieuc">Saint-Brieuc</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is one of the <a href="/wiki/Brittany#Religion" title="Brittany">seven founder saints of Brittany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-celtic_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-celtic-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Brioc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Very little is known about Brioc's early life, as his 9th century <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/acta" class="extiw" title="wikt:acta">Acta</a></i> is not altogether reliable. It states that he came from <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a> where the church at <a href="/wiki/Llandyfriog" title="Llandyfriog">Llandyfriog</a> was originally dedicated to him.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He received his education in Ireland and then studied under <a href="/wiki/Germanus_of_Auxerre" title="Germanus of Auxerre">Germanus of Auxerre</a>. He is believed to have spent time at <a href="/wiki/Rothesay,_Bute" class="mw-redirect" title="Rothesay, Bute">Rothesay</a> on the Isle of Bute in Scotland, where a church was dedicated to him and his name was commemorated in the annual St. Bruix Fair.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  He most likely returned to France early in 431, accompanied by Saint <a href="/wiki/Illtud" title="Illtud">Illtud</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 480, he settled in Armorica, and founded a monastery at Landebaeron. He then traveled to Upper Brittany where he established an <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Brieuc" title="Saint-Brieuc">St Brieuc-des-Vaux</a>, between St. Malo and Land Triguier, where he eventually became the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of a monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Authorities differ as to date of Saint Brioc's death, but it was probably in 502, or in the early years of the sixth century. He died in his own monastery at St. Brieuc-des-Vaux and was interred in his cathedral church, dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen" title="Saint Stephen">Saint Stephen</a>.
<a href="/wiki/Rothesay,_Bute" class="mw-redirect" title="Rothesay, Bute">Rothesay, Bute</a>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Brioc&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <i>Acta</i> cites numerous miracles, especially his cure of Count Riguel, who gave the saint his own Palace of Champ-du-Rouvre as also the whole manorial estates.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is represented as treading on a dragon or presented with a column of fire as seen at his ordination.
</p><p>Saint Brioc's relics were moved to the Church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus of <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a> in 865, and again, in a more solemn manner, on 31 July 1166. However, in 1210, a portion of the relics was restored to <a href="/wiki/St._Brieuc_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Brieuc Cathedral">St. Brieuc Cathedral</a>, where the saint's ring is also preserved.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In honour of Saint Brioc's link between <a href="/wiki/Ceredigion" title="Ceredigion">Ceredigion</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, the town of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Brieuc" title="Saint-Brieuc">St Brieuc</a> has been twinned with <a href="/wiki/Aberystwyth" title="Aberystwyth">Aberystwyth</a> since 1974<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and a road, Boulevard St Brieuc, is located in the town. 
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">feast day</a> is 1 May. The festival of Saint Brioc was celebrated on 1 May, but in 1804, the festival has been held on the second Sunday after Easter. Churches in England, Ireland, and Scotland are dedicated to this early Celtic saint,<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> including the parish church of <a href="/wiki/St_Breock" title="St Breock">St Breock</a> in Cornwall.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He is considered the patron saint of pursemakers.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
