<p><b>Eucherius</b> (c. 380&#160;&#8211;&#32;c. 449) was a high-born and high-ranking ecclesiastic in the Christian church in <a href="/wiki/Roman_Gaul" title="Roman Gaul">Roman Gaul</a>. He is remembered for his letters advocating extreme self-abnegation. From 439, he served as <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Lyon" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Lyon">Archbishop of Lyon</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Henry_Wace_(Anglican_priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry Wace (Anglican priest)">Henry Wace</a> ranked him "the most distinguished occupant of that see" after <a href="/wiki/Irenaeus" title="Irenaeus">Irenaeus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> within the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1913CE_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1913CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eucherius_of_Lyon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eucherius was married to <a href="/wiki/Galla_(wife_of_Eucherius)" title="Galla (wife of Eucherius)">Galla</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> They had two sons: <a href="/wiki/Veranus_of_Vence" title="Veranus of Vence">Veranus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Salonius" title="Salonius">Salonius</a>, who were born c.400.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> According to some sources, they also had two daughters, Consortia and Tullia.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>After their sons were born, Eucherius suggested that they adopt a more ascetic life together.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Galla and Eucherius' marriage evolved to a 'marriage of friendship' like others undertaken by other religious figures such as Paulinus and <a href="/wiki/Therasia_of_Nola" title="Therasia of Nola">Therasia of Nola</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The family practised "unwealth" - where life was restricted to the minimum in order to support prayer and devotion.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On the death of his wife <a href="/wiki/Galla_(wife_of_Eucherius)" title="Galla (wife of Eucherius)">Galla</a>, as was common in the 5th century, Eucherius withdrew with his sons, <a href="/wiki/Veranus_of_Vence" title="Veranus of Vence">Veranus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Salonius" title="Salonius">Salonius</a>, to the <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">monastery of Lérins</a> for a time.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Both sons were later sent to visit Paulinus of Nola.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> There he lived a severely simple life of study, devoting himself to the education of his sons. Soon afterwards he withdrew further, to the neighbouring island of Lerona (now <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Ele_Sainte-Marguerite" title="Île Sainte-Marguerite">Île Sainte-Marguerite</a>), where he devoted his time to study and <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification of the flesh</a>. With the thought that he might join the <a href="/wiki/Anchorites" class="mw-redirect" title="Anchorites">anchorites</a> in the deserts of the East, he consulted <a href="/wiki/John_Cassian" title="John Cassian">John Cassian</a>, the famed hermit who had arrived from the East in <a href="/wiki/Marseille" title="Marseille">Marseille</a>. Cassian dedicated the second set of his <i>Collationes</i> (nos. 11–17) to Eucherius and <a href="/wiki/Honoratus" title="Honoratus">Honoratus</a>, the founder of Lérins. These describe the daily lives of the hermits of the <a href="/wiki/Thebaid" title="Thebaid">Egyptian Thebaid</a> and discuss the important themes of grace, free will and scripture.<sup id="cite_ref-1913CE_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1913CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Though imitating the <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic lifestyle</a> of the Egyptian hermits, Eucherius kept in touch with men renowned for learning and piety: Cassian, Honoratus, <a href="/wiki/Hilary_of_Arles" title="Hilary of Arles">Hilary of Arles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Claudianus_Mamertus" title="Claudianus Mamertus">Claudianus Mamertus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Agroecius,_bishop_of_Sens" class="mw-redirect" title="Agroecius, bishop of Sens">Agroecius</a> (who dedicated a book to him) and <a href="/wiki/Sidonius_Apollinaris" title="Sidonius Apollinaris">Sidonius Apollinaris</a>. The fame of Eucherius was soon so widespread in southeastern Gaul that he was chosen bishop of Lyon.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> This was probably in 434. It is certain, at least, that he attended the first <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Orange_(441)" title="Council of Orange (441)">council of Orange</a> as metropolitan of Lyon in 441, and that he retained this dignity until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-1913CE_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1913CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded in the bishopric by his son Veranus, while Salonius became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Geneva" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Geneva">Bishop of Geneva</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eucherius_of_Lyon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eucherius wrote many works in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>.
</p><p>Around 428, Eucherius wrote his epistolary essay <i>De laude eremi</i> ("in praise of the desert"), addressed to Hilary of Arles.<sup id="cite_ref-1913CE_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1913CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His <i>Liber formularum spiritalis intelligentiae</i>, addressed to his son Veranus, is a defence of the lawfulness of <a href="/wiki/Allegorical_interpretation_of_the_Bible" title="Allegorical interpretation of the Bible">allegorical readins of the Bible</a>, bringing to bear the metaphors in <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a> and such phrases as "the hand of God." The term <i><a href="/wiki/Anagoge" title="Anagoge">anagoge</a></i> (ἀναγωγὴ) is employed for the application of Scripture to the heavenly Jerusalem to come, and there are other examples of what would become classic medieval hermeneutics. Among Eucherius' other letters are his <i>Institutiones ad Salonium</i>, addressed to his other son, and a letter to <a href="/wiki/Faustus_of_Riez" title="Faustus of Riez">Faustus of Lérins</a> describing his pilgriamge to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eucherius' <i>Epistola paraenetica ad Valerianum cognatum, de contemptu mundi</i> ("Epistle of exhortation to his kinsman Valerian, On the contempt of the world") is an expression of the despair for the present and future of the world in its last throes shared by many educated men of <a href="/wiki/Late_Antiquity" class="mw-redirect" title="Late Antiquity">Late Antiquity</a>, with hope for a world to come. <a href="/wiki/Desiderius_Erasmus" class="mw-redirect" title="Desiderius Erasmus">Desiderius Erasmus</a> thought so highly of its Latin style that he edited and published it at Basel (1520).
</p><p>Many homilies have been attributed to Eucherius.
</p>
