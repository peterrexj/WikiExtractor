<p><b>Saint Sabinus of Canosa</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Sabino</i>) (461 – 9 February 566), venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> church, was <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Canosa_di_Puglia" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Canosa di Puglia">bishop of Canosa di Puglia</a> from 514.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabinus_of_Canosa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was sent twice as a papal envoy to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, in 525, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_I" title="Pope John I">Pope John I</a> and in 536 to accompany <a href="/wiki/Pope_Agapitus_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Agapitus I">Pope Agapitus I</a>, who lost his life on the journey, to defend the true faith against the <a href="/wiki/Monophysite" class="mw-redirect" title="Monophysite">Monophysite</a> heresy. He attended the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Constantinople_(536)" title="Council of Constantinople (536)">Council of Constantinople (536)</a>.
</p><p>In 531, in the papacy of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_II" title="Pope Boniface II">Pope Boniface II</a>, he took part in the Synod of Rome. He was a builder of churches and other religious buildings, according to the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> discipline of <i><a href="/wiki/Ora_et_labora" title="Ora et labora">Ora et labora</a></i> ("Work and pray").
</p><p>He died after 52 years as bishop, on 9 February 566.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cult">Cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabinus_of_Canosa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sabinus was a friend of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">Saint Benedict</a>, whom he visited at <a href="/wiki/Montecassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Montecassino">Montecassino</a> and to whom, as recorded by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, he once expressed his preoccupations on the incursions of the <a href="/wiki/Ostrogoth" class="mw-redirect" title="Ostrogoth">Ostrogoth</a> King <a href="/wiki/Totila" title="Totila">Totila</a> into the Italian peninsula. According to the hagiographic legend, he succeeded in saving <a href="/wiki/Canosa_di_Puglia" title="Canosa di Puglia">Canosa di Puglia</a> from the threat of the latter. There is a story that in 548 Totila wanted to test the prophetic gifts of Sabinus, who was by then old and blind. The king, pretending to be a servant, offered him a goblet of wine, but Sabinus was not deceived and thanked him by name, which impressed Totila so much that he renounced his pillaging.
</p><p>Another legend of Sabinus relates that a jealous <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">archdeacon</a> tried to poison him. Sabinus drank the poison but did not die; but the archdeacon did. For this reason he appeared in the liturgy as a protector against poisons.
</p><p>His relics were translated to the present <a href="/wiki/Canosa_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Canosa Cathedral">Canosa Cathedral</a> on 1 August in an unknown year of the 8th century by bishop Pietro. After the destruction of the town by the Saracens, the relics were rescued from the ruins by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Angelarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Angelarius">Saint Angelarius</a> in 844 and taken to <a href="/wiki/Bari_Cathedral" title="Bari Cathedral">Bari Cathedral</a>.
</p><p>Saint Sabinus is venerated in <a href="/wiki/Canosa_di_Puglia" title="Canosa di Puglia">Canosa</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bari" title="Bari">Bari</a>, in both of which places the cathedrals are dedicated to him, in <a href="/wiki/Torremaggiore" title="Torremaggiore">Torremaggiore</a> and <a href="/wiki/Furci" title="Furci">Furci</a>.
</p><p>The only church in the <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a> dedicated to him is the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Sabinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St Sabinus">Church of St Sabinus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Woolacombe" title="Woolacombe">Woolacombe</a> in <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>.
</p>
