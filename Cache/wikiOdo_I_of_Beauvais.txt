<p><b>Odo I</b> (or <b>Eudes I</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West Frankish</a> prelate who served as <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Corbie" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Corbie">abbot of Corbie</a> in the 850s and as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Beauvais" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Beauvais">bishop of Beauvais</a> from around 860 until his death in 881. He was a courtier and a diplomat, going on missions to <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Francia</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">Holy See</a>.
</p><p>He wrote a lost treatise on Easter against the Greek practice.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEReuter199254_n._4_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEReuter199254_n._4-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He also wrote a passion of <a href="/wiki/Lucian_of_Beauvais" class="mw-redirect" title="Lucian of Beauvais">Saint Lucian</a>, modelled on the hagiographical work of <a href="/wiki/Hilduin" title="Hilduin">Hilduin</a>, and was the first to portray Lucian as the founding bishop of Beauvais.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot">Abbot</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odo_I_of_Beauvais&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Abbot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 852, or at least before April 853, <a href="/wiki/Paschasius_Radbertus" title="Paschasius Radbertus">Paschasius Radbertus</a> was removed and Odo installed as abbot of Corbie.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992179_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992179-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 855 Corbie received a privilege from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992179_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992179-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During Odo's abbacy, the monk <a href="/wiki/Ratramnus" title="Ratramnus">Ratramnus</a> wrote the treatise <i>De anima</i> (On the Soul).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick198033_n._8_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKitterick198033_n._8-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The two developed an important working relationship, with Odo depending on Ratramnus to right tracts on pressing issues even after Odo became a bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZola200841_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZola200841-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 859, <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> under <a href="/w/index.php?title=Weland_(Viking)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Weland (Viking) (page does not exist)">Weland</a> attacked Corbie, which Odo ably defended, according to <a href="/wiki/Lupus_of_Ferri%C3%A8res" class="mw-redirect" title="Lupus of Ferrières">Lupus of Ferrières</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson199191_n._9_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson199191_n._9-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was in this same wide-ranging raid that <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ermenfrid&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ermenfrid (page does not exist)">Ermenfrid</a>, Odo's predecessor at Beauvais, was probably killed.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson199191_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson199191-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The date of his death is established as 25 June in an obituary calendar preserved in <a href="/wiki/Beauvais_Cathedral" title="Beauvais Cathedral">Beauvais Cathedral</a>, but the exact year is disputed. Most probably it was in 859, as indicated by the <i><a href="/wiki/Annales_Bertiniani" title="Annales Bertiniani">Annales Bertiniani</a></i>, but the canons of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Tuzey" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Tuzey">council of Tuzey</a>, dated 22 October 860, bear Ermenfrid's signature. <a href="/wiki/Philip_Grierson" title="Philip Grierson">Philip Grierson</a> and Charles Delettre both accepted the authenticity of the Tuzey canons and thus placed his death in June 861, pushing back the start of Odo's episcopate by two years.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_n._17_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_n._17-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The electors initially chose one Fromold to succeed Ermenfrid, but he was rejected as unqualified, and their second choice was Odo. A letter of Hincmar's may allude to Fromold's rejection by a synod, which would probably be that of Tuzey. If that is the case, then Odo's election would have occurred in October–November 860.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_n._18_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_n._18-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The validity of the election was upheld in a decree (<i>decretum</i>) Odo had drawn up and witnessed by Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Hincmar_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Hincmar of Reims">Hincmar of Reims</a>. He was consecrated before November 860, since he was senior to Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Raginelm&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Raginelm (page does not exist)">Raginelm</a> of Noyon, who was consecrated on 7 November that year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop">Bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odo_I_of_Beauvais&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first notice of Odo as bishop is of his attendance at the meeting of sovereigns at <a href="/wiki/Savonni%C3%A8res" title="Savonnières">Savonnières</a> in October–November 862.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_n._18_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeck1959281_n._18-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In the early 860s, when a monk of the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Germer-de-Fly_Abbey" title="Saint-Germer-de-Fly Abbey">Saint-Germer-de-Fly</a>, which the bishop of Beauvais controlled, affirmed the heretical doctrine of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Macarius_the_Irishman&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Macarius the Irishman (page does not exist)">Macarius the Irishman</a> that there is only one soul that all men share, Odo contracted Ratramnus to write a tract, <i>Liber de anima ad Odonem Bellovacenem</i>, refuting Macarius.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZola200841_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZola200841-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When in 867 a Greek synod <a href="/wiki/Photian_schism" title="Photian schism">deposed the pope</a>, Nicholas I asked Hincmar of Reims to have a refutation of the Greeks composed. In 868, Hincmar asked Odo to do the same, and Odo commissioned Ratramnus to write it. The result was <i>Contra Graecorum opposita</i>, which defended <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">papal supremacy</a> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Filioque" title="Filioque">filioque</a></i> clause.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZola200841_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZola200841-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Odo became a courtier and favourite of King <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEZola2008233_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEZola2008233-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> (He may have been the <a href="/wiki/Palatine" title="Palatine">palatine</a> <a href="/wiki/Archchaplain" title="Archchaplain">archchaplain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992265_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992265-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>) He served Charles as an envoy to the pope in Rome in 863.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992241_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992241-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> On 6 March 870, Odo was one of the envoys of Charles who met at <a href="/wiki/Frankfurt" title="Frankfurt">Frankfurt</a> with those of his brother, King <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>, and swore to work out a partition of the kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Lotharingia" title="Lotharingia">Lotharingia</a> between the two brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992224_n._16_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992224_n._16-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 16 July 876, Odo spoke at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Ponthion" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Ponthion">Synod of Ponthion</a> in favour of recognising the primacy of <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Sens" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Sens">Archdiocese of Sens</a> in Gaul, a position that put him at odds with his metropolitan, Hincmar of Reims.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1991194_n._19_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1991194_n._19-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> After the synod, on 28 August, Charles the Bald sent Odo as ambassador to his brother, Louis the German, along with the legates <a href="/w/index.php?title=Leo_of_Sabina&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Leo of Sabina (page does not exist)">Leo of Sabina</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Fossombrone&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Peter of Fossombrone (page does not exist)">Peter of Fossombrone</a> and the bishops <a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Toscanella&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John of Toscanella (page does not exist)">John of Toscanella</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Arezzo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="John of Arezzo (page does not exist)">John of Arezzo</a>. Louis died before the embassy could reach him, and they instead dealt with his sons, <a href="/wiki/Carloman_of_Bavaria" title="Carloman of Bavaria">Carloman</a>, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Younger" title="Louis the Younger">Louis</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Fat" title="Charles the Fat">Charles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1991194_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1991194-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 14 June 877, Charles issued the famous <a href="/wiki/Capitulary_of_Quierzy" title="Capitulary of Quierzy">Capitulary of Quierzy</a>. In it he specified the membership of the council that was to supervise the king's son, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Stammerer" title="Louis the Stammerer">Louis</a>, in the exercise of the royal functions while Charles was absent in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992249–50_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992249–50-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Odo and two other bishops were charged with keeping in touch with Charles while he was away.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTENelson1992249–50_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTENelson1992249–50-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odo_I_of_Beauvais&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Odo is venerated in Catholic Church<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and Eastern Orthodox Church on 28 January<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> (<a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Beauvais" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Beauvais">Diocese of Beauvais</a> - 11 February)<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
