<p><b>Jenaro Sánchez y Delgadillo</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Mexican_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Mexican people">Mexican</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> who was executed by the Mexican military during the <a href="/wiki/Cristero_War" title="Cristero War">Cristero War</a> in that country, born on 19 September 1886 and died on 17 January 1927. He is now honored as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jenaro_S%C3%A1nchez_Delgadillo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sánchez Delgadillo was born in the town of Agualele, in the <a href="/wiki/Municipality" title="Municipality">municipality</a> of <a href="/wiki/Zapopan,_Jalisco" class="mw-redirect" title="Zapopan, Jalisco">Zapopan, Jalisco</a>, the son of Cristóbal Sánchez and Julia Delgadillo, on 19 September 1886.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> With a scholarship, he entered the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> of  the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Guadalajara" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Guadalajara">Archdiocese of Guadalajara</a> and was later <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest of the Archdiocese by <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> José de Jesús Ortíz y Rodríguez on 20 August 1911.
</p><p>Sánchez then served as a <a href="/wiki/Curate" title="Curate">curate</a> in various parishes of the Archdiocese, becoming known for his humility and his obedience to the <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastors</a> under whom he served. The care of the sick was a major focus of his ministry, as well as teaching the <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to the children of the parish.<sup id="cite_ref-Barba_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barba-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> When he was stationed in <a href="/wiki/Cocula,_Jalisco" title="Cocula, Jalisco">Cocula, Jalisco</a>, he taught classes at a <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> established within the parish.
</p><p>As a result of the increasing tension between the Catholic Church and the government of Mexico, in 1917 the Archbishop of Guadalajara, <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Orozco_y_Jim%C3%A9nez" title="Francisco Orozco y Jiménez">Francisco Orozco y Jiménez</a>, issued a <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_letter" title="Pastoral letter">pastoral letter</a> on behalf of the bishops of Mexico—most of whom were then in exile in the United States—in which he detailed the sufferings he was enduring for defending the rights of the Church against government interference. For reading this letter publicly at the Sunday service in his church, Sánchez was jailed by the local police.
</p><p>In 1923 Sánchez was appointed as the <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a> of the village of Tamazulita,<sup id="cite_ref-Barba_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barba-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> within the <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tecolotlan" class="mw-redirect" title="Tecolotlan">Tecolotlan</a>. Due to the prohibition of public worship by the Republic of Mexico, Sánchez conducted secret <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Masses</a> in private homes.<sup id="cite_ref-Staley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Staley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He and his parents were given shelter by the Castillo family at their home at Rancho La Cañada. On 17 January 1927, he was out hunting with Herculano, Crescenciano and Cresencio Castillo, Lucio Camacho and Ricardo Brambila. Soldiers were waiting for him. Though everyone said he should escape, he decided to stay and face the consequences. The soldiers took them prisoner and tied everyone back to back. The others were released but the soldiers hanged Sánchez from a nearby tree.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Before dawn, the soldiers returned shot Sánchez in the left shoulder and then lowered the body to the ground. One of the soldiers then pierced his chest with a bayonet.<sup id="cite_ref-Barba_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barba-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Sánchez' body was then taken to a private home from which it was buried in the cemetery of Tecolotlan.
</p><p>In 1934, with the approval of the Curia of Guadalajara, the remains were transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish church</a> in Tecolotlan Cocula, Jalisco. He was beatified on 22 November 1992 by John Paul II and canonized by that same pope at the Jubilee of 2000, on 21 May of that year.<sup id="cite_ref-Staley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Staley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
