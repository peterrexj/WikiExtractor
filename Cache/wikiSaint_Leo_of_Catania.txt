<p><b>Saint Leo of Catania</b>, nicknamed the <i><a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgus" class="mw-redirect" title="Thaumaturgus">Thaumaturgus</a></i>, also known as <b>St Leo the Wonderworker</b> in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> (May 703 or 709 – 20 February 789),<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was the fifteenth bishop of Catania, famed also for his love and care toward the poor. His feast day occurs on 20 February,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the day of his death in which he is venerated as a saint both by <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholics</a> and by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>. He lived in the lapse of time between the reigns of the Emperors <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Constantine_VI" title="Constantine VI">Constantine VI</a>. He struggled especially against the <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">paganism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maleficium_(sorcery)" title="Maleficium (sorcery)">sorcery</a> still prevalent in the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>.
</p><p>He left the memory of prodigies and charitable deeds, an admirable apostolate that deserved him his Greek epithet. For the natives of Catania he was simply  <i>Leone " il Maraviglioso"</i> (the <i>Wonderworker</i> or <i>He who performs Miracles</i>).
</p><p>Catania dedicated to him a peripheral suburb built around the homonymous Catholic Parish but even the name of the sole Eastern Orthodox church of the city, harboured in a temple that still maintains
the primal name of <i><a href="/wiki/Michael_(archangel)" title="Michael (archangel)">Saint Michael</a> the Lesser</i>, restored recently and consecrated again to the purpose.
</p><p>He is, moreover, the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicilian</a> localities of <a href="/wiki/Rometta" title="Rometta">Rometta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Longi" title="Longi">Longi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sinagra,_Sicily" title="Sinagra, Sicily">Sinagra</a>. The hamlet of <a href="/wiki/Saracena" title="Saracena">Saracena</a> in <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a> celebrates him twice a year in spring and in late summer.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leo_of_Catania&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Leo was born at <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> (then under the suzerainty of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Roman_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Roman Empire">Eastern Roman Empire</a>) and at a very young age he became a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a>, he then moved to <a href="/wiki/Reggio_Calabria" title="Reggio Calabria">Reggio Calabria</a> in Southern Italy where the local bishop Cyril nominated him to be an archdeacon. He stayed there until his episcopal election for the vacant Diocese of Catania.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A local legend asserts that the Catanians, who needed a new bishop, had a collective dream wherein an Angel suggested them to search for the selected person in the <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabrian</a> city of <a href="/wiki/Reggio_Calabria" title="Reggio Calabria">Reggio</a> where a man in odour of sanctity lived in an hermitage. That stranger would have become the right guide to fill suchlike post. At first Leo was reluctant as he considered himself not worthy for this ordeal so he did not accept and decided to refute politely such summary acclaim. Afterwards, when the umpteen solicitations from Catania became doubtless and heartfelt, he persuaded at last. In fact on 765 he was appointed to rule over that Community of Christians.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In the coming epoch, in every regions of the Byzantine Empire (of whom Catania with the entire Sicily was a dominion) began the fierce and unrestrainable destruction of the sacred icons, the so-called "<a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">iconoclasm</a>", which Leo openly opposed to.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Owing to his firmness, the Byzantine Governor of Sicily ordered his arrest, forcing him to leave Catania and find refuge on the Tyrrhenian territories of the island. He wandered for many years through the woody Nebrodian heights, in the whereabouts between <a href="/wiki/Longi" title="Longi">Longi</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sinagra,_Sicily" title="Sinagra, Sicily">Sinagra</a>, protected by the people that recognized him as a fervent opposer of the Imperial power.
</p><p>He reached, during this long peregrination a place called <a href="/wiki/Rometta" title="Rometta">Rometta</a>. Here, on the <a href="/wiki/Monti_Peloritani" class="mw-redirect" title="Monti Peloritani">Monti Peloritani</a> backing <a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a>, he lived in a cave he hollowed out all by himself with his very hands and fingernails. Shortly after, when his persecution seemed to calm down he was able to come back to Catania where he repossessed his bishopric to keep on fighting with more strength than ever against the iconoclast laws and the new and growing gurgles of heresy.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He died in Catania on 20 February 789.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="A_Catanian_legend:_the_Thaumaturge_and_the_Charmer">A Catanian legend: the Thaumaturge and the Charmer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Leo_of_Catania&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: A Catanian legend: the Thaumaturge and the Charmer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a local account, among the candidates who were excluded from being vested with the episcopate, there was a character called <a href="/wiki/Heliodorus_of_Catania" title="Heliodorus of Catania">Heliodorus</a>. Catanian dignitary of noble birth, he probably denied his Christian belief because of envy and rage for a choice so sudden and unexplainable to him, bearing a malice towards the foreign-born nominee and his fellow-citizens.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>For this reason, he began devoting himself to the occult and magic with the sole aim to grow into Leo's worst adversary and noisome disturber to fascinate and lead astray with any sort of wizardries his occasional spectators in order to acquire easily compliant followers.
</p><p>On the other hand, Leo always tried peacefully to convince Heliodorus that his behavior and deviltries were thoroughly wrong. But in vain. They met each other for the last time on 778 AD and their final clash will have a large echo throughout Sicily, to rebound even to the Imperial court of Constantinople.
</p><p>During a Mass officiated by the Saint Prelate in the whilom mother-church of Catania, Heliodorus noisily rushed into the nave, slinking away along the pews to spellbind and confound the believers engrossed in the Sunday rite. In further versions of the same tale, many story-tellers want him to clamour inside the temple in the likeness of a black elephant or with the retinue of a trumpeting pachyderm.
</p><p>Leo was constrained to conclude the Liturgy and determined on ending those roaring witcheries he drew away from the altar and forced his way through the parishioners to face that "demonic jester". Deranged by sorrow, he drew the conclusion that all his mild approaches and patient argumentations would have not been efficient any more. So, he decided to dare that impious enchanter to show publicly and prove baldly he who professed the rightest creed.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>After ordering to heap up wood for a pyre in a furnace inside the close Achillean Thermal Baths, Leo suddenly enwrapped his <a href="/wiki/Omophorion" title="Omophorion">Omophorion</a> round the abashed miscreant dragging him towards the chosen place where the balefire was already crackling. Both were immediately enfolded by highest flames that transformed their lineations and clothes in embers.
</p><p>Only Leo will survive to this test of faith. He came out of the stake with undamaged vestments that kept shining about his unscathed body. The other unwilling contestant was fated to succumb instead. The only traces of his funeral pile were, as a matter of fact, a mass of smoking and glittering ashes.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
