<p><b>Maughold</b> (also known as <b>Macaille, Maccaldus, Machalus, Machaoi, Machella, Maghor, Mawgan, Maccul, Macc Cuill</b>; died c. 488 AD) is venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Man" title="Isle of Man">Isle of Man</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Tradition states that he was an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> prince and captain of a band of freebooters who was converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>. His feast day is 25 April.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His original name is unclear,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> but was probably adapted from Bishop MacCaille of Croghan, County Offaly, who received <a href="/wiki/Brigit_of_Kildare" class="mw-redirect" title="Brigit of Kildare">Brigit of Kildare</a> into religious life<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maughold&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg/220px-Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg/330px-Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg/440px-Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Maughold_Head_-_geograph.org.uk_-_459826.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Maughold head</div></div></div>
<p>One local legend relates that Maughold tried to make a fool out of Patrick. Maughold had, according to this story, placed a living man in a shroud. He then called for Patrick to try to revive the allegedly dead man. Patrick came, placed a hand on the shroud, and left. When Maughold and his friends opened the shroud, they found the man had died in the interim. One of Maughold's friends, a fellow named Connor, went over to Patrick's camp and apologized to him. Patrick returned and baptized all of the men assembled. He then blessed the man who had died, who immediately returned to life, and was also baptized. Patrick then criticized Maughold, saying he should have been helping his men to lead good lives, and told him he must make up for his evil.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As penance for his previous crimes, Patrick ordered him to abandon himself to God in a wicker boat without oars.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Maughold drifted to this isle, where two of Patrick's disciples, Romulus and Conindrus (Romuil and Conindri), were already established. Tradition says he landed on the northeast corner of the Isle near Ramsey, at the foot of a headland since called <a href="/wiki/Maughold_Head" title="Maughold Head">Maughold Head</a>, where he established himself in a cave on the mountainside. He is said to have been chosen by the <a href="/wiki/Manx_people" title="Manx people">Manx people</a> to succeed Romuil and Conindri as bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Maughold is today best remembered on the Isle of Man for his kind disposition toward the Manx natives. Several places on the island, including, <a href="/wiki/Maughold_(parish)" title="Maughold (parish)">Maughold parish</a>, St Maughold's Well,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and St Maughold's Chair are named after him.
</p>
