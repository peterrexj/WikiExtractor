					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Ancient Roman mythological figure</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other uses, see <a href="/wiki/Amata_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Amata (disambiguation)">Amata (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard" style="width: 22.5em;"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above fn" style="background: #99BADD; color: #000000;"><div class="fn" style="display:inline">Amata</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-subheader">Queen of <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latins</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Spouse(s)</th><td class="infobox-data" style="text-align: left;"><a href="/wiki/Latinus" title="Latinus">Latinus</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Issue</th><td class="infobox-data" style="text-align: left;"><a href="/wiki/Lavinia" title="Lavinia">Lavinia</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Roman_mythology" title="Roman mythology">Roman mythology</a>, <b>Amata</b> <span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="/eɪ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;face&#39;">eɪ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span></span>/</a></span></span> (also called <b>Palanto</b>) was the wife of <a href="/wiki/Latinus" title="Latinus">Latinus</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Latins_(Italic_tribe)" title="Latins (Italic tribe)">Latins</a>, and the mother of their only child, <a href="/wiki/Lavinia" title="Lavinia">Lavinia</a>.  In the <a href="/wiki/Aeneid" title="Aeneid">Aeneid</a> of <a href="/wiki/Virgil" title="Virgil">Virgil</a>, she commits suicide during the conflict between <a href="/wiki/Aeneas" title="Aeneas">Aeneas</a> and <a href="/wiki/Turnus" title="Turnus">Turnus</a> over which of them would marry Lavinia.  
</p><p>When Aeneas asks for Lavinia's hand, Amata objects, because she has already been promised to Turnus, the king of the <a href="/wiki/Rutuli" class="mw-redirect" title="Rutuli">Rutulians</a>.  Hiding her daughter in the woods, she enlists the other Latin women to instigate a war between the two. Turnus, and his ally <a href="/wiki/Mezentius" title="Mezentius">Mezentius</a>, leader of the <a href="/wiki/Etruscan_civilization" title="Etruscan civilization">Etruscans</a>, are defeated by Aeneas with the assistance of the Pelasgian colonists from Arcadia and <a href="/wiki/Aborigines_(mythology)" title="Aborigines (mythology)">Italic natives</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pallantium" title="Pallantium">Pallantium</a>, led by that city's founder, the <a href="/wiki/Arcadia_(ancient_region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Arcadia (ancient region)">Arcadian</a> <a href="/wiki/Evander_of_Pallene" class="mw-redirect" title="Evander of Pallene">Evander of Pallene</a>.   The story of this conflict fills the greater part of the seventh book of <a href="/wiki/Virgil" title="Virgil">Virgil</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Aeneid" title="Aeneid">Aeneid</a></i>. When Amata believes that Turnus had fallen in battle, she hangs herself.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-DGRBM_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DGRBM-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h2><span id="Dante.27s_purgatorio"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Dante's_purgatorio">Dante's purgatorio</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amata&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Dante&#039;s purgatorio">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Amata's suicide is also referred to in Canto 17 of <i><a href="/wiki/Purgatorio" title="Purgatorio">Purgatorio</a></i>, the second canticle of <a href="/wiki/Dante_Alighieri" title="Dante Alighieri">Dante Alighieri</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Divine_Comedy" title="Divine Comedy">Divine Comedy</a></i>, to demonstrate dreadful effects of anger. Dante imagines a mournful Lavinia, reproaching her mother, Amata, for the grief which her suicide has inflicted. Parallels have been drawn between Dante and his representation of Amata in <i>Purgatorio</i>. After his exile from Florence and the <a href="/wiki/Guelphs_and_Ghibellines" title="Guelphs and Ghibellines">Black Guelph</a> takeover, Dante may have experienced that same self-recrimination experienced by Amata, which led to her suicide.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
