<p><b>Saint Ginés de la Jara</b> (also known as <b>Ginés de la Xara</b>, <b>Ginés el Franco</b>, <b>Genesius Sciarensis</b>) is a semi-legendary <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>.  He is associated with the region surrounding <a href="/wiki/Cartagena,_Spain" title="Cartagena, Spain">Cartagena</a>, of which he is co-<a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a>.  A <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> was founded adjacent to the <a href="/wiki/Mar_Menor" title="Mar Menor">Mar Menor</a>, and ruins of a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> bearing his name date from before the Moorish conquest of 711 AD, that is, from the <a href="/wiki/Visigothic" class="mw-redirect" title="Visigothic">Visigothic</a> era.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity_and_legends">Historicity and legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gin%C3%A9s_de_la_Jara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Historicity and legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pre-Christian or <a href="/wiki/Muslim" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim">Muslim</a> origins for the cult of Saint Ginés have been suggested, including identification with the cult of a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Genius_(mythology)" title="Genius (mythology)">genius</a> or with an Islamic <a href="/wiki/Genie" class="mw-redirect" title="Genie">jinn</a>; as well as with an ancient <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Carthage" title="Ancient Carthage">Carthaginian</a> site dedicated to the god <a href="/wiki/Ba%27al" class="mw-redirect" title="Ba&#39;al">Ba'al</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The subsequent association of the site with Christian hermits and <a href="/wiki/Anchorites" class="mw-redirect" title="Anchorites">anchorites</a> is indisputable.  However, there is no actual tomb or sepulchre for Ginés: the location of his relics was a cause for the invention of multiple legends.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Some scholars believe the saint may be identical with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Genesius_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Genesius of Arles">Saint Genesius of Arles</a>, in Spanish known as <i>San Ginés de Arlés</i>, who was martyred in the 4th century.<sup id="cite_ref-elsanto_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-elsanto-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  His feast day is identical to that of Genesius of Arles, a connection that some scholars consider as proof that they are identical.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  According to Serafino Prete, the spread and popularity of Genesius’ cult in other cities of Gaul and beyond gave rise to the multiplication and “localization” of his cult, so that the saints Genesius of Alvernia, Genesius of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%A9ziers" title="Béziers">Béziers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Genesius_of_Rome" title="Genesius of Rome">Genesius of Rome</a>, Genesius of Cordoba and Ginés de la Jara are actually variations on the same saint and saint’s cult.<sup id="cite_ref-santiebeati_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santiebeati-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A legend that appears in a manuscript dating from 1243, <i>Liber Sancti Iacobi</i>, states that the martyr of Arles was buried at Arles but that his head was transported miraculously "in the hands of angels" to Cartagena.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  This may represent an attempt to explain the existence of the cult of the same saint in two separate locations.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  An additional variation on the legend states that after Ginés was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">decapitated</a> in southern France, he <a href="/wiki/Cephalophore" title="Cephalophore">picked up his head</a> and threw it into the <a href="/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Rhône River">Rhône</a>.  The head was carried by sea to the coast of Murcia, where it was venerated as a relic.<sup id="cite_ref-gettyguide_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gettyguide-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>No definite dates regarding his birth and death exist.<sup id="cite_ref-elsanto_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-elsanto-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> However, a vigorous set of legends surrounding him arose.  He is believed to have sailed from <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> around 800 AD and to have been <a href="/wiki/Shipwreck" title="Shipwreck">shipwrecked</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Murcia" title="Murcia">Murcian</a> coast, where he established a monastery.  Another legend made him a kinsman of <a href="/wiki/Roland" title="Roland">Roland</a>.  Ginés refused any claim to the throne of France.  After his death, the <a href="/wiki/Coffin" title="Coffin">coffin</a> bearing his remains were brought to France.  However, they were miraculously empty when they arrived there; the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> remained near the Mar Menor.<sup id="cite_ref-elsanto_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-elsanto-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Additional stories state that he went on a <a href="/wiki/Way_of_Saint_James" class="mw-redirect" title="Way of Saint James">pilgrimage to Compostela</a>, having various adventures on the way.  On the hill known as Cabezo del Miral, he remained until his death.  His fame grew and his <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> became a place of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a>.  Miracles multiplied there.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gin%C3%A9s_de_la_Jara&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The spot of Ginés' supposed hermitage at the Mar Menor survived as a sacred site during the age of <a href="/wiki/Al-Andalus" title="Al-Andalus">Muslim</a> rule (and was mentioned by Moorish authors).<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  After the area’s conquest by the <a href="/wiki/Castilians" title="Castilians">Castilians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile" title="Alfonso X of Castile">Alfonso X of Castile</a> restored the bishopric and founded the monastery of San Ginés de la Jara (1250).<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The site of his monastery was officially declared a holy place and place of pilgrimage by Alfonso X.  It was a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> monastery before passing to the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a>.
</p><p>The monastery, re-founded in 1491 and rebuilt in the 16th century, is the centre of the cult of this saint.  It is considered the resting place of his relics.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
His cult has been described as essentially local, though it spread to nearby areas, such as <a href="/wiki/Lorca,_Spain" title="Lorca, Spain">Lorca</a>, Murcia, <a href="/wiki/Orihuela" title="Orihuela">Orihuela</a>, and even <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sierraminera_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sierraminera-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ginés inspired great devotion, and he was considered by local <a href="/wiki/Vintner" class="mw-redirect" title="Vintner">vintners</a> their patron.  He was considered the protector of agricultural labourers and of the fields.  Sailors also invoked his aid against storms.  He was also invoked against illnesses and conditions such as <a href="/wiki/Hernia" title="Hernia">hernias</a> in children.<sup id="cite_ref-elsanto_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-elsanto-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1541, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a> officially <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> him.  His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 25 August.<sup id="cite_ref-elsanto_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-elsanto-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1692, <a href="/wiki/La_Roldana" class="mw-redirect" title="La Roldana">La Roldana</a> made a <a href="/wiki/Polychrome" title="Polychrome">polychromed</a> sculpture of Ginés de la Jara (now at the <a href="/wiki/Getty_Center" title="Getty Center">Getty Center</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-gettyguide_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gettyguide-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
