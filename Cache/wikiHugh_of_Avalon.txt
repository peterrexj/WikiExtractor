<p><b>Hugh of Lincoln</b>, O.Cart. (<span title="circa">c.</span><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1140</span><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup> &#8211; 16 November 1200), also known as <b>Hugh of Avalon</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a>-born <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> monk, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lincoln" title="Bishop of Lincoln">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Lincoln" title="Diocese of Lincoln">Lincoln</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">Kingdom of England</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">saint</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_the_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of the saints">feast</a> is observed by Catholics on 16 November and by Anglicans on 17 November.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_of_Lincoln&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hugh was born at the château of <a href="/wiki/Avalon,_France" title="Avalon, France">Avalon</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-BHOLincoln_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOLincoln-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-birth2_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-birth2-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-birth3_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-birth3-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> at the border of the <a href="/wiki/Dauphin%C3%A9" title="Dauphiné">Dauphiné</a> with <a href="/wiki/Savoy" title="Savoy">Savoy</a>, the son of Guillaume, seigneur of Avalon. His mother Anne de Theys died when he was eight and, because his father was a soldier, he went to a boarding school for his education.<sup id="cite_ref-huntington_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huntington-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Guillaume retired from the world to the Augustinian monastery of Villard-Benoît, near Grenoble, and took his son Hugh, with him.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of fifteen, Hugh became a religious novice and was ordained a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> at the age of nineteen.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> About 1159, he was sent to be <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of the nearby monastery at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maximin,_Is%C3%A8re" title="Saint-Maximin, Isère">Saint-Maximin</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> presumably already a <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>. From that community, he left the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine Order</a> and entered the <a href="/wiki/Grande_Chartreuse" title="Grande Chartreuse">Grande Chartreuse</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-BHOLincoln_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOLincoln-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> then at the height of its reputation for the rigid austerity of its rules and the earnest piety of its members. There he rose to become <a href="/wiki/Procurator_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Procurator (Catholic Church)">procurator</a> of his new Order, in which office he served until he was sent in 1179<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> to become <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Witham_Charterhouse" title="Witham Charterhouse">Witham Charterhouse</a> in Somerset, the first Carthusian house in England.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOLincoln_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOLincoln-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II of England</a>, as part of his penance for the murder of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a>, in lieu of going on crusade as he had promised in his first remorse, had established a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">charterhouse</a> sometime before, which was settled by monks brought from the Grande Chartreuse. There were difficulties in advancing the building works, however, and the first prior was retired and a second soon died. It was by the special request of the English king that Hugh, whose fame had reached him through one of the nobles of Maurienne, was made prior.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hugh found the monks in dire straits, living in log huts and with no plans yet advanced for the more permanent monastery building. Hugh interceded with the king for royal patronage and at last, probably on 6 January 1182, Henry issued a charter of foundation and endowment for Witham Charterhouse.  His first attention was given to the building of the Charterhouse. He prepared his plans and submitted them for royal approbation, exacting full compensation from the king for any tenants on the royal estate who would have to be evicted to make room for the building.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Hugh presided over the new house till 1186 and attracted many to the community. Among the frequent visitors was King Henry, for the charterhouse lay near the borders of the king's chase in <a href="/wiki/Selwood_Forest" title="Selwood Forest">Selwood Forest</a>, a favourite hunting ground. Hugh admonished Henry for keeping dioceses vacant in order to keep their income for the royal chancellery.
</p><p>In May 1186, Henry summoned a council of bishops and barons at <a href="/wiki/Eynsham" title="Eynsham">Eynsham</a> Abbey to deliberate on the state of the Church and the filling of vacant bishoprics, including Lincoln. On 25 May 1186 the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral chapter</a> of Lincoln was ordered to elect a new bishop and Hugh was elected.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOLincoln_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOLincoln-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Hugh insisted on a second, private election by the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a>, securely in their <a href="/wiki/Chapter_house" title="Chapter house"> chapterhouse</a> at Lincoln rather than in the king's chapel. His election was confirmed by the result.
</p><p>Hugh was consecrated <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lincoln" title="Bishop of Lincoln">Bishop of Lincoln</a> on 21 September 1186<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook255_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook255-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> at <a href="/wiki/Westminster_Abbey" title="Westminster Abbey">Westminster</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOLincoln_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOLincoln-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Almost immediately he established his independence of the King, excommunicating a royal forester and refusing to seat one of Henry's courtly nominees as a <a href="/wiki/Prebend" class="mw-redirect" title="Prebend">prebendary</a> of Lincoln; he softened the king's anger by his diplomatic address and tactful charm. After the excommunications, he came upon the king hunting and was greeted with dour silence. He waited several minutes and the king called for a needle to sew up a leather bandage on his finger. Eventually Hugh said, with gentle mockery, "How much you remind me of your cousins of <a href="/wiki/Falaise,_Calvados" title="Falaise, Calvados">Falaise</a>" (where <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of England">William I</a>'s unmarried mother <a href="/wiki/Herleva" title="Herleva">Herleva</a>, a tanner's daughter, had come from). At this Henry just burst out laughing and was reconciled. As a bishop, he was exemplary, constantly in residence or travelling within his diocese, generous with his charity, and scrupulous in the appointments he made. He raised the quality of education at the cathedral school.  Hugh was also prominent in trying to protect the <a href="/wiki/Judaism_in_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Judaism in England">Jews</a>, great numbers of whom lived in Lincoln, in the persecution they suffered at the beginning of <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_England" title="Richard I of England">Richard I</a>'s reign, and he put down popular violence against them – as later occurred following the death of <a href="/wiki/Little_Saint_Hugh_of_Lincoln" title="Little Saint Hugh of Lincoln">Little Saint Hugh of Lincoln</a> – in several places.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:LincCathplanDehio.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/LincCathplanDehio.jpg/260px-LincCathplanDehio.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="126" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/LincCathplanDehio.jpg/390px-LincCathplanDehio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d5/LincCathplanDehio.jpg/520px-LincCathplanDehio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1372" data-file-height="664" /></a><figcaption>A plan of Lincoln Cathedral drawn by G Dehio (died 1932)</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Lincoln_Cathedral" title="Lincoln Cathedral">Lincoln Cathedral</a> had been badly damaged by an earthquake in 1185, and Hugh set about rebuilding and greatly enlarging it in the new <a href="/wiki/Gothic_architecture" title="Gothic architecture">Gothic style</a>; however, he only lived to see the choir well begun. In 1194, he expanded the <a href="/wiki/St_Mary_Magdalen%27s_Church,_Oxford" title="St Mary Magdalen&#39;s Church, Oxford">St Mary Magdalen's Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a>. Along with <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salisbury" title="Bishop of Salisbury">Bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Herbert_Poore" title="Herbert Poore">Herbert</a> of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Salisbury" title="Diocese of Salisbury">Salisbury</a>, Hugh resisted the <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_England" title="Richard I of England">king</a>'s demand for 300 <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knights</a> for a year's service in <a href="/wiki/Richard_I_of_England#Later_years_and_death" title="Richard I of England">his French wars</a>; the entire revenue of both men's offices was then seized by royal agents.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As one of the premier bishops of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">Kingdom of England</a> Hugh more than once accepted the role of diplomat to France for Richard and then for <a href="/wiki/John_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="John of England">King John</a> in 1199, a trip that ruined his health. He consecrated <a href="/wiki/St_Giles%27_Church,_Oxford" title="St Giles&#39; Church, Oxford">St Giles' Church, Oxford</a>, in 1200. There is a cross consisting of interlaced circles cut into the western column of the tower that is believed to commemorate this. Also in commemoration of the consecration, <a href="/wiki/St_Giles%27_Fair" title="St Giles&#39; Fair">St Giles' Fair</a> was established and continues to this day each September.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> While attending a national council in London, a few months later, he was stricken with an unnamed ailment and died two months later on 16 November 1200.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook255_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook255-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in <a href="/wiki/Lincoln_Cathedral" title="Lincoln Cathedral">Lincoln Cathedral</a>.
</p><p>Bishop Hugh was responsible for the building of the first (wooden) Bishop's Palace at <a href="/wiki/Buckden,_Cambridgeshire" title="Buckden, Cambridgeshire">Buckden</a> in Huntingdonshire, halfway between Lincoln and London. Later additions to the Palace were more substantial and a tall brick tower was added in 1475, protected by walls and a moat, and surrounded by an outer bailey. It was used by the bishops until 1842. The Palace, now known as <a href="/wiki/Buckden_Towers" title="Buckden Towers">Buckden Towers</a>, is owned by the <a href="/wiki/Claretians" title="Claretians">Claretians</a> and is used as a retreat and conference centre. A Catholic church, dedicated to St Hugh, is located on the site.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_of_Lincoln&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg/350px-Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg" decoding="async" width="350" height="149" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg/525px-Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg/700px-Tour_d%27Avalon_Saint-Maximin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4912" data-file-height="2091" /></a><figcaption>Tour d'Avalon, <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maximin,_Is%C3%A8re" title="Saint-Maximin, Isère">Saint-Maximin, Isère</a>, marking St Hugh's birthplace</figcaption></figure>
<p>Hugh was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> on 17 February 1220,<sup id="cite_ref-BHOLincoln_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOLincoln-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of sick children, sick people, shoemakers and swans. Hugh is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">honoured</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church_in_the_United_States_of_America)" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church in the United States of America)">Episcopal Church (USA)</a> on <a href="/wiki/November_17" title="November 17">17 November</a>.
</p><p>Hugh's <i>Vita</i>, or written life, was composed by his chaplain <a href="/wiki/Adam_of_Eynsham" title="Adam of Eynsham">Adam of Eynsham</a>, a Benedictine monk and his constant associate; it remains in manuscript form in the <a href="/wiki/Bodleian_Library" title="Bodleian Library">Bodleian Library</a> in Oxford.
</p><p>Hugh is the <a href="/wiki/Eponym" title="Eponym">eponym</a> of <a href="/wiki/St_Hugh%27s_College,_Oxford" title="St Hugh&#39;s College, Oxford">St Hugh's College, Oxford</a>, where a 1926 statue of the saint stands on the stairs of the Howard Piper Library. In his right hand, he holds an effigy of Lincoln Cathedral, and his left hand rests on the head of a swan.
</p><p>At Avalon, a round tower in the <a href="/wiki/Gothic_Revival" class="mw-redirect" title="Gothic Revival">Romantic Gothic style</a> was built by the Carthusians in 1895 in Hugh's honour on the site of the castle where he was born.<sup id="cite_ref-Avalon_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Avalon-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_of_Lincoln&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hugh's primary emblem is a white <a href="/wiki/Swan" title="Swan">swan</a>, in reference to the story of the swan of <a href="/wiki/Stow,_Lincolnshire" title="Stow, Lincolnshire">Stow, Lincolnshire</a> (site of a palace of the bishops of Lincoln) which had a <a href="/wiki/Imprinting_(psychology)" title="Imprinting (psychology)">deep and lasting friendship</a> with the saint, even guarding him while he slept. The swan would follow him about, and was his constant companion while he was at Lincoln. Hugh loved all the animals in the monastery gardens, especially a wild swan that would eat from his hand and follow him about, and yet the swan would attack anyone else who came near Hugh.<sup id="cite_ref-huntington_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huntington-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hugh_of_Lincoln&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Both <a href="/wiki/Buckden_Towers" title="Buckden Towers">Buckden Towers</a>, and the local Roman Catholic Church in nearby <a href="/wiki/St_Neots" title="St Neots">St Neots</a>, are administered by the <a href="/wiki/Claretians" title="Claretians">Claretians</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In Lincoln, there is the Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/St_Hugh%27s_Church,_Lincoln" title="St Hugh&#39;s Church, Lincoln">St Hugh's Church</a>. There are many parish churches dedicated to St Hugh of Lincoln throughout England including the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Hugh_of_Lincoln,_Letchworth" title="Church of St Hugh of Lincoln, Letchworth">Church of St Hugh of Lincoln</a> in <a href="/wiki/Letchworth" title="Letchworth">Letchworth</a> founded by <a href="/wiki/Adrian_Fortescue" title="Adrian Fortescue">Adrian Fortescue</a>.
</p><p>A number of churches are dedicated to St Hugh of Lincoln in the United States, including: Episcopal Churches in Elgin, Illinois;<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> and Allyn, Washington;<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> St Hugh of Lincoln Roman Catholic Church, Huntington Station, New York<sup id="cite_ref-huntington_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-huntington-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and St Hugh of Lincoln Roman Catholic Church in Milwaukee, Wisconsin,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> St Hugh Roman Catholic Church and School in Coconut Grove, Miami, Florida.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2018 St Hugh was made a subject of the BBC Radio 4 drama <i>The Man who bit Mary Magdalene</i> by Colin Bytheway, starring <a href="/wiki/David_Jason" title="David Jason">David Jason</a> as the bishop in search of relics that would help in the construction of Lincoln Cathedral.
</p>
