<p><b>Joan of France</b> (French: <i lang="fr">Jeanne de France, Jeanne de Valois</i>; 23 April 1464 – 4 February 1505), was briefly <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen of France">Queen of France</a> as wife of <a href="/wiki/King_Louis_XII" class="mw-redirect" title="King Louis XII">King Louis XII</a>, in between the death of her brother, <a href="/wiki/Charles_VIII_of_France" title="Charles VIII of France">King Charles VIII</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Annulment" title="Annulment">annulment</a> of her marriage. After that, she retired to her domain, where she soon founded the <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_orders" title="Enclosed religious orders">monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Annunciation_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, where she served as <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>. From this Order later sprang the <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> of the Apostolic Sisters of the Annunciation, founded in 1787 to teach the children of the poor. She was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on 28 May 1950.<sup id="cite_ref-AC_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joan_of_France,_Duchess_of_Berry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Joan was born on 23 April 1464 in the castle of <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_II_de_Br%C3%A9z%C3%A9" class="extiw" title="fr:Pierre II de Brézé">Pierre II de Brézé</a>, a trusted supporter of her grandfather, King <a href="/wiki/Charles_VII_of_France" title="Charles VII of France">Charles VII of France</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Nogent-le-Roi" title="Nogent-le-Roi">Nogent-le-Roi</a> in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Dreux" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Dreux">County of Dreux</a>. She was the second daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Louis_XI_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XI of France">Louis XI of France</a> and of his second wife <a href="/wiki/Charlotte_of_Savoy" title="Charlotte of Savoy">Charlotte of Savoy</a>; her surviving siblings were King Charles VIII of France and <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_France" title="Anne of France">Anne of France</a>. Shortly after her birth, the king signed an agreement to marry her to his second cousin Louis, the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Duke of Orléans">Duke of Orléans</a>, later King <a href="/wiki/Louis_XII_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XII of France">Louis XII of France</a>, who was aged two at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-OVM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OVM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Joan was born sickly and deformed. In <i>Women Saints – Lives of Faith and Courage</i>, Kathleen Jones says that Joan had a hump on her back and walked with a limp, suggesting that she had an abnormal curvature of the spine.<sup id="cite_ref-Gibson_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gibson-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg/200px-Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="316" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg/300px-Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg/400px-Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg 2x" data-file-width="981" data-file-height="1550" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Victor_Schnetz_1835_Ste_Jeanne_de_France.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Portrait Joan of Valois as <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen of France">Queen of France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Often away on royal duties, King Louis entrusted his daughters, Joan and Anne, to the <a href="/wiki/Baron" title="Baron">Baron</a> François de Linières and his wife, Anne de Culan. The couple, who were childless, lavished affection on Joan. Taking charge of her education, they had her taught both poetry and mathematics, painting, embroidery and how to play the <a href="/wiki/Lute" title="Lute">lute</a>.
</p><p>The couple were also faithful Catholics and instilled in the members of their household a solid grounding in the faith. At a young age, her father asked her to name the <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> she wanted. She gave him the only name she knew, that of Friar Jean de La Fontaine, <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Guardian</a> of the Franciscan friary in <a href="/wiki/Amboise" title="Amboise">Amboise</a>. The king approved her choice and appointed the friar to this post. Despite the distance between them, he would travel regularly to hear the princess's <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a>. Joan began to develop a strong pleasure in prayer, and would pass long periods in the castle chapel. The baron supported her in this and had a path paved between the castle and the chapel built for easier walking in poor weather. Under the friar's guidance she was admitted into the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-AC_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1471 King Louis XI ordered the <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> of reciting the <a href="/wiki/Hail_Mary" title="Hail Mary">Hail Mary</a> for peace to be practised throughout the kingdom. Joan had a strong attachment to this particular prayer. She would later write that it was in that same year that she had received a prophecy from the Virgin Mary that some day she would found a religious community in honor of Our Lady.
<sup id="cite_ref-Capes_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Capes-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joan_of_France,_Duchess_of_Berry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg/150px-COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg.png" decoding="async" width="150" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg/225px-COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1c/COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg/300px-COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="610" data-file-height="670" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:COA_french_queen_Jeanne_de_France.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Coat-of-arms" class="mw-redirect" title="Coat-of-arms">Coat-of-arms</a> for the Queen Joan of France.</div></div></div>
<p>In 1473 King Louis had signed <a href="/wiki/Marriage_contract" class="mw-redirect" title="Marriage contract">marriage contracts</a> for his daughters. On 8 September 1476, at the age of 12, Joan was married to the young <a href="/wiki/Louis_XII_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XII of France">Louis, Duke of Orléans</a> in <a href="/wiki/Montrichard" title="Montrichard">Montrichard</a>. The marriage was arranged because Joan, due to her malformation, was supposed to be sterile. By doing so, Louis XI hoped to extinguish the Orléans <a href="/wiki/Cadet_branch" title="Cadet branch">cadet branch</a> of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Valois" title="House of Valois">House of Valois</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Louis was displeased at the forced marriage, and his treatment of his new wife reflected this.
</p><p>King Louis died in 1483 and was succeeded by his son Charles, but as he was still a child, his sister, Anne de Beaujeu, was made <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">Regent</a> of the kingdom. By 1484, the duke had begun a series of military campaigns against the kingdom. This lasted until 1488, when he was taken captive by the royal forces. During this period, he fathered an illegitimate son, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Michel_de_Bussy&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Michel de Bussy (page does not exist)">Michel de Bussy</a>, who was later appointed the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bourges" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bourges">Bishop of Bourges</a>. Joan administered his domain during his imprisonment, especially the Italian cities of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Asti" title="Asti">Asti</a>. Joan, imagining virtues in her husband that did not exist, exerted herself to mitigate his sufferings and to get him freed.<sup id="cite_ref-Capes_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Capes-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Duke Louis was released in 1491. Within a few years, he accompanied King Charles on his military campaign in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-OVM_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OVM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:188px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Joan_of_Valois_Queen_of_France.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f2/Joan_of_Valois_Queen_of_France.jpg" decoding="async" width="186" height="271" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="186" data-file-height="271" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Joan_of_Valois_Queen_of_France.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A contemporary portrait of Joan of France.</div></div></div>
<p>When Louis ascended to the throne in April 1498 after the accidental death of Joan's brother, King <a href="/wiki/Charles_VIII_of_France" title="Charles VIII of France">Charles VIII</a>, he appealed to the pope to have the marriage annulled in order to marry the late king's widow, <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Brittany" title="Anne of Brittany">Anne of Brittany</a>, in the hope of annexing the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brittany" title="Duchy of Brittany">Duchy of Brittany</a> to the French Crown.
</p><p>In what has been described as "one of the seamiest lawsuits of the age",<sup id="cite_ref-Hale,_p.15_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hale,_p.15-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Louis did not, as might be expected, argue the marriage to be void due to <a href="/wiki/Consanguinity" title="Consanguinity">consanguinity</a> (the general excuse for the dissolution of a marriage at that time). Louis argued that he had been below the legal age of consent (fourteen) to marry and that the marriage had never been consummated<sup id="cite_ref-Capes_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Capes-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> due to her physical deformity, and provided a rich variety of detail as to how she was malformed. Joan, unsurprisingly, fought this uncertain charge fiercely, producing witnesses to Louis boasting of having "mounted my wife three or four times during the night."<sup id="cite_ref-Hale,_p.15_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hale,_p.15-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Louis also claimed that his sexual performance had been inhibited by witchcraft; Joan responded by asking how, in that case, he was able to know what it was like to try to make love to her.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Joan would likely have won, for Louis's case was exceedingly weak, however <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VI" title="Pope Alexander VI">Pope Alexander VI</a> was committed for political reasons to grant the annulment.
</p><p>The commission of investigation appointed by the pope established that the marriage with Joan was invalid for lack of consent and that it never had been consummated.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Accordingly, he ruled against the Queen.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> The annulment was declared on 15 December 1498. Joan stepped aside, saying that she would pray for her former husband. She was made <a href="/wiki/Duchess_of_Berry" class="mw-redirect" title="Duchess of Berry">Duchess of Berry</a> and retired to <a href="/wiki/Bourges" title="Bourges">Bourges</a>, capital of the duchy.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life_and_sainthood">Religious life and sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joan_of_France,_Duchess_of_Berry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Religious life and sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Stjeannedevalois.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Stjeannedevalois.jpg/200px-Stjeannedevalois.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="300" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Stjeannedevalois.jpg/300px-Stjeannedevalois.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Stjeannedevalois.jpg/400px-Stjeannedevalois.jpg 2x" data-file-width="682" data-file-height="1024" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Stjeannedevalois.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Joan of Valois, foundress of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Annunciation_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Once settled in her new domain, Joan confided to her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> Gabriel Mary, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">O.F.M.</a>, <sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> her call to monastic life. He supported her in this venture, and she began to make plans for the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Annunciation_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a>, a new <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Enclosed religious order">enclosed religious order</a> in honour of the <a href="/wiki/Annunciation" title="Annunciation">Annunciation</a> (the announcement to Virgin Mary by an angel that she would become the mother of Jesus, although she was a virgin). It was established as an independent branch of the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a>. By May 1500 she had received 11 <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulants</a>, the nucleus of the new Order. The Rule of Life she had written for the Order was approved on 12 February 1502 by Pope Alexander. Construction of the first monastery was begun the following year. On <a href="/wiki/Pentecost_Sunday" class="mw-redirect" title="Pentecost Sunday">Pentecost Sunday</a> 1504, Joan and Friar Gabriel Mary made private commitments to follow the Rule, and thereby established themselves as co-founders of the Order. On 21 November of that same year, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">Feast</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Presentation_of_Mary" title="Presentation of Mary">Presentation of Mary</a>, Joan and the other women publicly and legally committed themselves to the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-OVM_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OVM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Joan died on 4 February 1505 and was buried in the chapel of the Annonciade monastery. Her grave, however, was desecrated during the <a href="/wiki/French_Wars_of_Religion" title="French Wars of Religion">French Wars of Religion</a> and her body, which was found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a>, was burned by the <a href="/wiki/Huguenot" class="mw-redirect" title="Huguenot">Huguenots</a> during their sack of Bourges on 27 May 1562.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Soon after her death, miracles and healings attributed to her were said to have occurred. The cause for her canonization was begun in 1631 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> her on 21 April 1742. She was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 28 May 1950<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joan_of_France,_Duchess_of_Berry&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister" title="Religious sister">religious sisters</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Annunciation_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Order of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary</a> still maintain their way of life in monasteries in France, Belgium, Costa Rica and Poland. The sisters serve in Belgium, Burundi, Cameroon, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, France and Guatemala.<sup id="cite_ref-OVM_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OVM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
