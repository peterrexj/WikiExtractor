<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox biography vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above" style="font-size:125%;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="font-size: 77%; </div></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Edigna</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1055</span>–1109) is a <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> figure in <a href="/wiki/Puch_(F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck)" class="mw-redirect" title="Puch (Fürstenfeldbruck)">Puch</a>, and is <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. Her historical existence is debated.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to legend, Edigna was a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_France" title="Henry I of France">Henry I of France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anne_of_Kiev" title="Anne of Kiev">Anne of Kiev</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and was born <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1055</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1074, at the age of 19,<sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> she fled to <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a> on a farmer's <a href="/wiki/Bullock_cart" title="Bullock cart">bullock cart</a> to escape an <a href="/wiki/Arranged_marriage" title="Arranged marriage">arranged marriage</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The farmer stopped in <a href="/wiki/Puch_(F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck)" class="mw-redirect" title="Puch (Fürstenfeldbruck)">Puch</a>, <a href="/wiki/F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck" title="Fürstenfeldbruck">Fürstenfeldbruck</a>, where a rooster in the cart crowed and a bell rang. Edigna took this as a sign that she should leave the cart. She remained in Puch until her death on 26 February, 1109, living as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in a hollowed-out <a href="/wiki/Linden_tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Linden tree">linden tree</a> and revered by the people as a <a href="/wiki/Miracle_worker" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracle worker">miracle worker</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She did not reveal her royal background, but it was discovered after her death.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When she died, <a href="/wiki/Chrism" title="Chrism">holy oil</a> flowed from the tree, but it dried up when attempts were made to sell it.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Edigna has been <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> since her death, and regarded as the <a href="/wiki/Patroness_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness saint">patroness saint</a> of Puch.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historical_evidence">Historical evidence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Historical evidence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="In_support">In support</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In support">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1347, a document related to the death of <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Louis IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Louis IV</a> near Puch contained the first known written mention of Edigna. In 1624, <a href="/wiki/Matthew_Rader" title="Matthew Rader">Matthäus Rader</a> examined her corpse and subsequently wrote a biography of her.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Edigna was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> in 1600.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1976, a grave in the church was discovered, which could have been the burial site of Edigna.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A 1639 <a href="/wiki/Votive_tablet" class="mw-redirect" title="Votive tablet">votive tablet</a> describes a child from <a href="/wiki/Mammendorf" title="Mammendorf">Mammendorf</a> who recovered from an illness immediately after completing a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to the site.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Against">Against</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Against">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Henry I of France and Anne of Kiev are known to have had four children, named Phillip, Robert, Hugo, and Emma. However, Emma and Edigna may be the same person, because few details about Emma are known.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_modern_day">In the modern day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In the modern day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Puch_(F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck)_St._Sebastian_203.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Altar depicting Edigna at the Church of St. Sebastian in Puch" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Puch_%28F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck%29_St._Sebastian_203.jpg/220px-Puch_%28F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck%29_St._Sebastian_203.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Puch_%28F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck%29_St._Sebastian_203.jpg/330px-Puch_%28F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck%29_St._Sebastian_203.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Puch_%28F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck%29_St._Sebastian_203.jpg/440px-Puch_%28F%C3%BCrstenfeldbruck%29_St._Sebastian_203.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3053" data-file-height="4580" /></a><figcaption>Altar depicting Edigna at the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Church_of_St._Sebastian_(Puch)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Church of St. Sebastian (Puch) (page does not exist)">Church of St. Sebastian</a> in Puch</figcaption></figure>
<p>A street in Puch called the <span title="German-language text"><i lang="de">Edignaweg</i></span> leads past the local <a href="/w/index.php?title=Church_of_St._Sebastian_(Puch)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Church of St. Sebastian (Puch) (page does not exist)">Church of St. Sebastian</a>, in which an altar is dedicated to Edigna, and past a linden tree.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Ukrainians" title="Ukrainians">Ukrainians</a> often make pilgrimages to Puch because Edigna's mother, Anne of Kiev, was from Ukraine. In 2007, <a href="/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko" title="Viktor Yushchenko">Viktor Yushchenko</a> made such a visit while <a href="/wiki/President_of_Ukraine" title="President of Ukraine">President of Ukraine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> An <a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna_Association&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Edigna Association (page does not exist)">Edigna Association</a> and <a href="/wiki/Decennial" class="mw-redirect" title="Decennial">decennial</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna_Games&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Edigna Games (page does not exist)">Edigna Games</a> exist in Puch. As of January&#160;2021<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup>, three women in Puch were named Edigna, while eight others had it as their middle name.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/W%C3%B6rth_an_der_Donau" title="Wörth an der Donau">Wörth an der Donau</a>, Edigna is venerated by a church called the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Church_of_St._Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Church of St. Edigna (page does not exist)">Church of St. Edigna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Edigna is considered to be the <a href="/wiki/Patroness_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness saint">patroness saint</a> of Puch<sup id="cite_ref-:0_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and a patroness against <a href="/wiki/Cattle_diseases" class="mw-redirect" title="Cattle diseases">cattle diseases</a> and <a href="/wiki/Theft" title="Theft">theft</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Edignalinde">The <i>Edignalinde</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edigna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: The Edignalinde">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A linden tree in Puch called the <i><span title="German-language text"><i lang="de">Edignalinde</i></span></i>, said to be the same tree in which Edigna lived, is near the town cemetery. <a href="/wiki/Julius_Langbehn" title="Julius Langbehn">Julius Langbehn</a>, a <a href="/wiki/German_nationalism" title="German nationalism">German nationalist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Antisemitism" title="Antisemitism">antisemite</a> who admired Edigna, was buried near the tree in 1907 at his own request; a nearby street is also named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
