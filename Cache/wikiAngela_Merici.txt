<p><b>Angela Merici</b> or <b>Angela de Merici</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;r&#39; in &#39;rye&#39;">r</span><span title="/iː/: &#39;ee&#39; in &#39;fleece&#39;">iː</span><span title="/tʃ/: &#39;ch&#39; in &#39;China&#39;">tʃ</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling">mə-<span style="font-size:90%">REE</span>-chee</i></a>, <small>Italian:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="it-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Italian" title="Help:IPA/Italian">[ˈandʒela (de) meˈriːtʃi]</a></span>; 21 March 1474 – 27 January 1540) was an Italian religious educator who is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. She founded the <a href="/wiki/Company_of_St._Ursula" class="mw-redirect" title="Company of St. Ursula">Company of St. Ursula</a> in 1535 in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>, in which women dedicated their lives to the service of the church through the education of girls. From this organisation later sprang the monastic <a href="/wiki/Ursulines" title="Ursulines">Order of Saint Ursula</a>, whose nuns established places of prayer and learning throughout Europe and, later, worldwide, most notably in North America.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_Merici&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Merici was born in 1474 on a farm near <a href="/wiki/Desenzano_del_Garda" title="Desenzano del Garda">Desenzano del Garda</a>, a small town on the southwestern shore of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Garda" title="Lake Garda">Lake Garda</a> in Lombardy, Italy. She and her older sister, Giana Maria, were left orphans when she was ten years old.<sup id="cite_ref-missouricity_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-missouricity-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> They went to live with their uncle in the town of <a href="/wiki/Sal%C3%B2" title="Salò">Salò</a>. Young Angela was very distressed when her sister suddenly died without receiving the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">last rites</a> of the church and prayed that her sister's soul rest in peace. It is said that in a vision she received a response that her sister was in heaven in the company of the saints.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She joined the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a> around that time. People began to notice Angela's beauty and particularly to admire her hair. As she had promised herself to God, and wanted to avoid the worldly attention, she dyed her hair with soot.
</p><p>Merici's uncle died when she was twenty years old and she returned to her home in Desenzano, and lived with her brothers,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> on her own property, given to her in lieu of the dowry that would otherwise have been hers had she married. She later had another vision that revealed to her that she was to found an association of virgins who were to devote their lives to the religious training of young girls. This association was a success and she was invited to start another school in the neighboring city of <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg/220px-Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="331" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg/330px-Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg/440px-Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg 2x" data-file-width="563" data-file-height="846" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ang%C3%A8le_M%C3%A9rici.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St. Angela Merici (17th century)</div></div></div>
<p>According to legend, in 1524, while traveling to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, Merici suddenly became blind when she was on the island of <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a>. Despite this, she continued her journey to the Holy Land and was ostensibly cured of her blindness on her return, while praying before a <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a>, at the same place where she had been struck with blindness a few weeks earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-ott_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ott-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1525, she journeyed to Rome in order to gain the <a href="/wiki/Indulgence" title="Indulgence">indulgences</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christian)">Jubilee Year</a> then being celebrated. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a>, who had heard of her virtue and success with her school, invited her to remain in Rome. Merici disliked notoriety, however, and soon returned to Brescia.
</p><p>On 25 November 1535, Merici gathered with 12 young women who had joined in her work in a small house in Brescia near the Church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Afra" title="Saint Afra">St. Afra</a>, where together they committed themselves in the founding of the <b>Company of St. Ursula</b>, placed under the protection of the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patroness</a> of medieval universities. Her goal was to elevate family life through the Christian education of future wives and mothers. They were the first teaching order of women religious.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Four years later the group had grown to 28.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Merici taught her companions to serve God, but to remain in the world, teaching the girls of their own neighborhood, and to practice a religious form of life in their own homes.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> The members wore no special habit and took no formal <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a>.  Merici wrote a Rule of Life for the group, which specified the practice of <a href="/wiki/Celibacy" title="Celibacy">celibacy</a>, poverty and obedience in their own homes. The Ursulines opened orphanages and schools. On 18 March 1537, she was elected "Mother and Mistress" of the group. The Rule she had written was approved in 1544 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Merici died in Brescia on 27 January 1540,<sup id="cite_ref-missouricity_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-missouricity-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> there were 24 communities of the Company of St. Ursula serving the Catholic Church through the region.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Her body was clothed in the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> of a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Secular_Franciscan_Order" title="Secular Franciscan Order">tertiary</a> and was interred in the <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Afra,_Brescia" title="Sant&#39;Afra, Brescia">Church of Sant'Afra</a>.
</p><p>The traditional view is that Merici believed that better Christian education was needed for girls and young women, to which end she dedicated her life. Querciolo Mazzonis argues that the Company of St. Ursula was not originally intended as a charitable group specifically focused on the education of poor girls, but that this direction developed after her death in 1540, sometime after it received formal recognition in 1546.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_Merici&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg/220px-320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg/330px-320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg/440px-320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:320BresciaSAngelaInside.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt body</a> of Saint Angela Merici in <a href="/wiki/Brescia" title="Brescia">Brescia</a>, Italy</div></div></div>
<p>During her life, Merici had often prayed at the tombs of the Brescian martyrs at the Church of St. Afra in Brescia. She lived in small rooms attached to a priory of the <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular_of_the_Lateran" title="Canons Regular of the Lateran">Canons Regular of the Lateran</a>. According to her wishes, after her death, she was interred in the Church of St Afra to be near the martyrs' remains. There her body remained until the complete destruction of this church and its surrounding area by Allied bombing during the <a href="/wiki/Second_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="Second World War">Second World War</a>, on 2 March 1945, in which the <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> and many townspeople died. The church and corresponding buildings were afterwards rebuilt, and reopened on 10 April 1954. The church was consecrated on 27 January 1956, with a new dedication to Saint Angela Merici, while the Parish of St. Afra was transferred to the neighboring Church of St. Eufemia.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Merici was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 30 April 1768, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a>. She was later <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 24 May 1807 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_Merici&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Merici was not included in the 1570 <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> because she was not canonized until 1807. In 1861, her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was included in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Calendar">Roman Calendar</a> – not on the day of her death, 27 January, since this date was occupied by the feast day of Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">John Chrysostom</a>, but instead on 31 May. In 1955, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> assigned this date to the new feast of the <a href="/wiki/Queen_of_Heaven" title="Queen of Heaven">Queenship of Mary</a>, and moved Merici's feast to 1 June. The celebration was ranked as a Double until 1960, when <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> gave it the equivalent rank of Third-Class Feast. Lastly, in the major 1969 reform of the liturgy, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> moved the celebration, ranked as a <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">Memorial</a>, to the saint's day of death, 27 January.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
