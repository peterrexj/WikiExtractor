<p><b>Saint Begga</b> (also <b>Begue</b>, <b>Begge</b>) (b. 613 – d. 17 December 693 AD) was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Landen" title="Pepin of Landen">Pepin of Landen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_palace" title="Mayor of the palace">mayor of the palace</a> of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>, and his wife <a href="/wiki/Itta" class="mw-redirect" title="Itta">Itta</a> of Metz. She is also the grandmother of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a>, who is the grandfather of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Begga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The daughter of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Landen" title="Pepin of Landen">Pepin of Landen</a> and his wife, <a href="/wiki/Itta" class="mw-redirect" title="Itta">Itta</a>, Begga was the older sister of St <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles" title="Gertrude of Nivelles">Gertrude of Nivelles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She married <a href="/wiki/Ansegisel" title="Ansegisel">Ansegisel</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Arnulf,_Bishop_of_Metz" class="mw-redirect" title="Arnulf, Bishop of Metz">Arnulf, Bishop of Metz</a>, and had three children: <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Heristal" class="mw-redirect" title="Pepin of Heristal">Pepin of Heristal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Laon" title="Martin of Laon">Martin of Laon</a>, and Clotilda of <a href="/wiki/Heristal" class="mw-redirect" title="Heristal">Heristal</a>, who married <a href="/wiki/Theuderic_III_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="Theuderic III of the Franks">Theuderic III of the Franks</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Ansegisel was killed sometime before 679, slain in a feud by his enemy Gundewin. Begga made a pilgrimage to Rome and upon her return, she took the <a href="/wiki/Veil" title="Veil">veil</a>, she had seven churches built at <a href="/wiki/Andenne" title="Andenne">Andenne</a> on the Meuse.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There she spent the rest of her days as abbess. She was buried in Saint Begga's Collegiate Church in Andenne.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Begga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She is commemorated on 17 December.
</p><p>Some hold that the <a href="/wiki/Beguine" class="mw-redirect" title="Beguine">Beguine</a> movement which came to light in the 12th century was actually founded by St Begga; and the church in the beguinage of <a href="/wiki/Lier,_Belgium" title="Lier, Belgium">Lier, Belgium</a>, has a statue of St Begga standing above the inscription: <i>St. Begga, our foundress</i>. The Lier beguinage dates from the 13th century.
</p><p>Another popular theory, however, claims that the Beguines derived their name from that of the priest <a href="/wiki/Lambert_le_B%C3%A8gue" title="Lambert le Bègue">Lambert le Bègue</a>, under whose protection the witness and ministry of the Beguines flourished.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
