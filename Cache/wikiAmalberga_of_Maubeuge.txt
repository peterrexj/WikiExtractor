<p><b>Saint Amalberga of Maubeuge</b> (also  <b>Amalia</b>, or <b>Amelia</b> of <b><a href="/wiki/Lobbes" title="Lobbes">Lobbes</a></b> or <b><a href="/wiki/Binche" title="Binche">Binche</a></b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and saint who lived in the 7th century.  
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Narrative">Narrative</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amalberga_of_Maubeuge&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Narrative">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Amalberga's father was Saint <a href="/wiki/Geremarus" title="Geremarus">Geremarus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
She was born in Brabant. She is said to have been the niece of <a href="/wiki/Pippin_of_Landen" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin of Landen">Pippin of Landen</a> and married Count Witger, <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Lorraine">Duke of Lorraine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In her biography she is presented as the mother of five saints: <a href="/wiki/Emebert" title="Emebert">Emebert</a>, <a href="/wiki/Reineldis" title="Reineldis">Reineldis</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pharaildis" title="Pharaildis">Pharaildis</a>, Ermelindis and <a href="/wiki/Gudula" title="Gudula">Gudula</a>.  
</p><p>Sometime after the birth of their youngest child, Gudula, Witger decided to become a Benedictine in Lobbes; Amalberga joined the Benedictine nuns of Maubeuge.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her feast is celebrated on July 10. The translation of her <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> from Lobbes to Binche in the 15th century is celebrated on June 10.
</p><p>Amalberga of Maubeuge is not to be confused with the virgin <a href="/wiki/Amalberga_of_Temse" title="Amalberga of Temse">Amalberga of Temse</a> (venerated in <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, <a href="/wiki/Temse" title="Temse">Temse</a> and <a href="/wiki/Munsterbilzen" class="mw-redirect" title="Munsterbilzen">Munsterbilzen</a>) who died in 772, and whose feast day is July 10 or October 27.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amalberga_of_Maubeuge&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The biography of Amalberga of Maubeuge (<a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/la:Vita_S._Amalbergae_viduae" class="extiw" title="s:la:Vita S. Amalbergae viduae">s:la:Vita S. Amalbergae viduae</a>) is probably written by Abbott Hugo of Lobbes (1033–1063) between 1033 and 1048. Apart from a few Merovingian details, her genealogy was copied from another 11th-century hagiography, namely the <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/la:Passio_S._Katharinae" class="extiw" title="s:la:Passio S. Katharinae">Martyr story of Catherine of Alexandria</a>. The biographical profile of her legendary husband, duke Witger of Lotharingia, is based on an historical figure from the 10th century, <a href="/wiki/Wigeric_of_Lotharingia" title="Wigeric of Lotharingia">Wigeric of Lotharingia</a>. It is largely considered legendary and unreliable.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
