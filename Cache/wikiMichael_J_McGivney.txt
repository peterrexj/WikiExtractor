					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Founder of the Knights of Columbus and Blessed</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Michael J. <p><b>Michael Joseph McGivney</b> (August 12, 1852&#160;&#8211;&#32;August 14, 1890) was an Irish-American <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> priest based in <a href="/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut" title="New Haven, Connecticut">New Haven, Connecticut</a>. He founded the <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_Columbus" title="Knights of Columbus">Knights of Columbus</a> at a local parish to serve as a mutual aid and fraternal insurance organization, particularly for immigrants and their families. It developed through the 20th century as the world's largest Catholic fraternal organization.
</p><p>The cause for his <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Roman_Catholic_Church" title="Canonization">canonization</a> started in the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Hartford" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Hartford">Archdiocese of Hartford</a> in 1996; in March 2008, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> declared McGivney "Venerable" in recognition of his "heroic virtue".<sup id="cite_ref-zenit_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zenit-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On May 27, 2020, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> announced that McGivney had been approved for beatification, which officially occurred on October 31, 2020.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsagency.com_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsagency.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Michael_J._McGivney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born to Irish immigrant parents, Patrick and Mary (née Lynch) McGivney. He was the eldest of 13 children, six of whom died in infancy or childhood. His father worked as a molder in a <a href="/wiki/Waterbury,_Connecticut" title="Waterbury, Connecticut">Waterbury, Connecticut</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brass_mill" title="Brass mill">brass mill</a>. Michael attended the local Waterbury district school but left at 13 to work in one of the brass mills' spoon-making departments.<sup id="cite_ref-ph_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ph-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Studies">Studies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Michael_J._McGivney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Studies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1868, at the age of 16, he entered the <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9minaire_de_Saint-Hyacinthe" class="mw-redirect" title="Séminaire de Saint-Hyacinthe">Séminaire de Saint-Hyacinthe</a> in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Hyacinthe" title="Saint-Hyacinthe">Saint-Hyacinthe</a>, <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>. He continued his studies at <a href="/wiki/Niagara_University" title="Niagara University">Our Lady of Angels Seminary</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Niagara_Falls,_New_York" title="Niagara Falls, New York">Niagara Falls, New York</a><sup id="cite_ref-ph_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ph-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> (1871–1872) and at the Jesuits' St. Mary's College, in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, Quebec. While in seminary, he and other seminarians formed a <a href="/wiki/Baseball" title="Baseball">baseball</a> team and McGivney was a "naturally talented ballplayer."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He had to leave the seminary, returning home to help finish raising his siblings after his father's death in June 1873.<sup id="cite_ref-cpbn_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cpbn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> McGivney later resumed his studies at <a href="/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Seminary_and_University" title="St. Mary&#39;s Seminary and University">St. Mary's Seminary</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland" class="mw-redirect" title="Baltimore, Maryland">Baltimore, Maryland</a>; he was ordained a priest on December 22, 1877, by <a href="/wiki/James_Gibbons" title="James Gibbons">James Gibbons</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Baltimore" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Baltimore">Archbishop of Baltimore</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_the_National_Shrine_of_the_Assumption_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary" title="Basilica of the National Shrine of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary">Cathedral of the National Shrine of the Assumption</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ph_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ph-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founding_of_the_Knights_of_Columbus">Founding of the Knights of Columbus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Michael_J._McGivney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Founding of the Knights of Columbus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>From his own experience, McGivney recognized the devastating effect on immigrant families of the father and wage earner's untimely death. Many Catholics were still struggling to assimilate into the American economy.<sup id="cite_ref-cpbn_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cpbn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On March 29, 1882, while an assistant <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a> at <a href="/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Church,_New_Haven" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Mary&#39;s Church, New Haven">Saint Mary's Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/New_Haven,_Connecticut" title="New Haven, Connecticut">New Haven, Connecticut</a>, McGivney founded the Knights of Columbus, with a small group of parishioners,<sup id="cite_ref-cpbn_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cpbn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> as a mutual aid society, to provide financial assistance, in the event of the men's deaths, to their widows and orphans. The organization developed as a <a href="/wiki/Fraternal_society" class="mw-redirect" title="Fraternal society">fraternal society</a>. McGivney was also known for his tireless work among his parishioners.<sup id="cite_ref-zenit_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-zenit-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>McGivney spent seven years at St. Mary's, then became pastor of St. Thomas Church in Thomaston in 1884. He died from <a href="/wiki/Pneumonia" title="Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> at the age of 38 in Thomaston on the eve of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption</a> in 1890.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>The Knights of Columbus was among the first groups to recruit blood donors, with formal efforts dating to 1937 during the <a href="/wiki/Great_Depression" title="Great Depression">Great Depression</a>. As of 2013, the order had more than 1.8&#160;million member families and 15,000 councils. During the 2012 fraternal year, the order donated $167&#160;million and 70&#160;million <a href="/wiki/Man-hour" title="Man-hour">man-hours</a> to charity.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 2020, the order reached the 2&#160;million member mark.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> During the 2019 fraternal year, the order donated $187&#160;million and 77&#160;million <a href="/wiki/Man-hour" title="Man-hour">man-hours</a> to charity.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cause_of_beatification_and_canonization">Cause of beatification and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Michael_J._McGivney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cause of beatification and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1996, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Hartford" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Hartford">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Hartford</a> opened the cause for canonization, an investigation into McGivney's life with a view towards formal recognition by the Catholic Church of his sainthood. Gabriel O'Donnell is the postulator of McGivney's cause. He is also the director of the Fr. McGivney Guild, which now has 150,000 members supporting his cause.<sup id="cite_ref-Annual2013_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Annual2013-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The diocesan investigation was closed in 2000, and the case was passed to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>. On March 15, 2008, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> approved a decree recognizing McGivney's <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>, thus declaring him as "<a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-cpbn_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cpbn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As of May&#160;5,&#32;2015<sup class="plainlinks noexcerpt noprint asof-tag update" style="display:none;"><a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Michael_J._McGivney&amp;action=edit">&#91;update&#93;</a></sup>, a miracle attributed to McGivney's intercession was under investigation at the Vatican.<sup id="cite_ref-Annual2013_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Annual2013-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> On May 27, 2020, the miracle attributed to the intercession of McGivney was approved by Congregation for the Causes of Saints and authorized by Pope Francis.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicnewsagency.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicnewsagency.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Miracle involved the healing of Michael "Mikey" Schachle in his mother's womb after being given a zero percent chance of survival by doctors at Vanderbilt Medical Center in 2015.<sup id="cite_ref-tennesseeregister_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tennesseeregister-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On October 31, 2020, the <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> Mass of Michael McGivney was celebrated at the Cathedral of Saint Joseph in <a href="/wiki/Hartford,_Connecticut" title="Hartford, Connecticut">Hartford, Connecticut</a> with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Joseph_W._Tobin" title="Joseph W. Tobin">Joseph W. Tobin</a>  presiding on the Pope's behalf.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Michael_J._McGivney&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>1989, the <a href="/wiki/York_Catholic_District_School_Board" title="York Catholic District School Board">York Catholic District School Board</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ontario" title="Ontario">Ontario</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>, founded a school named <a href="/wiki/Father_Michael_McGivney_Catholic_Academy" title="Father Michael McGivney Catholic Academy">Father Michael McGivney Catholic Academy</a> in his honor.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> It is located in <a href="/wiki/Markham,_Ontario" title="Markham, Ontario">Markham</a> and currently<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Dates_and_numbers#Chronological_items" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers"><span title="The time period mentioned near this tag is ambiguous. (December 2022)">when?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> has 1,400 students.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Douglas_Brinkley" title="Douglas Brinkley">Douglas Brinkley</a> and <a href="/wiki/Julie_M._Fenster" title="Julie M. Fenster">Julie M. Fenster</a>'s biography of Fr. McGivney, <a href="/wiki/Parish_Priest_(book)" title="Parish Priest (book)"><i>Parish Priest: Father Michael McGivney and American Catholicism</i></a>, was published by <a href="/wiki/William_Morrow_and_Company" title="William Morrow and Company">William Morrow and Company</a> in 2006.</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/The_Catholic_University_of_America" class="mw-redirect" title="The Catholic University of America">The Catholic University of America</a> renamed a prominent building on their campus as McGivney Hall.</li></ul>
<ul><li>A stained-glass window depicting McGivney was dedicated September 12, 2009, at St. John Fisher Seminary in Stamford, Connecticut, by Bishop <a href="/wiki/William_E._Lori" title="William E. Lori">William E. Lori</a>, of Bridgeport. The window was created by Rohl's Stained and Leaded Glass Studio of <a href="/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York" title="New Rochelle, New York">New Rochelle, New York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-guild_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guild-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Father_McGivney_Catholic_High_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Father McGivney Catholic High School">Father McGivney Catholic High School</a> in <a href="/wiki/Glen_Carbon,_Illinois" title="Glen Carbon, Illinois">Glen Carbon, Illinois</a></li></ul>
