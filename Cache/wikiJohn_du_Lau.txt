<p><b>Jean-Marie du Lau d'Allemans</b> (30 October 1738, <a href="/wiki/Biras" title="Biras">Biras</a> – 2 September 1792, <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>) was the last <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Arles">Archbishop of Arles</a>, and was one of the Catholic Martyrs of September 1792, killed in the course of the <a href="/wiki/September_Massacres" title="September Massacres">September Massacres</a> which occurred during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 17 October 1926 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean_Marie_du_Lau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg/250px-Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="294" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg/375px-Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7c/Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg/500px-Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1329" data-file-height="1563" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Mgr_Du_Lau_par_Carli.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A <a href="/wiki/Bas-relief" class="mw-redirect" title="Bas-relief">bas-relief</a> in marble of Jean-Marie du Lau by <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francois_Carli" class="extiw" title="fr:Francois Carli">Francois Carli</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Trophime,_Arles" title="Church of St. Trophime, Arles">Church of St. Trophime, Arles</a>, which had served as his <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>Lau was born on 30 October 1738 at the Château de la Côte at <a href="/wiki/Biras" title="Biras">Biras</a>, then in the Province of <a href="/wiki/Perigord" class="mw-redirect" title="Perigord">Perigord</a>, of an aristocratic family which had provided many members into the higher ranks of the clergy. His father was Armand du Lau, Lord of La Coste, and his mother Françoise de Salleton.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Churchman">Churchman</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean_Marie_du_Lau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Churchman">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After studies at the <a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A8ge_de_Navarre" class="mw-redirect" title="Collège de Navarre">Collège de Navarre</a>, Lau gained a <a href="/wiki/Licentiate_of_Theology" title="Licentiate of Theology">Licentiate of Theology</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">Sorbonne</a> and then embarked on his ecclesiastical career, aided by his uncle, the <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A9" title="Abbé">Abbé</a> Jean du Lau, <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint-Sulpice" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Saint-Sulpice">Church of Saint-Sulpice</a> in Paris since 1750. As was the system, he passed from one diocese to another in a rising curve of authority and prestige: canon and treasurer at <a href="/wiki/Pamiers" title="Pamiers">Pamiers</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vicar_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicar General">Vicar General</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Bordeaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Bordeaux">Diocese of Bordeaux</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">Prior</a> of Gabillon and in 1770 attained the notable rank of <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agent_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_du_clerg%C3%A9_de_France" class="extiw" title="fr:Agent général du clergé de France">Agent General of the Clergy of France</a>. On 1 October 1775, he was promoted by <a href="/wiki/King_Louis_XVI" class="mw-redirect" title="King Louis XVI">King Louis XVI</a> to the office of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Arles">Archbishop of Arles</a>, the youngest of the king's <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">episcopal</a> appointments.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Archbishop_of_Arles">Archbishop of Arles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean_Marie_du_Lau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Archbishop of Arles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Starting in 1777, Lau embarked on a <a href="/wiki/Canonical_visitation" title="Canonical visitation">pastoral visitation</a> of the diocese and, in 1779, he had a report on the state of the diocese drawn up by the Abbé <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Bonnemant" class="extiw" title="fr:Laurent Bonnemant">Laurent Bonnemant</a> with a view to introducing reforms. Like that of many reforming bishops, the archbishop's interest extended to the preparation of midwives and <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> of children. He also undertook building works, such as the imposing facade of the archbishop's palace, which he had rebuilt in 1786.
</p><p>With the convocation of the <a href="/wiki/Estates-General_of_1789" class="mw-redirect" title="Estates-General of 1789">Estates-General of 1789</a>, Lau was chosen as one of the representatives of the clergy. As the revolutionary situation developed, the archbishop began a bitter conflict with the newly elected mayor of <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pierre-Antoine_Antonelle" title="Pierre-Antoine Antonelle">Pierre-Antoine Antonelle</a>, an aristocrat who had sided with radical wing of the Revolution.
</p><p>The conflict was short-lived for, on 12 July 1790, the <a href="/wiki/National_Assembly" title="National Assembly">National Assembly</a> voted for measures that included the abolition of the Archdiocese of Arles. Jean-Marie du Lau d'Allemans was in fact the last Archbishop of Arles.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean_Marie_du_Lau&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lau died a violent death on 2 September 1792 in an improvised prison inside the closed <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">Friars</a> in central Paris, where he was being held with two priests of his archdiocese, <a href="/wiki/Armand_de_Foucauld_de_Pontbriand" title="Armand de Foucauld de Pontbriand">Armand de Foucauld de Pontbriand</a> and Pierre François Pazery de Thorame, and a large number of other clergy and <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">religious</a>. Those killed included two other bishops, <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Beauvais#Bishops_of_Beauvais" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Beauvais">Francois-Joseph de la Rochefoucauld</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Diocese_of_Saintes#Bishops" title="Ancient Diocese of Saintes">Pierre-Louis de la Rochefoucauld</a>, priests, clerics and <a href="/wiki/Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools" class="mw-redirect" title="Brothers of the Christian Schools">Brothers of the Christian Schools</a>, for a total of 94 men, who were beatified as a group by Pope Pius XI.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lau's remains, along with those of his fellow victims, are entombed in the cemetery of the former Carmelite priory, 70 rue de Vaugirard, Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
