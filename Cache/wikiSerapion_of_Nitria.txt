<p><b>Serapion of Nitria</b>, (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Σεραπίων</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Serapíon</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Серапион</span>) <b>Serapion of Thmuis</b>, or <b>Serapion the Scholastic</b> was an early <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism" title="Christian monasticism">Christian monk</a> and bishop of <a href="/wiki/Thmuis" title="Thmuis">Thmuis</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lower_Egypt" title="Lower Egypt">Lower Egypt</a> (modern-day <a href="/wiki/Tell_el-Timai" class="mw-redirect" title="Tell el-Timai">Tell el-Timai</a>), born in the 4th century.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is notable for fighting alongside <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a> against <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Thmuis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Serapion is quoted in four sections of the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>, where he is called <a href="/wiki/Ab_(Semitic)" title="Ab (Semitic)">Abba</a> Serapion.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was given the title "The Angel of the Church of Thmuis" by <a href="/wiki/Evagrius_Ponticus" title="Evagrius Ponticus">Evagrius Ponticus</a> in <i>Gnostikos</i>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monasticism">Monasticism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Thmuis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monasticism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Before becoming a monk, Serapion was educated in <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He then became the abbot of the Monastery of Arsina (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Arseonita</i>), which at one point held as many as eleven-thousand monks.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was given the title "the Great" by the early Christian historians <a href="/wiki/Sozomen" title="Sozomen">Sozomen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia" title="Palladius of Galatia">Palladius</a>.
</p>

<p>As a monk, he was a companion and disciple of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a>, who at his deathbed bequeathed to him one of his two sheepskin cloaks (the other went to <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Thmuis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In his later life <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;339</span>, he was made the bishop of Thmuis (near Diospolis) where he served until his death <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;358</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a> in his work <i>On Illustrous Men</i> noted that Serapion was given the apellation "Scholasticus" (the Scholastic) because of his meticulous scholarship. During his episcopate, he helped Athanasius fight against <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a> in Alexandria, and at his request, Athanasius wrote to him a series of three <a href="/wiki/Dogmatic" class="mw-redirect" title="Dogmatic">dogmatic</a> letters on the theology of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Spirit_in_Christianity" title="Holy Spirit in Christianity">Holy Spirit</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> These letters, which were written <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;339–359</span>, are considered to be among the earliest Christian texts dedicated exclusively to the Holy Spirit.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Serapion was one of the most trusted companions of Athanasius and even took care of his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">episcopal see</a> during one of his exiles.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In AD 353, Athanasius placed Serapion at the head of a delegation to <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Emperor Constantius II</a> to plead guilty against the charges of the Arians.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 343, Serapion attended the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Serdica" title="Council of Serdica">Council of Serdica</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Serapion was exiled by the Arians in AD 350, and died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;360</span>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Story_from_the_Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers">Story from the <i>Sayings of the Desert Fathers</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Thmuis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Story from the Sayings of the Desert Fathers">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Serapion is said of have paid a prostitute, but instead of engaging in relations with her, prayed all night in front of her and eventually <a href="/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity" title="Conversion to Christianity">converted</a> her to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>. She later became a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> at a monastery, practicing extreme <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> labors.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The same story also exists in a poetic Hymn of Praise in <i><a href="//archive.org/details/ThePrologueFromOhrid_BishopNikolaiVelimirovich" class="extiw" title="iarchive:ThePrologueFromOhrid BishopNikolaiVelimirovich">The Prologue of Ohrid</a>.</i><sup id="cite_ref-:0_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="List_of_works">List of works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Thmuis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: List of works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<li><i><a href="/wiki/Sacramentary_of_Serapion_of_Thmuis" title="Sacramentary of Serapion of Thmuis">Sacramentary of Serapion of Thmuis</a></i> (a prayer book which includes the earliest written <a href="/wiki/Sanctus" title="Sanctus">Sanctus</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-:3_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>Treatise against the <a href="/wiki/Manichaeism" title="Manichaeism">Manichees</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>Treatises on the Titles of the Psalms</i> (quoted by <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, but which are now lost).<sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>A Letter to the Disciples of Anthony</i> (written after Anthony's death in 356. First published in the 1950s, exists only in Syriac and Armenian).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>A Letter of Consolation to a Bishop</i> (written to a certain Bishop Eudoxius).<sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>A Letter to the Solitaries of Alexandria</i> on the dignity of the religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><i>A Letter on the Father and the Son</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-:4_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:4-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Selected_quotes">Selected quotes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Serapion_of_Thmuis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Selected quotes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>"Do not think that sickness is difficult; only sin is difficult. Sickness follows the sinners only in life, but sin follows the sinner into the grave." (<i>Sayings of the Desert Fathers</i>).</li></ul>
<ul><li>"When the soldiers of the emperor are standing at attention, they cannot look to the right or left; it is the same for the man who stands before God and looks towards Him in fear at all time; he cannot then fear anything from the enemy." (<i>Sayings of the Desert Fathers</i>).</li>
<li>"As soon as this earth's great elder, the blessed Antony, who prayed for the whole world, departed, everything has been torn apart and is in anguish, and the wrath devastates Egypt. While he was truly on earth, he extended his hands and prayed and spoke with God all day long. He did not let the wrath descend on us. Lifting up his thoughts, he kept it from coming down. But now that those hands are closed, no one else can be found who might halt the violence." (<i>A Letter to the Disciples of Anthony</i>).</li></ul>
