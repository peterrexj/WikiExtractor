					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Fintan_of_Clonenagh" title="Fintan of Clonenagh">Saint Fintan of Clonenagh</a> or <a href="/wiki/Saint_Munde" title="Saint Munde">Saint Munde</a></div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Fintán</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">635</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data">Eastern Orthodox<br />Roman Catholic<br /> Church of Ireland</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">21 October</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Influences</th><td class="infobox-data">Abbot Sinell at Cluain-inis</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Fintán</b>, or <b>Munnu</b>  (died 635) is one of the saints of Ireland and Britain who served in Ireland and Scotland being the founder and abbot of the abbey at Teach-Mhunn - The House of Saint Munn - where his bed may be visited is a pilgrimage site; today <a href="/wiki/Taghmon" title="Taghmon">Taghmon</a> is in the <a href="/wiki/County_Wexford" title="County Wexford">County Wexford</a>, in the province of Leinster <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In Scotland, he is venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Clan_Campbell" title="Clan Campbell">Clan Campbell</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Alternate_names">Alternate names</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Alternate names">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was known in Ireland as <i>St Fintán</i> or <i>St Munnu</i>. In Scotland, he was recorded as <i>St Mun</i>, <i>St Munn</i>, <i>St Munda</i>, <i>St Mundas</i> and <i>St Mund</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The name Fionn was preceded by the term of endearment 'mo', followed by "og", meaning "young". Mo-Fhionn-Og became Munno, the name by which he is popularly known.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Based on certain linguistic characteristics and some historical references, the earliest life of Fintán/Munna is believed to date around the year 800.<sup id="cite_ref-Culleton_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Culleton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fintán was born of the dynasty of Niall, son of Tulchan (or Tulcan)<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and Feidelmia.<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to the <a href="/wiki/%C3%93engus_of_Tallaght" title="Óengus of Tallaght">Felire of Oengus</a>, Tulchan was a druid.<sup id="cite_ref-Culleton_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Culleton-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Fintán may have first studied at the school of <a href="/wiki/Bangor_Abbey" title="Bangor Abbey">Bangor Abbey</a> under <a href="/wiki/Comgall" title="Comgall">St. Comgall</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and later in the school of Kilmore Deathrib.  It is more certain that he studied 18 years under the abbot Sinell, son of Maynacur, at Cluain-inis in <a href="/wiki/Lough_Erne" title="Lough Erne">Lough Erne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">St. Columba</a> also studied at Cluain-inis under Sinell, but left prior to Munnu's arrival.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry_in_Scotland">Ministry in Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Ministry in Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Iona_monastery">Iona monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Iona monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Fintán  arrived in Scotland in 579 A.D.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  He was said to be assisted by two wolves in guarding his sheep.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 597, Fintán  resolved to go to Í (now called <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a>) to join the monastery there under <a href="/wiki/Columba" title="Columba">St. Columba</a>.  Just before his departure, news reached him that St. Columba had died and that <a href="/wiki/Baith%C3%A9ne_mac_Br%C3%A9naind" title="Baithéne mac Brénaind">St. Baithen</a> had succeeded him.  Undeterred, Fintán made the journey and applied to St. Baithen for reception into the community. Munnu responded "humbly" as St. Baithen made the customary inquiries into his background.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> However, when Munnu requested to be admitted as a monk, Baithen replied:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>" I thank my God, that you are come to this place; but this you must know: that you cannot be a monk of ours." Fintan much afflicted at these words said; "Is it that I am unworthy of being one?" "No" answered Baithen, "but, although I should be very glad to keep you with me, I must obey the orders of my predecessor Columba, who some time ago said to me in the spirit of prophecy; Baithen remember these words of mine; immediately after my departure from this life, a brother, who is now regulating his youthful age by good conduct, and well versed in sacred studies, named Fintan, of the race of MocuMoie, (74) and son of Failchan, will come to you from Ireland (Scotia) and will supplicate to be reckoned among the monks. But it is predetermined by God, that he is to be an abbot presiding over monks, and a guide of souls. Do not therefore let him remain in these islands of ours, but direct him to return in peace to Ireland (Scotia), - that he may there establish a monastery in a part of Leinster not far from the sea, and labour for the good of souls."<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Kilmun_monastery">Kilmun monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Kilmun monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Mun lived on the Island on Loch Leven, before making his way to <a href="/wiki/Kilmun" title="Kilmun">Kilmun</a>, where he founded a monastic community<sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-assoc1879_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-assoc1879-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> at the site of the modern <a href="/wiki/St_Munn%27s_Parish_Church,_Kilmun" class="mw-redirect" title="St Munn&#39;s Parish Church, Kilmun">Kilmun Church</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry_in_Ireland">Ministry in Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Ministry in Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Wexford_monastery">Wexford monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Wexford monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Fintán  returned to Ireland shortly thereafter to found his monastery.  A local lord in what is modern day <a href="/wiki/Wexford" title="Wexford">Wexford</a> in <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, named Dímma mac Áeda Croin, granted Fintán land circa 597. Munnu, in turn, granted, Dímma his personal guarantee that Dímma would gain the kingdom of heaven.  Fintán at some point took Dímma's son into fosterage, and Dímma later became a cleric and was buried among the monks at the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-Charles-Edwards_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles-Edwards-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The monastery was named Teach-munnu or "House of Munnu" and became a center of learning. Fintán was versed in scientific knowledge and gave frequent public lectures where Christian revelation was illuminated by the sciences and mathematics.<sup id="cite_ref-Brenan_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brenan-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Munnu presided over 152 monks at Teach-munnu.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
Munnu probably founded a religious establishment at "Kilmund" (<a href="/wiki/Kilmun" title="Kilmun">Kilmun</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> and may have done so at <a href="/wiki/Eilean_Munde" title="Eilean Munde">Eilean Munde</a> as well.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, it is improbable that he is founder of Clonenagh, <a href="/wiki/Fintan_of_Clonenagh" title="Fintan of Clonenagh">Fintan of Clonenagh</a>, though they are often confused.<sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He did have an abbey in Heli (or Hele, perhaps in <a href="/wiki/County_Westmeath" title="County Westmeath">County Westmeath</a>), which he and his monks forsook, giving it to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cera" title="Saint Cera">Saint Cera</a> and her companions.  <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cera" title="Saint Cera">Saint Cera</a> named the place, according to Fintán's instructions, after St. Telle who had given three jubilations in the plain of Miodhluachra that day.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Lanigan_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lanigan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 129–131">&#58;&#8202;129–131&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> There are several references to Munnu being a leper.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Toward the end of his life he opposed the adoption of the Roman method for determining the date of Easter.  He strongly promoted the Irish method in opposition to <a href="/wiki/Molaise_of_Leighlin" title="Molaise of Leighlin">Laserian</a>.  A synod convened at Magh Lene in 631 to resolve the matter, but the parties were unable to resolve the matter so a delegation was sent to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>  Munnu withdrew his opposition, and adopted the Roman system with the rest of southern Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He died in 636,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and was buried in the cemetery at <a href="/wiki/Kilmun_Parish_Church_and_Argyll_Mausoleum" title="Kilmun Parish Church and Argyll Mausoleum">St Munn's Parish Church, Kilmun</a>, Scotland.<sup id="cite_ref-Catholic_Online_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Catholic_Online-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_as_a_Saint">Veneration as a Saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Fint%C3%A1n_of_Taghmon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Veneration as a Saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His feast day is on 21 October.<sup id="cite_ref-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-History_of_Saint_Mun_-_OLIC-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Fintán is associated with several tales of miracles and is recorded to have been visited by an angel.<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_5-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
