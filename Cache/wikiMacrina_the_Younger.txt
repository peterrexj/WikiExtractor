<p><b>Macrina the Younger</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Μακρίνα η Νέα; c.  327 – 19 July 379) was an <a href="/wiki/History_of_Christianity#Early_Christianity" title="History of Christianity">early Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgin" title="Consecrated virgin">consecrated virgin</a>. She is regarded as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> churches. Macrina was elder sister of <a href="/wiki/Basil_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Basil the Great">Basil the Great</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa" title="Gregory of Nyssa">Gregory of Nyssa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Naucratius" title="Naucratius">Naucratius</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Sebaste" title="Peter of Sebaste">Peter of Sebaste</a>. Gregory of Nyssa wrote a work entitled <i>Life of Macrina</i> in which he describes her sanctity and <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> throughout her life. Macrina lived a chaste and humble life, devoting her time to prayer and the spiritual education of her younger brother Peter.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Macrina_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Macrina was born at <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Mazaca" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea Mazaca">Caesarea</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>. Her parents were <a href="/wiki/Basil_the_Elder" title="Basil the Elder">Basil the Elder</a> and <a href="/wiki/Emmelia_of_Caesarea" title="Emmelia of Caesarea">Emmelia</a>, and her grandmother was <a href="/wiki/Macrina_the_Elder" title="Macrina the Elder">Macrina the Elder</a>. Among her nine siblings were two of the three <a href="/wiki/Cappadocian_Fathers" title="Cappadocian Fathers">Cappadocian Fathers</a>, her younger brothers Basil the Great and Gregory of Nyssa, as well as Peter of Sebaste and the famous Christian jurist <a href="/wiki/Naucratius" title="Naucratius">Naucratius</a>. Her father arranged for her to marry, but her fiancé died before the wedding. After having been betrothed, Macrina did not believe it was appropriate to marry another man, but saw <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Christ</a> as her eternal bridegroom.<sup id="cite_ref-Peter_Brown_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Peter_Brown-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Macrina had a profound influence on her brothers and her mother with her adherence to an ascetic ideal. In Gregory of Nyssa's <i>Life of Macrina</i> he remembers her as a child who was devoted to study of the scriptures, especially the <a href="/wiki/Wisdom_of_Solomon" class="mw-redirect" title="Wisdom of Solomon">Wisdom of Solomon</a>, and those parts of it which have an ethical bearing, "such parts as you would think were incomprehensible to young children were the subject of the girl's studies".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Macrina, who resolved never to leave her mother,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> moved with her to one of their rural estates and lived within a community of virgins who came from both an aristocratic and a non-aristocratic background.<sup id="cite_ref-Cvetković_2021_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cvetković_2021-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> All members were free, and slaves got the same rights and obligations as their masters. The death of the brother Naucratius shocked her mother and gave to Macrina a priority role in the domestic life.<sup id="cite_ref-Cvetković_2021_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cvetković_2021-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 379, Macrina died at her family's estate in <a href="/wiki/Pontus_(region)" title="Pontus (region)">Pontus</a>, which with the help of her younger brother Peter she had turned into a convent of <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_virgins" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecrated virgins">virgins</a>. Gregory of Nyssa composed a <i>Dialogue on the Soul and Resurrection</i> <i>(peri psyches kai anastaseos)</i>, entitled <i>ta Makrinia</i> (P.G. XLVI, 12 sq.), to commemorate Macrina, in which Gregory purports to describe the conversation he had with the dying Macrina, in a literary form modelled on Plato's <i><a href="/wiki/Phaedo" title="Phaedo">Phaedo</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Even when dying, Macrina continued to live a life of sanctity, as she refused a bed, and instead chose to lie on the ground. Her feast day is 19 July.
</p><p>Macrina is significant in that she set the standard for being a holy Early Christian woman. She contributed to her brother's writings and his belief that virginity reflected the "radiant purity of God".<sup id="cite_ref-Peter_Brown_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Peter_Brown-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Macrina_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Christian_universalism" title="Christian universalism">Universalists</a>, including <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Allin_(Anglican)" title="Thomas Allin (Anglican)">Thomas Allin</a> and <a href="/wiki/J._W._Hanson" class="mw-redirect" title="J. W. Hanson">J. W. Hanson</a>, claim Macrina as a committed <a href="/wiki/Universalist" class="mw-redirect" title="Universalist">universalist</a>, citing passages from the <i>Dialogue on the Soul and Resurrection</i> which they believe demonstrate her conviction that all sinners and demons will at last be purified and confess Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Macrina is honored in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/July_19" title="July 19">19 July</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
