<p><b>William of York</b> (late 11th century – 8 June 1154)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> was an English priest and twice <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a>, before and after a rival, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Murdac" title="Henry Murdac">Henry Murdac</a>. He was thought to be related to King <a href="/wiki/Stephen_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen of England">Stephen of England</a>, who helped to secure his election to the <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">province</a> after several candidates had failed to gain papal confirmation. William faced opposition from the <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a>, who after the election of the Cistercian Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III" title="Pope Eugene III">Eugene III</a>, had William deposed in favour of a Cistercian, Murdac. From 1147 until 1153, William worked to be restored to York, which he achieved after the deaths of Murdac and Eugene III. He did not hold the province long, dying shortly after his return, allegedly from poison in the chalice he used to celebrate Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-Wells_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wells-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Miracles were reported at his tomb from 1177. He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> in 1226.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born William fitzHerbert in York,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> William was the son of <a href="/wiki/Herbert_of_Winchester" title="Herbert of Winchester">Herbert of Winchester</a>, or Herbert fitzAlberic,<sup id="cite_ref-DD151_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DD151-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> chancellor and treasurer of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_England" title="Henry I of England">Henry I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Treasury268_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Treasury268-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Most sources say his mother was Emma, half-sister of King Stephen and <a href="/wiki/Henry_of_Blois" title="Henry of Blois">Henry of Blois</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Winchester" title="Bishop of Winchester">Bishop of Winchester</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-BHOYork_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOYork-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and that she was an illegitimate daughter of <a href="/wiki/Stephen_II,_Count_of_Blois" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephen II, Count of Blois">Stephen II, Count of Blois</a>, Stephen's father.<sup id="cite_ref-Davis172_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davis172-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> New research, however, suggests that Emma may have been a daughter of Hunger fitzOdin, who held lands in <a href="/wiki/Dorset" title="Dorset">Dorset</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Domesday_Book" title="Domesday Book">Domesday survey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> William was born sometime before the 1090s, but the date is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>William held the <a href="/wiki/Prebendary" title="Prebendary">prebendary</a> of Weighton in the diocese of Yorkshire between 27 June 1109 and 24 February 1114.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOWeigh_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOWeigh-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Sometime between 1109 and 1114 he was appointed <a href="/w/index.php?title=Treasurer_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Treasurer of York (page does not exist)">Treasurer of York</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOYork_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOYork-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was also appointed <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon_of_the_East_Riding_of_Yorkshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdeacon of the East Riding of Yorkshire">archdeacon of the East Riding of Yorkshire</a> at an unknown date between 1125 and 1133.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOERid_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOERid-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The influence of his rich and powerful father, who had many landholdings in Yorkshire, may have been of benefit in gaining him these offices at a relatively early age.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> William apparently held both of these offices until his election as Archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOERid_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOERid-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Serving under Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Thurstan" title="Thurstan">Thurstan</a> of York, William became involved in Thurstan's dispute with King Henry I after Henry demanded that the Archbishops of York accept subordination to the Archbishops of Canterbury. William accompanied Thurstan into exile in Europe and on embassies to the papal court.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY34_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY34-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Reconciliation with Henry allowed a return to York in 1121. A papal ruling in favour of the independence of the Archbishops of York was finally delivered in 1127.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY61_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY61-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Election_problems">Election problems</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Election problems">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In January 1141 William was elected Archbishop of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook281_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook281-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Originally, the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral chapter</a> of York had elected <a href="/wiki/Waltheof_of_Melrose" title="Waltheof of Melrose">Waltheof</a> in 1140, but that election was set aside, as one of Waltheof's supporters had made an <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">uncanonical</a> gift (bribe) to secure Waltheof's election. Then Henry of Blois tried to secure the see for <a href="/wiki/Henry_de_Sully,_Abbot_of_F%C3%A9camp" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry de Sully, Abbot of Fécamp">Henry de Sully</a>, another nephew of Stephen and Henry's. Sully's election was opposed by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_II" title="Pope Innocent II">Innocent II</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Crouch304_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crouch304-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> who refused to confirm him as Archbishop while he remained Abbot of Fécamp.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY81_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY81-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Only at a third election in January 1141 was William chosen. Whether he had been a candidate in the previous two elections is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-Crouch304_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Crouch304-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The election was opposed by the Cistercian monasteries of Yorkshire and by the archdeacons of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Church98_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Church98-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The Cistercians opposed on the grounds that the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Second Council of the Lateran">Second Lateran Council</a> in 1139 had given the religious houses of a diocese the right to participate in electing the bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Burton77_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Burton77-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Theobald_of_Bec" title="Theobald of Bec">Theobald of Bec</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, refused to recognise William's election due to allegations of <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simony</a> (acquisition of church positions by bribery), and of interference by King Stephen.<sup id="cite_ref-Poole191_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Poole191-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> In 1143, Pope Innocent II ruled that William could be confirmed in office if he swore under oath that the allegations were false. After he swore the oath, Henry of Blois, who was also <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a>, found William innocent, and he was consecrated as archbishop on 26 September 1143.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOYork_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOYork-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="First_archiepiscopate_and_deposition">First archiepiscopate and deposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: First archiepiscopate and deposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg/220px-Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="271" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg/330px-Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg/440px-Saved_from_the_Ouse.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2541" data-file-height="3131" /></a><figcaption>Carved plaque. William of York crosses the River Ouse; the Ouse bridge collapses but no one is killed.</figcaption></figure>
<p>As Archbishop, William undertook several ecclesiastical reforms and became popular with the people of York. However, he still needed a <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a>, the sign of an archbishop's authority from the Pope, which he had not yet received. The Cistercians, who were still adamantly opposed to his being Archbishop, were determined to prevent his receiving it. William travelled to Rome in an attempt to obtain the pallium. The election of Pope Eugene III, a Cistercian, in 1145, was a setback for his cause.<sup id="cite_ref-Davis97_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davis97-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a>, the famous Cistercian abbot and religious leader, exerted all his influence to ensure William's suspension, sending a series of complaints to the new Pope that William had been intruded by secular powers into the see, that he was oppressing the Cistercian monasteries and that he had irregularly appointed <a href="/wiki/William_of_St._Barbara" title="William of St. Barbara">William of St. Barbara</a> as <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_York" title="Dean of York">Dean of York</a>. In the winter of 1145–1146 Eugene re-examined the case, declared that William had not been validly consecrated and suspended him from office.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY118_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY118-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> William was required to obtain an in-person refutation of the old charges by William of St Barbara, by then the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Durham" title="Bishop of Durham">Bishop of Durham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOYork_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOYork-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Church98_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Church98-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While awaiting the final decision in his case, William took up residence with one of his friends, <a href="/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily" title="Roger II of Sicily">Roger II</a>, <a href="/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Sicily" class="mw-redirect" title="List of monarchs of Sicily">King of Sicily</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY118_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY118-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Hearing of his suspension, some of William's supporters in York launched a damaging attack on <a href="/wiki/Fountains_Abbey" title="Fountains Abbey">Fountains Abbey</a>, which destroyed many of the buildings.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY120_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY120-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> William was formally deposed by Eugene in early 1147 and the deposition was confirmed at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Reims" title="Council of Reims">Council of Reims</a> on 21 March 1148. Another election to York was held, and the candidates included <a href="/wiki/Hilary_of_Chichester" title="Hilary of Chichester">Hilary of Chichester</a>, who was the king's candidate, and Henry Murdac, the Cistercian <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of Fountains Abbey. Murdac's supporters included the Cistercians and most of the clergy of the diocese, including William's former ally, William of St Barbara. Both sides appealed to the Pope, and the Pope confirmed Murdac as the successful candidate.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOYork_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOYork-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Church98_16-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Church98-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> William then returned to Winchester, the city he had left 40 years earlier to begin his career in York.<sup id="cite_ref-StWY124_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StWY124-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_archiepiscopate">Second archiepiscopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Second archiepiscopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>King Stephen refused to accept William's deposition and the appointment of Murdac, and prevented Murdac from taking up residence in York. Stephen probably wished to trade recognition of Murdac for support for his son <a href="/wiki/Eustace_IV_of_Boulogne" class="mw-redirect" title="Eustace IV of Boulogne">Eustace</a>, whose coronation as his successor he was trying to secure during his own lifetime, to defeat rival claims to the throne by <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry of Anjou</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Davis103_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Davis103-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> Within a few years both Murdac and the Pope had died and William travelled to Rome to plead with the new <a href="/wiki/Pope_Anastasius_IV" title="Pope Anastasius IV">Anastasius IV</a> for restoration to office. The Pope concurred and William's reappointment was confirmed on 20 December 1153.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOYork_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOYork-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Church102_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Church102-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> On his return to York, while crossing the <a href="/wiki/Ouse_Bridge,_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Ouse Bridge, York">Ouse Bridge in York</a> in triumphal procession, the bridge collapsed, but no one was killed.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh627_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh627-26">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_sainthood">Death and sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg/220px-Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg/330px-Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg/440px-Shrine_of_St._William_of_York_in_York_Minster.jpg 2x" data-file-width="534" data-file-height="800" /></a><figcaption>The sarcophagus of William between an altar and a mural of his image in the crypt of York Minster.</figcaption></figure>
<p>After less than a month back in York, William died on 8 June 1154,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook281_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook281-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> allegedly due to poison administered in the chalice at Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-Church102_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Church102-25">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> One of William's clerks accused <a href="/wiki/Osbert_de_Bayeux" title="Osbert de Bayeux">Osbert de Bayeux</a>, an archdeacon of York, of the murder, and Osbert was summoned before the king to be tried at the royal court. Stephen died before the trial could take place.<sup id="cite_ref-Govern288_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Govern288-27">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> William was buried in <a href="/wiki/York_Minster" title="York Minster">York Minster</a><sup id="cite_ref-Norton145_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norton145-28">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> and within a few months of his death, miracles were attributed to his intervention and a sweet smell came from his tomb when it was damaged during a fire. Nor was the body decayed or burnt.<sup id="cite_ref-Norton149_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norton149-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Pope <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Honorius III</a> then ordered an investigation into the miracles and canonized him in Rome in 1226.<sup id="cite_ref-Norton149_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norton149-29">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>William's <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is marked on 8 June, the day of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-Saint_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Saint-30">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Although his veneration was largely localised in York, among his devotees was <a href="/wiki/Margery_Kempe" title="Margery Kempe">Margery Kempe</a> (1373–1438) of <a href="/wiki/Bishop%27s_Lynn" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop&#39;s Lynn">King's Lynn</a> in Norfolk, who "cried copiously" before his tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-Wells_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wells-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Traditional <a href="/wiki/Iconography" title="Iconography">iconography</a> and <a href="/wiki/Stained_glass" title="Stained glass">windows</a> often depict William's crossing of the Tweed; some iconography shows him crossing in a boat. William's <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> is blazoned: <i><a href="/wiki/Tincture_(heraldry)" title="Tincture (heraldry)">Or</a>, seven <a href="/wiki/Mascle" class="mw-redirect" title="Mascle">mascles</a> <a href="/wiki/Tincture_(heraldry)" title="Tincture (heraldry)">Gules</a>, 3, 3 and 1</i>. This actual shield at one time hung on the west wall of St Wilfrid's Church, <a href="/wiki/Bognor_Regis" title="Bognor Regis">Bognor Regis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>An extant church in Sheffield (in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Hallam" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Hallam">Roman Catholic Diocese of Hallam</a>) was dedicated to St William in 1932.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="St_William.27s_College"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St_William's_College">St William's College</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: St William&#039;s College">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg/220px-StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg/330px-StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg/440px-StWilliamsCollegeYork2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3424" data-file-height="2340" /></a><figcaption>St William's College near the Minster</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/St_William%27s_College" title="St William&#39;s College">St William's College</a>, which was named for him is next to York Minster. It was established between 1465 and 1467 with the permission of King <a href="/wiki/Edward_IV_of_England" title="Edward IV of England">Edward IV</a> as the home for <a href="/wiki/Chantry" title="Chantry">chantry</a> priests of the Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-College_33-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-College-33">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="St_William.27s_Chapel"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St_William's_Chapel">St William's Chapel</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: St William&#039;s Chapel">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A chapel to St William was built in York in the later 12th century. It was placed on the northern edge of the <a href="/wiki/Bridges_of_York#OuseBridge" title="Bridges of York">Ouse Bridge</a> to mark a miracle associated with the collapse of the bridge when William returned to York in 1154. It remained as a functional chapel until 1550. The chapel was removed during the renovation of the bridge between 1810 and 1818.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Shrines">Shrines</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_York&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Shrines">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The earliest shine to St William in York Minster dates to the c. 1220s in which William's sarcophagus was buried 10 inches (25&#160;cm) into the floor and a <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> structure erected above it - this left the lid of the sarcophagus proud of the structure to be accessible to pilgrims.<sup id="cite_ref-SH_35-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> In 1284 Bishop Bek paid for the translation of Williams body to a new shrine in the choir behind the high altar; no trace of the shrine has been recorded.<sup id="cite_ref-SH_35-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> There is evidence of two larger shrines in York Minster – one at his tomb site and the other a separate, principal, shrine.
</p><p>The tomb shrine was built in c. AD 1330 on the site of William's original burial place in the eastern-most bay of the <a href="/wiki/Nave" title="Nave">nave</a>; it comprised a large stone or marble slab five or six feet in height supported by a series of decorative arches (four on the longer sides, two on the shorter) with an upper stage of decoration rising above it. The whole structure stood at least 3.5 metres (11&#160;ft) in height.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> It was decorated with small statues in niches throughout and each of the four corner <a href="/wiki/Spandrels" class="mw-redirect" title="Spandrels">spandrels</a> depicts imagery associated with the <a href="/wiki/Four_Evangelists" title="Four Evangelists">Four Evangelists</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SH_35-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The latest, principal shrine, located near the Minster's <a href="/wiki/Altar_(Catholic_Church)" title="Altar (Catholic Church)">High Altar</a> was built in 1471-1472 and probably designed by Robert Spillesby, the master mason of the Minster from 1466-1473. This later shrine was in the <a href="/wiki/Perpendicular_Gothic" title="Perpendicular Gothic">Perpendicular Gothic</a> style made from a darker marble than the rest of the Minster and comprised a large marble pedestal on which a <a href="/wiki/Reliquary" title="Reliquary">reliquary</a> of William was placed and covered by a wooden canopy.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> 
It was 3.3 metres (11&#160;ft), 1.4 metres (4.6&#160;ft) wide and 4.15 metres (13.6&#160;ft) long.<sup id="cite_ref-SH_35-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>There is no evidence of large scale pilgrimage to William's shrines.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> However, numerous panels from the stained glass window in the Minster depict petitioners and worshippers at the shrines. A lead <a href="/wiki/Ampulla" title="Ampulla">ampulla</a> found in York and depicting an archbishop on one side has been argued to represent Saint William.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> The upper face of William's sarcophagus has been worn smooth, probably from the hands of pilgrims touching it.<sup id="cite_ref-SH_35-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SH-35">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> In the 1890s Catholic pilgrims who were part of the <a href="/wiki/Guild_of_Our_Lady_of_Ransom" title="Guild of Our Lady of Ransom">Guild of Our Lady of Ransom</a> pilgrimage through York were given permission to kiss the shrine.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 22 September 1541 the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">Privy Council</a> met in York and ordered the Archbishop to demolish all of the shrines in his province.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> William's shrines were taken apart and buried at <a href="/wiki/Precentor%27s_Court" title="Precentor&#39;s Court">Precentor's Court</a>. In 1715 <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Thoresby" title="Ralph Thoresby">Ralph Thoresby</a> reported that he had acquired some architectural fragments in marble which had been 'enclosed betwixt two walls and in a private House' from Precentor's Court.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> These were probably the first rediscoveries of fragments of St. William's shrines.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> Several fragments from 18th century interventions at or near this house were later discovered spread around York  including in the gardens near Clifford's Tower and used as domestic furniture. Further discoveries of shrine fragments from Precentor's Court were made in 1882-1883 and 1927-1928. Fragments of both later and earlier shrines were discovered during each excavation.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sarcophagus containing William's remains was rediscovered in 1732 and again in 1968, at which point they were removed.<sup id="cite_ref-shrines_36-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-shrines-36">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> His remains are now held in a shrine in the crypt at York Minster.<sup id="cite_ref-Minster_40-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Minster-40">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Parts of the shrines are now in the <a href="/wiki/Yorkshire_Museum" title="Yorkshire Museum">Yorkshire Museum</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> This is the only example of a saint's shrine in a museum collection in England.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
