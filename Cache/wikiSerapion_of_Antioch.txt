<p><b>Serapion</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch" title="Patriarch of Antioch">Patriarch of Antioch</a> (191–211). He is known primarily through his theological writings, although all but a few fragments of his works have perished.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is celebrated on October 30.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Serapion was considered one of the chief theologians of his era.
<a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a> refers to three works of Serapion in his history, but admits that others probably existed: first is a private letter addressed to Caricus and Pontius against <a href="/wiki/Montanism" title="Montanism">Montanism</a>, from which Eusebius quotes an extract (<i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Historia ecclesiastica</a></i> V, 19), as well as ascriptions showing that it was circulated amongst bishops in Asia and Thrace; next is a work addressed to a certain Domninus, who in time of persecution abandoned Christianity for the error of "Jewish will-worship" (<i>Hist. Eccles</i>, VI, 12).<sup id="cite_ref-healy_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lastly, Eusebius quotes (vi.12.2) from a pamphlet Serapion wrote concerning the <a href="/wiki/Docetism" title="Docetism">Docetic</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Peter" title="Gospel of Peter">Gospel of Peter</a></i>, in which Serapion presents an argument to the Christian community of <a href="/wiki/Arsuz" title="Arsuz">Rhossus</a> in Syria against this gospel and condemns it.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eusebius also alludes to a number of personal letters Serapion wrote to Pontius, Caricus, and others about this <i>Gospel of Peter</i>.
</p><p>Serapion also acted (<a href="/wiki/Pantaenus" title="Pantaenus">Pantaenus</a> supported him) against the influence of <a href="/wiki/Gnosticism" title="Gnosticism">Gnosticism</a> in <a href="/wiki/Osroene" title="Osroene">Osroene</a> by consecrating <a href="/wiki/Palut" class="mw-redirect" title="Palut">Palut</a> as bishop of <a href="/wiki/Edessa,_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Edessa, Mesopotamia">Edessa</a>, where Palut addressed the increasingly Gnostic tendencies that the churchman <a href="/wiki/Bardesanes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bardesanes">Bardesanes</a> was introducing to its Christian community. He ordained <a href="/wiki/Pantaenus" title="Pantaenus">Pantaenus</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">Priest</a> or <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> in Edessa.
</p><p>Serapion was succeeded as bishop of Antioch by <a href="/wiki/Asclepiades_of_Antioch" title="Asclepiades of Antioch">Asclepiades</a> (Eusebius <i>Historia ecclesiastica</i> VI, 11, 4).
</p>
