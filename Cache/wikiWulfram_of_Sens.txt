<p><b>Wulfram of Sens</b> or <b>Wulfram of Fontenelle</b> (also <b>Vuilfran</b>, <b>Wulfrann</b>, <b>Wolfran</b>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Wulframnus</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Vulfran</i> or <i>Vulphran</i>; c. 640 – 20 March 703<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Sens" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Sens">Archbishop of Sens</a>. His life was recorded eleven years after he died by the monk <a href="/w/index.php?title=Jonas_of_Fontenelle&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Jonas of Fontenelle (page does not exist)">Jonas of Fontenelle</a>. However, there seems to be little consensus about the precise dates of most events whether during his life or <i>post mortem</i>.
</p><p>Wulfram is depicted in art as <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptising</a> a young king or the son of <a href="/wiki/King_Radbod" class="mw-redirect" title="King Radbod">King Radbod</a>. Sometimes the young king is near him and sometimes Wulfram is shown arriving by ship with monks to baptise the king. There are two churches dedicated to him in England, at <a href="/wiki/Grantham,_Lincolnshire" class="mw-redirect" title="Grantham, Lincolnshire">Grantham, Lincolnshire</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Ovingdean" title="Ovingdean">Ovingdean</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sussex" title="Sussex">Sussex</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Dale205_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dale205-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and two in France, one at <a href="/wiki/Abbeville" title="Abbeville">Abbeville</a>, in the <i><a href="/wiki/D%C3%A9partement_in_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Département in France">département</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Somme_(department)" title="Somme (department)">Somme</a>, the other in Butot, in the département of the Seine Maritime. As a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>, he protects against the dangers of the sea.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfram_of_Sens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg/220px-Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="343" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg/330px-Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg/440px-Abbeville_Saint-Vulfran.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1471" data-file-height="2296" /></a><figcaption>Saint Wulfran,<br />Saint-Wulfran church in <a href="/wiki/Abbeville" title="Abbeville">Abbeville</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Wulfram was born in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Meaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Meaux">diocese of Meaux</a>, at <i>Mauraliacus</i>, an insecurely identified place near <a href="/wiki/Fontainebleau" title="Fontainebleau">Fontainebleau</a>, probably <a href="/wiki/Milly-la-For%C3%AAt" title="Milly-la-Forêt">Milly-la-Forêt</a>, <a href="/wiki/Essonne" title="Essonne">Essonne</a>. He was the son of a certain Fulbert, a <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a> attached to the court of <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>. King Dagobert's kingdom was divided on his death, and it was close to the court of his partial successor, <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burgundy" title="Kingdom of Burgundy">Burgundy</a> that Wulfram is likely to have been born a little after Dagobert had died in 639.
</p><p>Wulfram was educated at Clovis' court and showed a gift for academic learning. He took <a href="/wiki/Holy_orders" title="Holy orders">holy orders</a> and seems to have intended a quiet life but was called to the court of <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodoric III">Theodoric III</a> (Thierry III) of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>. This seems to have propelled him into greater prominence since, in 692, he was elected <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">Archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a>. There are various versions of the date for this, the earliest being in 682. However, by 693 he was in the post as he attended an assembly of bishops at <a href="/wiki/Valenciennes" title="Valenciennes">Valenciennes</a>.
</p><p>In 695, he resigned the archbishopric in favor of <a href="/wiki/Amatus_of_Nusco" title="Amatus of Nusco">Amatus of Nusco</a>, who, it seems, he thought would be better at that sort of work, and retired to the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> called "Fontenelle". There are several places called <a href="/wiki/Fontenelle_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Fontenelle (disambiguation)">Fontenelle</a>, but this was probably at <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Wandrille" title="Abbey of Saint Wandrille">St-Wandrille</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Caudebec-en-Caux" title="Caudebec-en-Caux">Caudebec-en-Caux</a> on the lower <a href="/wiki/River_Seine" class="mw-redirect" title="River Seine">Seine</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Rouen">Diocese of Rouen</a>. However, Johannes Madey<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> places it at Fontenelle in the extreme north of the département of <a href="/wiki/Aisne" title="Aisne">Aisne</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_mission_in_Friesland">The mission in Friesland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfram_of_Sens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The mission in Friesland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg/200px-Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="244" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg/300px-Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg/400px-Eug%C3%A8ne_Boudin_-_La_Place_de_l%27%C3%A9glise_Saint-Vulfran_%C3%A0_Abbeville.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2000" data-file-height="2443" /></a><figcaption><div class="center" style="width:auto; margin-left:auto; margin-right:auto;"><i>The Square of the Church of Saint Vulfran in Abbeville</i>, Eugène Boudin, 1884</div></figcaption></figure>
<p>It is said<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Unsupported_attributions" title="Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Words to watch"><span title="The material near this tag may use weasel words or too-vague attribution. (October 2019)">by whom?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>} that when <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a> withdrew from his missionary work in <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Friesland</a>, in order to visit <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for the second time, Wulfram stood in for him in Friesland.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> However, Boniface was a younger contemporary, his first and abortive mission in Frisia began in 716 or so, probably after Wulfram had died.
</p><p>Whatever the order of these events, in <a href="/wiki/Frisia" title="Frisia">Frisia</a>, Wulfram converted the son of King Radbod and was allowed to preach. The custom was that people, including children, were sacrificed to the local gods having been selected by a form of lottery. Wulfram, having remonstrated with Radbod on the subject, was told that the king was unable to change the custom but Wulfram was invited to save them if he could. Wulfram then waded into the sea to save two children who had been tied to posts and left to drown as the tide rose. 
</p><p>According to the story, the turning point came with the rescue of a man, Ovon, who had been chosen by lot to be sacrificed by hanging. Wulfram begged King Radbod to stop the killing, but the people were outraged at the <a href="/wiki/Sacrilege" title="Sacrilege">sacrilege</a> proposed. In the end, they agreed that Wulfram's God could have a chance to save Ovon's life, and if he did, Wulfram and the God could have him. Ovon was hanged, and left for a couple of hours, while Wulfram prayed. When the Frisians decided to leave Ovon for dead, the rope broke, Ovon fell and was still alive. Ovon became Wulfram's slave, his follower, a monk, and then a priest at <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Wandrille" title="Abbey of Saint Wandrille">Fontenelle Abbey</a>. The faith of the missionaries (and their power to work miracles) frightened and awed the people, who were baptized and turned away from paganism.
</p><p>Even Radbod seemed ready for conversion, but just before his baptism, he asked where his ancestors were. Wulfram told him that <a href="/wiki/Idolatry" title="Idolatry">idolaters</a> went to Hell. Rather than be apart from his ancestors, he chose to stay as he was.
</p>
<h2><span id="Wulfram.27s_death_and_after"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Wulfram's_death_and_after">Wulfram's death and after</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfram_of_Sens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Wulfram&#039;s death and after">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He retired to Fontenelle, where he died in 703. His year of death is sometimes given as 720, but his interred body is said to have been moved in 704. Regardless of the exact year, Saint Wulfram's <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is kept on 20 March. He was buried in St. Paul’s chapel in the abbey, but in 704, he was re-buried in the main church. The body was again moved in 1058, this time to the <a href="/wiki/College" title="College">collegiate church</a> of Our Lady in Abbeville, which was then re-dedicated in Wulfram's name. The translation of his body to Abbeville is commemorated on 15 October.
</p><p>At about this time or later, perhaps when his body was again moved, this time to Rouen, his arm was taken as a relic to <a href="/wiki/Croyland_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Croyland Abbey">Croyland Abbey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>. The interest in him there may have arisen from <a href="/wiki/Ingulph" class="mw-redirect" title="Ingulph">Ingulph</a>, the abbot being a former monk of Fontenelle. Also, everyone concerned was a Benedictine. Ingulph, who died in 1109, was secretary to <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of England">William I</a>, who made him <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Crowland" title="Abbot of Crowland">Abbot of Crowland</a> in 1086.
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographical</a> account of his miracles was produced at the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Wandrille" title="Abbey of Saint Wandrille">Abbey of Saint Wandrille</a> before 1066. Among the miracles are two pertaining to childbirth and children. In one, Wulfram is credited with the miraculous delivery of a stillborn baby, the mother having commenced labor on 20 January (the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian" title="Saint Sebastian">Saint Sebastian</a>). A week after Easter, prayers to Wulfram caused her belly to split open so the dead child could be delivered, after which the wound healed as if it had never been, leaving only a "token of the cut". In the other, Wulfram is credited with the safe passage of an accidentally swallowed clothespin, which left the body of a two-year-old boy after three days without having injured it: "Is it not miraculous how through all the twists of the boy's intestines, as if through fine small round tubes, the copper sharp object, now going up high, now going down low, could travel without getting stuck anywhere or causing wounds, and so at last through Nature's lower parts find a way out all in one piece?"<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the building at Crowland was damaged by fire, there was no longer a suitable place for keeping the relic, so it went to Grantham for safe-keeping. For two or three hundred years, it was kept in the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> chapel below the <a href="/wiki/Lady_Chapel" class="mw-redirect" title="Lady Chapel">Lady Chapel</a>, where the pilgrims helped to wear the hollow, now to be seen in stone step before the altar. Later, towards 1350, the arm went to the specially added chapel above the north porch. At some stage in the long process of the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>, this relic was lost.
</p>
