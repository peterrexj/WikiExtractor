<p><b>Monica</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;332</span> – 387) was an early North African <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and the mother of <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>. She is remembered and honored in the Catholic and Orthodox Churches, albeit on different feast days, for her outstanding <a href="/wiki/Christian_virtues" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian virtues">Christian virtues</a>, particularly the suffering caused by her husband's <a href="/wiki/Adultery" title="Adultery">adultery</a>, and her prayerful life dedicated to the reformation of her son, who wrote extensively of her pious acts and life with her in his <i><a href="/wiki/Confessions_(St._Augustine)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confessions (St. Augustine)">Confessions</a></i>. Popular Christian legends recall Monica <a href="/wiki/Lacrimation" class="mw-redirect" title="Lacrimation">weeping</a> every night for her son Augustine.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Monica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Monica is assumed to have been born in <a href="/wiki/Thagaste" title="Thagaste">Thagaste</a> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Souk_Ahras" title="Souk Ahras">Souk Ahras</a>, <a href="/wiki/Algeria" title="Algeria">Algeria</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrettFentress199771_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrettFentress199771-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She is believed to have been a <a href="/wiki/Berbers" title="Berbers">Berber</a> on the basis of her name.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPower1999353–354_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPower1999353–354-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was married early in life to Patricius, a <a href="/wiki/Decurion_(administrative)" class="mw-redirect" title="Decurion (administrative)">decurion</a> pagan, in Thagaste.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Patricius reportedly had a violent temper and appears to have been of dissolute habits; apparently his mother exhibited similar behaviours. Monica's <a href="/wiki/Alms" title="Alms">almsgiving</a>, deeds and prayer habits annoyed Patricius, but it is said that he always held her in respect.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Monica had three children who survived infancy: two sons, Augustine and Navigius, and a daughter, <a href="/wiki/%27Perpetua%27_of_Hippo" class="mw-redirect" title="&#39;Perpetua&#39; of Hippo">'Perpetua' of Hippo</a>. Unable to secure baptism for them, she grieved heavily when Augustine fell ill. In her distress she asked Patricius to allow Augustine to be baptized; he agreed, then withdrew this consent when the boy recovered.
</p><p>But Monica's relief at Augustine's recovery turned to anxiety as he misspent his renewed life being wayward and, as he himself says, lazy. He was finally sent to school at <a href="/wiki/Madauros" title="Madauros">Madauros</a>. He was 17 and studying rhetoric in <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a> when Patricius died.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Augustine had become a <a href="/wiki/Manichaeism" title="Manichaeism">Manichaean</a> at Carthage. When, upon his return home, he shared his views regarding Manichaeism, Monica drove him away from her table. However, she is said to have experienced a vision that convinced her to reconcile with him.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg/170px-Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="219" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg/255px-Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg/340px-Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1242" data-file-height="1600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Augustine_and_Saint_Monica.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Augustine and his mother, Saint Monica,</i> by <a href="/wiki/Ary_Scheffer" title="Ary Scheffer">Ary Scheffer</a> (painting from 1846)</div></div></div>
<p>At this time she visited a certain (unnamed)  bishop who consoled her with the words, "the child of those tears shall never perish." Monica followed her wayward son to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he had gone secretly; when she arrived he had already gone to <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, but she followed him again. Here she found <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a> and through him she ultimately saw Augustine convert to Christianity after 17 years of resistance.
</p><p>
In his book <i>Confessions</i>, Augustine wrote of a peculiar practice of his mother in which she "brought to certain oratories, erected in the memory of the saints, offerings of porridge, bread, water and wine."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> When she moved to Milan, the bishop Ambrose forbade her to use the offering of wine, since "it might be an occasion of gluttony for those who were already given to drink". So, Augustine wrote of her: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>In place of a basket filled with fruits of the earth, she had learned to bring to the oratories of the martyrs a heart full of purer petitions, and to give all that she could to the poor – so that the communion of the Lord's body might be rightly celebrated in those places where, after the example of his passion, the martyrs had been sacrificed and crowned.</p><div class="templatequotecite">—&#8201;<cite><i>Confessions</i> 6.2.2</cite></div></blockquote>
<p>Monica and her son spent six peaceful months at <i>Rus Cassiciacum</i> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Cassago_Brianza" title="Cassago Brianza">Cassago Brianza</a>) after which Augustine was baptized by Ambrose in the church of St John the Baptist at Milan. Monica and Augustine left for Africa and they set out on their journey, stopping at <a href="/wiki/Civitavecchia" title="Civitavecchia">Civitavecchia</a> and at <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica" title="Ostia Antica">Ostia</a>. Here Monica died, and Augustine's grief inspired his <i>Confessions</i>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Monica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:MonicaSAgostino.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/MonicaSAgostino.jpg/220px-MonicaSAgostino.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="168" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/MonicaSAgostino.jpg/330px-MonicaSAgostino.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/MonicaSAgostino.jpg/440px-MonicaSAgostino.jpg 2x" data-file-width="755" data-file-height="576" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:MonicaSAgostino.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Monica's tomb, <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_Sant%27Agostino" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di Sant&#39;Agostino">Basilica di Sant'Agostino</a>, Rome</div></div></div>
<p>Monica was buried at <a href="/wiki/Ostia_Antica" title="Ostia Antica">Ostia</a> and at first seems to have been almost forgotten, though her body was removed during the 6th century to a hidden <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> in the church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Aurea" title="Santa Aurea">Santa Aurea</a> in Ostia. Monica was buried near the tomb of <a href="/wiki/Aurea_of_Ostia" title="Aurea of Ostia">Aurea of Ostia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ostia_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ostia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her tomb was later transferred to the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Agostino,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Agostino, Rome">Basilica of Sant'Agostino, Rome</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Anicius_Auchenius_Bassus_(consul_408)" title="Anicius Auchenius Bassus (consul 408)">Anicius Auchenius Bassus</a> wrote Monica's funerary epitaph, which survived in ancient manuscripts.<sup id="cite_ref-ostia_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ostia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The actual stone on which it was written was rediscovered in the summer of 1945 in the church of Santa Aurea. The fragment was discovered after two boys were digging a hole to plant a <a href="/wiki/Association_football" title="Association football">football</a> post in the courtyard beside Santa Aurea.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBrown2000124_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrown2000124-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A translation from <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> by Douglas Boin reads:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Here the most virtuous mother of a young man set her ashes, a second light to your merits, Augustine. As a priest, serving the heavenly laws of peace, you taught [or, you teach] the people entrusted to you with your character. A glory greater than the praise of your accomplishments crowns you both – Mother of the Virtues, more fortunate because of her offspring.<sup id="cite_ref-ostia_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ostia-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>During the 13th century, however, the cult of Saint Monica began to spread and a feast in her honour was kept on 4 May. In 1430, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_V" title="Pope Martin V">Pope Martin V</a> ordered that the relics be brought to Rome. Many miracles are said to have occurred on the way, and the <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a> of Saint Monica was definitely established. Later the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Rouen" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rouen">archbishop of Rouen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Guillaume_d%27Estouteville" title="Guillaume d&#39;Estouteville">Guillaume d'Estouteville</a>, built a church at Rome in honour of Augustine, the Basilica di Sant'Agostino, and deposited the relics of Saint Monica in a chapel to the left of the high altar. The Office of St. Monica, however, does not seem to have found a place in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Breviary" title="Roman Breviary">Roman Breviary</a> before the 16th century.
</p><p>In 1934, in the Dutch city of Utrecht, the order of the 'Zusters Augustinessen van Sint-Monica' was founded, doing social work, offering a shelter for women with unwanted pregnancies or women who were the victims of domestic violence or abuse. The <a href="/wiki/Religious_sister_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sister (Catholic)">sisters</a> also started a number of primary schools. In their heyday the order had six convents, in Amsterdam, Utrecht, Sittard, Maastricht, Hilversum and Arnouville on the outskirts of Paris. Since the Hilversum convent, City of God, was closed in 2014, only Utrecht remains as a rest home for the elderly among the sisters, and Casella, a woodland retreat near Hilversum, where young people are still welcome for a meditative sojourn.   
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg/220px-Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg/330px-Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg/440px-Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3168" data-file-height="4752" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_Saint_Monica_in_Santa_Monica_California.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Saint Monica in <a href="/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California" title="Santa Monica, California">Santa Monica, California</a></div></div></div>
<p>The city of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California" title="Santa Monica, California">Santa Monica, California</a>, is named after Monica. A legend states that in the 18th century Father <a href="/wiki/Juan_Cresp%C3%AD" title="Juan Crespí">Juan Crespí</a> named a local dripping spring <i>Las Lágrimas de Santa Mónica</i> ("Saint Monica’s Tears"; today known as the <a href="/wiki/Serra_Springs_(California)" class="mw-redirect" title="Serra Springs (California)">Serra Springs</a>) that was reminiscent of the tears that Monica shed over her son's early impiety.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEScott200417–18_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEScott200417–18-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> As recorded in his diary, however, Crespí actually named the place <i>San Gregorio</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEScott200417–18_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEScott200417–18-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> What is known for certain is that by the 1820s, the name Santa Monica was in use and its first official mention occurred in 1827 in the form of a grazing permit.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEScott200417–18_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEScott200417–18-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> There is a statue of this saint in Santa Monica's Palisades Park by sculptor <a href="/wiki/Eugene_Morahan" title="Eugene Morahan">Eugene Morahan</a>; it was completed in 1934.<sup id="cite_ref-morahan_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-morahan-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Monica is honored in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> on 4 May.<sup id="cite_ref-CofECalendar_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CofECalendar-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Monica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Patricia_McGerr" title="Patricia McGerr">Patricia McGerr</a> fictionalized her life in the 1964 novel <i>My Brothers, Remember Monica: A Novel of the Mother of Augustine</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcGerr1964_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGerr1964-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 2010 film <i><a href="/wiki/Restless_Heart:_The_Confessions_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Restless Heart: The Confessions of Saint Augustine">Restless Heart: The Confessions of Saint Augustine</a></i>, Saint Monica is portrayed by Italian actress <a href="/wiki/Monica_Guerritore" title="Monica Guerritore">Monica Guerritore</a>.
</p><p>In the oratorio <i>La conversione di Sant'Agostino</i> (1750) composed by <a href="/wiki/Johann_Adolph_Hasse" title="Johann Adolph Hasse">Johann Adolph Hasse</a> (libretto by <a href="/wiki/Duchess_Maria_Antonia_of_Bavaria" title="Duchess Maria Antonia of Bavaria">Duchess Maria Antonia of Bavaria</a>), Monica's role in the conversion of her son Augustine is dramatized.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESmither197798_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESmither197798-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his poem "Confessional", <a href="/wiki/Frank_Bidart" title="Frank Bidart">Frank Bidart</a> compares the relationship between Monica and her son Augustine to the relationship between the poem's speaker and his mother.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBidart1983_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBidart1983-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In “The Angel of Warning,” the fifth episode of season 3 of the TV series <i><a href="/wiki/Evil_(TV_series)" title="Evil (TV series)">Evil</a></i>, David explains that Monica was Black, although traditionally portrayed as white in religious art. In the tenth episode of season 3, he calls on St. Monica while jogging past a demonic apparition of sexual temptation in the guise of his colleague Kristen. He continues to pray for deliverance from temptation and is visited by St. Monica.
</p>
