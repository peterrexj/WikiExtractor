<p><b>Saint Alypius the Stylite</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἀλύπιος ὁ Στυλίτης</span></span>) was a seventh-century <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> saint.  He is revered as a <a href="/wiki/Monastic" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic">monastic</a> founder, an <a href="/wiki/Intercessor" class="mw-redirect" title="Intercessor">intercessor</a> for the infertile, and a protector of children<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>.  During his lifetime he was a much sought-after <a href="/wiki/Starets" title="Starets">starets</a> (guide in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">Christian</a> spiritual life).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alypius_the_Stylite&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Alypius_the_Stylite_(Tzanes).png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Alypius_the_Stylite_%28Tzanes%29.png/150px-Alypius_the_Stylite_%28Tzanes%29.png" decoding="async" width="150" height="204" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Alypius_the_Stylite_%28Tzanes%29.png/225px-Alypius_the_Stylite_%28Tzanes%29.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Alypius_the_Stylite_%28Tzanes%29.png/300px-Alypius_the_Stylite_%28Tzanes%29.png 2x" data-file-width="1208" data-file-height="1640" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Alypius_the_Stylite_(Tzanes).png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Alypius the Stylite by <a href="/wiki/Emmanuel_Tzanes" title="Emmanuel Tzanes">Emmanuel Tzanes</a></div></div></div>
<p>Alypius was born in the city of Hadrianopolis in <a href="/wiki/Paphlagonia" title="Paphlagonia">Paphlagonia</a>. His mother, who had been <a href="/wiki/Widow" title="Widow">widowed</a> early, was very pious. She sent her son to be educated by the bishop Theodore, gave all of her livelihood to the poor, and herself became a <a href="/wiki/Deaconess" title="Deaconess">deaconess</a> and lived an <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a> life.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Alypius yearned to practice the life of a hermit, but Bishop Theodore would not give him permission to do so. Alypius built a church in honour of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Martyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Martyr">Great Martyr</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Euphemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Euphemia">Saint Euphemia</a> the All-Praised on the site of a dilapidated pagan temple.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He erected a pillar beside the church and lived atop it for the majority of his adult life.  Two <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> were built beside his pillar, one for <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a> and one for <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>, and Saint Alypius served as spiritual director of both.  It is claimed that, after standing upright for fifty-three years, Alypius found his feet no longer able to support him, but instead of descending from his pillar lay down on his side and spent the remaining fourteen years of his life in that position.<sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Alypius died in 640, at the claimed age of 118.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alypius_the_Stylite&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alypius is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and those <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Eastern Catholic Churches</a> which follow the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a>, as well as the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> on November 26.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> For those churches which follow the <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a> November 26 currently falls on December 9 of the modern <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>. After his death his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were interred in the Church of St. Euphemia which he had built.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His head is preserved in the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Koutloumousiou" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Koutloumousiou">Monastery of Koutloumousiou</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>.
</p><p>Alypius is recognised as one of the three great <a href="/wiki/Stylite" title="Stylite">stylite</a> ascetics along with <a href="/wiki/Simeon_Stylites" title="Simeon Stylites">Simeon Stylites the Elder</a> and <a href="/wiki/Daniel_the_Stylite" title="Daniel the Stylite">Daniel the Stylite</a>. <a href="/wiki/Herbert_Thurston" title="Herbert Thurston">Herbert Thurston</a> says of the Stylites that they did, in an age of terrible corruption and social decadence, impress the need of penance more than anything else could have done upon the minds and imagination of Eastern Christians.<sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
