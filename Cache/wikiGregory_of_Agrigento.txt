<p><b>Gregory</b> (559–630) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Agrigento" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Agrigento">bishop of Agrigento</a><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> from 590 until at least 603 and a correspondent of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a>. He is the probable subject of two semi-legendary <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">saint's lives</a> and possible author of a commentary on <i><a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastes" title="Ecclesiastes">Ecclesiastes</a></i>, although both of these identifications have been questioned.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_of_Agrigento&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to his biography, Gregory was born near <a href="/wiki/Agrigento" title="Agrigento">Agrigento</a> on <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> in 559.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His mother's name was Theodote.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> At the age of eighteen,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999158_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999158-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup> he went on a pilgrimage in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, traveling via <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a> to <a href="/wiki/Tripoli,_Lebanon" title="Tripoli, Lebanon">Tripoli</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999158_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999158-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was almost sold into slavery by a <i>naukleros</i> (ship-owner) in Carthage. The account of his travels in his biography has a romantic character and seems to have been an influence on the 10th-century <i>Life</i> of <a href="/wiki/Gregentios" title="Gregentios">Gregentios</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999158_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999158-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While in <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, he was ordained a deacon by Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Makarios_II_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Makarios II of Jerusalem">Makarios II</a> (c.&#8201;563&#8201;–&#8201;c.&#8201;575).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He returned to Agrigento via <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 590,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> two factions with their respective candidates for the vacant see of Agrigento traveled to Rome to seek  the pope's decision. Pope Gregory bypassed both candidates and consecrated Gregory of Agrigento.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was found hiding in a monastic garden out of reticence for high office when Gregory chose him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200412n_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200412n-11">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The biography attributes to Gregory an education in <a href="/wiki/Classics" title="Classics">classics</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rhetoric" title="Rhetoric">rhetoric</a> and <a href="/wiki/Christian_theology" title="Christian theology">theology</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He is said to have read the <i>Life</i> of <a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a> many times and the <i><a href="/wiki/Passio_Sanctorum_Machabaeorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Passio Sanctorum Machabaeorum">Passion of the Holy Maccabees</a></i>. At the request of a bishop, he interpreted the writings of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus" title="Gregory of Nazianzus">Gregory of Nazianzus</a> for a group of deacons. The biographer praises him as a second <a href="/wiki/John_Chrysostom" title="John Chrysostom">Chrysostom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999153_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999153-12">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> By a miracle, he was able to fast constantly. He is also credited with performing healing miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 591, Gregory had been falsely accused of wrongdoing and was imprisoned.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn2004244–245_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn2004244–245-13">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> According to the biography, the accusers were a certain Sabinus and Crescentius.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999156_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1999156-14">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> A papal letter of August 591 ordered him to appear along with the bishops of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Catania" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Catania">Catania</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Palermo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Palermo">Palermo</a> before the sub-deacon Peter, a papal agent. In November 592, the pope wrote to Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Maximian_of_Syracuse&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Maximian of Syracuse (page does not exist)">Maximian of Syracuse</a> demanding that he send Gregory's accusers and some documentation to Rome without delay. In this letter, the pope refers to a letter he addressed to Gregory that has not survived.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn2004244–245_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn2004244–245-13">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Some sources have him as deposed from his see by 594,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but the pope in a letter names Gregory as still bishop in January 603.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELanzoni1927641_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELanzoni1927641-9">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200429_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200429-15">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> According to some sources, he died in 630.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEVan_Pelt20201_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEVan_Pelt20201-16">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_of_Agrigento&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:292px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg/290px-Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="203" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg/435px-Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg/580px-Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1075" data-file-height="753" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Menologion_of_Basil_029.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Gregory depicted in the <i><a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a></i> (c.&#8201;1000)</div></div></div>
<p>A life of Gregory was written by Leontios<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;d&#93;</a></sup> of the <a href="/wiki/San_Saba,_Rome" title="San Saba, Rome">monastery of San Saba</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Its full title is <i>An Account of the Life of Saint Gregory, Bishop of the Church of Agrigento</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;e&#93;</a></sup> It is a lengthy work in <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It was translated into <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> in the 18th century by <a href="/wiki/Stefano_Antonio_Morcelli" title="Stefano Antonio Morcelli">Stefano Antonio Morcelli</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Its two most recent editors disagree regarding the date of its composition and its relative historicity.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Albrecht Berger assigns it to the period between 750 and 828 on the grounds that it relies on the <a href="/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine" title="Donation of Constantine">Donation of Constantine</a> (unknown before the mid-8th century).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He rejects an early date on the grounds that there is no evidence for Greek-speaking monasteries in Rome before 649.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> John Martyn, arguing from correspondences between the biography and the papal letters, assigns it an early date of around 640.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Leontios is by some said to have died in 688, providing a <i><a href="/wiki/Terminus_ante_quem" class="mw-redirect" title="Terminus ante quem">terminus ante quem</a></i> if he is the author.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The editors' assessments of Gregory's biography's historical value also differ. For Berger, "though it has a historical core, [it] is in large parts legendary."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerger200626–27-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He does not think that the historical person at the core was the bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241_19-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> For Martyn, it is "an important, contemporary document on the cities, clergy and people of Agrigento, Jerusalem, Antioch, Constantinople and Rome during" the papacy of Gregory I and one of very few 7th-century sources on Sicily.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> There are contradictions in the biography and in the account in the <i><a href="/wiki/Synaxarion_of_Constantinople" title="Synaxarion of Constantinople">Synaxarion of Constantinople</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;f&#93;</a></sup> The latter has him alive during the patriarchate of Makarios II and the reign of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a> (685–711) over a century later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The biography depicts him as a contemporary of the <a href="/wiki/Monothelitism" title="Monothelitism">monothelite controversy</a>, which began in 629. When he is arrested in Agrigento, the Emperor Justinian intervenes with the pope to secure his release. The biography depicts the Sicilian episcopate as supporting Gregory against the papacy and in general has an anti-papal tone.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Morcelli, in his Latin edition, argued that the anti-papal tone stemmed from some pamphlets directed against Gregory I that circulated in Rome after his death. To Morcelli, it was evidence of the early date of the biography.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241_19-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDemacopoulos2013240–241-19">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The biography of Gregory survives in twenty manuscripts.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERe2011248_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERe2011248-21">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Besides the original work of Leontios (<a href="/wiki/Bibliotheca_Hagiographica_Graeca" title="Bibliotheca Hagiographica Graeca">BHG</a> 707), there is also a biography (BHG 708) by <a href="/wiki/Niketas_David_Paphlagon" title="Niketas David Paphlagon">Niketas David Paphlagon</a> (fl.&#8201;c.&#8201;900). This was the text used by the compiler <a href="/wiki/Simeon_Metaphrastes" class="mw-redirect" title="Simeon Metaphrastes">Simeon Metaphrastes</a> in the 10th century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHøgel2002188_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHøgel2002188-22">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> It was one of only 14 texts out of 148 that Simeon left intact and did not rework,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHøgel200292_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHøgel200292-23">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and one of only seven that he promised the reader would give them pleasure to read.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHøgel2002143_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHøgel2002143-24">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> There are also two shorter reworkings of Leontios' biography, one (BHG 707p) attributed to Mark, <i><a href="/wiki/Hegoumenos" class="mw-redirect" title="Hegoumenos">hegoumenos</a></i> of San Saba, and another (BHG 708f) anonymous.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERe2011248_21-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERe2011248-21">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMorciano2001944n_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorciano2001944n-25">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory's feast is celebrated on 23 or 24 November in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It is on 24 November in the work of Simeon Metaphrastes.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHøgel2002188_22-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHøgel2002188-22">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> It was introduced to the <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i> by Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Caesar_Baronius" title="Caesar Baronius">Caesar Baronius</a> on 23 November.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The popularity of Gregory's cult can be gauged by the large number of surviving iconographic representations of him.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERe2011248_21-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERe2011248-21">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commentary_on_Ecclesiastes">Commentary on <i>Ecclesiastes</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gregory_of_Agrigento&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Commentary on Ecclesiastes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:292px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon,_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon%2C_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg/290px-St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon%2C_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg" decoding="async" width="290" height="308" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon%2C_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg/435px-St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon%2C_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon%2C_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg/580px-St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon%2C_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="671" data-file-height="712" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St._Gregory_of_Agrigentum_and_St._Antipas_of_Pergamon,_south_wall_of_diakonikon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Fresco depicting Gregory and <a href="/wiki/Antipas_of_Pergamum" title="Antipas of Pergamum">Antipas of Pergamum</a> in the Church of the Theotokos Peribleptos in <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a> (13th century)</div></div></div>
<p>The hagiography supplies a list of works by Gregory, one of which was dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrew">Saint Andrew</a>, described as "chief" (<i>koryphaios</i>) of the apostles.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A Greek commentary on <i>Ecclesiastes</i><sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;g&#93;</a></sup> is traditionally attributed to the bishop of Agrigento.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426_2-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartyn200426-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan1991-8">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This attribution is rejected by some, who think the exegete must have been writing in the time of Justinian II.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossLivingstone2009-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Since the earliest manuscripts of the commentary date from the 8th or 9th centuries, the commentator can only securely be placed in the 7th century. The result of this theory is the existence of two distinct Gregories of Agrigentum, the bishop (fl.&#8201;c.&#8201;600) and the exegete (fl.&#8201;c.&#8201;700).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKazhdan199925–26-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The commentary attributed to Gregory is considered one of the best on <i>Ecclesiastes</i> from antiquity.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFerguson1999_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFerguson1999-27">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
