<p><b>Columba of Spain</b> (also <b>Columba of Córdoba</b>) was a virgin and nun who was born in <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba, Spain</a>, and martyred around 853 by the Muslim rulers in Spain, during a persecution of Christians. She is a part of the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Martyrs of Córdoba">Martyrs of Córdoba</a> and venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. Her feast day is September 17. Her cult was probably a combination of two virgin martyrs, Colomba of Spain and <a href="/wiki/Columba_of_Sens" title="Columba of Sens">Columba of Sens</a>, a third century French martyr.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Columba_of_Spain&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Columba was born in <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba, Spain</a>, the youngest of three children. According to hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, Columba's biography was recorded by <a href="/wiki/Eulogius_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Eulogius of Córdoba">St. Eulogius of Córdoba</a>, in <i>The Memorial of the Saints</i>, his account of the Christian persecution in Spain that began in 850.<sup id="cite_ref-butler-162_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-162-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Also according to Butler, "even allowing for exaggeration, [Columba] had a high reputation for holiness".<sup id="cite_ref-butler-162_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-162-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her sisters Elizabeth and Martinus, along with Elizabeth's husband, founded a double monastery at Tábanos, a mountainous region north of Córdoba.<sup id="cite_ref-butler-162_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-162-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-wolf-31_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-31-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Columba's brother Martin was abbot of the men's section of the monastery. Columba was inspired by their example and was determined to become a nun, but her plans were, for a short time, thwarted by her mother, a widow who wanted Columba to marry. Shortly after her mother realized that her opposition was fruitless, she died, and Columba entered Tábanos.<sup id="cite_ref-butler-162_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-162-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to historian <a href="/wiki/Kenneth_Baxter_Wolf" title="Kenneth Baxter Wolf">Kenneth B. Wolf</a>, Eulogious' account of Columba's experience at Tábonos is full of "hyperbole", but "revealing".<sup id="cite_ref-wolf-112_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-112-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Wolf reported that Columba "suffered from overwhelming anxiety about her own shortcomings",<sup id="cite_ref-wolf-112_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-112-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and experienced "a profound uncertainty about her own ability to resist temptation".<sup id="cite_ref-wolf-112_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-112-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Following her sisters to Tábanos was a way, Wolfe said, for Columba to relieve her anxieties, but Wolf did not think it was successful, for she increased her self-punishments after taking her vows. He said, "Chastity, the keystone of the penitential regimen, was not enough for Columba".<sup id="cite_ref-wolf-113_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-113-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Martyrdom was a way to "contribute in a positive way toward her own salvation".<sup id="cite_ref-wolf-113_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-113-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 852, after the persecution in Spain had been going on for two years, the community was forced out of Tábanos and took refuge in a house in Córdoba, near the church of <a href="/wiki/Cyprian" title="Cyprian">St. Cyprian</a>. She ignored the bishops' ruling not to provoke persecution, left the house, presented herself before the town's Muslim magistrate, and denounced <a href="/wiki/Muhammad" title="Muhammad">Muhammed</a> and his law. The magistrate condemned her to be beheaded on September 17, 853. She was the sixth martyr executed under <a href="/wiki/Muhammad_I_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Muhammad I of Córdoba">Mohammed I</a>, and was one of the <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" title="Martyrs of Córdoba">Martyrs of Córdoba</a>, a group of 48 martyrs killed during this period. Her body was thrown into the <a href="/wiki/Guadalquivir_Marshes" title="Guadalquivir Marshes">Guadalquivir Marshes</a>, but was recovered by other Christians and buried at the basilica of St. Eulalia at Fragellas. Her relics are reported to be taken later to and venerated at two churches, the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_la_Real_of_N%C3%A1jera" title="Santa María la Real of Nájera">Santa Maria de Nájera</a> and to its dependent <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a>, which was dedicated to Saint Columba.<sup id="cite_ref-butler-162_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-162-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-wolf-31_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wolf-31-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Historian Allyson M. Poska states that Columba's cult was "probably a combination of two virgin martyrs":<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Columba_of_Sens" title="Columba of Sens">St. Columba of Sens</a> (or Comba), a third century French martyr from <a href="/wiki/Sens" title="Sens">Sens</a>, and Columba of Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Poska speculates that a <a href="/wiki/Visigoths" title="Visigoths">Visigothic</a> church dedicated to Saint Columba in <a href="/wiki/Bande,_Ourense" title="Bande, Ourense">Bande</a> (near the Portuguese border) may indicate the spread of her devotion before the Muslims invaded Spain. The legend probably became conflated with Columba of Sens when settlers repopulated <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Spain)" title="Galicia (Spain)">Galicia</a> after the Muslims were expelled from the region.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
