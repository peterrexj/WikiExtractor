Mosaic from the Monastery of Hosios Loukas.</div></div></div>
<p><b>Luke of Steiris</b> (also <b>Luke <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgus" class="mw-redirect" title="Thaumaturgus">Thaumaturgus</a></b>, <b>Luke the Younger</b>, <b>Luke of Hellas</b>, <b>Luke the Wonder-worker</b>; 896 — 953 AD) was a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine">Byzantine</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the tenth century AD who lived in the <a href="/wiki/Theme_(Byzantine_district)" title="Theme (Byzantine district)">themes</a> (provinces) of <a href="/wiki/Hellas_(theme)" title="Hellas (theme)">Hellas</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peloponnese" title="Peloponnese">Peloponnese</a> in <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Greece" title="Byzantine Greece">Greece</a>, and who founded the Monastery of <a href="/wiki/Hosios_Loukas" title="Hosios Loukas">Hosios Loukas</a> (Venerable Luke) on the slopes of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Helicon" title="Mount Helicon">Mount Helicon</a>, between Delphi and Levadia, near the coast of the Gulf of Corinth in Boeotia, Greece. He was one of the earliest <a href="/wiki/Flying_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Flying saints">saints to be seen levitating in prayer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The principal source for Luke's life is an anonymous <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography"><i>Life</i></a> written by a monk of Hosios Loukas who had been one of Luke's followers. His feast day is commemorated on <a href="/wiki/February_7_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="February 7 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">February 7</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX1_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and the translation of his relics on <a href="/wiki/May_3_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="May 3 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">May 3</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> are preserved in his <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hosios_Loukas" title="Hosios Loukas">Hosios Loukas</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luke_of_Steiris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood">Childhood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luke_of_Steiris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Childhood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:262px;"><a href="/wiki/File:%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery,_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_(1).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery%2C_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg/260px-%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery%2C_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="260" height="173" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery%2C_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg/390px-%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery%2C_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery%2C_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg/520px-%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery%2C_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1064" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:%22Hosios_Lukas%22_monastery,_Greece_-_%C2%A9_UNESCO_World_Heritage_property_-_panoramio_(1).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The monastery of <a href="/wiki/Hosios_Loukas" title="Hosios Loukas">Hosios Loukas</a></div></div></div>
<p>Luke was a native of the Greek village of Kastorion in <a href="/wiki/Phocis" title="Phocis">Phocis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was the third of the seven children of Stephen and Euphrosyne. The <i>Life</i>, typically for the genre, begins with signs of Luke's closeness to God as a child. For instance, Luke is recorded as having done 'nothing in a childish fashion',<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and is seen to be close to God when his mother witnesses him levitating in prayer.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The son of poor farmers, the saint worked in the fields and tended sheep.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As a child Luke tried twice to leave home to seek a solitary life of prayer. The first time, he attempted to withdraw to <a href="/wiki/Thessaly" title="Thessaly">Thessaly</a>, but was captured by soldiers lying in wait for escaped <a href="/wiki/Slavery_in_medieval_Europe" title="Slavery in medieval Europe">slaves</a> and was returned home.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The second time he had more success, meeting two monks <a href="/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage" title="Christian pilgrimage">journeying</a> from <a href="/wiki/History_of_Rome#Medieval_Rome" title="History of Rome">Rome</a> to <a href="/wiki/History_of_Jerusalem_(Middle_Ages)" class="mw-redirect" title="History of Jerusalem (Middle Ages)">Jerusalem</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> who took him to a monastery in <a href="/wiki/History_of_athens#Byzantine_Athens" class="mw-redirect" title="History of athens">Athens</a> where he received the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Small_habit&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Small habit (page does not exist)">small habit</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> After Luke's mother prayed for her son's return however, God made her appear in a dream to the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and commanded him to return Luke to his home.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Asceticism">Asceticism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luke_of_Steiris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Asceticism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the age of 14, with his mother's blessing, he went to a solitary place on a mountain called Ioannou (or Ioannitza)<sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and for 7 years lived as an ascetic.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Life</i> records with symmetry that during this time Luke received the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Great_habit&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Great habit (page does not exist)">great habit</a> from two monks travelling from Jerusalem to Rome (presumably the same two from whom he had received the small habit on their outward journey).<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Luke's fame spread and a number of miracles are ascribed to him during this period, such as revealing to two brothers the location of their dead father's buried treasure.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Numerous proofs of Luke's holiness are also given, such as sleeping in a trench to remind himself of death,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> or being visited in dream by an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> who let a hook down Luke's mouth and 'drew out a certain fleshly member therefrom', freeing him from the temptations of the flesh.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Exodus">Exodus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luke_of_Steiris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Exodus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Luke was forced to leave Ioannitza by an invasion of the <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgarian</a> emperor <a href="/wiki/Simeon_I_of_Bulgaria" title="Simeon I of Bulgaria">Symeon</a> (which Luke had predicted).<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Luke, followed by the local villagers, fled to a nearby island, almost perishing when attacked by Bulgarians in a stolen ship.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> After the invaders withdrew, Luke, now aged 21, enrolled in a school in <a href="/wiki/Corinth" title="Corinth">Corinth</a>, but soon left after he found the other students insufficiently serious.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Instead, he went to serve a <a href="/wiki/Stylite" title="Stylite">stylite</a> at Zemena for the next ten years, until he was stranded in the Peloponnese on an errand when the harbour master refused to allow him to return to Hellas, fearing raids (whether Bulgarian or <a href="/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Arab_Wars_(780%E2%80%931180)" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine–Arab Wars (780–1180)">Arabic</a> is not mentioned).<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> When Luke was ejected from his oratory in the Peloponnese after a rainstorm, his hagiographer comments that 'God perhaps arranged these things beneficially, lest dwelling too long in the <a href="/wiki/Peloponnese" title="Peloponnese">land of Pelops</a> he do an injustice to his fatherland', perhaps indicating a rival cult of the saint in the Peloponnese and providing an interesting example of patriotism towards the theme.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Return">Return</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luke_of_Steiris&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Return">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <i>Life</i> also cites Symeon's death in 927 and the succession of his more pacific son <a href="/wiki/Peter_I_of_Bulgaria" title="Peter I of Bulgaria">Peter</a> as a reason why Luke returned to Ioannitza to build his own community.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Luke drew so many followers that he found the distractions unbearable and decided to retreat further into the wilderness.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Three years later, however, Luke was displaced again, this time by a <a href="/wiki/Magyars" class="mw-redirect" title="Magyars">Magyar</a> invasion. Just as before, Luke retreated with the local villagers to a nearby island.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Once there, Luke found the desert island to be a suitable place to pursue his solitary ascetic life, and stayed for three years, enduring terrible thirsts.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eventually Luke's companions persuaded him to leave, and he settled for the remainder of his life in the far more amenable environment of the present <a href="/wiki/Hosios_Loukas" title="Hosios Loukas">Hosios Loukas</a>, where he founded his hermitage c. 946 AD in the area of <a href="/wiki/Steiri" title="Steiri">Stiris</a> (which may be a corruption of Soterion, or place of healing).<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Here brethren gathered to the elder, and a small monastery grew up, the church of which was dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Barbara" title="Saint Barbara">Great Martyr Barbara</a>. Dwelling in the monastery, the saint performed many miracles, healing sicknesses of soul and of body.<sup id="cite_ref-SYNAX1_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SYNAX1-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-oca_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
