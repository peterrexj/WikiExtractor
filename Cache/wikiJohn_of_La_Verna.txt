<p><b>John of Fermo</b>, O.F.M., more often called <b>John of La Verna</b>, from his time spent on <a href="/wiki/La_Verna" title="La Verna">that mountain</a> (1259 – 10 August 1322) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>, who was a noted <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> and preacher.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_La_Verna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was born at <a href="/wiki/Fermo" title="Fermo">Fermo</a> in the <a href="/wiki/March_of_Ancona" title="March of Ancona">March of Ancona</a>. After a youth of precocious piety, he was received at the age of ten among the <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a> of the Priory of St. Peter's at Fermo. Three years later, desirous of leading a more austere life, he entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a>, and under the direction of a noted friar, <a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_Fallerone&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="James of Fallerone (page does not exist)">James of Fallerone</a>, soon made rapid progress in the spiritual life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobinson1910_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinson1910-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after his <a href="/wiki/Profession_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Profession (religious)">profession</a>, John was sent by the <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a> of the Order to Mount <a href="/wiki/La_Verna" title="La Verna">La Verna</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>, where <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> had received the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a>. There he spent many years in solitude, <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> and <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplation</a>, receiving ecstasies and celestial visions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobinson1910_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinson1910-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John's later years, however, were devoted to <a href="/wiki/Christian_ministry" title="Christian ministry">Christian ministry</a>, and he preached at <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, <a href="/wiki/Arezzo" title="Arezzo">Arezzo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> and many other towns of northern and central Italy, working wonders everywhere. He was a close friend of the poet, Friar <a href="/wiki/Jacopone_of_Todi" class="mw-redirect" title="Jacopone of Todi">Jacopone of Todi</a>, and administered the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">last rites</a> to him in 1306.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobinson1910_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinson1910-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John is said to have composed the <a href="/wiki/Preface" title="Preface">Preface</a> which is said in the <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> of St. Francis. Feeling the approach of death at <a href="/wiki/Cortona" title="Cortona">Cortona</a> while on his way to Assisi, John returned to La Verna and died there at the age of 63 in 1322.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobinson1910_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinson1910-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_La_Verna&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was buried on the holy mountain, where many miracles were reportedly wrought through his intercession, and where his cell is still shown. The <i><a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a></i> of John was approved by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a> in 1880.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERobinson1910_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERobinson1910-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John's <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is kept in the Order of Friars Minor on 9 August.
</p>
