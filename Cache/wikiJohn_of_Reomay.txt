					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">French Christian abbot</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">John</div><br /><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">of Réôme</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">425</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Hometown</th><td class="infobox-data">near <a href="/wiki/Langres" title="Langres">Langres</a>, France</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">539<br /><a href="/wiki/Moutiers-Saint-Jean_Abbey" title="Moutiers-Saint-Jean Abbey">Réôme Abbey</a>, France</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">28 January</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>John of Réôme</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Jean de Réôme</i>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Iohannis Reomaensis</i>; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;539</span>) was an early Christian abbot in what is now <a href="/wiki/Moutiers-Saint-Jean" title="Moutiers-Saint-Jean">Moutiers-Saint-Jean</a> in the <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B4te-d%27Or" title="Côte-d&#39;Or">Côte-d'Or</a> department of France.
</p><p>His feast is on 28 January.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_R%C3%A9%C3%B4me&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg/220px-Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="163" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg/330px-Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg/440px-Abbaye_Saint-Jean-de-R%C3%A9ome_dans_Monasticon_Gallicanum.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4282" data-file-height="3170" /></a><figcaption>Abbaye Saint-Jean-de-Réome in <i>Monasticon Gallicanum</i> (17th century)</figcaption></figure>
<p>John of Réôme was born in Courtangy, France, around 450.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
At the age of 20 he became a hermit at Réôme (now Ménétreux), but was joined by disciples.
When their numbers grew too great he escaped to <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">Lérins Abbey</a>, where he became a monk.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins2015PT614_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins2015PT614-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
At Lérins Abbey he lived under the rule of <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Macarius of Egypt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
John was at Lérins Abbey in the period 506–510, when <a href="/wiki/Porcarius_I" title="Porcarius I">Porcarius I</a> (fl. 489–495) was abbot there.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDel_Cogliano2003_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDel_Cogliano2003-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
Porcarius was the author of the <i>Monita</i> (Counsels), a short collection of spiritual wisdom.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDel_Cogliano2003_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDel_Cogliano2003-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELe_Pree2010_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELe_Pree2010-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
John was discovered and his bishop recalled him to found a monastery at Réôme in the commune of <a href="/wiki/Corsaint" title="Corsaint">Corsaint</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins2015PT614_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins2015PT614-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
The <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9%C3%B4me_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Réôme Abbey">Réôme Abbey</a> also followed the rule of Macarius, and was one of the first in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Burgundians" title="Kingdom of the Burgundians">Burgundy</a>.
He had a high reputation for sanctity and was said to have worked various miracles.
He died between 539 and 544.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Jonas_of_Susa" class="mw-redirect" title="Jonas of Susa">Jonas of Susa</a> wrote John of Réôme's biography.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
His <i>Vita Iohannis Reomaensis</i> shows the influence of Porcarius's <i>Monita</i> on him in its idealization of the spiritual life.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWood2018164_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWood2018164-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
John's remains were preserved during the <a href="/wiki/Umayyad_invasion_of_Gaul" title="Umayyad invasion of Gaul">Saracen invasion</a> in 731, and again when the <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> invaded in 888.
In 1793 the main relics escaped being destroyed during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHalton1967_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHalton1967-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monks_of_Ramsgate_account">Monks of Ramsgate account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_R%C3%A9%C3%B4me&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monks of Ramsgate account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The monks of <a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey,_Ramsgate" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey, Ramsgate">St Augustine's Abbey, Ramsgate</a>, wrote in their <i>Book of Saints</i> (1921),
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>JOHN (St.) Abbot. (Jan.28)<br />(6th century) Styled by the Roman Martyrology "A man of God." Born near Langres in France, early in life he built a monastery on the banks of the little river Reaume, of which he became the Abbot. His death is placed at about A.D. 539.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921148_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESt._Augustine&#39;s_Abbey,_Ramsgate1921148-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Butler.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Butler's_account">Butler's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_R%C3%A9%C3%B4me&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Butler&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The hagiographer <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>  1710–1773) wrote in his <i>Lives of the Fathers, Martyrs, and Other Principal Saints</i>,
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>St JOHN of Reomay, now called Moutier-Saint-Jean, in Burgundy, Ab. He was a native of the diocess of Langres, and took the monastic habit at Lerins. He was called into his own country by the bishop of Langres to found the abbey from which he received his surname. He settled it under the rule of Saint Macarius, governed it many years with great reputation of sanctity, and was rendered famous by miracles. He went to God about the year 405, being almost 120 years old, and was one of the holy institutors of the monastic state in France. Saint Gregory of Tours gives an account of him in the 87th chapter of his book, <i>On the glory of Confessors</i>. His life was also compiled by Jonas, the disciple of Columban, extant in Bollandus. See P. Rover. Hist. Monast. S. Joan. Reom. Paris. 1637.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler1798397–398_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler1798397–398-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> </p></blockquote>
<h2><span id="Ranbeck.27s_account"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Ranbeck's_account">Ranbeck's account</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_R%C3%A9%C3%B4me&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Ranbeck&#039;s account">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ægidius Ranbeck (1608–1692) wrote in his <i>Heiliges Benedictiner-Jahr</i> (Saints of the Order of Saint Benedict; 1677),
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>Saint John, Abbot
</p><p>
Saint John is renowned as having lived to the almost unheard of age of a hundred and twenty years. His parents wished to have brought him up to be a soldier, but his ambition was of quite another kind, and the only victory he longed to achieve was that of virtue over vice, so he fled from his home, and built himself a log hut in a remote village, where, with two boys for his companions, he led a hermit’s life. But even in the forest Saint John could not command the solitude he desired, for the fame of his sanctity caused many people to seek his counsel, who aspired to lead a holy life; therefore, in order to escape their assiduity, he fled by night with his companions and visited the monasteries of Gaul, finally taking up his abode at Lerins. There, after some time, he was discovered by one of his former subjects, who appealed to the Bishop of Langres to prevail on him to return to his previous abode. Here he became renowned for his miracles. Once when there was no water for the use of his Community, a well was discovered in answer to his prayers, by which their thirst could have been abundantly supplied, but when the water was tasted it was found to be so noxious that no one could drink it. Again the Saint was applied to, and, in answer to his prayers, the water became wholesome and pure. At his voice devils would come out of those who were tormented by them, while he encouraged all by his piety to lead a life of holy fear, in order that they might come to a happy death. Once when his monks were at manual labour, chopping trees, the bell suddenly summoned them to the church for the Divine Office, and they left their axes cleft in the boughs. On their return to work, they found that the axes had been stolen, and immediately reported the loss to Saint John. He ordered them to apply themselves to study, and forthwith set himself to pray, and with such success, that the thief found himself obliged, nolens, volens, to restore the axes. Saint John died at the age of one hundred and twenty years, in the year 700, and many miracles were wrought at his tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERanbeck1896_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERanbeck1896-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p> </blockquote>
