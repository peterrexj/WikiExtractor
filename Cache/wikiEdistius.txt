<p><b>Saint Edistus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Sant'Edisto</i>) (also known as <b>Aristus, Orestes, Horestes</b>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches.  His legend states that he was martyred on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Laurentina" title="Via Laurentina">Via Laurentina</a> and his <i>passio</i> places his martyrdom during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>, on October 12, 60 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-bautz.de_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bautz.de-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His companions are named as <b>Thermantia (Termanzia), Christina</b>, his servant <b>Victoria</b>, and the priest <b>Priscus</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-bautz.de_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bautz.de-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edistus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>, there existed at <a href="/wiki/San_Paolo_fuori_le_Mura" class="mw-redirect" title="San Paolo fuori le Mura">San Paolo fuori le Mura</a> a monastery dedicated to Edistus.  In the seventh century, his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>, as well as those belonging to Christina and Victoria, were still venerated there.  The <a href="/wiki/Sepulcher" class="mw-redirect" title="Sepulcher">sepulcher</a> of Edistus was located at the sixteenth milestone of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a>.  A church in honor of him existed there, which was restored during the papacy of <a href="/wiki/Adrian_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Adrian I">Adrian I</a> (772-795).  There also existed a papal estate (<i><a href="/wiki/Domusculta" title="Domusculta">domusculta</a></i>) there called <i>Sancti Edisti</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-bautz.de_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bautz.de-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-prolocosantoreste.com_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prolocosantoreste.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is patron of an old church on the <a href="/wiki/Monte_Soratte" title="Monte Soratte">Monte Soratte</a>, near which a town, <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Oreste" title="Sant&#39;Oreste">Sant'Oreste</a>, is named after him.  The first mention of Sant'Oreste is made by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Soracte" title="Benedict of Soracte">Benedict of Soracte</a> in his <i>Chronicon</i> in 747 AD, in which he mentions <i>Curtis Sancii Heristi</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Comune_di_Sant&#39;Oreste_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Comune_di_Sant&#39;Oreste-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  One source states that the <a href="/wiki/Toponym" class="mw-redirect" title="Toponym">toponym</a> derives from the family of the Aristi or Edisti.  A member of this family was martyred for his faith around 68 AD.  Linguistic corruptions transformed the name from <i>Sanctus Edistus</i> to <i>Sanctus Heristus, Santo Resto, San Tresto, Sant'Oreste</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Comune_di_Sant&#39;Oreste_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Comune_di_Sant&#39;Oreste-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Soratte01.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Soratte01.JPG/250px-Soratte01.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Soratte01.JPG/375px-Soratte01.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Soratte01.JPG/500px-Soratte01.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Soratte01.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Mount Soratte seen from <a href="/wiki/Via_Flaminia" title="Via Flaminia">Via Flaminia</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>According to the legendary account of his martyrdom, he was a soldier who had been christened by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a>.  At a site called <i>Laurento</i>, a <a href="/wiki/Sandpit" title="Sandpit">sandpit</a> (<i>arenario</i>), Edistus participated in a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">mass</a> at which Priscus was officiating.  Thermantia, Christina, and the servant Victoria were present.  When they were discovered by the authorities, they were <a href="/wiki/Premature_burial" title="Premature burial">buried alive</a> in the same sandpit, with the exception of Victoria, who managed to escape, but who was later killed in a nearby forest.<sup id="cite_ref-bautz.de_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bautz.de-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A church and village grew on the site of sandpit.  This was the <i>curtis Sancti Heristi</i>.  The village later moved to the slopes of Mount Soratte for better defense against raiders.  This became the <i>Castrum Sancti Heristi</i>, later Sant'Oreste. The <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a> church of Sant'Edisto still exists.<sup id="cite_ref-bautz.de_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bautz.de-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-prolocosantoreste.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-prolocosantoreste.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
