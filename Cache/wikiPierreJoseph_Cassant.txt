<p><b>Pierre-Joseph Cassant</b> (6 March 1878 - 17 June 1903) was a French <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> and professed member of the <a href="/wiki/Trappists" title="Trappists">Trappists</a>. During his novitiate he received the religious name of <b>Marie-Joseph</b> and was known for his strong determination to his studies to fulfil his lifelong wish of being <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the priesthood.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cassant suffered from extreme <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> around the time of his ordination and died not long after he was made a priest.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> celebrated his beatification on 3 October 2004.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre-Joseph_Cassant&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pierre-Joseph Cassant was born on 6 March 1878 in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> to parents who were orchard-keepers as the second child born to them; his older brother was Emile (b. 1869) who was nine at the time of Cassant's birth.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was a lodger at a boarding school that the <a href="/wiki/De_La_Salle_Brothers" title="De La Salle Brothers">De La Salle Brothers</a> ran and it was here that his abilities in memorizing things were quite diminished and led to increasing learning difficulties. Despite these failings he was seen as a quiet and caring child.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was at the age of 14 that he realized that he wanted to become a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> but learning difficulties prevented him from entering the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a>, so went to the parish priest Father Filhol for advice; he suggested he seek out the <a href="/wiki/Trappists" title="Trappists">Trappists</a>. Cassant entered a <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> convent in Sainte-Marie du Désert on 5 December 1894 where he was placed under the charge of Father André Mallet who said to him: "only trust and I will help you to love <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> During his novitiate he received the name of "Marie-Joseph" and he often spent time meditating upon Christ in his Passion and on the Cross, and he depended on Him for strength during his studies. To further advance his chances of studying for the priesthood, he underwent further studies of the French language and began to learn <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>. It was around this time that he formed his personal motto, "all for Jesus, all through <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Mary</a>". He made his final vows on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Ascension" title="Feast of the Ascension">Feast of the Ascension</a> on 24 May 1900. From this point onwards he focused on becoming a priest and he viewed <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> in relation to the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> as being a critical facet of the duties of a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around this time, at the age of 21, he was summoned for mandatory military service and was discharged for reasons of health in March 1900.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He commenced his theological studies for the priesthood and suffered difficulties in doing so due to the same issue that plagued him when he was in school. The monk assigned to teach him humiliated and ridiculed him in public and said: "You are totally limited! It is useless for you to study. You will not learn any more. To ordain you would be a dishonor to the priesthood", yet Mallet assisted him with the course.<sup id="cite_ref-VC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Among his fellow seminarians he became well regarded, and some said of him: "He was always happy. It's what made the beauty of his face". Despite difficulties he was ordained as a priest on 12 October 1902. Immediately after this on 13 October, he was granted seven weeks of rest due to the advancement of <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> he suffered. Despite his time of rest, his lungs were damaged beyond the point of repair and it made his breathing ever more difficult, and his health continued to worsen when he returned to the monastery on 2 December 1902. In his illness it was Mallet who became his close aid and support. Cassant said "when I can no longer say Mass, Jesus can take me from this world", in relation to his rapidly declining health.
</p><p>Cassant became part of the Association of Victim Souls dedicated to the oblation of the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a> of Jesus Christ and signed an Act of Oblation to emulate the tenets of the organization. Cassant followed in the footsteps of those who were part of the congregation such as <a href="/wiki/Charles_de_Foucauld" title="Charles de Foucauld">Charles de Foucauld</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alfredo_Ildefonso_Schuster" title="Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster">Alfredo Ildefonso Schuster</a>, and Giuseppe Melchiorre Sarto, the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-VC_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cassant celebrated his final Mass on 31 May 1903 and received the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">last rites</a> on the following 1 June.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died of tuberculosis in the dawn of 17 June 1903 after receiving Communion during a private Mass that Mallet celebrated for him; his final words were: "Jesus, Mary, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Joseph</a>, assist me in my last agony". Since his death there have been more than 2200 people from 30 countries that have reported miracles attributed to his intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-CF_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CF-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre-Joseph_Cassant&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process opened in <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a> in 1935 and concluded before the formal introduction of the cause on 19 February 1956 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>; this process enabled him to receive the title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. Following this there was one final process to continue the work of the first process. Once that was completed the postulation compiled the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to evaluate.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> proclaimed him to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 9 June 1984 on the account of his model Christian life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and beatified him on 3 October 2004 after the approval of a miracle: the healing of a nine year old from cerebrospinal meningitis who was healed a day after praying to Cassant.
</p><p>The current postulator of the cause is Sister Augusta Tescari.
</p>
