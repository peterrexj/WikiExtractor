<p><b>Simeon Stylites</b> or <b>Symeon the Stylite</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;n 1&#93;</a></sup> (c. 390 &#8211; 2 September 459) was a Syrian <a href="/wiki/Asceticism#Christianity" title="Asceticism">Christian ascetic</a>, who achieved notability by living 37&#160;years on a small platform on top of a pillar near <a href="/wiki/Aleppo" title="Aleppo">Aleppo</a> (in modern <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>). Several other <a href="/wiki/Stylite" title="Stylite">stylites</a> later followed his model (the <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> word <i>style</i> means "pillar"). Simeon is venerated as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> Churches. He is known formally as <b>Simeon Stylites the Elder</b> to distinguish him from <a href="/wiki/Simeon_Stylites_the_Younger" title="Simeon Stylites the Younger">Simeon Stylites the Younger</a>, <a href="/wiki/Simeon_Stylites_III" title="Simeon Stylites III">Simeon Stylites III</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Symeon_Stylites_of_Lesbos" title="Symeon Stylites of Lesbos">Symeon Stylites of Lesbos</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sources">Sources</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Sources">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There exist three major early biographies of Simeon. The first of these is by <a href="/wiki/Theodoret" title="Theodoret">Theodoret</a>, bishop of <a href="/wiki/Cyrrhus" title="Cyrrhus">Cyrrhus</a>, and is found within his work <i>Religious History</i>. This biography was written during Simeon's lifetime, and Theodoret relates several events of which he claims to be an eyewitness. The narrator of a second biography names himself as <a href="/wiki/Antonius_(monk)" title="Antonius (monk)">Antonius</a>, a disciple of Simeon's. This work is of unknown date and provenance. The third is a Syriac source, which dates to 473. This is the longest of the three, and the most effusive in its praise of Simeon; it places Simeon on a par with the Old Testament prophets, and portrays him as a founder of the Christian church. The three sources exhibit signs of independent development; although they each follow the same rough outline, they have hardly any narrative episodes in common.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>All three sources have been translated into English by Robert Doran.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The Syriac life has also been translated by Frederick Lent.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is possible that traditional sources for the life of Simeon Stylites misrepresent his relation to Chalcedonian Christianity. Syriac letters in the British Museum attributed to Simeon Stylites indicate that he was a <a href="/wiki/Miaphysitism" title="Miaphysitism">Miaphysite</a> and opposed the result of the Chalcedonian council (<a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a> AD 451).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Simeon was the son of a shepherd.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He was born in <a href="/wiki/Kozan,_Adana" title="Kozan, Adana">Sis</a>, now the <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkish</a> town of Kozan in <a href="/wiki/Adana_Province" title="Adana Province">Adana Province</a>. Sis was in the Roman province of <a href="/wiki/Cilicia#Roman_Cilicia" title="Cilicia">Cilicia</a>. After the division of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a> in 395 A.D., Cilicia became part of the Eastern Roman Empire. <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> took hold quickly there.
</p><p>According to Theodoret, Simeon developed a zeal for Christianity at the age of 13, following a reading of the <a href="/wiki/Beatitudes" title="Beatitudes">Beatitudes</a>. He entered a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> before the age of 16. From the first, he gave himself up to the practice of an austerity so extreme and to all appearance so extravagant, that his brethren judged him to be unsuited to any form of community life.<sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They asked Simeon to leave the monastery.
</p><p>He shut himself up in a hut for one and a half years, where he passed the whole of <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a> without eating or drinking. When he emerged from the hut, his achievement was hailed as a miracle.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He later took to standing continually upright so long as his limbs would sustain him.
</p><p>After one and a half years in his hut, Simeon sought a rocky eminence on the slopes of what is now the Sheik Barakat Mountain, part of <a href="/wiki/Mount_Simeon" title="Mount Simeon">Mount Simeon</a>. He chose to live within a narrow space, less than 20&#160;meters in diameter. But crowds of pilgrims invaded the area to seek him out, asking his counsel or his prayers, and leaving him insufficient time for his own devotions. This eventually led him to adopt a new way of life.<sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Atop_the_pillar">Atop the pillar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Atop the pillar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg/220px-MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="287" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg/330px-MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg/440px-MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2100" data-file-height="2738" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:MHS_Szymon_Slupnik_XVI_w_Kostarowce_p.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>16th-century <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of Simeon Stylites. At the base of the pillar is his mother's body. (Historic Museum in <a href="/wiki/Sanok" title="Sanok">Sanok</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>)</div></div></div>
<p>In order to get away from the ever-increasing number of people who came to him for prayers and advice, leaving him little if any time for his private austerities, Simeon discovered a pillar which had survived among ruins in nearby <a href="/wiki/Taladah" title="Taladah">Telanissa</a> (modern-day Taladah in <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> and formed a small platform at the top. He determined to live out his life on this platform. For sustenance small boys from the nearby village would climb up the pillar and pass him parcels of flat bread and goats' milk. He may also have pulled up food in buckets via a pulley.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2016)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>When the monastic <a href="/wiki/Elder_(Christianity)" title="Elder (Christianity)">Elders</a> living in the desert heard about Simeon, who had chosen a new and strange form of asceticism, they wanted to test him to determine whether his extreme feats were founded in humility or pride. They decided to order Simeon under obedience to come down from the pillar. They decided that if he disobeyed, they would forcibly drag him to the ground, but if he was willing to submit, they were to leave him on his pillar. Simeon displayed complete obedience and humility, and the monks told him to stay where he was.
</p><p>The first pillar that Simeon occupied was little more than 3 meters (10&#160;ft). He later moved his platform to others, the last in the series reportedly more than 15 meters (50&#160;ft) from the ground.<sup id="cite_ref-Thurston_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Thurston-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the top of the pillar was a platform, which is believed to have been about one square meter and surrounded by a <a href="/wiki/Baluster" title="Baluster">baluster</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Edward_Gibbon" title="Edward Gibbon">Edward Gibbon</a> in his <i><a href="/wiki/The_History_of_the_Decline_and_Fall_of_the_Roman_Empire" title="The History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire">History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire</a></i> describes Simeon's life as follows:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>In this last and lofty station, the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Syria" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Syria">Syrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Anachoret" class="mw-redirect" title="Anachoret">Anachoret</a> resisted the heat of thirty summers, and the cold of as many winters. Habit and exercise instructed him to maintain his dangerous situation without fear or giddiness, and successively to assume the different postures of devotion. He sometimes prayed in an erect attitude, with his outstretched arms in the figure of a cross, but his most familiar practice was that of bending his meagre skeleton from the forehead to the feet; and a curious spectator, after numbering twelve hundred and forty-four repetitions, at length desisted from the endless account. The progress of an ulcer in his thigh might shorten, but it could not disturb, this <i>celestial</i> life; and the patient Hermit expired, without descending from his column.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Even on the highest of his columns, Simeon was not withdrawn from the world. If anything, the new pillar attracted even more people, both pilgrims who had earlier visited him and sightseers as well. Simeon was available each afternoon to talk with visitors. By means of a ladder, visitors were able to ascend within speaking distance. It is known that he wrote letters, the text of some of which have survived to this day, that he instructed disciples, and that he also lectured to those assembled beneath.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He especially preached against profanity and <a href="/wiki/Usury" title="Usury">usury</a>. In contrast to the extreme austerity that he practised, his preaching conveyed temperance and compassion, and was marked with common sense and freedom from fanaticism.
</p><p>Much of Simeon's public ministry, like that of other Syrian ascetics, can be seen as socially cohesive in the context of the Roman East. In the face of the withdrawal of wealthy landowners to the large cities, holy men such as Simeon acted as impartial and necessary patrons and arbiters in disputes between peasant farmers and within the smaller towns.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Fame_and_final_years">Fame and final years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Fame and final years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg/220px-Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg/330px-Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg/440px-Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2272" data-file-height="1704" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Column_of_Simeon_Syria.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Ruins of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Simeon" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Saint Simeon">Church of Saint Simeon</a> with remains of his column (centre, now topped with a boulder)</div></div></div>
<p>Reports of Simeon reached the church hierarchy and the imperial court. The Emperor <a href="/wiki/Theodosius_II" title="Theodosius II">Theodosius II</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Aelia_Eudocia" title="Aelia Eudocia">Aelia Eudocia</a> greatly respected Simeon and listened to his counsels, while the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Leo_I_of_the_Byzantine_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo I of the Byzantine Empire">Leo I</a> paid respectful attention to a letter he sent in favour of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a>. Simeon is also said to have corresponded with <a href="/wiki/Genevieve" title="Genevieve">Genevieve</a> of Paris.
</p><p>Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Domnus_II_of_Antioch" title="Domnus II of Antioch">Domninos II</a> (441–448) of Antioch visited the monk, and celebrated the Divine Liturgy on the pillar.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Once when Simeon was ill, Theodosius sent three bishops to beg him to come down and allow himself to be attended by physicians, but Simeon preferred to leave his cure in the hands of God, and before long he recovered.
</p><p>A double wall was raised around him to keep the crowd of people from coming too close and disturbing his prayerful concentration. Women were, in general, not permitted beyond the wall, not even his own mother, who he reportedly told, "If we are worthy, we shall see one another in the life to come." She submitted to this, remaining in the area, and embraced the monastic life of silence and prayer. When she died, Simeon asked that her coffin be brought to him, and he reverently bade farewell to his dead mother.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Accounts differ with regard to how long Simeon lived upon the pillar, with estimates ranging from 35 to 42 years.<sup id="cite_ref-mann_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mann-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He died on 2 September 459. A disciple found his body stooped over in prayer. The Patriarch of Antioch, <a href="/wiki/Martyrius_of_Antioch" title="Martyrius of Antioch">Martyrius</a>, performed the funeral of the monk before a huge crowd. He was buried not far from the pillar.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred,_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred%2C_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg/220px-W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred%2C_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="313" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred%2C_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg/330px-W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred%2C_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred%2C_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg/440px-W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred%2C_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4840" data-file-height="6888" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:W.E.F._Britten_-_Alfred,_Lord_Tennyson_-_St._Simeon_Stylites.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A 1901 illustration by <a href="/wiki/W._E._F._Britten" class="mw-redirect" title="W. E. F. Britten">W. E. F. Britten</a> for <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Tennyson" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfred Tennyson">Alfred Tennyson</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/St._Simeon_Stylites_(poem)" title="St. Simeon Stylites (poem)">St. Simeon Stylites</a></i>.</div></div></div>
<p>Simeon inspired many imitators. For the next century, ascetics living on pillars, <a href="/wiki/Stylite" title="Stylite">stylites</a>, were a common sight throughout the Christian <a href="/wiki/Levant" title="Levant">Levant</a>.
</p><p>He is commemorated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church" title="Coptic Orthodox Church">Coptic Orthodox Church</a>, where his feast is on <a href="/wiki/Pashons_29_(Coptic_Orthodox_liturgics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Pashons 29 (Coptic Orthodox liturgics)">29 Pashons</a>. He is commemorated 1 September by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Catholic">Eastern Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_Church" title="Christian Church">Churches</a>, and 5 January in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p><p>A contest arose between <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> and <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> for the possession of Simeon's remains. The preference was given to Antioch, and the greater part of his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were left there as a protection to the unwalled city.
</p><p>The ruins of the vast edifice erected in his honour and known in <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a> as the <i><a href="/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Simeon" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of Saint Simeon">Qalaat Semaan</a></i> ("the Fortress of Simeon") can still be seen. They are located about 30&#160;km northwest of <a href="/wiki/Aleppo" title="Aleppo">Aleppo</a> (<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1073938472">.mw-parser-output .geo-default,.mw-parser-output .geo-dms,.mw-parser-output .geo-dec{display:inline}.mw-parser-output .geo-nondefault,.mw-parser-output .geo-multi-punct{display:none}.mw-parser-output .longitude,.mw-parser-output .latitude{white-space:nowrap}</style><span class="plainlinks nourlexpansion"><a class="external text" href="//geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Simeon_Stylites&amp;params=36_20_03_N_36_50_38_E_type:landmark"><span class="geo-default"><span class="geo-dms" title="Maps, aerial photos, and other data for this location"><span class="latitude">36°20′03″N</span> <span class="longitude">36°50′38″E</span></span></span><span class="geo-multi-punct">&#xfeff; / &#xfeff;</span><span class="geo-nondefault"><span class="geo-dec" title="Maps, aerial photos, and other data for this location">36.33417°N 36.84389°E</span><span style="display:none">&#xfeff; / <span class="geo">36.33417; 36.84389</span></span></span></a></span><span style="font-size: small;"><span id="coordinates"><a href="/wiki/Geographic_coordinate_system" title="Geographic coordinate system">Coordinates</a>: <link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1073938472"/><span class="plainlinks nourlexpansion"><a class="external text" href="//geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Simeon_Stylites&amp;params=36_20_03_N_36_50_38_E_type:landmark"><span class="geo-default"><span class="geo-dms" title="Maps, aerial photos, and other data for this location"><span class="latitude">36°20′03″N</span> <span class="longitude">36°50′38″E</span></span></span><span class="geo-multi-punct">&#xfeff; / &#xfeff;</span><span class="geo-nondefault"><span class="geo-dec" title="Maps, aerial photos, and other data for this location">36.33417°N 36.84389°E</span><span style="display:none">&#xfeff; / <span class="geo">36.33417; 36.84389</span></span></span></a></span></span></span>) and consist of four <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilicas</a> built out from an octagonal court towards the four points of the compass to form a large cross. In the centre of the court stands the base of the <i>style</i> or column on which Simeon stood. On 12 May 2016, the pillar within the church reportedly took a hit from a missile, fired from what appeared to be Russian jets backing the Syrian government.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_references">Cultural references</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Simeon_Stylites&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Cultural references">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The life of Simeon Stylites inspired an 1842 poem by <a href="/wiki/Alfred_Tennyson" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfred Tennyson">Alfred Tennyson</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._Simeon_Stylites_(poem)" title="St. Simeon Stylites (poem)">"St. Simeon Stylites"</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Modern works based upon the life of the saint include <a href="/wiki/Luis_Bu%C3%B1uel" title="Luis Buñuel">Luis Buñuel</a>'s 1965 film <i><a href="/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_del_desierto" class="mw-redirect" title="Simón del desierto">Simón del desierto</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Hans_Zender" title="Hans Zender">Hans Zender</a>'s 1986 opera <i><a href="/wiki/Stephen_Climax" title="Stephen Climax">Stephen Climax</a></i>, and the 1998 film <i><a href="/wiki/At_Sachem_Farm" title="At Sachem Farm">At Sachem Farm</a></i>.
</p>
