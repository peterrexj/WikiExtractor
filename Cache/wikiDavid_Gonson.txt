<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>

<p><b>David Gonson</b> (1510 – 12 July 1541), also known as David Gunston, was a Knight of Malta and Catholic martyr of the English Reformation.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=David_Gonson&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sir David Gonson was born in Deptford, Kent, the son of <a href="/wiki/William_Gonson" title="William Gonson">William Gonson</a> and his wife Bennett Walter.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His was a naval family, with his father serving as a Vice-Admiral and well connected with the nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father had been born in Melton Mowbray and his uncle, Bartholomew, served as a priest there, during which time he erected the Gonson Memorial in the church paying tribute to his parents.
David Gonson was received into the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Malta" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Malta">Order of Malta</a> at the English Auberge in <a href="/wiki/Valletta" title="Valletta">Valletta</a> on 20 October 1533.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He served on board ships of the Order in the Mediterranean until 1540 when he returned to England.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> As part of the actions taken during the Reformation, the Order was suppressed in England by King Henry VIII on 10 May 1540. Gonson refused to recognise the authority of the king in spiritual matters. The writ against him claimed that in Malta "He denied that King Henry was supreme head of the Church of England and that he called the King a heretic" with the accusation being laid by a fellow knight, Sir Philip Babington.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> For this he was imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> and subsequently at <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea Prison</a>.
</p><p>In 1541 he was put on trial and convicted of treason for his denial of the king's authority in spiritual matters as laid down in law by the Act passed by Parliament in the preceding year that had dissolved the Order in England claiming the members 'sustained and maintained the usurped power and authority of the Bishop of Rome...untruly upholding, knowledging and affirming maliciously and traitorously the same Bishop to be the Supreme Head of Christ's Church'.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was executed by being hanged, drawn and quartered at <a href="/wiki/Old_Kent_Road" title="Old Kent Road">St Thomas' Waterings</a> in Southwark on 12 July 1541. This site was commonly used in the 1500s for the execution of those who were religious dissenters or found guilty of treason.
</p><p>He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> in Rome on 15 December 1929 as one of the <a href="/wiki/One_Hundred_and_Seven_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="One Hundred and Seven Martyrs of England and Wales">One Hundred and Seven Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p>
