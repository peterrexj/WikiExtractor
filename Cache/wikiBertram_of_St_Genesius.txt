<p><b>Bertrand</b> (or <b>Bertram</b>) <b>of Saint-Geniès</b> (1258 – 6 June 1350) was the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Aquileia" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Aquileia">patriarch of Aquileia</a> from 1334 until his death.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bertrand_of_Saint-Geni%C3%A8s&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bertram was born in <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>, and became a jurist in the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Toulouse" title="University of Toulouse">University of Toulouse</a> and papal chaplain. On 4 June 1334 he was appointed as patriarch of Aquileia, which was not only a simple diocese but a powerful state occupying what is now <a href="/wiki/Friuli" title="Friuli">Friuli</a>.
</p><p>Bertram proved a skilled diplomat and military leader. He allied with the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Republic of Venice</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Austria" title="Duchy of Austria">Duchy of Austria</a>. In 1335 he defeated <a href="/w/index.php?title=Rizzardo_da_Camino&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Rizzardo da Camino (page does not exist)">Rizzardo da Camino</a>, who had invaded the patriarchate during the vacancy period; by this victory he was able to recover also the <a href="/wiki/Cadore" title="Cadore">Cadore</a> (1347). Later he sided with <a href="/wiki/Louis_I_of_Hungary" title="Louis I of Hungary">Louis I of Hungary</a> in his expedition to <a href="/wiki/Zadar" title="Zadar">Zadar</a> (May–June 1346), which had been caught by the Venetians. Later, allied with the <a href="/wiki/Counts_of_Gorizia" class="mw-redirect" title="Counts of Gorizia">counts of Gorizia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Albert_III,_Count_of_Gorizia" title="Albert III, Count of Gorizia">Albert IV of Istria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Albert_II,_Duke_of_Austria" title="Albert II, Duke of Austria">Albert II of Austria</a> and the Croats, he  invaded <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a> (1348). <a href="/wiki/Koper" title="Koper">Koper</a> rebelled to the Venetians, but the latter intervened and forced Bertram to a peace which brought him no gains. Subsequently, Bertram attacked the count of Gorizia, personally leading an army to reconquer <a href="/wiki/Venzone" title="Venzone">Venzone</a> (1336), <a href="/wiki/Braulins" class="mw-redirect" title="Braulins">Braulins</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cormons" title="Cormons">Cormons</a>. The Gorizians reacted and invaded the patriarchal lands, but Bertram besieged <a href="/wiki/Gorizia" title="Gorizia">Gorizia</a> and forced the count to a definitive truce in 1341.  
</p><p>Once restored the foreign situation, he devoted himself to suppress the power and the autonomies gained by the Friulian feudal lords. He also strengthened the Alpine passes, building a castle (<i>Rocca Bertranda</i>) between <a href="/wiki/Moggio_Udinese" title="Moggio Udinese">Moggio Udinese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pontebba" title="Pontebba">Pontebba</a>, and had a new line of walls built in <a href="/wiki/Sacile" title="Sacile">Sacile</a> (1347). From the religious point of view, he held two councils in Udine (1335) and <a href="/wiki/Aquileia" title="Aquileia">Aquileia</a> (1339), followed by four synods in which he reformed the Patriarchate's religious life and established norms against the heresies which had spread in its lands.
</p><p>Bertram was also a patron of the arts: he established the university of Cividale in 1344 and restored the <a href="/wiki/Udine_Cathedral" title="Udine Cathedral">Udine Cathedral</a>. He also introduced new coins.
</p><p>The Friulian nobles, discontented by Bertram's policies, formed an alliance against him at <a href="/wiki/Spilimbergo" title="Spilimbergo">Spilimbergo</a> in 1348, and conquered several villages; Udine, which had been deprived of water, was forced to accept a truce while a papal legate attempted to find a diplomatic solution. Once any possibility of a military victory waned, the rebels set a plot against the aged patriarch, who was assassinated  by Enrico da Spilimbergo at <a href="/wiki/San_Giorgio_della_Richinvelda" title="San Giorgio della Richinvelda">San Giorgio della Richinvelda</a> on 6 June 1350.
