<p><b>Bregowine</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (died August 764) was a medieval <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>. Little is known of his origins or his activities as archbishop, although a number of stories were told about his possible origins after the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman conquest</a> in 1066. There are no records of him prior to his becoming archbishop. He possibly owed his elevation to the <a href="/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Kent" title="List of monarchs of Kent">Kentish monarch</a>. The records after his elevation to Canterbury are mainly about disputes over land, but knowledge of his time in office is hampered by the destruction of many of the contemporary records. After his death, he was <a href="/wiki/Pre-congregation" class="mw-redirect" title="Pre-congregation">considered</a> a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">life</a> about him was written in the 12th&#160;century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bregowine&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Various stories have been told about Bregowine's origins, including that he was a nobleman and a continental Saxon who converted to Christianity and came to <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> because of the saintly reputation of <a href="/wiki/Theodore_of_Tarsus" title="Theodore of Tarsus">Theodore of Tarsus</a>. Others say that he owed his elevation to King <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelbert_II_of_Kent" title="Æthelbert II of Kent">Æthelbert II of Kent</a>, but all these stories rest on works that were written after the Norman conquest of England in 1066. There are no contemporary records of Bregowine before he was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a>. It does not appear, however, that he was of Mercian origins like his predecessors <a href="/wiki/Tatwin" class="mw-redirect" title="Tatwin">Tatwin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Nothhelm" title="Nothhelm">Nothhelm</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Whatever his upbringing, Bregowine was consecrated as archbishop on 27&#160;September&#160;761.<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His election took place in a brief period when <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Kent" title="Kingdom of Kent">Kent</a> was free of <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mercia" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Mercia">Mercian</a> dominance between 756 and 764, so the story that he owed his election to Æthelbert does fit with the time frame.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks80_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks80-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He wrote letters to Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Lullus" title="Lullus">Lul</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a> which still exist, and which discuss an earlier meeting between the two men. Other activities as archbishop are recorded in surviving <a href="/wiki/Charter" title="Charter">charters</a>. One records that he protested at the loss of a church at <a href="/wiki/Cookham" title="Cookham">Cookham</a> that was confiscated by King <a href="/wiki/Cynewulf_of_Wessex" title="Cynewulf of Wessex">Cynewulf of Wessex</a> sometime after 760. Another surviving charter from Dunwald, a <a href="/wiki/Thegn" title="Thegn">thegn</a> of King Æthelbert, concerning land in Canterbury, records that Bregowine consented to the gift of land. Unfortunately, many of the early charters of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Canterbury" title="Diocese of Canterbury">diocese of Canterbury</a> are lost, which restricts knowledge of Bregowine's activities as archbishop.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bregowine died in 764<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook214_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook214-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and was originally buried in the baptistry in Canterbury, but his remains were moved to the <a href="/wiki/Choir_(architecture)" title="Choir (architecture)">choir</a> of <a href="/wiki/Canterbury_Cathedral" title="Canterbury Cathedral">Canterbury Cathedral</a> in 1123. This followed an attempt in around 1121 to remove his remains to another monastery, which came to nothing.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The remains were placed by the altar of St Gregory in the south <a href="/wiki/Transept" title="Transept">transept</a>, after having been briefly placed in the north transept.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS75_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS75-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bregowine was later <a href="/wiki/Pre-congregation" class="mw-redirect" title="Pre-congregation">considered</a> a saint, with a feast day of 26&#160;August, although <a href="/wiki/Florence_of_Worcester" title="Florence of Worcester">Florence of Worcester</a>, a 12th-century writer, recorded his death date as 24&#160;August.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS75_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS75-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Other sources record the death date as 25&#160;August. His life was later written by <a href="/wiki/Eadmer" title="Eadmer">Eadmer</a> in the 12th&#160;century.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
