					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">French ecclesiastic, hagiographer and theologian</div><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the bishop and theologian. For the medieval Moravian artist, see <a href="/wiki/Hildebert_and_Everwin" title="Hildebert and Everwin">Hildebert and Everwin</a>.</div>

<p><b>Hildebert of Lavardin</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (c. 1055&#160;&#8211;&#32;18 December 1133) was a French ecclesiastic, hagiographer and theologian. From 1096–97 he was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Mans">bishop of Le Mans</a>, then from 1125 until his death <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Tours">archbishop of Tours</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hildebert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hildebert was born of poor parents at <a href="/wiki/Lavardin,_Loir-et-Cher" title="Lavardin, Loir-et-Cher">Lavardin</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Vend%C3%B4me" title="Vendôme">Vendôme</a>, and was intended for the church. He was probably a pupil of <a href="/wiki/Berengar_of_Tours" title="Berengar of Tours">Berengar of Tours</a>, and became master (<i>scholasticus</i>) of the school at Le Mans; in 1091 he was made <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">archdeacon</a> and in 1096 or 1097 <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Le_Mans" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Le Mans">bishop of Le Mans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He had to face the hostility of a section of his clergy and also of the <a href="/wiki/William_II_of_England" title="William II of England">English king, William II</a>, who captured Le Mans and carried the bishop with him to England for about a year.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hildebert then (in 1100 or 1103)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> travelled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and sought permission to resign his bishopric, which <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a> refused. In 1116 his diocese was thrown into great confusion owing to the preaching of <a href="/wiki/Henry_of_Lausanne" title="Henry of Lausanne">Henry of Lausanne</a>, who was denouncing the higher clergy, especially the bishop. Hildebert compelled him to leave the neighborhood of <a href="/wiki/Le_Mans" title="Le Mans">Le Mans</a>, but the effects of his preaching remained.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1125 Hildebert was <a href="/wiki/Translation_(ecclesiastical)" title="Translation (ecclesiastical)">translated</a> unwillingly to the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Tours">archbishopric of Tours</a>, where he came into conflict with the <a href="/wiki/Louis_VI_of_France" title="Louis VI of France">French king Louis VI</a> about the rights of ecclesiastical patronage, and with the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Dol" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Dol">bishop of Dol</a> about the authority of his see in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>. He presided over the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Synod_of_Nantes&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Synod of Nantes (page does not exist)">Synod of Nantes</a>, and died at Tours probably on December 18, 1133. Hildebert built part of the cathedral at Le Mans. Some writers have referred to him with the title of saint, but there appears to be no authority for this. He was not a man of very strict life; his contemporaries, however, had a very high opinion of him and he was called <i>egregius versificator</i> by <a href="/wiki/Orderic_Vitalis" title="Orderic Vitalis">Orderic Vitalis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hildebert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The extant writings of Hildebert consist of letters, poems, a few sermons, two lives and one or two treatises. An edition of his works prepared by the <a href="/wiki/Maurist" class="mw-redirect" title="Maurist">Maurist</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Antoine_Beaugendre&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Antoine Beaugendre (page does not exist)">Antoine Beaugendre</a>, and entitled <i>Venerabilis Hildeberti, prima Cenomanensis episcopi, deinde Turonensis archiepiscopi, opera tam edita quam inedita</i>, was published in Paris in 1708 and was reprinted with additions by J-J Bourassé in 1854. These editions, however, are faulty. They credit Hildebert with numerous writings by others and omit some genuine writings. Revelation of this fact has affected Hildebert's position in the history of medieval thought.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His former standing as a philosopher rested on his supposed authorship of the important <i>Tractatus theologicus</i>&#8212;but this is now regarded as the work of <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_St_Victor" class="mw-redirect" title="Hugh of St Victor">Hugh of St Victor</a>. Consequently, Hildebert is no longer thought of as a philosopher. His genuine writings include many letters. These Epistolae enjoyed great popularity in the 12th and 13th centuries, and were frequently used as classics in the schools of France and Italy. They include two letters concerning the struggle between the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry V">emperor Henry V</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a>, which were edited by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ernst_Sackur&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ernst Sackur (page does not exist)">Ernst Sackur</a> and printed in the <i><a href="/wiki/Monumenta_Germaniae_historica" class="mw-redirect" title="Monumenta Germaniae historica">Monumenta Germaniae historica</a>. Libelli de lite ii.</i> (1893),<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> but whose attribution to Hildebert is very doubtful.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His poems on various subjects were also very popular. Hildebert attained celebrity also as a preacher both in <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> and <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, but only a few of his genuine <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a> exist, most of the 144 his editors attributed to him being the work of <a href="/wiki/Peter_Lombard" title="Peter Lombard">Peter Lombard</a> and others.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Vitae</i> Hildebert wrote are the lives of <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Cluny" title="Hugh of Cluny">Hugh of Cluny</a> and of <a href="/wiki/St_Radegunda" class="mw-redirect" title="St Radegunda">St Radegunda</a>. His <i>liber de Querimonia et Conflictu carnis et Spiritus seu animae</i> is also undoubtedly his. Hildebert was an excellent Latin scholar, being acquainted with <a href="/wiki/Cicero" title="Cicero">Cicero</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ovid" title="Ovid">Ovid</a> and other authors, and his spirit is rather that of a <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> than of a Christian writer.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He sent letters and poetry to <a href="/wiki/Adela_of_Normandy" title="Adela of Normandy">Adela of Normandy</a> advising her on clemency, and praised her regency of <a href="/wiki/Blois" title="Blois">Blois</a>.
</p>
