<p><b>Saint Gal of Clermont</b> (also <b>Gall</b><sup id="cite_ref-orthodox_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthodox-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) (c. 489 – 554) was the sixteenth <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Clermont" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Clermont">Bishop of Clermont</a>, holding that <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> from 527 to 551. He shares a name with a later bishop of the diocese, who, though less illustrious than the first Gal, is also revered as a saint. Gal played an important role in the politics of the Church, as the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Clermont_(535)" title="Council of Clermont (535)">Council of Clermont (535)</a> was hosted under his episcopate. He was the uncle and teacher of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gal_I_(Bishop_of_Clermont)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gal was the scion of a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Senate" title="Roman Senate">senatorial</a> family, born in <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a>, <a href="/wiki/Auvergne_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Auvergne (province)">Auvergne</a> circa 489. His mother was descended from the family of Vettius Apagatus, a revered martyr from <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> While his parents proposed to have him married to a daughter of a respectable senator, Gal had other plans, and privately withdrew to a monastery at <a href="/wiki/Cournon" title="Cournon">Cournon</a>. Once he received the consent of his parents, he joyfully embraced a life of religious poverty. Gal's intelligence and piety caused his recommendation as councilor to Quintianus, the bishop of Clermont, who ordained him a priest.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Theuderic_I" title="Theuderic I">Theuderic I</a>, the <a href="/wiki/List_of_Frankish_kings#Merovingian_kings_of_the_Franks" title="List of Frankish kings">king of Austrasia</a>, invaded Auvergne and took Gal prisoner, attaching him to the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> in the palace. Gal regained his liberty after a few years and returned to Clermont.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Quintianus died in 527, Gal was chosen as the successor to the Bishopric of Clermont. During his tenure as bishop, he embellished the tomb of <a href="/wiki/Amabilis_of_Riom" title="Amabilis of Riom">Saint Amabilis</a> at the collegiate church in <a href="/wiki/Riom" title="Riom">Riom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gal was distinguished for his gentleness and charity.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was during this time that Gal's extraordinary equanimity was most tested: one story reports that the bishop was "struck on the head by a brutal man, [yet] he discovered not the least emotion of anger or resentment, and by this meekness disarmed the savage of his rage."<sup id="cite_ref-lives_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> A similar anecdote involves a man named Evodius, a priest who had once been a senator. Although the proud man acted insultingly to Gal, the bishop's reaction was simply to rise from his seat and make a visit to the churches of the city. Touched by Gal's patience, Evodius cast himself at the feet of the bishop in the middle of the street.<sup id="cite_ref-lives_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lives-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gal played a major political and religious role as Bishop of Clermont. He became known as a defender of the rights of the Church against Sivigald, the governor appointed by Theuderic. The chief event of his episcopate was the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Clermont_(535)" title="Council of Clermont (535)">Council of Clermont</a> in 535. He also took part in the Fourth (541) and <a href="/wiki/Fifth_Council_of_Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Fifth Council of Orléans">Fifth</a> (549) Councils of Orléans.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gal died in the year 553. His feast day in the Roman Catholic Church is 3 July;<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in the Eastern Orthodox Church it is 1 July.<sup id="cite_ref-orthodox_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-orthodox-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
