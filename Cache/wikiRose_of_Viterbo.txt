<p><b>Rose of Viterbo</b>, <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">T.O.S.F.</a> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Rosa da Viterbo</i>; c. 1233 – 6 March 1251), was a young woman born in <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>, then a contested <a href="/wiki/Comune" title="Comune">commune</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>. She spent her brief life as a <a href="/wiki/Recluse" title="Recluse">recluse</a>, who was outspoken in her support of the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a>. Otherwise leading an unremarkable life, she later became known for her <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystical</a> gifts of prophecy and having miraculous powers. She is honoured as a saint by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rose_of_Viterbo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The chronology of her life remains uncertain, as the acts of her canonization, the chief historical sources, record no dates.<sup id="cite_ref-cleary_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cleary-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Most scholars agree she was probably born around the year 1233.<sup id="cite_ref-univ_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-univ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Born of poor and pious parents, even as a child Rose had a great desire to pray and to aid the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She prayed much for the conversion of sinners.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Rose was not yet 10 years old when the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> is said to have instructed her to take the habit of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a> and to preach <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> in Viterbo, at that time under the rule of <a href="/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cleary_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cleary-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Hombourg_Rose.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Hombourg_Rose.jpg/220px-Hombourg_Rose.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="418" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Hombourg_Rose.jpg/330px-Hombourg_Rose.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Hombourg_Rose.jpg/440px-Hombourg_Rose.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1000" data-file-height="1900" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Hombourg_Rose.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Rose</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo,_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo%2C_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg/220px-Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo%2C_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="251" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo%2C_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg/330px-Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo%2C_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo%2C_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg/440px-Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo%2C_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2362" data-file-height="2692" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Comuni%C3%B3n_de_Santa_Rosa_de_Viterbo,_por_Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Communion of Saint Rose of Viterbo</i>, by <a href="/wiki/Juan_Antonio_de_Fr%C3%ADas_y_Escalante" title="Juan Antonio de Frías y Escalante">Juan Antonio de Frías y Escalante</a></div></div></div>
<p>She lived a life of prayer, penance and solitude in her father's home.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Every now and then she would emerge from her solitude to entreat the people to do penance.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her mission seems to have lasted for about two years. In January 1250, Viterbo, her native city, was then in revolt against the pope. When she was 12, she began preaching in the streets against Frederick's occupation of Viterbo. She and her family were exiled from the city and took refuge in <a href="/wiki/Soriano_nel_Cimino" title="Soriano nel Cimino">Soriano nel Cimino</a>. When the pope's side won in Viterbo, Rose was allowed to return.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On December 5, 1250, Rose allegedly foretold the speedy death of the emperor, a prophecy fulfilled on December 13. Soon afterwards she went to <a href="/wiki/Vitorchiano" title="Vitorchiano">Vitorchiano</a>, whose inhabitants, according to surviving reports, were affected by a supposed <a href="/wiki/Witchcraft" title="Witchcraft">sorceress</a>. Rose secured the conversion of all, even of the sorceress, reportedly by standing unscathed for three hours in the flames of a burning <a href="/wiki/Pyre" title="Pyre">pyre</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cleary_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cleary-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rose wished to enter the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clare" class="mw-redirect" title="Poor Clare">Poor Clare</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a> of St. Mary in the city, but was refused because of her poverty, as she was not able to provide the <a href="/wiki/Dowry" title="Dowry">dowry</a> required for admission. She accepted her rejection, nonetheless foretelling her admission to the monastery after her death.
</p><p>Rose died on March 6, 1251, in her father's home.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It was long believed that Rose had died of <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>. In 2010, however, researchers examining her remains concluded that she had died of complications due to <a href="/wiki/Pentalogy_of_Cantrell" title="Pentalogy of Cantrell">Pentalogy of Cantrell</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rose_of_Viterbo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:La_Macchina.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/La_Macchina.JPG/220px-La_Macchina.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/La_Macchina.JPG/330px-La_Macchina.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/La_Macchina.JPG/440px-La_Macchina.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2604" data-file-height="3472" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:La_Macchina.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>La Macchina of Saint Rose</div></div></div>
<p>The process of Rose's <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> was opened in the year of her death by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a>, but was not definitively undertaken until 1457. Originally buried at the <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish church</a> of Santa Maria in Poggio, in 1257 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_IV" title="Pope Alexander IV">Pope Alexander IV</a> ordered it moved to the monastery she had desired to enter, at which time it was renamed in her honor.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the liturgical calendar of the Roman Catholic Church was reformed after the <a href="/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council" title="Second Vatican Council">Second Vatican Council</a>, her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> was transferred to the date of her death. September 4 is the date of the translation of her relics to the Monastery of St. Damian. It is this latter date on which her feast is celebrated in Viterbo and by the Franciscans.
</p><p>On September 3, the eve of the feast of St. Rose, the people of Viterbo follow the transportation of <a href="/wiki/Macchina_di_Santa_Rosa" title="Macchina di Santa Rosa"><i>La Macchina</i></a> ("the Machine of St. Rose") a massive 28-metre-high (92&#160;ft) tower, illuminated with 3,000 tiny electric lights and 880 candles, and topped off with a statue of her, which is carried for 1,200 metres through the darkened streets of the old medieval town on the backs of around 100 volunteers called "facchini". The tradition goes all the way back to September 4, 1258, when the body of the saint was exhumed and transported to the Monastery of Saint Damian; but it was not until 1664, following seven years of plague in the city, that a "machine" first appeared. In gratitude for having survived such a terrible pestilence the citizens voted to renew the veneration of their saint every year.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/St._Rose_of_Viterbo_Convent" title="St. Rose of Viterbo Convent">St. Rose of Viterbo Convent</a>, named for her, in <a href="/wiki/La_Crosse,_Wisconsin" title="La Crosse, Wisconsin">La Crosse, Wisconsin</a>, is the <a href="/wiki/Motherhouse" title="Motherhouse">motherhouse</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Sisters_of_Perpetual_Adoration" title="Franciscan Sisters of Perpetual Adoration">Franciscan Sisters of Perpetual Adoration</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The convent is listed on the <a href="/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places" title="National Register of Historic Places">National Register of Historic Places</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Viterbo_University" title="Viterbo University">Viterbo University</a> is a Catholic, Franciscan university in the liberal arts tradition, founded by these Franciscan Sisters, also located in La Crosse, Wisconsin.<sup id="cite_ref-univ_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-univ-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Rose of Viterbo is one of the religious figures featured in the award-winning series of saint plays by <a href="/wiki/Erik_Ehn" title="Erik Ehn">Erik Ehn</a>. The play celebrating her life premiered in October 2008 at <a href="/wiki/Goshen_College" title="Goshen College">Goshen College</a>.
</p>
