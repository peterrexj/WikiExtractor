<p><b>Saint Aemilian</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span><span title="/iː/: &#39;ee&#39; in &#39;fleece&#39;">iː</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span></span>/</a></span></span>; (in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> <i>Emilianus</i> or <i>Aemilianus</i>) (12 November 472 &#8211; 11 June 573) is an <a href="/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula" title="Iberian Peninsula">Iberic</a> saint, widely revered throughout <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, who lived during the age of <a href="/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom" title="Visigothic Kingdom">Visigothic rule</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aemilian_of_Cogolla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to his <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a></i>, written by <a href="/wiki/Braulio_of_Zaragoza" title="Braulio of Zaragoza">Braulio</a>, the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Caesaraugusta" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesaraugusta">Caesaraugusta</a> (modern Zaragoza) roughly a hundred years after the saint's death,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Aemilian was born in Vergaja,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which is identified with <a href="/wiki/Berceo" title="Berceo">Berceo</a> in <a href="/wiki/La_Rioja_(autonomous_community)" class="mw-redirect" title="La Rioja (autonomous community)">La Rioja</a>, where he was a shepherd.
</p><p>Aemilian had a religious experience, perhaps around the age of twenty, which led him to decide to dedicate himself to God's service. He sought out an experienced hermit in <a href="/wiki/Bilibio" title="Bilibio">Bilibio</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/San_Felices" title="San Felices">Felix</a> (more usually known by the Spanish form of his common name, <i>San Felices</i>), where Aemilian lived for a number of years.
</p><p>After leaving his teacher, Aemilian lived as a hermit (perhaps even a <a href="/wiki/Gyrovagus" class="mw-redirect" title="Gyrovagus">gyrovagus</a>) in the mountains or on the historic Roman road which became the <a href="/wiki/Camino_de_Santiago" title="Camino de Santiago">Camino de Santiago</a>. Didymus the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Tarazona" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Tarazona">Bishop of Tarazona</a> ordained Aemilian and appointed him parish priest of  Vergaja.  However, Aemilian aroused the opposition of his fellow priests because of his heavy distribution of alms or reputation for holiness or miracle working. Aemilian returned to the wilderness, and a small community of disciples gathered around his cell.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He died at a venerable age, and his body, was initially interred at his hermitage, but later transferred to a monastery built in memory of him.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Aemilian_of_Cogolla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Braulio_of_Zaragoza" title="Braulio of Zaragoza">Braulio</a> also recorded miracles that occurred after death of Aemilian, but the fame of the  <a href="/wiki/San_Mill%C3%A1n_de_la_Cogolla" title="San Millán de la Cogolla">San Millán de la Cogolla</a> monastery dedicated in his memory (and which held his tomb) eclipsed that of its founder. San Millán de Suso monastery is known for its <a href="/wiki/Mozarabic" class="mw-redirect" title="Mozarabic">Mozarabic</a> architecture, and its growing popularity during the heyday of the pilgrimage rout led to its expansion in the eleventh century with San Millán de Yuso (the communities however having separate abbots until the 12th century). 
</p><p>The longstanding monastic community (<a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> by the mid-medieval era) is credited with one of the oldest books written in the Spanish language, the <i>Vida de San Millán de la Cogolla</i> (biography by <a href="/wiki/Gonzalo_de_Berceo" title="Gonzalo de Berceo">Gonzalo de Berceo</a>). St. <a href="/wiki/Dominic_of_Silos" title="Dominic of Silos">Dominic of Silos</a> (the miracleworker for whom <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Saint Dominic</a> was named) also received his education and began his religious career at San Millan monastery. 
</p><p>He is a patron saint of La Rioja; the longer name of <i>San Millán de la Cogolla</i> refers to the monastic <a href="/wiki/Cowl" title="Cowl">cowl</a>. Because of the monastery's role on the traditional pilgrimage route, representations of Aemilian can be mixed with that of <a href="/wiki/James_the_Greater" class="mw-redirect" title="James the Greater">Saint James the Moor-slayer</a>, such as a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> on horseback with a banner and sword.
</p>
