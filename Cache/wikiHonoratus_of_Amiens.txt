<p><b>Saint Honoratus of Amiens</b> (<b>Honoré</b>, sometimes <b>Honorius</b>) (d. 16 January ca. 600)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> was the seventh <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Amiens" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Amiens">bishop of Amiens</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;b&#93;</a></sup>  His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is May 16. (<a href="/wiki/Honoratus" title="Honoratus">Honoratus of Lérins</a> (c. 350 – 429) was <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Arles" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Arles">Archbishop of Arles</a>.)
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honoratus_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Honoratus was born in <a href="/wiki/Port-le-Grand" title="Port-le-Grand">Port-le-Grand</a> (<a href="/wiki/Ponthieu" title="Ponthieu">Ponthieu</a>) near Amiens to a noble family. Noting his pious inclinations, his family entrusted his education to his predecessor in the bishopric of Amiens, Saint <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Amiens#Ordinaries" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Amiens">Beatus</a> (Beat). Honoratus resisted being elected bishop of Amiens, believing himself unworthy of this honour.<sup id="cite_ref-Marabini_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marabini-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his bishopric, he discovered the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of <a href="/wiki/Victoricus,_Fuscian,_and_Gentian" title="Victoricus, Fuscian, and Gentian">Victoricus, Fuscian, and Gentian</a>, which had remained hidden for 300 years.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honoratus_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Vie de Saint-Honoré</i> was composed towards the end of the 11th century by a canon of Amiens from ancient manuscripts and local legends.<sup id="cite_ref-Marabini_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marabini-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiographic tradition</a>, a ray of light of divine origin descended upon his head upon his election as bishop.  There also appeared holy oil of unknown origin on his forehead.
</p><p>When it was known in his hometown that he had been proclaimed bishop, his nursemaid, who was baking bread for the family, refused to believe that Honoratus had been elevated to such a position.  She remarked that she would believe the news only if the <a href="/wiki/Peel_(tool)" title="Peel (tool)">peel</a> she had been using to bake bread put down roots and turned itself into a tree.  When the peel was placed into the ground, it was transformed into a <a href="/wiki/Morus_nigra" title="Morus nigra">mulberry tree</a> that gave flowers and fruit.<sup id="cite_ref-Prichep_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prichep-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This miraculous tree was still being shown in the sixteenth century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honoratus_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His devotion was widespread in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> following reports of numerous <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> when his body was exhumed in 1060.
</p><p>After his death, his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were invoked against <a href="/wiki/Drought" title="Drought">drought</a> and floods to ensure a good wheat harvest.<sup id="cite_ref-Prichep_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prichep-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Guy_(bishop_of_Amiens)" title="Guy (bishop of Amiens)">Bishop Guy</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Enguerrand_I,_Count_of_Ponthieu" title="Enguerrand I, Count of Ponthieu">Enguerrand I, Count of Ponthieu</a>, ordered that a procession be held, in which an <a href="/wiki/Urn" title="Urn">urn</a> holding Honoratus' relics were carried around the walls of the city.
</p><p>In 1202, a baker named Renold Theriens (Renaud Cherins) donated to the city of Paris some land to build a chapel in honor of the saint. The chapel became one of the richest in Paris, and gave its name to <a href="/wiki/Rue_du_Faubourg_Saint-Honor%C3%A9" title="Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré">Rue du Faubourg Saint-Honoré</a>.
</p><p>In 1240, during construction of the cathedral of Amiens, the relics of Honoratus were carried through the surrounding countryside in a quest for funds.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;c&#93;</a></sup> A statue of Honoratus stands in the southern portal of <a href="/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral" title="Amiens Cathedral">Amiens Cathedral</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honoratus_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-honor%C3%A9_(5680550003).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Saint-honor%C3%A9_%285680550003%29.jpg/220px-Saint-honor%C3%A9_%285680550003%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Saint-honor%C3%A9_%285680550003%29.jpg/330px-Saint-honor%C3%A9_%285680550003%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Saint-honor%C3%A9_%285680550003%29.jpg/440px-Saint-honor%C3%A9_%285680550003%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint-honor%C3%A9_(5680550003).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>gâteau Saint-Honoré</div></div></div>
<p>Saint Honoré is the patron of a <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> establishment at <a href="/wiki/Abbeville" title="Abbeville">Abbeville</a>, which was founded in 1306.
</p><p>He is the patron saint of bakers and pastry chefs.<sup id="cite_ref-Marabini_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Marabini-4">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1400, the bakers of Paris established their <a href="/wiki/Guild" title="Guild">guild</a> in the church of Saint Honoratus, celebrating his feast on 16 May and spreading his cult. In 1659, <a href="/wiki/Louis_XIV" title="Louis XIV">Louis XIV</a> ordered that every baker observe the feast of Saint Honoratus, and give donations in honor of the saint and for the benefit of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/St._Honor%C3%A9_Cake" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Honoré Cake">St. Honoré Cake</a> is named for him.<sup id="cite_ref-Prichep_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prichep-5">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Honoratus_of_Amiens&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg/220px-Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="350" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg/330px-Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg/440px-Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3138" data-file-height="4990" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Paris_France_Rue_Saint_Honore_-_panoramio.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Rue Saint Honore, Paris</div></div></div>
<p>It is from him that a well-known church (Saint Honore) and thoroughfare in Paris, take their name.
</p>
