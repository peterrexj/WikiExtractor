<p><b>William Lacy (Lacey)</b> (died 1582) was an English Catholic priest and martyr. He and fellow priest <b>Richard Kirkman</b> were executed at York on 22 August 1582.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Lacy_(Catholic_priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>William was born at <a href="/wiki/Houghton,_East_Riding_of_Yorkshire" title="Houghton, East Riding of Yorkshire">Houghton</a> or Tosside, West Riding). He married a widow, named Cresswell, whose sons, Arthur and Joseph, became <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>. Little is related of his family by his biographers. He had a brother Ralph of Preston in Amounderness, a sister Barbara, and nephews (apparently her sons) Robert and William (Cal. S. P., Dom. add. 1566-79, London, 1871, p.&#160;562). He held a position of emolument under the Crown, possibly as coroner, till about 1565. One of this name, probably a relative, was a coroner for the West Riding in 1581-2 (Dasent, "Acts of the Privy Council", xiii, 358).<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was one of those gentlemen whose house was open to priests that came from the colleges abroad. Having been advised by them that he could not in good conscience attend Protestant churches, his absence was noted and he was forced to give up his position. He was subject to repeated fines, until he had to leave his home and stay with friends, but never long in one place. For fourteen years he suffered persecution for his faith, which included imprisonment at Hull. After the death of his wife, he went abroad and arrived at Reims, 22 June 1580. On 25 September following he went on to <a href="/wiki/Pont-%C3%A0-Mousson" title="Pont-à-Mousson">Pont-à-Mousson</a>, and thence to Rome, where, after obtaining a dispensation, he became a priest. The dispensation was necessary before ordination, as Lacy had been married to a widow.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On 10 May 1581, he was at <a href="/wiki/Loreto_(AN)" class="mw-redirect" title="Loreto (AN)">Loreto</a> on his way to England.
</p><p>He was arrested after a Mass said by Thomas Bell, afterwards an apostate, in York Castle, 22 July 1582. He suffered great hardships, being loaded with heavy irons, confined in an underground dungeon, and subjected to numerous examinations. He was arraigned on 11 August, probably under the <a href="/wiki/Bulls,_etc.,_from_Rome_Act_1571" title="Bulls, etc., from Rome Act 1571">Bulls, etc., from Rome Act 1571</a> and <a href="/wiki/13_Eliz._1" class="mw-redirect" title="13 Eliz. 1">13 Eliz. 1</a>.c. 3. He was executed at York, 22 August 1582.<sup id="cite_ref-Wainewright_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wainewright-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Richard Kirkman</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Priest and Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Addingham" title="Addingham">Addingham</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">22 August 1582<br /><a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>, England</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">29 December 1886 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">22 August</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Noose in neck, book, martyr's palm</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Richard_Kirkman">Richard Kirkman</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_Lacy_(Catholic_priest)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Richard Kirkman">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><b>Richard Kirkman</b> was born at <a href="/wiki/Addingham" title="Addingham">Addingham</a> in the <a href="/wiki/West_Riding" class="mw-redirect" title="West Riding">West Riding</a> of Yorkshire.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He arrived at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> in 1577 and, after the transference of the English College to Reims,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> he was ordained subdeacon there, along with <a href="/wiki/Richard_Thirkeld" title="Richard Thirkeld">Richard Thirkeld</a>, on 14 March 1579. Kirkman was ordained priest on the following Holy Saturday. He said his first Mass in the Abbey Church of the Benedictine nuns of St. Peter's.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg/220px-ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="132" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg/330px-ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg/440px-ScrivelsbyCourt.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="300" /></a><figcaption>Scrivelsby Court</figcaption></figure>
<p>He left for the English mission on 3 August 1579, with <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Briant" title="Alexander Briant">Alexander Briant</a> and three others. On his return to England in August he seems to have found a refuge with <a href="/wiki/Robert_Dymoke" title="Robert Dymoke">Robert Dymoke</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hereditary_Champion_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Hereditary Champion of England">hereditary Champion of England</a> (died in Lincoln gaol for his faith, 11 September 1580), at <a href="/wiki/Scrivelsby" title="Scrivelsby">Scrivelsby</a>, Lincolnshire. Kirkman was represented as a schoolmaster for Dymoke's sons. He laboured for four years on the English Mission. When Dymoke was arrested on the charge of <a href="/wiki/Recusancy" title="Recusancy">recusancy</a>, Kirkman fled north. Being questioned as a stranger in those parts, he was eventually arrested near <a href="/wiki/Wakefield" title="Wakefield">Wakefield</a> on 8 August 1582 by Francis Wortley of Wortley, <a href="/wiki/Justice_of_the_Peace" class="mw-redirect" title="Justice of the Peace">J.P.</a>, and seems to have been arraigned a day or two after under 23 Eliz. c. 1. After condemnation the two priests shared one cell in a turret till 10 August, when Kirkman was removed to an underground dungeon. He was executed on 22 August 1582.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
