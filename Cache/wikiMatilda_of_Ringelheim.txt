<p><b>Matilda of Ringelheim</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 892 – 14 March 968<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1990_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1990-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>), also known as <b>Saint Matilda</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Saxon">Saxon</a> noblewoman. Due to her marriage to <a href="/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler" title="Henry the Fowler">Henry I</a> in 909, she became the first <a href="/wiki/Ottonian" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottonian">Ottonian</a> <a href="/wiki/Queen_regnant" title="Queen regnant">queen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeumann1969_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeumann1969-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her eldest son, <a href="/wiki/Otto_the_Great" title="Otto the Great">Otto I</a>, restored the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a> in 962.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1999_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1999-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Matilda founded several spiritual institutions and women's convents. She was considered to be extremely pious, righteous and charitable. Matilda's two <a href="/wiki/Hagiographical" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographical">hagiographical</a> biographies and <i><a href="/wiki/The_Deeds_of_the_Saxons" title="The Deeds of the Saxons">The Deeds of the Saxons</a></i> serve as authoritative sources about her life and work.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_marriage_with_Henry_I">Early life and marriage with Henry I</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matilda_of_Ringelheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and marriage with Henry I">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Matilda, daughter of Reinhild and the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony" title="Duchy of Saxony">Saxon</a> count <a href="/wiki/Dietrich_of_Ringelheim" title="Dietrich of Ringelheim">Dietrich</a> (himself a descendant of the Saxon duke <a href="/wiki/Widukind" title="Widukind">Widukind</a> who fought against Charlemagne) was born in around 892, and was raised by her grandmother Matilda in <a href="/wiki/Herford_Abbey" title="Herford Abbey">Herford Abbey</a>. She had three sisters; Amalrada, Bia and <a href="/wiki/Frederuna" title="Frederuna">Fridarun</a>, who married <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Simple" title="Charles the Simple">Charles III</a>, king of <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West Francia</a>; and a brother, Beuve II, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ch%C3%A2lons-sur-Marne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Châlons-sur-Marne">Bishop of Châlons-sur-Marne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1990_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1990-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Due to Fridarun's marriage to count <a href="/wiki/Wichmann_the_Elder" title="Wichmann the Elder">Wichmann the Elder</a>, there was an alliance between the House of <a href="/wiki/Billung" title="Billung">Billung</a> and the Ottonian family, which expanded their possessions to the west.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTERieckenbergFreytag1955_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTERieckenbergFreytag1955-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In 909, she married Henry, at the time <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Saxony" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Saxony">Duke of Saxony</a> and later <a href="/wiki/East_Francia" title="East Francia">East Franconian</a> king, after his first marriage to <a href="/wiki/Hatheburg_of_Merseburg" title="Hatheburg of Merseburg">Hatheburg of Merseburg</a> was cancelled.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeumann1969_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeumann1969-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201811_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201811-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 929, Matilda received her <a href="/wiki/Dowry" title="Dowry">dowry</a>, which Henry gave to her in the so-called <i>Hausordnung</i>. It consisted of goods in <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg" title="Quedlinburg">Quedlinburg</a>, <a href="/wiki/P%C3%B6hlde_Abbey" title="Pöhlde Abbey">Pöhlde</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nordhausen,_Thuringia" title="Nordhausen, Thuringia">Nordhausen</a>, Grona (near <a href="/wiki/G%C3%B6ttingen" title="Göttingen">Göttingen</a>) and <a href="/wiki/Duderstadt" title="Duderstadt">Duderstadt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1990_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1990-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> As queen, she took an interest in women's <a href="/wiki/Monasteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Monasteries">monasteries</a> and is said to have had an influence on her husband's reign by having a strong sense of justice.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201817_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201817-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Children">Children</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matilda_of_Ringelheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Children">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Through Henry, Matilda gave birth to five children:  
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Otto_the_Great" title="Otto the Great">Otto</a> (912–973), who was crowned the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> in 962;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1999_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1999-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Henry_I,_Duke_of_Bavaria" title="Henry I, Duke of Bavaria">Henry</a> (919/22–955), who was appointed <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bavaria">Duke of Bavaria</a> in 948;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBeumann1969_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeumann1969-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Bruno_the_Great" title="Bruno the Great">Bruno</a> (925–965), who was elected <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Cologne" title="Archbishop of Cologne">Archbishop of Cologne</a> in 953 and <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Lorraine">Duke of Lorraine</a> in 954;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOediger1955_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOediger1955-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Hedwig_of_Saxony" title="Hedwig of Saxony">Hedwig</a> (d. 965/80), who married the <a href="/wiki/West_Frankish" class="mw-redirect" title="West Frankish">West Frankish</a> duke <a href="/wiki/Hugh_the_Great" title="Hugh the Great">Hugh the Great</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Gerberga_of_Saxony" title="Gerberga of Saxony">Gerberga</a> (d. 968/69), who first married <a href="/wiki/Gilbert,_Duke_of_Lorraine" title="Gilbert, Duke of Lorraine">Gilbert, Duke of Lorraine</a> and later the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carolingian">Carolingian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV_of_France" title="Louis IV of France">Louis IV of France</a>.</li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_as_a_widow">Life as a widow</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matilda_of_Ringelheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Life as a widow">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After Henry's death 936 in <a href="/wiki/Memleben_Abbey" title="Memleben Abbey">Memleben</a>, he was buried in Quedlinburg, where Queen Matilda founded a convent the same year.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEhlers1998259_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEhlers1998259-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> She lived there during the following years and took care of the family's <a href="/wiki/Memorialization" title="Memorialization">memorialization</a>. Thus, <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg_Abbey" title="Quedlinburg Abbey">Quedlinburg Abbey</a> became the most important center of prayer and commemoration of the dead in the East Franconian empire.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1984169–179_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1984169–179-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Like in other convents, daughters of noble families were raised in Quedlinburg, to later become Abbesses in order to secure the families influence. One of them was her own granddaughter, <a href="/wiki/Matilda,_Abbess_of_Quedlinburg" title="Matilda, Abbess of Quedlinburg">Matilda</a>, daughter of Otto I and <a href="/wiki/Adelaide_of_Italy" title="Adelaide of Italy">Adelheid of Burgundy</a>, to whom she passed on the conducting of the convent in 966, after 30 years of leadership. The younger Matilda therefore became the first abbess of the convent in Quedlinburg.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller-Wiegand200398_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller-Wiegand200398-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> With her other goods, Queen Matilda founded further convents, one of them in 947 in <a href="/wiki/Enger" title="Enger">Enger</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Her last foundation was the convent of Nordhausen in 961.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201827_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201827-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Matilda's handling of her dowry, which she had received from King Henry I previous to his death, was subject to a dispute between her and Otto I during the years 936–946. Otto made a claim on his mother's possessions, which eventually led to her fleeing into exile. Otto's wife, Queen <a href="/wiki/Eadgyth" title="Eadgyth">Eadgyth</a>, is said to have brought about the reconciliation in which Matilda left her goods and Otto was forgiven for his actions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMüller-Wiegand2003124_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMüller-Wiegand2003124-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The exact circumstances of this feud are still controversial to this day, but in order to protect her goods, Matilda acquired papal privileges for all monasteries in eastern Saxony in the period before her death in early 968.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1993263_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1993263-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> However, these efforts were ignored when <a href="/wiki/Theophanu" title="Theophanu">Theophanu</a>, the wife of <a href="/wiki/Otto_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto II, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto II</a>, received Matilda's dowry after she died.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201843_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff201843-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matilda_of_Ringelheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After a long illness, Queen Matilda died on 14 March 968,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1993261_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlthoff1993261-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> in the convent of <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg" title="Quedlinburg">Quedlinburg</a>. She was buried in <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg_Abbey" title="Quedlinburg Abbey">Quedlinburg Abbey</a>, next to her late husband.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEhlers1998257_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEhlers1998257-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Throughout her life, Matilda was dedicated to charity and her spiritual foundations – as expressed several times in her two hagiographies.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESchütte1994_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESchütte1994-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (March 2020)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> A commemorative plaque dedicated to her can be found in the <a href="/wiki/Walhalla_memorial" class="mw-redirect" title="Walhalla memorial">Walhalla memorial</a> near <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a>, Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Matilda_of_Ringelheim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Matilda is the patron of the St. Mathilde church in <a href="/wiki/Laatzen" title="Laatzen">Laatzen</a> (Germany), the St. Mathilde church in <a href="/wiki/Quedlinburg" title="Quedlinburg">Quedlinburg</a> (Germany), the Melkite church in <a href="/wiki/Aleppo" title="Aleppo">Aleppo</a> (Syria), and the Mathilden-Hospital in <a href="/wiki/Herford" title="Herford">Herford</a> (Germany). Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 14 March.
</p>
