<p>Saint <b>Rudesind</b> (<a href="/wiki/Galician_language" title="Galician language">Galician</a>: <i lang="gl">San Rosendo, Rudesindo</i>; <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">São Rosendo</i> <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Rudesindus</i>) (November 26, 907 – March 1, 977) was a <a href="/wiki/Galician_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Galician people">Galician</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>. He was also a regional administrator and military leader under his kinsmen, the Kings of León.
</p><p>Rudesind was born into the nobility: his father was Count <a href="/wiki/Gutierre_Men%C3%A9ndez" class="mw-redirect" title="Gutierre Menéndez">Gutierre Menéndez</a> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gutiher Ermegildi</i>), brother-in-law to <a href="/wiki/Ordo%C3%B1o_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordoño II">Ordoño II</a> and supporter of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Le%C3%B3n" class="mw-redirect" title="Alfonso III of León">Alfonso III of León</a>, and his mother was St. Ilduara Eriz (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Hilduara Erici</i>),<sup id="cite_ref-Centenario_de_la_Diócesis_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Centenario_de_la_Diócesis-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo_Celanova_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo_Celanova-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> daughter of count <a href="/wiki/Ero_Fern%C3%A1ndez" title="Ero Fernández">Ero Fernández</a>. His sister Hermesenda became wife of Count Pelayo González and mother-in-law of count <a href="/wiki/Gonzalo_Men%C3%A9ndez" title="Gonzalo Menéndez">Gonzalo Menéndez</a>. Rudesind was the grandson of Ermesenda Gatónez, sister of his predecessor in the see of <a href="/wiki/Mondo%C3%B1edo" title="Mondoñedo">Mondoñedo</a> (<i>Dumium</i>), Bishop <a href="/wiki/Savaric_II_(Bishop_of_Mondo%C3%B1edo)" class="mw-redirect" title="Savaric II (Bishop of Mondoñedo)">Sabarico II</a>, and was also related to the abbess <a href="/wiki/Saint_Senorina" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Senorina">Saint Senorina</a>. He became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> at a young age and became bishop of Mondoñedo at the age of 18 (as <b>Rudesind II</b>).<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo_Celanova_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo_Celanova-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He served as bishop from 925 to 950, then after a hiatus in which his nephew <a href="/w/index.php?title=Arias_N%C3%BAnez&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Arias Núnez (page does not exist)">Arias Núnez</a> filled the role, again served briefly from 955 to 958, to be followed again by Arias.  Another nephew, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Arias_Pel%C3%A1ez&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Arias Peláez (page does not exist)">Arias Peláez</a>, would later hold the see.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Founder_of_monasteries">Founder of monasteries</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rudesind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Founder of monasteries">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro,_Galicia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro%2C_Galicia.jpg/180px-Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro%2C_Galicia.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="135" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro%2C_Galicia.jpg/270px-Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro%2C_Galicia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro%2C_Galicia.jpg/360px-Mosteiro_de_San_Xo%C3%A1n_de_Caaveiro%2C_Galicia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>Monastery of Saint John of Caaveiro.</figcaption></figure>
<p>He founded, allegedly under the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Rule">Benedictine Rule</a>, monasteries such as <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_of_Caaveiro" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John of Caaveiro">Saint John of Caaveiro</a> (San Xoán de Caaveiro) (934) and the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_San_Salvador_de_Celanova" title="Monastery of San Salvador de Celanova">Monastery of San Salvador de Celanova</a> (September 12, 936).
</p><p>To build Celanova, he managed to make his brother <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Fruela</i> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Froyla</i>) and his cousin <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Jimena</i> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Scemena</i>) give up their rights to the land of <i>Villar</i>, where he founded this monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Administrative_and_military_career">Administrative and military career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rudesind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Administrative and military career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 955, he was named by <a href="/wiki/Ordo%C3%B1o_III_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Ordoño III of León">King Ordoño III</a> governor of the lands of Celanova. Rudesind also had jurisdiction over the lands that extended from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Riocaldo&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Riocaldo (page does not exist)">Riocaldo</a> (the southern boundary of Galicia) to Santa Maria de <a href="/wiki/Ortigueira" title="Ortigueira">Ortigueira</a> (on the <a href="/wiki/Bay_of_Biscay" title="Bay of Biscay">Cantabrian</a> coast).<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo_Celanova_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo_Celanova-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Later, at the request of <a href="/wiki/Elvira_Ram%C3%ADrez_of_Le%C3%B3n" class="mw-redirect" title="Elvira Ramírez of León">Elvira Ramírez of León</a> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Geloyra Renamiri</i>), aunt of the future <a href="/wiki/Ramiro_III_of_Le%C3%B3n" title="Ramiro III of León">Ramiro III</a>, he was made governor of Galicia, from spring of 968 to early 969.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo_Celanova_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo_Celanova-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He led forces against <a href="/wiki/Norsemen" title="Norsemen">Norse</a> and <a href="/wiki/Moorish" class="mw-redirect" title="Moorish">Moorish</a> armies. The Moors had crossed the <a href="/wiki/Mondego_River" title="Mondego River">Mondego</a> and had reached the <a href="/wiki/Minho_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Minho River">Minho</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 966, the Norsemen had raided <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Galicia" title="Kingdom of Galicia">Galicia</a> and killed the warlord and bishop of Santiago de Compostela Sisnand in battle, but Rudesind later rallied the local forces and killed their leader <a href="/wiki/Gunrod" title="Gunrod">Gundered</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_Episcopal_career">Second Episcopal career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rudesind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Second Episcopal career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rudesind had already served as bishop of Mondoñedo - Dumio. After the killing of Sisnand in the battle of Fornelos, Rudesind was appointed administrator of the See of <a href="/wiki/Iria_Flavia" title="Iria Flavia">Iria Flavia</a>, and he was in charge of that diocese from 968 to 977.  After 977, he retired from his sees (he was succeeded at Compostela by Pelayo Rodríguez, a monk of Celanova).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career_as_abbot">Career as abbot</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rudesind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Career as abbot">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rudesind succeeded <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Franquila&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Franquila (page does not exist)">St. Franquila</a> (originally from the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Ribas_de_Sil" title="Ribas de Sil">Ribas de Sil</a>) as abbot of Celanova.  As abbot of Celanova, he was a leading figure of his time, and received visits from religious leaders throughout <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Spain)" title="Galicia (Spain)">Galicia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> who wanted spiritual advice.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Rudesind_-_Catholic_Online_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Rudesind_-_Catholic_Online-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  A <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> named Egila, in a donation that he made to Celanova, wrote this to Rudesind: "To you, eminent bishop, Rudesind, holiest father, true teacher, who teaches your subjects with your words and deeds...".<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  He earned a reputation for performing <a href="/wiki/Miracles" class="mw-redirect" title="Miracles">miracles</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Rudesind_-_Catholic_Online_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Rudesind_-_Catholic_Online-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rudesind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1601, his <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were exhumed and placed in a silver <a href="/wiki/Urn" title="Urn">urn</a> at the principal altar of the church of Celanova Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo_Celanova_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo_Celanova-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends">Legends</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rudesind&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legends">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A legend told of Rudesind concerns his birth.  His mother had previous children, but they had all died in infancy.  When her husband Gutierre went on an expedition to <a href="/wiki/Coimbra" title="Coimbra">Coimbra</a> with Alfonso III, Ilduara accompanied him.  She prayed at the hermitage of San Salvador on Monte Cordova, after climbing up to it alone and barefoot.  There she received the knowledge, from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Michael" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Michael">Saint Michael</a>, that she would bear a son who would become not only a great leader of men but also a holy man.  In gratitude, she ordered the construction of a church there and remained until Rudesind had been born.  She wanted to baptize her son at San Salvador, but the cart used to haul up the <a href="/wiki/Baptismal_font" title="Baptismal font">baptismal font</a> broke down.  The workers went to get another cart.  Meanwhile, however, Saint Michael had the broken cart move up the hill on its own accord.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Rosendo,_Obispo_Y_Abad-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
