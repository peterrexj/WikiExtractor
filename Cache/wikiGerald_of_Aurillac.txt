<p><b>Gerald of Aurillac</b> (or <b>Saint Gerald</b>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 855 – c. 909<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) is a French <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, also recognized by other <a href="/wiki/Religious_denomination" title="Religious denomination">religious denominations</a> of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>.
</p><p>Gerald was born into the <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> nobility, counting <a href="/wiki/Cesarius_of_Arles" class="mw-redirect" title="Cesarius of Arles">Cesarius of Arles</a> among his forebears,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> though the title "Count of <a href="/wiki/Aurillac" title="Aurillac">Aurillac</a>" was not held by his father, to whose estates he succeeded, and was assumed by him in later life.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The details of his life known today come primarily from <i>The Life of St. Gerald of Aurillac</i> (c. 930–931) written by <a href="/wiki/Odo_of_Cluny" title="Odo of Cluny">Odo of Cluny</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
Writing twenty years after the event, Abbot Odo of Cluny described how William, duke of Aquitaine, had entreated Gerald to abandon the <i>militia regia</i>, the <a href="/wiki/Feudalism" title="Feudalism">feudal service</a> performed directly to the king and pay homage to himself, "for the sake of love".<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gerald resisted, having recently assumed the title of <i><a href="/wiki/Comes" title="Comes">comes</a></i> and doubtless preferring to owe his fealty to the more distant liege, the king at Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Odo, Gerald suffered an illness as a child, sufficient in duration to advance his reading, and may have been disfigured by <a href="/wiki/Acne" title="Acne">acne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In later life he was to suffer blindness. He seriously considered joining a religious order, but was persuaded against it by his friend Geusbert, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rodez" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rodez">Bishop of Rodez</a>, on the grounds that with his social position he could do more good by remaining in the world as a layman. Nevertheless, secretly <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> under his habitual cap, he consecrated his life in service to God, gave away his possessions, took a personal vow of chastity and prayed the <a href="/wiki/Breviary" title="Breviary">breviary</a> each day.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He founded a church and abbey on his estate of Aurillac, where he was buried after dying at Cezeinac/Cézerniac,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> name of an unclearly identified locality —possibly <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A9zens" title="Cézens">Cézens</a> or <a href="/wiki/Saint-Cirgues,_Lot" title="Saint-Cirgues, Lot">Saint-Cirgues</a> today—, on a Friday 13 October, probably in 909. His memorial feast day is October 13. The validation of his local cult by Odo of Cluny served to establish his wider veneration. Saint Gerald, considered by his Church and his followers as a great example of a <a href="/wiki/Celibacy" title="Celibacy">celibate Christian</a> aristocrat, is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">counts</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bachelor" title="Bachelor">bachelors</a>. Because of his poor health and blindness, more emphasized in his developing cult than in Odo's <i>Life</i>, he is also the patron saint of the disabled, handicapped, and physically challenged. He also became the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Auvergne_(province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Auvergne (province)">Upper Auvergne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
