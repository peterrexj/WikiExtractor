<p><b>Laurent-Joseph-Marius Imbert</b> (23 March 1796 – 21 September 1839), sometimes called <b>Laurent-Marie-Joseph Imbert</b> and affectionately known in Korea as <b>Bishop Imbert Bum</b> (<a href="/wiki/Korean_name" title="Korean name">Korean name</a>: <i>Bum Se-hyeong</i>) was a French missionary <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholicism)">bishop</a> in Asia. Most notable among the Koreans, he was appointed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> in August 1836 when first Bishop <a href="/wiki/Barth%C3%A9lemy_Brugui%C3%A8re" title="Barthélemy Bruguière">Barthélemy Bruguière</a> died in <a href="/wiki/Manchuria" title="Manchuria">Manchuria</a>.
</p><p>Eventually, he was executed in the Kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Joseon" title="Joseon">Joseon</a> for his <a href="/wiki/Catholic_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic faith">Catholic faith</a>; it is estimated that 8,000 to 10,000 were killed for their faith in 19th-century Korea—the <a href="/wiki/Korean_Martyrs" title="Korean Martyrs">Korean Martyrs</a>. 103 of them, including Imbert, were <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> as saints in 1984. His feast day is 21 September,<sup id="cite_ref-martyrology_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-martyrology-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and he is also venerated with the rest of the 103 <a href="/wiki/Korean_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Korean martyrs">Korean martyrs</a> on 20 September.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Laurent-Joseph-Marius_Imbert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Imbert was born at <a href="/wiki/Marignane" title="Marignane">Marignane</a>, to parents who were residents of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hamlet_(town)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hamlet (town) (page does not exist)">hamlet</a> of Callas, in the <a href="/wiki/Communes_of_France" title="Communes of France">commune</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cabri%C3%A8s" title="Cabriès">Cabriès</a> in the <a href="/wiki/French_departments" class="mw-redirect" title="French departments">Department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bouches-du-Rh%C3%B4ne" title="Bouches-du-Rhône">Bouches-du-Rhône</a>. When he became of age, he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Aix-en-Provence" title="Aix-en-Provence">Aix</a> to pursue his studies. According to reports, he paid his expenses by making and selling <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosaries</a>. He then enrolled at the <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Paris Foreign Missions Society</a> on 8 October 1818.<sup id="cite_ref-MEP_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MEP-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 5 March 1819, Imbert was <a href="/wiki/Incardination" class="mw-redirect" title="Incardination">incardinated</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Paris" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Paris">Archdiocese of Paris</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> on 18 December of that same year, having received an <a href="/wiki/Indult" title="Indult">indult</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> due to his not having reached the legal age. He then set sail from France on 20 March 1820, bound for <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> service in China.
</p><p>Imbert's first stop was in <a href="/wiki/Penang" title="Penang">Penang</a>, <a href="/wiki/British_Malaya" title="British Malaya">Malaya</a>, where he was asked to replace a teacher at the <a href="/wiki/College_General" title="College General">College General</a> (Major Seminary), who had taken ill. He taught there from April 1821 to January 1822
</p><p>In 1821, <a href="/wiki/Esprit-Marie-Joseph_Florens" title="Esprit-Marie-Joseph Florens">Esprit-Marie-Joseph Florens</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Vicar_Apostolic" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicar Apostolic">Vicar Apostolic</a> of <a href="/wiki/Siam" class="mw-redirect" title="Siam">Siam</a>, requested for him to call at Singapore. The bishop had been contemplating opening a <a href="/wiki/Mission_(station)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (station)">missionary station</a> in Singapore. He was not very certain, though, whether there was any urgency or he was aware of the circumstances prevailing in the island. Therefore, the young missionary was to check on the situation. He reached Singapore on 11 December 1821 and spent about a week there. Imbert might have been the first priest to celebrate <a href="/wiki/Mass_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mass (Catholic Church)">Mass</a> on the island.
</p><p>In February 1822, Imbert sailed for <a href="/wiki/Macau" title="Macau">Macau</a>, but unable to go directly there, he spent the next two years in <a href="/wiki/Tonkin" title="Tonkin">Tonkin</a>, <a href="/wiki/French_Indochina" title="French Indochina">French Indochina</a>. Only then was he able to enter China, where he spent twelve years in <a href="/wiki/Sichuan" title="Sichuan">Sichuan</a> and founded a seminary in Moupin.
</p><p>On 26 April 1836, Imbert was appointed <a href="/wiki/Vicar_Apostolic" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicar Apostolic">Vicar Apostolic</a> of Korea and <a href="/wiki/Titular_Bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Titular Bishop">Titular Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Capsa_(see)" class="mw-redirect" title="Capsa (see)">Capsa</a>. He was <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> on 14 May 1837 by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Giacomo_Luigi_Fontana&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Giacomo Luigi Fontana (page does not exist)">Giacomo Luigi Fontana</a> M.E.P., the Vicar Apostolic of <a href="/wiki/Huguang" title="Huguang">Huguang</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He then crossed secretly from Manchuria to Korea that same year. During this time, Korea was going through a period of <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> persecution.
</p><p>On 10 August 1839, Imbert, who was secretly going about his missionary work, was betrayed. Realizing that it was only a matter of time before he was arrested and killed, he celebrated Mass and surrendered himself to those who lay in waiting for him. He was taken to <a href="/wiki/Seoul" title="Seoul">Seoul</a>, where he was tortured to reveal the whereabouts of foreign missionaries. Mistakenly believing that his <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">converts</a> would be spared if all foreign missionaries came out from hiding and gave themselves up, he wrote a note to his fellow missionaries, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pierre-Philibert_Maubant&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pierre-Philibert Maubant (page does not exist)">Pierre-Philibert Maubant</a> and Jacques-Honoré Chastan, asking them to surrender to the Korean authorities as well, which they did.
</p><p>All three of them were imprisoned together. They were taken before an <a href="/wiki/Interrogator" class="mw-redirect" title="Interrogator">interrogator</a> and questioned for three days to reveal the names and whereabouts of their converts. As torture failed to break them down, they were sent to another prison and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> on 21 September 1839 at Saenamteo, Korea. Their bodies remained exposed for several days but were finally <a href="/wiki/Burial" title="Burial">buried</a> on Nogu Mountain.
</p><p>The three were among the 79 <a href="/wiki/Korean_Martyrs" title="Korean Martyrs">Korean Martyrs</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1925, and among the 103 <a href="/wiki/Korean_Martyrs" title="Korean Martyrs">Korean Martyrs</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in Seoul on 6 May 1984.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">feast day</a> of Saint Laurent Imbert is celebrated on 20 September (formerly 10 June). Accordingly, a religious statue of Imbert Bum is also enshrined at a side chapel of the <a href="/wiki/Myeongdong_Cathedral" title="Myeongdong Cathedral">Myeongdong Cathedral</a>, where pious women have vested the image in the traditional <a href="/wiki/Hanbok" title="Hanbok">Hanbok</a> costume of South Korea.
</p>
