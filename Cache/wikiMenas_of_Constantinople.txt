					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople in the sixth-century</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the sixth-century patriarch. For other saints with the same name, see <a href="/wiki/Saint_Menas_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Saint Menas (disambiguation)">Saint Menas (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Menas of Constantinople</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><a href="/wiki/File:%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg" class="image"><img alt="Патриарх Мина.jpg" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg/220px-%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="407" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg/330px-%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg/440px-%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%85_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0.jpg 2x" data-file-width="865" data-file-height="1599" /></a></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Patriarch of Constantinople</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">25 August 552</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">25 August</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Controversy</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Three-Chapter_Controversy" title="Three-Chapter Controversy">Three-Chapter Controversy</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Menas</b> (<b>Minas</b>) (<a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ancient Greek language">Ancient Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Μηνάς</span>) (died 25 August 552) considered a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Chalcedonian_Christianity" title="Chalcedonian Christianity">Calcedonian affirming church</a> and by extension both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> of our times, was born in Alexandria, and enters the records in high ecclesiastical office as presbyter and director of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_medicine" title="Byzantine medicine">Hospital of Sampson</a> in Constantinople, where tradition has him linked to Saint Sampson directly, and in the healing of Justinian from the <a href="/wiki/Plague_of_Justinian" title="Plague of Justinian">bubonic plague</a> in 542.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was appointed <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">Patriarch of Constantinople</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a> on 13 March 536.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Agapetus_I" title="Pope Agapetus I">Pope Agapetus I</a> consecrated him to succeed <a href="/wiki/Anthimus_I_of_Constantinople" title="Anthimus I of Constantinople">Anthimus</a>, who was condemned as a <a href="/wiki/Monophysite" class="mw-redirect" title="Monophysite">monophysite</a>.  This was the first time that a <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Roman Pope</a> consecrated a Patriarch of Constantinople.
</p><p>At some date very soon after his election he received the order (keleusis) from the Emperor, whose text is not preserved, but which instructed him to call a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synodos endemousa</a> to examine the case of Anthimus, which would be heard at a series of five sessions, beginning on 2 May and ending 4 June 536.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This Synod condemned Anthimus, as noted in <a href="/wiki/Novellae_Constitutiones" title="Novellae Constitutiones">Novel</a> XLII from Justinian, addressed directly to Menas.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Within this same effort from Justinian to seal the growing rift between the Patriarch in Constantinople and that of Jerusalem, Menas later took a position against <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>, a crisis merging into the <a href="/wiki/Three-Chapter_Controversy" title="Three-Chapter Controversy">Three-Chapter Controversy</a>, an attempt to condemn the writings of certain <a href="/wiki/Non-Chalcedonian" class="mw-redirect" title="Non-Chalcedonian">non-Chalcedonian</a> figures. Because of these moves, he was excommunicated by Agapetus in 547 and in 551 for taking positions counter to those held by the Pope; but in both cases the sentence of excommunication was quickly lifted. Menas' patriarchate represents the greatest extent of papal influence in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.  Almost immediately after the events of 536, which may be viewed as a Chalcedonian victory over monophysites, the ordination of an independent network of alleged monophysite / self-professed <a href="/wiki/Miaphysite" class="mw-redirect" title="Miaphysite">miaphysite</a> bishops claiming <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_succession" title="Apostolic succession">apostolic authority</a> would begin, leading eventually to the formation of a separate non-Chalcedonian church, the still-existing <a href="/wiki/Syrian_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Syrian Orthodox Church">Syrian Orthodox Church</a> that would be in communion with other <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodox">excommunicated sees of the same theological persuasion</a>. Justinian and Menas' efforts for doctrinal Church unity would meet with failure.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was during his patriarchate that emperor Justinian's church of <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a>, then the largest building in the world and the seat of the Patriarchs, was consecrated. Also, in 551 the Emperor compelled Menas to call what would be the <a href="/wiki/Fifth_Ecumenical_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Fifth Ecumenical Council">Fifth Ecumenical Council</a>, to reconcile the Western and Eastern Churches around the Three-Chapter Controversy, to be chaired ultimately by his successor Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Eutychius_of_Constantinople" title="Eutychius of Constantinople">Eutychius of Constantinople</a> in 553.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died peacefully in 552. His feast day in both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> traditions is observed on August 25.
</p>
