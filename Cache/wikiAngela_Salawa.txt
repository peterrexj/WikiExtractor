<p><b>Angela Salawa</b> (9 September 1881 - 12 March 1922) was a Polish woman who served in <a href="/wiki/Hospitals" class="mw-redirect" title="Hospitals">hospitals</a> in <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> She was the 11th child and lived in a very religious family. She became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Secular_Franciscan_Order" title="Secular Franciscan Order">Secular Franciscan Order</a> and worked in hospitals throughout World War I. She later became sick due to working alongside the sick and died in 1922.
</p><p>On the road to sainthood <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> beatified her in 1991.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_Salawa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Angela Salawa came from the village of <a href="/wiki/Siepraw" title="Siepraw">Siepraw</a> near <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a>, Poland. She was born in 1881 to Bartłomiej Salawa and Ewa Bochenek. There were twelve children in her family, with Salawa being the eleventh. Her father Bartłomiej was a <a href="/wiki/Blacksmith" title="Blacksmith">blacksmith</a>. Salawa was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> four days after her birth. The family was poor, and because she was weak and sickly, Salawa was not as able help with chores as much as her more physically robust siblings. She was an obedient child who tried to do her best to help her family. From an early age she felt <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Christ</a>'s call on her heart.
</p><p>At the age of 16, Salawa left home to work as a <a href="/wiki/Maid" title="Maid">maid</a> in Kraków. While there, she became caught up in worldly pursuits and her religious fervor waned. She was much affected by the death of her sister Teresa, who had appealed to Salawa to reconsider her worldly values. While dancing at a wedding reception, Salawa perceived Christ standing nearby, asking her how she could prefer dancing to following him. The experience was a turning-point in her life.  She immediately went to a church to pray and became devoted to adoring Christ in the Blessed Sacrament.
</p><p>Salawa considered a religious vocation, but her weak physical health was an impediment. She decided to remain in the world, taking private vows of purity and virtue in 1900. She continued to work as a maid, but suffered due to a breach between herself and her family and from false accusations by her employer.
</p><p>In 1912, Salawa became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Secular_Franciscan_Order" title="Secular Franciscan Order">Secular Franciscan Order</a>. She felt an affinity with <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>, who, like Salawa herself, had broken with his family.
</p><p>When <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> broke out in 1914, Salawa remained in Kraków, nursing soldiers. Her own health was deteriorating, but no one noticed her suffering.  In 1916 her employer accused her of stealing, and she lost her employment.  She was homeless, in pain and ill, but she was discharged from the hospital because she appeared to be well. Eventually she was alone in the world, living in a basement room, abandoned by family, friends and neighbors.
</p><p>She died on 12 March 1922.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_Salawa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:162px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Salawa1.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Salawa1.JPG/160px-Salawa1.JPG" decoding="async" width="160" height="120" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Salawa1.JPG/240px-Salawa1.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Salawa1.JPG/320px-Salawa1.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3264" data-file-height="2448" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Salawa1.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The tomb of Aniela Salawa.</div></div></div>
<p>The cause of beatification was introduced on 30 March 1981 which bestowed upon her the title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> - documentation on her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> - was forwarded to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in 1987 which resulted in <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>'s declaration of her heroic virtue on 23 October 1987. This granted her the title of <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a>.
</p><p>In 1990 in <a href="/wiki/Nowy_Targ" title="Nowy Targ">Nowy Targ</a> in Poland there was a young boy who suffered a severe brain injury. The intercession of Angela Salawa was asked to help the boy, and he made a full recovery. This was investigated and was later validated on 12 April 1991. John Paul II approved the miracle on 6 July 1991 and beatified her on 13 August 1991.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
