<p><b>Mariana of Jesus de Paredes</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Mariana</i> or <span title="Spanish-language text"><i lang="es">María Ana de Jesús de Paredes</i></span>; October 31, 1618 &#8211; May 26, 1645) is a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and was the first person to be <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> from what is now Ecuador. She was a <a href="/wiki/Recluse" title="Recluse">recluse</a> who is said to have sacrificed herself for the salvation of her city. She was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> in 1853 and canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> in 1950. She is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of Ecuador and <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Society_of_Jesus_(Quito,_Ecuador)" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of the Society of Jesus (Quito, Ecuador)">Church of the Society of Jesus</a> in Quito. Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on May 26 by the nation and on May 28 by the Franciscan Order.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mariana_de_Jes%C3%BAs_de_Paredes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born <b>Maríana de Paredes Flores y Granobles y Jaramillo</b> in the city of Quito, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru" title="Viceroyalty of Peru">Viceroyalty of Peru</a>, on October 31, 1618. Born of aristocratic parents on both sides of her family, her father was Jerónimo de Paredes Flores y Granobles, a nobleman of <a href="/wiki/Toledo,_Spain" title="Toledo, Spain">Toledo</a>, and her mother was Mariana Jaramillo, a descendant of one of the leading <a href="/wiki/Conquistador" title="Conquistador">conquistadors</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Mariana was the youngest of eight children, and it is claimed her birth was accompanied by most unusual phenomena in the heavens, clearly connected with the child and juridically attested at the time of the process of her beatification.<sup id="cite_ref-fisher_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fisher-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Orphaned at the age of four, she was taken in and raised by her older sister, Jerónima de Paredes, and the latter's husband, Cosme de Caso.  Drawn to a spiritual life, her sister and brother-in-law allowed her to live in seclusion in their house, leading an <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetical</a> lifestyle, similar to <a href="/wiki/Rose_of_Lima" title="Rose of Lima">Rose of Lima</a> to whom she is often compared. She refused entry into a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, despite urging from her brother-in-law and guardian Cosme de Caso. She subjected herself to bodily mortification, with the aid of her Indian servant.  She did not live in total seclusion, but rather centered her spiritual life on the nearby <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> church, where she participated in the <a href="/wiki/Sodality_of_Our_Lady" title="Sodality of Our Lady">Sodality of Our Lady</a>, established by the Society in their various churches around the world to help the laity in their desire to deepen their spiritual lives.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is reported that the <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fast</a> which Paredes kept was so strict that she took scarcely an ounce of dry bread every eight or ten days. The food which miraculously sustained her life, as in the case of <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rose_of_Lima" title="Rose of Lima">Rose of Lima</a>, was, according to the sworn testimony of many witnesses, the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Eucharist (Catholic Church)">Eucharist</a> alone, which she received every morning at <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-fisher_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fisher-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paredes' spiritual life was closely connected to the Jesuits, but, at the suggestion of her <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a>, she became a member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> This was likely advised to her as enrolling in that Order gave her an official status reflective of her penitential way of life in Spanish society, for which the Jesuits had no equivalent. The <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> she assumed at that time, <b>Mariana of Jesus</b>, was no doubt indicative as to where her spiritual heart lay. According to her Jesuit hagiographer, she did not go to the Franciscan church to receive the <a href="/wiki/Scapular" title="Scapular">scapular</a> and rope cincture proclaiming membership in that life, but sent someone else.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following Paredes' death in 1645, her funeral and burial were held in the Jesuit church. The funeral sermon that the priest Alonso de Rojas preached emphasized her bodily mortification and renunciation of the flesh, and put her forward as a model for females in Quito to emulate. "Learn girls of Quito, from your fellow countrywoman, [to prefer] holiness over beauty, virtues over ostentation."<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  The sermon became a key document in the long process to establish her saintliness, <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> (1853), and final canonization (1950).
</p><p>The Friars Minor claimed Paredes as a saint of the Franciscan Order. She did wear the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Scapular" title="Scapular">scapular</a> and cord, but her 17th-century Jesuit hagiographer, Jacinto Morán de Butrón, claims that the Jesuits nurtured her spiritual life. Soon after her death, the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">Province</a> of Peru, based in Lima, included a biography of Mariana in the history of the province, citing the Jesuit funeral sermon as a source.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paredes possessed an ecstatic gift of prayer and is said to have been able to predict the future, see distant events as if they were passing before her, read the secrets of hearts, cure diseases by a mere sign of the <a href="/wiki/Cross" title="Cross">Cross</a> or by sprinkling the sufferer with <a href="/wiki/Holy_water" title="Holy water">holy water</a>, and at least once restored a dead person to life.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> During the 1645 earthquakes and subsequent epidemics in Quito, she publicly offered herself as a victim for the city and died shortly thereafter. It is also reported that, on the day she died, her <a href="/wiki/Sacred" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacred">sanctity</a> was revealed in a wonderful manner: Immediately after her death, a pure white <a href="/wiki/Lilium" title="Lilium">lily</a> sprang up from her blood, blossomed and bloomed, a prodigy which has given her the title of "The Lily of Quito".<sup id="cite_ref-fisher_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fisher-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Republic of Ecuador has declared her a national heroine.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mariana_de_Jes%C3%BAs_de_Paredes&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Soon after Paredes' death, at the urging of the Jesuit Fathers, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Quito" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Quito">Bishop of Quito</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_de_la_Pe%C3%B1a_y_Montenegro" title="Alfonso de la Peña y Montenegro">Alfonso de la Peña y Montenegro</a>, initiated the first preliminary steps towards the <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of Paredes as well as her niece, Sebastiana de Caso, and instituted the process of inquiring into and collecting evidence for the sanctity of her life, her virtues and her miracles. King <a href="/wiki/Charles_II_of_Spain" title="Charles II of Spain">Charles II of Spain</a> took up the cause of their canonization, in an effort to promote the connection of native-born colonists in the Americas with the Spanish nation, as well as proving the faith of the colonial population.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Divine_Worship_and_the_Discipline_of_the_Sacraments" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments">Sacred Congregation of Rites</a>, having discussed and approved of this process, decided in favor of the formal introduction of the cause, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV" title="Pope Benedict XIV">Pope Benedict XIV</a> signed the commission for introducing the cause on December 17, 1757. After the <a href="/wiki/Suppression_of_the_Society_of_Jesus" title="Suppression of the Society of Jesus">Suppression of the Society of Jesus</a> throughout the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Empire" title="Spanish Empire">Spanish Empire</a> in 1767, the cause for Paredes' canonization was taken over by the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish Crown">Spanish Crown</a>, which appointed a Chilean priest as <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">Postulator</a> of the cause to the Holy See. Her veneration was encouraged in the various Spanish colonies of South America.
</p><p>The Apostolic process concerning the virtues of Mariana de Paredes was drawn up and examined in due form by the two <a href="/wiki/Historical_process_of_beatification_and_canonization" class="mw-redirect" title="Historical process of beatification and canonization">Preparatory Congregations and by the General Congregation of Rites</a>, and orders were given by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> for the publication of the decree attesting the <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic character of her virtues</a>. The process concerning the two miracles wrought through the intercession of this servant of God was subsequently prepared and was examined and accepted by the three congregations, and formally approved on January 11, 1817, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The General Congregation having decided in favor of proceeding to the beatification, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> commanded the Brief of beatification to be prepared. <a href="/wiki/Peter_Jan_Beckx" title="Peter Jan Beckx">Peter Jan Beckx</a>, <a href="/wiki/Superior_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Superior General">Superior General</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, petitioned the <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Costantino_Patrizi_Naro" title="Costantino Patrizi Naro">Costantino Patrizi Naro</a> to order the publication of the Brief, and his request was granted. The Brief was read and the solemn beatification took place in the <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">Basilica of St Peter</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on November 10, 1853. Many miracles have been claimed to have been the reward of those who have invoked her intercession, especially in America. An additional miracle having been confirmed by the General Congregation, her canonization was approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> and celebrated in 1950.
</p>

<p>Since Parades was beatified, many <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parishes</a> and educational facilities have been placed under her patronage  throughout Latin America. A <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">congregation</a> of teaching <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sister" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sister">Religious Sisters</a> named for her was founded in 1873 by <a href="/wiki/Mercedes_de_Jes%C3%BAs_Molina" title="Mercedes de Jesús Molina">Mercedes de Jesús Molina</a>.
</p><p>Christ Child Crucified on Mount Pichincha or Holy Child of Mount Pichincha is a Roman Catholic image of the Christ Child crucified that appeared to Mariana de Jesús de Paredes.
</p>
