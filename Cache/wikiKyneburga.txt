					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Canonized 7th-century Mercian princesses</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saints Kyneburga, Kyneswide and Tibba</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Abbesses</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">7th century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican Church">Anglican Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">Canonized</a></th><td class="infobox-data">Pre-Congregation</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">6 March</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Kyneburga, Kyneswide and Tibba</b> were female members of the <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercian</a> royal family in 7th century <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a> who were venerated as saints.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Kyneburga_and_Kyneswide">Kyneburga and Kyneswide</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kyneburga,_Kyneswide_and_Tibba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Kyneburga and Kyneswide">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><b>Kyneburga</b> (d. c. 680) (also called Cyneburh in Old English); the name being also rendered as <i>Kinborough</i> and in occasional use as a Christian name<sup id="cite_ref-Withycombe_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Withycombe-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) and <b>Kyneswide</b> (Cyneswitha) were sisters, the daughters of <a href="/wiki/King_Penda_of_Mercia" class="mw-redirect" title="King Penda of Mercia">King Penda of Mercia</a> (who remained true to <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a>). She was eldest daughter of Penda. Although her father was an opponent of Christianity, she and all her siblings converted.
<a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> wrote that Penda tolerated the preaching of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> in <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a> itself, despite his own beliefs:
</p>
<dl><dd>"Nor did King Penda obstruct the preaching of the word among his people, the Mercians, if any were willing to hear it; but, on the contrary, he hated and despised those whom he perceived not to perform the works of faith, when they had once received the faith, saying, <i>They were contemptible and wretched who did not obey their God, in whom they believed</i>.</dd></dl>
<p>This was begun two years before the death of King Penda.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Their mother was Queen Cyneswise. Tibba is believed to have been a <a href="/wiki/Kinship" title="Kinship">relative</a>.
</p><p>Kyneburga married <a href="/wiki/Alhfrith_of_Deira" class="mw-redirect" title="Alhfrith of Deira">Alhfrith of Deira</a>, co-regent of <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbria</a> (who attended the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a> in 664),<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  and later founded an <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> for both <a href="/wiki/Monks" class="mw-redirect" title="Monks">monks</a> and <a href="/wiki/Nuns" class="mw-redirect" title="Nuns">nuns</a> in <a href="/wiki/Castor,_Cambridgeshire" title="Castor, Cambridgeshire">Castor</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Soke_of_Peterborough" title="Soke of Peterborough">Soke of Peterborough</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She became  the first <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> and was later joined by Kyneswide and Tibba. Kyneswide succeeded Kyneburga as abbess and she was later succeeded by <b>Tibba</b>. She was buried in her church, but the remains of Kyneburga and Kyneswide were translated, before 972,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> to Peterborough Abbey, now <a href="/wiki/Peterborough_Cathedral" title="Peterborough Cathedral">Peterborough Cathedral</a>.
</p><p>Kyneburga had been one of the signatories, together with her brother <a href="/wiki/Wulfhere" class="mw-redirect" title="Wulfhere">Wulfhere</a>, of the founding charter of Burh Abbey, dated 664, per <a href="/wiki/William_Dugdale" title="William Dugdale">William Dugdale</a>'s <i>Monasticon</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> (Burh Abbey was later dedicated to St Peter, becoming "Peterborough"). She was much esteemed as a saint by the monks of Peterborough, and features as one of the saints remembered annually on 6 March in several ancient Peterborough-produced Kalendars,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> (a section of  a psalter).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She died on 15 September AD 680 and was buried at Castor where she soon became revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. In 963 <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">her body</a> was moved to <a href="/wiki/Peterborough" title="Peterborough">Peterborough</a>, with those of her sister, Cuneswitha, and their kins woman, Tibba. Her remains were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Thorney_Abbey" title="Thorney Abbey">Thorney Abbey</a> some time later. Her feast day is celebrated on 6 March.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is remembered in a chapel at Peterborough Cathedral, the 12th century St Kyneburga's parish church in Castor, Lady Conyburrow's Way (a ridge in a field near Castor), Kimberwell spring, <a href="/wiki/Bedfordshire" title="Bedfordshire">Bedfordshire</a>, the villages of <a href="/wiki/Kimberley,_Norfolk" title="Kimberley, Norfolk">Kimberley, Norfolk</a> and <a href="/wiki/West_Yorkshire" title="West Yorkshire">West Yorkshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There was another lady by the name of Kyneburg, the wife of <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Northumbria" title="Oswald of Northumbria">Oswald of Northumbria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Tibba">Tibba</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kyneburga,_Kyneswide_and_Tibba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Tibba">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Tibba, patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Falconry" title="Falconry">falconers</a>, is believed to have lived at <a href="/wiki/Ryhall" title="Ryhall">Ryhall</a>, Rutland, in the 7th century. She was buried there, but in the 11th century her relics were translated to Peterborough Abbey, by Abbot Ælfsige (1006&#8211;1042).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> According to legend, St Tibba was a niece of <a href="/wiki/Penda_of_Mercia" title="Penda of Mercia">King Penda</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>  The remains of a small <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> associated with the saint can be seen on the west side of the north aisle of Ryhall church.
</p><p>There was at Ryhall a shrine and a <a href="/wiki/Holy_well" title="Holy well">holy well</a> dedicated to Saint Tibba. Robert Charles Hope placed the location on the brow of Tibbal's Hill (Tibb's-well-hill), "upon the hill going from <a href="/wiki/Tolethorpe_Hall" title="Tolethorpe Hall">Tolethorpe</a> to <a href="/wiki/Belmesthorpe" class="mw-redirect" title="Belmesthorpe">Belmsford</a> Bridge".<sup id="cite_ref-Hope_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hope-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tibba had a cousin Eabba, who lived with her. Hope suggests the holy well dedicated to her was just north of Tibba's, on the other side of a ford of the <a href="/wiki/River_Gwash" title="River Gwash">River Gwash</a> and the name "St. Eabba's-well-ford was corrupted to Stableford when a bridge was later built there. St. Eabba's well came to be called by local shepherds "Jacob's well".<sup id="cite_ref-Hope_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hope-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> (For the relationship between St Tibba and St Ebba ("Domne Eafe"), see e.g. Rollason, D.W., <i>The Mildrith Legend A Study in Early Medieval Hagiography in England</i>, Leicester University Press, 1982, p.&#160;77)
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Translation">Translation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Kyneburga,_Kyneswide_and_Tibba&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Translation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Originally buried at <a href="/wiki/Castor,_Cambridgeshire" title="Castor, Cambridgeshire">Castor</a> and Ryhall, their <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> were bought in the 10th century by Peterborough Abbey under the direction of  Abbot Aelfsige of Peterborough, as part of a <a href="/wiki/Peterborough_Abbey#Monastic_life" class="mw-redirect" title="Peterborough Abbey">policy of relic acquisition</a> by the abbey. Their relics at the abbey were lost or destroyed in the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">Reformation</a>.
</p>
