<p><b>Didacus of Alcalá</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Diego de Alcalá</i>), also known as <b>Diego de San Nicolás</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Spaniards" title="Spaniards">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> who served as among the first group of missionaries to the newly conquered <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>. He died at <a href="/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares" title="Alcalá de Henares">Alcalá de Henares</a> on 12 November 1463 and is now honored by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg/170px-Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg/255px-Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg/340px-Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5050" data-file-height="4888" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Augustins_-_San_Diego_de_Alcala_en_extase_devant_la_croix_-_Murillo_-_D_1846_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Didacus in Ecstasy Before the Cross</i> by <a href="/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Murillo" title="Bartolomé Esteban Murillo">Murillo</a>, 1645–46</div></div></div>
<p>Didacus was born <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1400</span> into a poor but pious family in the small village of <a href="/wiki/San_Nicol%C3%A1s_del_Puerto" title="San Nicolás del Puerto">San Nicolás del Puerto</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Seville" title="Kingdom of Seville">Kingdom of Seville</a>. As a child, he embraced the <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> life and, later, placed himself under the direction of a hermit priest living not far from his native town. He then led the life of a wandering hermit. Feeling called to the religious life, he applied for admission to the Observant (or Reformed) branch of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">Order of Friars Minor</a> at the friary in <a href="/wiki/Albaida,_Valencia" class="mw-redirect" title="Albaida, Valencia">Albaida</a> and was sent to the friary in Arruzafa, near <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain" title="Córdoba, Spain">Córdoba</a>, where he was received as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his years living in that location, he journeyed to the villages in the regions surrounding Córdoba, Cádiz and Seville, where he would preach to the people. A strong devotion to him still exists in those towns.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary">Missionary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Missionary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Didacus was sent to the new friary of the Order in <a href="/wiki/Arrecife" title="Arrecife">Arrecife</a> on the island of <a href="/wiki/Lanzarote" title="Lanzarote">Lanzarote</a>, part of the <a href="/wiki/Canary_Islands" title="Canary Islands">Canary Islands</a>. That island had been conquered by <a href="/wiki/Jean_de_B%C3%A9thencourt" title="Jean de Béthencourt">Jean de Béthencourt</a> about 40 years earlier and was still in the process of introducing the native <a href="/wiki/Guanches" title="Guanches">Guanche</a> people to Christianity. He was assigned to the post of <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">porter</a>.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg/170px-Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="266" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg/255px-Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg/340px-Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="705" data-file-height="1102" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Bernardo_Strozzi_-_The_Miracle_of_St_Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>The Miracle of Didacus of Alcalá</i> by <a href="/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi" title="Bernardo Strozzi">Bernardo Strozzi</a></div></div></div>
<p>In 1445, Didacus was appointed as <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Guardian</a> of the Franciscan community on the island of <a href="/wiki/Fuerteventura" title="Fuerteventura">Fuerteventura</a>, where the Observant Franciscans soon founded the Friary of St. <a href="/wiki/Bonaventure" title="Bonaventure">Bonaventure</a>. There, though it was an exception to the ordinary rules for a lay brother to be named to this position, his great zeal, prudence, and sanctity justified this choice.
</p><p>In 1450, Didacus was recalled to Spain, from which point he went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to share in the <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christianity)#Subsequent_Jubilees" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christianity)">Jubilee Year</a> proclaimed by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V" title="Pope Nicholas V">Pope Nicholas V</a>, and to be present at the <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bernardine_of_Siena" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernardine of Siena">Bernardine of Siena</a>. In addition to the vast crowds of <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> arriving in Rome for Jubilee Year, thousands of friars had headed to Rome to take part in the celebration of one of the pillars of their Order. These travelers brought with them various infections, which broke out into an epidemic in the city. Didacus spent three months caring for the sick at the friary attached to the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Ara_Coeli" title="Santa Maria in Ara Coeli">Santa Maria in Ara Coeli</a>, and his biographers record the miraculous cure of many whom he attended through his pious intercession. He was then recalled again to Spain and was sent by his superiors to the Friary of Santa María de Jesús in Alcalá, where he spent the remaining years of his life in penance, solitude, and the delights of contemplation. There he died on 12 November 1463 due to an <a href="/wiki/Abscess" title="Abscess">abscess</a>. It was said that it amazed everyone that instead of a foul odor, fragrance emitted from his infection. His body was also rumored to have <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">remained incorrupt</a>, did not undergo <a href="/wiki/Rigor_mortis" title="Rigor mortis">rigor mortis</a> and continued to emit a pleasant odor.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A chapel, the Ermita de San Diego, was built in Didacus's birthplace between 1485 and 1514 to enshrine his remains in his native town.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sandiegojf.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Sandiegojf.JPG/170px-Sandiegojf.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Sandiegojf.JPG/255px-Sandiegojf.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Sandiegojf.JPG/340px-Sandiegojf.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="4608" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sandiegojf.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Side altar and icon of San Diego de Alcalá in <a href="/wiki/San_Diego_de_Alcala_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="San Diego de Alcala Church">San Diego de Alcala Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>, dedicated to him</div></div></div>
<p>Didacus was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_V" title="Pope Sixtus V">Pope Sixtus V</a> in 1588, the first after a long hiatus following the Reformation, and the first of a lay brother of the Order of Friars Minor. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 13 November, since 12 November, the anniversary of his death, was occupied, first by that of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_I" title="Pope Martin I">Pope Saint Martin I</a>, then by that of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Basil" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Basil">Basilian monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Catholic">Eastern Catholic</a> bishop and martyr, <a href="/wiki/Josaphat_Kuntsevych" title="Josaphat Kuntsevych">Josaphat Kuntsevych</a>. Until 1969, the Franciscans celebrated his feast day on 12 November.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In the United States the feast day is celebrated on 7 November, due to the feast of St. <a href="/wiki/Frances_Xavier_Cabrini" title="Frances Xavier Cabrini">Frances Xavier Cabrini</a>.
</p><p>Didacus is the saint to whom the <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Diego_de_Alcal%C3%A1" title="Mission San Diego de Alcalá">Franciscan mission</a> that bears his name, and which developed into the City of <a href="/wiki/San_Diego" title="San Diego">San Diego</a>, <a href="/wiki/California" title="California">California</a>, was dedicated.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He is thereby the patron of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_San_Diego" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of San Diego">Roman Catholic Diocese of San Diego</a>.
</p><p>The Spanish painter <a href="/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_Est%C3%A9ban_Murillo" class="mw-redirect" title="Bartolomé Estéban Murillo">Bartolomé Estéban Murillo</a> is noted for painting several representations of Didacus of Alcalá.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>On a hunting trip, <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_Castile" title="Henry IV of Castile">Henry IV of Castile</a> fell from his horse and injured his arm. In intense pain and with his doctors unable to relieve his agony, he went to Alcalá and prayed to Didacus for a cure. The saint's body was removed from his casket and placed beside the king. Henry then kissed the body and placed the saint's hand on his injured arm. The king felt the pain disappear and his arm immediately regained its former strength.<sup id="cite_ref-MPS_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MPS-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Don_Carlos,_Prince_of_Asturias" class="mw-redirect" title="Don Carlos, Prince of Asturias">Don Carlos, Prince of Asturias</a>, son of King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain">Philip II of Spain</a>, was of a difficult and rebellious character. On the night of 19 April 1562, he was groping around in the dark after a night spent with some ladies when he fell down a flight of stairs and landed on his head. There he was found the next morning, unconscious and partially <a href="/wiki/Paralysis" title="Paralysis">paralyzed</a>. He later became blind, developed a high fever and his head swelled to an enormous size. In a moment of lucidity, he asked that he wanted to make a personal petition to St. Didacus. The saint's body was brought to his chambers. The prior of the convent placed one of Carlos' hands upon the chest of St. Didacus, whereupon the prince fell into a deep and peaceful sleep. Six hours later, he awoke and related that in a dream, he saw the saint telling him that he would not die. The prince recovered from his brush with death.<sup id="cite_ref-MPS_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MPS-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Other sources have Didacus's body laid on the prince's bed.<sup id="cite_ref-King_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mechanical_model">Mechanical model</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Mechanical model">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Automaton_in_the_form_of_a_monk,_probably_Spain,_possibly_circle_of_Juanelo_Turriano,_c._1550,_hardwood,_enamel,_leather,_metals,_paint_-_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_-_New_York_City_-_DSC07103.jpg" class="image"><img alt="A wooden figure. Gears are visible on the right." src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Automaton_in_the_form_of_a_monk%2C_probably_Spain%2C_possibly_circle_of_Juanelo_Turriano%2C_c._1550%2C_hardwood%2C_enamel%2C_leather%2C_metals%2C_paint_-_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_-_New_York_City_-_DSC07103.jpg/220px-thumbnail.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Automaton_in_the_form_of_a_monk%2C_probably_Spain%2C_possibly_circle_of_Juanelo_Turriano%2C_c._1550%2C_hardwood%2C_enamel%2C_leather%2C_metals%2C_paint_-_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_-_New_York_City_-_DSC07103.jpg/330px-thumbnail.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Automaton_in_the_form_of_a_monk%2C_probably_Spain%2C_possibly_circle_of_Juanelo_Turriano%2C_c._1550%2C_hardwood%2C_enamel%2C_leather%2C_metals%2C_paint_-_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_-_New_York_City_-_DSC07103.jpg/440px-thumbnail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3415" data-file-height="4806" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Automaton_in_the_form_of_a_monk,_probably_Spain,_possibly_circle_of_Juanelo_Turriano,_c._1550,_hardwood,_enamel,_leather,_metals,_paint_-_Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art_-_New_York_City_-_DSC07103.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The monk automaton exhibited in the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art" title="Metropolitan Museum of Art">Metropolitan Museum of Art</a> in 2020.</div></div></div>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution" title="Smithsonian Institution">Smithsonian Institution</a> holds a clockwork automaton of a monk.
The model would perform a number of set actions, including the beating of the breast which accompanies the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Mea_culpa" title="Mea culpa">Mea culpa</a></i></span> prayer.
A possible provenance would be a presumed model of Didacus, commissioned by Philip II of Spain to <a href="/wiki/Juanelo_Turriano" class="mw-redirect" title="Juanelo Turriano">Juanelo Turriano</a>, mechanic to <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Charles_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Charles V">Emperor Charles V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-King_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-King-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Historical theories for why the friar was built include that: Philip II wished to share the miracle of his son's recovery with his people; or the clockwork friar provided a portable model of "how to pray" which could be displayed around the kingdom.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
