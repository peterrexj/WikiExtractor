<p><b>Jacopone da Todi</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor" title="Order of Friars Minor">O.F.M.</a> (ca. 1230 &#8211; 25 December 1306) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> from <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>. He wrote several <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laude_(Jacopone_da_Todi)" class="extiw" title="it:Laude (Jacopone da Todi)"><i>laude</i></a> (songs in praise of the <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">Lord</a>) in the local vernacular. He was an early pioneer in Italian <a href="/wiki/Theatre" title="Theatre">theatre</a>, being one of the earliest <a href="/wiki/Scholars" class="mw-redirect" title="Scholars">scholars</a> who dramatised <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospel</a> subjects.

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacopone_da_Todi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born <b>Jacopo dei Benedetti</b>, he was a member of a minor noble family. He studied <a href="/wiki/Law" title="Law">law</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> and became a successful <a href="/wiki/Lawyer" title="Lawyer">lawyer</a>. At some point in his late 20s, he married a young noblewoman, Vanna di Bernardino di Guidone, who according to some accounts, was a pious and generous woman. Due to his reputation as a worldly and greedy man, she took it upon herself to <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortify</a> her flesh in atonement for his behavior.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Not long after their wedding, Benedetti urged his wife to attend a public tournament. In the course of the spectacle, she was killed when part of the stand in which she was sitting gave way. Rushing to her side, he discovered that she had been wearing a <a href="/wiki/Cilice" title="Cilice">hairshirt</a>. The shock of his wife's death and the evidence of her secret penance made made such an impression on him that he abandoned his profession and became a Franciscan tertiary. 
</p><p>Benedetti gave up his legal practice, gave away all his possessions and from about 1268 lived as a wandering <a href="/wiki/Ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetic">ascetic</a>, joining the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>. During this period, he gained a reputation as a madman, due to his eccentric behavior, acting out his spiritual vision, earning him the nickname he was to embrace of <b>Jacopone</b>. Examples of this behavior included appearing in the public square of Todi, wearing a saddle and crawling on all fours. On another occasion, he appeared at a wedding in his brother's house, tarred and feathered from head to toe.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG/220px-Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG/330px-Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG/440px-Todi_piazza_del_popolo_2.JPG 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="375" /></a><figcaption>The Piazza del Popolo in Todi, where Jacopone crawled around on one occasion</figcaption></figure>
<p>After about ten years of this life, Benedetti sought admission to the Friars Minor, but they were reluctant to accept him due to his reputation. He soon composed a beautiful poem on the vanities of the world, which led to his admission into the Order in 1278. He chose to live as a <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By this time, two broad factions had arisen in the Franciscan Order, one with a more lenient, less <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystical</a> attitude and one being more severe, preaching absolute poverty and <a href="/wiki/Penitence" class="mw-redirect" title="Penitence">penitence</a> (known as the "Spirituals" or <i><a href="/wiki/Fraticelli" title="Fraticelli">Fraticelli</a></i>). Benedetti believed that the friars’ engagement with the University of Paris undermined the concepts of <i>humilitas</i> and <i>simplicitas</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was connected with the latter group and in 1294 they sent a deputation to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_V" title="Pope Celestine V">Pope Celestine V</a> to ask permission to live separately from the other friars and to observe the Franciscan Rule in its perfection. The request was granted but Celestine resigned the papacy before action was taken and was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII" title="Pope Boniface VIII">Pope Boniface VIII</a>, who opposed the more rigorous views. 
</p><p>During the struggle that followed, Benedetti publicized the Spirituals' cause by writing verses highly critical of their opponents, the pope included. When two brother-cardinals, the Colonnas, sided with the Spirituals and with the king of France against Pope Boniface, and Benedetti gave his support to the Colonnas, politics and even war entered upon the scene. The pope <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunicated</a> them. A battle between the two rival parties ensued, ending with the <a href="/wiki/Siege" title="Siege">siege</a> of <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> and the imprisonment and excommunication of Benedetti in 1298. He was freed in 1303 upon the death of Boniface, having been specifically excluded from the <a href="/wiki/Jubilee_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jubilee (Christian)">Jubilee Year</a> of 1300 by <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Broken and in poor health, Benedetti retired to <a href="/wiki/Collazzone" title="Collazzone">Collazzone</a>, a small town situated on a hill between <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> and Todi, where he was cared for by a community of <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clares</a>. His condition deteriorated toward the end of 1306, and he sent word requesting that his old friend, <a href="/wiki/John_of_La_Verna" title="John of La Verna">John of La Verna</a>, come to give him the <a href="/wiki/Last_rites" title="Last rites">last rites</a>. John arrived on Christmas Eve and comforted him, as he died about midnight.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Benedetti's body was originally buried in the monastery church. In 1433 his grave was discovered and his remains transferred to a crypt in the Franciscan Church of San Fortunato in Todi.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Poetry">Poetry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacopone_da_Todi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Poetry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benedetti's satirical and denunciatory Laude witness to the troubled times of the warring <a href="/wiki/City-states" class="mw-redirect" title="City-states">city-states</a> of northern Italy and the material and spiritual crisis that accompanied them. The <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laude_(Jacopone_da_Todi)" class="extiw" title="it:Laude (Jacopone da Todi)">laude</a> are written in his native Umbrian <a href="/wiki/Dialect" title="Dialect">dialect</a> and represent the popular poetry of the region. Many hundreds of manuscripts attest to the broad popularity of his poems in many contexts - although anonymous poems are often attributed to him by the tradition. Other laude extol the spiritual value of poverty.
</p><p>Some of his laude were especially in use among the so-called Laudesi and the <a href="/wiki/Flagellants" class="mw-redirect" title="Flagellants">Flagellants</a>, who sang them in the towns, along the roads, in their confraternities and in sacred dramatical representations. With hindsight, the use of the laude may be seen as an early seed of Italian drama that came to fruition in later centuries.
</p><p>The Latin poem <a href="/wiki/Stabat_Mater" title="Stabat Mater"><i>Stabat Mater Dolorosa</i></a> is generally attributed to Benedetti, although this has been disputed. It is a fine example of religious <a href="/wiki/Lyric_poetry" title="Lyric poetry">lyric</a> in the Franciscan tradition. It was inserted into the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Missal" title="Roman Missal">Roman Missal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Breviary" title="Breviary">Breviary</a> in 1727  for the <i>Feast of the Seven Sorrows of the Blessed Virgin Mary</i>, celebrated on the Friday before <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a>. Following changes by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a>, it now appears on the <i>Feast of Our Lady's Sorrows</i> celebrated on 15 September. Many composers have set it to music, including <a href="/wiki/Josquin_des_Prez" title="Josquin des Prez">Josquin des Prez</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Palestrina" class="mw-redirect" title="Giovanni Palestrina">Giovanni Palestrina</a>,  <a href="/wiki/Alessandro_Scarlatti" title="Alessandro Scarlatti">Alessandro Scarlatti</a>,  <a href="/wiki/Domenico_Scarlatti" title="Domenico Scarlatti">Domenico Scarlatti</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Pergolesi" title="Giovanni Battista Pergolesi">Giovanni Battista Pergolesi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gioacchino_Rossini" class="mw-redirect" title="Gioacchino Rossini">Gioacchino Rossini</a>, <a href="/wiki/Toivo_Kuula" title="Toivo Kuula">Toivo Kuula</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k" title="Antonín Dvořák">Antonín Dvořák</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ern%C5%91_Dohn%C3%A1nyi" class="mw-redirect" title="Ernő Dohnányi">Ernő Dohnányi</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacopone_da_Todi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Todi037.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Todi037.jpg/220px-Todi037.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="292" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Todi037.jpg/330px-Todi037.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Todi037.jpg/440px-Todi037.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1952" data-file-height="2592" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Jacopone in <a href="/wiki/San_Fortunato,_Todi" title="San Fortunato, Todi">San Fortunato, Todi</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>From the time of his death, Benedetti was considered to have been a saint by his followers, both within and outside of the Franciscan Order. He is honored as <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a> within the Order.
</p><p>Several attempts were made over the centuries to have the Catholic Church recognize his sanctity. In the 17th century, both the City Council and the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_chapter" title="Cathedral chapter">cathedral chapter</a> of Todi petitioned the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> to do so. In the 19th century, the <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">Postulator</a> for the causes of saints of the Order of Friars Minor collected documents for this step.
</p><p>To date, however, the church has never formally approved this devotion. One possible reason for this may be the conflict between Benedetti and Pope Boniface VIII.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jacopone_da_Todi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Benedetti was steadfast in condemning corruption, especially through his satirical Italian poems. Benedetti would not recant his position on the requirement of ascetic poverty, believing that the mainstream church had become corrupt and that its ministers were not interested in the welfare of the poor. It was a time of <a href="/wiki/Famine" title="Famine">famine</a> and poverty in Italy and many mystics and preachers like <a href="/wiki/Gioacchino_da_Fiore" class="mw-redirect" title="Gioacchino da Fiore">Gioacchino da Fiore</a> anticipated the end of the world and the coming of <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>. They also said kings and clergy had become too attached to material goods and too interested in their personal wars rather than the welfare of the country.
</p><p>Benedetti's preaching attracted many enthusiasts and <a href="/wiki/Dante" class="mw-redirect" title="Dante">Dante</a> praised him in his <a href="/wiki/Paradiso_(Dante)" title="Paradiso (Dante)">Paradiso</a>.
</p>
