<p><b>Cunigunde of Luxembourg</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">OSB</a>  (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Kunigunde</i>) (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 975 – 3 March 1033), also called <b>Cunegundes</b>, <b>Cunegunda</b>, and <b>Cunegonda</b> and, in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <b>Cunegundis</b><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Kinigundis</b>, was Empress of the Holy Roman Empire by marriage to <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a>. She ruled as interim <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">regent</a> after the death of her spouse in 1024. She is a saint and the <a href="/wiki/Patroness" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness">patroness</a> of <a href="/wiki/Luxembourg" title="Luxembourg">Luxembourg</a>; her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 3 March.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cunigunde_of_Luxembourg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Kunigunde.png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Kunigunde.png/220px-Kunigunde.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="294" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Kunigunde.png/330px-Kunigunde.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Kunigunde.png/440px-Kunigunde.png 2x" data-file-width="1551" data-file-height="2073" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Kunigunde.png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of St. Cunigunde as Holy Roman Empress, in <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a>.</div></div></div> 
<p>Cunigunde was one of eleven children born to <a href="/wiki/Siegfried_I_of_Luxembourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Siegfried I of Luxembourg">Siegfried I of Luxembourg</a> (922 – 15 August 998) and a woman called Hedwig. Numerous genealogists have tried to find out which Hedwig it is; there are different views. One of the most famous theses regarding her identity is from Joseph Depoin, who claims that Hedwig is the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Gilbert,_Duke_of_Lorraine" title="Gilbert, Duke of Lorraine">Duke Gilbert of Lorraine</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Gerberga_of_Saxony" title="Gerberga of Saxony">Gerberga of Saxony</a>, the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler" title="Henry the Fowler">Henry I</a>. If this is correct, then Cunigunde married her distant cousin, but <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a> was strongly against consanguineous marriage, so it can be assumed that this is not the case.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It can also often be read that she is the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Berthold_of_Schweinfurt" title="Berthold of Schweinfurt">Berthold of Schweinfurt</a> from the House of <a href="/wiki/Babenberg" title="Babenberg">Babenberg</a> and Eilika of Walbeck, daughter of Count Lothar.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Cunigunde's paternal grandmother, also called Cunigunde, was a Carolingian. So Cunigunde of Luxembourg was a seventh-generation descendant of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>. She married <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor_Henry_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Emperor Henry II">King Henry</a> in 999.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It is said that she had long wanted to be a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-calendar_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-calendar-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and that her marriage to Henry II was a spiritual one (also called a "<a href="/wiki/Mariage_blanc" title="Mariage blanc">white marriage</a>"); that is, they married for companionship alone, and by mutual agreement did not consummate their relationship.  It has been claimed that Cunigunde made a vow of virginity with Henry's consent prior to their marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-gardenofmary.com_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gardenofmary.com-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The truth of this is debatable; while the couple were both certainly childless, it is supposed by some authors that later <a href="/wiki/Hagiographers" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographers">hagiographers</a> mistakenly construed the fact to imply a virginal marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Others, however, accept that the marriage was purely platonic.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During their marriage, her husband, <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor_Henry_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Emperor Henry II">Henry II</a>, then only <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Bavaria">Duke of Bavaria</a>, was crowned <a href="/wiki/King_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Germany">king of Germany</a> ("Rex Romanorum"). The couple were crowned on 9 July 1002 in <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, by <a href="/wiki/Willigis" title="Willigis">Willigis</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Mainz" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Mainz">Archbishop of Mainz</a>. Cunigunde was crowned on 10 August<sup id="cite_ref-gardenofmary.com_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gardenofmary.com-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 1002<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, also by Willigis. It is the first known crowning of a German queen.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Her predecessors were married to a king who was already crowned and therefore were not crowned alone. They directly were crowned empress in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Later, her husband was also crowned as <a href="/wiki/King_of_Italy" title="King of Italy">king of Italy</a> on 15 May 1004 in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, but no evidence has been given of her being crowned as queen of Italy.
</p><p>It appears that Cunigunde was active politically. The title <i>consors regni</i> (meaning "partner in the rule") for the wives of the Ottonian rulers was often used in <a href="/wiki/Charter" title="Charter">charters</a>. In one-third of <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry</a>'s remaining charters, Cunigunde emerges as an advocate or initiator.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> As the closest adviser of her husband, she took part in Imperial councils.  She is also reported to have exerted an influence on her husband in his endowments of land to the Church.  These included the cathedral and monastery at <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.
</p><p>Cunigunde traveled with her husband to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for his coronation as <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Holy Roman Emperor</a> ("Romanorum Imperator") as was the tradition for the <a href="/wiki/King_of_Germany" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Germany">King of Germany</a>, and was crowned as <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empress" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Empress">Holy Roman Empress</a><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> with him on 14 February 1014 in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, receiving together with Henry the <a href="/wiki/Imperial_Crown" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperial Crown">Imperial Crown</a> from the hands of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_VIII" title="Pope Benedict VIII">Pope Benedict VIII</a>.  During her reign she suffered from a grave illness and made a vow that if she were to regain her health, she would found a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery at <a href="/wiki/Kassel" title="Kassel">Kassel</a>.  Upon her recovery, she kept her oath and work began on the building;<sup id="cite_ref-calendar_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-calendar-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  however, Henry died in 1024 before it was finished.  Upon his death, Cunigunde was obliged to assume the office of <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">Regent</a> of the Empire.  This she did with her brother, and later handed over the Imperial insignia when <a href="/wiki/Conrad_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Conrad II, Holy Roman Emperor">Conrad II</a> was elected to succeed her late husband on 8 September 1024.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_life_and_death">Religious life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cunigunde_of_Luxembourg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Religious life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg/220px-Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg/330px-Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg/440px-Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2048" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Crown_of_Cunigude.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Crown of the Empress Cunigunde</div></div></div>
<p>As a widow, Cunigunde was left comparatively poor, owing to the enormous wealth given away by her and Henry in charitable works.<sup id="cite_ref-hugoh_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hugoh-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1025, exactly one year after the death of her husband, Cunigunde retired to <a href="/wiki/Kaufungen_Abbey" title="Kaufungen Abbey">Kaufungen Abbey</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Hesse" title="Hesse">Hesse</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, where she entered the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> she had founded there.  At the dedication of the monastery, she offered a relic of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a>, removed her regalia, and donned the habit of the nun.  There she remained at the monastery, performing charitable works, caring for the sick and devoting her time to prayer.  She died on 3 March 1033 at <a href="/wiki/Kaufungen" title="Kaufungen">Kaufungen</a>. She was buried at <a href="/wiki/Bamberg_Cathedral" title="Bamberg Cathedral">Bamberg Cathedral</a> beside her husband, but may have been buried elsewhere first and then re-interred at the Cathedral in 1201 after her canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-saints.sqpn.com_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saints.sqpn.com-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization_and_veneration">Canonization and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cunigunde_of_Luxembourg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Canonization and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG/220px-BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="158" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG/330px-BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG/440px-BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2695" data-file-height="1940" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:BambergDom-GrabHeinrichKunigunde.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Emperor Henry II and Empress Cunigunde's tomb by <a href="/wiki/Tilman_Riemenschneider" title="Tilman Riemenschneider">Tilman Riemenschneider</a>.</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg/200px-Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="263" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg/300px-Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg 2x" data-file-width="397" data-file-height="523" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Ordeal_by_red-hot_iron.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>After being accused of adultery Cunigunde proved her innocence by walking over red-hot <a href="/wiki/Ploughshare" class="mw-redirect" title="Ploughshare">ploughshares</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>Cunigunde was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a> on  29 March 1200, 53 years after the canonization of her husband <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor_Henry_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman Emperor Henry II">Henry II</a> in July 1147. To prepare a case for canonization her biography was compiled.  This and the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">Papal bull</a> for her canonization relate several instances of miracles to have been worked by the Empress.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One of these relates how, when <a href="/wiki/Calumniator" class="mw-redirect" title="Calumniator">calumniators</a> accused her of scandalous conduct, her innocence was signally vindicated by <a href="/wiki/Divine_providence" title="Divine providence">divine providence</a> as she walked over pieces of flaming irons without injury, to the great joy of her husband, the Emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-hugoh_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hugoh-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>  Another tells of Cunigunde falling asleep one night and being carried into bed. Her maid also fell asleep and a candle set the bed on <a href="/wiki/Fire" title="Fire">fire</a>. The blaze awoke both of them and upon Cunigunde executing the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Sign of the Cross">Sign of the Cross</a>, the fire immediately disappeared, saving them from burning. A final legend tells of one of Cunigunde's nieces, Judith, the abbess of <a href="/wiki/Kaufungen_Abbey" title="Kaufungen Abbey">Kaufungen Abbey</a>.  A frivolous young woman, Judith preferred feasting and carousing with the young sisters to the Sabbath rituals.  Cunigunde remonstrated with her, to little effect.  Finally Cunigunde became so vexed with her niece that she slapped her across the face; the marks remained on her face for the rest of her life, serving as a warning to those of the community who would not take their vows or observances seriously.
</p><p>Cunigunde is widely venerated. As well as churches that are dedicated to her, such as St. Cunegunda Church in <a href="/wiki/Detroit" title="Detroit">Detroit</a>, USA,  <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Bamberg" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Bamberg">Archdiocese of Bamberg</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>, she is the <a href="/wiki/Patroness" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness">Patroness</a> of <a href="/wiki/Luxembourg" title="Luxembourg">Luxembourg</a>, where the parish church of <a href="/wiki/Clausen,_Luxembourg" title="Clausen, Luxembourg">Clausen</a> (which has on Sundays and holy days a celebration of the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Mass" title="Tridentine Mass">Tridentine Mass</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> is dedicated to her.
</p><p>Saint Cunigunde of Luxembourg is venerarted in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> on <a href="/wiki/March_3_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="March 3 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">3 March</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reception_and_iconography">Reception and iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cunigunde_of_Luxembourg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Reception and iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As well as in his political life, <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a> often included his wife in his imperial representation.
</p><p>Her participation in the reign of her husband is underlined on the dedication picture of the <a href="/wiki/Pericopes_of_Henry_II" title="Pericopes of Henry II">Pericopes of Henry II</a>. The couple commissioned the book in 1012 to the monks on the island Reichenau in <a href="/wiki/Lake_Constance" title="Lake Constance">Lake Constance</a>, which was a leading center for <a href="/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript" title="Illuminated manuscript">book illumination</a> at the time. With its precious cover and the 28 full-page miniatures, this is one of the most important works of book art at the time. On the dedication picture, the coronation of the couple by <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus Christ</a> is shown. The couple is led by the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">Apostles</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a>. <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry</a> is shown with an <a href="/wiki/Globus_cruciger" title="Globus cruciger">orb</a> and scepter. The role of Cunigunde, who is represented in the same size as her husband, consists in the hand movement, pointing directly to Christ. This active hand movement can be seen as a sign of her political action readiness.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> In the accompanying dedicatory poem, she is referred to as co-regent (<i>conregnans</i>). Again, the aspect of the ruler couple moves forward, she appears as a partner in the rule of <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>, representations of her as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> can be seen in ecclesiastical art, especially in the <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconian</a> area. As the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Dome" title="Dome">Dome</a> and <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">Diocese</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a>, she is to be seen together with her husband in the group of figures of the doorcase of the <i>Adamspforte</i> at <a href="/wiki/Bamberg_Cathedral" title="Bamberg Cathedral">Bamberg Cathedral</a>. This doorcase, created in 1235, is the most significant monumental depiction of Cunigunde in the High Middle Ages. On the left are <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen" title="Saint Stephen">Stephen</a>, Cunigunde and <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry</a>, on the right opposite side, Peter, <a href="/wiki/Adam_and_Eve" title="Adam and Eve">Adam and Eve</a> can be seen. Stephen, the first Christian <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> is in the guise of a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>. He stands next to the Empress. As a donor, she is holding the model of a church in the right hand, as in many representations. With her left hand, she is pointing to her husband, <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a>, the founder of the diocese.
</p><p>Cunigunde's usual attribute is a ploughshare. According to the legend, she walked over glowing ploughshares without being hurt. This scene is shown on the Tumba made of limestone at <a href="/wiki/Bamberg_Cathedral" title="Bamberg Cathedral">Bamberg Cathedral</a>, created in the years 1499-1513 by <a href="/wiki/Tilman_Riemenschneider" title="Tilman Riemenschneider">Tilman Riemenschneider</a>, the most important German sculptor of the time. The double tomb of the imperial couple shows the lying couple with two lions and the Bavarian-Luxembourgish double coat of arms with blue-silver diamonds and the <i>Roude Leiw</i> at their feet. In such <a href="/wiki/Tomb_effigy" title="Tomb effigy">tomb effigies</a>, the lion, as a symbol of strength, is usually reserved for men. At the feet of women, there usually is a dog, as a sign of loyalty. The fact, that Cunigunde was also assigned a lion, is a reference of posterity to the duchess, queen and empress, who exercises her own sovereign rights.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Roude Leiw</i> (meaning "red lion" in Luxembourgish) is also the coat of arms of Luxembourg since 1235–1239. Three of the four sides of the Tumba are decorated with scenic reliefs and architectural ornaments. Those depict events from the legends of the saints. Cunigunde is depicted as a miraculous saint in the "Ploughshare test" and the "Key miracle".<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Luxembourg, where she is the only female saint, there is the parish Church of Saint Cunigunde in <a href="/wiki/Clausen,_Luxembourg" title="Clausen, Luxembourg">Clausen</a>, which was consecrated in 1865 and decorated with murals depicting scenes from the life of Cunigunde in 1906. In 1959, the Cunigunde bell (<i>Cloche St. Cunégonde</i>) was consecrated. In addition to a wooden sculpture of the saints, a tooth <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of Cunigunde is worshipped in this church.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> There is also an octagonal Cunigunde Chapel in Heiderscheidergrund (<i>Heischtergronn</i>), built in 1848–1852. A statue is located there to the left of the main altar.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> There are two churches consecrated to her in the parish of Park Hosingen, the chapel of the village of Oberschlinder dedicated to her and the <a href="/wiki/Michael_(archangel)" title="Michael (archangel)">Archangel Michael</a> in 1875, and the church of Hoscheid-Dickt in 1852, which is also the oldest church in Luxembourg dedicated to the Holy Empress.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
