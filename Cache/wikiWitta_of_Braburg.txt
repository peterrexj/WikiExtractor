</p>
<dl><dd><i>See <a href="/wiki/Witta,_son_of_Wecta" title="Witta, son of Wecta">Witta, son of Wecta</a> for the mythological Jutish chieftain.</i></dd></dl>
<p><b>Witta of Büraburg</b> (also known as <b>Albuin</b> or <b><a href="/wiki/Vito" title="Vito">Vito</a> Albinus</b>, a close <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> translation of his <a href="/wiki/Germanic_languages" title="Germanic languages">Germanic</a> name) (born in <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>; died 26 October 747) was one of the early <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> missionaries in <a href="/wiki/Hesse" title="Hesse">Hesse</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a> in central Germany, disciple and companion of Saints <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lullus" title="Lullus">Lullus</a>. Following the establishment by Boniface of the bishopric of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCraburg" title="Büraburg">Büraburg</a> near <a href="/wiki/Fritzlar" title="Fritzlar">Fritzlar</a> in 741, Witta was the first and only bishop there. After his death on 26 October 747, no successor was appointed and Lullus, then archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Mainz" title="Mainz">Mainz</a>, incorporated the bishopric into his own because he wanted to have control over the Christian missionary efforts towards the East. Witta was buried in the chapel of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sturm" title="Saint Sturm">Saint Sturm</a> (Sturmius, Sturmi) which Lullus later (769) used as the nucleus for the new and influential <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Hersfeld_Abbey" title="Hersfeld Abbey">Hersfeld Abbey</a>.
</p><p>His feast day falls on 26 October.
</p>
