<p><b>Pope Gregory II</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gregorius II</i>; 669 – 11 February 731) was the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">bishop of Rome</a> from 19 May 715 to his death.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His defiance of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Leo_III_the_Isaurian" title="Leo III the Isaurian">Leo III the Isaurian</a> as a result of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm" title="Byzantine Iconoclasm">iconoclastic controversy</a> in the Eastern Empire prepared the way for a long series of revolts, schisms, and civil wars that eventually led to the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Temporal_power_of_the_Holy_See" title="Temporal power of the Holy See">temporal power of the popes</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born into a noble Roman family in the year 669,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Gregory was the son of Marcellus and Honesta.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Gregory II was an alleged collateral ancestor to the Roman <a href="/wiki/Savelli_family" title="Savelli family">Savelli family</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> according to a 15th-century chronicler, but this is unattested in contemporary documents and very likely unreliable. The same was said of the seventh-century <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_II" title="Pope Benedict II">Pope Benedict II</a>, but nothing certain is known about a kinship between the two of them.
</p><p>As a young man, he was placed in the papal court, and was made a <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">subdeacon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sacellarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacellarius">sacellarius</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_See" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman See">Roman See</a> during the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Sergius I</a> (687–701). Later he was made a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and placed in charge of the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Library" title="Vatican Library">Vatican Library</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-m145_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-m145-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> During <a href="/wiki/Pope_Constantine" title="Pope Constantine">Constantine</a>'s pontificate, Gregory was made a papal secretary, and accompanied the pope to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 711 to deal with the issues raised by Rome’s rejection of the <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canons</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Quinisext_Council" title="Quinisext Council">Quinisext Council</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The actual negotiations on the contentious articles were handled by Gregory, with the result that Emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a> agreed that the papacy could disregard whichever of the council’s decisions it wished to.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After Constantine’s death on 9 April 715, Gregory was elected <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, and was consecrated as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> on 19 May 715.<sup id="cite_ref-m145_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-m145-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="First_years_and_expanding_missionary_activity">First years and expanding missionary activity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: First years and expanding missionary activity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Almost immediately, Gregory began the task of repairing Rome's <a href="/wiki/Aurelian_Walls" title="Aurelian Walls">Aurelian Walls</a>, beginning at the <a href="/wiki/Porta_Tiburtina" title="Porta Tiburtina">Porta Tiburtina</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-m145_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-m145-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Work on this task was delayed in October 716 when the river <a href="/wiki/Tiber" title="Tiber">Tiber</a> burst its banks and flooded Rome, causing immense damage and only receding after eight days.<sup id="cite_ref-m145_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-m145-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gregory ordered a number of litanies to be said to stem the floods, which spread over the <a href="/wiki/Campus_Martius" title="Campus Martius">Campus Martius</a> and the so-called Plains of Nero, reaching the foot of the <a href="/wiki/Capitoline_Hill" title="Capitoline Hill">Capitoline Hill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The first year of his pontificate also saw a letter arrive from Patriarch <a href="/wiki/John_VI_of_Constantinople" title="John VI of Constantinople">John VI of Constantinople</a>, who attempted to justify his support of <a href="/wiki/Monothelitism" title="Monothelitism">Monothelitism</a>, while at the same time seeking sympathy from the pope over the position he was in, with respect to the emperor. Gregory responded by sending a letter outlining the traditional Roman position against Monothelitism.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then in 716, Gregory received an official visit from Duke <a href="/wiki/Theodo_of_Bavaria" title="Theodo of Bavaria">Theodo of Bavaria</a> to discuss the continuing conversion of his lands to Christianity. As a result of this meeting, Gregory gave specific instructions to his delegates who were to travel to Bavaria, coordinate with the duke, and establish a local church hierarchy, overseen by an <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Gregory maintained an interest in Bavaria; in 726 he forced an unwilling <a href="/wiki/Corbinian" title="Corbinian">Corbinian</a>, after reviewing his appeal through a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a>, to abandon his monastic calling, and become <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Freising" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Freising">bishop of Freising</a> in upper <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory next turned his attention to Germany. In 718, he was approached by an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon">Anglo-Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Winfrid" class="mw-redirect" title="Winfrid">Winfrid</a>, who proposed undertaking missionary work in Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Gregory agreed, and after changing his name to Boniface, commissioned him in May 719 to preach in Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After hearing of the work that had been done so far, in 722 Gregory summoned Boniface back to Rome to answer rumors concerning Boniface’s doctrinal purity.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> At this face-to-face meeting, Boniface complained that he found Gregory’s <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> difficult to understand, a clear indication that <a href="/wiki/Vulgar_Latin" title="Vulgar Latin">Vulgar Latin</a> had already started to evolve into the <a href="/wiki/Romance_languages" title="Romance languages">Romance languages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> After examining Boniface’s written profession of faith, Gregory was satisfied enough that he made Boniface a bishop in November 722, and returned him to Germany to continue his mission.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Continued successes saw Gregory write to Boniface in December 724 to offer his congratulations, followed in November 726 by a response to Boniface’s questions about how to structure the newly emergent churches in Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory also strengthened papal authority in the churches of <a href="/wiki/Great_Britain" title="Great Britain">Britain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. In 726 Gregory was visited by <a href="/wiki/Ine_of_Wessex" title="Ine of Wessex">Ine</a>, the former <a href="/wiki/King_of_Wessex" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Wessex">King of Wessex</a>, who had abdicated the throne in order to undertake a pilgrimage to Rome and end his life there.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Local_church_activities">Local church activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Local church activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gregory also concerned himself with establishing or restoring monasteries. He turned his family mansion in Rome into a monastery, St. Agatha in Suburra, endowing it with expensive and precious vessels for use at the altar,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> and also established a new church, dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Eustachio" title="Sant&#39;Eustachio">Sant'Eustachio</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In 718 he restored <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>, which had not recovered from an attack by the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a> in 584, and he intervened in a dispute at the Monastery of St. Vincent on the <a href="/wiki/Volturno" title="Volturno">Volturno</a> over the deposition of the <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 721, Gregory held a <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Rome_(721)" title="Synod of Rome (721)">synod in Rome</a>, for the purpose of fixing issues around illegitimate marriages.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Then in 723, the longstanding dispute between the patriarchs of <a href="/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Aquileia_(Episcopal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarchate of Aquileia (Episcopal)">Aquileia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Grado" title="Patriarch of Grado">Grado</a> flared up again. Upon the request of the <a href="/wiki/King_of_the_Lombards" class="mw-redirect" title="King of the Lombards">Lombard king</a>, <a href="/wiki/Liutprand,_King_of_the_Lombards" title="Liutprand, King of the Lombards">Liutprand</a>, Gregory had given the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> to Bishop Serenus, granting him the patriarchate of Aquileia. Soon afterward, however, Gregory received a letter from Donatus, Patriarch of Grado, complaining that Serenus had overstepped his authority, and was interfering within what was Grado’s ecclesiastical jurisdiction.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> At the same time, Gregory reprimanded Donatus for complaining about Gregory’s decision to grant the pallium to Serenus in the first place.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Then in 725, upon Donatus’ death, the Grado patriarchate was usurped by Peter, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Pore%C4%8D-Pula" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Poreč-Pula">Bishop of Pola</a>. Gregory responded by depriving Peter of both sees, and he wrote to the people of the diocese, reminding them to only elect bishops in accordance with church law, whereupon they elected Antoninus, with Gregory’s approval.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory also mandated a number of practices within the Church. He decreed that in <a href="/wiki/Lent" title="Lent">Lent</a>, on Thursdays, people should fast, just as they were required to do during the other days of the week. Apparently, the practice had been frowned upon by popes of previous centuries, as <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagans</a> had fasted on Thursday as part of their worship of <a href="/wiki/Jupiter_(mythology)" title="Jupiter (mythology)">Jupiter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> He also prescribed the offices be said during church services on Thursdays in Lent, as prior to this, the Mass of the preceding Sunday was said on those Thursdays.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_the_Lombards">Relations with the Lombards</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Relations with the Lombards">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gregory attempted to remain on good diplomatic terms with the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a>, and especially with their king, Liutprand. In April 716 he managed to get Liutprand to agree not to retake the <a href="/wiki/Cottian_Alps" title="Cottian Alps">Cottian Alps</a>, which had been granted to the Roman Church in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Aripert_II" title="Aripert II">Aripert II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> However, the semi-independent Lombard <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento" title="Duchy of Benevento">Duchy of Benevento</a>, under the expansionist duke <a href="/wiki/Romuald_II_of_Benevento" title="Romuald II of Benevento">Romuald II</a>, resumed hostilities by capturing <a href="/wiki/Cumae" title="Cumae">Cumae</a> in 717, cutting <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> off from <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Neither threats of divine retribution nor outright bribery made an impression on Romuald, and so Gregory appealed to Duke <a href="/wiki/John_I_of_Naples" title="John I of Naples">John I of Naples</a>, funding his campaign to successfully retake Cumae.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That same year saw the Lombard duke <a href="/wiki/Faroald_II_of_Spoleto" title="Faroald II of Spoleto">Faroald II of Spoleto</a>, capture <a href="/wiki/Port_of_Ravenna" title="Port of Ravenna">Classis</a>, the port of <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a>. Gregory brokered a deal with Liutprand, who forced Faroald to return it to the <a href="/wiki/Exarch_of_Ravenna" class="mw-redirect" title="Exarch of Ravenna">Exarch of Ravenna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> Perceiving that the Lombard threat would continue to fester and they would take imperial territory in Italy a piece at a time, in around 721 Gregory appealed to the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Franks</a>, asking <a href="/wiki/Charles_Martel" title="Charles Martel">Charles Martel</a> to intervene and drive out the Lombards. Charles however, did not respond to the request.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> A law issued by Liutprand in 723 (No. 33) prohibits a man from marrying the widow of his cousin on either mother's or father's side, and specifically states that the "Pope in the city of Rome" [papa urbis romae] had sent him a letter exhorting him to issue this legislation,<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> indicating a degree of cordial communication between them. <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Imperial</a> weakness in Italy encouraged further Lombard incursions, and in 725, they captured the fortress of <a href="/wiki/Narni" title="Narni">Narni</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
Then in 727, with the <a href="/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna" title="Exarchate of Ravenna">Exarchate of Ravenna</a> in chaos over the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Byzantine Emperor</a>’s <a href="/wiki/Iconoclast" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconoclast">iconoclast</a> decrees (see below), the Lombards captured and destroyed Classis and overran the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_the_Pentapolis" title="Duchy of the Pentapolis">Pentapolis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> Although Classis was retaken in 728, fighting continued between Byzantine forces and the Lombards until 729, when Gregory brokered a deal between Liutprand and the Byzantine <a href="/wiki/Exarch" title="Exarch">exarch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eutychius_(exarch)" title="Eutychius (exarch)">Eutychius</a>, bringing about a temporary ceasing of hostilities that held until Gregory’s death.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> Gregory and Liutprand met in 729 at the ancient city of <a href="/wiki/Sutri" title="Sutri">Sutri</a>. Here, the two reached an agreement, known as the <i>Donation of Sutri</i>, whereby Sutri and some hill towns in <a href="/wiki/Latium" title="Latium">Latium</a> (see <a href="/wiki/Vetralla" title="Vetralla">Vetralla</a>) were given to the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> They were the first extension of papal territory beyond the confines of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Rome" title="Duchy of Rome">Duchy of Rome</a>, and in effect marked the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Conflict_with_Emperor_Leo_III">Conflict with Emperor Leo III</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Conflict with Emperor Leo III">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Solidus-Leo_III.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Solidus-Leo_III.jpg/150px-Solidus-Leo_III.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="143" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Solidus-Leo_III.jpg/225px-Solidus-Leo_III.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/Solidus-Leo_III.jpg 2x" data-file-width="237" data-file-height="226" /></a><figcaption>Byzantine Emperor Leo III who sought to impose iconoclastic doctrines in the west</figcaption></figure>
<p>Tensions between Gregory and the imperial court began around 722 when emperor <a href="/wiki/Leo_III_the_Isaurian" title="Leo III the Isaurian">Leo III</a> attempted to raise taxes on the papal patrimonies in Italy, draining the Papacy’s monetary reserves. Leo required this revenue to pay for the ongoing <a href="/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Arab_Wars" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine–Arab Wars">Arab war</a>, while Gregory needed it to provide local foodstuffs for the city of Rome, thereby relieving Rome on its reliance upon the long-distance supply of grain.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> The result of which was, through refusing to pay the additional taxes, Gregory encouraged the Roman populace to drive the imperial governor of Rome from the city, and Leo was unable to impose his will upon Rome, as Lombard pressure kept the exarch of Ravenna from fielding an army to bring the pope to heel.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, in 725, possibly at the emperor’s request, Marinus, who had been sent from <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> to govern the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Rome" title="Duchy of Rome">Duchy of Rome</a>, encouraged a conspiracy to murder the pope. Involving a <a href="/wiki/Duke_(Lombard)" title="Duke (Lombard)">duke</a> named Basil, the <a href="/wiki/Chartoularios" title="Chartoularios">Chartoularios</a> Jordanes, and a <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">subdeacon</a> named Laurion, the departure of Marinus paused the plot, only to see it resume with the arrival of the new exarch, <a href="/wiki/Paul_(exarch)" title="Paul (exarch)">Paul</a>. However, the plot was uncovered, and the conspirators were put to death.<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then in 726, Leo issued an <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">iconoclast</a> edict, condemning possession of any <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of the saints.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> Although Leo made no move to enforce this edict in the west beyond having it read in Rome and Ravenna, Gregory immediately rejected the edict.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> Upon hearing this, the <a href="/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna" title="Exarchate of Ravenna">Exarchate of Ravenna</a> rose in revolt against the imperial imposition of <a href="/wiki/Iconoclasm" title="Iconoclasm">iconoclasm</a>. The armies of <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> and the Duchy of the Pentapolis mutinied, denouncing both Exarch Paul and Leo III, and overthrew those officers who remained loyal. Paul rallied the loyalist forces and attempted to restore order, but was killed. The armies discussed electing their own emperor and marching on <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, but were dissuaded by Pope Gregory from acting against Leo.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> At the same time, the self-described “duke” Exhilaratus and his son Hadrian rebelled in <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Naples" title="Duchy of Naples">Naples</a>, sided with the emperor and marched on Rome in order to kill Gregory, but were overthrown by the people and killed.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 727, Gregory summoned a synod to condemn iconoclasm.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> According to Greek sources, principally <a href="/wiki/Theophanes_the_Confessor" title="Theophanes the Confessor">Theophanes</a>, it was at this point that Gregory <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> Leo. However, no western source, in particular, the <a href="/wiki/Liber_pontificalis" class="mw-redirect" title="Liber pontificalis">Liber pontificalis</a>, confirms this act by Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> He then dispatched two letters to Leo, denying the Imperial right to interfere in matters of doctrine. He wrote:
</p>
<blockquote><p>"You say: ‘We worship stones and walls and boards.’ But it is not so, O Emperor; but they serve us for remembrance and encouragement, lifting our slow spirits upwards, by those whose names the pictures bear and whose representations they are. And we worship them not as God, as you maintain, God forbid!... Even the little children mock at you. Go into one of their schools, say that you are the enemy of images, and straightway they will throw their little tablets at your head, and what you have failed to learn from the wise you may pick up from the foolish... In virtue of the power which has come down to us from St. Peter, the Prince of the Apostles, we might inflict punishment upon you, but since you have invoked one on yourself, have that, you and the counselors you have chosen... though you have so excellent a high priest, our brother Germanus, whom you ought to have taken into your counsels as father and teacher. . . . The dogmas of the Church are not a matter for the emperor, but for the bishops."<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p> Gregory's letters to Leo have been accused of being apocryphal,<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> and may not accurately reflect the real content of Gregory's correspondence with Leo.
</p><p>In 728, Leo sent to Italy a new exarch, <a href="/wiki/Eutychius_(exarch)" title="Eutychius (exarch)">Eutychius</a>, to try to retrieve the situation.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup> Eutychius sent an emissary to Rome, with instructions to kill Gregory and the chief nobility in the city, but the plot was uncovered and foiled. Next, he attempted to turn the Lombard king and dukes against the pope, but they retained their ambivalent stance, not committing one way or the other.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup> That same year Gregory wrote to <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Germanus_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Germanus I of Constantinople">Patriarch Germanus I of Constantinople</a>, giving the patriarch his support, and when Germanus abdicated, Gregory refused to acknowledge the new patriarch, <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Anastasius_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Anastasius of Constantinople">Anastasius</a>, nor the iconoclast rulings of a council summoned by Leo.<sup id="cite_ref-48" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-48">&#91;48&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 729, Eutychius finally managed to bring about an alliance with the Lombard king, Liutprand, and both agreed to help the other deal with their rebellious subjects. After they had subjugated the dukes of Spoleto and Benevento, bringing them under Liutprand’s authority, they turned to Rome with the intent of bringing Gregory to heel.<sup id="cite_ref-49" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-49">&#91;49&#93;</a></sup> However, outside Rome, Gregory managed to break up the alliance against him, with Liutprand returning to <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>. After this, Eutychius reached an uneasy truce with Gregory, and the pope in return forged a temporary truce between the Lombards and the Byzantines.<sup id="cite_ref-50" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-50">&#91;50&#93;</a></sup> Regardless, Gregory was still a devoted and vigorous defender of the empire. This was demonstrated in 730 when there arose another usurper, <a href="/wiki/Tiberius_Petasius" title="Tiberius Petasius">Tiberius Petasius</a>, who raised a revolt in <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. He was defeated by the exarch Eutychius, who received steady support from Pope Gregory.<sup id="cite_ref-51" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-51">&#91;51&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory died on 11 February 731, and was buried in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%E2%80%99s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter’s Basilica">St. Peter’s Basilica</a>. The location of his tomb has since been lost. He was subsequently <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> and is commemorated as a saint in the Roman calendar and martyrology on 13 February, although some martyrologies list him under 11 February.<sup id="cite_ref-52" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-52">&#91;52&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Miracle_at_the_Battle_of_Toulouse_.28721.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracle_at_the_Battle_of_Toulouse_(721)">Miracle at the Battle of Toulouse (721)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_II&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Miracle at the Battle of Toulouse (721)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A miracle concerning Gregory II is linked to the <a href="/wiki/Victory" title="Victory">victory</a> over <a href="/wiki/Muslim" class="mw-redirect" title="Muslim">Muslim</a> forces at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Toulouse_(721)" title="Battle of Toulouse (721)">Battle of Toulouse (721)</a>. According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i>, in 720 Pope Gregory sent to <a href="/wiki/Odo_the_Great" title="Odo the Great">Odo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Aquitaine" title="Duke of Aquitaine">Duke of Aquitaine</a>, "three blessed sponges/baskets of bread". The Duke kept these, and just before the battle outside of <a href="/wiki/Toulouse" title="Toulouse">Toulouse</a>, he distributed small portions of these to be eaten by his troops. After the battle, it was reported that no one who had eaten a part of the sponges/baskets of bread had been killed or wounded.<sup id="cite_ref-53" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-53">&#91;53&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
