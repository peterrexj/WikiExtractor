<p><b>Raymond of Penyafort</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">OP</a></span> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Sant Ramon de Penyafort</i>, <small>IPA:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="ca-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Catalan" title="Help:IPA/Catalan">[ˈsan rəˈmon də ˌpɛɲəˈfɔɾ]</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Raimundo de Peñafort</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1175 &#8211; 6 January 1275) was a <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalan</a> Dominican friar in the 13th century, who compiled the <a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX">Decretals of Gregory IX</a>, a collection of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canonical laws</a> that remained a major part of Church law until the <a href="/wiki/1917_Code_of_Canon_Law" title="1917 Code of Canon Law">1917 Code of Canon Law</a> abrogated it. He is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Canon_lawyer" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon lawyer">canon lawyers</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Raymond of Penyafort was born in  <a href="/wiki/Vilafranca_del_Pened%C3%A8s" title="Vilafranca del Penedès">Vilafranca del Penedès</a>, a small town near <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>, <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Catalonia" title="Principality of Catalonia">Principality of Catalonia</a>, around 1175. Descended from a noble family with ties to the royal house of Aragon, he was educated in Barcelona and at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">University of Bologna</a>, where he received <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorates</a> in both <a href="/wiki/Civil_law_(legal_system)" title="Civil law (legal system)">civil</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a>. From 1195 to 1210, he taught canon law. In 1210, he moved to <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>, where he remained until 1222, including three years occupying the <a href="/wiki/Chair_(academic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chair (academic)">Chair</a> of canon law at the university. He came to know the newly founded <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> there.<sup id="cite_ref-AC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Raymond was attracted to the Dominican Order by the preaching of Blessed Reginald, prior of the Dominicans of Bologna, and received the habit at the age of 47,<sup id="cite_ref-laity_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-laity-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in the Dominican Convent of Barcelona, to which he had returned from Italy in 1222.<sup id="cite_ref-okane_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-okane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mercedarians">Mercedarians</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mercedarians">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Raymond was instrumental in the founding of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary_of_Mercy" title="Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy">Mercedarian friars</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Attwater_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Attwater-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> When approached by <a href="/wiki/Peter_Nolasco" title="Peter Nolasco">Peter Nolasco</a>, Raymond encouraged and assisted him in obtaining the consent of King <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon" title="James I of Aragon">James I of Aragon</a> for the foundation of the Order.
</p><p>The need to study oriental languages was affirmed by the General Chapter of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> in 1236.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Raymond established the first school of the <i><a href="/wiki/Studia_Linguarum" title="Studia Linguarum">Studia Linguarum</a></i> in <a href="/wiki/Tunis" title="Tunis">Tunis</a>, where it was known as the <i>Studium arabicum</i>. The objective of the schools was to help the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a> liberate Christian captives in Islamic lands.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Summa_de_casibus_poenitentiae"><i>Summa de casibus poenitentiae</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Summa de casibus poenitentiae">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Summa_de_casibus_poenitentiae" title="Summa de casibus poenitentiae">Summa de casibus poenitentiae</a></div>
<p>Raymond had written for <a href="/wiki/Confessor#Confession_of_sins" title="Confessor">confessors</a> a book of cases, the <i><a href="/wiki/Summa_de_casibus_poenitentiae" title="Summa de casibus poenitentiae">Summa de casibus poenitentiae</a></i>. More than simply a list of sins and suggested penances, it discussed pertinent doctrines and laws of the Church that pertained to the problem or case brought to the confessor, and is widely considered an authoritative work on the subject.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1229 Raymond was appointed theologian and penitentiary to the Cardinal Archbishop of Sabina, John of Abbeville, and was summoned to Rome in 1230 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a>, who appointed him chaplain and grand penitentiary.<sup id="cite_ref-okane_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-okane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Gregorian_Decretals">Gregorian Decretals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Gregorian Decretals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX">Decretals of Gregory IX</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Decretals_glossa.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Decretals_glossa.jpg/220px-Decretals_glossa.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="358" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Decretals_glossa.jpg/330px-Decretals_glossa.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Decretals_glossa.jpg/440px-Decretals_glossa.jpg 2x" data-file-width="444" data-file-height="723" /></a><figcaption>Decretals with Glossa</figcaption></figure>
<p>Knowing Raymond's reputation in the juridical sciences, Gregory IX asked him to help in the rearranging and codifying of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon law</a>. <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">Canon laws</a>, which were previously found scattered in many publications, were to be organized into one set of documents. 
</p><p>Papal <a href="/wiki/Decretal" title="Decretal">decretal</a> letters had been changing the law over the course of the previous 100 years since the publication of the <i><a href="/wiki/Decretum_Gratiani" title="Decretum Gratiani">Decretum</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Gratian_(jurist)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gratian (jurist)">Gratian</a>. Being pleased with Raymond's efforts, the pope announced the new publication in a <a href="/wiki/Papal_Bull" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Bull">Bull</a> directed to the doctors and students of <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">Paris</a> and <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">Bologna</a> in September 1234, commanding that the work of Raymond alone should be considered authoritative, and should alone be used in the schools. His collection of canon law, known as the <i><a href="/wiki/Decretals_of_Gregory_IX" title="Decretals of Gregory IX">Decretals of Gregory IX</a></i>, became a standard for almost 700 years. Canon law was finally fully codified by 1917.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Most_famous_miracle">Most famous miracle</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Most famous miracle">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Raymond of Penyafort served as the confessor for King <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon" title="James I of Aragon">James I of Aragon</a>. While on the island of <a href="/wiki/Majorca" class="mw-redirect" title="Majorca">Majorca</a> to initiate a campaign to help convert the Moors living there, the king brought his mistress with him. Raymond reproved the king and asked him repeatedly to dismiss his concubine. The king refused to do so. Finally, the saint told the king that he could remain with him no longer and made plans to leave for Barcelona. But the king forbade Raymond to leave the island, and threatened punishment to any ship captain who dared to take him. 
</p><p>Raymond and his Dominican companion went down to the seashore where Raymond took off his <i>cappa</i> (the long black cloak worn by Dominicans over the white tunic and scapular), and spread one end of it on the water while rigging the other end to his walking staff. Having thus formed a miniature mast, Raymond bid the other Dominican to hop on, but his companion, lacking the saint’s faith, refused to do so. Then Raymond bid him farewell, and with the sign of the cross he pushed away from the shore and sailed away on his cloak. Skirting around the very boats that had forbidden him passage, the saint was seen by scores of sailors who shouted in astonishment and urged him on. Raymond sailed the 160 miles to <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> in the space of six hours, where his landing was witnessed by a crowd of amazed spectators. In awe of this miracle, King James I mended his ways.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Having reached his 60th year, Raymond retired to a reclusive life in Barcelona. Within the year, however, Raymond was appointed to the position of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Tarragona" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Tarragona">Archbishop of Tarragona</a>, the ecclesiastical capital of the <a href="/wiki/Principality_of_Catalonia" title="Principality of Catalonia">Principality of Catalonia</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon" title="Crown of Aragon">Crown of Aragon</a>, but declined.<sup id="cite_ref-okane_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-okane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Raymond returned to <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> in 1236. Not long able to remain in seclusion, however, he was elected the <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">Master of the Order of Preachers</a> by the <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a> of 1238. He immediately set out on foot to visit all the houses of friars and nuns of the Order. Even in the midst of this, he was able to draft a new set of Constitutions of the Order, in which he included a resignation clause for the Master. When it was adopted by the next General Chapter of 1240, he immediately took advantage of that option and resigned within two years.<sup id="cite_ref-AC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although not an inquisitor, as an advisor to <a href="/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon" title="James I of Aragon">James I of Aragon</a> he was often consulted regarding questions of law regarding the practices of the Inquisition in the king's domains. "...[T]he lawyer's deep sense of justice and equity, combined with the worthy Dominican's sense of compassion, allowed him to steer clear of the excesses that were found elsewhere in the formative years of the inquisitions into heresy."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Raymond approved of <a href="/wiki/Conjugal_visit" title="Conjugal visit">conjugal visits</a> for those imprisoned so that the spouse should not be exposed to the risk of possible adultery.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Conversion_of_Jews_and_Muslims">Conversion of Jews and Muslims</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Conversion of Jews and Muslims">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Rejoicing to see himself again free of office, he applied himself with fresh vigor to the <a href="/wiki/Christian_ministry" title="Christian ministry">Christian ministry</a>, especially working for the conversion of the Moors. To this end he encouraged <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas" title="Thomas Aquinas">Thomas Aquinas</a> to write his work <i>Against the Gentiles</i>. He instituted the teaching of Arabic and Hebrew in several houses of the friars. He also founded priories in <a href="/wiki/Murcia" title="Murcia">Murcia</a> (then still ruled by Arabs) and in <a href="/wiki/Tunis" title="Tunis">Tunis</a>. Additionally he went to help establish the Church in the recently conquered island of <a href="/wiki/Mallorca" title="Mallorca">Mallorca</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Disputation_of_Barcelona">Disputation of Barcelona</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Disputation of Barcelona">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Disputation_of_Barcelona" title="Disputation of Barcelona">Disputation of Barcelona</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg/170px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="237" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg/255px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg/340px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Capella_de_Sant_Ramon_de_Penyafort.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5393" data-file-height="7509" /></a><figcaption>Tomb of Saint Raymond in <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Barcelona">Cathedral of Barcelona</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>He exercised great influence over King James of Aragon and succeeded in persuading him to order a public debate, concerning Judaism and Christianity, between <a href="/wiki/Moshe_ben_Nahman" class="mw-redirect" title="Moshe ben Nahman">Moshe ben Nahman</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Rabbi" title="Rabbi">rabbi</a> in <a href="/wiki/Girona" title="Girona">Girona</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Paulus_Christiani" class="mw-redirect" title="Paulus Christiani">Paulus Christiani</a>, a baptized Jew of <a href="/wiki/Montpellier" title="Montpellier">Montpellier</a> who belonged to the Dominicans. In this debate, which took place in the royal palace and other venues at Barcelona from 20–31 July 1263, in the presence of the king and of many of the higher clergy, Raymond took an important part. He was at the head of the theologians present, and in agreement with the king gave the rabbi perfect freedom of speech. Raymond simply observed to Moses ben Nachman that he must not allow himself to blaspheme Christianity, to which Moses replied that he knew what the laws of propriety demanded. On the <a href="/wiki/Jewish_Sabbath" class="mw-redirect" title="Jewish Sabbath">Jewish Sabbath</a> following the close of the debate, the king, together with many preaching friars and other clergy, visited the <a href="/wiki/Synagogue" title="Synagogue">synagogue</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Raymond died at the age of 100 in Barcelona in 1275 and was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> in 1601. He was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Santa_Eulalia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Santa Eulalia">Cathedral of Santa Eulalia</a> in Barcelona.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Raymond of Peñafort's feast day was inserted in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1671 for celebration on 23 January. In 1969 it was moved to 7 January, the day after that of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon lawyers</a>, specifically, and lawyers, in general.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Influence_and_tribute">Influence and tribute</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Penyafort&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Influence and tribute">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg/220px-UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg/330px-UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3d/UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg/440px-UST_-_St._Raymond_Pe%C3%B1afort_Building.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>The St. Raymond Peñafort Building</figcaption></figure>
<p>The St. Raymond Peñafort Building at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" title="University of Santo Tomas">University of Santo Tomas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a>, which houses the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas_College_of_Commerce_and_Business_Administration" title="University of Santo Tomas College of Commerce and Business Administration">College of Commerce and Business Administration</a> and the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas_Faculty_of_Arts_and_Letters" title="University of Santo Tomas Faculty of Arts and Letters">Faculty of Arts and Letters</a>, is named in his honor.
</p>
