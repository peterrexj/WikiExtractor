<p><b>Saint Pantaleon</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Παντελεήμων</span>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Пантелеи́мон</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian" title="Romanization of Russian">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Russian-language romanization"><i lang="ru-Latn">Panteleímon</i></span>; "all-compassionate"), counted in <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western Christianity</a> as among the late-medieval <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a> and in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">Eastern Christianity</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Unmercenary_Healers" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Unmercenary Healers">Holy Unmercenary Healers</a>, was a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nicomedia" title="Nicomedia">Nicomedia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">Diocletianic Persecution</a> of 305 AD.
</p><p>Though there is evidence to suggest that a martyr named Pantaleon existed, some consider the stories of his life and death to be purely legendary.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_of_Pantaleon">Life of Pantaleon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life of Pantaleon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>, Pantaleon was the son of a rich pagan, Eustorgius of Nicomedia, and had been instructed in Christianity by his Christian mother, Saint Eubula; however, after her death he fell away from the Christian church, while he studied medicine with a renowned physician Euphrosinos; under the patronage of Euphrosinos he became physician to the emperor, <a href="/wiki/Galerius" title="Galerius">Galerius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_(10-11_c.,_GIM)_by_shakko.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_%2810-11_c.%2C_GIM%29_by_shakko.jpg/220px-S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_%2810-11_c.%2C_GIM%29_by_shakko.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="196" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_%2810-11_c.%2C_GIM%29_by_shakko.jpg/330px-S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_%2810-11_c.%2C_GIM%29_by_shakko.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_%2810-11_c.%2C_GIM%29_by_shakko.jpg/440px-S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_%2810-11_c.%2C_GIM%29_by_shakko.jpg 2x" data-file-width="993" data-file-height="883" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:S._Pantaleon_from_Nicomedian_catacomb_(10-11_c.,_GIM)_by_shakko.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Pantaleon on a tenth-century Byzantine ceramic tile in the State Historical Museum, Moscow</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Church_of_St._Panteleimon_(Nerezi).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Church_of_St._Panteleimon_%28Nerezi%29.jpg/220px-Church_of_St._Panteleimon_%28Nerezi%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Church_of_St._Panteleimon_%28Nerezi%29.jpg/330px-Church_of_St._Panteleimon_%28Nerezi%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Church_of_St._Panteleimon_%28Nerezi%29.jpg/440px-Church_of_St._Panteleimon_%28Nerezi%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1920" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Church_of_St._Panteleimon_(Nerezi).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Panteleimon_(Nerezi)" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Panteleimon (Nerezi)">Church of St. Panteleimon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gorno_Nerezi" title="Gorno Nerezi">Gorno Nerezi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Skopje" title="Skopje">Skopje</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a></div></div></div>

<p>He was won back to Christianity by Saint Hermolaus (characterized as a bishop of the church at Nicomedia in the later literature), who convinced him that <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> was the better physician, signalling the significance of the <i><a href="/wiki/Exemplum" title="Exemplum">exemplum</a></i> of Pantaleon that faith is to be trusted over medical advice.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Maria_de_Liguori" class="mw-redirect" title="Alphonsus Maria de Liguori">St. Alphonsus Liguori</a> wrote regarding this incident:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>He studied medicine with such success, that the Emperor Maximian appointed him his physician. One day as our saint was discoursing with a holy priest named Hermolaus, the latter, after praising the study of medicine, concluded thus: "But, my friend, of what use are all thy acquirements in this art, since thou art ignorant of the science of salvation?<sup id="cite_ref-liguori_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liguori-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>By miraculously healing a blind man by invoking the name of Jesus over him, Pantaleon converted his father, upon whose death he came into possession of a large fortune. He freed his slaves and, distributing his wealth among the poor, developed a great reputation in Nicomedia. Envious colleagues denounced him to the emperor during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecution. The emperor wished to save him and sought to persuade him to <a href="/wiki/Apostasy" title="Apostasy">apostasy</a>. Pantaleon, however, openly confessed his faith, and as proof that Christ was the true God, he healed a paralytic. Notwithstanding this, he was condemned to death by the emperor, who regarded the <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> as an exhibition of <a href="/wiki/Magic_(paranormal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Magic (paranormal)">magic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the legend, Pantaleon's flesh was first burned with torches, whereupon Christ appeared to all in the form of Hermolaus to strengthen and heal Pantaleon. The torches were extinguished. Then a bath of molten lead was prepared; when the apparition of Christ stepped into the cauldron with him, the fire went out and the lead became cold. Pantaleon was now thrown into the sea, loaded with a great stone, which floated. He was thrown to wild beasts, but these fawned upon him and could not be forced away until he had blessed them. He was bound on the wheel, but the ropes snapped, and the wheel broke. An attempt was made to behead him, but the sword bent, and the executioners were converted to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Pantaleon implored Heaven to forgive them, for which reason he also received the name of <i>Panteleimon</i> ("mercy for everyone" or "all-compassionate"). It was not until he himself desired it that it was possible to behead him, upon which there issued forth blood and a white liquid like milk.
</p><p>St. Alphonsus wrote:
</p>
<blockquote><p>
At <a href="/wiki/Ravello" title="Ravello">Ravello</a>, a city in the kingdom of Naples, there is a vial of his blood, which becomes blood every year [on his feastday], and may be seen in this state interspersed with the milk, as I, the author of this work, have seen it.<sup id="cite_ref-liguori_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-liguori-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_veneration">Early veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vitae</a></i> containing these miraculous features are all late in date and "valueless" according to the <i>Catholic Encyclopedia</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Yet the fact of his martyrdom itself seems to be supported by a veneration for which there is testimony in the 5th century, among others in a sermon on the martyrs by <a href="/wiki/Theodoret" title="Theodoret">Theodoret</a> (died c. 457);<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Procopius_of_Caesarea" class="mw-redirect" title="Procopius of Caesarea">Procopius of Caesarea</a> (died c. 565?), writing on the churches and shrines constructed by <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> tells that the emperor rebuilt the shrine to Pantaleon at Nicomedia; and there is mention of Pantaleon in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_in_the_East">Veneration in the East</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration in the East">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg/220px-Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="218" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg/330px-Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0e/Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg/440px-Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="1013" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Byzantine_-_Saint_Panteleimon_-_Walters_4820864.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Panteleimon, is shown here with a lancet in his right hand. This tile probably formed a frieze on a church wall or altar screen.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/The_Walters_Art_Museum" class="mw-redirect" title="The Walters Art Museum">The Walters Art Museum</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>The Eastern tradition concerning Pantaleon follows more or less the medieval Western hagiography, but lacks any mention of a visible apparition of Christ.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Accuracy_dispute#Disputed_statement" title="Wikipedia:Accuracy dispute"><span title="The material near this tag is possibly inaccurate or nonfactual. (July 2019)">dubious</span></a>&#32;<span class="metadata"> &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Talk:Saint_Pantaleon#Concerning_the_apparition_of_Christ" title="Talk:Saint Pantaleon">discuss</a></span></i>&#93;</sup> It states instead that Hermolaus was still alive while Pantaleon's torture was under way, but was martyred himself only shortly before Pantaleon's beheading along with two companions, Hermippas and Thermocrates. The saint is canonically depicted as a beardless young man with a full head of curly hair.
</p><p>Pantaleon's <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, venerated at Nicomedia, were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>. Numerous churches, shrines, and monasteries have been named for him; in the West most often as St. Pantaleon and in the East as St. Panteleimon; to him is consecrated the <a href="/wiki/Rossikon" class="mw-redirect" title="Rossikon">St. Panteleimon Monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>, <a href="/wiki/Agios_Panteleimon_Monastery" title="Agios Panteleimon Monastery">Agios Panteleimon Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._Panteleimon_Monastery_(Myrtou)" title="St. Panteleimon Monastery (Myrtou)">St Panteleimon monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Myrtou" title="Myrtou">Myrtou</a>, Cyprus, and the 12th-century <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Panteleimon_(Nerezi)" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Panteleimon (Nerezi)">Church of St. Panteleimon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Gorno_Nerezi" title="Gorno Nerezi">Gorno Nerezi</a>, <a href="/wiki/North_Macedonia" title="North Macedonia">North Macedonia</a>.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Movses_Kaghankatvatsi" title="Movses Kaghankatvatsi">Movses Kaghankatvatsi</a>, his relics were brought to church built in the town of Tsri (then <a href="/wiki/Caucasian_Albania" title="Caucasian Albania">Caucasian Albania</a>, near modern <a href="/wiki/Barda,_Azerbaijan" title="Barda, Azerbaijan">Barda, Azerbaijan</a>) by <a href="/wiki/Grigoris_(catholicos)" title="Grigoris (catholicos)">St. Grigoris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Catholicos Lazar of Albania built Church of St. Pantaleon in his honor in Beghame'j (near modern <a href="/wiki/Aghjabadi" title="Aghjabadi">Ağcabədi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Azerbaijan" title="Azerbaijan">Azerbaijan</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Vachagan_III" title="Vachagan III">King Vachagan III of Albania</a> rediscovered his remains and interred them in the <a href="/wiki/Amaras_Monastery" title="Amaras Monastery">Amaras Monastery</a>, located in modern <a href="/wiki/Nagorno_Karabakh" class="mw-redirect" title="Nagorno Karabakh">Nagorno Karabakh</a> and named his firstborn son after the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_in_Western_Europe">Veneration in Western Europe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration in Western Europe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the <a href="/wiki/Black_Death" title="Black Death">Black Death</a> of the mid-14th century in Western Europe, as a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of physicians and midwives, he came to be regarded as one of the fourteen guardian martyrs, the <a href="/wiki/Fourteen_Holy_Helpers" title="Fourteen Holy Helpers">Fourteen Holy Helpers</a>. Relics of the saint are found at <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St_Denis" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St Denis">Saint Denis</a> at Paris; his head is venerated at Lyon. A <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St._Pantaleon" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of St. Pantaleon">Romanesque church</a> was dedicated to him in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a> in the 9th century at the latest.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="England">England</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: England">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/British_Library" title="British Library">British Library</a> there is a surviving manuscript, written in <a href="/wiki/Old_English" title="Old English">Old English</a>, of <i>The Life of St Pantaleon</i> (British Library, MS Cotton Vitellius D XVII), dating from the early eleventh century, possibly written for Abbot <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfric_of_Eynsham" title="Ælfric of Eynsham">Ælfric of Eynsham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The Canons' Vestry off the south transept of <a href="/wiki/Chichester_Cathedral" title="Chichester Cathedral">Chichester Cathedral</a> was formerly a square-plan chapel dedicated to Saint Pantaleon - it was possibly under construction just before the cathedral's great fire of 1187.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="France">France</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: France">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In France, he was depicted in a window in <a href="/wiki/Chartres_Cathedral" title="Chartres Cathedral">Chartres Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In southern France there are six <a href="/wiki/Commune_in_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Commune in France">communes</a> under the protective name of Saint-Pantaléon. Though there are individual churches consecrated to him elsewhere, there are no communes named for him in the north or northwest of France. The six are:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Pantal%C3%A9on,_Lot" title="Saint-Pantaléon, Lot">Saint-Pantaléon</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Lot_(department)" title="Lot (department)">Lot</a> <i><a href="/wiki/D%C3%A9partement_in_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Département in France">département</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Midi-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es" title="Midi-Pyrénées">Midi-Pyrénées</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Pantal%C3%A9on,_Vaucluse" title="Saint-Pantaléon, Vaucluse">Saint-Pantaléon</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Vaucluse" title="Vaucluse">Vaucluse</a> <i>département</i>, <a href="/wiki/Provence" title="Provence">Provence</a> - a wine-growing village</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Pantal%C3%A9on-de-Lapleau" title="Saint-Pantaléon-de-Lapleau">Saint-Pantaléon-de-Lapleau</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Corr%C3%A8ze" title="Corrèze">Corrèze</a> <i>département</i>, <a href="/wiki/Limousin_(r%C3%A9gion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Limousin (région)">Limousin</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Pantal%C3%A9on-de-Larche" title="Saint-Pantaléon-de-Larche">Saint-Pantaléon-de-Larche</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Corr%C3%A8ze" title="Corrèze">Corrèze</a> <i>département</i>, at the border of <a href="/wiki/P%C3%A9rigord" title="Périgord">Périgord</a> and <a href="/wiki/Quercy" title="Quercy">Quercy</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Saint-Pantal%C3%A9on-les-Vignes" title="Saint-Pantaléon-les-Vignes">Saint-Pantaléon-les-Vignes</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Dr%C3%B4me" title="Drôme">Drôme</a> <i>département</i>, <a href="/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes" title="Rhône-Alpes">Rhône-Alpes</a> – a wine-growing village<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> that is part of the <a href="/wiki/C%C3%B4tes_du_Rh%C3%B4ne" class="mw-redirect" title="Côtes du Rhône">Côtes du Rhône</a> vineyard region</li>
<li>Saint-Pantaléon, in the <a href="/wiki/Sa%C3%B4ne-et-Loire" title="Saône-et-Loire">Saône-et-Loire</a> <i>département</i>, <a href="/wiki/Bourgogne" class="mw-redirect" title="Bourgogne">Bourgogne</a> – administratively linked to <a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a>, bishopric see</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Germany">Germany</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Germany">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Cologne a 10th-century <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_church" class="mw-redirect" title="Romanesque church">Romanesque church</a>, partly built by the daughter of the Byzantine emperor, <a href="/wiki/Theophanu" title="Theophanu">Theophanu</a>, who married the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Otto_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto II">Otto II</a> in 972
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/St._Pantaleon%27s_Church,_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Pantaleon&#39;s Church, Cologne">Saint Pantaleon</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a></li></ul>
<p>At the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Vierzehnheiligen" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of the Vierzehnheiligen">Basilica of the Vierzehnheiligen</a> near <a href="/wiki/Staffelstein" class="mw-redirect" title="Staffelstein">Staffelstein</a> in <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconia</a>, St. Pantaleon is venerated with his hands nailed to his head, reflecting another legend about his death.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Italy">Italy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Italy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Italy, Pantaleon gives favourable lottery numbers, victories and winners in dreams.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> A phial containing some of his blood was long preserved at <a href="/wiki/Ravello" title="Ravello">Ravello</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On the feast day of the saint, the blood was said to become fluid and to bubble (compare <a href="/wiki/Saint_Januarius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Januarius">Saint Januarius</a>). <a href="/wiki/Paolo_Veronese" title="Paolo Veronese">Paolo Veronese's</a> painting of Pantaleon can be found in the church of <a href="/wiki/San_Pantalon" title="San Pantalon">San Pantalon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>; it shows the saint healing a child. Another painting of Pantaleon by <a href="/wiki/Fumiani" class="mw-redirect" title="Fumiani">Fumiani</a> is also in the same church.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was depicted in an 8th-century fresco in <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_Antiqua" title="Santa Maria Antiqua">Santa Maria Antiqua</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, and in a 10th-century cycle of pictures in the crypt of <a href="/wiki/San_Crisogono" class="mw-redirect" title="San Crisogono">San Crisogono</a> in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In Calabria, there is a small town named Papanice, after Pantaleon. Each year on his feast day, a statue of the saint is carried through the town to give a blessing for all those who seek it.
</p><p>San Pantaleone or Pantalone was a popular saint in Venice, and he therefore gave his name to a character in the <i><a href="/wiki/Commedia_dell%27arte" title="Commedia dell&#39;arte">commedia dell'arte</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Pantalone" title="Pantalone">Pantalone</a>, a silly, wizened old man (<a href="/wiki/Shakespeare" class="mw-redirect" title="Shakespeare">Shakespeare</a>'s "lean and slippered Pantaloon") who was a caricature of Venetians. This character was portrayed as wearing trousers rather than knee breeches, and so became the origin of the name of a type of trouser called "<a href="/wiki/Trousers" title="Trousers">pantaloons</a>," which was later shortened to "pants".<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Portugal">Portugal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Portugal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Pantaleon (<i>São Pantaleão</i> in Portuguese) is one of the patron saints of the city of <a href="/wiki/Porto" title="Porto">Porto</a> in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Anglo-American_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Anglo-American-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> together with <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Our Lady of Vendome</a>. Part of his relics were brought by <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenian</a> refugees to the city after the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople" title="Fall of Constantinople">Turkish occupation of Constantinople</a> in 1453.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Later, in 1499, these relics were transferred from the Church of Saint Peter of Miragaia to the cathedral, where they have been kept to this day.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Eponym">Eponym</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Pantaleon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Eponym">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<ul><li>Portuguese explorer <a href="/wiki/Bartolomeu_Dias" title="Bartolomeu Dias">Bartolomeu Dias</a>, the first European known to have sailed around the <a href="/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope" title="Cape of Good Hope">Cape of Good Hope</a>, took a ship named <i>São Pantaleão</i> on that expedition.</li>
<li>The Russian battleship <a href="/wiki/Russian_battleship_Potemkin" title="Russian battleship Potemkin"><i>Potemkin</i></a> was renamed <i>Panteleimon</i> after her recovery after the <a href="/wiki/Mutiny" title="Mutiny">mutiny</a> of 1905</li>
<li>St. Pantaleon is the eponym of the character <a href="/wiki/Pantalaimon" class="mw-redirect" title="Pantalaimon">Pantalaimon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Philip_Pullman" title="Philip Pullman">Philip Pullman</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/His_Dark_Materials" title="His Dark Materials">His Dark Materials</a></i> series of novels</li></ul>
