<p><b>Abib</b> and <b>Apollo</b> were two <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Ascetics" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascetics">ascetics</a> from <a href="/wiki/Akhmim" title="Akhmim">Akhmim</a>, <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>. They are mentioned in the <i>Synaxarion, das ist der Heiligen-Kalendar der Koptischen Christen</i>. Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on November 4.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abib_and_Apollo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Apollo (also called Apollonios)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was born in the City of <a href="/wiki/Akhmim" title="Akhmim">Akhmim</a>. His father's name was Amani (Hamai) and his mother's name was Eyse (Isa). From his early years Apollo grew and developed in  saintliness, studying the subjects of Divinity. He was prepared from his youth to his life in a monastery. This decision was confirmed when he met a friend Abib and together they joined the monastery in Upper Egypt and became monks.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
They both tended to ascetic works and their life was full of good deeds.<sup id="cite_ref-codsus_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-codsus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Abib became a deacon and later died.<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Apollo, distressed, moved deeper into the desert, near Mount Abluj, followed by a group of ascetics. <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Macarius of Egypt</a> wrote a letter to Apollo to confirm him and the monastery in their good works.<sup id="cite_ref-codsus_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-codsus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
Apollo knew by the spirit that Macarius was writing them a letter and the time at which the letter was actually being drafted. He said to his followers: "Listen my brethren, behold the great Abba Macarius is writing to us a letter full of comforting and spiritual teachings." Apollo was the one who went to <a href="/wiki/Ammonas" class="mw-redirect" title="Ammonas">Ammonius</a> and was one of the witnesses who saw the holy woman that "stood in the middle of the fire but did not burn".<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id=".22Historia_Monachorum.22_of_the_archdeacon_Timothy_of_Alexandria"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="&quot;Historia_Monachorum&quot;_of_the_archdeacon_Timothy_of_Alexandria">"Historia Monachorum" of the archdeacon Timothy of Alexandria</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abib_and_Apollo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: &quot;Historia Monachorum&quot; of the archdeacon Timothy of Alexandria">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The author of the <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Monachorum_in_Aegypto" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia Monachorum in Aegypto">Historia Monachorum in Aegypto</a></i>, the archdeacon Timothy of Alexandria (around 400), whose Latin translation of <a href="/wiki/Tyrannius_Rufinus" title="Tyrannius Rufinus">Rufinus</a> was equally published by <a href="/wiki/Heribert_Rosweyde" title="Heribert Rosweyde">Rosweyde</a> as book II of the <a href="/wiki/Vitae_Patrum" title="Vitae Patrum">Vitae Patrum</a>, recounts the visit he made with his companions to Apollo.
According to his recollections Apollo lived in the <a href="/wiki/Thebaid" title="Thebaid">Tebaide</a>, in the region of <a href="/wiki/Hermopolis" title="Hermopolis">Hermopolis</a> (today Asmunayn), not far from the sanctuary of <a href="/wiki/Deir_el-Muharraq" title="Deir el-Muharraq">Dayr Al-Muharrag</a>. From this book it appears that after forty years, at the call of an angel, Apollo approached the inhabited places while continuing his life in an isolated cave. This happened under the reign of Julian the Apostate (361-363 AD). His reputation for sanctity quickly attracted numerous disciples around him, who built an immense monastery: 500 monks were there at the time of Timothy's visit.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
