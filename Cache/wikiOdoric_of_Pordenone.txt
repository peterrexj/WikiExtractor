<p><b>Odoric of Pordenone</b>, <small><a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">OFM</a></small> (1286–1331), also known as <b>Odorico Mattiussi</b>/<b>Mattiuzzi</b>, <b>Odoricus of Friuli</b> or <b>Orderic of Pordenone</b>, was an Italian late-medieval <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> and missionary explorer. He traveled through <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Greater_Sunda_Islands" title="Greater Sunda Islands">Greater Sunda Islands</a>, and <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a>, where he spent three years in <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a>. After his death, he became an object of popular devotion and was beatified in 1755.
</p><p>Odoric wrote a narrative of his travels, which has been preserved in Latin, French, and Italian manuscripts. It includes accurate descriptions of Asian social and religious customs. His account was an important source for the account of <a href="/wiki/John_Mandeville" title="John Mandeville">John Mandeville</a>. Many of the incredible reports in Mandeville have proven to be garbled versions of Odoric's eyewitness descriptions.
<span class="anchor" id="Biography"></span><span class="anchor" id="History"></span>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odoric_of_Pordenone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Europeans_in_Medieval_China" title="Europeans in Medieval China">Europeans in Medieval China</a></div>
<p>Odoric was born at <a href="/wiki/Villanova_di_Pordenone" class="mw-redirect" title="Villanova di Pordenone">Villanova</a>, a hamlet now belonging to the town of <a href="/wiki/Pordenone" title="Pordenone">Pordenone</a> in <a href="/wiki/Friuli" title="Friuli">Friuli</a>, in or about 1286. He came from the Italian family of the Mattiussi, one of the families in charge of defending the town of Pordenone in the name of <a href="/wiki/Ottokar_II_of_Bohemia" title="Ottokar II of Bohemia">Ottokar II</a>, King of Bohemia. Otto Hartig, writing in the <i>Catholic Encyclopedia</i>, says his family was Czech.<sup id="cite_ref-otto_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Andrea Tilatti, in <i>Treccani</i>, says this is unsubstantiated.<sup id="cite_ref-hartig_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hartig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the ecclesiastical biographers, in early years he took the vows of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> order and joined their convent at <a href="/wiki/Udine" title="Udine">Udine</a>, the capital of <a href="/wiki/Friuli" title="Friuli">Friuli</a>. In 1296 Odoric went as a missionary to the <a href="/wiki/Balkan_Peninsula" class="mw-redirect" title="Balkan Peninsula">Balkans</a>, and then to the <a href="/wiki/Mongols" title="Mongols">Mongols</a> in southern Russia.<sup id="cite_ref-habig_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:272px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Persepolis_,_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Persepolis_%2C_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg/270px-Persepolis_%2C_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg" decoding="async" width="270" height="400" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Persepolis_%2C_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg/405px-Persepolis_%2C_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Persepolis_%2C_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg/540px-Persepolis_%2C_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1811" data-file-height="2682" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Persepolis_,_view_the_ruins_3_by_Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The ancient Persian city of <a href="/wiki/Persepolis" title="Persepolis">Persepolis</a> through which Odoric passed. The columns are nearly 25 meters (84 feet) tall. Drawing by <a href="/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Flandin" title="Eugène Flandin">Eugène Flandin</a> in 1840.</div></div></div>
<p>Odoric was dispatched to the East in April 1318. Starting from Padua, he went to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> via <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> and then crossed the <a href="/wiki/Black_Sea" title="Black Sea">Black Sea</a> to <a href="/wiki/Trabzon" title="Trabzon">Trebizond</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-hartig_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-hartig-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> From there he traveled and preached in <a href="/wiki/Armenia" title="Armenia">Armenia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Media_(region)" title="Media (region)">Media</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Persia" class="mw-redirect" title="Persia">Persia</a>. In all these countries the Franciscans had founded mission centers. From <a href="/wiki/Sultanieh" class="mw-redirect" title="Sultanieh">Sultanieh</a> he proceeded by <a href="/wiki/Kashan" title="Kashan">Kashan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Yazd" title="Yazd">Yazd</a>, and turning thence followed a somewhat indirect route by <a href="/wiki/Persepolis" title="Persepolis">Persepolis</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Shiraz,_Iran" class="mw-redirect" title="Shiraz, Iran">Shiraz</a> and <a href="/wiki/Baghdad" title="Baghdad">Baghdad</a> regions, to the <a href="/wiki/Persian_Gulf" title="Persian Gulf">Persian Gulf</a>. With another friar, <a href="/wiki/James_of_Ireland" title="James of Ireland">James of Ireland</a>, as his companion, he sailed from <a href="/wiki/Ormus" title="Ormus">Ormus</a> to India,<sup id="cite_ref-habig_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-habig-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> landing at <a href="/wiki/Thane" title="Thane">Thane</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Mumbai" title="Mumbai">Mumbai</a>.
</p><p>At this city <a href="/wiki/St_Thomas_of_Tolentino" class="mw-redirect" title="St Thomas of Tolentino">St&#160;Thomas of Tolentino</a> and his three <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscan</a> companions had recently been <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrdom">martyred</a> for "<a href="/wiki/Blasphemy_in_Islam" class="mw-redirect" title="Blasphemy in Islam">blaspheming</a>" <a href="/wiki/Muhammad" title="Muhammad">Muhammad</a> before the local <a href="/wiki/Qadi" title="Qadi">qadi</a> during a <a href="/wiki/Domestic_violence" title="Domestic violence">domestic violence</a> case.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Their remains had been gathered by <a href="/wiki/Jordan_of_Severac" class="mw-redirect" title="Jordan of Severac">Jordan of Severac</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> who had left them a short time before and who later became the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Quilon" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Quilon">first Catholic bishop in India</a>. He interred them at the church in Supera, near <a href="/wiki/Vasai" title="Vasai">Vasai</a>, about 26 miles north of Mumbai. Odoric relates that he disinterred these <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> and carried them with him on his further travels. From Thane, he travelled down the <a href="/wiki/Malabar_coast" class="mw-redirect" title="Malabar coast">Malabar coast</a>, stopping at <a href="/wiki/Kodungallur" title="Kodungallur">Kodungallur</a> and <a href="/wiki/Quilon" class="mw-redirect" title="Quilon">Quilon</a>. From there, he proceeded around <a href="/wiki/Cape_Comorin" class="mw-redirect" title="Cape Comorin">Cape Comorin</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Coromandel_Coast" title="Coromandel Coast">Coromandel Coast</a>. Here, he visited the <a href="/wiki/St._Thomas_Mount" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Thomas Mount">Church of St. Thomas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He also visited <a href="/wiki/Puri" title="Puri">Puri</a>, giving one of the earliest accounts of the <a href="/wiki/Ratha-Yatra_(Puri)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ratha-Yatra (Puri)">Chariot Festival</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Hindu" class="mw-redirect" title="Hindu">Hindu</a> God <a href="/wiki/Jagannath" title="Jagannath">Jagannath</a> to the western world.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMitter197710_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMitter197710-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In his own account of 1321, Odoric reported how the people put the "idols" on chariots, and the King and Queen and all the people drew them from the "church" with song and music.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEStarza1993129_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEStarza1993129-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEDasa198248_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDasa198248-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From India, Odoric sailed in a <a href="/wiki/Junk_(ship)" title="Junk (ship)">junk</a> to <a href="/wiki/Sumatra" title="Sumatra">Sumatra</a>, visiting various ports on the northern coast of that island. Thence, he visited <a href="/wiki/Java_(island)" class="mw-redirect" title="Java (island)">Java</a>, <a href="/wiki/Borneo" title="Borneo">Borneo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Champa" title="Champa">Champa</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Maspero_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maspero-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 91">&#58;&#8202;91&#8202;</span></sup> via <a href="/wiki/Great_Nicobar_Island" title="Great Nicobar Island">Great Nicobar Island</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> An account on the official site of the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicariate_of_Brunei_Darussalam" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicariate of Brunei Darussalam">Apostolic Vicariate of Brunei Darussalam</a> stated that he travelled Borneo, and probably came to Brunei, in 1325.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He travelled from <a href="/wiki/Ceylon" class="mw-redirect" title="Ceylon">Ceylon</a> to <a href="/wiki/Guangzhou" title="Guangzhou">Guangzhou</a> (which he knew as "Chin-Kalan" or "Mahachin"). From Guangzhou, he travelled overland to the great port of <a href="/wiki/Quanzhou" title="Quanzhou">Quanzhou</a> ("Zayton") where there were two houses of his order. In one of these, he deposited most of the remains of the Four Martyrs of Thane, although he continued to carry <a href="/wiki/St_Thomas_of_Tolentino" class="mw-redirect" title="St Thomas of Tolentino">St&#160;Thomas</a>'s head until he delivered it to the Franciscans of the martyr's hometown of <a href="/wiki/Tolentino" title="Tolentino">Tolentino</a>.
</p><p>From <a href="/wiki/Fuzhou" title="Fuzhou">Fuzhou</a> Odoric struck across the mountains into <a href="/wiki/Zhejiang" title="Zhejiang">Zhejiang</a> and visited <a href="/wiki/Hangzhou" title="Hangzhou">Hangzhou</a> ("Cansay"). It was at the time one of the great cities of the world and Odoric —like <a href="/wiki/Marco_Polo" title="Marco Polo">Marco Polo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_de%27_Marignolli" title="Giovanni de&#39; Marignolli">Marignolli</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Ibn_Batuta" class="mw-redirect" title="Ibn Batuta">Ibn Batuta</a>—gives details of its splendors. He mentions Hangzhou as "greater city than any other in the world, being 100 miles around, with everywhere within inhabited; often a house has 10 or 12 families. The city has twelve gates and at each gate at about eight miles are cities larger than <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a> or <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Passing northward by <a href="/wiki/Nanjing" title="Nanjing">Nanjing</a> and crossing the <a href="/wiki/Yangtze_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Yangtze River">Yangzi</a>, Odoric embarked on the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Canal_of_China" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Canal of China">Grand Canal</a> and travelled to the headquarters of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Khan" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Khan">Great Khan</a> (probably <a href="/wiki/Yes%C3%BCn_Tem%C3%BCr_Khan" class="mw-redirect" title="Yesün Temür Khan">Yesün Temür Khan</a>) at <a href="/wiki/Khanbaliq" title="Khanbaliq">Khanbaliq</a> (within present-day <a href="/wiki/Beijing" title="Beijing">Beijing</a>). He remained there for three years, probably from 1324 to 1327. He was attached, no doubt, to one of the churches founded by the Franciscan Archbishop <a href="/wiki/John_of_Monte_Corvino" class="mw-redirect" title="John of Monte Corvino">John of Monte Corvino</a>, at this time in extreme old age.<sup id="cite_ref-otto_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He also visited <a href="/wiki/Yangzhou" title="Yangzhou">Yangzhou</a> where <a href="/wiki/Katarina_Vilioni" title="Katarina Vilioni">Katarina Vilioni</a>'s tombstone was found in 1951.
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:272px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Odoric_Tomb.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Odoric_Tomb.jpg/270px-Odoric_Tomb.jpg" decoding="async" width="270" height="203" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Odoric_Tomb.jpg/405px-Odoric_Tomb.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Odoric_Tomb.jpg/540px-Odoric_Tomb.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3488" data-file-height="2616" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Odoric_Tomb.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Odoric's tomb in <a href="/wiki/Udine" title="Udine">Udine</a></div></div></div>
<p>Odoric did not return to Italy till the end of 1329 or the beginning of 1330; but, as regards intermediate dates, all that we can deduce from his narrative or other evidence is that he was in western India soon after 1321 (pretty certainly in 1322) and that he spent three years in China between the opening of 1323 and the close of 1328. On one of his trips, his ship was nearly capsized by a typhoon but they landed safely in <a href="/wiki/Bolinao,_Pangasinan" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolinao, Pangasinan">Bolinao</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pangasinan" title="Pangasinan">Pangasinan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>. He is said to have held a <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> there, in around 1324. That would have pre-dated the Mass celebrated in 1521 by Pedro de Valderrama for the crew of <a href="/wiki/Magellan%27s_circumnavigation" class="mw-redirect" title="Magellan&#39;s circumnavigation">Magellan's circumnavigation</a>, which is generally regarded as the first Mass in the Philippines, by some 197 years. However, historian <a href="/wiki/William_Henry_Scott_(historian)" title="William Henry Scott (historian)">William Henry Scott</a> concluded after examining Odoric's writings about his travels that he likely never set foot on Philippine soil and, if he did, there is no reason to think that he celebrated Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Odoric's return voyage is less clearly described. Returning overland across Asia, through the Land of <a href="/wiki/Prester_John" title="Prester John">Prester John</a> (possibly <a href="/wiki/Mongolia" title="Mongolia">Mongolia</a>), and through Casan, the adventurous traveller seems to have entered <a href="/wiki/Tibet" title="Tibet">Tibet</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-otto_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-otto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and even perhaps to have visited <a href="/wiki/Lhasa" title="Lhasa">Lhasa</a>. After this we trace the friar in northern <a href="/wiki/Iran" title="Iran">Persia</a>, in what he calls "Millestorte", once famous as the Land of the Assassins, i.e. the <a href="/wiki/Alamut" title="Alamut">Rudbar of Alamut</a>. No further indications of his homeward route (to <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>) are given, though it is almost certain that he passed through <a href="/wiki/Tabriz" title="Tabriz">Tabriz</a>. The vague and fragmentary character of the narrative, in this section,  forcibly contrasts with the clear and careful tracing of the outward way.
</p><p>During a part at least of these long journeys the companion of Odoric was <a href="/wiki/James_of_Ireland" title="James of Ireland">James of Ireland</a>, an Irishman, as appears from a record in the public books of Udine, showing that shortly after Odoric's death a present of two marks was made to this Irish friar, <i>Socio beau Fratris Odorici, amore Dei et Odorici</i>. Shortly after his return Odoric betook himself to the Minorite house attached to the Friary of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua" title="Anthony of Padua">St. Anthony</a> at <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>, and it was there that in May 1330 he related the story of his travels, which was taken down in homely Latin by Friar <a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_Solagna&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="William of Solagna (page does not exist)">William of Solagna</a>.
</p><p>Travelling towards the papal court at <a href="/wiki/Avignon" title="Avignon">Avignon</a>, Odoric fell ill at <a href="/wiki/Pisa" title="Pisa">Pisa</a>, and turning back to Udine, the capital of his native province, died there.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Odoric_in_context">Odoric in context</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odoric_of_Pordenone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Odoric in context">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Odoric's journey is perhaps best seen as a diplomatic mission, in addition to its religious dimensions. Nearly a century earlier, Mongols had entered Europe itself in the <a href="/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe" title="Mongol invasion of Europe">Mongol invasion of Europe</a>. Between 1237 and 1238 they pillaged most of Russia, and by 1241 they had devastated Poland and Hungary. Then they suddenly retreated. At the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Lyon" title="First Council of Lyon">First Council of Lyon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_IV" title="Pope Innocent IV">Pope Innocent IV</a> organized the first missions to the Great Khan Tartary in 1245, entrusted to the Franciscans, as were subsequent Papal missions over the next century. Niccolò, Matteo, and <a href="/wiki/Marco_Polo" title="Marco Polo">Marco Polo</a> made two voyages in 1260 and 1271, and in 1294 the missionary <a href="/wiki/John_of_Monte_Corvino" class="mw-redirect" title="John of Monte Corvino">John of Monte Corvino</a> made a similar journey for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV" title="Pope Nicholas IV">Pope Nicholas IV</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Contemporary_fame_of_his_journeys">Contemporary fame of his journeys</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odoric_of_Pordenone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Contemporary fame of his journeys">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg/250px-Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="421" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg/375px-Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg/500px-Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1452" data-file-height="2448" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Odoryk_z_Pordenone_1.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Chinese depiction of the Blessed Odoric (c.1930)</div></div></div>
<p>The fame of his vast journeys appears to have made a much greater impression on the laity of his native territory than on his Franciscan brethren. The latter were about to bury him—without delay or ceremony, but the gastald or chief magistrate of the city interfered and appointed a public funeral; rumours of his wondrous travels and of posthumous miracles were diffused, and excitement spread like wildfire over Friuli and <a href="/wiki/Carniola" title="Carniola">Carniola</a>; the ceremony had to be deferred more than once, and at last took place in presence of the patriarch of <a href="/wiki/Aquileia" title="Aquileia">Aquileia</a> and all the local dignitaries. Popular acclamation made him an object of devotion, the municipality erected a noble shrine for his body, and his fame as saint and traveller had spread far and wide before the middle of the century, but it was not till four centuries later (1755) that the papal authority formally sanctioned his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>. A bust of Odoric was set up at Pordenone in 1881.
</p><p>There are a few passages in the book that stamp Odoric as a genuine and original traveller. He is the first European, after Marco Polo, who distinctly mentions the name of Sumatra. The cannibalism and community of wives which he attributes to certain people of that island do certainly belong to it, or to islands closely adjoining.<sup id="cite_ref-odoric_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-odoric-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> His description of <a href="/wiki/Sago" title="Sago">sago</a> in the archipelago is not free from errors, but they are the errors of an eye-witness.
</p><p>Regarding China, his mention of Guangzhou by the name of Censcolam or Censcalam (Chin-Kalan), and his descriptions of the custom of <a href="/wiki/Cormorant_fishing" title="Cormorant fishing">fishing with tame cormorants</a>, of the habit of letting the fingernails grow extravagantly, and of the <a href="/wiki/Foot_binding" title="Foot binding">compression of women's feet</a>, are peculiar to him among the travellers of that age; Marco Polo omits them all. Odoric was one who not only visited many countries, but wrote about them so that he could share his knowledge with others.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odoric_of_Pordenone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Moved by the many miracles that were wrought at the tomb of the Odoric, Pope <a href="/wiki/Benedict_XIV" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict XIV">Benedict XIV</a>, in the year 1755, approved the veneration which had been paid to Blessed Odoric. In the year 1881 the city of Pordenone erected a magnificent memorial to its distinguished son.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Manuscripts_and_published_editions">Manuscripts and published editions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odoric_of_Pordenone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Manuscripts and published editions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg/220px-Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="335" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg/330px-Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg/440px-Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1091" data-file-height="1660" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Vita_Odoryk_z_Pordenone.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The title page Life of Bl. Odoric of Pordenone. Ed. 1891.</div></div></div>
<p>Seventy-three manuscripts of Odoric's narrative are known to exist in Latin, French and Italian: of these the chief, of about 1350, is in the <a href="/wiki/Biblioth%C3%A8que_Nationale_de_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Bibliothèque Nationale de France">Bibliothèque Nationale de France</a>, Paris (Manuscripts lat. 2584, fols. 118 r. to 127 v.. The narrative was first printed at <a href="/wiki/Pesaro" title="Pesaro">Pesaro</a> in 1513, in what <a href="/wiki/Apostolo_Zeno" title="Apostolo Zeno">Apostolo Zeno</a> (1668–1750) calls <i>lingua inculta e rozza</i>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Ramusio" title="Giovanni Battista Ramusio">Giovanni Battista Ramusio</a> first includes Odoric's narrative in the second volume of the second edition (1574) (Italian version), in which are given two versions, differing curiously from one another, but without any prefatory matter or explanation. (See also edition of 1583, vol. ii. fols. 245 r256 r.) Another (Latin) version is given in the <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i> (<a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a>) under 14 January. The curious discussion before the papal court respecting the beatification of Odoric forms a kind of blue-book issued <i>ex typographia rev. camerae apostolicae</i> (Rome, 1755). <a href="/w/index.php?title=Friedrich_Kunstmann&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Friedrich Kunstmann (page does not exist)">Friedrich Kunstmann</a> of Munich devoted one of his papers to Odoric's narrative (<i>Histor.-polit. Blätter von Phillips und Görres</i>, vol. xxxvii. pp.&#160;507–537).
