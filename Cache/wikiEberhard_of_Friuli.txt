					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
<b>Eberhard</b> (c. 815 &#8211; 16 December 867) was the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Friuli" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Friuli">Duke of Friuli</a> from 846. His name is alternatively spelled <b>Everard</b>, <b>Evrard</b>, <b>Erhard</b>, or <b>Eberard</b>; in Latinized fashion, <i>Everardus</i>, <i>Eberardus</i>, or <i>Eberhardus</i>. He wrote his own name "Evvrardus".<sup id="cite_ref-dec16_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dec16-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was an important political, military, and cultural figure in the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian Empire</a> during his lifetime. He kept a large library, commissioned works of <a href="/wiki/Latin_literature" title="Latin literature">Latin literature</a> from <a href="/wiki/Lupus_Servatus" title="Lupus Servatus">Lupus Servatus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sedulius_Scottus" title="Sedulius Scottus">Sedulius Scottus</a>, and maintained a correspondence with the theologians and church leaders <a href="/wiki/Gottschalk_(theologian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gottschalk (theologian)">Gottschalk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rabanus_Maurus" title="Rabanus Maurus">Rabanus Maurus</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Hincmar" title="Hincmar">Hincmar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dec16_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dec16-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eberhard was from an illustrious <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> family, though his parentage is debated.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His marriage to the daughter of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> cemented his eminent position at the Frankish court.
</p>
<h3><span id="Children_.28with_Gisela.2C_daughter_of_Louis_the_Pious.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Children_(with_Gisela,_daughter_of_Louis_the_Pious)">Children (with <a href="/wiki/Gisela,_daughter_of_Louis_the_Pious" title="Gisela, daughter of Louis the Pious">Gisela, daughter of Louis the Pious</a>)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Children (with Gisela, daughter of Louis the Pious)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<ul><li>Eberhard (c. 837 &#8211; 840)</li>
<li>Ingeltrude (837 or 840 &#8211; April 870), possibly married <a href="/wiki/Henry,_Margrave_of_the_Franks" title="Henry, Margrave of the Franks">Henry, Margrave of the Franks</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Unruoch_III_of_Friuli" title="Unruoch III of Friuli">Unruoch III</a> (c. 840 &#8211; 874)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Berengar_I_of_Italy" title="Berengar I of Italy">Bèrenger</a> (c. 845 &#8211; 924), <a href="/wiki/King_of_Italy" title="King of Italy">King of Italy</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Adelhard_von_Burc" title="Adelhard von Burc">Adélard</a> (d. 874)</li>
<li>Rudolf (d. 892)</li>
<li>Heilwise (b. 860)</li>
<li>Gisèle (d. 863)</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Judith_(daughter_of_Eberhard_of_Friuli)" title="Judith (daughter of Eberhard of Friuli)">Judith of Friuli</a> (died ca. 881), first married <a href="/wiki/Arnulf_I_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Arnulf I of Bavaria">Arnulf I of Bavaria</a>, second married <a href="/wiki/Conrad_II_of_Auxerre" class="mw-redirect" title="Conrad II of Auxerre">Conrad II of Auxerre</a>.</li></ul>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Disputed_parentage">Disputed parentage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Disputed parentage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<dl><dt>Paternity theories</dt></dl>
<ul><li>His father was <a href="/wiki/Unruoch_II_of_Friuli" title="Unruoch II of Friuli">Unruoch II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-royalancestry_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-royalancestry-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"His father was <a href="/wiki/Berengar_the_Wise" title="Berengar the Wise">Berengar</a>, the son of Count <a href="/wiki/Unruochings" title="Unruochings">Unroch</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"After other authors, <a href="/wiki/Unruochings" title="Unruochings">Unroch</a>, the grandfather of Saint Evrard, should have been the Duke of Frioul."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"Alas, some have written that Saint Evrard had for his father <a href="/wiki/Carloman_I" title="Carloman I">Carloman I</a>, the brother of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"His grandfather was, it is said, the Count <i><a href="/wiki/Unruochings" title="Unruochings">Unroch</a></i> who was leaving the court of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> and signatory to the will of the emperor."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<dl><dt>Maternity theories</dt></dl>
<ul><li>His mother was <a href="/w/index.php?title=Engeltron_of_Paris&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Engeltron of Paris (page does not exist)">Engeltron of Paris</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Beggo_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Beggo of Paris">Begue</a>, <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">Count</a> of <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-royalancestry_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-royalancestry-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>"As for his mother, she was, <a href="/wiki/Buzelin" title="Buzelin">Buzelin</a> says, the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Desiderius" title="Desiderius">Didier</a>, king of the <a href="/wiki/Lombards" title="Lombards">Lombards</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Although a layman, Eberhard was not only literate but possessed an extensive library, which is detailed in his will. He acted as a patron for the theologian and heretic <a href="/wiki/Gottschalk_of_Orbais" title="Gottschalk of Orbais">Gottshalk of Orbais</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Warlike_exploits_and_role_as_mediator_under_Louis_the_Pious">Warlike exploits and role as mediator under Louis the Pious</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Warlike exploits and role as mediator under Louis the Pious">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As soon as his age permitted him to carry arms, Eberhard took part in numerous military expeditions.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Named Duke of Friuli and Count or Marquis<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> de <a href="/wiki/Treviso" title="Treviso">Trévise</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, he defended his country against invasion by the <a href="/wiki/Bulgars" title="Bulgars">Bulgars</a> and managed to completely drive them from the peninsula—825-830.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He rendered service unto <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> that was still more distinguished.  During the tragic years (830-839) where the emperor had suffered at the hand of his son's revolt the most undignified treatment, Count Eberhard remained inviolably loyal.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He exercised his influence in <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothair</a>'s sphere (the elder son of the emperor) to bring about a reconciliation between father and son.  It is certain that it was on his counsel in 839 that <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothaire</a> went to <i><a href="/wiki/Worms,_Germany" title="Worms, Germany">Worms</a></i> to implore the pardon of his father.<sup id="cite_ref-Leuridan_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leuridan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_and_life_at_Cysoing">Marriage and life at Cysoing</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Marriage and life at Cysoing">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In return for his services, the emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> gave Count Evrard the highest honor possible: the hand of his (acknowledged) daughter, the <a href="/wiki/Gisela,_daughter_of_Louis_the_Pious" title="Gisela, daughter of Louis the Pious">Princess Gisèle</a>, in marriage.
</p><p>Gisèle, a woman of piety and virtue,<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis le Débonaire</a> and his second wife, the empress <a href="/wiki/Judith_(died_843)" class="mw-redirect" title="Judith (died 843)">Judith</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Leuridan_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leuridan-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
Among the rich domains the Princess brought with her in her dowry, Count Evrard found the <a href="/wiki/Fisc" title="Fisc">fisc</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>. One gives the name <a href="/wiki/Fisc" title="Fisc">fisc</a>, in this age, to large, rural properties separate from the royal domains; that is, to sorts of farms with a residence for the master and homes for settlers.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The Royal <a href="/wiki/Fisc" title="Fisc">Fisc</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>, situated at the center of the country of <a href="/w/index.php?title=P%C3%A8vele&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pèvele (page does not exist)">Pèvele</a>, was one of the most beautiful in the region.  The stay seemed so agreeable to Saint Evrard and the Princess Gisèle that they made it one of their regular residences.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Already, in the century before (in 752), the little hamlet established on the royal <a href="/wiki/Fisc" title="Fisc">fisc</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a> has been made famous through the martyrdom of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Arnoul_of_Cysoing" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Arnoul of Cysoing">Saint Arnoul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Arnoul_of_Cysoing" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Arnoul of Cysoing">Saint Arnoul</a>, a courageous warrior, who was, it is said, the father of Godefroid, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cambrai-Arras" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cambrai-Arras">Bishop of Cambrai-Arras</a>, had been attached to the court of a noble lord, his relative.  "His virtues and his merits were so radiant that God accorded his prayers more than one miracle during his life.  He became even more glorious through his martyrdom."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was so devoted to his master that he eventually died for him<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> thus attaining martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Arnoul_of_Cysoing" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Arnoul of Cysoing">Saint Arnoul</a> was already honored at <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a> when Saint Evrard and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Princess_Gis%C3%A8le&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Princess Gisèle (page does not exist)">Princess Gisèle</a> went to take possession of their domain.  His relics were conserved there. Cysoing therefore had <i>a church</i>, or less <i>a chapel</i> that was without doubt the same chapel as the royal <a href="/wiki/Fisc" title="Fisc">fisc</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Evrard, at Cysoing, had a chaplain named <a href="/w/index.php?title=Walgaire&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Walgaire (page does not exist)">Walgaire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> They (Evrard and Gisèle) decided to found a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>.  The project was long and difficult, and was not complete at the time of Evrard's or Gisèle's deaths.  The <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> was initially made in honor of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Saveur&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Saveur (page does not exist)">Saint Saveur</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Mary (mother of Jesus)</a>.  The religious lived there under <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a> in a community with all the rigors of the <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloister</a>.  Their special function was singing solemnly in the church.  They maintained public prayer.  Saint Evrard was known to enjoy singing with the choir.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After his later campaigns in the defense of Italy, the remains of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Callixtus_I" title="Pope Callixtus I">Pope Callixtus I</a> were re-interred in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_at_Cysoing&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey at Cysoing (page does not exist)">Abbey at Cysoing</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-dec16_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dec16-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Character">Character</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Character">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eberhard organized his home in a way so perfectly that it was more like a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> than a <a href="/wiki/Chateau" class="mw-redirect" title="Chateau">castle</a>.  He was seconded in this task by his pious wife, Gisèle, who dedicated herself to the education of their many children.  The poor and ill were sure of finding not only security at <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>, but also help and protection.  The social question of the time, that of <a href="/wiki/Serfs" class="mw-redirect" title="Serfs">serfs</a>, also preoccupied Saint Evrard.  He had freed a good number.  In their testimony, he expressly refrained from impeding their liberty.  He never forgot those who he didn't free, and tried to improve their lots.  Though he was a courageous and formidable, he worked all his life for peace.  His private virtues were no less remarkable.  In his elevated position, he strove to preserve modesty and humility, to avoid splendor and arrogance.  His zeal for the glory of God, to spread the Truth, to convert the infidels, was celebrated throughout the Church.  Also, his piety, his taste for ceremonies of worship, his devotion to the saints, and his respect for the precious relics was apparent in his every act.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pacifier">Pacifier</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Pacifier">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eberhard's activity was not limited to the royal <a href="/wiki/Fisc" title="Fisc">fisc</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>, as he involved himself freely with matters of other domains and the empire in general.  Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Debonaire</a> went to die (840) and the war, a cruel war without mercy, exploded between the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Lothair_II_of_Lotharingia" class="mw-redirect" title="Lothair II of Lotharingia">Lothaire</a> and his two brothers, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a> and <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a>.  Eberhard strongly deplored this fighting/battling and fratricide and made all efforts to bring it to an end.  After the bloody <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Fontenay_(841)" class="mw-redirect" title="Battle of Fontenay (841)">battle of Fontenay</a> (25 June 841), he left the ambassadorial envoy of <a href="/wiki/Lothair_II_of_Lotharingia" class="mw-redirect" title="Lothair II of Lotharingia">Lothaire</a> near that of <a href="/wiki/Lothair_II_of_Lotharingia" class="mw-redirect" title="Lothair II of Lotharingia">Lothaire</a>'s brothers for peace negotiations.  The preparatory conference took place in 842 at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Milin,_Champagne&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Milin, Champagne (page does not exist)">Milin</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Ch%C3%A2lons-en-Champagne" title="Châlons-en-Champagne">Châlons</a> in <a href="/wiki/Champagne,_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Champagne, France">Champagne</a>.  It was decided to divide the empire between the three brothers.  The negotiators, among which Evrard could be found, were charged with making the partitioning equitable/fair.  It was not before August 843 that they presented their report to the three kings at <a href="/wiki/Verdun" title="Verdun">Verdun</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Wars_with_the_Saracens">Wars with the Saracens</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Wars with the Saracens">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The negotiations ended and peace was re-established between the three brothers, Saint Evrard left in haste for <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>.  Italy was under threat from "<a href="/w/index.php?title=African_Saracens&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="African Saracens (page does not exist)">African Saracens</a>".  These <a href="/wiki/Saracens" class="mw-redirect" title="Saracens">Saracens</a><sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> had been named as helpers, in 842, by the Duke of <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Benevento" title="Duchy of Benevento">Benevento</a> and they would soon become a threat to regimes throughout the peninsula.  They menaced <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and pillaged it many times.  Saint Evrard, in his position as <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Friuli" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Friuli">Duke of Friuli</a>, was made a captain/leader of the resistance.  The war wore on for several years and ended in 851 with the defeat of the Saracens.
</p><p>"Evrard has a reputation for being both a courageous soldier and able leader throughout these battles.  In the tradition of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, Evrard forced the vanquished to convert to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, meritoriously teaching them the Gospel, himself."<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Testament_and_death">Testament and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Testament and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sometime after this solemnity, Eberhard returned to <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. We find him in 858 among the ambassadors whom the emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Younger" title="Louis the Younger">Louis the Younger</a>, son of <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothaire</a>, sent to <a href="/wiki/Ulm" title="Ulm">Ulm</a>, near his uncle <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a>. After this date, we know nothing more about Eberhard until his testament or will, whose authenticity is certain and in which we are given information on the life of Eberhard. This will was made in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, at <a href="/w/index.php?title=Musiestro_Castle&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Musiestro Castle (page does not exist)">Musiestro Castle</a>, in the county of <a href="/wiki/Treviso" title="Treviso">Trévise</a>, in 867.  Eberhard and his consort meticulously recorded not only their lands and possessions within a prepared will, but the identities and relationships of family members and neighboring royals. With the agreement of his spouse, Gisèle, Eberhard portioned his goods among his seven children.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The eldest, <a href="/wiki/Unruoch_III_of_Friuli" title="Unruoch III of Friuli">Unroch</a>, got all properties in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> and Germany.  The second, <a href="/wiki/Berengar_I_of_Italy" title="Berengar I of Italy">Berengar</a>, got <a href="/wiki/Annappes" title="Annappes">Annappes</a> with its dependencies less <a href="/wiki/Gruson" title="Gruson">Gruson</a> and the other properties in the <a href="/wiki/Hesbaye" title="Hesbaye">Hesbaye</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> of and in the <a href="/wiki/Condroz" title="Condroz">Condroz</a>.  The third, <a href="/wiki/Adelhard_von_Burc" title="Adelhard von Burc">Adélard</a>, got the lands of <a href="/wiki/Cysoing" title="Cysoing">Cysoing</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Camphin&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Camphin (page does not exist)">Camphin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gruson" title="Gruson">Gruson</a> and <a href="/wiki/Somain,_Nord" title="Somain, Nord">Somain</a>, with charges and respects of all the properties of the Abbey in these regions.  The fourth, Rodolphe, got <a href="/wiki/Vitry-en-Artois" title="Vitry-en-Artois">Vitry-en-Artois</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Mestucha&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Mestucha (page does not exist)">Mestucha</a>, except for the church at Vitry which was given with the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Abbey_at_Cysoing&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Abbey at Cysoing (page does not exist)">Abbey at Cysoing</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>The three daughters of Eberhard, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ingletrude&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ingletrude (page does not exist)">Ingletrude</a>, <a href="/wiki/Judith_of_Friuli" class="mw-redirect" title="Judith of Friuli">Judith</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Heilwich&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Heilwich (page does not exist)">Heilwich</a>, got various other domains&#160;: <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ermen_(Saxony)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ermen (Saxony) (page does not exist)">Ermen</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Marshem&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Marshem (page does not exist)">Marshem</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Balghingham&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Balghingham (page does not exist)">Balghingham</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Heliwsheim&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Heliwsheim (page does not exist)">Heliwsheim</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Hostrenheim&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Hostrenheim (page does not exist)">Hostrenheim</a>, <a href="/wiki/Luisinga" class="mw-redirect" title="Luisinga">Luisinga</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Wendossa&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Wendossa (page does not exist)">Wendossa</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Engerresteim&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Engerresteim (page does not exist)">Engerresteim</a>.  Eberhard had another daughter who carried the name of Gisèle, her mother.  But she was dead at the time of his testimony.  The testimony split equally the jewels and ornaments of the saint, the precious objects of his chapel and the books of his library.  It is dated 867, the 24th year of the reign of <a href="/wiki/Lothair_I" title="Lothair I">Lothaire</a>'s son, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Younger" title="Louis the Younger">Louis the Younger</a>. Eberhard died the same year, 16 December.<sup id="cite_ref-BATAILLE_4-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BATAILLE-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonisation">Canonisation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eberhard_of_Friuli&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Canonisation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eberhard was later canonised as a saint, and his feast day is on <b>16 December</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
