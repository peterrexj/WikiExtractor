<p><b>Cellach of Killala</b> (<a href="/wiki/Fl." class="mw-redirect" title="Fl.">fl.</a> mid-6th century) is supposed to be an early <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Killala" title="Bishop of Killala">Bishop of Killala</a>, in Ireland.
</p><p>Cellach appears among the saints of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_Fiachrach" title="Uí Fiachrach">Uí Fiachrach</a> in <i>Genealogiae Regum et Sanctorum Hiberniae</i>, where Walsh suggests he may have been the <i>Cellan Ua Fiachrach</i> who appears under 1 May. It is not certain if he ever existed, as the only source, <i>Betha Chellaig</i>, is a much later <a href="/wiki/Pseudo" class="mw-redirect" title="Pseudo">pseudo</a>-historical account found in <a href="/wiki/Leabhar_Breac" class="mw-redirect" title="Leabhar Breac">Leabhar Breac</a>.
</p><p>The account states he was the eldest son of <a href="/wiki/Eogan_Bel" class="mw-redirect" title="Eogan Bel">Eogan Bel</a>, King of Connacht, was taught by <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Ciarán of Clonmacnoise</a>. Ciarán made him a monk, and Cellach stayed with him until Eogan Bel was killed in the battle of Slicech (<a href="/wiki/Sligo" title="Sligo">Sligo</a>) against the northern <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Uí Néill">Uí Néill</a>, dated at 543, 546 or 547. He succeeded his father but under a curse from Ciarán, who foretold a dire fate.
</p><p>Cellach was eventually ousted and returned to Ciarán, with whom he remained until elevated to bishop of Killala in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Tuathal_Maelgarb" class="mw-redirect" title="Tuathal Maelgarb">Tuathal Maelgarb</a>. He later fled to a hermitage on <a href="/wiki/Lough_Conn" title="Lough Conn">Lough Conn</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Mayo" title="County Mayo">County Mayo</a>. He was said to have been murdered by <a href="/wiki/Guaire_Aidne_mac_Colm%C3%A1in" title="Guaire Aidne mac Colmáin">Guaire Aidhne</a>, who had him killed by four of his students who dumped his body in a hollow tree. Cellach's brother, Muiredach, is said to have avenged his death, and the four murderers buried under the <a href="/wiki/Dolmen_of_the_Four_Maols" title="Dolmen of the Four Maols">Dolmen of the Four Maols</a>, outside Ballina.
</p><p>The account has many chronological discrepancies.
</p>
