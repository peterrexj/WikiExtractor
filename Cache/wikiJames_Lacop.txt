<p>The <b>Martyrs of Gorkum</b> (<a href="/wiki/Dutch_language" title="Dutch language">Dutch</a>: <i lang="nl">Martelaren van Gorcum</i>) were a group of 19 <a href="/wiki/Dutch_people" title="Dutch people">Dutch</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> clerics, secular and religious,  who were hanged on 9 July 1572 in the town of <a href="/wiki/Brielle" title="Brielle">Brielle</a> by militant Dutch <a href="/wiki/Calvinists" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinists">Calvinists</a> during the <a href="/wiki/European_wars_of_religion" title="European wars of religion">16th-century religious wars</a>—specifically, the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_Revolt" class="mw-redirect" title="Dutch Revolt">Dutch Revolt</a> against Spanish rule, which developed into the <a href="/wiki/Eighty_Years%27_War" title="Eighty Years&#39; War">Eighty Years' War</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Events">Events</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Gorkum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Events">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the first half of the 16th century, various forms of <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestantism</a>—particularly, <a href="/wiki/Lutheranism" title="Lutheranism">Lutheranism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Calvinism" title="Calvinism">Calvinism</a>—were spreading through Western Europe. In the <a href="/wiki/Low_Countries" title="Low Countries">Low Countries</a>, then under the rule of Spain, Emperor <a href="/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor">Charles V</a> and his son King <a href="/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain" title="Philip II of Spain"> Philip II</a> instituted a systematic campaign to root out the new religious movements, which resulted in political resentment towards the authorities, including the Catholic Church. Inhabitants of the northern Netherlands who were primarily Protestant began to turn against the Catholic priests and monastics present.<sup id="cite_ref-Melveren_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Melveren-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 1572 the Netherlands were in open revolt against Spanish rule, while in the internal rivalry among the Protestant denominations, Calvinism managed to suppress Lutheranism. On 1 April of the next year, Calvinist forces and a rebel group called the <i>Watergeuzen</i> (<a href="/wiki/Sea_Beggars" class="mw-redirect" title="Sea Beggars">Sea Beggars</a>) <a href="/wiki/Capture_of_Brielle" title="Capture of Brielle"> captured Brielle</a> (Den Briel) and later <a href="/wiki/Vlissingen" title="Vlissingen">Vlissingen</a> (Flushing).<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In June, <a href="/wiki/Dordrecht" title="Dordrecht">Dordrecht</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gorkum" class="mw-redirect" title="Gorkum">Gorkum</a> fell, and at the latter the rebels captured nine <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> priests: <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Pieck" title="Nicholas Pieck">Nicholas Pieck</a>, guardian of Gorkum; Hieronymus of Weert, vicar; Theodorus van der Eem of Amersfoort; Nicasius Janssen of Heeze; Willehad of Denmark; Godefried of Mervel; Antonius of Weert; Antonius of Hoornaer, and Franciscus de Roye of <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a>. To these were added two <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brothers</a> from the same friary, Petrus of Assche and Cornelius of <a href="/wiki/Wijk_bij_Duurstede" title="Wijk bij Duurstede">Wijk bij Duurstede</a>. At almost the same time the Calvinists arrested the <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> of Gorkum, Leonardus Vechel of <a href="/wiki/%27s-Hertogenbosch" title="&#39;s-Hertogenbosch">'s-Hertogenbosch</a>, and his assistant.<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Also imprisoned were Godefried van Duynsen of Gorkum, a priest in his native city, and Joannes Lenartz of Oisterwijk, a <a href="/wiki/Canon_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon Regular">Canon Regular</a> from a nearby priory and <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> for the monastery of Augustinian nuns in Gorkum. To these fifteen were later added four more companions: <a href="/wiki/Joannes_van_Hoornaer" class="mw-redirect" title="Joannes van Hoornaer">Joannes van Hoornaer</a> (alias known as John of Cologne), a <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> of the Cologne province and parish priest not far from Gorkum, who when apprised of the incarceration of the clergy of Gorkum hastened to the city in order to administer the sacraments to them and was seized and imprisoned with the rest; Jacobus Lacops of Oudenaar, a <a href="/wiki/Norbertine" class="mw-redirect" title="Norbertine">Norbertine</a>, who became a <a href="/wiki/Curate" title="Curate">curate</a> in <a href="/wiki/Monster,_South_Holland" title="Monster, South Holland">Monster, South Holland</a>; Adrianus Janssen of <a href="/wiki/Hilvarenbeek" title="Hilvarenbeek">Hilvarenbeek</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Premonstratensian" class="mw-redirect" title="Premonstratensian">Premonstratensian</a> canon and at one time parish priest in Monster, who was sent to Brielle with Jacobus Lacops. Last was <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Wouters" title="Andrew Wouters"> Andreas Wouters</a> of Heynoord.<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg/220px-Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="304" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg/330px-Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg/440px-Johan_Zierneels_-_The_Apotheosis_of_the_Martyrs_of_Gorkum.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="2135" data-file-height="2946" /></a><figcaption>The Apotheosis of the Martyrs of Gorkum 1572, print made by <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Nolin" title="Jean-Baptiste Nolin">Jean-Baptiste Nolin</a> after a painting by <a href="/wiki/Johan_Zierneels" title="Johan Zierneels">Johan Zierneels</a>, 1675</figcaption></figure>
<p>In prison at Gorkum (from 26 June to 6 July 1572), the first 15 prisoners were transferred to Brielle, arriving there on 8 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Dominicans_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dominicans-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On their way to Dordrecht they were exhibited for money to the curious. The following day, <a href="/wiki/William_II_de_la_Marck" class="mw-redirect" title="William II de la Marck">William de la Marck, Lord of Lumey</a>, commander of the <i><a href="/wiki/Gueux_de_mer" class="mw-redirect" title="Gueux de mer">Gueux de mer</a></i>, had them interrogated and ordered a <a href="/wiki/Disputation" title="Disputation">disputation</a>. In the meantime, four others arrived. It was demanded of each that he abandon his belief in the <a href="/wiki/Transubstantiation" title="Transubstantiation">Transubstantiation</a>, the doctrine of the real presence of Christ in the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>, as well as the belief in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">Papal supremacy</a>. All remained firm in their faith. Meanwhile, there came a letter from the <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Orange" title="Prince of Orange">Prince of Orange</a>, <a href="/wiki/William_the_Silent" title="William the Silent">William the Silent</a>, which enjoined all those in authority to leave priests and <a href="/wiki/Religious_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious (Catholicism)">religious</a> unmolested. Despite this call, on 9 July, they were hanged in a <a href="/wiki/Peat" title="Peat">turfshed</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Gorkum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A shrub bearing 19 white flowers is said to have sprung up at the site of their martyrdom. Many miracles have since been attributed to the intercession of the Gorkum Martyrs, especially the curing of hernias.<sup id="cite_ref-Dominicans_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dominicans-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
The <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> of the martyrs took place on 14 November 1675, and their <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> on 29 June 1867.<sup id="cite_ref-oxford_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxford-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Their elevation to sainthood, which took place on the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">Feast of Saints Peter and Paul</a>, was part of grand celebrations marking 1,800 years since the traditional year for the martyrdom of the two apostles in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For many years the place of their martyrdom in Brielle has been the scene of numerous <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimages</a> and processions. The reliquary of their remains is now enshrined in the Church of Saint Nicholas, Brussels, Belgium.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_19_martyrs">The 19 martyrs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Gorkum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: The 19 martyrs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Godefroid_Coart.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Godefroid_Coart.jpg/90px-Godefroid_Coart.jpg" decoding="async" width="90" height="194" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Godefroid_Coart.jpg/135px-Godefroid_Coart.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Godefroid_Coart.jpg/180px-Godefroid_Coart.jpg 2x" data-file-width="278" data-file-height="598" /></a><figcaption>St. <a href="/wiki/Godfried_Coart" title="Godfried Coart">Godfried of Mervel, O.F.M.</a></figcaption></figure> 
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg/90px-Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg" decoding="async" width="90" height="196" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg/135px-Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg/180px-Gorcum_Antonius_van_Weert.jpg 2x" data-file-width="635" data-file-height="1381" /></a><figcaption>St. Anthony of Weert, O.F.M.</figcaption></figure> 
<p>There were 11 <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> friars or <i>Minderbroeders</i> (Friars Minor), one <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> friar or <i>Predikheer</i>, two <a href="/wiki/Norbertine" class="mw-redirect" title="Norbertine">Norbertine</a> <a href="/wiki/Canons_regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Canons regular">canons regular</a> and a local canon regular, or <i>witheren</i> and five <i>wereldheren</i> (<a href="/wiki/Secular_clergy" title="Secular clergy">secular clergy</a>). The 19 put to death on 9 July 1572 were:<sup id="cite_ref-albers_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-albers-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-butler_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ol><li>Leonard van Veghel (born 1527), spokesman, secular priest, and since 1566 pastor of <a href="/wiki/Gorinchem" title="Gorinchem">Gorkum</a></li>
<li>Peter of Assche (born 1530), Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Andrew_Wouters" title="Andrew Wouters">Andrew Wouters</a> (born 1542), secular priest, pastor of <a href="/wiki/Heinenoord" title="Heinenoord">Heinenoord</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Hoeksche_Waard" title="Hoeksche Waard">Hoeksche Waard</a></li>
<li>Nicasius of Heeze (born 1522), Franciscan friar, theologian and priest</li>
<li>Jerome of Weert (born 1522), Franciscan friar, priest, pastor in Gorcum</li>
<li>Anthony of Hoornaar, Franciscan friar and priest</li>
<li>Godfried van Duynen (born 1502), secular priest, former pastor in northern France</li>
<li>Willehad of Denmark (born 1482), Franciscan friar and priest</li>
<li>James Lacobs (born 1541), Norbertine canon</li>
<li>Francis of Roye (born 1549), Franciscan friar and priest</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/John_of_Cologne" title="John of Cologne">John of Cologne</a>, Dominican friar, pastor in <a href="/wiki/Hoornaar" title="Hoornaar">Hoornaar</a> near Gorkum</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Anthony_of_Weert" title="Anthony of Weert">Anthony of Weert</a> (born 1523), Franciscan friar and priest</li>
<li>Theodore of der Eem (born c. 1499–1502), Franciscan friar and priest, chaplain to a community of Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order">Tertiary</a> Sisters in Gorkum</li>
<li>Cornelius of Wijk bij Duurstede (born 1548), Franciscan lay brother</li>
<li>Adrian van Hilvarenbeek (born 1528), Norbertine canon and pastor in <a href="/wiki/Monster,_South_Holland" title="Monster, South Holland">Monster, South Holland</a></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Godfried_Coart" title="Godfried Coart">Godfried of Mervel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">Vicar</a> of Melveren, <a href="/wiki/Sint-Truiden" title="Sint-Truiden">Sint-Truiden</a> (born 1512), Franciscan priest, <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a> of the friary in Gorkum</li>
<li>Jan of Oisterwijk (born 1504), Augustinian <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a>, a chaplain for the <a href="/wiki/Beguinage" title="Beguinage">Beguinage</a> in Gorkum</li>
<li>Nicholas Poppel (born 1532), secular priest, <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> in Gorkum</li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Pieck" title="Nicholas Pieck">Nicholas Pieck</a> (born 1534), Franciscan friar, priest and theologian, <a href="/wiki/Custos_(Franciscans)" title="Custos (Franciscans)">Guardian</a> of the friary in Gorkum, his native city</li></ol>
