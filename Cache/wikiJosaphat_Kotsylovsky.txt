<p><b>Josaphat Joseph Kotsylovsky </b>(<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Йосафат Йосиф Коциловський</span>) was a <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic">Ukrainian Greek Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p><p>Kotsylovsky was born 3 March 1876 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Pakoszowka" class="mw-redirect" title="Pakoszowka">Pakoszowka</a> (then <a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austria-Hungary</a>, now <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a>), of the <a href="/wiki/Lemko_Region" title="Lemko Region">Lemko Region</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Sianok district,<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Kotsylovsky was of <a href="/wiki/Lemko" class="mw-redirect" title="Lemko">Lemko</a> origin, and <a href="/wiki/Ukrainians" title="Ukrainians">Ukrainian</a> national orientation.<sup id="cite_ref-e_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-e-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After graduating from the elementary folk school in <a href="/wiki/Lesko" title="Lesko">Lesko</a> he studied at the <a href="/wiki/Sanok" title="Sanok">Sanok</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sambir" title="Sambir">Sambir</a> gymnasia. From 1896 he studied at the law department of <a href="/wiki/University_of_Lviv" title="University of Lviv">Lviv University</a>. Soon he interrupted the studio and graduated from the school of artillery in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, and of 1900 he was sent to serve in the <a href="/wiki/Lviv" title="Lviv">Lviv</a> garrison. After leaving the military service, and with the assistance of the <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemysl</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Kostyantyn_Chekhovych" title="Kostyantyn Chekhovych">Kostyantyn Chekhovych</a>, he began the philosophical and theological studies in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. He studied <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and graduated in 1907, later that year on 9 October he was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-u_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-u-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Soon after, he was made <a href="/wiki/Vice-rector" class="mw-redirect" title="Vice-rector">vice-rector</a> and <a href="/wiki/Professor" title="Professor">professor</a> of theology at the Greek-Catholic seminary in <a href="/wiki/Stanislaviv" class="mw-redirect" title="Stanislaviv">Stanislaviv</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 2 October 1911 he entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Basil_the_Great" title="Order of Saint Basil the Great">Order of Saint Basil the Great</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-b_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On 23 September 1917 Kotsylovsky was ordained bishop in <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemyśl</a> (Poland) by <a href="/wiki/Andrey_Sheptytsky" title="Andrey Sheptytsky">Andrey Sheptytsky</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  As bishop, he worked to improve the church's educational system and supported <a href="/wiki/Monastic_orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic orders">monastic orders</a>. He also took steps to combat the rising <a href="/wiki/Western_Ukrainian_Russophiles" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Ukrainian Russophiles">Russophile</a> movement by appointing Ukrainian priests and funding <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian language</a> <a href="/wiki/Magazine" title="Magazine">journals</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-e_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-e-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 10 July 1941 he welcomed the <a href="/wiki/Wehrmacht" title="Wehrmacht">Wehrmacht</a> forces entering <a href="/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl" title="Przemyśl">Przemyśl</a>. On 4 July 1943 Kotsylovsky led a Mass in the name of the volunteers entering the <a href="/wiki/14th_Waffen_Grenadier_Division_of_the_SS_(1st_Galician)" title="14th Waffen Grenadier Division of the SS (1st Galician)">14th SS Division</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>At the end of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, <a href="/wiki/Communist_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist Poland">Communist Poland</a> assisted the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet Union</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic#Soviet_annexation_of_the_Eastern_Poland_and_liquidation_of_the_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Greek Catholic">liquidation of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church</a>.  In September 1945 the Polish security service arrested Kotsylovsky, then released him and arrested him again in 1946.<sup id="cite_ref-b_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-b-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  They then handed him over to the Soviet security service. He died on 17 November 1947 in a prison camp near <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kyiv</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-u_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-u-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 27 June 2001.<sup id="cite_ref-v_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-v-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-s_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-s-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The relics of Josaphat Kotsylovsky are kept in the church of the Annunciation of the Blessed Virgin Mary in <a href="/wiki/Stryi" title="Stryi">Stryi</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Testimony_of_Father_Josaphat_Kavatsiv">Testimony of Father Josaphat Kavatsiv</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Josaphat_Kotsylovsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Testimony of Father Josaphat Kavatsiv">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Josaphat Kavatsiv states a second hand account of Bishop Josaphat Kotsylovskyi martyrdom as following: 
</p><p>"I came to the Protection Monastery and the hegumena [prioress] told me the story. When Bishop Kotsylovskyi was arrested, their Orthodox bishop of Kyiv was arrested at the same time. When they brought a package to Chapaievka, the Orthodox bishop said: 'Uniate Bishop Josaphat Kotsylovskyi is confined in the same camp with me.'  And he asked these nuns, if they could, to bring a package to Bishop Josaphat as well. So they brought packages- one for each of the bishops... Once when she brought a package,the bishop said that Kotsylovskyi had died. And he asked her, because the dead were all thrown into one hole,if they could borrow some money or get some money somewhere. He asked her 'to bury him in a separate grave, because this was a holy man.'"<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
