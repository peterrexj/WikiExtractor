<p><b>Agnes of Jesus</b>, OP (born <b>Agnès Galand</b> and also known as <b>Agnes of Langeac</b>; November 17, 1602 – October 19, 1634<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>. She was <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prioress</a> of her monastery at <a href="/wiki/Langeac" title="Langeac">Langeac</a>, and is venerated in the Catholic Church, having been <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on November 20, 1994.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Agnès Galand was born on November 17, 1602, in <a href="/wiki/Le_Puy-en-Velay" title="Le Puy-en-Velay">Le Puy-en-Velay</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>, the third of seven children of Pierre Galand, a <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cutler" class="extiw" title="wikt:cutler">cutler</a> by trade, and his wife, Guillemette Massiote.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-vita_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vita-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> When she was five years old, Galand was entrusted to a <a href="/wiki/Catholic_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic religious order">religious institute</a> for her education.<sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Even from that early age, she showed a strong sense of spiritual maturity. She consecrated herself to the Virgin Mary at the age of seven.
</p><p>Galand joined the Dominican Monastery of St. Catherine of Siena at <a href="/wiki/Langeac" title="Langeac">Langeac</a> in 1623.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-moniales1_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moniales1-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> At her receiving of the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> she took the name <b>Agnes of Jesus</b>. Soon after her own <a href="/wiki/Profession" title="Profession">profession</a>, she was assigned to serve as the <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">Mistress of novices</a> for the community. Galand was elected to lead her community as <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">prioress</a> in 1627.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-moniales2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moniales2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She was later deposed from this office, but she accepted her demotion with indifference and grace.<sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She died on October 19, 1634, in Langeac.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spiritual_legacy">Spiritual legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Spiritual legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Notable_visions">Notable visions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Notable visions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Galand was noted even during her lifetime as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Louis_de_Montfort" title="Louis de Montfort">Louis Marie de Montfort</a> records the following anecdote:
</p>
<blockquote><p>I shall simply relate an incident which I read in the life of Mother Agnes of Jesus, a Dominican nun of the convent of Langeac in Auvergne. ... One day the Blessed Virgin appeared to Mother Agnes and put a gold chain around her neck to show her how happy she was that Mother Agnes had become the slave of both her and her Son. And St. Cecilia, who accompanied our Lady, said to her, "Happy are the faithful slaves of the Queen of Heaven, for they will enjoy true freedom."<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>In 1631, Galand experienced the most famous of her visions, in which the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> urged her to pray for an unknown <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">priest</a> with the command, "Pray to my Son for the <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A9" title="Abbé">Abbé</a> of Prébrac (near <a href="/wiki/Cugnaux" title="Cugnaux">Cugnaux</a>)."<sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Jean-Jacques_Olier" title="Jean-Jacques Olier">Jean-Jacques Olier</a> was the current holder of that office, and while at a retreat led by <a href="/wiki/Vincent_de_Paul" title="Vincent de Paul">Vincent de Paul</a>, he experienced a vision in which Galand appeared to him, though he was unacquainted with her.<sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He sought out the nun who had appeared to him in the dream. When he met Galand, she told him: "I have received orders from the Holy Virgin to pray for you. God has destined you to open the first seminaries in France."<sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Olier would go on to found the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Saint-Sulpice" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Saint-Sulpice">Society of Saint-Sulpice</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-moniales2_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moniales2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Before her death, she related to her community her great desire that they pray for priests.<sup id="cite_ref-moniales2_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moniales2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> She also had visions of both her <a href="/wiki/Guardian_angel" title="Guardian angel">guardian angel</a> and <a href="/wiki/Satan" title="Satan">Satan</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_beatification">Veneration and beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Agnes_of_Jesus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration and beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>A cause for her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> was introduced on April 19, 1713.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> She was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a> on March 19, 1808, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a>. Pope John Paul II beatified her on November 20, 1994.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At her beatification ceremony, John Paul II called Galand "truly blessed", noting her willingness to submit to <a href="/wiki/Will_of_God" title="Will of God">God's plan</a> for her, "offering her intellect, will, and freedom to the Son of Man, that he might transform them and harmonize them totally with his own!"<sup id="cite_ref-jp2_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jp2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-cristore_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cristore-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Her feast day is October 19.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Hyacinthe-Marie_Cormier" title="Hyacinthe-Marie Cormier">Hyacinthe-Marie Cormier</a>, beatified on the same day as Galand, cited the example of Galand's life as his inspiration for joining the Dominican Order.<sup id="cite_ref-cristore_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cristore-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> He would go on to be elected the seventy-sixth <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">Master General</a> of the Dominicans in 1904.
</p>
