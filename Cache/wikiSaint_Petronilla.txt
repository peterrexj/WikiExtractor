<p><b>Petronilla</b> (<b>Aurelia Petronilla</b>)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> is an <a href="/wiki/List_of_early_Christian_saints" title="List of early Christian saints">early Christian saint</a>. She was venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">martyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.  She died in <a href="/wiki/History_of_Rome#Roman_Empire" title="History of Rome">Rome</a>  at the end of the 1st century, or possibly in the 3rd century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Identity">Identity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petronilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Identity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Petronilla is traditionally identified as the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a>, though this may stem simply from the similarity of names.  It is believed she may have been a convert of Peter (and thus a "spiritual daughter"), or a follower or servant.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  It is said that Peter cured her of <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/palsy" class="extiw" title="wikt:palsy">palsy</a>.
</p><p>Roman inscriptions, however, identify her simply as a martyr.  She may have been related to <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Domitilla_(wife_of_Clemens)" title="Flavia Domitilla (wife of Clemens)">Domitilla</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Stories associated with her include those that relate that she was so beautiful that Peter had locked her up in a tower to keep her from eligible men; that a pagan king named <a href="/wiki/Flaccus" class="mw-redirect" title="Flaccus">Flaccus</a>, wishing to marry her, led Petronilla to go on a <a href="/wiki/Hunger_strike" title="Hunger strike">hunger strike</a>, from which she died.
</p><p>In the 4th-century Roman catalogue of martyrs' feasts, which is used in the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrologium_Hieronymianum" title="Martyrologium Hieronymianum">Martyrologium Hieronymianum</a></i>, her name seems not to have been inserted. It occurs in the latter martyrology,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> but only as a later addition. Her name is given under 31 May and the Martyrologies of <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> and his imitators adopt the same date.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The absence of her name from the 4th-century Roman calendar of feasts suggests that Petronilla died at the end of the first or during the 2nd century, since no special feasts for martyrs were celebrated during this period.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> After the erection of the basilica over her remains and those of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Nereus_and_Achilleus" title="Saints Nereus and Achilleus">Nereus and Achilleus</a> in the 4th century, her cult extended widely and her name was therefore admitted later into the martyrology. A legend, the existence of which in the 6th century is proved by its presence in the list of the tombs of the Roman martyrs prepared by Abbot John at the end of this century,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> regards Petronilla as a real daughter of the apostle Peter. In the <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a> <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Peter" title="Acts of Peter">Acts of Peter</a>, dating from the 2nd century, a daughter of Peter is mentioned, although her name is not given.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Petronilla was identified at Rome with this supposed daughter of Peter, probably because of her name and the great antiquity of her tomb. She appears in the legendary Acts of the martyrs Nereus and Achilleus and in the <i>Liber Pontificalis</i>, but as a virgin rather than as a martyr. From this legend of Nereus and Achilleus, a similar notice was admitted into the historical martyrologies of the Middle Ages and from there into the modern <i>Roman Martyrology</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petronilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Burial">Burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petronilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Almost all the 6th- and 7th-century lists of the tombs of the most highly venerated Roman martyrs mention Petronilla's grave as situated in the <a href="/wiki/Via_Ardeatina" title="Via Ardeatina">Via Ardeatina</a> near Nereus and Achilleus.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> These notices have been completely confirmed by the excavations in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacomb">Catacomb</a> of Domitilla. One topography of the graves of the Roman martyrs, <i>Epitome libri de locis sanctorum martyrum</i>, locates on the Via Ardeatina a church of St. Petronilla, in which Nereus and Achilleus, as well as Petronilla, were buried.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This church, built into the above-mentioned catacomb, has been discovered, and the memorials found in it removed all doubt that the tombs of the three saints were once venerated there.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A painting, in which Petronilla is represented as receiving a deceased person (named Veneranda) into heaven, was discovered on the closing stone of a tomb in an underground crypt behind the apse of the basilica.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Beside the saint's picture is her name: Petronilla Mart. (yr). That the painting was done shortly after 356, is proved by an <a href="/wiki/Inscription" class="mw-redirect" title="Inscription">inscription</a> found in the tomb.
</p><p>It is thus clearly established that Petronilla was venerated at Rome as a martyr in the 4th century, and the testimony must be accepted as certainly historical, notwithstanding the later legend which recognizes her only as a virgin (see below). Another known, but unfortunately no longer extant, memorial was the marble <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> which contained her remains, under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_I" title="Pope Paul I">Pope Paul I</a> translated to <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>. In the account of this in the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i> the inscription carved on the sarcophagus is given thus: <i>Aureae Petronillae Filiae Dulcissimae</i> ("of the golden Petronilla, the sweetest daughter"). The sarcophagus was discovered, in the very chapel dedicated to her in <a href="/wiki/Old_St_Peter%27s" class="mw-redirect" title="Old St Peter&#39;s">Old St Peter's</a>, under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sixtus_IV" title="Pope Sixtus IV">Pope Sixtus IV</a>, who hastened to inform <a href="/wiki/Louis_XI_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis XI of France">Louis XI of France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Extant 16th-century notices concerning this sarcophagus assert that the first word was <i>Aur,</i> (Aureliae), so that the martyr's name was Aurelia Petronilla. The second name comes from <i><a href="/wiki/Petro" title="Petro">Petro</a></i> or <a href="/wiki/Petronius" title="Petronius">Petronius</a>, and, as the name of the great-grandfather of the Christian consul, <a href="/wiki/Titus_Flavius_Clemens_(consul)" title="Titus Flavius Clemens (consul)">Titus Flavius Clemens</a>, was <a href="/wiki/Titus_Flavius_Petro" title="Titus Flavius Petro">Titus Flavius Petro</a>, it is very possible that Petronilla was a relative of the <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Flavia_(gens)" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavia (gens)">Flavii</a>, who were descended from the senatorial family of the <a href="/wiki/Aurelia_(gens)" class="mw-redirect" title="Aurelia (gens)">Aurelii</a>. This theory would also explain why Petronilla was buried in the catacomb of the <a href="/wiki/Flavia_Domitilla_(saint)" class="mw-redirect" title="Flavia Domitilla (saint)">Flavian Domitilla</a>. Like the latter, Petronilla may have suffered during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Domitian" title="Domitian">Domitian</a>, perhaps not till later.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Chapel_of_St_Petronilla">Chapel of St Petronilla</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petronilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Chapel of St Petronilla">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Honorius" title="Mausoleum of Honorius">Mausoleum of Honorius</a></div>
<p>In 757 the <a href="/wiki/Coffin" title="Coffin">coffin</a> containing the mortal remains of Petronilla was transferred to an old circular building (the <a href="/wiki/Mausoleum" title="Mausoleum">mausoleum</a> of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Honorius_(emperor)" title="Honorius (emperor)">Honorius</a><sup id="cite_ref-cambridge.org_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cambridge.org-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> dating from the end of the 4th century) near <a href="/wiki/Old_St_Peter%27s" class="mw-redirect" title="Old St Peter&#39;s">Old St Peter's</a>. This building was altered and became the Chapel of St Petronilla.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her chapel became the burial place for French kings.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>  Her association with the French crown stems from the fact that <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carloman,_son_of_Pippin_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Carloman, son of Pippin III">Carloman</a> were considered Peter's adopted sons after 800.  Petronilla, as the supposed daughter of Peter, became their patroness and of the treaties concluded between the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> emperors.
</p><p>When St Peter's was rebuilt in the 16th century, the old chapel and former mausoleum was demolished<sup id="cite_ref-cambridge.org_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cambridge.org-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Pearce_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pearce-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> and Petronilla's relics were translated to an altar dedicated to her in the upper end of the right side-aisle of the new basilica (near the cupola). The chapel includes embellishments by <a href="/wiki/Michelangelo" title="Michelangelo">Michelangelo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bramante" class="mw-redirect" title="Bramante">Bramante</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Guercino" title="Guercino">Guercino</a> painted an altarpiece called <i><a href="/wiki/The_Burial_of_St._Petronilla" title="The Burial of St. Petronilla">The Burial of St. Petronilla</a></i> in 1623.  In 1730 the painting was moved to the Quirinal Palace. Its place over the altar was taken by a fine mosaic copy executed by Pietro Paolo Cristofari (1685-1743). Guercino's original was shipped to Paris in 1797, was returned to Rome in 1815, and is today in the Capitoline Museums. The painting simultaneously depicts the burial and the welcoming to heaven of the martyred Saint Petronilla. The altar is dedicated to the saint, and contains her <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.
</p><p>Her feast falls on 31 May.  Mass on this day in St. Peter's is offered for France and attended by French residents of Rome.
</p><p>She is patroness of the <a href="/wiki/Dauphin_of_France" title="Dauphin of France">dauphins</a> of France because a <a href="/wiki/Dolphin" title="Dolphin">dolphin</a> (in <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>, <i>dauphin</i>) was reputedly found carved on her <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saintpetronille.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/Saintpetronille.jpg/220px-Saintpetronille.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="339" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/da/Saintpetronille.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="306" data-file-height="472" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saintpetronille.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A fictional portrait of Saint Petronilla.</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ambrogio_Lorenzetti" title="Ambrogio Lorenzetti">Ambrogio Lorenzetti</a> painted a <i>Santa Petronilla Altarpiece</i> in the 1340s.  <i>The Healing of St Petronilla</i> is the name of a tapestry made for Guillaume de Hellande, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Beauvais" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Beauvais">Bishop of Beauvais</a>, in the 15th century.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="St_Petronilla.27s.2C_Whepstead"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St_Petronilla's,_Whepstead">St Petronilla's, Whepstead</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Petronilla&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: St Petronilla&#039;s, Whepstead">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The only <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Anglican</a> church dedicated to Petronilla is the parish church of the village of <a href="/wiki/Whepstead" title="Whepstead">Whepstead</a> in <a href="/wiki/Suffolk" title="Suffolk">Suffolk</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> At the time of the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a> part of the income of the leper hospital of St Petronilla in <a href="/wiki/Bury_St_Edmunds" title="Bury St Edmunds">Bury St Edmunds</a> came from its holdings in Whepstead, which may explain the village's unusual dedication.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In Gloucester a chapel and altar were built to Saint Petronilla in the Abbey built by Osric, a Mercian Prince in 679 AD. This is now probably located beneath the Lady Chapel in the present Norman Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
