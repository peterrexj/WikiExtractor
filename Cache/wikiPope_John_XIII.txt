<p><b>Pope John XIII</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ioannes XIII</i>; died 6 September 972) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 1 October 965 to his death.<sup id="cite_ref-ce_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His pontificate was caught up in the continuing conflict between the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman emperor">Holy Roman emperor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Otto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I">Otto I</a>, and the Roman nobility. After long and arduous negotiations, he succeeded in arranging a Byzantine marriage for <a href="/wiki/Otto_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto II">Otto II</a>, in an effort to legitimize the <a href="/wiki/Ottonian" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottonian">Ottonian</a> claim to imperial dignity. He also established church hierarchy in Poland and Bohemia.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family_and_early_career">Family and early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family and early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, John was the son of another John, who was a <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>. It has been conjectured that his father was the Roman noble John Crescentius, a member of the <a href="/wiki/Crescentii" title="Crescentii">Crescentii</a> family who had married into the family of Count <a href="/wiki/Theophylact_I_of_Tusculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Theophylact I of Tusculum">Theophylact I of Tusculum</a>. If so, his father had previously been a duke, and possibly even appointed consul, prior to his ordination as bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._286_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._286-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Consequently, John was probably the brother of <a href="/wiki/Crescentius_the_Elder" title="Crescentius the Elder">Crescentius the Elder</a> (the <i><a href="/wiki/Patrician_(post-Roman_Europe)" title="Patrician (post-Roman Europe)">patricius romanorum</a></i>), as well as Stephania, lady of <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a> (who married Count Benedict, rector of the <a href="/wiki/Sabina_(region)" title="Sabina (region)">Sabina</a>) and Marozia, who married Count <a href="/wiki/Gregory_I_of_Tusculum" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory I of Tusculum">Gregory I of Tusculum</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brought up at the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Lateran palace">Lateran palace</a>, he was a member of the <i><a href="/wiki/Schola_cantorum_(papal_choir)" class="mw-redirect" title="Schola cantorum (papal choir)">schola cantorum</a></i>, and his career during that time saw him pass through a number of positions, including that of <i><a href="/wiki/Ostiarius" title="Ostiarius">Ostiarius</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Reader_(liturgy)" title="Reader (liturgy)">Reader</a>, <a href="/wiki/Exorcist" title="Exorcist">Exorcist</a> and <a href="/wiki/Acolyte" title="Acolyte">Acolyte</a> before reaching the ranks of <a href="/wiki/Subdeacon" title="Subdeacon">Subdeacon</a> and then <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">Deacon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> After leaving the schola, he took an active part in papal administration, serving in the <a href="/wiki/Chancery_of_Apostolic_Briefs" class="mw-redirect" title="Chancery of Apostolic Briefs">Chancery of Apostolic Briefs</a> under popes <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XII" title="Pope John XII">John XII</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_VIII" title="Pope Leo VIII">Leo VIII</a>. He also served as <a href="/wiki/Vatican_Library" title="Vatican Library">Librarian of the Holy Apostolic See</a> in 961.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._284_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._284-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> At some point he was appointed as the bishop of <a href="/wiki/Narni" title="Narni">Narni</a>, as which he participated in the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Rome_(963)" title="Synod of Rome (963)">Synod of Rome (963)</a> which deposed <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XII" title="Pope John XII">Pope John XII</a>, as well as the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Rome_(964)" title="Synod of Rome (964)">Synod of Rome (964)</a> which saw his restoration.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Accession_and_rebellion">Accession and rebellion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Accession and rebellion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the death of Leo VIII, the Roman nobility asked the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman emperor">Holy Roman emperor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Otto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I">Otto I</a>, for the reinstatement of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_V" title="Pope Benedict V">Pope Benedict V</a>. When this was not possible, Bishop John Crescentius was suggested as a compromise candidate by Otto's envoys, the bishops Otger of Spiers and <a href="/wiki/Liutprand_of_Cremona" title="Liutprand of Cremona">Liutprand of Cremona</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Elected unanimously, John was consecrated on Sunday, 1 October 965, five months after the death of Leo VIII.
</p><p>John immediately took on the task of curbing the power of the Roman nobility. He gave members of the <a href="/wiki/Crescentii" title="Crescentii">Crescentii</a> family important positions to shore up his support, while he also sought closer ties with the emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> However, with the emperor back in Germany, various local powers decided to take advantage of his absence to intrigue against John XIII. The former king <a href="/wiki/Adalbert_of_Italy" title="Adalbert of Italy">Adalbert of Italy</a> had appeared in front of an army in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a>, whilst the Roman nobility, disliking John's behaviour, and resenting his imposition by a foreign power, staged a revolt. Under the leadership of Peter, the <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">Prefect</a> of the city, together with Rofred, the Count of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Campagna" title="Roman Campagna">Roman Campagna</a> and the <i><a href="/wiki/Vestararius" title="Vestararius">Vestararius</a></i> Stephen, they roused the Roman nobility by declaring that 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>”The Saxon kings were going to destroy their power and influence, and were going to lead their children into captivity.”<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._286_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._286-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The leaders of the Roman militia captured the pope on December 16, 965, and imprisoned him in <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Castel Sant'Angelo</a>. However, fearing John's presence there would inspire resistance from his followers, the pope was moved to one of Rofred's castles in the Campagna.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Word eventually reached Otto of all these disturbances, who entered Italy in late summer of 966 at the head of an enormous army. In the meantime, John had managed to escape from Campagna, and made his way to <a href="/wiki/Capua" title="Capua">Capua</a>, placing himself under the protection of <a href="/wiki/Pandulf_Ironhead" title="Pandulf Ironhead">Pandulf Ironhead</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In thanks for Pandulf's aid, John converted Capua into a <a href="/wiki/Metropolis_(religious_jurisdiction)" title="Metropolis (religious jurisdiction)">Metropolitan see</a>, and consecrated as its first <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> Pandulf's brother John, on August 14, 966.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In Rome, the pope's supporters rose up, and Rofred and Stephen were killed by John Crescentius, the pope's nephew. Pope John left Capua, and crossed into <a href="/wiki/Sabina_(region)" title="Sabina (region)">Sabina</a>, where he was met by his brother-in-law, Benedict, who also offered John his support. With Rome effectively back in his hands, John returned and was welcomed back into the city on November 14, 966.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Although he was initially lenient towards the rebels, the arrival of Otto saw a change in approach. The emperor banished to Germany the two men appointed consul; the twelve principal militia leaders (the <i>Decarcones</i>, one appointed to each of the city's <a href="/wiki/14_regions_of_Medieval_Rome" title="14 regions of Medieval Rome">twelve regions</a>) were hanged. Other plotters were either executed or blinded. The Prefect of the City, Peter, was handed over to John, who ordered him to be hung by his hair from the <a href="/wiki/Equestrian_Statue_of_Marcus_Aurelius" title="Equestrian Statue of Marcus Aurelius">Equestrian Statue of Marcus Aurelius</a>, after which he was taken down, placed naked and backwards on an ass. A bag of feathers was placed upon his head and two more at his thighs. With a bell fastened round its neck, Peter was driven through the city, and after being thus exposed to the ridicule of the people, Peter was cast into a dungeon before finally being sent by the emperor into Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In gratitude for the emperor's intervention, John lauded him by declaring him to be the liberator and restorer of the Church, the illustrious guest, and three times blessed emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 969, he met Gerbert d'Aurillac, the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Pope Sylvester II</a>, and was so impressed by his scholarly brilliance that he kept him on in Rome in order to learn from him.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Troubles_with_the_Byzantine_Empire">Troubles with the Byzantine Empire</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Troubles with the Byzantine Empire">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_(cropped).png" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_%28cropped%29.png/220px-Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_%28cropped%29.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="273" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_%28cropped%29.png/330px-Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_%28cropped%29.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_%28cropped%29.png/440px-Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_%28cropped%29.png 2x" data-file-width="1407" data-file-height="1747" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Rivista_italiana_di_numismatica_1890_p_092_(cropped).png" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Coins of John XIII</div></div></div>
<p>After John XIII's restoration, he worked with the Emperor on ecclesiastical improvements. It was decided in a council held at Rome in the beginning of 967 in the emperor's presence that <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Grado" title="Patriarch of Grado">Grado</a> was to be the patriarchal and metropolitan church of the whole of the <a href="/wiki/Veneto" title="Veneto">Veneto</a>. At another council at <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> in April 967, Otto again "restored to the apostolic Pope John the city and territory of Ravenna and many other possessions which had for some time been lost to the Popes."<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> At around this time he also created, at Otto's request, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Magdeburg" title="Archbishopric of Magdeburg">Archbishopric of Magdeburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Then, on Christmas Day in 967, John XIII crowned Otto I's son <a href="/wiki/Otto_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto II, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto II</a> as co-emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Various synods were held before the emperors left Rome for the south of Italy, in which, sometimes at their request, John XIII took several German monasteries under his special protection, or decided that in some cases they were to remain forever "under the patronage (<i>mundiburdium</i>) of the kings or emperors."<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> With Otto I seeking a marriage alliance with the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> through his son and a Byzantine princess, John XIII lent his support to Otto's cause. He wrote a letter to the Eastern emperor, <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_II_Phokas" title="Nikephoros II Phokas">Nikephoros II Phokas</a>, but ended up insulting him by referring to him, not as "emperor of the <a href="/wiki/Names_of_the_Greeks#Romans_(Ῥωμαῖοι)" title="Names of the Greeks">Romans</a>", but as "emperor of the Greeks".<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> As his price for the marriage, Otto demanded a dowry from the Eastern Empire, that of the <a href="/wiki/Theme_(Byzantine_district)" title="Theme (Byzantine district)">themes</a> of <a href="/wiki/Longobardia" title="Longobardia">Longobardia</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Calabria_(theme)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Calabria (theme) (page does not exist)">Calabria</a>. Nikephoros retorted by instead demanding the restitution of the <a href="/wiki/Exarchate_of_Ravenna" title="Exarchate of Ravenna">Exarchate of Ravenna</a>, which included Rome and the Papal States, as the price for the imperial marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> When negotiations broke down, Nikephoros refused to write to John XIII in his own hand, instead sending him a threatening letter written by his brother, <a href="/wiki/Leo_Phokas_the_Younger" title="Leo Phokas the Younger">Leo Phokas the Younger</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the failure of negotiations, Nikephoros attempted to extend the ecclesiastical jurisdiction of the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch of Constantinople">patriarch of Constantinople</a> into the pope's jurisdiction in southern Italy. The eastern emperor ordered the patriarch to transform the bishopric of <a href="/wiki/Otranto" title="Otranto">Otranto</a> into a metropolitan see, and to ensure that services were no longer said in Latin, but in Greek only. <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Polyeuctus_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Polyeuctus of Constantinople">Patriarch Polyeuctus of Constantinople</a> quickly addressed an order to the head of the Church of Otranto giving him authority to consecrate bishops in the churches of <a href="/wiki/Acerenza" title="Acerenza">Acerenza</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tursi" title="Tursi">Tursi</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gravina_in_Puglia" title="Gravina in Puglia">Gravina</a>, <a href="/wiki/Matera" title="Matera">Matera</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Tricarico" title="Tricarico">Tricarico</a>, all previously dependent on the Church of Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> In response, and at the request of the Western emperor, John convened a <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> in 969, which elevated the bishopric of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Benevento" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Benevento">Benevento</a> into a metropolitan see, thus reducing the influence of the Byzantine Empire and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> there.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The death of Nikephoros Phokas in 969 saw the elevation of <a href="/wiki/John_I_Tzimiskes" title="John I Tzimiskes">John I Tzimiskes</a>. He entered into negotiations with Otto I, and soon Otto II was betrothed to <a href="/wiki/Theophanu" title="Theophanu">Theophanu</a>, the niece of Tzimiskes. The marriage was performed by John XIII at Rome on 14 April 972.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relations_with_other_states">Relations with other states</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Relations with other states">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was also involved in the development of ecclesiastical structures across Europe. In 968, John appointed the first bishop in Poland, <a href="/wiki/Jordan_(Bishop_of_Poland)" class="mw-redirect" title="Jordan (Bishop of Poland)">Jordan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kłoczowski2000_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kłoczowski2000-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> In 973, John appointed <a href="/wiki/Mlada_(abbess)" title="Mlada (abbess)">Mlada</a>, a sister of Duke <a href="/wiki/Boleslaus_II_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Boleslaus II of Bohemia">Boleslaus II of Bohemia</a>, as an <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine Order</a>. He gave her a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> which authorised the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Prague" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Prague">Bishopric of Prague</a> in accordance with the wishes of Boleslaus, which had been made through Mlada. John decreed that the church of <a href="/wiki/St._Vitus_Cathedral" title="St. Vitus Cathedral">Saints Vitus and Wenceslaus</a> should be the new cathedral church. At the church of St. George, a convent of nuns was to be established, over which Mlada was to preside. Finally, the <a href="/wiki/Latin_rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin rite">Latin rite</a> and not the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine rite">Byzantine rite</a> was to be followed, and someone who was well instructed in Latin literature had to be chosen as the first bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 971, John XIII published a bull supporting the action of the English king <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceful" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceful">Edgar the Peaceful</a> and <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_Dunstan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop Dunstan">Archbishop Dunstan</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canons</a> of <a href="/wiki/Winchester_Cathedral" title="Winchester Cathedral">Winchester Cathedral</a>, who refused to give up their wives and concubines.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> In that same year, John confirmed the privileges which King Edgar had granted the monks of <a href="/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey" title="Glastonbury Abbey">Glastonbury Abbey</a>, and declared that it was under papal protection.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> Further, John sent a letter to an <a href="/wiki/Ealdorman" title="Ealdorman">ealdorman</a> named Aelfric, commanding him to cease taking money from Glastonbury.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>, John appointed the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Trier">archbishop of Trier</a> as the <a href="/wiki/Papal_vicar" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal vicar">papal vicar</a>, responsible for promoting the decrees of any synods held in Germany or <a href="/wiki/West_Francia" title="West Francia">West Francia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup> He also granted numerous privileges across Europe. In one case, dated September 29, 970, for the monastery of St. Vincent of <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>, we find the first recorded grant of the <a href="/wiki/Pontificals" class="mw-redirect" title="Pontificals">Pontificals</a>. Its abbot was granted the use, under certain conditions, of the <a href="/wiki/Dalmatic" title="Dalmatic">Dalmatic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_sandals" title="Episcopal sandals">Episcopal sandals</a>. John was also the recipient of many requests for help. In one case, the monks of the monastery of St. Peter at Novalisa, asked for the pope to intervene to help protect them against a local count named Ardoin.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> In another case, in November 971, Archbishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Adalberon_of_Reims&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Adalberon of Reims (page does not exist)">Adalberon of Reims</a> went to Rome to ask the pope to confirm the archbishop's decision to leave some property to the monks of Mouzon Abbey, thereby protecting his donation from King <a href="/wiki/Louis_IV_of_France" title="Louis IV of France">Louis IV of France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 970, John bestowed the town of <a href="/wiki/Praeneste" class="mw-redirect" title="Praeneste">Praeneste</a> as a hereditary lease to Senatrix Stephania, who was probably his sister. Praeneste was to belong to her, her children, and her grandchildren, for a yearly rent of ten gold <a href="/wiki/Solidi" class="mw-redirect" title="Solidi">solidi</a>, but it was afterwards to return to the Church. It is one of the first examples of the introduction of the system of <a href="/wiki/Feudalism" title="Feudalism">feudalism</a> into Roman territory.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_Lore">Death and Lore</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_John_XIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and Lore">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John XIII died on 6 September 972, and was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Paul_Outside_the_Walls" title="Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls">Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._303_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._303-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Benedict_VI" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict VI">Benedict VI</a>. The earliest surviving written mention of the ancient practice of the blessing of church bells comes from his reign.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Referred to by one chronicler after his death as "The Good",<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._303_37-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._303-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> John was noted for his reverence and piety, as well as being highly learned in both scripture and <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canon law</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> From childhood he carried the nickname of "the White Hen", due to his light colored hair.<sup id="cite_ref-Mann,_pg._284_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mann,_pg._284-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His epitaph used to be in the basilica where he was buried, between the front door and the first column, and it read:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p> "Here, where in death the good pastor would have them placed, are the remains of Pope John. By the mercy of God and the merits of St. Paul, freed from the bonds of death, may he hence ascend into heaven, and share in the happiness of the blessed above. Do you who piously read this epitaph pray that Christ, who with His sacred Blood redeemed the world, may have pity on His servant and free him from his sins."<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>There is a legend which attached itself to the reign of John XIII. According to <a href="/wiki/Dietrich_I_of_Metz" title="Dietrich I of Metz">Dietrich I of Metz</a>, one of the nobles attached to the court of the emperor Otto I was possessed by an evil spirit, resulting in his tearing at his own face, and biting his hands and arms. The emperor ordered that the nobleman be taken to Pope John XIII, with instructions that the <a href="/wiki/Liberation_of_Saint_Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="Liberation of Saint Peter">Chains of Saint Peter</a> be placed upon him, and so cure him. According to the legend, John placed several chains on the afflicted man, each of which were copies, but to no effect. However, when John placed the true chain of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> on him, a thick smoke issued from the nobleman's body, cries were heard in the air, and the evil spirit left the nobleman.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
