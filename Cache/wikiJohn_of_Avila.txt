<p><b>John of Ávila</b> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Juan de Ávila</i>; 6 January 1499<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>– 10 May 1569) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a>, <a href="/wiki/Preacher" title="Preacher">preacher</a>, <a href="/wiki/Scholasticism" title="Scholasticism">scholastic</a> author, and religious <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a>, who has been <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">declared</a> a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. He is called the "<a href="/wiki/Apostle" title="Apostle">Apostle</a> of <a href="/wiki/Andalusia" title="Andalusia">Andalusia</a>", for his extensive ministry in that region.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo" title="Almodóvar del Campo">Almodóvar del Campo</a>, in the modern-day <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Ciudad_Real" title="Province of Ciudad Real">Province of Ciudad Real</a>, to Alfonso de Ávila, of Jewish <a href="/wiki/Converso" title="Converso">converso</a> descent, and Catalina Xixón (or <i>Gijón</i>), a wealthy and pious couple.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At the age of fourteen, in 1513, he was sent to the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Salamanca" title="University of Salamanca">University of Salamanca</a> to study law; he withdrew in 1517, however, without receiving a degree.<sup id="cite_ref-AF_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AF-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Returning home, Ávila spent the next three years in the practice of austere piety. His sanctity impressed a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> journeying through Almodóvar, on whose advice he resumed his studies by matriculating at the University of <a href="/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares" title="Alcalá de Henares">Alcalá de Henares</a> (which was moved to the national capital in the 19th century and renamed the <a href="/wiki/Complutense_University_of_Madrid" title="Complutense University of Madrid">Complutense University of Madrid</a>). There he undertook the study of philosophy and theology, in which he was fortunate to have as his teacher the noted <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> friar <a href="/wiki/Domingo_de_Soto" title="Domingo de Soto">Domingo de Soto</a>. It appears that Ávila earned his bachelor's degree during his years at Alcalá and then left without completing requirements for the licentiate degree.<sup id="cite_ref-AF_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AF-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Both his parents died while Ávila was still a student, and after his <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordination</a> in spring 1526, he celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> in the church where they were buried. He then sold the family property and gave the proceeds to the poor. He saw in the severing of natural ties a vocation to foreign missionary work and prepared to go to Mexico. He therefore traveled to <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a> to await departure for the Indies in January 1527 with the Dominican friar, <a href="/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Garc%C3%A9s" title="Julián Garcés">Julián Garcés</a>, appointed the first <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Puebla_de_los_Angeles" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Puebla de los Angeles">Bishop of Tlaxcala</a>. While waiting in Seville, his unusually great devotion in celebrating <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>, and his skills in catechesis and preaching, attracted the attention of Hernando de Contreras, a local priest, who mentioned him to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Seville" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Seville">Archbishop of Seville</a> and <a href="/wiki/Inquisitor_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Inquisitor General">Inquisitor General</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alonso_Manrique_de_Lara" title="Alonso Manrique de Lara">Alonso Manrique de Lara</a>. The archbishop saw in the young cleric a powerful instrument to stir up the faith in <a href="/wiki/Andalusia" title="Andalusia">Andalusia</a>, and after considerable persuasion Juan was induced to abandon his journey to America.<sup id="cite_ref-AF_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AF-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg/220px-Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="295" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg/330px-Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg/440px-Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1936" data-file-height="2592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Cueva_en_la_casa_natal_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The basement of the family home of John of Ávila in <a href="/wiki/Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo" title="Almodóvar del Campo">Almodóvar del Campo</a>, <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Ciudad_Real" title="Province of Ciudad Real">Ciudad Real</a>, Spain</div></div></div>
<p>John seems to have lived in the initial years after 1526 in a small house in Seville with another priest, probably Contreras, and disciples gathered around him, in a loosely structured fraternal life. It was at the request of the younger sister of one of these disciples, Sancha Carrillo, that he began in 1527 to write the <i>Audi, filia</i> (<i>Listen, Daughter</i>), a work he continued expanding and editing until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-AF_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AF-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Apostle_of_Andalusia">Apostle of Andalusia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Apostle of Andalusia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>John's first sermon was preached on 22 July 1529, and immediately established his reputation. During his nine years of missionary work in Andalusia, crowds packed the churches at all his sermons. However, his strong pleas for reform and his denunciation of the behaviour of the <a href="/wiki/Aristocracy" title="Aristocracy">aristocracy</a> meant that he was denounced to the office of the Inquisition in <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a> in 1531, and put in prison in the summer of 1532. He was charged with exaggerating the dangers of wealth and with closing the gates of heaven to the rich. The charges were refuted and he was declared innocent and released in July 1533.<sup id="cite_ref-W964_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-W964-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around the end of 1534 or the beginning of 1535, John of Ávila was <a href="/wiki/Incardination" class="mw-redirect" title="Incardination">incardinated</a> into the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_C%C3%B3rdoba" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Córdoba">Diocese of Córdoba</a>, from which he received a small <a href="/wiki/Benefice" title="Benefice">benefice</a>. This city became his base for directing his disciples and moving around Andalusia, preaching and establishing schools and colleges in various neighbouring cities such as <a href="/wiki/Granada" title="Granada">Granada</a>, <a href="/wiki/Baeza,_Spain" title="Baeza, Spain">Baeza</a>, <a href="/wiki/Montilla,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Montilla, Spain">Montilla</a> and <a href="/wiki/Zafra" title="Zafra">Zafra</a>. It is thought that during this time Ávila received the title of <a href="/wiki/Master_of_Sacred_Theology" title="Master of Sacred Theology">Master of Sacred Theology</a>, probably in <a href="/wiki/Granada" title="Granada">Granada</a> around 1538.
</p><p>Of special importance was the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Baeza" title="University of Baeza">University of Baeza</a>, established in 1538 by a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ujaen_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ujaen-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Ávila served as its first <a href="/wiki/Rector_(academic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Rector (academic)">rector</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Biedma_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Biedma-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and it became a model for seminaries and for the schools of the Jesuits.<sup id="cite_ref-W964_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-W964-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ávila stayed in Granada from 1538–9, where it appears some kind of community was taking shape. Likewise, during the years 1546 to 1555, John lived with about 20 disciples in Córdoba, making it seem that he intended to begin some kind of formal foundation of apostolic priests. However, the foundation and fast expansion of the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuits</a> meant that these ideas never came to fruition; from early 1551, when Ávila began to experience poor health, he began actively encouraging his disciples who so desired to join the Jesuits (around 30 in total seem to have joined).<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>From early 1551 Ávila was in constant ill-health. He spent the last years of his life in semi-retirement in the town of <a href="/wiki/Montilla,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Montilla, Spain">Montilla</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%B3rdoba_(Spain)" title="Province of Córdoba (Spain)">Province of Córdoba</a>. He died there on 10 May 1569, and in accordance with his wishes was buried in that city, in the Jesuit Church of the Incarnation, which now serves as the <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary" title="Sanctuary">sanctuary</a> to his memory.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Spanish critical edition of 1970 presents Ávila's work in 6 volumes. These consist of two redactions he made of the <i>Audi, filia</i>, 82 sermons and spiritual conferences, a few Biblical commentaries, 257 letters (representing the period from 1538 until his death), several treatises on reform and other minor treatises. John wrote letters to prelates who consulted him, such as Pedro Guerrero, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Granada" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Granada">Archbishop of Granada</a>, and <a href="/wiki/John_de_Ribera" class="mw-redirect" title="John de Ribera">Juan de Ribera</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_of_Villanova" title="Thomas of Villanova">Thomas of Villanova</a>, both <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Valencia_in_Spain" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Valencia in Spain">archbishops of Valencia</a> who were later canonised. He also wrote to <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_of_God" title="John of God">John of God</a> and <a href="/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila" title="Teresa of Ávila">Teresa of Ávila</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern_editions">Modern editions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Modern editions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint John of Ávila's works were collected at <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a> in 1618, 1757, 1792 and 1805; a <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a> translation by D'Andilly was published at <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> in 1673; and a <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a> translation by Schermer in six volumes was issued at <a href="/wiki/Regensburg" title="Regensburg">Regensburg</a> between 1856 and 1881.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The <i>Audi, filia</i> was translated into English in 1620.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> A modern translation was published by the American scholar <a href="/wiki/Joan_Frances_Gormley" title="Joan Frances Gormley">Joan Frances Gormley</a> in 2006.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>A selection of his letters, entitled <i>Spiritual Letters</i>, were translated into English in 1631, a facsimile edition of which was printed in 1970.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Influence">Influence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Influence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ávila's saintly reputation, as well as his efforts to reform the clergy, to establish schools and colleges, and to catechise the laity, were the inspiration for many later reformers. He is especially revered by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-W964_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-W964-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Their development in Spain is attributed to his friendship and support for the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-W964_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-W964-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a church in <a href="/wiki/Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares" title="Alcalá de Henares">Alcalá de Henares</a> dedicated to him: <a href="/wiki/San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila,_Alcal%C3%A1_de_Henares" title="San Juan de Ávila, Alcalá de Henares">St. John of Ávila Parish Church</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_%C3%81vila&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG/220px-Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="164" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG/330px-Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG/440px-Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1936" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Reliquia_de_San_Juan_de_%C3%81vila_en_Almod%C3%B3var_del_Campo.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a> of St. John of Ávila preserved in Almodóvar del Campo</div></div></div>
<p>Ávila was declared <a href="/wiki/The_Venerable" title="The Venerable">Venerable</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a> on 8 February 1759 and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on 15 November 1893. On 31 May 1970 he was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> named him a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a> on 7 October 2012, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Holy_Rosary" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Holy Rosary">Feast of the Holy Rosary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2doctors_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2doctors-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The proclamation of the two new Doctors of the Church was made by Pope Benedict before tens of thousands of people in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> During his <a href="/wiki/Homily" title="Homily">homily</a>, Pope Benedict said that John of Ávila was a "profound expert on the sacred Scriptures, he was gifted with an ardent missionary spirit. He knew how to penetrate in a uniquely profound way the mysteries of the redemption worked by Christ for humanity. A man of God, he united constant prayer to apostolic action. He dedicated himself to preaching and to the more frequent practice of the sacraments, concentrating his commitment on improving the formation of candidates for the priesthood, of religious and of lay people, with a view to a fruitful reform of the Church".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_letter" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic letter">Apostolic letter</a> for the proclamation to Doctor of the Church, John of Ávila explained that "Christian spiritual life, as a participation in the life of the Blessed Trinity, begins with faith in the God who is Love, is grounded in God's goodness and mercy as expressed in the merits of Christ, and is wholly guided by the Spirit". More than a venerable professor, "Father Master Avila"  was primarily a preacher and closely friend of <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Ignatius Loyola">Ignatius Loyola</a>. John of Ávila authored a "prayerful and sapiential" theology, based on the "primacy of Christ and of grace in relation to the love of God, for which he showed "a profound knowledge of the Bible", without setting forth a systematic synthesis.<sup id="cite_ref-Benetican.va,_Oct_7,_2012_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Benetican.va,_Oct_7,_2012-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Divine_Worship_and_the_Discipline_of_the_Sacraments" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments">Congregation for Divine Worship and the Discipline of the Sacraments</a> fixed his <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">optional memorial</a> as 10 May.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
