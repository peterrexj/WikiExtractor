<p><b>Ulphia</b> (also <b>Ulphe</b>, <b>Olfe</b>, <b>Wulfe</b>, <b>Wolfia</b>, or <b>Wulfia</b> and other variants; d. 8th century AD) of <a href="/wiki/Amiens" title="Amiens">Amiens</a> is a Christian saint, venerated particularly at Amiens. She was said to be a young girl living on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Noye" title="Noye">Noye</a> in the who became a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> at what would become <a href="/wiki/Saint-Acheul" title="Saint-Acheul">Saint-Acheul</a>, near Amiens in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of the Franks">Kingdom of the Franks</a>, under the spiritual direction of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Domitius" title="Saint Domitius">Saint Domitius</a> (Domice).  At the end of her life, she formed and directed a community of religious women at Amiens.  Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is January 31.
</p><p>Legend states that Ulphe placed the <a href="/wiki/Frog" title="Frog">frogs</a> in the area around her <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> (which was built in a <a href="/wiki/Swamp" title="Swamp">swampy</a> area) under <a href="/wiki/Interdict" title="Interdict">interdict</a> as a result of their loud croaking, which kept her awake at nights.  Thus, in her <a href="/wiki/Saint_symbology" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint symbology">iconography</a>, she is depicted as a young nun seated in prayer on a rock with a frog in the pool near her.
</p><p>A 19th century <a href="/wiki/Hagiographer" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographer">hagiographer</a> noted that the frogs in the area around the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> of Saint Ulphe were, indeed, very quiet.  However, if these frogs were taken elsewhere, they became boisterous once again.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A statue of Ulphia stands in the portal of <a href="/wiki/Amiens_Cathedral" title="Amiens Cathedral">Amiens Cathedral</a><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and a painting of Ulphia with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Domitius" title="Saint Domitius">Saint Domitius</a> by the 19th century painter Jean de Franqueville, hangs inside the cathedral.
</p>
