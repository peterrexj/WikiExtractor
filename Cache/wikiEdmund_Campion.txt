<p><b>Edmund Campion</b>, SJ (25 January 1540&#160;&#8211;&#32;1 December 1581) was an <a href="/wiki/English_people" title="English people">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> priest and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. While conducting an underground ministry in officially <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican</a> England, Campion was arrested by <a href="/wiki/Priest_hunter" title="Priest hunter">priest hunters</a>. Convicted of <a href="/wiki/High_treason" class="mw-redirect" title="High treason">high treason</a>, he was <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a>. Campion was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1886 and <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> in 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>. His feast day is celebrated on 1&#160;December.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span id="Early_years_and_education_.281540.E2.80.931569.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years_and_education_(1540–1569)">Early years and education (1540–1569)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years and education (1540–1569)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> on 25 January 1540, Campion was the son of a bookseller in <a href="/wiki/Paternoster_Row" title="Paternoster Row">Paternoster Row</a>, near <a href="/wiki/St_Paul%27s_Cathedral" title="St Paul&#39;s Cathedral">St Paul's Cathedral</a>. He received his early education at <a href="/wiki/Christ%27s_Hospital" title="Christ&#39;s Hospital">Christ's Hospital</a> school and, at the age of 13, was chosen to make the complimentary speech when <a href="/wiki/Mary_I_of_England" title="Mary I of England">Queen Mary</a> visited the city in August 1553.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-trhschap_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trhschap-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: p30">&#58;&#8202;p30&#8202;</span></sup> <a href="/wiki/William_Chester_(mayor)" title="William Chester (mayor)">William Chester</a>, a governor of Christ's Hospital, took a special interest in him, and sponsored him as a scholar to <a href="/wiki/St_John%27s_College,_Oxford" title="St John&#39;s College, Oxford">St John's College, Oxford</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> where he became junior fellow in 1557<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and took the required <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a>, probably on the occasion of his B.A. degree in 1560.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He took a master's degree at Oxford in 1564.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two years later, Campion welcomed <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth</a> to the university, and won her lasting regard.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was selected to lead a public debate in front of the Queen. By the time the Queen had left Oxford, Campion had earned the patronage of the powerful <a href="/wiki/William_Cecil,_1st_Baron_Burghley" title="William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley">William Cecil</a> and also the <a href="/wiki/Robert_Dudley,_1st_Earl_of_Leicester" title="Robert Dudley, 1st Earl of Leicester">Earl of Leicester</a>, tipped by some to be future husband of the young Queen.
</p><p>When Sir <a href="/wiki/Thomas_White_(merchant)" title="Thomas White (merchant)">Thomas White</a>, the founder of the college, was buried in 1567, it fell to Campion to give the <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> oration.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Rejecting_Anglicanism">Rejecting Anglicanism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Rejecting Anglicanism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Religious difficulties then arose but despite holding <a href="/wiki/Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholicism">Catholic</a> doctrines, at the persuasion of <a href="/wiki/Richard_Cheyney" title="Richard Cheyney">Richard Cheyney</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Gloucester" title="Bishop of Gloucester">Bishop of Gloucester</a>, he received <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">Holy Orders</a> in 1564 as a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> in the Anglican Church. Inwardly "<i>he took a remorse of conscience and detestation of mind.</i>" Rumours of his opinions began to spread and he left Oxford in 1569 and went to Ireland for private study and research, but not, as Simpson said (now corrected by P. Joseph's revision of Simpson, 2010) to take part in a proposed establishment of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Dublin" title="University of Dublin">University of Dublin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Ireland_.281569.E2.80.931570.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Ireland_(1569–1570)">Ireland (1569–1570)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Ireland (1569–1570)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Campion went to Ireland with his university friend, Richard Stanihurst, where he was the guest of Richard and his father, James Stanihurst, the Speaker of the Irish House of Commons. Warned of his imminent arrest by the Lord Deputy Sir Henry Sidney, he was transferred through Stanihurst's arrangement to the house of <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Barnewall" title="Christopher Barnewall">Christopher Barnewall</a> at <a href="/wiki/Turvey_House,_County_Dublin" title="Turvey House, County Dublin">Turvey House</a> in <a href="/wiki/The_Pale" title="The Pale">the Pale</a>. For some three months he eluded his pursuers, going by the name "Mr Patrick" and occupying himself by writing <i>A Historie of Ireland</i>.
</p>
<h3><span id="Douai_.281571.E2.80.931573.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Douai_(1571–1573)">Douai (1571–1573)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Douai (1571–1573)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the year of 1571, Campion left Ireland in secret and escaped to <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> in the Low Countries (now France) where he was reconciled to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and received the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> that he had denied himself for the past twelve years. He entered the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> founded by <a href="/wiki/William_Allen_(cardinal)" title="William Allen (cardinal)">William Allen</a>. The enrollment of the college grew, and a papal subsidy was granted a little time after Campion's arrival. Campion found himself reunited with Oxford friends. He was to teach rhetoric while there and finish studying for the degree of <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Divinity" title="Bachelor of Divinity">Bachelor of Divinity</a>, which was granted him by the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Douai" title="University of Douai">University of Douai</a> on 21 January 1573. He received minor orders after this and was ordained sub-deacon.<sup id="cite_ref-guiney55_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guiney55-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Rome.2C_Br.C3.BCnn_and_Prague_.281573.E2.80.931580.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Rome,_Brünn_and_Prague_(1573–1580)">Rome, Brünn and Prague (1573–1580)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Rome, Brünn and Prague (1573–1580)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Campion then travelled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on foot, alone and in the guise of a pilgrim, to join the Jesuits. In April 1573, in Rome, he became the first novice accepted into the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> by <a href="/wiki/Everard_Mercurian" title="Everard Mercurian">Mercurianus</a>, the order's fourth <a href="/wiki/Superior_General_of_the_Society_of_Jesus" title="Superior General of the Society of Jesus">Superior General</a>. He was assigned to the Austrian Province as there was not yet an English province of the Jesuits and began his two-year <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at <a href="/wiki/Brno" title="Brno">Brünn</a> (now Brno) in <a href="/wiki/Moravia" title="Moravia">Moravia</a>. He was ordained deacon and priest by <a href="/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Brus_z_Mohelnice" title="Antonín Brus z Mohelnice">Anthony Brus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_the_Cross_with_the_Red_Star" title="Knights of the Cross with the Red Star">OMCRS</a>, Archbishop of Prague<sup id="cite_ref-simpson_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-simpson-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and said his first Mass on 8 September 1578.<sup id="cite_ref-guiney69_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guiney69-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> For six years, Campion taught at the Jesuit college in Prague as professor of both rhetoric and philosophy.<sup id="cite_ref-waugh_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-waugh-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In 1578 his play <i>Ambrosia</i> was staged in Prague by the students of the recently founded Jesuit College <a href="/wiki/Clementinum" title="Clementinum">Clementinum</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Mission_to_England_.281580.E2.80.931581.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Mission_to_England_(1580–1581)">Mission to England (1580–1581)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Mission to England (1580–1581)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1580, the Jesuit mission to England began. The mission was strictly forbidden, according to Campion's <i>Challenge to the Privy Council</i>, "to deal in any respects with matters of state or policy of this [English] realm..."<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Campion accompanied <a href="/wiki/Robert_Persons" title="Robert Persons">Robert Persons</a> who, as superior, was intended to counterbalance his own fervour and impetuousness. He had been surprised to learn that he was chosen to take part in the mission, and expressed the fear that he lacked constitutional courage.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The members of the mission were instructed to avoid the company of boys and women and to avoid giving the impression of being legacy hunters. Before embarking, the members of the mission were embarrassed to receive news of a landing by papal-sponsored forces in the Irish province of <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> in support of the Irish rebel <a href="/wiki/James_Fitzmaurice_Fitzgerald" class="mw-redirect" title="James Fitzmaurice Fitzgerald">James Fitzmaurice Fitzgerald</a>. They also learned that a letter detailing their party and mission had been intercepted and that they were expected in England.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:242px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG/240px-Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG" decoding="async" width="240" height="320" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG/360px-Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG/480px-Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1704" data-file-height="2272" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Edmond_Campion%27s_%27Brag_and_Challenge%27.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Part of Campion's <i>Challenge to the Privy Council</i></div></div></div><p> Campion finally entered England in the guise of a jewel merchant, arriving in London on 24 June 1580, and at once began to preach. His presence soon became known to the authorities and to his fellow Catholics lying in London's prisons. Among the latter was <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Pounde" title="Thomas Pounde">Thomas Pounde</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Marshalsea" title="Marshalsea">Marshalsea</a>, where a meeting was held to discuss means of counteracting rumours circulated by the Privy Council to the effect that Campion's mission was political and treasonous. Pounde rode in haste after Campion and explained the need for Campion to write a brief declaration of the true causes of his coming.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The diffusion of this declaration, known as the <i>Challenge to the Privy Council</i>, or, <i>Campion's Brag</i>, made his position more difficult. He led a hunted life, administering the sacraments and preaching to Catholics in <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Northamptonshire" title="Northamptonshire">Northamptonshire</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During this time he wrote his <i>Decem Rationes</i> ("Ten Reasons"), arguments against the validity of the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican Church">Anglican Church</a>. This pamphlet, in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, was printed in a clandestine press at <a href="/wiki/Stonor_Park" title="Stonor Park">Stonor Park</a>, <a href="/wiki/Henley-on-Thames" title="Henley-on-Thames">Henley</a>, and 400 copies were found on the benches of St Mary's, <a href="/wiki/Oxford" title="Oxford">Oxford</a>, at the Commencement, on 27 June 1581. It caused great sensation, and the hunt for Campion was stepped up. On his way to <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a>, he stopped at <a href="/wiki/Lyford,_Oxfordshire" title="Lyford, Oxfordshire">Lyford</a> Grange, the house of Francis Yate, then in <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a>, where he preached on 14 July and the following day, by popular request.<sup id="cite_ref-rbhaec_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rbhaec-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Here, he was captured by a spy named <a href="/wiki/George_Eliot_(spy)" class="mw-redirect" title="George Eliot (spy)">George Eliot</a> and taken to London with his arms pinioned and bearing on his hat a paper with the inscription <i>"Campion, the Seditious Jesuit".</i><sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Imprisonment.2C_torture_and_disputations"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Imprisonment,_torture_and_disputations">Imprisonment, torture and disputations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Imprisonment, torture and disputations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Imprisoned for four days in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> in a tiny cell called "<a href="/wiki/Little_Ease" title="Little Ease">Little Ease</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Campion was then taken out and questioned by three Privy Councillors—Lord Chancellor <a href="/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Bromley" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Thomas Bromley">Sir Thomas Bromley</a>, Vice-Chamberlain of the Royal Household <a href="/wiki/Sir_Christopher_Hatton" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Christopher Hatton">Sir Christopher Hatton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Dudley,_Earl_of_Leicester" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester">Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester</a>—on matters including whether he acknowledged <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I" title="Elizabeth I">Queen Elizabeth</a> to be the true Queen of England. He replied that he did, and was offered his freedom, wealth and honours, including a possibility of the Archbishopric of Canterbury,<sup id="cite_ref-trhschap_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trhschap-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: pp.32–33">&#58;&#8202;pp.32–33&#8202;</span></sup><sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> which he could not accept in good conscience.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Campion was imprisoned in the Tower more than four months and tortured on the <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">rack</a> two<sup id="cite_ref-trhschap_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trhschap-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: p.33">&#58;&#8202;p.33&#8202;</span></sup> or three times.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> False reports of a retraction and of a confession by Campion were circulated.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> He had four public disputations with his Anglican adversaries, on 1, 18, 23 and 27 September 1581, at which they attempted to address the challenges of Campion's <i>Challenge to the Privy Council</i> and <i>Decem Rationes</i>. Although still suffering from the effects of his torture, and allowed neither time nor books for preparation, he reportedly conducted himself so easily and readily that "even the spectators in the court looked for an acquittal".<sup id="cite_ref-trhschap_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-trhschap-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: p.33">&#58;&#8202;p.33&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>He was arraigned and indicted on 14 November 1581<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> with several others at <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Westminster" title="Palace of Westminster">Westminster</a> on a charge of having conspired, in Rome and <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, to raise a sedition in the realm and dethrone the Queen.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Campion.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Campion.jpg/220px-Campion.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="340" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Campion.jpg/330px-Campion.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/25/Campion.jpg 2x" data-file-width="426" data-file-height="658" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Campion.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Print of Edmund Campion in 1631</div></div></div>
<h2><span id="Trial.2C_sentence_and_execution"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Trial,_sentence_and_execution">Trial, sentence and execution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Trial, sentence and execution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The trial was held on 20 November 1581. After hearing the pleadings for three hours, the jury deliberated an hour before delivering its verdict:<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Campion and his fellow defendants were found guilty of treason. He answered the verdict: 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>In condemning us, you condemn all your own ancestors, all our ancient bishops and kings, all that was once the glory of England—the island of saints, and the most devoted child of the See of Peter.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="/wiki/Christopher_Wray_(English_judge)" title="Christopher Wray (English judge)">Lord Chief Justice Wray</a> read the sentence: "You must go to the place from whence you came, there to remain until ye shall be drawn through the open city of London upon <a href="/wiki/Hurdle" title="Hurdle">hurdles</a> to the place of execution, and there be hanged and let down alive, and your <a href="/wiki/Human_male_reproductive_system" class="mw-redirect" title="Human male reproductive system">privy parts</a> cut off, and your entrails taken out and burnt in your sight; then your heads to be cut off and your bodies divided into four parts, to be disposed of at Her Majesty’s pleasure. And God have mercy on your souls."<sup id="cite_ref-simpson308-9_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-simpson308-9-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On hearing the death sentence, Campion and the other condemned men broke into the words of the <i><a href="/wiki/Te_Deum" title="Te Deum">Te Deum</a></i>. After spending his last days in prayer he was dragged with two fellow priests, <a href="/wiki/Ralph_Sherwin" title="Ralph Sherwin">Ralph Sherwin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Briant" title="Alexander Briant">Alexander Briant</a>, to <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a> where the three were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> on 1 December 1581. Campion was 41 years of age.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_feast_day">Veneration and feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Edmund_Campion&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Veneration and feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Edmund Campion was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on 9 December 1886.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> Edmund Campion was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> nearly eighty-four years later in 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> as one of the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>. His feast day is celebrated on 1 December, the day of his martyrdom. 
</p><p>The actual ropes used in his execution are now kept in glass display tubes at <a href="/wiki/Stonyhurst_College" title="Stonyhurst College">Stonyhurst College</a><sup id="cite_ref-muir_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-muir-26">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a>; each year they are placed on the altar of St Peter's Church for Mass to celebrate Campion's feast day—which is always a holiday for the school.
</p>
