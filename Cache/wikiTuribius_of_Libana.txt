					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Santo-Toribio.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Santo-Toribio.jpg/220px-Santo-Toribio.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="87" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Santo-Toribio.jpg/330px-Santo-Toribio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Santo-Toribio.jpg/440px-Santo-Toribio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="590" /></a><figcaption>Monastery founded by Turibius at Liébana</figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Turibius of Liébana</b> (<i><a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl.</a></i> <i><abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;530</span></i>), also known as <b>Turbius the Monk</b> (<i>Turibius Monachus</i>, <i>Toribio el Monje</i>), was an early <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monk" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monk">Benedictine monk</a>. He was born probably in <a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turieno" class="extiw" title="es:Turieno">Turieno</a> and spent most of his life in the region of <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A9bana" title="Liébana">Liébana</a>. He received a letter full of praise from Bishop Montanus of <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Toledo" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Toledo">Toledo</a> in 527.<sup id="cite_ref-loring_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loring-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In his preaching, he condemned as heresy the <a href="/wiki/Priscillianism" title="Priscillianism">Priscillianism</a> still rampant in Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-loring_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loring-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Around the middle of the century, he and five companions took up the Benedictine habit and founded the <a href="/wiki/Santo_Toribio_de_Li%C3%A9bana" title="Santo Toribio de Liébana">monastery of Liébana</a>, either the second or third oldest Benedictine establishment in Spain, dedicating it to <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a>.
</p><p>Turibius of Liébana has been conflated with <a href="/wiki/Turibius_of_Astorga" title="Turibius of Astorga">Turibius of Astorga</a> (died 460), also a saint, whose bones came to rest in the monastery the former Turibius had founded and where he also was buried.<sup id="cite_ref-loring_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loring-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i>Chronicon</i> attributed to Hauberto, a Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Mozarab" class="mw-redirect" title="Mozarab">Mozarab</a> of the reign of <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_III_of_Asturias" title="Alfonso III of Asturias">Alfonso III of Asturias</a>, but actually a forgery by Antonio Lupian Zapata,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Turibius became <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Palencia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Palencia">Bishop of Palencia</a> in 533. He was also said to have <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> the body of <a href="/wiki/Antoninus_of_Pamiers" title="Antoninus of Pamiers">Antoninus of Pamiers</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Palencia" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Palencia">Cathedral of Palencia</a>, thereafter named in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast day is 11 November, or 16 April in other calendars.
</p>
