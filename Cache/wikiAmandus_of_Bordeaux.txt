					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">5th-century bishop and Catholic saint</div>
<p><b>Amandus</b> (died c. 431) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Bordeaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Bordeaux">bishop of Bordeaux</a> for two non-consecutive periods between about 404 and 431.
</p><p>Amandus was raised in a Christian home and educated in the <a href="/wiki/Christian_Bible" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian Bible">Christian Bible</a>. Recognizing his qualities, Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Delphinus_of_Bordeaux&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Delphinus of Bordeaux (page does not exist)">Delphinus</a> had him ordained a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBangley2005s.v._June_18_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBangley2005s.v._June_18-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was the priest who prepared <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_of_Nola" title="Paulinus of Nola">Paulinus</a> for baptism by Delphinus in 389. He wrote a <i><a href="/wiki/Consolatio" title="Consolatio">consolatio</a></i> to Paulinus, then living in Italy, on the death of the latter's brother. Paulinus' response survives.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEConybeare20004_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConybeare20004-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In another letter, Paulinus reproves Amandus for having exaggerated Paulinus' literary skills to the point where Delphinus was requesting Paulinus write him a letter.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEConybeare200012–13_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConybeare200012–13-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The earliest collection of Paulinus' letters contains six to Amandus.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEConybeare2000161_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConybeare2000161-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Amandus may also have corresponded with <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETrout1991258_n89_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETrout1991258_n89-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Amandus succeeded Delphinus as bishop of Bordeaux around 404.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEConybeare20006_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEConybeare20006-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> When his health began to fail, he urged the people to elect a replacement. He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Severinus_of_Bordeaux" title="Severinus of Bordeaux">Severinus</a>, who had already served as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Trier" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Trier">bishop of Trier</a>. When Severinus died not long after in 420, Amandus resumed the bishopric.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEFarmer2011475_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarmer2011475-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> These events are known through the letters of Paulinus.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Amandus is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and his <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 18 June in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. He is listed in the revised <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> (2004), but not the revised <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> (1969).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWatkins201632-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
