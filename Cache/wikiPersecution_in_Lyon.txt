					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Persecution of Christians in Lugdunum, Gaul  (c.177)</div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG/220px-03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG/330px-03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG/440px-03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4272" data-file-height="2848" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:03._Amphith%C3%A9atre_des_Trois_Gaules.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Amphitheatre_of_the_Three_Gauls" title="Amphitheatre of the Three Gauls">Amphitheatre of the Three Gauls</a>, in Lyon. The pole in the arena is a memorial to the people killed during this persecution.</div></div></div> 
<p>The <b>persecution in Lyon</b> in AD 177 was a legendary persecution of Christians in <a href="/wiki/Lugdunum" title="Lugdunum">Lugdunum</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Gaul" title="Roman Gaul">Roman Gaul</a> (present-day <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>, France), during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a> (<span style="white-space:nowrap;"><abbr title="reigned">r.</abbr>&#160;</span>161–180). As there is no coeval account of this persecution the earliest source is a letter preserved in <a href="/wiki/Eusebius" title="Eusebius">Eusebius</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Ecclesiastical History</a></i>, book 5, chapter 1, written 150 years later in palestine. <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> describes the persecution in the 6th century in <i>De Gloria martyrum</i>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Persecution_in_Lyon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Lugdunum was an important Roman city in Gaul. Founded on the <a href="/wiki/Rhone" class="mw-redirect" title="Rhone">Rhone</a> river in 43 BC by Lucius Munatius Plancus, it served as the capital of the Roman province <a href="/wiki/Gallia_Lugdunensis" title="Gallia Lugdunensis">Gallia Lugdunensis</a>. The emperor <a href="/wiki/Claudius" title="Claudius">Claudius</a> was born in Lugdunum. The first known Christian community established in Lugdunum some time in the 2nd century was led by a bishop named <a href="/wiki/Saint_Pothinus" title="Saint Pothinus">Pothinus</a> from Asia Minor.
</p><p>In the first two centuries of the Christian era, it was local Roman officials who were largely responsible for persecutions. In the second century, the Caesars were largely content to treat Christianity as a local problem, and leave it to their subordinates to deal with. Until the reign of emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> (249-251) persecution was local and sporadic. For Roman governors being a Christian was in itself a subversive act, because it entailed a refusal to sacrifice to the gods of Rome, including the deified emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalton2005_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalton2005-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Account_of_the_persecution">Account of the persecution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Persecution_in_Lyon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Account of the persecution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>By 177, a number of the Christians in the area of Vienne and Lyons were Greeks from Asia.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEButler18662_June_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEButler18662_June-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Before the actual outbreak of violence, Christians were forbidden from the marketplace, the forum, the baths, or to appear in any public places.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEusebiusn.d.Book_5,_Chapter_1_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEusebiusn.d.Book_5,_Chapter_1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> If they did appear in public they were subject to being mocked, beaten, and robbed by the mob. The homes of Christians were vandalized. (<i>Hist. Eccl.</i>, 5.1.5,7). The martyrs of Lyons were accused of "Thyestean banquets and Oedipean intercourse," a reference to cannibalism and incest.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWalton2005_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalton2005-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>How long all of this lasted is not indicated, but eventually the authorities seized the Christians and questioned them in the forum in front of the populace.  They were then imprisoned until the arrival of the governor.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEusebiusn.d.Book_5,_Chapter_1_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEusebiusn.d.Book_5,_Chapter_1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>According to Eusebius (Hist. Eccl.,5.4), while yet a presbyter or elder, St. <a href="/wiki/Irenaeus" title="Irenaeus">Irenaeus</a> was sent with a letter, from certain members of the Church of Lyons awaiting martyrdom, to <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eleutherius" title="Pope Eleutherius">Eleutherus</a>, bishop of Rome.
</p><p>When the governor arrived at Lugdunum, he interrogated them in front of the populace again, mistreating them to such a degree that Vettius Epagathus, a Christian and man of high social standing, requested permission to testify on behalf of the accused.  This request was refused and instead the governor arrested Vettius Epagathus when he confessed to being a Christian (5.1.9-10).
</p><p>These Christians endured torture while the authorities continued to apprehend others.  Two of their pagan servants were seized and, fearing torture, falsely charged the Christians with incest and cannibalism (Hist. Eccl., 5.1.12-13).
</p><p>What followed was the torture of the captive Christians by various means.  In the end, all were killed, some of whom had recanted but later returned to the faith (Hist. Eccl., 5.1.45-46).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKirsch1910_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKirsch1910-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:BlandineCallot.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/BlandineCallot.jpg/220px-BlandineCallot.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="282" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/BlandineCallot.jpg/330px-BlandineCallot.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/BlandineCallot.jpg 2x" data-file-width="374" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:BlandineCallot.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Blandine</div></div></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Deaths">Deaths</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Persecution_in_Lyon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Deaths">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There were 48 victims at Lugdunum, half of them were of Greek origin, half Gallo-Roman.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGoyau1910_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoyau1910-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The elderly Bishop Pothinus, first Bishop of Lugdunum, was beaten and scourged, and died shortly after in prison.
</p><p>A slave, <a href="/wiki/Blandina" title="Blandina">Blandina</a> was subjected to extreme torture. She was initially exposed, hung on a stake, to be the food of the beasts let loose upon her. As none of the beasts at that time touched her; she was brought back again to the prison, before being cast in a net and thrown before a bull.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWhiteheadn.d._6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhiteheadn.d.-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Also martyred at this time were Attalus, <a href="/wiki/Epipodius_and_Alexander" title="Epipodius and Alexander">Epipodius and Alexander</a>, Maturus, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ponticus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ponticus">Saint Ponticus</a>, a fifteen-year-old boy, and
Sanctus, a deacon from Vienne.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEEusebiusn.d.Book_5,_Chapter_1_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEEusebiusn.d.Book_5,_Chapter_1-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
