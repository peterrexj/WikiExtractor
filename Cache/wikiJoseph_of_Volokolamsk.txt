					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_of_Volokolamsk&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseph of Volokolamsk">Joseph of Volokolamsk</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Josephvolotsk.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Josephvolotsk.jpg/200px-Josephvolotsk.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="261" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Josephvolotsk.jpg/300px-Josephvolotsk.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/62/Josephvolotsk.jpg 2x" data-file-width="345" data-file-height="450" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Josephvolotsk.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Russian icon of Saint Joseph Volotsky</div></div></div>
<p><b>Joseph Volotsky</b> — also known as <b>Joseph of Volotsk</b> or <b>Joseph of Volokolamsk</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Ио́сиф Во́лоцкий</span>); secular name <b>Ivan Sanin</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Ива́н Са́нин</span>) (1439 or 1440 &#8211; September 9, 1515) — was a prominent <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russian</a> <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Christian_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Christian theology">theologian</a> and early proponent of <a href="/wiki/Tsarist_autocracy" title="Tsarist autocracy">tsarist autocracy</a>, who led the party defending monastic landownership. The <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> considers him a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> (along with his most notable opponent, <a href="/wiki/Nilus_of_Sora" title="Nilus of Sora">Nilus of Sora</a>); his memory is celebrated on 9 September and 18 October (dates in the <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a>).
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Volotsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Joseph Volotsky came from a family of a wealthy landowner (a <i><a href="/wiki/Votchina" title="Votchina">votchinnik</a></i>) whose property consisted of the Yazvishche village in the Principaity of <a href="/wiki/Volokolamsk" title="Volokolamsk">Volokolamsk</a>, <a href="/wiki/Moscow_Oblast" title="Moscow Oblast">Moscow Oblast</a>. He learned to read and write at the local monastery and then took the tonsure at the <a href="/wiki/Borovsk" title="Borovsk">Borovsk Monastery</a> in 1459. Upon the death of its <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=St._Paphnutius_of_Borovsk&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="St. Paphnutius of Borovsk (page does not exist)">St. Paphnutius of Borovsk</a>, Joseph Volotsky took his place and attempted to introduce a strict monastic charter.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monks</a>, however, rebuffed his idea, and he had to leave the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> for good. After having lived in a few other monasteries, Joseph became disappointed with their lax morals and founded his own <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloister</a> in 1479 near <a href="/wiki/Volokolamsk" title="Volokolamsk">Volokolamsk</a>, which would become known as the <a href="/wiki/Joseph-Volokolamsk_Monastery" title="Joseph-Volokolamsk Monastery">Joseph-Volokolamsk Monastery</a>. Joseph's charter prescribed a monk's chief virtue as absolute obedience to his abbot. All aspects of a monk's life at the monastery were regulated and controlled.
</p><p>Initially, Joseph Volotsky was connected with the <a href="/wiki/Appanage" title="Appanage">appanage</a> princes of Volokolamsk (brothers of <a href="/wiki/Ivan_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Ivan III">Ivan III</a>) and defended the right of local <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical" title="Ecclesiastical">ecclesiastical</a> and <a href="/wiki/Secular" class="mw-redirect" title="Secular">secular</a> <a href="/wiki/Feudatory" class="mw-redirect" title="Feudatory">feudatories</a> to oppose the authority of the <a href="/wiki/Grand_prince" title="Grand prince">grand prince</a>. Later in his life he severed his relations with the opposition and took the side of the grand prince, sealing this alliance by transferring Joseph-Volokolamsk Monastery to the patronage of <a href="/wiki/Vasili_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Vasili III">Vasili III</a> in 1507.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Struggle_against_the_non-possessors">Struggle against the non-possessors</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Volotsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Struggle against the non-possessors">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the Church <a href="/wiki/Sobor_of_1503" title="Sobor of 1503">Sobor of 1503</a>, Joseph Volotsky and his supporters (Josephites) managed to scrap the project of elimination of monastic landownership, proposed by the <i>nestiazhateli</i>, or <a href="/wiki/Non-possessors" title="Non-possessors">non-possessors</a> (those who opposed monastic landownership), in the persons of the so-called <a href="/w/index.php?title=Transvolgan_elders&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Transvolgan elders (page does not exist)">Transvolgan elders</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Nilus_of_Sora" title="Nilus of Sora">Nilus of Sora</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vassian_Patrikeyev" title="Vassian Patrikeyev">Vassian Patrikeyev</a>. Joseph Volotsky addressed a number of <a href="/wiki/Epistle" title="Epistle">epistles</a> to the <i>nestiazhateli</i>, in which he tried to prove the legality of monastic landownership and to justify the rich décor of churches. 
Eventually, Joseph Volotsky gained the upper hand and the monasteries preserved their landholdings.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ideas_on_the_authority_of_the_tsar">Ideas on the authority of the tsar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Volotsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Ideas on the authority of the tsar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The triumph of the possessor party went hand in hand with Joseph's efforts to bolster the position of Russia's grand princes, who were now increasingly referred to as <a href="/wiki/Tsar" title="Tsar">tsars</a>, succeeding to the title of universal <a href="/wiki/Emperor" title="Emperor">emperors</a> of <a href="/wiki/Byzantium" title="Byzantium">Byzantium</a>. Joseph Volotsky restated the formula of an early Byzantine ecclesiastical writer <a href="/wiki/Agapetus_(deacon)" title="Agapetus (deacon)">Agapetus</a> that the tsar was a "man in essence, but his power is that of God" and that he was God's deputy on Earth.  According to Joseph, however, since the main duty of the tsar is to care for the well-being of the <a href="/wiki/Christian_Church" title="Christian Church">Christian Church</a>, he is <a href="/wiki/Legitimacy_(political)" title="Legitimacy (political)">legitimate</a> only as long as he adheres to Church rules and moral obligations.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Struggle_against_heresies">Struggle against heresies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joseph_Volotsky&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Struggle against heresies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:JosephVolotskyBook.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/JosephVolotskyBook.JPG/220px-JosephVolotskyBook.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="167" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/JosephVolotskyBook.JPG/330px-JosephVolotskyBook.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/JosephVolotskyBook.JPG/440px-JosephVolotskyBook.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2337" data-file-height="1779" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:JosephVolotskyBook.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Writing by Joseph Volotsky</div></div></div>
<p>Joseph Volotsky is also known to have been a staunch opponent of the <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heretical</a> sect which was spreading in <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a> at that time ("<a href="/wiki/Sect_of_Skhariya_the_Jew" title="Sect of Skhariya the Jew">Judaizers</a>"). During the Church Sobor of 1504, he demanded that all heretics be executed by the state.
</p><p>In his major work, called <i>The Enlightener</i> (<i>Просветитель</i>), which consisted of 16 chapters, he tried to prove the wrongfulness of the "new teaching" in order to be able to prosecute the heretics and convince people not to believe in the sincerity of their <a href="/wiki/Repentance" title="Repentance">repentance</a>. Taking inspiration from the Roman-Byzantine treatment of heresiarchs and the Dominican-led persecutions in Spain and Portugal, he called for a civil inquisition against heretics and championed their imprisonment and execution. As with the controversy over monastic ownership of estates, Joseph Volotsky was opposed in this matter by Saint Nilus of Sora.
</p><p>Joseph's sainthood was officially accepted by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> following his local <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> in 1579 and national canonization in 1591.
</p>
