<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p><p><b>Cumméne Fota</b> or <b>Fada</b>, anglicised <b>Cummian</b>  (<i>fl</i>. <i>c</i>. 591 – 12 November 661 or 662), was an Irish bishop and <i>fer léignid</i> (<a href="/wiki/Lector" title="Lector">lector</a>) of <i>Cluain Ferta Brénainn</i> (<a href="/wiki/Clonfert" title="Clonfert">Clonfert</a>). He was an important theological writer in the early to mid 7th century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cumm%C3%A9ne_Fota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cumméne Fota was son to Fiachna, king of West Munster. He early embraced a monastic state, and after some years was made abbot of Keltra, an isle in the lake Dergdarg, upon the river Shannon, sixteen miles from Limerick.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="De_controversia_paschali">De controversia paschali</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cumm%C3%A9ne_Fota&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: De controversia paschali">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is famous for a <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Paschal</a> letter (<i>De controversia paschali</i>) which displays his high level of learning.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It was written around 632 to <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A9g%C3%A9ne_mac_Fiachna%C3%AD" title="Ségéne mac Fiachnaí">Ségéne mac Fiachnaí</a>, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Iona_Abbey" title="Iona Abbey">Hy</a>, in an  attempt to persuade the monks of that house, whose authority bore great sway in the Pictish and Irish churches, to join with the Roman universal church as to the time of celebrating Easter.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It consists of five manuscript folios, contains quotes from the <a href="/wiki/Vulgate" title="Vulgate">Vulgate</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vetus_Latina" title="Vetus Latina">Vetus Latina</a> <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a>; patristic commentary by <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cyprian" title="Cyprian">Cyprian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origen</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ambrosiaster" title="Ambrosiaster">Ambrosiaster</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory the Great">Gregory the Great</a>; extracts from <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">Canon law</a>, ecclesiastical history and <a href="/wiki/Synodal" class="mw-redirect" title="Synodal">synodal</a> decrees from Nicea and Arles in their original, uncontaminated forms, in addition to <i>a decretum that enjoined on the Irish that, if all else failed, they should take their problems to Rome.</i>
</p><p>Cummian was afterward made bishop of Clonfert. He also owned - among a library of at least forty manuscripts - ten Paschal tracts including one he attributed to <i>"santus Patricius, papa noster"</i> and possibly a letter of <a href="/wiki/Pelagius" title="Pelagius">Pelagius</a>. He may have written a computational manual, and a commentary on <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Mark" title="Gospel of Mark">Mark</a>. As Cummianus Longus he may be the author of a penitential, and a hymn on the apostles.
</p><p>In 1162 his relics, along with those of Bishop <a href="/wiki/M%C3%B3enu" title="Móenu">Maeineann</a> of Clonfert "were removed from the earth by the clergy of Brenainn, and they were enclosed in a protecting shrine."
</p><p>He is said to have been a foster-son of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ita" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ita">Saint Ita</a>.
</p>
