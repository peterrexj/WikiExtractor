<p><b>Zeno of Verona</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Zenone da Verona</i>; about 300 – 371 or 380) was either an early Christian <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Verona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Verona">Bishop of Verona</a> or a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. He is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the Roman Catholic Church and in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_historicity">Life and historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Zeno_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a Veronese author named Coronato, a <a href="/wiki/Notary" title="Notary">notary</a> of the 7th century, Zeno was a native of <a href="/wiki/Mauretania" title="Mauretania">Mauretania</a>. He taught many children of Africa about the Catholic religion and he also helped them with their school work. The children could rely on someone who could help them.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Another theory is that Zeno was a follower of <a href="/wiki/Athanasius" class="mw-redirect" title="Athanasius">Athanasius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch of Alexandria">patriarch of Alexandria</a>, who accompanied his master when the latter visited <a href="/wiki/Verona" title="Verona">Verona</a> in 340.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The style of the 90 or so <i>Sermones</i> attributed to Zeno has also been considered evidence of his African origins due to its literary style, since Christian African writers of the time frequently used neologisms and wordplay.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Many of the <i>Sermones</i> concern <a href="/wiki/Old_Testament" title="Old Testament">Old Testament</a> <a href="/wiki/Exegesis" title="Exegesis">exegesis</a> and "have a definite <a href="/wiki/Anti-Semitic" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-Semitic">anti-Semitic</a> element in them".<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1995_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1995-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> This opinion is not shared by Giuseppe Laiti, expert on San Zeno’s tractatus.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Staying in the city, Zeno entered the monastic, living as a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> until around 362, when he was elected successor to the See of Verona after the death of Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Gricinus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Gricinus (page does not exist)">Gricinus</a> (Cricinus, Cricino).<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Zeno had "received a good <a href="/wiki/Classics" title="Classics">classical education</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and as bishop <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> many people, won converts back from <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a>, lived a life of poverty, trained priests to work in the diocese, set up a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> for women, reformed how the <a href="/wiki/Agape_feast" title="Agape feast">Agape feast</a> was celebrated, and forbade <a href="/wiki/Funeral_mass" class="mw-redirect" title="Funeral mass">funeral masses</a> being accompanied by attendees' loud groans and wailing.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Zeno's other reforms included instructions concerning <a href="/wiki/Adult_baptism" class="mw-redirect" title="Adult baptism">adult baptism</a> (which occurred by <a href="/wiki/Baptism_by_immersion" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptism by immersion">complete immersion</a>) and issuing medals to people newly baptized to the Catholic faith.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Zeno's episcopate lasted for about ten years, and the date of his death is sometimes given as 12 April 371.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Zeno is described as a <a href="/wiki/Confessor_of_the_faith" class="mw-redirect" title="Confessor of the faith">confessor of the faith</a> in early <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gregory_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gregory the Great">Saint Gregory the Great</a> calls him a martyr in his <i>Dialogues</i>; <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ambrose" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ambrose">Saint Ambrose</a>, a contemporary of Zeno, does not.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ambrose speaks of Zeno's "happy death", although as a confessor, Zeno may have suffered <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians" title="Persecution of Christians">persecution</a> (but not execution) during the reigns of <a href="/wiki/Constantius_II" title="Constantius II">Constantius II</a> and <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The entry in the current <i><a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a></i>  lists him on 12 April, but makes no mention of martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The first evidence for his existence is found in a letter written by Saint Ambrose to Bishop <a href="/w/index.php?title=Syagrius_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Syagrius of Verona (page does not exist)">Syagrius of Verona</a> in which Ambrose refers to the holiness of Zeno.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Later, Bishop Saint <a href="/w/index.php?title=Petronius_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Petronius of Verona (page does not exist)">Petronius of Verona</a> (r. 412–429) wrote of Zeno's virtues and also confirmed the existence of a cult dedicated to Saint Zeno.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A poem written between 781 and 810, called the <i><a href="/wiki/Versus_de_Verona" title="Versus de Verona">Versus de Verona</a></i>, an <a href="/wiki/Elegy" title="Elegy">elegy</a> of the city in verse, states that Zeno was the eighth bishop of Verona.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Zeno_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg/150px-Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="292" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg/225px-Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg/300px-Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2408" data-file-height="4682" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Left_panel_-_Pala_di_San_Zeno_by_Andrea_Mantegna_-_San_Zeno_-_Verona_2016.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/San_Zeno_Altarpiece" class="mw-redirect" title="San Zeno Altarpiece">San Zeno Altarpiece</a>. Zeno is on the far right.</div></div></div>
<p>Zeno's liturgical <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on 12 April, but in the diocese of Verona, it is also celebrated on 21 May, in honor of the <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translation</a> of his relics on 21 May 807.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG/220px-St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG/330px-St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8b/St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG/440px-St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="2592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Zeno%27s_body.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Zeno's body ready for his feast day procession on 21 May 2012</div></div></div>
<p>Tradition states that Zeno built the first basilica in Verona, situated in the area probably occupied by the present-day cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His eponymous church in its present location dates to the early ninth century, when it was endowed by <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> and his son <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Italy" title="Pepin of Italy">Pepin, King of Italy</a>. It was consecrated on 8 December 806; two local <a href="/wiki/Hermits" class="mw-redirect" title="Hermits">hermits</a>, Benignus and Carus, were assigned the task of translating Zeno's relics to a new marble <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> King Pepin was present at the ceremony, as were the Bishops of <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Cremona" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Cremona">Cremona</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Salzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Salzburg">Salzburg</a>, as well as an immense crowd of townspeople.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The church was damaged at the beginning of the tenth century by Hungarians, though the relics of Zeno remained safe.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The basilica was rebuilt again, and made much larger and stronger. Financial support was provided by <a href="/wiki/Otto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I">Otto I</a>, and it was re-consecrated in 967, at a ceremony presided over by the Bishop <a href="/wiki/Ratherius" title="Ratherius">Ratherius</a> of Verona.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Zeno" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of San Zeno">present church of San Zeno</a> in Verona is a work of the twelfth, thirteenth and early fifteenth centuries for the most part. It is well known for its bronze doors (c.&#160;1100&#160;&#8211; c.&#160;1200) which depict, besides stories from the Bible, the miracles of Saint Zeno, images drawn from stories, including those recorded by the notary Coronato,<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the facade sculpture signed by Nicholaus and an associate Guglielmus, and the <a href="/wiki/Rose_window" title="Rose window">rose window</a> (c.&#160;1200), which is the work of Brioloto.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends_and_iconography">Legends and iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Zeno_of_Verona&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legends and iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Adige_Verona.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Adige_Verona.jpg/150px-Adige_Verona.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="225" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Adige_Verona.jpg/225px-Adige_Verona.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Adige_Verona.jpg/300px-Adige_Verona.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Adige_Verona.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Adige" title="Adige">Adige</a> flowing through Verona</div></div></div>
<p>Zeno is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Fishermen" class="mw-redirect" title="Fishermen">fishermen</a> and anglers, the city of Verona, newborn babies as well as children learning to speak and walk. Some 30 churches or chapels have been dedicated to him, including <a href="/wiki/Pistoia_Cathedral" title="Pistoia Cathedral">Pistoia Cathedral</a>.
</p><p>According to legend he was stolen at birth and briefly replaced by a <a href="/wiki/Demon" title="Demon">demonic</a> <a href="/wiki/Changeling" title="Changeling">changeling</a>.  One story relates that Saint Zeno, one day fishing on the banks of the <a href="/wiki/Adige" title="Adige">Adige</a>, which he did in order to feed himself (rather than as recreation), saw a peasant crossing the river in a horse and cart. The horses began to get strangely skittish. Zeno, believing this to be the work of the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">devil</a>, made the <a href="/wiki/Sign_of_the_cross" title="Sign of the cross">sign of the cross</a>, and the horses calmed down.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Zeno was often said to combat the devil, and is sometimes depicted treading on a demon.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Another story relates that he exorcised a demon from the body of the daughter of the Emperor Gallienus (though Zeno probably did not live during the reign of Gallienus). The story relates that the grateful Gallienus allowed Zeno and other Christians freedom of worship in the empire.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Gregory the Great, at the end of the 6th century, relates a miracle associated with the <a href="/wiki/Intercession_of_saints" title="Intercession of saints">divine intercession</a> of Zeno.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 588, the Adige flooded its banks, inundating Verona. The floodwater reached the church dedicated to Saint Zeno, but miraculously did not enter it, even though the door was wide open. The church was donated to <a href="/wiki/Theodelinda" title="Theodelinda">Theodelinda</a>, an alleged eyewitness to the miracle and wife of king <a href="/wiki/Authari" title="Authari">Authari</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Santiebeati_1-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Santiebeati-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Zeno is most often represented with fishing-related items such as a fish, fishing rod, or as a bishop holding a fishing rod, or with a fish hanging from his <a href="/wiki/Crozier" class="mw-redirect" title="Crozier">crozier</a>.  "Local tradition says the bishop was fond of fishing in the nearby river Adige," writes <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, "but it is more likely that originally it was a symbol of his success in bringing people to baptism."<sup id="cite_ref-Alban_Butler_1995_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Alban_Butler_1995-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
