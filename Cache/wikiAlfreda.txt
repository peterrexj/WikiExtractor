					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">9th-century English saint, virgin and recluse</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other people with the same name, see <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfthryth" class="mw-redirect" title="Ælfthryth">Ælfthryth</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" <p><b>Ælfthryth</b>, also known as <b>Alfreda,</b> <b>Alfritha</b>, <b>Aelfnryth</b>, or <b>Etheldritha</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-catholicorg_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicorg-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Recluse" title="Recluse">recluse</a>, venerated in both the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Orthodox Church of Antioch">Antiochian Orthodox Church</a>.  She was a daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Offa" class="mw-redirect" title="Offa">Offa</a> of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a> and his consort, <a href="/wiki/Cynethryth" title="Cynethryth">Cynethryth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicorg_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicorg-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ælfthryth was "either betrothed to or loved by"<sup id="cite_ref-catholicorg_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicorg-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelberht_II_of_East_Anglia" title="Æthelberht II of East Anglia">St. Ethelbert</a>, the king of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia" title="Kingdom of East Anglia">East Angles</a>.  In 793, after visiting Ælfthyth, he was taken captive and murdered by her mother so that Cynethryth's brother could ascend to the throne instead; Ælfthryth's was horrified by the murder, so she departed the court and retired to the <a href="/wiki/Crowland_Abbey" title="Crowland Abbey">Crowland Abbey</a> in the marshes of <a href="/wiki/Crowland" title="Crowland">Crowland</a>, where she lived as a recluse for 40 years, until her death of natural causes in 835.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicorg_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicorg-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Ælfthryth's sister Aelfreda also lost a husband due to their parents' political intrigue.<sup id="cite_ref-catholicorg_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholicorg-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i>Oxford Dictionary of Saints</i>, Ælfthryth was "famous for her prophecies".<sup id="cite_ref-oxford_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxford-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Her tomb was arranged around <a href="/wiki/Guthlac_of_Crowland" title="Guthlac of Crowland">St. Guthlac's</a>.  A Crowland tradition states that Ælfthryth's relics were destroyed in 870 when Danes destroyed the abbey, but there is little evidence for it.<sup id="cite_ref-oxford_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oxford-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Her feast day is 2 August.<sup id="cite_ref-antiochian_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-antiochian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
