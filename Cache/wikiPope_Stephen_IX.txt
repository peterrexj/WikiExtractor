<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">"Stephen IX" redirects here. <p><b>Pope Stephen IX</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Stephanus</i>, christened <b>Frederick</b>; c. 1020<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> – 29 March 1058) was the head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 3 August 1057 to his death in 29 March 1058. He was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Ardenne-Verdun_family" class="mw-redirect" title="Ardenne-Verdun family">Ardenne-Verdun family</a>, who ruled the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Lorraine" title="Duchy of Lorraine">Duchy of Lorraine</a>, and started his ecclesiastical career as a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>. He was invited to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo IX</a>, who made him <a href="/wiki/Papal_chancellor" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal chancellor">chancellor</a> in 1051 and one of three <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">legates</a> to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 1054. The failure of their negotiations with Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Michael_I_Cerularius" title="Michael I Cerularius">Michael I Cerularius</a> of Constantinople and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Leo_of_Ohrid" title="Leo of Ohrid">Leo of Ohrid</a> led to the permanent <a href="/wiki/East-West_Schism" class="mw-redirect" title="East-West Schism">East-West Schism</a>. He continued as chancellor to the next <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_II" title="Pope Victor II">Victor II</a>, and was elected abbot of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastery of <a href="/wiki/Montecassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Montecassino">Montecassino</a>.
</p><p>Stephen was <a href="/wiki/Papal_selection_before_1059" title="Papal selection before 1059">elected</a> to succeed Victor on 2 August 1057. As pope, Stephen retained the Montecassino abbacy, enforced the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Reform" title="Gregorian Reform">Gregorian Reform</a>, and continued Leo IX's efforts to expel <a href="/wiki/Normans_from_southern_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Normans from southern Italy">Normans from southern Italy</a>. He died in <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, apparently poisoned by Romans, while endeavouring to crown his brother <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_the_Bearded" title="Godfrey the Bearded">Godfrey the Bearded</a> as <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Roman emperor">Holy Roman emperor</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Christened Frederick,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> he was a younger brother of Duke <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_the_Bearded" title="Godfrey the Bearded">Godfrey the Bearded</a> of Lorraine,<sup id="cite_ref-Healy50_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy50-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and part of the <a href="/wiki/Ardennes-Verdun_dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Ardennes-Verdun dynasty">Ardennes-Verdun dynasty</a> that played a prominent role in the politics of the period.<sup id="cite_ref-Brucker1889_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brucker1889-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Another older brother, Duke <a href="/wiki/Gothelo_II_of_Lower_Lorraine" title="Gothelo II of Lower Lorraine">Gothelo II of Lower Lorraine</a>, died in 1046. His younger sister, Regelinde, married Count <a href="/wiki/Albert_II_of_Namur" class="mw-redirect" title="Albert II of Namur">Albert II of Namur</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Another sister, Oda, married Count <a href="/wiki/Lambert_II_of_Louvain" class="mw-redirect" title="Lambert II of Louvain">Lambert II of Louvain</a>. Frederick's youngest sister, Mathilda, was married to Count Palatine <a href="/wiki/Henry_I_of_Lotharingia" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry I of Lotharingia">Henry I of Lotharingia</a>. The family had strong ties to the abbey of St. Vanne.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy50_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy50-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pre-papal_career">Pre-papal career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Pre-papal career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Frederick held a <a href="/wiki/Canonry" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonry">canonry</a><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/St._Lambert%27s_Cathedral_in_Li%C3%A8ge" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Lambert&#39;s Cathedral in Liège">St. Lambert's Cathedral in Liège</a> and then became the <a href="/wiki/Archdeacon" title="Archdeacon">archdeacon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy50_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy50-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1049, or perhaps 1051, he met <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX" title="Pope Leo IX">Pope Leo IX</a>, and was invited to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> He was appointed <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-deacon" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-deacon">cardinal-deacon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Domnica" title="Santa Maria in Domnica">Santa Maria in Domnica</a> by Leo IX. He was also appointed "bibliothecarius et cancellarius", in succession to Odo, the primicerius of Toul, who was named a bishop, and whose latest signature as chancellor is on 16 January 1051.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Frederick appears as a signatory to papal documents from 9 March 1051 to 21 December 1053.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Travels">Travels</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Travels">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>As chancellor, Frederick's duties in preparing and issuing papal documents, especially bulls, required his constant attendance on the pope. He therefore followed Leo in various travels. He participated in the pope's southern journey from May to August 1052; he signed a bull at S. Germano on 20 May 1052, and on 1 July he was in <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a>. He attended Leo in his trip to Germany, where the pope met with <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Henry_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Henry III">Emperor Henry III</a>, and attempted to reconcile him with King <a href="/wiki/Andrew_I_of_Hungary" title="Andrew I of Hungary">Andrew I of Hungary</a>; they were at Bamberg with the emperor, on 18 October, when Frederick signed a bull, and spent Christmas in Worms.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> During their return journey, Bishop Peter of Le Puy was consecrated at Ravenna on 14 March 1053, and Chancellor Frederick signed the certificate of consecration.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The return from Germany had a purpose behind it. Along with Frederick came his brother Godefroy, and numerous troops. Pope Leo, who had negotiated an agreement with the Byzantine <a href="/wiki/Catepan_of_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Catepan of Italy">catepan of Italy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Argyrus_(catepan_of_Italy)" title="Argyrus (catepan of Italy)">Argyros</a>, was planning a war against the Normans, which was launched in April 1053. Argyrus proved to be no help at all. The papal forces, filled with Germans, suffered a disastrous <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Civitate" title="Battle of Civitate">defeat at Civitate</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Capitanata" class="mw-redirect" title="Capitanata">Capitanata</a> on 18 June 1053. The pope and his cardinals were driven out of their refuge in <a href="/wiki/San_Paolo_di_Civitate" title="San Paolo di Civitate">Civitate</a>, and forced to sue for peace. They were seized by the Normans and conveyed to Benevento, where he was installed by 23 June. The pope remained in custody all winter, not setting out for Rome until 12 March 1054.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Humbert-Kerularij.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Humbert-Kerularij.jpg/220px-Humbert-Kerularij.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="160" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Humbert-Kerularij.jpg/330px-Humbert-Kerularij.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Humbert-Kerularij.jpg/440px-Humbert-Kerularij.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="437" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Humbert-Kerularij.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Patriarch Michael enthroned, <i><a href="/wiki/Madrid_Skylitzes" title="Madrid Skylitzes">Madrid Skylitzes</a></i></div></div></div>
<p>Frederick was with Leo throughout the campaign against the Normans until he was appointed to the embassy to Constantinople. On 28 May 1053, he was at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Montecassino" class="mw-redirect" title="Montecassino">Montecassino</a>, on 10 June at Sale, and on 18 June at Civitate.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In January 1054, the chancellor Frederick was appointed, along with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Humbert_of_Silva_Candida" title="Humbert of Silva Candida">Humbert of Silva Candida</a> and Archbishop Pietro of Amalfi, to a legation from Pope Leo IX to the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_Monomachos" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine Monomachos">Constantine Monomachos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Their purpose was to persuade the emperor to come to the aid of the pope, join forces with Henry III, and destroy the Normans.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> When the legates reached Constantinople, they were hospitably received by the emperor and stayed at the imperial palace for some time. Negotiations between them, Patriarch <a href="/wiki/Michael_Cerularius" class="mw-redirect" title="Michael Cerularius">Michael Cerularius</a> of Constantinople, and Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Leo_of_Ohrid" title="Leo of Ohrid">Leo of Ohrid</a>, however, did not succeed. Pope Leo died during the negotiations, on 19 April 1054. On 16 July 1054, Cardinal Humbert placed the bull of excommunication of the patriarch and archbishop on the high altar of <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a>. Cardinal Frederick discharged the function of one of the three <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legates</a> participating in the events that led to the <a href="/wiki/East-West_Schism" class="mw-redirect" title="East-West Schism">East-West Schism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTESiecienski2010_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTESiecienski2010-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> When the legates announced their intended departure, the emperor bestowed upon them, in honor of their work, imperial gifts, as well as substantial gifts to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Refuge">Refuge</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Refuge">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Codex_Aureus.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Codex_Aureus.jpg/220px-Codex_Aureus.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="192" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Codex_Aureus.jpg/330px-Codex_Aureus.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Codex_Aureus.jpg/440px-Codex_Aureus.jpg 2x" data-file-width="639" data-file-height="557" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Codex_Aureus.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Emperor Henry III and Empress Agnes kneeling before the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Golden_Gospels_of_Henry_III" title="Golden Gospels of Henry III">Speyer Evangeliary</a></div></div></div>
<p>Frederick apparently brought back a substantial amount of additional money (<i>pecunia</i>) with him from Constantinople, sufficient to bring suspicion upon him on the part of Emperor Henry III.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> In November 1054, the emperor named <a href="/wiki/Gebhard_of_Dollnstein-Hirschberg" class="mw-redirect" title="Gebhard of Dollnstein-Hirschberg">Gebhard of Dollnstein-Hirschberg</a> as the next <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, and the latter was enthroned as Victor II on 13 April 1055. Frederick was back at the papal chancellery in 1055, but, when the Emperor visited Italy in the summer of 1055, he commanded that Victor arrest Frederick and send him to the German court immediately. Frederick sought refuge at Montecassino late in 1055, where he became a monk of the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Order of St. Benedict</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> To escape the imperial and papal agents, with the abbot's permission, he withdrew to the Island of Termiti in the Adriatic, off the port of Termoli.
</p><p>In August 1056, Pope Victor, harassed by political problems on all sides, travelled to Germany to consult with and gain help from Emperor Henry. He was received by the emperor at Goslar on 8 September, and was with him at <a href="/wiki/Bodfeld" title="Bodfeld">Bodfeld</a> on 21 September; but, on 5 October 1056, Henry died. He was buried at Speyer on 21 October. Shortly thereafter, the imperial court was at <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a>, where the pope enthroned Henry III's six-year-old son <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV</a>. A reconciliation between the Lorrainers and the imperial family became possible. At a meeting in Cologne in December, the pope reconciled Count <a href="/wiki/Baldwin_V_of_Flanders" class="mw-redirect" title="Baldwin V of Flanders">Baldwin V of Flanders</a> and Duke Godfrey of Lorraine, Frederick's brother, to <a href="/wiki/Empress_Agnes" class="mw-redirect" title="Empress Agnes">Empress Agnes</a>, widow of Henry III and <a href="/wiki/Regent" title="Regent">regent</a> for their son Henry IV.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbacy">Abbacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Abbacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt,_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt%2C_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG/170px-Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt%2C_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="223" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt%2C_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG/255px-Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt%2C_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt%2C_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG/340px-Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt%2C_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG 2x" data-file-width="881" data-file-height="1157" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gebhard_I_Bischof_von_Eichst%C3%A4tt,_als_Victor_II_Papst.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Pope Victor in the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pontifical_of_Gundekar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pontifical of Gundekar (page does not exist)">Pontifical of Gundekar</a></div></div></div>
<p>Pope Victor was annoyed by the fact that, following the death of Abbot Richer of Montecassino in December 1055, the papacy had not been consulted in the selection of his successor, Abbot Peter. The situation was made worse by dissensions inside the community, and reports to the pope of various alleged misdoings. Official attempts to justify the traditional procedure of election of abbots did not satisfy the reforming agenda of Roman officials.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> In May 1057, shortly after <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Humbert_of_Silva_Candida" title="Humbert of Silva Candida">Humbert of Silva Candida</a>, sent as papal legate, began a series of investigations at the monastery. On 22 May he persuaded Abbot Peter to resign. And on 23 May, in a chapter meeting of all of the brothers, presided over by Cardinal Humbert, they began to elect a new abbot; the successful candidate was Cardinal Frederick of Lorraine, who was a monk of Montecassino. Ten days after his election, he and Cardinal Humbert proceeded to the papal court, which was in Tuscany at the time. He was consecrated the thirty-sixth abbot of Montecassino by Pope Victor on 24 June 1057,<sup id="cite_ref-Ganzer,_p._16_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ganzer,_p._16-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> and ruled for a little over ten months.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> He obtained for his abbey the privilege of being seated ahead of all other abbots at synods, and for the abbots of Montecassino the privilege of wearing the sandals and dalmatic at religious ceremonies.<sup id="cite_ref-Ganzer,_p._16_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ganzer,_p._16-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 14 June 1057, Frederick was promoted to the position of <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-presbyter" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-presbyter">cardinal-presbyter</a> of <a href="/wiki/San_Crisogono,_Rome" title="San Crisogono, Rome">San Crisogono</a> by Victor.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Frederick took part in a synod at Arezzo on 23 July, and then began the return journey to Montecassino. He reached Rome, where he took possession of S. Crisogono, his titular church, and took up residence at the Benedictine house, the Pallaria, on the Palatine Hill.<sup id="cite_ref-Ganzer,_p._16_23-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ganzer,_p._16-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Pope Victor died at Arezzo on 28 July, while Frederick was still in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Papacy">Papacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Papacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>News of Victor's death was brought to Rome by Cardinal Boniface of Albano on 31 July. For the rest of that day, and all the next day, the clergy and populace of Rome discussed what to do next, and specifically whether they should proceed to a papal election. From their discussions, five names emerged as viable candidates: Cardinal Humbert, Cardinal Giovanni of Velletri, Bishop Ottcharius of Perugia, Cardinal Pietro of Tusculum (Frascati), and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Hildebrand</a>, the subdeacon of the Roman Church. None of these seemed quite satisfactory, and they compelled Cardinal Frederick to leave his refuge at S. Maria in Pallaria and attend their assembly at the Basilica of S. Peter in vinculis.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> On 2 August 1057,<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Frederick was <a href="/wiki/Papal_selection_before_1059" title="Papal selection before 1059">chosen</a> to become the new pope. He took the name Stephen IX.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> He was escorted to the <a href="/wiki/Lateran_Palace" title="Lateran Palace">Lateran Palace</a>, and next day escorted to <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>, where he was <a href="/wiki/Papal_coronation" title="Papal coronation">crowned</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Church_discipline">Church discipline</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Church discipline">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>As pope, Stephen enforced the policies of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Reform" title="Gregorian Reform">Gregorian Reform</a>. He held several synods in Rome from August to November 1057, focusing most vigorously on the importance of <a href="/wiki/Clerical_celibacy" title="Clerical celibacy">clerical celibacy</a>. He held a synod at the Lateran Basilica in which he remarked that the Eastern Church tradition allowed priests, deacons, and subdeacons to marry, but the Western Church did not.<sup id="cite_ref-Jaffé,_p._554_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jaffé,_p._554-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stephen visited the monastery at Montecassino from 30 November 1057 to 10 February 1058. He was still its abbot, and remained so until his death. There he immediately began a program of reform, by every means at his disposal, persuading, exhorting and haranguing the monks to rid themselves of the lax practices that had crept in over the years. He even banned the use of <a href="/wiki/Ambrosian_chant" title="Ambrosian chant">Ambrosian chant</a>, mandating the sole use of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_chant" title="Gregorian chant">Gregorian chant</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> In December 1057, he was so ill with "the Roman fever" that he believed he was going to die.<sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In regional politics, Stephen ordered the bishops of England not to seek <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">consecration</a> from Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Stigand" title="Stigand">Stigand</a> of Canterbury, who was <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> and usurping the archbishopric.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Secular_politics">Secular politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Secular politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Italy_1000_AD.svg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Italy_1000_AD.svg/220px-Italy_1000_AD.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="316" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Italy_1000_AD.svg/330px-Italy_1000_AD.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Italy_1000_AD.svg/440px-Italy_1000_AD.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="565" data-file-height="811" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Italy_1000_AD.svg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Italy in the 11th century</div></div></div>
<p>Stephen was planning for the expulsion of the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_southern_Italy" title="Norman conquest of southern Italy">Normans from southern Italy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> At some point in 1057 or 1058, he held a synod in which the citizens of Capua, the capital of the Norman principality, and a cleric named Lando were excommunicated.<sup id="cite_ref-Jaffé,_p._554_31-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Jaffé,_p._554-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> In order to advance his aims in the south, he decided, at the beginning of 1058, to send a delegation to the new Byzantine Emperor <a href="/wiki/Isaac_I_Komnenos" title="Isaac I Komnenos">Isaac I Komnenos</a> (1057-1059). The legates were Cardinal Stephen, the Benedictine abbot of the monastery of Ss. Andrea e Gregorio in Rome; Cardinal Mainard, a Benedictine monk of Montecassino; and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Victor_III" title="Pope Victor III">Desiderius</a>, the Benedictine abbot-elect of Montecassino.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> These papal delegates departed from Rome, but when they reached Byzantine-held <a href="/wiki/Bari" title="Bari">Bari</a>, news came that Stephen IX has died, and the mission was abandoned.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBloch198638_37-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBloch198638-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his recovery from his fever, Pope Stephen returned to Rome, and on 8 March 1058, consecrated Bishop <a href="/wiki/Alfanus_I" title="Alfanus I">Alfanus I</a> of Salerno. In mid-March, he ordered the provost of Montecassino to collect all of the gold and silver of his church and secretly send it immediately to Rome; the pope promised that he would soon return it, with interest. Despite their deep distress, the monks complied, sending their gold and silver to the pope. The provost was on his way back to the monastery, having obeyed the pope's commands, when he received a message to return to the pope, who returned Montecassino's treasures to him. Pope Stephen, however was relieved, by force on the part of the Romans, of the treasure which he had brought to Rome from Constantinople.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup> The pope was planning the elevation of his brother to the imperial throne.<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> For this purpose, and, if the Roman annals are to be believed, out of anger at being plundered, he set out on a journey to Tuscany, planning to crown and lead his brother back to Rome, and from there march against the Normans.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> Before he departed Rome, the pope summoned a meeting of the bishops, clergy, and people of Rome, and strictly commanded them (<i>sub districta nimis interdictione constituit</i>) that if he should die before the return of the subdeacon Hildebrand from the imperial court, they should on no account proceed to the election of a pope, but await his return.<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As he was travelling up the road from Rome to Florence, by way of Arezzo, Pope Stephen conceived of the idea of visiting Abbot <a href="/wiki/John_Gualbert" title="John Gualbert">John Gualbert</a> at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Vallombrosians" title="Vallombrosians">Vallombrosa</a>. He sent to ask for an interview, and John replied with a refusal, naming illness as his excuse. The pope sent a second time, and received a second refusal. He then moved on to Florence without the two ever having come face to face.<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Stephen_IX&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Stephen IX died at <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> on 29 March 1058. He was attended at his deathbed by Abbot Hugh of Cluny.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup> The Roman annals report that he was given poison on the journey to Florence by an agent of the Romans. The Romans also asserted that Duke Godfrey, the pope's brother, had dispatched 500 troops and money to regain control of Rome, which motivated the Romans to act.<sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stephen was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Santa_Reparata,_Florence" title="Santa Reparata, Florence">Church of Santa Reparata</a>, which was demolished in 1357 to make way for the construction of the new cathedral, <a href="/wiki/Florence_Cathedral" title="Florence Cathedral">Santa Maria del Fiore</a>. During the excavations his tomb was discovered next to the altar of S. Zenobio, and identified by an inscription as well as pontifical insignia with which he had been clothed. The present whereabouts of the remains is unknown.<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup> Members of his chapel, which he had brought along from Montecassino, were afraid to return through Rome, and were therefore escorted back to their monastery by Florentine soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Stephen IX is considered by the modern Roman <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> to have been succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II" title="Pope Nicholas II">Nicholas II</a>, though others consider his successor to be <a href="/wiki/Benedict_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedict X">Benedict X</a>, who came to be regarded as an <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a> by some, in the 14th century. Those who rejected the papacy of Benedict X reported a vacancy of the papal throne of nine months and eight days after the death of Stephen IX.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
