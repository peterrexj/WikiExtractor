<p><b>Aloysius Liguda</b> (January 23, 1898 &#8211; December 8, 1942), was a priest and is venerated as a blessed martyr of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_the_Divine_Word" title="Society of the Divine Word">Society Of The Divine Word Missionaries</a> (SVD). Liguda was a chaplain, and teacher. He died at Dachau concentration camp in the course of medical experimentation.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alojzy_Liguda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Alojzy Liguda was born in <a href="/wiki/Win%C3%B3w" title="Winów">Winów</a> near <a href="/wiki/Opole" title="Opole">Opole</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poland" title="Poland">Poland</a> in 1892, the youngest of seven children of Wojciech and Rozalia Przybyl Liguda.<sup id="cite_ref-Ogiolda_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ogiolda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His father used to lead pilgrimages to the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Visitation,_Wambierzyce" title="Basilica of the Visitation, Wambierzyce">Basilica of the Visitation</a> in  <a href="/wiki/Wambierzyce" title="Wambierzyce">Wambierzyce</a> and to <a href="/wiki/St._Anne_(hill)" title="St. Anne (hill)">Mount St. Anne</a>. Liguda was a good student; he became interested in the missions in China and Africa from reading magazines.<sup id="cite_ref-curia_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-curia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of fifteen, Liguda entered the Divine Word minor seminary at <a href="/wiki/Nysa,_Poland" title="Nysa, Poland">Nysa</a>. He was drafted in 1917 and sent to the French front as an artilleryman. After the war, he returned to the seminary to complete his studies. In 1920 he went to the Society of the Divine Word novitiate in <a href="/wiki/M%C3%B6dling" title="Mödling">Mödling</a>, Austria. He was then sent for teacher training to <a href="/wiki/Pieniezno" class="mw-redirect" title="Pieniezno">Pieniezno</a>, where he taught Latin and mathematics at the minor seminary. From there he returned to St. Gabriel's in Mödling for further studies in dogmatics and church history. At age 35, in 1927, he was ordained as a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-curia_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-curia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Liguda was assigned to the provincial house in <a href="/wiki/G%C3%B3rna_Grupa" title="Górna Grupa">Górna Grupa</a>. After validating his secondary education certificate he was accepted to the Polish Philology Faculty at the <a href="/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University_in_Pozna%C5%84" title="Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań">University of Poznań</a>. In Poznań he also served a chaplain and religious education teacher at the Ursuline Sisters school for girls.<sup id="cite_ref-curia_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-curia-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He returned to Grupa and taught at the minor seminary; he also served as chaplain to the garrison in Grupa on Sundays. In the summer of 1939 he became the rector of the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Gorna_Grupa" class="mw-redirect" title="Gorna Grupa">Gorna Grupa</a>. 
</p><p>In February 1940, turned the SVD house into a makeshift detention camp for the religious and seminarians. He was sent from one camp to another: Nowy Port in Gdańsk, Sachsenhausen, and Stutthof. By December he was in Dachau, and since he knew German, he was a translator for most of the internees. After the war, it turned out that he could successfully apply for release from the camp. The family had German citizenship, he himself was a war veteran, and two brothers died at the front. Liguda did not apply because he felt responsible for his Polish pupils. Always he tried to keep up morale with his calm demeanor and a ready joke.<sup id="cite_ref-Ogiolda_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ogiolda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Liguda had contracted tuberculosis but by December 1942 his health had much improved. Nonetheless, he was placed on the "disabled" list by a kapo in revenge for Liguda's criticism of the kapo's unfair distribution of food. Liguda and fellow inmates were transported by German soldiers into a nearby water reservoir where they all drowned. The Germans were conducting medical experiments to determine the effect of ice cold water on the human body, relative to the rescue of downed pilots. The family was advised, however, that the cause of death was tuberculosis.<sup id="cite_ref-Ogiolda_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ogiolda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Winau_Kirche.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Winau_Kirche.jpg/220px-Winau_Kirche.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="123" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Winau_Kirche.jpg/330px-Winau_Kirche.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/Winau_Kirche.jpg/440px-Winau_Kirche.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3264" data-file-height="1832" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Winau_Kirche.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Winau Kirche</div></div></div>
<p>His body was burnt in the Dachau crematorium; the coffin with ashes was handed over to relatives and buried in the family tomb. In 2016, some of the ashes, as well as those of other martyrs of the Dachau concentration camp were place in the Church of the Holy Spirit in his hometown of Winów.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"In the Dachau prison camp near Munich in Germany, Blessed Louis Liguda, priest of the Society of the Divine Word and martyr, who, during the wartime invasion of Poland, was slain by the prison guards witness to Christ the Lord until death."(Roman Martyrology)<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>   
</p>
