<p><b>Sir Adrian Fortescue</b> (c. 1476 – 9 July 1539) was a courtier at the court of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII of England</a> and member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Dominic" title="Third Order of Saint Dominic">Third Order of Saint Dominic</a> who was executed in 1539 and later beatified as a Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Fortescue_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Adrian Fortescue was the son of Sir John Fortescue of Ponsbourne Park at <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Street_Village" class="mw-redirect" title="Newgate Street Village">Newgate Street Village</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hertfordshire" title="Hertfordshire">Hertfordshire</a>. He descended from Richard Fortescue, younger brother of <a href="/wiki/Henry_Fortescue_(Lord_Chief_Justice)" title="Henry Fortescue (Lord Chief Justice)">Sir Henry Fortescue</a> (<a href="/wiki/Fl." class="mw-redirect" title="Fl.">fl.</a> 1426), <a href="/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Common_Pleas" title="Chief Justice of the Common Pleas">Lord Chief Justice of the Common Pleas</a> in Ireland, and of <a href="/wiki/John_Fortescue_(judge)" title="John Fortescue (judge)">Sir John Fortescue</a> (ca. 1394&#160;&#8211;&#32;ca. 1480), <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chief_Justice_of_England_and_Wales" title="Lord Chief Justice of England and Wales">Lord Chief Justice of England and Wales</a>, all sons of Sir <a href="/wiki/John_Fortescue_(Captain_of_Meaux)" title="John Fortescue (Captain of Meaux)">John Fortescue</a> (fl.1422) of <a href="/wiki/Whympston,_Modbury" class="mw-redirect" title="Whympston, Modbury">Whympston</a> in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Modbury" title="Modbury">Modbury</a>, Devon, appointed in 1422 Captain of the captured <a href="/wiki/Meaux" title="Meaux">Castle of Meaux</a>, 25 miles NE of <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His mother Alice was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_Boleyn" title="Geoffrey Boleyn">Geoffrey Boleyn</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lord_Mayor_of_London" title="Lord Mayor of London">Lord Mayor of London</a>, and great aunt to <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII" title="Henry VIII">Henry VIII</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Wives_of_Henry_VIII" title="Wives of Henry VIII">second wife</a>, <a href="/wiki/Anne_Boleyn" title="Anne Boleyn">Anne Boleyn</a>.
</p><p>He was made a <a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Bath" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Bath">Knight of the Bath</a> in 1503. He spent most of his time in the country, busy with his lands and with county affairs. He lived at his wife's family seat at <a href="/wiki/Stonor_Park" title="Stonor Park">Stonor Park</a> in <a href="/wiki/Oxfordshire" title="Oxfordshire">Oxfordshire</a>, where he served as a <a href="/wiki/Justice_of_the_Peace" class="mw-redirect" title="Justice of the Peace">Justice of the Peace</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Dominicans_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dominicans-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Fortescue participated in England's wars against France in 1513 and 1523 and was present at the meeting in 1520 between Henry VIII and <a href="/wiki/Francis_I_of_France" title="Francis I of France">Francis I of France</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Field_of_the_Cloth_of_Gold" title="Field of the Cloth of Gold">Field of the Cloth of Gold</a>.
</p><p>He was made a Knight of the <a href="/wiki/Sovereign_Military_Order_of_Malta" title="Sovereign Military Order of Malta">Order of St. John</a> in 1532 and the following year became a Dominican Tertiary of the Blackfriars of Oxford. He attended the coronation of Anne Boleyn in June of that year.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 29 August 1534, he was arrested without any stated reason and taken to Woodstock, where he was questioned. He was freed after a period of months.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In February 1539 he was again arrested, and in April he was among those condemned for <a href="/wiki/Treason" title="Treason">treason</a> without a trial by Parliament for unspecified acts presumably relating to hostility to <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>'s church policies.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was beheaded at the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a> on 9 July 1539. His servants were also killed for treason on the same day but were hanged, drawn and quartered at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Dominicans_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dominicans-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Fortescue_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Fortescue was twice married: 
</p>
<ul><li>first to Anne, daughter of Sir William Stonor and Anne Neville, daughter of <a href="/wiki/John_Neville,_1st_Marquess_of_Montagu" title="John Neville, 1st Marquess of Montagu">John Neville, 1st Marquess of Montagu</a>. Anne died in 1518. By his first wife, Fortescue had two daughters:
<ul><li>Margaret, married to <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Wentworth,_1st_Baron_Wentworth" title="Thomas Wentworth, 1st Baron Wentworth">Thomas Wentworth</a>, 1st baron Wentworth;</li>
<li>Frances, married to <a href="/wiki/Thomas_FitzGerald,_10th_Earl_of_Kildare" title="Thomas FitzGerald, 10th Earl of Kildare">Thomas Fitzgerald</a>, 10th earl of Kildare.</li></ul></li>
<li>secondly to Anne, daughter of Sir William Rede, of <a href="/wiki/Boarstall" title="Boarstall">Boarstall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Buckinghamshire" title="Buckinghamshire">Buckinghamshire</a> and widow of Sir Giles Greville. By his second wife he had three sons and two daughters:
<ul><li>Sir <a href="/wiki/John_Fortescue_of_Salden" title="John Fortescue of Salden">John Fortescue of Salden</a>, Chancellor of the Exchequer;</li>
<li>Sir Thomas Fortescue, MP Wallingford;</li>
<li>Sir <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Fortescue" title="Anthony Fortescue">Anthony Fortescue</a>;</li>
<li>Elizabeth, married to Sir <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Bromley" title="Thomas Bromley">Thomas Bromley</a>, lord chancellor of England;</li>
<li>Mary, whose son was <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cavendish" title="Thomas Cavendish">Thomas Cavendish</a> the circumnavigator.</li></ul></li></ul>
<p>Anne survived her husband, and afterwards married Sir Thomas Parry, comptroller of Queen Elizabeth's household. She was granted the manor of <a href="/wiki/Great_Washbourne" title="Great Washbourne">Great Washbourne</a> in 1557.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_Fortescue_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._John_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. John of Jerusalem">Order of St. John of Jerusalem</a> (Order of Malta) has advocated devotion to Blessed Adrian as a martyr since the 17th century and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> him on 13 May 1895.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>In 1970 a <b>Sub-Priory of the Blessed Adrian Fortescue</b> of the Order of Malta was founded and renamed in 1993 as <a href="/wiki/List_of_the_priors_of_Saint_John_of_Jerusalem_in_England" title="List of the priors of Saint John of Jerusalem in England">Grand Priory of England</a>.
</p>
