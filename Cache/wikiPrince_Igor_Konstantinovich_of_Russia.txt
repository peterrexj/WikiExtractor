<p><b>Prince Igor Constantinovich of Russia</b> (<i>Игорь Константинович</i>; 10 June 1894 – 18 July 1918)<sup id="cite_ref-angel_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-angel-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was the sixth child of <a href="/wiki/Grand_Duke_Constantine_Constantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Duke Constantine Constantinovich of Russia">Grand Duke Constantine Constantinovich of Russia</a> by his wife Elisaveta Mavrikievna née <a href="/wiki/Princess_Elisabeth_of_Saxe-Altenburg_(1865%E2%80%931927)" title="Princess Elisabeth of Saxe-Altenburg (1865–1927)">Princess Elisabeth of Saxe-Altenburg</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Prince_Igor_Constantinovich_of_Russia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Igor was born on June 10, 1894, and attended the <a href="/wiki/Corps_des_Pages" class="mw-redirect" title="Corps des Pages">Corps des Pages</a>, an imperial military academy in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Petersburg" title="Saint Petersburg">Saint Petersburg</a>. He enjoyed theatre.
</p><p>During <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, he was a cornet in the His Majesty's Hussar Guards Regiment. His health was quite fragile: he had <a href="/wiki/Pleurisy" title="Pleurisy">pleurisy</a> and lung complications in 1915, and even if he returned to the trenches, he couldn't walk quickly and often coughed and spat blood.
</p><p>On 4 April 1918, he was exiled to the Urals by the Bolsheviks and murdered in July the same year in a mineshaft<sup id="cite_ref-alex_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-alex-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  near the town of <a href="/wiki/Alapaevsk" class="mw-redirect" title="Alapaevsk">Alapaevsk</a>, along with his brothers <a href="/wiki/Prince_John_Constantinovich_of_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince John Constantinovich of Russia">Prince John Constantinovich</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prince_Constantine_Constantinovich_of_Russia" title="Prince Constantine Constantinovich of Russia">Prince Constantine Constantinovich</a>, his cousin Prince <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_Pavlovich_Paley" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir Pavlovich Paley">Vladimir Pavlovich Paley</a> and other relatives and friends.<sup id="cite_ref-serf_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-serf-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His body was eventually buried in the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> cemetery in <a href="/wiki/Beijing,_China" class="mw-redirect" title="Beijing, China">Beijing</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-beijing_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-beijing-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which was destroyed in 1986 and is now a parking lot.
</p>
