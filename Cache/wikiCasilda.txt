<p><b>Casilda</b> is a city in the <a href="/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina" title="Provinces of Argentina">province</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Fe_Province" title="Santa Fe Province">Santa Fe</a>, Argentina. It is the head town of the <a href="/wiki/Caseros_Department" title="Caseros Department">Caseros Department</a>, and lies about 45&#160;km (28&#160;mi) west of <a href="/wiki/Rosario" class="mw-redirect" title="Rosario">Rosario</a> and 202&#160;km south-southwest of the provincial capital <a href="/wiki/Santa_Fe,_Argentina" title="Santa Fe, Argentina">Santa Fe</a>, on <a href="/w/index.php?title=National_Route_33_(Argentina)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="National Route 33 (Argentina) (page does not exist)">National Route 33</a>. It has a population of about 35.058 inhabitants (2010 <a href="/wiki/Census" title="Census">census</a> <small>[<a href="/wiki/National_Institute_of_Statistics_and_Census_of_Argentina" title="National Institute of Statistics and Census of Argentina">INDEC</a>]</small>). Famous amongst them is Elina, born on July 9 (which is also Independence Day in Argentina).
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Casilda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Casilda began as an <a href="/wiki/Agricultural_colonies_in_Argentina" title="Agricultural colonies in Argentina">agricultural colony</a>, created in 1870 by the Spanish merchant and banker <a href="/wiki/Carlos_Casado_del_Alisal" title="Carlos Casado del Alisal">Carlos Casado del Alisal</a>, on land acquired from the ranch <i>Los Desmochados</i>. Casado del Alisal named it Colonia Candelaria (<i>Candelaria</i> was a stop on the way from Rosario to <a href="/wiki/Mendoza,_Argentina" title="Mendoza, Argentina">Mendoza</a>). On 11 November 1873 the colony officially became a town, named Villa Casilda after Casado del Alisal's mother.
</p><p>Casilda produced the first Argentine shipment of <a href="/wiki/Wheat" title="Wheat">wheat</a> to the European market. In 1883 the railway <a href="/wiki/Ferrocarril_Oeste_Santafesino" class="mw-redirect" title="Ferrocarril Oeste Santafesino">Ferrocarril Oeste Santafesino</a> reached the town, prompting an accelerated growth and bringing prosperity. Casilda became a city on 19 November 1907.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Culture">Culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Casilda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The city is home to a Rural Extension Agency of the <a href="/wiki/Instituto_Nacional_de_Tecnolog%C3%ADa_Agropecuaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Instituto Nacional de Tecnología Agropecuaria">National Agricultural Technology Institute</a> (INTA) and the Faculty of Veterinary Sciences of the <a href="/wiki/National_University_of_Rosario" title="National University of Rosario">National University of Rosario</a> (UNR).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Economy">Economy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Casilda&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Economy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The economy of the area is based on agriculture, especially <a href="/wiki/Soybean" title="Soybean">soybean</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wheat" title="Wheat">wheat</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maize" title="Maize">corn</a>; <a href="/wiki/Cattle" title="Cattle">cattle</a> farming has been largely displaced to less fertile lands and <a href="/wiki/Feed_lot" class="mw-redirect" title="Feed lot">feed lots</a> by the advance of these crops. Casilda also has flour <a href="/wiki/Gristmill" title="Gristmill">mills</a>, <a href="/wiki/Fertilizer" title="Fertilizer">fertilizer</a> factories, and many other industries, usually in the form of <a href="/wiki/Small_and_medium_enterprise" class="mw-redirect" title="Small and medium enterprise">small and medium enterprises</a> or <a href="/wiki/Cooperative" title="Cooperative">cooperatives</a>. In addition, Casilda produces <a href="/wiki/Honey" title="Honey">honey</a> and has received the title of Provincial Capital of Honey.
</p>
