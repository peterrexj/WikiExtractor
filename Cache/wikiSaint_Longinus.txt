<p><b>Longinus</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="/ɒ/: &#39;o&#39; in &#39;body&#39;">ɒ</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span><span title="/aɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;tide&#39;">aɪ</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span>) is the name given to the unnamed Roman soldier who pierced the side of <a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a> with a lance and who in medieval and some modern <a href="/wiki/Christian_tradition" title="Christian tradition">Christian traditions</a> is described as a convert to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His name first appeared in the <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a> <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Nicodemus#Naming_of_New_Testament_figures" title="Gospel of Nicodemus">Gospel of Nicodemus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Barber_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Barber-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The lance is called in Christianity the "<a href="/wiki/Holy_Lance" title="Holy Lance">Holy Lance</a>" (<i><a href="/wiki/Lancea_(weapon)" title="Lancea (weapon)">lancea</a></i>) and the story is related in the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">Gospel of John</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Crucifixion_of_Jesus" title="Crucifixion of Jesus">Crucifixion</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This act is said to have created the last of the <a href="/wiki/Five_Holy_Wounds" title="Five Holy Wounds">Five Holy Wounds</a> of Christ.
</p><p>This person, <a href="/wiki/Unnamed_people_in_the_Bible" class="mw-redirect" title="Unnamed people in the Bible">unnamed</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">Gospels</a>, is further identified in some versions of the legend as the <a href="/wiki/Centurion" title="Centurion">centurion</a> present at the <a href="/wiki/Crucifixion" title="Crucifixion">Crucifixion</a>, who said that Jesus was the son of God,<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> so he is considered as one of the first Christians and Roman converts. Longinus' legend grew over the years to the point that he was said to have converted to Christianity after the Crucifixion, and he is traditionally venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and several other Christian communions. 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origins_of_the_story">Origins of the story</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Longinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origins of the story">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>No name for this soldier is given in the canonical Gospels; the name <i>Longinus</i> is instead found in the <a href="/wiki/Apocryphal" class="mw-redirect" title="Apocryphal">apocryphal</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Nicodemus" title="Gospel of Nicodemus">Gospel of Nicodemus</a></i>. Longinus did not start out as a saint. An early tradition, found in a sixth or seventh century pseudepigraphal "<a href="/wiki/Letter_of_Herod_to_Pilate" class="mw-redirect" title="Letter of Herod to Pilate">Letter of Herod to Pilate</a>", claims that Longinus suffered for having pierced Jesus, and that he was condemned to a cave where every night a lion came and mauled him until dawn, after which his body healed back to normal, in a pattern that would repeat until the end of time.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Later traditions turned him into a Christian convert, but as <a href="/wiki/Sabine_Baring-Gould" title="Sabine Baring-Gould">Sabine Baring-Gould</a> observed: "The name of Longinus was not known to the Greeks previous to the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Germanus_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Germanus I of Constantinople">patriarch Germanus</a>, in 715. It was introduced amongst the Westerns from the Apocryphal Gospel of Nicodemus. There is no reliable authority for the Acts and martyrdom of this saint."<sup id="cite_ref-Baring-Gould_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baring-Gould-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The name is probably Latinized from the Greek <i>lonche</i> (λόγχη), the word used for the lance mentioned in <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_John" title="Gospel of John">John</a> <a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/John#19:34" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/John">19:34</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It first appears lettered on an <a href="/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript" title="Illuminated manuscript">illumination</a> of the Crucifixion beside the figure of the soldier holding a spear, written, perhaps contemporaneously, in horizontal Greek letters, <small><i>LOGINOS</i> (ΛΟΓΙΝΟϹ)</small>, in the Syriac <a href="/wiki/Rabula_Gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Rabula Gospels">gospel manuscript illuminated by a certain Rabulas</a> in the year 586, in the <a href="/wiki/Laurentian_Library" title="Laurentian Library">Laurentian Library</a>, Florence. The spear used is known as the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Lance" title="Holy Lance">Holy Lance</a>, and more recently, especially in occult circles, as the "Spear of Destiny", which was revered at <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> by the sixth century, although neither the centurion nor the name "Longinus" were invoked in any surviving report. As the "Lance of Longinus", the spear figures in the legends of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Grail" title="Holy Grail">Holy Grail</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Blindness or other eye problems are not mentioned until after the tenth century.<sup id="cite_ref-Sticca_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sticca-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Petrus_Comestor" title="Petrus Comestor">Petrus Comestor</a> was one of the first to add an eyesight problem to the legend and his text can be translated as "blind", "dim-sighted" or "weak-sighted". The <i><a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a></i> says that he saw celestial signs before conversion and that his eye problems might have been caused by illness or age.<sup id="cite_ref-Webber_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Webber-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The touch of <a href="/wiki/Blood_of_Christ" title="Blood of Christ">Jesus's blood</a> cures his eye problem:
</p>
<blockquote><p>Christian legend has it that Longinus was a blind Roman centurion who thrust the spear into Christ's side at the crucifixion. Some of Jesus's blood fell upon his eyes and he was healed. Upon this miracle Longinus believed in Jesus.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The body of Longinus is said to have been lost twice, and that its second recovery was at <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a> in 1304, together with the Holy Sponge stained with Christ's blood, wherewith it was told—extending Longinus' role—that Longinus had assisted in cleansing Christ's body when it was taken down from the cross. The relic, corpules of alleged blood taken from the Holy Lance, enjoyed a revived cult in late 13th century <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> under the combined impetus of the Grail romances, the local tradition of eucharistic miracles, the chapel consecrated to Longinus, the Holy Blood in the Benedictine monastery church of Sant'Andrea,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and the patronage of the Bonacolsi.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The relics are said to have been divided and then distributed to <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> and elsewhere, with the body taken to the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Agostino,_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Agostino, Rome">Basilica of Sant'Agostino</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. However, official guides of the Basilica do not mention the presence of any tomb associated with Saint Longinus. It is also said that the body of Longinus was found in <a href="/wiki/Sardinia" title="Sardinia">Sardinia</a>; Greek sources assert that he suffered martyrdom in Gabala, Cappadocia.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2014)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Present-day_veneration">Present-day veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Longinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Present-day veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg/170px-LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="260" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg/255px-LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/20/LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg 2x" data-file-width="294" data-file-height="450" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:LonginusFyodorZubov.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Longinus the Centurion. <a href="/wiki/Russian_icon" class="mw-redirect" title="Russian icon">Russian icon</a> by <a href="/wiki/Fyodor_Zubov" title="Fyodor Zubov">Fyodor Zubov</a>, 1680.</div></div></div>
<p>Longinus is venerated, generally as a martyr, in the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, and the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church" title="Armenian Apostolic Church">Armenian Apostolic Church</a>. His feast day is kept on 16 October in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, which mentions him, without any indication of martyrdom, in the following terms: "At Jerusalem, commemoration of Saint Longinus, who is venerated as the soldier opening the side of the crucified Lord with a lance".<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The pre-1969 feast day in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> is 15 March.  The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> commemorates him on 16 October. In the Armenian Apostolic Church, his feast is commemorated on 22 October.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The statue of <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Longinus_(Bernini)" title="Saint Longinus (Bernini)">Saint Longinus</a></i>, sculpted by Gian Lorenzo Bernini, is one of four in the niches beneath the dome of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a>, <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>. A spearpoint fragment said to be from the Holy Lance is also conserved in the Basilica.
</p><p>Longinus and his legend are the subject of the <a href="/wiki/Moriones_Festival" title="Moriones Festival">Moriones Festival</a> held during <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week_in_the_Philippines" title="Holy Week in the Philippines">Holy Week</a> on the island of <a href="/wiki/Marinduque" title="Marinduque">Marinduque</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Brazil">Brazil</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Longinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Brazil">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Folkloric_role">Folkloric role</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Longinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Folkloric role">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Under the folk name "São Longuinho", Saint Longinus is attributed the power of finding missing objects. The saint's aid is summoned by the chant:
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><div class="poem">
<p>São Longuinho, São Longuinho, se eu achar [missing object], dou três pulinhos!<br />
(São Longuinho, São Longuinho, if I find [missing object], I'll hop three times!)
</p>
</div></blockquote>
<p>Folk tradition explains the association with missing objects with a tale from the saint's days in Rome. It is said he was of short stature and, as such, had unimpeded view of the underside of tables in crowded parties. Due to this, he would find and return objects dropped on the ground by the other attendants.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Accounts vary regarding the promised offering of three hops, citing either deference to an alleged limping of the saint or a plea to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Trinity">Holy Trinity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Brazilian_spiritism">Brazilian spiritism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Longinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Brazilian spiritism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Brazilian <a href="/wiki/Mediumship" title="Mediumship">medium</a> <a href="/wiki/Chico_Xavier" title="Chico Xavier">Chico Xavier</a> wrote <i>Brasil, Coração do Mundo, Pátria do Evangelho</i>, a <a href="/wiki/Psychography" class="mw-redirect" title="Psychography">psychographic book</a> of authorship attributed to the spirit of <a href="/wiki/Humberto_de_Campos_(journalist)" title="Humberto de Campos (journalist)">Humberto de Campos</a>. In the book, Saint Longinus is claimed to have been reincarnated as <a href="/wiki/Pedro_II_of_Brazil" title="Pedro II of Brazil">Pedro II</a>, the last Brazilian emperor.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<li>In the historical fiction series <i>End of the Line</i> (2019), Gaius Cornelius Longinus acts as a near immortal caretaker for the House of David, and is called upon by the Allied Powers to assist in artifact and relic recovery during the Second World War.</li>
<li>In the <i><a href="/wiki/Vampire:_The_Requiem" title="Vampire: The Requiem">Vampire: The Requiem</a></i> roleplaying books, Longinus is a mythical character who was cursed with vampirism when Jesus died. His story, called the <i>Testament of Longinus</i>, serves as the basis for a sect of modern Christian vampires called the Lancea et Sanctum.</li>
<li>In the historical fiction series <i><a href="/wiki/Britannia_(TV_series)" title="Britannia (TV series)">Britannia</a></i>, during a flashback to earlier times, a young <a href="/wiki/Aulus_Plautius" title="Aulus Plautius">Aulus Plautius</a> orders his second in command Perfectus to lance Jesus on the cross. Reluctantly he commits the act, reflecting the conflict Longinus may have felt considering his later conversion.</li>
<li>In the Japanese anime <i><a href="/wiki/Neon_Genesis_Evangelion" title="Neon Genesis Evangelion">Neon Genesis Evangelion</a></i>, the Lance of Longinus is a spear found in the abdomen of the Second Angel, Lilith.</li>
<li>In the <a href="/wiki/Beyblade_Burst" title="Beyblade Burst">Beyblade Burst</a> series, characters Lui Shirosagi (1° Season–Turbo, Surge–QuadDrive) and Lodin Hajima (Rise) own Longinus beys, represented by a white dragon. In the dub and Hasbro releases however, the bey had its name changed to Luinor.</li>
<li>The James Patterson/Andrew Cross novel The Jester has the main character coming home from The Crusades in 11th century France to find his family killed and his village ravaged by a team of knights, who are looking for a priceless artifact. Unbeknownst to him, that artifact has been hidden in a staff he has brought home.</li>
<li>In the Japanese light novel <i>Fate/Requiem</i> (2018-ongoing), Lucius Longinus is the Lancer-class Servant of Chitose Manazuru who won the Holy Grail War predating the events of the novel, and appears as a supporting character.</li></ul>
