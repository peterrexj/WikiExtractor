<p><b>Saint Veronica</b>, also known as <b>Berenike</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> was a widow from <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> who lived in the 1st century <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>, according to extra-biblical Christian <a href="/wiki/Sacred_tradition" title="Sacred tradition">sacred tradition</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Trinity_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trinity-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> A celebrated saint in many pious <a href="/wiki/Christianity_by_country" title="Christianity by country">Christian countries</a>, the 17th-century <i><a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a></i> published by the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandists</a> listed her feast under July 12,<sup id="cite_ref-Harper2001_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Harper2001-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> but the German <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> scholar Joseph Braun cited her commemoration in <i>Festi Marianni</i> on 13 January.
</p>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG/260px-0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG" decoding="async" width="260" height="138" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG/390px-0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG/520px-0_Ste_V%C3%A9ronique_et_les_saintes_Femmes_-_H%C3%B4tel-Dieu_%C3%A0_Cluny_-_Mac%27s.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4287" data-file-height="2271" /></a><figcaption>Saint Veronica and the Holy Women, Grégoire Guérard, c.1530</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to Church tradition, Veronica was moved with sympathy seeing <a href="/wiki/Christ_Carrying_the_Cross" title="Christ Carrying the Cross">Jesus carrying the cross to Calvary</a> and gave him her veil so that he could wipe his forehead. Jesus accepted the offer, and when he returned the veil the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Face_of_Jesus" title="Holy Face of Jesus">image of his face</a> was miraculously captured on it. The resulting relic  became known as the <a href="/wiki/Veil_of_Veronica" title="Veil of Veronica">Veil of Veronica</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-N&amp;Q_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-N&amp;Q-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AI_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AI-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Butler2000p=84_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler2000p=84-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The story of Veronica is celebrated in the sixth <a href="/wiki/Stations_of_the_Cross" title="Stations of the Cross">Station of the Cross</a> in <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Western_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Orthodox">Western Orthodox</a> churches.<sup id="cite_ref-Trinity_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Trinity-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Prothero2009p=284_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prothero2009p=284-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Veronica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_veronica.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Saint_veronica.jpg/170px-Saint_veronica.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Saint_veronica.jpg/255px-Saint_veronica.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Saint_veronica.jpg/340px-Saint_veronica.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1704" data-file-height="2272" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Veronica by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Mochi" title="Francesco Mochi">Francesco Mochi</a> in a niche of the pier supporting the main dome of <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cloth_of_St._Veronica,_Bernardino_Zaganelli,_c._1500,_oil_on_panel,_Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/27/Cloth_of_St._Veronica%2C_Bernardino_Zaganelli%2C_c._1500%2C_oil_on_panel%2C_Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art.jpg" decoding="async" width="191" height="253" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="191" data-file-height="253" /></a><figcaption><i>Cloth of Saint Veronica</i>, <a href="/wiki/Bernardino_Zaganelli" title="Bernardino Zaganelli">Bernardino Zaganelli</a>, c. 1500, oil on panel, <a href="/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art" title="Philadelphia Museum of Art">Philadelphia Museum of Art</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg/220px-AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="161" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg/330px-AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg/440px-AGAD_Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_%E2%80%93_The_Sudarium.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4827" data-file-height="3543" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer" title="Albrecht Dürer">Albrecht Dürer</a>'s 1513 <i>Veronica</i></figcaption></figure>
<p>There is no reference to the story of Veronica and her veil in the <a href="/wiki/Canonical_gospels" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonical gospels">canonical gospels</a>. The closest is <a href="/wiki/Jesus_healing_the_bleeding_woman" title="Jesus healing the bleeding woman">the miracle of the unnamed woman who was healed</a> by touching the hem of Jesus’s garment (<a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Luke%208:43–48&amp;version=nrsv">Luke 8:43–48</a>). The <a href="/wiki/Apocrypha" title="Apocrypha">apocryphal</a> <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_Nicodemus" title="Gospel of Nicodemus">Gospel of Nicodemus</a> gives her name as Berenikē or Beronike (<a href="/wiki/Koin%C4%93_Greek_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Koinē Greek language">Koinē Greek</a>: <span lang="grc">Βερενίκη</span>). The name Veronica is a Latinisation of this <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Macedonian_language" title="Ancient Macedonian language">ancient Macedonian</a> name. The story was later elaborated in the 11th century by adding that Christ gave her a portrait of himself on a cloth, with which she later cured the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Tiberius" title="Tiberius">Tiberius</a>. The linking of this with the bearing of the cross in the Passion occurs only around 1380 in the internationally popular book <i><a href="/wiki/Meditations_on_the_Life_of_Christ" title="Meditations on the Life of Christ">Meditations on the Life of Christ</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wilson1991p=175_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wilson1991p=175-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At some point a relic became associated with the story. <a href="/wiki/Pedro_Tafur" title="Pedro Tafur">Pedro Tafur</a>, a Spanish knight visiting Rome in 1436, describes the following in the Church of St. Peter in his 1454 travel account:<sup id="cite_ref-Letts1926_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Letts1926-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote">
<p>On the right hand is a pillar as high as a small tower, and in it is the holy Veronica. When it is to be exhibited an opening is made in the roof of the church and a wooden chest or cradle is let down, in which are two clerics, and when they have descended, the chest or cradle is drawn up, and they, with the greatest reverence, take out the Veronica and show it to the people, who make concourse there upon the appointed day. It happens often that the worshipers are in danger of their lives, so many are they and so great is the press.
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>However, he does not say specifically that he witnessed for himself this exhibition of the relic.
</p><p>Some academic sources suggest a different origin for the legend of St. Veronica: that the cloth bearing an image of Jesus's face was known in Latin as the <i>vera icon</i> ("true image"), and that this name for the relic was misinterpreted as the name of a saint. The <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> of 1913 writes:<sup id="cite_ref-Dégert1913_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dégert1913-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote">
<p>The belief in the existence of authentic images of Christ is connected with the old legend of King <a href="/wiki/Abgar_V" title="Abgar V">Abgar of Edessa</a> and the apocryphal writing known as the <i><a href="/wiki/Mors_Pilati" class="mw-redirect" title="Mors Pilati">Mors Pilati</a>&#8202;</i><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Special:EntityPage/Q3863244#sitelinks-wikipedia" class="extiw" title="d:Special:EntityPage/Q3863244"><span title="&quot;Mors Pilati&quot; in other languages">Wikidata</span></a>&#93;</span> (<i>"the Death of <a href="/wiki/Pontius_Pilate" title="Pontius Pilate">Pilate</a>"</i>). To distinguish at Rome the oldest and best known of these images it was called the <i>vera icon</i> (true image), which in the common tongue soon became <i>"Veronica".</i>
It is thus designated in several <a href="/wiki/Medieval" class="mw-redirect" title="Medieval">medieval</a> texts mentioned by the <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandists</a> (e.g. an old Missal of Augsburg has a Mass <i>"De S. Veronica seu Vultus Domini"</i> – <i>"Saint Veronica, or the Face of the Lord"</i>), and <a href="/wiki/Matthew_of_Westminster" title="Matthew of Westminster">Matthew of Westminster</a> speaks of the imprint of the image of the Savior which is called Veronica: <i>"Effigies Domenici vultus quae Veronica nuncupatur"</i> – <i>"effigy of the face of the Lord which is called a Veronica"</i>. By degrees, popular imagination mistook this word for the name of a person and attached thereto several legends which vary according to the country. [<i>translations in italics added</i>]
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>The reference to Abgar is related to a similar legend in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">Eastern Church</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Image_of_Edessa" title="Image of Edessa">Image of Edessa</a> or Mandylion.
</p><p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica" title="Encyclopædia Britannica">Encyclopædia Britannica</a></i> says this about the legend:<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote">
<p>Eusebius in his <i><a href="/wiki/Church_History_(Eusebius)" title="Church History (Eusebius)">Historia Ecclesiastica</a></i> (vii 18) tells how at <a href="/wiki/Caesarea_Philippi" class="mw-redirect" title="Caesarea Philippi">Caesarea Philippi</a> lived the <a href="/wiki/Jesus_healing_the_bleeding_woman" title="Jesus healing the bleeding woman">woman whom Christ healed of an issue of blood</a> (<a href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Matthew#Chapter_9" class="extiw" title="s:Bible (King James)/Matthew">Matthew 9</a>:20–22). Legend was not long in providing the woman of the Gospel with a name. In the West she was identified with <a href="/wiki/Martha" title="Martha">Martha of Bethany</a>; in the East she was called Berenike, or Beronike, the name appearing in as early a work as the <i>"<a href="/wiki/Acta_Pilati" class="mw-redirect" title="Acta Pilati">Acta Pilati</a>",</i> the most ancient form of which goes back to the fourth century. The fanciful derivation of the name Veronica from the words Vera Icon (eikon) "true image" dates back to the <i>"Otia Imperialia"</i> (iii 25) of <a href="/wiki/Gervase_of_Tilbury" title="Gervase of Tilbury">Gervase of Tilbury</a> (<a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl.</a> 1211), who says: <i>"Est ergo Veronica pictura Domini vera"</i> (translated: "The Veronica is, therefore, a true picture of the Lord.")
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Veronica was mentioned in the reported <a href="/wiki/Visions_of_Jesus_and_Mary" title="Visions of Jesus and Mary">visions of Jesus</a> by <a href="/wiki/Marie_of_St_Peter" class="mw-redirect" title="Marie of St Peter">Marie of St Peter</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Carmelites" title="Carmelites">Carmelite nun</a> who lived in <a href="/wiki/Tours" title="Tours">Tours</a>, France, and started the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_devotions" title="Catholic devotions">devotion</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Face_of_Jesus" title="Holy Face of Jesus">Holy Face of Jesus</a>. In 1844, Sister Marie reported that in a vision, she saw Veronica wiping away the spit and mud from the face of Jesus with her veil on the way to Calvary. She said that sacrilegious and blasphemous acts today are adding to the spit and mud that Veronica wiped away that day. According to Marie of St Peter, in her visions, Jesus told her that he desired devotion to His Holy Face in <a href="/wiki/Reparation_(legal)" title="Reparation (legal)">reparation</a> for <a href="/wiki/Sacrilege" title="Sacrilege">sacrilege</a> and <a href="/wiki/Blasphemy" title="Blasphemy">blasphemy</a>. <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Reparation_to_Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Acts of Reparation to Jesus Christ">Acts of Reparation to Jesus Christ</a> are thus compared to Veronica wiping the face of Jesus.<sup id="cite_ref-Cruz2003p=_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cruz2003p=-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ScallanScallan1994p=_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ScallanScallan1994p=-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Face_of_Jesus" title="Holy Face of Jesus">Holy Face of Jesus</a> was eventually approved by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> in 1885. Veronica is commemorated on 12 July.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Official_patronage">Official patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Veronica&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Official patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Veronica is the patron of the French <a href="/wiki/Mulquinerie" title="Mulquinerie">mulquiniers</a> whose representations they celebrated semi-annually (<a href="/wiki/Summer" title="Summer">summer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winter" title="Winter">winter</a>) as in many pious <a href="/wiki/Christianity_by_country" title="Christianity by country">Christian countries</a>. She is also the patron saint of photographers, and laundry workers.
</p>
