<p><b>Sabbas the Goth</b> (<a href="/wiki/Romanian_language" title="Romanian language">Romanian</a>: <i lang="ro">Sava Gotul</i>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Σάββας ο Γότθος</span>; died 12 April 372) is a <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyr" title="Christian martyr">Christian martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_persecution">Life and persecution</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabbas_the_Goth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and persecution">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sabbas (also Saba) was born in 334<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> in a village in the <a href="/wiki/Buz%C4%83u_(river)" title="Buzău (river)">Buzău river valley</a> and lived in what is now the <a href="/wiki/Wallachia" title="Wallachia">Wallachia</a> region in <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> as a youth.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> states that he was a <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goth</a> by race and may have been a cantor or a reader to the religious community there.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In <i>circa</i> 369 the <a href="/wiki/Thervings" class="mw-redirect" title="Thervings">Tervingi</a> king <a href="/wiki/Athanaric" title="Athanaric">Athanaric</a> began a <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Persecution of Christianity">persecution of the Christians</a> in his territory.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> First, a Gothic nobleman began the suppression of Christianity in Sabbas' area. When his agents came to the village where Sabbas lived they forced the villagers to eat pagan sacrificial meat. According to the tale, non-Christian villagers wanting to help their Christian neighbours tricked the authorities by exchanging the sacrificial meat for meat that had not been sacrificed. However, Sabbas made a conspicuous show of rejecting the meat altogether. His fellow villagers exiled him but after a while, he was allowed to return.
</p><p>Sometime after, the Gothic noble returned and asked if there were any Christians in the village. Sabbas stepped forward and proclaimed, "Let no-one swear an oath on my behalf. I am a Christian." Sabbas' neighbours then said that he was a poor man of no account. The leader dismissed him, saying, "This one can do us neither good nor harm."
</p><p>In the year 372, Sabbas celebrated Easter with the priest Sansalas. Three days after Easter <a href="/wiki/Atharid" title="Atharid">Atharid</a>, the son of Athanaric's sub-king <a href="/wiki/Rothesteus" title="Rothesteus">Rothesteus</a>, arrived in the village to arrest Sansalas. Saba was dragged naked through thorn bushes, then racked, alongside the priest Sansalas, to a wagon wheel, and whipped. The next day he was offered pagan sacrificed meat again. He was, however, still steadfast, and suggested they tell Atharid to kill him. Sabbas also so angered one of Atharid's retinue by insulting the prince that he hurled a pestle as if it were a javelin at Sabbas so hard that those nearby were sure he was dead, but it left no mark.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom_and_translation_of_relics">Martyrdom and translation of relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabbas_the_Goth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Martyrdom and translation of relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Gothic prince Atharid sentenced Sabbas to death, ordering him to be thrown in the river Musæus, a tributary of the Danube. As he went with the soldiers he praised <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> the whole way, denouncing the pagan and idolatrous ways of his captors. The soldiers, considering him a fool or insane, contemplated just letting him go, reasoning that the prince would never find out. Sabbas urged them to do their duty, proclaiming "Why do you waste time talking nonsense and not do what you were told to? For I see what you cannot see: over there on the other side, standing in glory, the saints who have come to receive me". At this the soldiers pushed him under the river with a branch against his neck and drowned him.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was martyred during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Valentinian_I" title="Valentinian I">Valentinian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Valens" title="Valens">Valens</a>, in the consulship of <a href="/wiki/Domitius_Modestus" title="Domitius Modestus">Modestus</a> and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Flavius_Arintheus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Flavius Arintheus (page does not exist)">Arintheus</a>, i.e. 372.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His remains were taken and hidden by the Christians until they could be sent for safe keeping to the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Here they were received by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Ascholius" title="Ascholius">Ascholius</a> of <a href="/wiki/Thessalonica" class="mw-redirect" title="Thessalonica">Thessalonica</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Basil_of_Caesarea" title="Basil of Caesarea">Basil of Caesarea</a> requested that the military commander<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>  of <a href="/wiki/Scythia_Minor_(Roman_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Scythia Minor (Roman province)">Scythia Minor</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Junius_Soranus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Junius Soranus (page does not exist)">Junius Soranus</a>, send him the relics of saints and the Dacian priests sent the relics of Sabbas to him in Caesarea, <a href="/wiki/Cappadocia" title="Cappadocia">Cappadocia</a>, in 373 or 374 accompanied by a letter, the 'Epistle of the Church of God in Gothia to the Church of God located in Cappadocia and to all the Local Churches of the Holy Universal Church'. This letter was written in Greek, possibly by St <a href="/wiki/Bretannio" title="Bretannio">Bretannio</a> of <a href="/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba" title="Constanța">Tomis</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Significance">Significance</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sabbas_the_Goth&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Significance">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In response, Basil replied with two letters to Bishop Ascholius where he extolled the virtues of Sabbas, calling him an 'athlete of Christ' and a 'martyr for the Truth'.
</p><p>Sabbas' feast day is on the date of his martyrdom, 12 April in the Roman Martyrology and 15 April in the Orthodox Church. The Orthodox Church commemorates him as "the holy, glorious, and right-victorious Great-martyr Sabbas."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The value of the <i>Passion</i> for historians lies in the unique insight it gives into Gothic village life and social structure as well as the information that can be inferred about Gothic government on all levels.
</p>
