<p><b>Eadnoth the Younger</b> or <b>Eadnoth I</b> was a medieval <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a>, successively <a href="/wiki/Abbot_of_Ramsey" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbot of Ramsey">Abbot of Ramsey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Mercian_Bishop_of_Dorchester" class="mw-redirect" title="Mercian Bishop of Dorchester">Bishop of Dorchester</a>. From a prominent family of priests in <a href="/wiki/The_Fens" title="The Fens">the Fens</a>, he was related to <a href="/wiki/Oswald_of_Worcester" title="Oswald of Worcester">Oswald</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a>, <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a> and founder of <a href="/wiki/Ramsey_Abbey" title="Ramsey Abbey">Ramsey Abbey</a>. Following in the footsteps of his illustrious kinsman, he initially became a monk at <a href="/wiki/Worcester,_England" title="Worcester, England">Worcester</a>. He is found at Ramsey supervising construction works in the 980s, and around 992 actually became Abbot of Ramsey. As abbot, he founded two daughter houses in what is now <a href="/wiki/Cambridgeshire" title="Cambridgeshire">Cambridgeshire</a>, namely, a monastery at <a href="/wiki/St_Ives,_Cambridgeshire" title="St Ives, Cambridgeshire">St Ives</a> and a nunnery at <a href="/wiki/Chatteris" title="Chatteris">Chatteris</a>. At some point between 1007 and 1009, he became Bishop of Dorchester, a <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_see" title="Episcopal see">see</a> that encompassed much of the eastern <a href="/wiki/Danelaw" title="Danelaw">Danelaw</a>. He died at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Assandun" title="Battle of Assandun">Battle of Assandun</a> in 1016, fighting <a href="/wiki/Cnut_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Cnut the Great">Cnut the Great</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eadnoth_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Eadnoth the Younger was the son of <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelstan_Mannessune" title="Æthelstan Mannessune">Æthelstan Mannessune</a> by a kinswoman of Oswald, Bishop of Worcester and Archbishop of York.<sup id="cite_ref-Wareham-49-50_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wareham-49-50-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  His father came from family of hereditary Fenland priests from in or around the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Ely" title="Isle of Ely">Isle of Ely</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Wareham-49-50_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wareham-49-50-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Æthelstan had lands in <a href="/wiki/Cambridgeshire" title="Cambridgeshire">Cambridgeshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/Huntingdonshire" title="Huntingdonshire">Huntingdonshire</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bedfordshire" title="Bedfordshire">Bedfordshire</a>, with "outlying" [Hart] estates in <a href="/wiki/Norfolk" title="Norfolk">Norfolk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lincolnshire" title="Lincolnshire">Lincolnshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Hart-215_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hart-215-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Eadnoth is styled "the Younger", <i>Iunior</i>, to distinguish him from Eadnoth "the Elder", <i>Senior</i>, the follower of Oswald who served as <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ramsey_Abbey" title="Ramsey Abbey">monastery of Ramsey</a> in the years before Eadnoth the Younger became abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eadnoth the Younger had one brother, Godric (died 1013), and at least two sisters, <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfwaru" title="Ælfwaru">Ælfwaru</a> (died 1007) and Ælfwyn, all of whom inherited estates  (in addition to a <a href="/wiki/Fishery" title="Fishery">fishery</a>) from their father.<sup id="cite_ref-Wareham-49-51_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wareham-49-51-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Eadnoth, by contrast, became a monk at <a href="/wiki/Worcester_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Worcester Abbey">Worcester Abbey</a>, where his mother's kinsman Oswald was bishop, and thus could not inherit anything.<sup id="cite_ref-Wareham-51_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wareham-51-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eadnoth appears for the first time in the 980s when, according to the <i>Liber Benefactorum Ecclesiae Ramesiensis</i>, he supervised the repair of the western tower of Ramsey Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Eadnoth became Abbot of Ramsey in 992,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> having probably already taken over Eadnoth the Elder's duties as prior from at least 991, if not earlier.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Ramsey">Abbot of Ramsey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eadnoth_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Abbot of Ramsey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Liber Benefactorum</i> calls Eadnoth the Younger the "first abbot of Ramsey".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It says that he was elected according to the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Rule">Benedictine Rule</a> by the monks of Ramsey, after <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelwine,_Ealdorman_of_East_Anglia" title="Æthelwine, Ealdorman of East Anglia">Ealdorman Æthelwine</a> had given Germanus enough money to found a new monastery at <a href="/wiki/Cholsey" title="Cholsey">Cholsey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Ramsey had had two communities of monks, those who had been moved by Oswald there from <a href="/wiki/Westbury_on_Trym,_Bristol" class="mw-redirect" title="Westbury on Trym, Bristol">Westbury on Trym</a> in the 960s, and those who had fled there in the 980s from <a href="/wiki/Winchcombe" title="Winchcombe">Winchcombe</a> because of the anti-monastic reaction in Mercia; until 992, Oswald, who died in 992, had been titular abbot of the former with Eadnoth the Elder as his prior, while the Winchcombe monks had Germanus as their abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to historian <a href="/wiki/Cyril_Roy_Hart" title="Cyril Roy Hart">Cyril Hart</a>, "there is no shred of doubt" that Eadnoth the Younger obtained this office through the influence of Oswald.<sup id="cite_ref-Hart,_Eadnoth,_p._621_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hart,_Eadnoth,_p._621-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  Although such <a href="/wiki/Nepotism" title="Nepotism">nepotism</a> contradicted the usual spirit of the Benedictine revival in England at the time, Oswald himself had similarly advanced because of family connections.<sup id="cite_ref-Hart,_Eadnoth,_p._621_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hart,_Eadnoth,_p._621-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> As abbot Eadnoth founded a nunnery on his family lands at Chatteris, and his younger sister Ælfwyn became its first <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 1007, the Chatteris nunnery received the lands of <a href="/wiki/Over,_Cambridgeshire" title="Over, Cambridgeshire">Over</a> and <a href="/wiki/Barley,_Hertfordshire" title="Barley, Hertfordshire">Barley</a>, following the death of their sister Ælfwaru.<sup id="cite_ref-Wareham-51-52_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wareham-51-52-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eadnoth also founded a monastery at St Ives, Cambridgeshire. Established as a daughter-house of Ramsey (like Chaterris), the monastery's entire endowment consisted of <i>Slepe</i> (what became St Ives) as well as part of <a href="/wiki/Elsworth" title="Elsworth">Elsworth</a> and Knapworth.<sup id="cite_ref-Hart-617-18_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hart-617-18-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> All of these lands (including their churches) had been the property of Eadnoth's father Æthelstan.<sup id="cite_ref-Hart-617-18_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hart-617-18-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Elsworth had been left to Ramsey in the will of Eadnoth's sister Ælfwaru.<sup id="cite_ref-Hart-617-18_15-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hart-617-18-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> On 24 April 1002, soon after founding St Ives, he translated its <a href="/wiki/Ivo_of_Ramsey" title="Ivo of Ramsey">eponymous saintly resident</a>—discovered by a ploughing peasant a year before—to Ramsey Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Dorchester">Bishop of Dorchester</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eadnoth_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bishop of Dorchester">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At some point between 1007 and 1009, Eadnoth became <a href="/wiki/Mercian_Bishop_of_Dorchester" class="mw-redirect" title="Mercian Bishop of Dorchester">Bishop of Dorchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Mercian_Diocese_of_Dorchester" class="mw-redirect" title="Mercian Diocese of Dorchester">Dorchester</a> was a sprawling diocese based far to the south of most of its territory in the eastern Danelaw, at <a href="/wiki/Dorchester_on_Thames" title="Dorchester on Thames">Dorchester on Thames</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> It was roughly equivalent to the pre-Viking east Mercian <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Leicester" title="Diocese of Leicester">diocese of Leicester</a>, and after Eadnoth's episcopate came to include the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Lindsey" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Lindsey">Diocese of Lindsey</a> too.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> In the Norman era the bishopric's seat returned north and became the well-known <a href="/wiki/Bishopric_of_Lincoln" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishopric of Lincoln">bishopric of Lincoln</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Little is known of Eadnoth's episcopate. His first appearance as bishop is as a witness to a charter of 1009, issued by <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelred_the_Unready" title="Æthelred the Unready">King Æthelred</a> to a thegn named Morcar; the last notice of Eadnoth's predecessor as bishop, Ælfhelm, occurs in a similar document of 1007.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Eadnoth subsequently subscribes at least another eight royal charters before his death, all between 1011 and 1013, with a possible further subscription in 1016.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> On 18 October 1016, Bishop Eadnoth fought and was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Assandun" title="Battle of Assandun">Battle of Assandun</a> in <a href="/wiki/Essex" title="Essex">Essex</a>, alongside Wulfsifge, his successor as abbot of Ramsey, and Æthelweard son of <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelwine,_Ealdorman_of_East_Anglia" title="Æthelwine, Ealdorman of East Anglia">Ealdorman Æthelwine</a> [of East Anglia].<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> He was fighting on behalf of <a href="/wiki/Edmund_Ironside" title="Edmund Ironside">Edmund Ironside</a> against <a href="/wiki/Cnut_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Cnut the Great">Cnut</a>, the Danish invader who was claiming the English crown.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eadnoth's body was taken north into the Fenlands, heading back to Ramsey. According to the <i><a href="/wiki/Liber_Eliensis" title="Liber Eliensis">Liber Eliensis</a></i>, the guards of the body stopped at <a href="/wiki/Ely_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Ely Abbey">Ely Abbey</a> and got drunk, during which the Ely monks seized and hid the body.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-Fairweather_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-Fairweather-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The plot was led by <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lfgar_of_Elmham" title="Ælfgar of Elmham">Ælfgar</a>, formerly <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Elmham" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Elmham">Bishop of Elmham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-Fairweather_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-Fairweather-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Thereafter the body remained at Ely, where Eadnoth the Martyr was venerated as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-Fairweather_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-Fairweather-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is thought that Abbot Eadnoth is the abbot depicted alongside a bishop (his kinsman Bishop Oswald) in one of the <a href="/wiki/Miniature_(illuminated_manuscript)" title="Miniature (illuminated manuscript)">miniatures</a> in the 14th-century Ramsey Psalter (not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Ramsey_Psalter" title="Ramsey Psalter">British Museum MS Harley 2904</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> Below Bishop Oswald is a ram, after the first element of the place-name Ramsey, and below Eadnoth a bull, in reference to the foundation legend of the abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
