<p><b>Benedict of Aniane</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Benedictus Anianensis</i>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Benedikt von Aniane</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 747 &#8211; 12 February 821 AD), born <b>Witiza</b> and called the <b>Second Benedict</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monk" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monk">Benedictine monk</a> and monastic reformer, who left a large imprint on the religious practice of the <a href="/wiki/Carolingian_Empire" title="Carolingian Empire">Carolingian Empire</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is either February 11 or 12, depending on <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgical calendar">liturgical calendar</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Benedict_of_Aniane&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Ardo_Smaragdus" title="Ardo Smaragdus">Ardo</a>, Benedict's biographer, he was the son of a <a href="/wiki/Visigoth" class="mw-redirect" title="Visigoth">Visigoth</a>, <b>Aigulf</b>, <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Maguelonne" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Maguelonne">Count of Maguelonne</a> (<i>Magalonensis comes</i>). Originally given the <a href="/wiki/Gothic_language" title="Gothic language">Gothic</a> name <b>Witiza</b>, he was educated at the Frankish court of <a href="/wiki/Pippin_the_Younger" class="mw-redirect" title="Pippin the Younger">Pippin the Younger</a>, and entered the royal service as a page.  He served at the court of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a>, and took part in the <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italian</a> campaign of <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> in 773 where he almost drowned in the <a href="/wiki/Ticino_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Ticino River">Ticino</a> near <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a> while attempting to save his brother. The experience led him to act on a resolve which had been slowly forming in him, to renounce the world and live the monastic life. He later left the court and was received into the monastery of Saint Sequanus, the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Seine" title="Abbey of Saint-Seine">Abbey of Saint-Seine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At Saint-Seine, Benedict was made cellarer, and then elected abbot, but realizing the monks would never conform to his strict practices he left and returned to his father's estates in Languedoc, where he built a hermitage.<sup id="cite_ref-Cistercian_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cistercian-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Around 780, he founded a monastic community based on Eastern asceticism at <a href="/wiki/Aniane" title="Aniane">Aniane</a> in Languedoc.  This community did not develop as he had intended. In 782, he founded another monastery based on <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Rule">Benedictine Rule</a>, at the same location. His success there gave him considerable influence, which he used to found and reform a number of other monasteries, and eventually becoming the effective abbot of all the monasteries of Charlemagne's empire.<sup id="cite_ref-Holweck_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Holweck-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 781 <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> became King of Aquitaine and asked Benedict to reform the monasteries in his territory. Later as Emperor, he  entrusted him with the coordination of practices and communication among the monasteries within his domains.<sup id="cite_ref-Cistercian_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cistercian-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He had a wide knowledge of patristic literature, and churchmen, such as <a href="/wiki/Alcuin" title="Alcuin">Alcuin</a> sought his counsel.
</p><p>In 814, Louis, now emperor, had Benedict found a monastery on the river Inde near the court at Aachen. The monastery was at first called the "Monastery of the Redeemer on the Inde", but came to be known as <a href="/wiki/Kornelim%C3%BCnster_Abbey" title="Kornelimünster Abbey">Kornelimünster Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was the head of a <a href="/wiki/Synods_of_Aachen_(816%E2%80%93819)" title="Synods of Aachen (816–819)">council of abbots</a> which in 817 at <a href="/wiki/Aachen" title="Aachen">Aachen</a> created a code of regulations, or "Codex regularum", which would be binding on all their houses.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Benedict sought to restore the primitive strictness of the monastic observance wherever it had been relaxed or exchanged for the less exacting canonical life. Shortly thereafter, he compiled a "Concordia regularum". Sections of the Benedictine rule (except ix-xvi) are given in their order, with parallel passages from the other rules included in the Liber regularum, so as to show the agreement of principles and thus to enhance the respect due to the Benedictine. He was primarily an ecclesiastic, who zealously placed his considerable theological learning at the service of orthodoxy and the cause of Benedictine monasticism.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Although these new codes fell into disuse shortly after the deaths of Benedict and his <a href="/wiki/Patron" class="mw-redirect" title="Patron">patron</a>, Emperor <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a>, they did have lasting effects on Western monasticism.
</p><p>Benedict died at <a href="/wiki/Kornelim%C3%BCnster_Abbey" title="Kornelimünster Abbey">Kornelimünster Abbey</a> on February 11, 821, in the monastery Louis had built for him to serve as the base for Benedict's supervisory work. He was buried the next day on February 12, hence why some list his feast day as the 11th<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and some the 12th.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
