<p><b>Finnian of Clonard</b> ('Cluain Eraird') – also <b>Finian</b>, <b>Fionán</b> or <b>Fionnán</b> in Irish; or <b>Finianus</b> and <b>Finanus</b> in its Latinised form<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (470–549) – was one of the early <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Irish monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a>, who founded <a href="/wiki/Clonard_Abbey" title="Clonard Abbey">Clonard Abbey</a> in modern-day <a href="/wiki/County_Meath" title="County Meath">County Meath</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Twelve_Apostles_of_Ireland" title="Twelve Apostles of Ireland">Twelve Apostles of Ireland</a> studied under him. Finnian of Clonard (along with <a href="/wiki/Enda_of_Aran" title="Enda of Aran">Enda of Aran</a>) is considered one of the fathers of Irish <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monasticism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Finnian_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Finnian was born at the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Leinster" title="Kingdom of Leinster">Kingdom of Leinster</a>, son of Findlog. His birthplace is generally supposed to have been near the present town of New Ross. He was a member of <a href="/wiki/Clanna_Rudhraighe" class="mw-redirect" title="Clanna Rudhraighe">Clanna Rudhraighe</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Ulaid" title="Ulaid">Ulaid</a>. <a href="/wiki/Abban" class="mw-redirect" title="Abban">Abban</a> baptised Finnian, and at an early age, he was placed under the care of Bishop Fortchern of Trim.<sup id="cite_ref-ier_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ier-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to some sources, Finnian studied for a time at the monastic centre of <a href="/wiki/Martin_of_Tours" title="Martin of Tours">Martin of Tours</a> in Gaul. Tours was noted for its austerity. He later went to <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a> and continued his studies at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Cadoc" title="Cadoc">Cadoc</a> the Wise, at <a href="/wiki/Llancarfan" title="Llancarfan">Llancarfan</a> (whose place-name translates as 'the <a href="/wiki/Llan_(placename)" title="Llan (placename)">Llan</a> of the stags') in <a href="/wiki/Glamorgan" title="Glamorgan">Glamorgan</a>. He remained there for years, at prayer and study.<sup id="cite_ref-brookline_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brookline-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Finnian made copies of Rome's classics and of St. Jerome's Vulgate.
</p><p>After a sojourn in Wales of thirty years, according to the <i><a href="/wiki/Codex_Salmanticensis" title="Codex Salmanticensis">Codex Salmanticensis</a></i>, he returned to his native land. Although, as Hickey (1996, p.&#160;9) notes, "Thirty years away from Ireland seems too long when we consider Finnian's achievements in Ireland".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundations">Foundations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Finnian_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Foundations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg/220px-Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg/330px-Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg/440px-Clonard_RC_Church_St_Finian_06_Detail_2007_08_26.jpg 2x" data-file-width="674" data-file-height="951" /></a><figcaption>Finnian and his pupils in a stained glass window at the Church of St. Finian in Clonard</figcaption></figure>
<p>Finnian came first to <a href="/wiki/Aghowle_Church" title="Aghowle Church">Aghowle</a> in County Wicklow at the foot of Sliabh Condala, where Oengus, the king of Leinster granted him a site. He then founded a monastic community on <a href="/wiki/Skellig_Michael" title="Skellig Michael">Skellig Michael</a>, off the coast of Kerry, 'though this is doubted by historians.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> From there, he went to Brigid's monastery at Kildare.<sup id="cite_ref-lapa_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lapa-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Around 520, he was at last led by an angel to Cluain Eraird (Clonard, County Meath) on the River Boyne, which he was told would be the place of his resurrection.
</p><p>At Clonard Finnian built a little cell and a church of clay and wattle, and entered on a life of study, mortification, and prayer. The fame of his learning and sanctity soon spread, and scholars of all ages flocked from every side to his monastic retreat.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Finnian established a monastery modelled on the practices of Welsh monasteries, and based on the traditions of the <a href="/wiki/Desert_Fathers" title="Desert Fathers">Desert Fathers</a> and the study of Scripture. The rule of Clonard was known for its strictness and asceticism.<sup id="cite_ref-lapa_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lapa-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The pupils of Finnian who became the founding fathers of monasteries are described as leaving Clonard bearing a book or crozier or some other object, suggesting that a working scriptorium and craft workshops were established at Clonard at an early date.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><i>The Penitential of Finnian</i> prescribes penances with a view to correcting sinful tendencies and cultivating the contrary virtue. The document shows wide learning and draws on the teaching of John Cassian on overcoming the eight evil tendencies – gluttony, fornication, covetousness, anger, dejection, accidie (laziness), vainglory and pride (<i>The Institutes</i>, Books 5–12).<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life_and_death">Later life and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Finnian_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Later life and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the Office of St. Finnian it is stated that there were no fewer than 3,000 pupils getting instruction at one time in the school in the green fields of Clonard.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The master excelled in exposition of the Sacred Scriptures, and to this fact must be mainly attributed the extraordinary popularity which his lectures enjoyed. Finnian's gift for teaching and his absolute dedication to the ascetic ideal inspired a whole generation. Clonard drew students from various parts of Europe. <a href="/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_of_Clonmacnoise" title="Ciarán of Clonmacnoise">Ciarán of Clonmacnoise</a> and <a href="/wiki/Columcille" class="mw-redirect" title="Columcille">Columcille</a> of <a href="/wiki/Iona" title="Iona">Iona</a> are among the many who trained under him. They and many others took seeds of knowledge from Finnian's monastery at Clonard and planted them abroad with great success.  Finnian died of the plague in 549.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Hickey (1996) says: "If we consider his achievements in life, rather than the fabulous age attributed to him by his biographer (140 years), we may guess his age at death as perhaps sixty or sixty-five."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> His burial-place is in his own church of Clonard.
</p><p>Finnian's sister, Regnach, was Abbess of Kilreynagh, near the present town of Banagher.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Finnian_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clonard became an important school because of the number of its students who went on to found other monasteries. For centuries after his death, the school continued to be renowned as a seat of Scriptural learning, but it suffered at the hands of the Danes, especially in the eleventh century, and two Irishmen, O'Rorke of Breifney and <a href="/wiki/Diarmait_Mac_Murchada" title="Diarmait Mac Murchada">Dermod McMurrough</a>, helped to complete the work which the <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Northmen</a> had begun.<sup id="cite_ref-healy_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-healy-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The relics of Finnian himself were enshrined at Clonard until 887, after which the shrine was destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> With the transference by the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Norman</a> Bishop of Rochfort, in 1206, of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Meath" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Meath">See of Meath</a> from Clonard to <a href="/wiki/Trim,_County_Meath" title="Trim, County Meath">Trim</a>, the glory of the former place departed forever.
</p><p>Finnian of Clonard's feast-day is 12 December,<sup id="cite_ref-ier_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ier-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which is first attested in a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">Martyrology</a> of the 9th century.  In later years the monastery of Clonard came under the rule of the <a href="/wiki/U%C3%AD_N%C3%A9ill" title="Uí Néill">Uí Néill</a>, and came to share an abbot with either <a href="/wiki/Kildare" title="Kildare">Kildare</a> or <a href="/wiki/Clonmacnoise" title="Clonmacnoise">Clonmacnoise</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-farmer_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-farmer-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Finnian_of_Clonard&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Finnian is the patron saint of the Diocese of Meath.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
