<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Iwig of Lindisfarne</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">7th-century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">Brittany</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Wilton_Abbey" title="Wilton Abbey">Wilton Abbey</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">8 October</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Iwig</b> (alternatively <b>Iwi</b>, <b>Iwigius</b>, or <b>Ywi of Lindisfarne</b>) was a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> venerated in <a href="/wiki/Wiltshire" title="Wiltshire">Wiltshire</a>, England in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>. He was reputedly a <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, said to have died and to have been buried in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-541_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-541-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Historian <a href="/wiki/David_Dumville" title="David Dumville">David Dumville</a> called him "the other principal saint of <a href="/wiki/Wilton,_Wiltshire" title="Wilton, Wiltshire">Wilton</a>", in reference to <a href="/wiki/Eadgyth_of_Wilton" class="mw-redirect" title="Eadgyth of Wilton">Saint Eadgyth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was supposedly a follower (<i>alumnus</i>) of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Cuthbert" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Cuthbert">Saint Cuthbert</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Birch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Birch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is listed in two 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Litany" title="Litany">litanies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-541_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-541-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A narrative of that century claimed that his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> had been brought to <a href="/wiki/Wilton_Abbey" title="Wilton Abbey">Wilton Abbey</a> by <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Breton</a> monks in the 10th century, and left for safe-keeping at the <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> of Saint Eadgyth.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-541_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-541-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The narrative claims that the relics subsequently became immovable [through the wish of the saint to reside there], though historian John Blair suspected that this story may have been invented to justify Wilton's theft of the relics.<sup id="cite_ref-Blair-541_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Blair-541-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast day was celebrated on 8 October.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Ivychurch_Priory" title="Ivychurch Priory">Priory of Ivychurch</a> in Wiltshire is thought to have been named after him.<sup id="cite_ref-Birch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Birch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
