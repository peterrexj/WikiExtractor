<p><b>The martyrs of Turon</b> were a group of eight members of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious-teaching congregation</a> <a href="/wiki/Institute_of_the_Brothers_of_the_Christian_Schools" class="mw-redirect" title="Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools">Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools</a>, also known as <i>De La Salle</i> Brothers, and one <a href="/wiki/Passionist" class="mw-redirect" title="Passionist">Passionist</a> priest who were executed by <a href="/wiki/Insurrection" class="mw-redirect" title="Insurrection">insurrectionists</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>, during the <a href="/wiki/Asturian_miners%27_strike_of_1934" title="Asturian miners&#39; strike of 1934">Asturias uprising</a> of October 1934. They were <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1999 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Turon_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Turon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1934 <a href="/wiki/Tur%C3%B3n_(Mieres)" class="mw-redirect" title="Turón (Mieres)">Turón</a>, a coal-mining town in the <a href="/wiki/Asturias_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Asturias Province">Asturias Province</a> in Northwestern Spain, was the centre of anti-government and anticlerical hostility in the years prior to the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War. The eight de la Salle brothers were involved in an educational mission in the town of Turón in <a href="/wiki/Asturias" title="Asturias">Asturias</a>, living in a community there and teaching in a church school.<sup id="cite_ref-lasallian_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lasallian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The Brothers were known to defy the ban on teaching religion and were openly escorting their students to Sunday Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-cbmw_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cbmw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Incident">Incident</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Turon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Incident">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In October 1934, following the calling of a general <a href="/wiki/Strike_action" title="Strike action">strike</a>, the miners of Asturias <a href="/wiki/Asturian_miners%27_strike_of_1934" title="Asturian miners&#39; strike of 1934">began to arm and organize themselves</a>, occupying several towns and setting up "revolutionary committees."  The Brothers' school was an irritant to the insurrectionists in charge of operations in Turón because of the religious influence it ostensibly exerted on the young.<sup id="cite_ref-lasallian_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lasallian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On Friday 5 October 1934, they forced their way into the school on the pretext of inspecting whether arms had been hidden inside.<sup id="cite_ref-lasallian_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lasallian-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They arrested all the Brothers present, as well as a Passionist priest who was visiting to hear <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confessions</a>.
</p><p>During the next days, they were tried by a revolutionary court  and sentenced to death. On 9 October 1934, in the early hours of the morning, they were executed and then buried in a common grave.<sup id="cite_ref-cbmw_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cbmw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The miners' revolt collapsed shortly after the event, defeated by government troops, with over 3,000 miners killed in the process. The government forces were led by <a href="/wiki/General" class="mw-redirect" title="General">General</a> <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Franco" title="Francisco Franco">Francisco Franco</a>, who would himself <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War#Military_coup" title="Spanish Civil War">rebel against the government</a> two years later.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Turon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The nine martyrs of Turon  were <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> on 7 September 1989, and <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> By Pope John Paul II<sup id="cite_ref-cbmw_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cbmw-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> on 29 April 1990. They were <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> on 21 November 1999.<sup id="cite_ref-institute_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-institute-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
Their memorial day is 9 October.
</p><p>The nine are  regarded by the Catholic church as <a href="/wiki/Martyrs_of_the_Spanish_Civil_War" title="Martyrs of the Spanish Civil War">Martyrs of the Spanish Civil War</a>. Although their deaths occurred two years prior to the outbreak of the war, it was part and parcel of the communal violence that was a feature of the conflict, and the times before and after. Of the 6,000 <a href="/wiki/Religious_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious (Catholicism)">religious persons</a> killed during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a> about one thousand have had their causes advanced  for beatification, though the Martyrs of Turon were the first to be canonized.
</p><p>The cause for the Martyrs of Turon has been linked to that of <a href="/wiki/Jaime_Hilario" class="mw-redirect" title="Jaime Hilario">Jaime Hilario</a>,  who was tried, convicted, and executed in 1937 for being a member of the <i>De La Salle</i> Brothers and was canonized by the Catholic Church on the same day as were the nine martyrs of Turon.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="List">List</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martyrs_of_Turon&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: List">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Martyrs of Turon were:<sup id="cite_ref-institute_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-institute-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Turon_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ul><li>Brother <b>Cirilo Bertrán</b>, director of the community of Turón, born José Sanz Tejedor on 20 March 1888 at Lerma, in the province of Burgos.</li>
<li>Brother <b><a href="/wiki/Marciano_Jos%C3%A9" title="Marciano José">Marciano-José</a></b>, born Filomeno Lopez Lopez on 17 November 1900 at El Pedregal (Guadalajara).</li>
<li>Brother <b>Julian-Alfredo</b>, born Vilfrido Fernández Zapico on 24 December 1903 at Cifuentes de Rueda, Léon.</li>
<li>Brother <b>Victoriano-Pio</b>, born Claudio Bernabé Cano on 7 July 1905 at San Milian de Lara, Burgos.</li>
<li>Brother <b>Benjamin-Julian</b>, born Vicente Alonso Andrés on 7 October 1908 at Jaramillo de la Fuente, Burgos.</li>
<li>Brother <b>Benito de Jesús</b>, born Héctor Valdivielso Sáez on 31 October 1910 in <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a>, <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a>, the first <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentine</a> saint.<sup id="cite_ref-Turon_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Turon-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Brother <b>Augusto-Andrés</b>, born Román Martínez Fernández on 9 May 1910 at Santander</li>
<li>Brother <b>Aniceto-Adolfo</b>, at twenty years old, the youngest Brother of the community, born Manuel Seco Gutierrez in October  1912 at Celada Marlantes, Santander.</li>
<li>Father <b><a href="/wiki/Inocencio_of_Mary_Immaculate" title="Inocencio of Mary Immaculate">Inocencio de la Inmaculada Concepción</a></b>, Passionist Father who had come to hear confessions, born  Manuel Canoura Arnau on 10 March 1887 at S. Cecilia del Valle de Oro, near the Cantabrian coast in the province of Lugo, Galicia.</li></ul>
