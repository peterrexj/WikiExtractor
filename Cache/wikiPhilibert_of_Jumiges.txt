<p><b>Philibert of Jumièges</b> (c. 608&#8211;684) was an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastic</a> founder, particularly associated  with <a href="/wiki/Jumi%C3%A8ges_Abbey" title="Jumièges Abbey">Jumièges Abbey</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philibert_of_Jumi%C3%A8ges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Philibert was born in <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>, the only son of Filibaud, a magistrate of Vicus Julius (now <a href="/wiki/Aire-sur-l%27Adour" title="Aire-sur-l&#39;Adour">Aire-sur-l'Adour</a>). When he came of age he was given a place at the court of <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_I" title="Dagobert I">Dagobert I</a> at <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>, where he met Saints <a href="/wiki/Ouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Ouen">Ouen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Wandregisel" title="Wandregisel">Wandregisel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He received permission of the king to become a monk and entered the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Rebais" title="Rebais">Rebais</a>, which had been founded by Ouen and his brothers on land donated by the king. In 650, he succeeded his friend <a href="/wiki/Agilus" title="Agilus">Agilus</a> as abbot, but internal disagreements arose as to the more or less rigorous practice of the rule of <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a>. He left and spent some time traveling round monasteries studying their Rules and constitutions.
</p>
<h3><span id="Jumi.C3.A8ges"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Jumièges">Jumièges</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philibert_of_Jumi%C3%A8ges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Jumièges">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_(15817560804).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_%2815817560804%29.jpg/220px-Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_%2815817560804%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="330" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_%2815817560804%29.jpg/330px-Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_%2815817560804%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ce/Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_%2815817560804%29.jpg/440px-Jumi%C3%A8ges_12-29_%2815817560804%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3840" data-file-height="5760" /></a><figcaption>Jumièges</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 654, through the influence of Queen <a href="/wiki/Balthild_of_Chelles" title="Balthild of Chelles">Balthild of Chelles</a>, Philibert received a gift of land from <a href="/wiki/Clovis_II" title="Clovis II">Clovis II</a> on which he founded <a href="/wiki/Jumi%C3%A8ges_Abbey" title="Jumièges Abbey">Notre Dame de Jumièges</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He drew up a Rule based on his studies for this and for his later foundations, drawing on several earlier Rules, including those of <a href="/wiki/Benedict_of_Nursia" title="Benedict of Nursia">Benedict</a>, <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Alexandria" title="Macarius of Alexandria">Macarius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Basil_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Basil the Great">Basil the Great</a> and particularly the strict rule of <a href="/wiki/Columbanus" title="Columbanus">Columbanus</a>. The monks' chief work was the reclaiming of waste lands.<sup id="cite_ref-monks_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-monks-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He welcomed the poor and pilgrims. An important spiritual place, Jumièges became renowned and several notables and abbots came to consult Philibert.
</p><p>Philibert did not hesitate to confront <a href="/wiki/Ebroin" title="Ebroin">Ebroin</a>, the mayor of the palace of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a>, about his treat of <a href="/wiki/Leodegar" title="Leodegar">Leodegar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Autun" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Autun">Bishop of Autun</a>, who had died a martyr. Ebroin then saw to it that for a time Philibert lost the favour of Ouen and the royal family. He was briefly imprisoned then not permitted to return to Jumieges. He chose exile from <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> and withdrew to <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a> and the court of Bishop <a href="/wiki/Ansoald" title="Ansoald">Ansoald</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a> who put his own foundation of Luçon Abbey under Philibert's charge.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Noirmoutier">Noirmoutier</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philibert_of_Jumi%C3%A8ges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Noirmoutier">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg/220px-Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="129" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg/330px-Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg/440px-Grotte_Saint_Philbert_P1010097mod.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2560" data-file-height="1500" /></a><figcaption>Grotto of Saint Philbert, Noirmoutier</figcaption></figure>
<p>Around 676, Philibert decided to found a new monastery on the island of Her (Noirmoutier) with the agreement of the bishop Ansoald who granted him some land. On the island, in addition to evangelization, the monks carried out important work: agricultural development, irrigation, communication routes, salt exploitation, and construction of monastic buildings. The bishop also gave him land at Déas which became the abbey of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Philbert-de-Grand-Lieu" title="Saint-Philbert-de-Grand-Lieu">Saint-Philbert-de-Grand-Lieu</a>. (Around 847, the monks of Noirmoutier built a Carolingian priory church in Saint-Philbert-de-Grand-Lieu in his honor.) Five years later, still with the bishop of Poitiers, he developed, with monks from Noirmoutier, a priory that would become the royal abbey of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Michel-en-l%27Herm" title="Saint-Michel-en-l&#39;Herm">Saint-Michel-en-l'Herm</a>, partly at the origin of the <a href="/wiki/Marais_Poitevin" title="Marais Poitevin">Marais Poitevin</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philibert_of_Jumi%C3%A8ges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the death of Ebroin in 681, Philibert returned briefly to Jumieges, but did not stay as he was by then deeply involved with Noirmoutier. Before leaving the place for the last time, he reconciled with his friend Saint Ouen. When he regained the favour of his patrons, he founded other houses, including the nunneries at <a href="/wiki/Pavilly" title="Pavilly">Pavilly</a> and in 682 <a href="/wiki/Montivilliers_Abbey" title="Montivilliers Abbey">Montivilliers Abbey</a>. On his return, he continued supervising his various foundations. Philibert died and was buried at Heriou.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Philibert_of_Jumi%C3%A8ges&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 836 the monks of <a href="/wiki/Noirmoutier" title="Noirmoutier">Noirmoutier</a> abandoned their home in the face of the <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Viking</a> attacks to seek refuge on the mainland, in 875 finally settling with the relics of Philibert in the abbey at <a href="/wiki/Tournus" title="Tournus">Tournus</a> named in his honour, where the great church of <a href="/wiki/St_Philibert_at_Tournus" class="mw-redirect" title="St Philibert at Tournus">St Philibert at Tournus</a> still stands. This translation by the monks of Noirmoutier gave rise to several foundations and numerous Carolingian endowments, creating a large monastic network.
</p><p>The relics of the saint are kept in the choir of the Saint-Philibert abbey of Tournus, inside a reliquary, the work of the artist <a href="/wiki/Goudji" title="Goudji">Goudji</a>. They were desecrated on January 25, 1998; the skull of the saint and two of his bones having been stolen.
</p><p>Among those inspired by Philibert's example was the Irish monk <a href="/wiki/Sidonius_(Irish_saint)" title="Sidonius (Irish saint)">Sidonius</a> of Jumieges, who founded the monastery at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Sa%C3%ABns,_Seine-Maritime" title="Saint-Saëns, Seine-Maritime">Saint-Saëns</a>.
</p><p>Philibert of Jumièges' feast day is 20 August. The filbert, or hazelnut, is said to have been named after him, since it ripens about August 20 in England.
</p><p>Villages bear his name in the <a href="/wiki/Rhone_Valley" class="mw-redirect" title="Rhone Valley">Rhone Valley</a>, in <a href="/wiki/County_of_Anjou" title="County of Anjou">Anjou</a>, in Normandy and in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>.
</p>
