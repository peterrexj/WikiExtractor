<p><b>Humphrey Middlemore</b>, (died 19 June 1535) was an <a href="/wiki/English_people" title="English people">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>, who was executed for treason during the <a href="/wiki/Tudor_period" title="Tudor period">Tudor period</a>. He is considered a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the Catholic Church, and, along with other members of his <a href="/wiki/Religious_order" title="Religious order">religious order</a> to meet that fate, was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> on 9 December 1886.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Humphrey_Middlemore&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Though the date of his birth is uncertain, his father was Thomas Middlemore of <a href="/wiki/Edgbaston" title="Edgbaston">Edgbaston</a>, <a href="/wiki/Warwickshire" title="Warwickshire">Warwickshire</a>, who had acquired his estate at Edgbaston by marriage with the heiress of Sir Henry Edgbaston. Humphrey's mother was Ann Lyttleton, of <a href="/wiki/Pillaton_Hall" title="Pillaton Hall">Pillaton Hall</a>, <a href="/wiki/Staffordshire" title="Staffordshire">Staffordshire</a>. Attracted to the Carthusian Order, he entered the <a href="/wiki/London_Charterhouse" title="London Charterhouse">London Charterhouse</a>, where he was <a href="/wiki/Profession" title="Profession">professed</a> and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a>. He was subsequently appointed to the office of <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/procurator" class="extiw" title="wikt:procurator">procurator</a>. He was esteemed by the <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a>, Dom <a href="/wiki/John_Houghton_(martyr)" title="John Houghton (martyr)">John Houghton</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-wintersgill_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wintersgill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1534 the question of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>'s marriage with <a href="/wiki/Anne_Boleyn" title="Anne Boleyn">Anne Boleyn</a> arose. The king was determined that the more prominent of his subjects should expressly acknowledge the validity of the marriage, and the right of succession of any issue therefrom. The Crown was at first anxious to secure the public acquiescence of the monks of the London Charterhouse in this matter, since they enjoyed great prestige for the austerity and sincerity of their mode of life. Accordingly, the <a href="/wiki/Royal_commission" title="Royal commission">royal commissions</a> paid a visit to the Charterhouse, and required the monks to take the oath to that effect. Doms John Houghton and Humphrey Middlemore refused, and were, in consequence, imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>; but, after a month's imprisonment, they were persuaded to take the oath conditionally, and were released.<sup id="cite_ref-wintersgill_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wintersgill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the following year, on 4 May 1535, the authorities sent to their death at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn_Tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn Tree">Tyburn Tree</a> for refusal to take the new <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a>, three leading English Carthusians, first among them John Houghton, prior of the London house, but also <a href="/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(martyr)" title="Robert Lawrence (martyr)">Robert Lawrence</a> and <a href="/wiki/Augustine_Webster" title="Augustine Webster">Augustine Webster</a>, respectively priors of <a href="/wiki/Beauvale_Charterhouse" class="mw-redirect" title="Beauvale Charterhouse">Beauvale</a> and <a href="/wiki/Axholme" class="mw-redirect" title="Axholme">Axholme</a>. This led to Middlemore becoming <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a> of the community.Meanwhile, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Bedyll" title="Thomas Bedyll">Thomas Bedyll</a>, one of the royal commissioners, had again visited the Charterhouse, and endeavored, both by conversation and writing, to shake the faith of Middlemore and his community in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">papal supremacy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-gurdon_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-gurdon-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His efforts left them unmoved, and, after debating with them, he obtained authority from <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Thomas Cromwell</a> to arrest three leading monks of the London house: Middlemore, as the vicar, and with him Doms <a href="/wiki/William_Exmew" title="William Exmew">William Exmew</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sebastian_Newdigate" title="Sebastian Newdigate">Sebastian Newdigate</a>. All three were thrown them into prison, where they were treated with cruelty, being bound to posts with chains round their necks and legs, and compelled to remain thus day and night for two weeks. They were then brought before the council, and required to take the oath. They refused, and gave arguments from Scripture and the Church Fathers in favor of the papal supremacy.<sup id="cite_ref-wintersgill_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-wintersgill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They were accordingly condemned to death, and executed at Tyburn Tree on 19 June, being <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a>. This was a little more than a month after their fellow monks. This process of attrition was to claim fifteen of the London Carthusians.
</p>
