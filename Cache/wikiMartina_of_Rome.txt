<p><b>Martina of Rome</b> was a <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> under the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Severus_Alexander" title="Severus Alexander">Severus Alexander</a>. A <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, she was martyred in 226, according to some authorities, more probably in 228, under the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_I" title="Pope Urban I">Pope Urban I</a>, according to others. Her <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is January 30.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martina_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The daughter of an ex-<a href="/wiki/Consul" title="Consul">consul</a> and orphaned at an early age, she was described as "a noble and beautiful virgin".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She so openly testified to her <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> faith that she could not escape the persecutions under <a href="/wiki/Severus_Alexander" title="Severus Alexander">Severus Alexander</a>. Arrested and commanded to return to idolatry, she refused, whereupon she was subjected to various tortures and was finally <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>These tortures according to her <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a> include being <a href="/wiki/Scourged" class="mw-redirect" title="Scourged">scourged</a>. She was <a href="/wiki/Damnatio_ad_bestias" title="Damnatio ad bestias">condemned to be devoured by wild beasts</a> in the amphitheater but was miraculously untouched by them. She was then thrown onto a burning pyre, from which she also escaped unhurt, and was finally <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>. Her <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> asserts that some of her executioners also converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> and were themselves beheaded.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martina_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ostia, at the mouth of the Tiber, was the scene of her martyrdom; but her body was transported to Rome,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> where a church near the <a href="/wiki/Mamertine_Prison" title="Mamertine Prison">Mamertine Prison</a>, <a href="/wiki/Santi_Luca_e_Martina" title="Santi Luca e Martina">Santi Luca e Martina</a>, was later dedicated in her honour.
<sup id="cite_ref-Merz_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Merz-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On October 25, 1634, during restoration of the crypt, as was common at this time in Rome, buried remains were found by the painter <a href="/wiki/Pietro_da_Cortona" title="Pietro da Cortona">Pietro da Cortona</a>, president of the Accademia di San Luca, and were attributed to the martyred Saint Martina. No doubt it was hoped that this would precipitate an influx of funds to shelter the relics in a new church.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Pope Urban VIII</a>, who occupied the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> at that time, had the church repaired and, it would seem, composed the hymns which are sung at her office.<sup id="cite_ref-Clugnet_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Clugnet-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
