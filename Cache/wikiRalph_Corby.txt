<p><b>Ralph Corbie</b> (Corby, Corbington, at times Corrington) (25 March 1598 – 7 September 1644) was an Irish <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a>. He is a Catholic martyr, <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1929.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ralph_Corbie&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Corbie was born near <a href="/wiki/Dublin" title="Dublin">Dublin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents were from Durham and returned to England when Ralph was about five years of age.<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> A brother of <a href="/wiki/Ambrose_Corbie" title="Ambrose Corbie">Ambrose Corbie</a>, he spent his childhood in the north of England. Then going overseas he studied at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Omer" title="Saint-Omer">Saint-Omer</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Valladolid" title="English College, Valladolid">English College, Valladolid</a>; where he was ordained. Having become a Jesuit about 1626, he came to England about 1631, where he was known by the name of "Carlington".<sup id="cite_ref-stanton_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stanton-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He worked at Durham for about twelve years.<sup id="cite_ref-ryan_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ryan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was seized by the Parliamentarians at <a href="/wiki/Hamsterley,_Bishop_Auckland" title="Hamsterley, Bishop Auckland">Hamsterley</a>, 8 July 1644, when clothed in his Mass vestments, conveyed to London, and committed to <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a> (22 July) with <a href="/wiki/John_Duckett" title="John Duckett">John Duckett</a>, a secular priest.<sup id="cite_ref-oye_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oye-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At their trial (<a href="/wiki/Old_Bailey" title="Old Bailey">Old Bailey</a>, 4 September), they both admitted their priesthood, were condemned to death. Corbie was a Jesuit and the Society tried to save him. When the pardon finally arrived, Corbie insisted that Duckett used it, since he was younger. But John refused. Both were condemned to death.<sup id="cite_ref-oye_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oye-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and executed at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a>, 7 September.<sup id="cite_ref-ryan_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ryan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was hung until dead, then disemboweled and quartered.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Stonyhurst" title="Stonyhurst">Stonyhurst</a> has a relic of Father Corbie.<sup id="cite_ref-ryan_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ryan-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
