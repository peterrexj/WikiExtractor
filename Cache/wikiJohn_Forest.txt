<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other people named John Forest, see <a href="/wiki/John_Forest_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="John Forest (disambiguation)">John Forest (disambiguation)</a>.</div>

<p><b>John Forest</b> (1471 – 22 May 1538) was an English Franciscan friar and martyr. Confessor to <a href="/wiki/Queen_Catherine_of_Aragon" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen Catherine of Aragon">Queen Catherine of Aragon</a>, Forest was burned to death at Smithfield for heresy, in that he refused to acknowledge the King as head of the church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Forest&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born in the Oxford area in 1471, John Forest became a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> Minor of the Regular Observance in 1491 in Greenwich. He went on to study <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Oxford" title="University of Oxford">University of Oxford</a>, later becoming provincial of all the Observant Friars in England, and confessor to Queen <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Aragon" title="Catherine of Aragon">Catherine of Aragon</a>, first wife to King <a href="/wiki/Henry_VIII_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry VIII of England">Henry VIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (The Greenwich friary was attached to the Royal Palace at Greenwich.)
</p><p>The King was eager to gain the sanction of learned men and of those esteemed highly to his plans in regard to the Church. Wealth and honours were offered to those who complied. Those who resisted were threatened.<sup id="cite_ref-camm_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-camm-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> From 1531 the Friars Minor had gained the enmity of the King by opposing his divorce and his movements toward <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestantism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-thaddeus_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thaddeus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In November, 1532, as Guardian of the Greenwich friary, Forest spoke to the friars of the plans the King had to suppress the Order in England and denounced from the pulpit at <a href="/wiki/St._Paul%27s_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Paul&#39;s Cross">St. Paul's Cross</a> Henry's plans for a divorce. In 1533 he was imprisoned in <a href="/wiki/Newgate_prison" class="mw-redirect" title="Newgate prison">Newgate prison</a> and condemned to death. In 1534 Henry suppressed the Observant friars and ordered them dispersed to other friaries. John was released from prison but by 1538 was in confinement in a Conventual Franciscan friary at Smithfield, his death sentence having been neither commuted nor carried out.<sup id="cite_ref-ofm_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ofm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Forest was sent to a convent in the north.
</p><p>Despite initially recanting, Forest was detained at <a href="/wiki/Newgate_Prison" title="Newgate Prison">Newgate Prison</a>, on the basis of denial of the king's supremacy, together with several other Friars, who persuaded him to stand fast in his Roman Catholic beliefs.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His confinement, therefore, was not strict, and he was allowed to celebrate <a href="/wiki/Mass_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Mass in the Catholic Church">mass</a> and hear confessions.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTETrice1889_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTETrice1889-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> From this confinement he could correspond with the Queen and he also wrote a tract entitled <i>De auctoritate Ecclesiae et Pontificis maximi</i> ("On the Authority of the Church and the Supreme Pontiff"), defending both the papal primacy and the independence of the Church from control by the State. He was denounced to the King for this tract and also for refusing to swear the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> demanded by <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Cromwell</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ofm_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ofm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cranmer" title="Thomas Cranmer">Thomas Cranmer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Latimer" title="Hugh Latimer">Hugh Latimer</a> acted as a team on <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Cromwell's</a> behalf in the proceedings which led to the friar's destruction. Forest was condemned for <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a> and <a href="/wiki/Heresy_in_Christianity" title="Heresy in Christianity">heresy</a>, the latter for opposing the King's policy of <a href="/wiki/Caesaropapism" title="Caesaropapism">Caesaropapism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEGasquet190658,_n.1_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGasquet190658,_n.1-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In accordance with the custom of the time, Bishop Latimer was selected to preach a final sermon at the place of execution urging recantation. In the end, Forest was burnt to death at <a href="/wiki/Smithfield,_London" title="Smithfield, London">Smithfield, London</a> on 22 May 1538,<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> where he was suspended over the fire in chains. John Forest was the only Catholic martyr to be burned at the stake during the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ofm_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ofm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Extra fuel for the pyre is said to have been provided by an enormous statue of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Derfel" title="Saint Derfel">St. Derfel</a> from the pilgrimage site of Llandderfel in north <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, and of which it was prophesied, would "one day set a forest on fire."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Forest&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Forest, together with 53 other English martyrs, was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a>, on 9 December 1886.<sup id="cite_ref-thaddeus_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-thaddeus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There is a statue of Forest in the nave of St. Ethelreda's Church in Ely Place, London.
</p><p>There is a mosaic of Forest in the Holy Name of Jesus Roman Catholic Church, <a href="/wiki/Oxford_Road,_Manchester" class="mw-redirect" title="Oxford Road, Manchester">Oxford Road</a>, <a href="/wiki/Chorlton-on-Medlock" title="Chorlton-on-Medlock">Chorlton-on-Medlock</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manchester" title="Manchester">Manchester</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are stained glass windows of the martyr in the following churches:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_and_the_English_Martyrs_Church" title="Our Lady and the English Martyrs Church">Our Lady and the English Martyrs</a>, Hills Road, <a href="/wiki/Cambridge" title="Cambridge">Cambridge</a><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Shrewsbury_Cathedral" title="Shrewsbury Cathedral">Shrewsbury Cathedral</a>, he is the first figure on the fifth window, next to Edmund Campion.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
