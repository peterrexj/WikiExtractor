<p><b>Salomea of Berg</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Salome von Berg</i>, <a href="/wiki/Polish_language" title="Polish language">Polish</a>: <i lang="pl">Salomea z Bergu</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1099/1101</span> – 27 July 1144) was a <a href="/wiki/Germans" title="Germans">German</a> noblewoman and, by marriage with Prince <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_III_Wrymouth" title="Bolesław III Wrymouth">Bolesław III Wrymouth</a> in 1115, <a href="/wiki/List_of_Polish_consorts" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Polish consorts">High Duchess of Poland</a> until her husband's death in 1138.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomea_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Salomea was the daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia" title="Duchy of Swabia">Swabian</a> count <a href="/wiki/Henry,_Count_of_Berg" class="mw-redirect" title="Henry, Count of Berg">Henry</a> of Berg Castle near <a href="/wiki/Ehingen" title="Ehingen">Ehingen</a> (not to be confused with the Rhenish <a href="/wiki/Berg_(state)" class="mw-redirect" title="Berg (state)">County of Berg</a>) by his wife Adelaide of Mochental (d. 1125/27),<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> probably a sister of Margrave <a href="/wiki/Diepold_III,_Margrave_of_Vohburg" title="Diepold III, Margrave of Vohburg">Diepold III of Vohburg</a>. According to some sources,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> her paternal grandmother was Princess Sophia, only daughter of King <a href="/wiki/Solomon,_King_of_Hungary" title="Solomon, King of Hungary">Solomon of Hungary</a> by his wife <a href="/wiki/Judith_of_Swabia" title="Judith of Swabia">Judith of Swabia</a> (by her second marriage stepmother of Salome's husband), but this hypothesis has been rejected by modern historiographers.
</p><p>The marriage of Salome's sister <a href="/wiki/Richeza_of_Berg" title="Richeza of Berg">Richeza</a> with Duke <a href="/wiki/Vladislaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladislaus I, Duke of Bohemia">Vladislav I of Bohemia</a> in 1110 had changed the status of the relative obscure Count Henry of Berg in political affairs. Another sister, Sophia, was married to a <a href="/wiki/Moravia" title="Moravia">Moravian</a> member of the <a href="/wiki/P%C5%99emyslid_dynasty" title="Přemyslid dynasty">Přemyslid dynasty</a>, Duke <a href="/wiki/Otto_II_the_Black" title="Otto II the Black">Otto II the Black</a> of <a href="/wiki/Olomouc" title="Olomouc">Olomouc</a>, in 1113.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage">Marriage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomea_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Polish ruler Bolesław III Wrymouth, after he had begun to expand his domains in the <a href="/wiki/Pomerelia" title="Pomerelia">Pomerelian</a> region, decided to normalize his relations with his southern <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemian</a> neighbors. This took place in 1114 at a great convention on the border river <a href="/wiki/Nysa_K%C5%82odzka" class="mw-redirect" title="Nysa Kłodzka">Nysa Kłodzka</a>. Participants included Bolesław III himself, as well as the Bohemian and Moravian Dukes of the Přemyslid line: Vladislav I, Otto II the Black and Vladislav's younger brother <a href="/wiki/Sobeslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia" class="mw-redirect" title="Sobeslaus I, Duke of Bohemia">Soběslav I</a>. It was decided that the Duke of Poland (a widower since 1112) should secondly marry the Swabian noblewoman Salomea, sister of the Duchesses of Bohemia and Olomouc.
</p><p>The marriage negotiations, led by Bishop <a href="/wiki/Otto_of_Bamberg" title="Otto of Bamberg">Otto of Bamberg</a>, ended successfully, and between March and July 1115 the wedding of Bolesław III Wrymouth and Salomea of Berg took place. By the end of that year, the new Duchess gave birth to a son named Leszek (d. 1131), the first of the thirteen children who she bore to her husband.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template noprint noexcerpt Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:NOTRS" class="mw-redirect" title="Wikipedia:NOTRS"><span title="This claim needs references to better sources. (August 2012)">better&#160;source&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Salomea began to participate actively in the Polish politics on behalf of her children; she feared that according to the <a href="/wiki/Primogeniture" title="Primogeniture">primogeniture</a> principle her stepson <a href="/wiki/W%C5%82adys%C5%82aw_II_the_Exile" title="Władysław II the Exile">Władysław II</a>, Bolesław III's first-born son from his marriage with <a href="/wiki/Zbyslava_of_Kiev" title="Zbyslava of Kiev">Zbyslava of Kiev</a>, would succeed his father as sole ruler and her sons would be at the mercy of their elder half-brother. In 1125 the powerful <a href="/wiki/Piotr_W%C5%82ostowic" title="Piotr Włostowic">Piotr Włostowic</a> was forced to resign his post of a Polish <a href="/wiki/Voivode" title="Voivode">Voivode</a> (<a href="/wiki/Count_palatine" title="Count palatine">Count palatine</a>); the main instigator of this decision was probably Salomea, who replaced him with Wszebor, a man she considered more likely to support herself and her sons against Władysław.
</p>
<h3><span id="Boles.C5.82aw_III.27s_Testament"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Bolesław_III's_Testament">Bolesław III's Testament</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomea_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Bolesław III&#039;s Testament">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Testament_of_Boles%C5%82aw_III_Krzywousty" class="mw-redirect" title="Testament of Bolesław III Krzywousty">Testament of Bolesław III Krzywousty</a></div>
<p>Bolesław III died on 28 October 1138. In his will he had divided the country between his sons according to the principle of <a href="/wiki/Agnatic_seniority" title="Agnatic seniority">agnatic seniority</a>, giving the <a href="/wiki/Seniorate_Province" title="Seniorate Province">Seniorate Province</a> of <a href="/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w" title="Kraków">Kraków</a> to his eldest son Władysław II. In addition Salomea received the town of <a href="/wiki/%C5%81%C4%99czyca" title="Łęczyca">Łęczyca</a>, several castles and towns throughout Poland (including <a href="/wiki/Paj%C4%99czno" title="Pajęczno">Pajęczno</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ma%C5%82ogoszcz" title="Małogoszcz">Małogoszcz</a>, <a href="/wiki/Radziej%C3%B3w" title="Radziejów">Radziejów</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kwieciszewo" title="Kwieciszewo">Kwieciszewo</a>) as her <a href="/wiki/Dower" title="Dower">dower</a>; this was the first documented case where a Polish ruler left his widow her own piece of land. The terms of the dower stated that the beneficiary could obtain the full sovereignty over the land for her life, and could lose it in two cases: if she remarried or became a nun (abdication would not count). The youngest child of the couple, <a href="/wiki/Casimir_II_the_Just" title="Casimir II the Just">Casimir II</a>, was not assigned a province; it is speculated that he was born after Bolesław III's death.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_Years">Later Years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomea_of_Berg&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later Years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>From her domains in Łęczyca, the now Dowager Duchess continue her intrigues against her stepson High Duke Władysław II. However, the hostilities only began openly in 1141, when Salomea, without the knowledge and consent of the High Duke, commenced to divide Łęczyca between her sons. Also, she tried to resolve the marriage of her youngest daughter Agnes and thus to find a suitable ally for her sons. The most appropriate candidate for a son-in-law had to be <a href="/wiki/Sviatoslav_III_of_Kiev" title="Sviatoslav III of Kiev">Sviatoslav III</a>, the son of Grand Prince <a href="/wiki/Vsevolod_II_of_Kiev" title="Vsevolod II of Kiev">Vsevolod II of Kiev</a>. After hearing the news about the events in Łęczyca, Władysław II decided to make a quick response, as a result of which the Grand Prince of Kiev not only broke all his pacts with the Junior Dukes, but also arranged the betrothal of his daughter Zvenislava to Władysław's eldest son <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_I_the_Tall" title="Bolesław I the Tall">Bolesław I the Tall</a>. The wedding took place one year later, in 1142.
</p><p>Salomea died at her mansion in Łęczyca on 27 July 1144. In accordance with the will of Boleslaw III, her province of Łęczyca reverted to the Senoriate. Unexpectedly, her bitter enemy Piotr Włostowic allied with her sons against High Duke Władysław II, who, after having captured and blinded Włostowic in 1145, was defeated and deposed by his half-brothers. Salomea's oldest surviving son, <a href="/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_IV_the_Curly" title="Bolesław IV the Curly">Bolesław IV the Curly</a> became the new High Duke of Poland in 1146.
</p>
