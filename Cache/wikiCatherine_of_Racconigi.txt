<p>Most of the information regarding Catherine Mattei is derived from a <i>vita</i> written by her friend, John Francis Pico, Prince of Mirandola.<sup id="cite_ref-rfo_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-rfo-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
Catherine Mattei (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Caterina Mattei</i>) was born in the <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> region of <a href="/wiki/Northwest_Italy" title="Northwest Italy">Northwest Italy</a> in 1487 into an impoverished household in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Cuneo" title="Province of Cuneo">Province of Cuneo</a>. Intermittent conflicts in the area brought widespread poverty. Her parents were Giorgio and Bilia de Ferrari Mattei. Her father was an unemployed tool-maker, while her mother was a <a href="/wiki/Weaver_(occupation)" class="mw-redirect" title="Weaver (occupation)">weaver</a> who was able to support the family. Her father was often despondent<sup id="cite_ref-opwest_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-opwest-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and quarrelsome, thus the family environment was usually one of conflict.
</p><p>She is said to have had, at about the age of nine, a vision of Jesus, who appeared to her as a boy of about ten. She would also begin to have visions of other saints such as the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominican order">Dominicans</a>  <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Verona" title="Peter of Verona">Peter Martyr</a>. The Virgin told her that Jesus wanted to have her as his bride, as a token of which she was given a <a href="/wiki/Wedding_ring" title="Wedding ring">wedding ring</a>, which she had all her life—but which only she could see. She began to have frequent <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">ecstasies</a> and visions of Jesus, who always appeared to her as the same age she was at the time of the vision.
</p><p>Many miracles would result from her visions: a broken dish was made whole again, and money and food would be provided when the family's poverty was extreme. In times of trial she received great consolation from the aspiration, "Jesus, is my only hope!"<sup id="cite_ref-opwest_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-opwest-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Catherine eventually joined the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> herself, but, like Catherine of Siena, she was opposed in this wish by her family. As a result, instead of entering a monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloistered</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> of the Order, she became a <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">tertiary</a> of the Order, continuing to live with her family. As the visions increased she received the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a>, although, the wounds were not visible until after her death.<sup id="cite_ref-opwest_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-opwest-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her mystical experiences roused a storm of gossip among her neighbors, who were terrified at the lights and sounds that came from her home; even the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friars</a> began to ostracize her. Eventually she was forced out of the town and settled in the city of Racconigi. During her lifetime, Catherine was sought for counsel and prayer. She would keep in her prayers in a special way the salvation of soldiers who were dying in battle.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Catherine_of_Racconigi&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Many miracles took place before and after her death and her detractors came to acknowledge the holiness of her life. She was beatified on 9 April 1808 by Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_VII" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius VII">Pius VII</a>.
</p>
