<p><b>Spencer Compton, 1st Earl of Wilmington</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a></span> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1674&#160;&#8211;&#32;2 July 1743)<sup id="cite_ref-CompODNB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CompODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/British_Whig" class="mw-redirect" title="British Whig">British Whig</a> statesman who served continuously in government from 1715 until his death in 1743. He sat in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_England" title="House of Commons of England">English</a> and <a href="/wiki/British_House_of_Commons" class="mw-redirect" title="British House of Commons">British House of Commons</a> between 1698 and 1728, and was then raised to the peerage and sat in the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a>. He served as the <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime minister of Great Britain">prime minister of Great Britain</a> from 1742 until his death in 1743. He is considered to have been Britain's second prime minister, after <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a>, but worked closely with the <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_(United_Kingdom)" title="Secretary of State (United Kingdom)">Secretary of State</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lord_Carteret" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Carteret">Lord Carteret</a>, in order to secure the support of the various factions making up the government.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Compton was born in Compton Wynyates, a <a href="/wiki/Tudor_architecture" title="Tudor architecture">Tudor castle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warwickshire" title="Warwickshire">Warwickshire</a>. He was the third son of <a href="/wiki/James_Compton,_3rd_Earl_of_Northampton" title="James Compton, 3rd Earl of Northampton">James Compton, 3rd Earl of Northampton</a>, who fought for the <a href="/wiki/Cavalier" title="Cavalier">royalists</a> during the <a href="/wiki/English_Civil_War" title="English Civil War">English Civil War</a> and his wife Mary Noel, daughter of <a href="/wiki/Baptist_Noel,_3rd_Viscount_Campden" title="Baptist Noel, 3rd Viscount Campden">Baptist Noel, 3rd Viscount Campden</a>. His father died in 1681 when Spencer was young and his brother, <a href="/wiki/George_Compton,_4th_Earl_of_Northampton" title="George Compton, 4th Earl of Northampton">George Compton</a>, become the sole beneficiary of the family's estates. Spencer was educated at <a href="/wiki/St_Paul%27s_School_(London)" class="mw-redirect" title="St Paul&#39;s School (London)">St Paul's School</a> and matriculated at <a href="/wiki/Trinity_College,_Oxford" title="Trinity College, Oxford">Trinity College, Oxford</a> on 28 February 1690, aged 15;<sup id="cite_ref-ALUM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ALUM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> thereafter he was admitted into <a href="/wiki/Middle_Temple" title="Middle Temple">Middle Temple</a> in 1687.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Political_career">Political career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Political career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="English_House_of_Commons">English House of Commons</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: English House of Commons">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Although his family were <a href="/wiki/High_Tories" class="mw-redirect" title="High Tories">High Tories</a>, Compton turned to the <a href="/wiki/Whigs_(British_political_party)" title="Whigs (British political party)">Whigs</a> after a quarrel with his brother, the <a href="/wiki/George_Compton,_4th_Earl_of_Northampton" title="George Compton, 4th Earl of Northampton">4th Earl of Northampton</a>. He first  stood for Parliament at <a href="/wiki/East_Grinstead_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="East Grinstead (UK Parliament constituency)">East Grinstead</a> on the interest of his kinsman the <a href="/wiki/Charles_Sackville,_6th_Earl_of_Dorset" title="Charles Sackville, 6th Earl of Dorset">Earl of Dorset</a>  at the <a href="/wiki/1695_English_general_election" title="1695 English general election">1695 English general election</a> but was unsuccessful. He was returned unopposed as <a href="/wiki/Member_of_Parliament" class="mw-redirect" title="Member of Parliament">Member of Parliament</a> for <a href="/wiki/Eye_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Eye (UK Parliament constituency)">Eye</a> at a by-election on 3 June 1698. In Parliament he soon stood out as prominent amongst the Whigs and began a partnership with <a href="/wiki/Robert_Walpole" title="Robert Walpole">Robert Walpole</a> that would last for over forty years. He was returned unopposed for Eye at the two general elections of <a href="/wiki/1701_English_general_election" title="1701 English general election">1701</a> and in <a href="/wiki/1702_English_general_election" title="1702 English general election">1702</a> and <a href="/wiki/1705_English_general_election" title="1705 English general election">1705</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HOP_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HOP-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Paymaster_of_Pensions">Paymaster of Pensions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Paymaster of Pensions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1707 Compton became <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_Pensions" title="Paymaster of Pensions">Paymaster of Pensions</a>, a post that he retained for the next six years. He was returned unopposed again at the <a href="/wiki/1708_British_general_election" title="1708 British general election">1708 British general election</a> and was particularly active in Parliament thereafter. He remained as chairman of the committee of privileges and elections, and was a teller on the Whig side in many divisions. He managed several bills and on  14 December 1709 was nominated to the committee to draw up the articles of impeachment against <a href="/wiki/Henry_Sacheverell" title="Henry Sacheverell">Dr Sacheverell</a>. At the <a href="/wiki/1710_British_general_election" title="1710 British general election">1710 British general election</a> he was dropped as a candidate for Eye by his patron <a href="/wiki/Charles_Cornwallis,_4th_Baron_Cornwallis" title="Charles Cornwallis, 4th Baron Cornwallis">Lord Cornwallis</a> after a disagreement, and he was unwilling to risk standing anywhere else because of his involvement with the Sacheverell case. However he retained his post as Paymaster of Pensions after the Tory government took office in that year. It is believed that the Tories retained him because they sought to maintain the support of the Compton family. At the <a href="/wiki/1713_British_general_election" title="1713 British general election">1713 British general election</a> he was returned as Whig MP for <a href="/wiki/East_Grinstead_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="East Grinstead (UK Parliament constituency)">East Grinstead</a> and when the Whigs took power in 1715 he was hopeful that he would enter a high office.<sup id="cite_ref-HOP_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HOP-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Speaker_of_the_Commons">Speaker of the Commons</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Speaker of the Commons">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg/220px-Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="284" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg/330px-Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg/440px-Spencer_Compton_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2400" data-file-height="3095" /></a><figcaption>The Earl of Wilmington, by <a href="/wiki/Godfrey_Kneller" title="Godfrey Kneller">Godfrey Kneller</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1710</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>Instead of the high political office he had hoped for, Compton received the Court appointment of Treasurer to the <a href="/wiki/Prince_of_Wales" title="Prince of Wales">Prince of Wales</a> (later <a href="/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain" title="George II of Great Britain">George II</a>); shortly afterwards, however, he was unanimously elected as <a href="/wiki/Speaker_of_the_House_of_Commons_(United_Kingdom)" title="Speaker of the House of Commons (United Kingdom)">Speaker of the House of Commons</a>. He held this post from 1715 to 1727. In 1716, he was invested a <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_Great_Britain" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of Great Britain">Privy Counsellor</a>. He maintained the role of Speaker despite the split in the Whigs in 1717, in which he joined the Walpole-<a href="/wiki/Charles_Townshend,_2nd_Viscount_Townshend" title="Charles Townshend, 2nd Viscount Townshend">Townshend</a> alliance and found himself in opposition to the government of the day. He managed to maintain his position though until 1720, when the split ended.<sup id="cite_ref-HOP2_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HOP2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Compton had a reputation for being a lax Speaker, once telling an MP who complained of being interrupted, "No sir, you have a right to speak, but the House have a right to judge whether they will hear you."
</p><p>When Walpole became the leading minister of the day in 1721 there was speculation about his future should <a href="/wiki/George_I_of_Great_Britain" title="George I of Great Britain">George I</a> pass away and be succeeded by his son, who was more favourably inclined towards Compton than Walpole and declared that he would replace the latter with the former on accession. In order to avoid this, Walpole sought to keep Compton on the margins of government, though he was appointed as <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a>, a very lucrative post, from 1722 until 1730. In 1725, Compton entered Walpole's government as <a href="/wiki/Lord_Privy_Seal" title="Lord Privy Seal">Lord Privy Seal</a> and was also created a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Bath" title="Order of the Bath">Knight of the Bath</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HOP2_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HOP2-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Accession_of_George_II">Accession of George II</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Accession of George II">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>

<p>In 1727, George II succeeded to the throne and sought to bring about the change in leadership he had promised.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> However, Compton was not perceived as a man of great ability. He was described by a contemporary as "a plodding, heavy fellow, with great application but no talents".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In particular he proved unable to compete with Walpole's proposals for an allowance for the king. At a meeting between the three, Compton declared he was not up to the task of government.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He maintained a hatred of Walpole for the humiliation. With this passed his last serious chance of holding real control over policy, and his influence sharply declined as a result.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="House_of_Lords_and_Patriot_Whigs">House of Lords and Patriot Whigs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: House of Lords and Patriot Whigs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Patriot_Whigs" title="Patriot Whigs">Patriot Whigs</a></div>
<p>In order to remove him from the Commons, Walpole raised Compton to the peerage as <b>Baron Wilmington</b>, of <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_East_Sussex" class="mw-redirect" title="Wilmington, East Sussex">Wilmington</a> in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Sussex" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Sussex">County of Sussex</a> on 8 January 1728;<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> two years later, on 14 May 1730, he was created <b>Viscount Pevensey</b>, of <a href="/wiki/Pevensey" title="Pevensey">Pevensey</a> in the County of Sussex and <b>Earl of Wilmington</b><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and was appointed <a href="/wiki/Lord_President_of_the_Council" title="Lord President of the Council">Lord President of the Council</a> in December of that year. He became increasingly associated with the <a href="/wiki/Patriot_Whigs" title="Patriot Whigs">Patriot Whigs</a>, those most critical of Walpole, but in Parliament generally stuck to the official line of the ministry. In 1730 he attempted to form a coalition between the <a href="/wiki/Patriot_Whigs" title="Patriot Whigs">Patriot Whigs</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Hanoverian_Tories" class="mw-redirect" title="Hanoverian Tories">Hanoverian Tories</a> to bring down Walpole, but this failed and he continued in office.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBlack201469_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlack201469-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> However, during the <a href="/wiki/Excise_Crisis" class="mw-redirect" title="Excise Crisis">Excise Crisis</a> of 1733, he failed to carry through a threat to resign, after being bought off with the promise to make him a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Garter" title="Order of the Garter">Knight of the Garter</a>, which he duly was. This further weakened any following he still commanded. He served as Lord President until 1742.
</p><p>He was involved in the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Foundling_Hospital" title="Foundling Hospital">Foundling Hospital</a> in 1739, which was an <a href="/wiki/Orphanage" title="Orphanage">orphanage</a> for <a href="/wiki/Child_abandonment" title="Child abandonment">abandoned children</a>. This charity became the capital's most popular way to prove one's <a href="/wiki/Philanthropy" title="Philanthropy">philanthropic</a> credentials and had very distinguished Board members (including Wilmington).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister">Prime Minister<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Prime Minister">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Carteret_ministry" title="Carteret ministry">Carteret ministry</a></div>

<p>In January 1742, he succeeded Walpole as <a href="/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury" title="First Lord of the Treasury">First Lord of the Treasury</a> and head of the <a href="/wiki/Carteret_ministry" title="Carteret ministry">Carteret ministry</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Wilmington's time in office was short and undistinguished and he was a poor leader. His opposition controlled the House of Commons so his direct influence was greatly limited. His brief premiership was dominated by dealing with foreign affairs, particularly in the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession" title="War of the Austrian Succession">War of the Austrian Succession</a>, fighting the forces of Prussia France and Spain.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> His strong work ethic took its toll, and his health gradually deteriorated. He remained in office until his death in 1743, when he was succeeded by the <a href="/wiki/Paymaster_of_the_Forces" title="Paymaster of the Forces">Paymaster of the Forces</a>, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Pelham" title="Henry Pelham">Henry Pelham</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Private_life">Private life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Private life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Compton_Place,_Eastbourne_(Geograph_Image_1278100).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Compton_Place%2C_Eastbourne_%28Geograph_Image_1278100%29.jpg/220px-Compton_Place%2C_Eastbourne_%28Geograph_Image_1278100%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Compton_Place%2C_Eastbourne_%28Geograph_Image_1278100%29.jpg/330px-Compton_Place%2C_Eastbourne_%28Geograph_Image_1278100%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Compton_Place%2C_Eastbourne_%28Geograph_Image_1278100%29.jpg/440px-Compton_Place%2C_Eastbourne_%28Geograph_Image_1278100%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="428" /></a><figcaption>Compton Place, Eastbourne, in 2009</figcaption></figure>
<p>He bought the East Borne estate in Eastbourne, Sussex in 1724 and renamed it <a href="/wiki/Compton_Place" title="Compton Place">Compton Place</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He engaged the architect <a href="/wiki/Colen_Campbell" title="Colen Campbell">Colen Campbell</a> (and after Campbell's death <a href="/wiki/William_Kent" title="William Kent">William Kent</a>) to rebuild the house. It was completed in 1731.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>He never married and died without issue, therefore all his titles became extinct upon his death. Over 1110 items from his "large and valuable library" were auctioned by <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Cock_(auctioneer)" title="Christopher Cock (auctioneer)">Christopher Cock</a> over 10 evenings, from to 27 February to 7 March 1733.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was buried at the <a href="/wiki/Family_seat" title="Family seat">family seat</a> of <a href="/wiki/Compton_Wynyates" title="Compton Wynyates">Compton Wynyates</a> in <a href="/wiki/Warwickshire" title="Warwickshire">Warwickshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CompODNB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CompODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Compton Place passed to his nephew, <a href="/wiki/James_Compton,_5th_Earl_of_Northampton" title="James Compton, 5th Earl of Northampton">James Compton, 5th Earl of Northampton</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Spencer_Compton,_1st_Earl_of_Wilmington&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The cities of <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware" title="Wilmington, Delaware">Wilmington, Delaware</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina" title="Wilmington, North Carolina">Wilmington, North Carolina</a>; the towns of <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_Massachusetts" title="Wilmington, Massachusetts">Wilmington, Massachusetts</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_Vermont" title="Wilmington, Vermont">Wilmington, Vermont</a>; and, secondarily, the neighbourhood of <a href="/wiki/Wilmington,_Los_Angeles" title="Wilmington, Los Angeles">Wilmington, Los Angeles</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> are named in his honour. In Wilmington, Delaware, the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Compton_Towers&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Compton Towers (page does not exist)">Compton Towers</a> housing project also bears his name.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
