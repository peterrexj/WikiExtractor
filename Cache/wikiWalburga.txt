<p><b>Walpurga</b> or <b>Walburga</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Wealdburg</i></span>, <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Valpurga, Walpurga, Walpurgis</i>, <a href="/wiki/Swedish_language" title="Swedish language">Swedish</a>: <i lang="sv">Valborg</i>; c. AD 710&#160;&#8211;&#32;25 February 777 or 779), also spelled <b>Valderburg</b> or <b>Guibor</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_mission" title="Anglo-Saxon mission">Anglo-Saxon missionary</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Empire">Frankish Empire</a>. She was canonized on 1 May c. 870 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_II" title="Pope Adrian II">Pope Adrian II</a>.  <a href="/wiki/Walpurgis_Night" title="Walpurgis Night">Saint Walpurgis Night</a> (or "Sankt Walpurgisnacht") is the name for the eve of her feast day in the Medieval period, which coincided with <a href="/wiki/May_Day" title="May Day">May Day</a>; her feast is no longer celebrated on that day, but the name is still used for May Eve.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walpurga was born in <a href="/wiki/Dumnonia" title="Dumnonia">Dumnonia</a>, roughly corresponding to modern <a href="/wiki/Devon" title="Devon">Devon</a>, during the period it was becoming incorporated into <a href="/wiki/Anglo_Saxon_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo Saxon England">Anglo Saxon England</a>. She was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Richard_the_Pilgrim" title="Richard the Pilgrim">Richard the Pilgrim</a>, a likely <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Britons" title="Celtic Britons">Britonnic</a> underking of the West Saxons, and of <a href="/wiki/Wuna_of_Wessex" title="Wuna of Wessex">Wuna of Wessex</a>, and had two brothers, <a href="/wiki/Willibald" title="Willibald">Willibald</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winibald" title="Winibald">Winibald</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_career">Religious career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Religious career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg/220px-Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg/330px-Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg/440px-Statue_of_St_Walpurga_.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3036" data-file-height="4048" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Walburgis at Walburgis Kapelle at <a href="/wiki/Kirchehrenbach" title="Kirchehrenbach">Kirchehrenbach</a>, Germany</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 721 Richard set out on a pilgrimage to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> with his two sons. Before leaving he entrusted Walburga, then 11 years old, to the abbess of the <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Wimborne_Minster_(church)" title="Wimborne Minster (church)">Wimborne Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Dorset" title="Dorset">Dorset</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-abbey_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abbey-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She had been there but a year, when she received word that her father had died at <a href="/wiki/Lucca" title="Lucca">Lucca</a>. After seeing to their father's burial in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Frediano" title="Basilica of San Frediano">Basilica of San Frediano</a>, her brothers completed the pilgrimage to Rome, where they both became seriously ill. (<a href="/wiki/Hygeburg" title="Hygeburg">Hygeburg</a>, who wrote the <i>Vita S. Willibaldi</i>, says they contracted the Black Death; Francis Mershman suggests <a href="/wiki/Malaria" title="Malaria">malaria</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After recovering, Winibald, who was not of a particularly strong constitution, remained at Rome to pursue further studies, while Willibald set out for the Holy Land. After about seven years of travelling, Willibald returned to Italy and became a monk at <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>. In 730, Winibald returned to England and engaged a third brother and several amongst his kindred and acquaintance to accompany him in his journey back to Rome to begin a monastic life there.<sup id="cite_ref-Stevens_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stevens-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During this time Walpurga remained at Wimborne where she was educated; and in the course of time became a nun. The nuns of Wimborne were skilled at copying and ornamenting manuscripts; and celebrated for <a href="/wiki/Opus_Anglicanum" title="Opus Anglicanum">Opus Anglicanum</a>, a fine needlework utilising gold and silver threads on rich <a href="/wiki/Velvet" title="Velvet">velvet</a> or linen, often decorated with <a href="/wiki/Gemstone" title="Gemstone">jewels</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pearl" title="Pearl">pearls</a>. Such English embroidery was in great demand across Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> She spent 26 years as a member of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-casanova_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-casanova-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 737, Walpurga's uncle (her mother's brother), <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface</a>, was in Rome and recruited his nephews to assist him in his religious work in Germany. Winnebald arrived in <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a> on 30 November, 740, and after being ordained a priest,<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> was placed in charge of seven churches.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Willibald, upon arriving at <a href="/wiki/Eichst%C3%A4tt" title="Eichstätt">Eichstätt</a>, was ordained by Boniface on 22 July, 741 and began missionary work in the area.
</p><p>Walpurga then travelled with her brothers, Willibald and Winibald, to <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a> (now <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg" title="Württemberg">Württemberg</a> and <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconia</a>) to assist Boniface in evangelizing the still-pagan Germans. Because of her rigorous training, she was able to write her brother Winibald's <i><a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">vita</a></i> and an account in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> of his travels in <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>. As a result, she is often called the first female author of both England and Germany.<sup id="cite_ref-casanova_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-casanova-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Walpurga became a nun in the <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Heidenheim_am_Hahnenkamm" class="mw-redirect" title="Heidenheim am Hahnenkamm">Heidenheim am Hahnenkamm</a>, which was founded by Willibald. He appointed her as his successor and following his death in 751, Walpurga became the abbess of the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-abbey_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abbey-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Upon Winibald's death in 760 she also succeeded him as superintendent of the Heidenheim monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walpurga died on 25 February 777 or 779 (the records are unclear) and was buried at Heidenheim; the day carries her name in the Catholic church calendar. In 870, Walpurga's remains were transferred to <a href="/wiki/Eichst%C3%A4tt" title="Eichstätt">Eichstätt</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sweden" title="Sweden">Sweden</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>, her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> commemorates the transfer of her relics on 1 May.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg/220px-Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg/330px-Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg/440px-Brugge_-_Sint-Walburgakerk_3.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1728" data-file-height="2304" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/St._Walburga_Church_(Bruges)" title="St. Walburga Church (Bruges)">St. Walburga Church</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bruges" title="Bruges">Bruges</a> was originally a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> church</figcaption></figure>
<p>Walpurga's feast day is 25 February, but the day of her canonization, 1 May (possibly 870), was also celebrated during the high medieval period, especially in the 11th century under <a href="/wiki/Anno_II,_Archbishop_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="Anno II, Archbishop of Cologne">Anno II, Archbishop of Cologne</a>, so that <a href="/wiki/Walpurgis_Night" title="Walpurgis Night">Walpurgis Night</a> is the eve of <a href="/wiki/May_Day" title="May Day">May Day</a>, celebrated in continental folklore with dancing.
</p><p>At Eichstätt, her bones were placed in a rocky niche, which allegedly began to exude a miraculously therapeutic oil, which drew pilgrims to her shrine.
</p><p>The two earliest <a href="/wiki/Hagiology" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiology">miracle narratives</a> of Walpurga are the <i>Miracula S. Walburgae Manheimensis</i> by Wolfhard von <a href="/wiki/Herrieden" title="Herrieden">Herrieden</a>, datable to 895 or 896, and the late 10th-century <i>Vita secunda</i> linked with the name of Aselbod, bishop of Utrecht. In the 14th-century <i>Vita S. Walburgae</i> by Phillipp von Rathsamhaüsen, bishop of Eichstätt (1306–22), the miracle of the tempest-tossed boat is introduced, which <a href="/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens" title="Peter Paul Rubens">Peter Paul Rubens</a> painted in 1610 for the altarpiece for the church of St. Walpurgis, Antwerp.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In addition, the 19th-century <a href="/wiki/John_Henry_Newman" title="John Henry Newman">Cardinal Newman</a> declared the exuded oil to be a credible miracle.<sup id="cite_ref-EB1911_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB1911-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:WalbKont1.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/WalbKont1.JPG/220px-WalbKont1.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="221" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/WalbKont1.JPG/330px-WalbKont1.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/WalbKont1.JPG/440px-WalbKont1.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2163" data-file-height="2171" /></a><figcaption>Statue in <a href="/wiki/Contern" title="Contern">Contern</a> church.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The earliest representation of Walpurga is in the early 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Hitda_Codex" title="Hitda Codex">Hitda Codex</a>, made in <a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>, and depicts her holding stylized stalks of grain. In other depictions, the object has been called a <a href="/wiki/Palm_branch_(symbol)" class="mw-redirect" title="Palm branch (symbol)">palm branch</a> which is not correct, since Walpurga was not <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a>. The grain attribute has been interpreted as an instance of a Christian saint (Walpurga) coming to represent an older pagan concept; in this case, the pagan <a href="/wiki/Corn_Mother" class="mw-redirect" title="Corn Mother">Grain Mother</a>. Peasant farmers fashioned her replica in a <a href="/wiki/Corn_dolly" title="Corn dolly">corn dolly</a> at harvest time and told tales to explain Saint Walpurga's presence in the grain sheaf.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Walpurga is the patroness of <a href="/wiki/Eichst%C3%A4tt" title="Eichstätt">Eichstätt</a> and Weilburg, Germany; <a href="/wiki/Oudenarde" class="mw-redirect" title="Oudenarde">Oudenarde</a>, <a href="/wiki/Veurne" title="Veurne">Veurne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a>, Belgium; <a href="/wiki/Tiel" title="Tiel">Tiel</a> (demolished), <a href="/wiki/Groningen" title="Groningen">Groningen</a> (demolished), <a href="/wiki/Arnhem" title="Arnhem">Arnhem</a>, <a href="/wiki/Amby_(Maastricht)" title="Amby (Maastricht)">Amby</a> and <a href="/wiki/Zutphen" title="Zutphen">Zutphen</a> the Netherlands; and she is invoked as special patroness against hydrophobia, in storms, and also by sailors.<sup id="cite_ref-casanova_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-casanova-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Walburga's Abbey is located at Eichstätt, Bavaria. A second Benedictine Abbey of St. Walburga is located in <a href="/wiki/Virginia_Dale,_Colorado" title="Virginia Dale, Colorado">Virginia Dale, Colorado</a>, near the Wyoming border. The abbey<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag may need clarification or removal of jargon. (March 2023)">clarification needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> is home to approximately 20 contemplative Catholic nuns and also has a retreat center.<sup id="cite_ref-abbey_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-abbey-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Sisters_of_St._Walburg_Monastery" title="Benedictine Sisters of St. Walburg Monastery">St. Walburg Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Covington,_Kentucky" title="Covington, Kentucky">Covington, Kentucky</a>, is a community of Benedictine sisters who arrived in Northern Kentucky in 1859. Their early teaching efforts later resulted in the establishment and development of Villa Madonna Academy and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More_University" title="Thomas More University">Thomas More University</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Walburge,_Preston" title="Church of St Walburge, Preston">Church of St. Walburge</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> church in <a href="/wiki/Preston,_Lancashire" title="Preston, Lancashire">Preston</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lancashire" title="Lancashire">Lancashire</a>, England, is a church famous for its spire. At 309 feet (94&#160;m), the spire is the tallest of any parish church in England, with only the spires of <a href="/wiki/Salisbury_Cathedral" title="Salisbury Cathedral">Salisbury</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norwich_Cathedral" title="Norwich Cathedral">Norwich Cathedrals</a> reaching higher.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/St._Walburg%27s_Hospital" title="St. Walburg&#39;s Hospital">St. Walburg's Hospital</a>, a 220-bed hospital in southern Tanzania was built in 1959.
</p>
<h2><span id="St._Walburga_Church_in_Antwerp_.28Belgium.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Walburga_Church_in_Antwerp_(Belgium)">St. Walburga Church in Antwerp (Belgium)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Walpurga&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: St. Walburga Church in Antwerp (Belgium)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Central in the first fortified city of <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a>, from the 11th century, was the church dedicated to Saint Walburga. Under French occupation in 1798, the church was confiscated and sold; it served as a warehouse. In 1816, the Dutch government confiscated the church building, and in 1817, it was demolished. The city mayor and aldermen decided to erect a statue of <a href="/wiki/Pieter_Paul_Rubens" class="mw-redirect" title="Pieter Paul Rubens">Pieter Paul Rubens</a> on the burg square left after the demolition. In 1880, when the (current) Scheldt quais were built, most of the area of the first fortified city from the 11th century was demolished and even the foundations of the St. Walburga Church disappeared, and the statue was moved to the (current) Groenplaats.<br />Some parts of the interior of that ancient church, which actively served for more than 700 years, were recovered: the altar piece painting <a href="/wiki/The_Elevation_of_the_Cross_(Rubens)" title="The Elevation of the Cross (Rubens)">The Elevation of the Cross (Rubens)</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Predella" title="Predella">predella</a> (foot of the altar) have been reused in the main altar of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Our_Lady_(Antwerp)" title="Cathedral of Our Lady (Antwerp)">Cathedral of Our Lady (Antwerp)</a>. Another altar was moved to the <a href="https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heikese_kerk" class="extiw" title="nl:Heikese kerk">nl:Heikese kerk</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tilburg" title="Tilburg">Tilburg</a> where it serves as the main altar.<br />In 1936, the city master builder (architect) <a href="https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flor_Van_Reeth" class="extiw" title="nl:Flor Van Reeth">nl:Flor Van Reeth</a> constructed a new modernistic church building with the same name on the Volkstraat near <a href="https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuid_%E2%80%93_Museum" class="extiw" title="nl:Zuid – Museum">Het Zuid</a>. This building was declared a monument 1995 and was restored in 2007.
</p>
