<p><b>Sergius</b> (or <b>Serge</b>) <b>and Bacchus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: Σέργιος &amp; Βάκχος), were fourth-century <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> soldiers revered as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> and <a href="/wiki/Military_saint" title="Military saint">military saints</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy" title="Eastern Orthodoxy">Eastern Orthodox</a> and <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodoxy">Oriental Orthodox</a> Churches. Their <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 7 October.
</p><p>According to their <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, Sergius and Bacchus were officers in <a href="/wiki/Galerius" title="Galerius">Galerius</a>' army, and were held high in his favor until they were exposed as secret Christians. They were then severely punished, with Bacchus dying during torture, and Sergius eventually beheaded.
</p><p>Sergius and Bacchus were very popular throughout <a href="/wiki/Late_Antiquity" class="mw-redirect" title="Late Antiquity">Late Antiquity</a>, and churches in their honor were built in several cities, including <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> and <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. The close friendship between the two is strongly emphasized in their hagiographies and traditions, making them one of the most famous examples of paired saints.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sergius_and_Bacchus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sergius_and_Bacchus_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Sergius_and_Bacchus_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/220px-Sergius_and_Bacchus_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="154" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Sergius_and_Bacchus_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/330px-Sergius_and_Bacchus_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/Sergius_and_Bacchus_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/440px-Sergius_and_Bacchus_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1089" data-file-height="763" /></a><figcaption>Martyrs Saints Sergius and Bacchus</figcaption></figure>
<p>The story of Sergius and Bacchus is told in the <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> text known as <i>The Passion of Sergius and Bacchus</i>. The story is ostensibly set during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Roman_emperor" title="Roman emperor">Roman emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Galerius" title="Galerius">Galerius</a> (305 to 311), though it contains a number of contradictions and anachronisms that make dating difficult. The work itself may date to the mid-5th century.<sup id="cite_ref-Woods_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woods-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the text, Sergius and Bacchus were Roman citizens and high-ranking officers of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a>, but their conversion to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> was discovered when they attempted to avoid accompanying a Roman official into a <a href="/wiki/Roman_temple" title="Roman temple">pagan temple</a> with the rest of his bodyguard. After they persisted in refusing to sacrifice to <a href="/wiki/Jupiter_(mythology)" title="Jupiter (mythology)">Jupiter</a> in Galerius's presence, they were publicly humiliated by being chained, dressed in female attire and paraded around town. Galerius then sent them to <a href="/wiki/Barbalissos" title="Barbalissos">Barbalissos</a> in <a href="/wiki/Roman_Mesopotamia" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Mesopotamia">Mesopotamia</a> to be tried by Antiochus, the military commander there and an old friend of Sergius. Antiochus could not convince them to give up their faith, however, and Bacchus was beaten to death. The next day, Bacchus's spirit appeared to Sergius and encouraged him to remain strong so they could be together forever. Over the next days, Sergius was also brutally tortured and finally executed at <a href="/wiki/Resafa" title="Resafa">Resafa</a>, where his death was marked by miraculous happenings.<sup id="cite_ref-Woods_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woods-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Popularity_and_veneration">Popularity and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sergius_and_Bacchus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Popularity and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Resafa,BasilikaA1.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Resafa%2CBasilikaA1.jpg/220px-Resafa%2CBasilikaA1.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Resafa%2CBasilikaA1.jpg/330px-Resafa%2CBasilikaA1.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a9/Resafa%2CBasilikaA1.jpg/440px-Resafa%2CBasilikaA1.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1920" data-file-height="1286" /></a><figcaption>Basilica of Saint Sergius, <a href="/wiki/Rasafa" class="mw-redirect" title="Rasafa">Rasafa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg/220px-26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="155" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg/330px-26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg/440px-26600-%DA%A9%D9%84%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%A7%DB%8C_%D8%A2%D8%B4%D9%88%D8%B1%DB%8C_%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D9%88%D8%B1%DA%AF%DB%8C%D8%B2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4848" data-file-height="3408" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East" title="Assyrian Church of the East">Assyrian Church of the East</a> of Mar Sarkis (Saint Sergius), <a href="/wiki/Tehran" title="Tehran">Tehran</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Veneration of the two saints dates to the fifth century. A shrine to Sergius was built in <a href="/wiki/Resafa" title="Resafa">Resafa</a> (renamed Sergiopolis around 425), but there is no certain evidence for his or Bacchus' cult much older than that. Their cult grew rapidly during the early fifth century, in accordance with the growth of the cult of martyrs, especially military martyrs, during the period. The Resafa shrine was constructed of <a href="/wiki/Mudbrick" title="Mudbrick">mudbrick</a>, evidently at the behest of bishop <a href="/wiki/Alexander_of_Hierapolis_(Syria)" title="Alexander of Hierapolis (Syria)">Alexander of Hierapolis</a>. The <i>Passion</i> has been dated to the mid-5th century on the grounds that it describes the construction of such a shrine as if it were a relatively recent occurrence. The original shrine was replaced with a sturdier stone structure in 518; this new site was patronized by important political figures including Roman emperor <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian I</a>, emperor <a href="/wiki/Khosrow_II" title="Khosrow II">Khosrow II</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Sassanid_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Sassanid Empire">Sassanid Empire</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Al-Mundhir_III_ibn_al-Harith" title="Al-Mundhir III ibn al-Harith">al-Mundhir III ibn al-Harith</a>, ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Ghassanids" title="Ghassanids">Ghassanids</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Woods_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woods-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Traditionally, the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Sergius and Bacchus has been celebrated on 7 October in the West.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> they shared the day with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Mark" title="Pope Mark">Pope Mark</a> and the martyred pair <a href="/wiki/Marcellus_and_Apuleius" title="Marcellus and Apuleius">Marcellus and Apuleius</a>. In 1716, this day became the feast of <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Rosary" title="Our Lady of the Rosary">Our Lady of the Rosary</a>, and the commemoration of Sergius, Bacchus and the other saints was moved to 8 October. They were restored to 7 October in 1969.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG/220px-Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG/330px-Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG/440px-Sergius_and_Bacchus_Church_February_2011.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4508" data-file-height="3007" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Little_Hagia_Sophia" title="Little Hagia Sophia">Little Hagia Sophia</a> (Church of the Saints Sergius and Bacchus), <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>, they were venerated as protectors of the army. A large monastery church, the <a href="/wiki/Little_Hagia_Sophia" title="Little Hagia Sophia">Little Hagia Sophia</a>, was dedicated to them in Constantinople by Justinian I, probably in 527. According to legend, during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Justin_I" title="Justin I">Justin I</a>, his nephew <a href="/wiki/Justinian_I" title="Justinian I">Justinian</a> had been accused of plotting against the throne and was sentenced to death, which was reversed after Saints Sergius and Bacchus appeared before Justin and vouched for Justinian's innocence. He was freed and restored to his title of <a href="/wiki/Caesar_(title)" title="Caesar (title)">Caesar</a>. Justinian, in gratitude, vowed that he would dedicate a church to the saints once he became emperor. The construction of this <a href="/wiki/Little_Hagia_Sophia" title="Little Hagia Sophia">Church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus</a>, between 527 and 536 AD (only a short time before the erection of the <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a> between 532 and 537), was one of the first acts of the reign of Justinian I.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG/220px-Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG/330px-Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG/440px-Ss._Sergio_e_Bacco_exterior.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3000" data-file-height="4000" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Santi_Sergio_e_Bacco" title="Santi Sergio e Bacco">Church of Saints Sergius and Bacchus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Sergius was a very popular saint in Syria and Christian <a href="/wiki/Arabia" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabia">Arabia</a>. The city of Resafa, which became a bishop's see, took the name Sergiopolis and preserved his relics in a fortified <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a>. Resafa was improved by Emperor Justinian and became one of the greatest pilgrimage centers in the East. Many other churches were built dedicated in the name of Sergius, sometimes with Bacchus. A church dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Santi_Sergio_e_Bacco" title="Santi Sergio e Bacco">Santi Sergio e Bacco</a> was built in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in the 9th century. Christian art represents the two saints as soldiers in military garb with branches of palm in their hands. Their feast is observed on 7 October, and a Mass is assigned to them in the "<a href="/wiki/Gelasian_Sacramentary" title="Gelasian Sacramentary">Sacramentarium</a>" of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Pope Gelasius</a>. The nomads of the desert looked upon Sergius as their special <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Church">Armenian Church</a> traditions, Sergius, or Sarkis, was venerated as a Christian general in the Roman army. He was martyred with his son, Martyros, for witnessing to their faith in Christ. The feast is preceded by three-day fasting where most abstain of food and water.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SerBac.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/8/8f/SerBac.jpg/220px-SerBac.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="273" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/8/8f/SerBac.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="264" data-file-height="327" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Robert_Lentz" title="Robert Lentz">Robert Lentz</a>'s 1994 <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of Saints Sergius and Bacchus</figcaption></figure>
<p>The close friendship between the two is strongly emphasized in their hagiographies and traditions, making them one of the most famous examples of paired saints; the scholar <a href="/wiki/John_Boswell" title="John Boswell">John Boswell</a> considers them to be the most influential set of such an archetype, more so than even <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">Saints Peter and Paul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In his <i><a href="/wiki/Same-Sex_Unions_in_Pre-Modern_Europe" title="Same-Sex Unions in Pre-Modern Europe">Same-Sex Unions in Premodern Europe</a></i>, Boswell further argues that Sergius and Bacchus' relationship can be understood as having a romantic dimension, noting that the oldest text of their martyrology describes them as <i>erastai</i>, which can be translated as "lovers".<sup id="cite_ref-Boswell_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boswell-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He suggested that the two were even united in a rite known as <i><a href="/wiki/Adelphopoiesis" title="Adelphopoiesis">adelphopoiesis</a></i> or "brother-making", which he argued was a type of early Christian <a href="/wiki/Same-sex_union" class="mw-redirect" title="Same-sex union">same-sex union</a> or blessing, reinforcing his view of tolerant <a href="/wiki/History_of_Christianity_and_homosexuality" title="History of Christianity and homosexuality">early Christian attitudes toward homosexuality</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Boswell_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Boswell-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Boswell's methodology and conclusions have been disputed by many historians.<sup id="cite_ref-Woods_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woods-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Shaw_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Shaw-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Regardless, in the wake of Boswell's work, Sergius and Bacchus have become popularly venerated in the gay Christian community.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> A 1994 <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of Sergius and Bacchus by the gay <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> iconographer <a href="/wiki/Robert_Lentz" title="Robert Lentz">Robert Lentz</a>, first displayed at Chicago's Gay Pride Parade, has become a popular gay symbol.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Historicity">Historicity</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sergius_and_Bacchus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Historicity">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><i>The Passion of Sergius and Bacchus</i> has been dismissed as an unreliable historical source. It has been dated to mid-5th century and there is no evidence for the cult of Sergius and Bacchus before 425, over a century after they are said to have died. There is no evidence for Sergius and Bacchus's <i><a href="/wiki/Scholae_Palatinae#Origins" title="Scholae Palatinae">schola gentilium</a></i> having been used by Galerius or any other emperor before <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a>, given that the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians" title="Persecution of Christians">persecution of Christians</a> had begun in the army considerably before the 4th century.<sup id="cite_ref-Woods_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Woods-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Christopher Walter considers Sergius analogous to <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a>, "whose historicity is accepted, even if nothing genuine about his life is known." He accepts that information in the passion is accurate.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Italian scholar Pio Franchi de Cavalieri has put forth that <i>The Passion of Sergius and Bacchus</i> was based on an earlier lost passion of <a href="/wiki/Juventinus_and_Maximinus" title="Juventinus and Maximinus">Juventinus and Maximinus</a>, two saints martyred under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a> in 363. He noted especially that the punishment of being paraded around in women's clothes reflected the treatment of Christian soldiers by Julian.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
