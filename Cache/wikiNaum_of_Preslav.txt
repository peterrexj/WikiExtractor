<p><b>Saint Naum</b> (<a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_language" title="Bulgarian language">Bulgarian</a> and <a href="/wiki/Macedonian_language" title="Macedonian language">Macedonian</a>: Свети Наум, <i>Sveti Naum</i>), also known as <b>Naum of Ohrid</b> or <b>Naum of Preslav</b> (c. 830 – December 23, 910) was a medieval <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgarian</a> writer, enlightener, one of the <a href="/wiki/Seven_Apostles_of_Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Seven Apostles of Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Seven Apostles</a> of the <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">First Bulgarian Empire</a> and missionary among the <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He was among the disciples of <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints Cyril and Methodius">Saints Cyril and Methodius</a> and is associated with the creation of the <a href="/wiki/Glagolitic_script" title="Glagolitic script">Glagolitic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic script</a>. Naum was among the founders of the <a href="/wiki/Pliska_Literary_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Pliska Literary School">Pliska Literary School</a>. Afterwards Naum worked at the <a href="/wiki/Ohrid_Literary_School" title="Ohrid Literary School">Ohrid Literary School</a>. He was among the first saints declared by the <a href="/wiki/Bulgarian_Orthodox_Church" title="Bulgarian Orthodox Church">Bulgarian Orthodox Church</a> after its foundation in the 9th century. The mission of Saint Naum played significant role by transformation of the local Slavs into Bulgarians.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Naum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Information about his early life is scarce. According to the Second Life of Saint Naum he grew up in <a href="/wiki/Moesia" title="Moesia">Moesia</a>, which was the Byzantine designation for Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> According to the <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a> of <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Ohrid" title="Clement of Ohrid">Clement of Ohrid</a> by <a href="/wiki/Theophylact_of_Ohrid" title="Theophylact of Ohrid">Theophylact of Ohrid</a> and some other sources, Naum took part in the historic mission to <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a> together with <a href="/wiki/Saints_Cyril_and_Methodius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints Cyril and Methodius">Saints Cyril and Methodius</a>, Clement, Angelarius, Gorazd and other <a href="/wiki/Slavs" title="Slavs">Slavic</a> missionaries in 863.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Great_Moravia_and_Lower_Panonia">Great Moravia and Lower Panonia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Naum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Great Moravia and Lower Panonia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>For the next 22 years, he worked with Cyril and Methodius and other missionaries in translating the <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Bible</a> into <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Old Church Slavonic</a> and promoted it in <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Slavs#Principality" class="mw-redirect" title="Pannonian Slavs">Principality of Lower Pannonia</a>. In 867 or 868 he became a priest in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, ordained along with two other disciples of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Cyril and Methodius</a>, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Gorazd&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Gorazd (page does not exist)">Saint Gorazd</a> and <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Ohrid" title="Clement of Ohrid">Saint Clement of Ohrid</a>, by bishops <a href="/wiki/Pope_Formosus" title="Pope Formosus">Formosus</a> and Gauderic. For the purpose of the mission to Moravia, the missionaries devised the <a href="/wiki/Glagolitic_alphabet" class="mw-redirect" title="Glagolitic alphabet">Glagolitic alphabet</a>, the first alphabet to match the specific features of the Slavic language. Its descendant script, <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic</a>, is still used by many languages today. The missionaries also wrote the first Slavic Civil Code, which was used in Great Moravia. However, the missionary work ran into opposition from <a href="/wiki/Germans" title="Germans">German</a> clerics who opposed their efforts to create a Slavic liturgy. By 885, the two main patrons for the missionaries, <a href="/wiki/Rastislav_of_Moravia" title="Rastislav of Moravia">Rastislav of Moravia</a> of <a href="/wiki/Great_Moravia" title="Great Moravia">Great Moravia</a> and Prince <a href="/wiki/Koce%C4%BE" class="mw-redirect" title="Koceľ">Koceľ</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pannonian_Slavs#Principality" class="mw-redirect" title="Pannonian Slavs">Lower Pannonia</a>, as well as Cyril and Methodius had died, and the pressure from the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Germany" title="Catholic Church in Germany">German Church</a> became increasingly more hostile. After a brief period of imprisonment due to the ongoing conflict with the German clerics, Naum, together with some of the missionaries headed to Bulgaria.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="First_Bulgarian_Empire">First Bulgarian Empire</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Naum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: First Bulgarian Empire">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 886 the governor of <a href="/wiki/Belgrade" title="Belgrade">Belgrade</a>, then in <a href="/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire" title="First Bulgarian Empire">Bulgaria</a>, welcomed the disciples of <a href="/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius" title="Cyril and Methodius">Cyril and Methodius</a>. Bulgaria was ruled then by <a href="/wiki/Tsar_Boris" class="mw-redirect" title="Tsar Boris">Tsar Boris</a>, who converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> in 864. After the <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">christianization</a> the religious ceremonies were conducted in <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> by a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Greeks" title="Byzantine Greeks">Byzantine</a> clergy. Fearing growing Byzantine influence Boris viewed the adoption of the <a href="/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic" title="Old Church Slavonic">Old Church Slavonic</a> as a way to preserve the political independence of Bulgaria. With such views, Boris made arrangements for the establishment of two literary academies where theology was to be taught in the Slavonic language. The first of the schools was founded in the capital, <a href="/wiki/Pliska" title="Pliska">Pliska</a>, and the second in <a href="/wiki/Ohrid" title="Ohrid">Ohrid</a>, in the region of <a href="/wiki/Kutmichevitsa" title="Kutmichevitsa">Kutmichevitsa</a>. The development of Old Church Slavonic literacy had the effect of preventing the assimilation into the neighboring cultures and promoted the formation of a distinct <a href="/wiki/Bulgarians" title="Bulgarians">Bulgarian</a> identity.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Naum moved initially to the capital Pliska together with <a href="/wiki/Clement_of_Ohrid" title="Clement of Ohrid">Clement</a>, Angelarius and possibly <a href="/wiki/Gorazd" title="Gorazd">Gorazd</a> (according to other sources, Gorazd was already dead by that time). In Bulgaria, he spent the next 25 years of his life. Naum  was one of the founders of the <a href="/wiki/Pliska_Literary_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Pliska Literary School">Pliska Literary School</a> where he worked between 886 and 893. The most reliable first-hand account of the activities at the time in Pliska is "An Account of Letters" (<i><a href="/wiki/O_pismenech" class="mw-redirect" title="O pismenech">O pismenech</a></i>), a treatise on Slavic literacy written in Old Church Slavonic, thought to be composed shortly after 893. The piece calls for the creation of a common Slavic alphabet. In 893, shortly after his rise to power, the new Bulgarian ruler <a href="/wiki/Simeon_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Simeon the Great">Simeon the Great</a>, summoned an ecclesiastical <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Preslav" title="Council of Preslav">council in the new capital</a> Preslav, where Clement was ordained bishop of <a href="/wiki/Debarca" title="Debarca">Drembica</a> and <a href="/wiki/Berat" title="Berat">Velika</a>. To replace Clement in Ohrid, Simeon sent Naum, who until then had been active in Preslav. Afterwards, Naum continued Clement's work at Ohrid, another important <a href="/wiki/Ohrid_Literary_School" title="Ohrid Literary School">centre of Slavic learning</a>. In these years the <a href="/wiki/Cyrillic_script" title="Cyrillic script">Cyrillic script</a> was created in the Preslav literary school,<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and was adopted in Bulgaria, possibly following Naum's initiative.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In 905 Naum founded a monastery on the shores of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Ohrid" title="Lake Ohrid">Lake Ohrid</a>, which later received his name. He died there in 910 and Clement initiated the process of his canonization.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In this way Naum became the first “native” saint of Bulgaria.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cult">Cult</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Naum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cult">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The cults towards saint Naum revered particularly in the region of Ohrid. It spread in the first half of the 18th century with the development of <a href="/wiki/Moscopole" title="Moscopole">Moscopole</a> as a center of Orthodox culture and with the development of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Ohrid" title="Archbishopric of Ohrid">Archbishopric of Ohrid</a>. His name became more popular and reached <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a> and even Vienna. The members of the <a href="/wiki/Bektashi_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Bektashi order">Bektashi order</a> also went on pilgrimage to the monastery of Saint Naum, believing their saint <a href="/wiki/Sar%C4%B1_Salt%C4%B1k" title="Sarı Saltık">Sarı Saltık</a> to be buried in it. Saint Naum's original feast day was December 23, but in 1727 on the authority of the Archbishop of Ohrid, it was changed to June 20.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Naum&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/St._Naum_Peak" title="St. Naum Peak">St. Naum Peak</a> on <a href="/wiki/Livingston_Island" title="Livingston Island">Livingston Island</a> in the <a href="/wiki/South_Shetland_Islands" title="South Shetland Islands">South Shetland Islands</a>, <a href="/wiki/Antarctica" title="Antarctica">Antarctica</a> is named for Saint Naum.
</p>
