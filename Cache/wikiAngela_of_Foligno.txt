<p><b>Angela of Foligno</b> (1248 – 4 January 1309)<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Francis" title="Third Order of Saint Francis">Franciscan tertiary</a> who became known as a <a href="/wiki/Christian_mysticism" title="Christian mysticism">mystic</a> from her extensive writings about her mystical <a href="/wiki/Private_revelation" title="Private revelation">revelations</a>. Due to the respect those writings engendered in the Catholic Church she became known as "Mistress of Theologians".
</p><p>Angela was noted not only for her spiritual writings, but also for founding a religious community which refused to become an <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Enclosed religious order">enclosed religious order</a> so that it might continue her vision of caring for those in need. It is still active.
</p><p>The Catholic Church declared Angela to be a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Her canonization was an “<a href="/wiki/Equivalent_canonization" title="Equivalent canonization">equivalent canonization</a>” (without executing the ordinary judicial process of canonization).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_conversion">Early life and conversion</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_of_Foligno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and conversion">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:AngelaFoligno.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/50/AngelaFoligno.jpg" decoding="async" width="169" height="300" class="mw-file-element" data-file-width="169" data-file-height="300" /></a><figcaption>A <a href="/wiki/Holy_card" title="Holy card">holy card</a> depicting Saint Angela</figcaption></figure>
<p>Angela's birth date, which is not known with certainty, is often listed as 1248. She was born into a wealthy family at <a href="/wiki/Foligno" title="Foligno">Foligno</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>. Married, perhaps at an early age, she had several children. Angela reports that she loved the world and its pleasures.<sup id="cite_ref-robinson_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robinson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Around the age of 40, she reportedly had a vision of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a> and recognized the emptiness of her life.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> From that time, she began to lead a life devoted to higher perfection.
</p><p>Three years later, Angela's mother died, followed, a few months later, by her husband and children.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> With one serving woman, Masazuola, as her companion, she began to divest herself of her possessions and to live as a penitent.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcGinn1998143_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGinn1998143-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Angela joined the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Third Order of St. Francis</a>, probably in 1291.<sup id="cite_ref-robinson_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robinson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She placed herself under the direction of a <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> named Arnoldo, who would serve as her <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works_and_later_life">Works and later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_of_Foligno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works and later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Considered a "great medieval mystic," Angela is said to have received mystical revelations, which she dictated to a scribe in the late 13th century. These accounts are contained in a compilation of two works, usually published under the title <i>Il Libro della Beata Angela da Foligno</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-asu_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-asu-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angela recorded the history of her conversion in her <i>Book of Visions and Instructions</i>. She dictated, in her <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbrian</a> dialect, an account of her spiritual progress, known as the <i>Memoriale</i>, which was transcribed in Latin by a man known as "Brother A." This work was probably begun in 1292.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The <i>Memorial</i> is the first part of two sections of Angela of Foligno's <i>Liber</i>. The second text is known as <i>Instructions</i> and is composed of thirty-six instructional texts, a note about her death, and an epilogue. These texts appear in different orders in different manuscripts, and there is not known to be one correct order.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Foligno088.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Foligno088.jpg/200px-Foligno088.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="151" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Foligno088.jpg/300px-Foligno088.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Foligno088.jpg/400px-Foligno088.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1952" /></a><figcaption>Angela of Foligno, fresco by <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Mancini_(1679%E2%80%931758)" title="Francesco Mancini (1679–1758)">Francesco Mancini</a>, Dome of <a href="/wiki/Foligno_Cathedral" title="Foligno Cathedral">Foligno Cathedral</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Brother A. remained with her until 1296 while she completed the higher and more difficult final ten stages, but since it proved impossible for him to understand these fully, he condensed them into seven ‘supplementary stages’ whose description takes up the larger portion of the <i>Memorial</i>. The text was finished by 1298, and submitted to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=James_of_Colonna&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="James of Colonna (page does not exist)">James of Colonna</a> and eight Friars Minor, who gave it their approval. It seems that Brother A. revised it shortly after, in 1299–1300.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcGinn1998143_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGinn1998143-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Between around 1296 and her death in early 1309, the fame of Angela's sanctity gathered around her a number of other tertiaries, both men and women, who strove under her direction to advance in holiness. Later she established at Foligno a community of other women tertiaries, who added to the Rule of the Third Order a commitment to a common life without, however, binding themselves to <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">enclosure</a>, so that they might devote their lives to works of charity.
</p><p>The final version of the <i>Book</i> appends a series of 36 <i>Instructions</i> to the <i>Memorial</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-robinson_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robinson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> These reflect Angela's teaching during this period. These teachings are rather more conventional in tone and have differences in vocabulary and emphasis from the <i>Memorial</i> – which may reflect redaction by several hands. Nevertheless, the Instructions seem to reflect Angela's teaching, albeit at some remove.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcGinn1998144_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGinn1998144-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
"No one can be saved without divine light. Divine light causes us to begin and to make progress, and it leads us to the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_counsels" title="Evangelical counsels">summit of perfection</a>. Therefore if you want to begin and to receive this divine light, pray. If you have begun to make progress, pray. And if you have reached the summit of perfection, and want to be super-illumined so as to remain in that state, pray. If you want faith, pray. If you want hope, pray. If you want charity, pray. If you want poverty, pray. If you want obedience, pray. If you want chastity, pray. If you want humility, pray. If you want meekness, pray. If you want fortitude, pray. If you want any virtue, pray."  (( from Voices of the Saints, Bert Ghezzi ))
</p>

<p>"And pray in this fashion: always reading the Book of Life, that is, the life of the God-man, Jesus Christ, whose life consisted of poverty, pain, contempt and true obedience." (from Voices of the Saints, Bert Ghezzi)
</p><p>At Christmas 1308, Angela told her companions she would die shortly. A few days later, she had a vision of Christ appearing to her and promising to come personally to take her to heaven. She died in her sleep on 3 January 1309.
</p><p>Angela died surrounded by her community of disciples. Her remains repose in the Church of St. Francis at Foligno. Many people attributed miracles to her, which were accomplished at her tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-robinson_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robinson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angela's authority as a spiritual teacher may be gathered from the fact that <a href="/wiki/Bollandus" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandus">Bollandus</a>, among other testimonials, quotes <a href="/wiki/Maximilian_van_der_Sandt" title="Maximilian van der Sandt">Maximilian van der Sandt</a>, of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, as calling her the "'Mistress of Theologians', whose whole doctrine has been drawn out of the Book of Life, Jesus Christ, Our Lord".<sup id="cite_ref-robinson_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-robinson-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angela_of_Foligno&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a> approved the veneration paid to her over the centuries in his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> of her on 11 July 1701 and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> extended the veneration to all the Church on 9 October 2013, declaring her a saint by <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Equipollent_canonization" title="Canonization">equipollent canonization</a>, recognizing the validity of the long-standing veneration of her.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated by the Third Order of Saint Francis, both Secular and Regular, on 4 January (7 January in the United States). Although the community she founded was not recognized as a <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> until the 20th-century, she is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Religious_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious (Catholicism)">religious</a>.
</p>
