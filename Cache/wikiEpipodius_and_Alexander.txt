					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saints Epipodius and Alexander</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyrs</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Epipodius: <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a><br />Alexander: <a href="/wiki/Phrygia" title="Phrygia">Phrygia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">178</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a>

<p><b>Epipodius</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Épipode</i>) and his companion <b>Alexander</b> (died 178) are venerated as <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saints</a>. Their <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 22 April, and Alexander is additionally commemorated on April 24 in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Epipodius was a native of <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a>; Alexander was said to be a native of <a href="/wiki/Phrygia" title="Phrygia">Phrygia</a>, and a physician by profession.<sup id="cite_ref-DGRBM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DGRBM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> They were both martyred during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius" title="Marcus Aurelius">Marcus Aurelius</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Epipodius_and_Alexander&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The earliest mention of Epipodius and Alexander is in a homily of St. <a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyon" title="Eucherius of Lyon">Eucherius</a>, about 440. Epipodius was born in Lyon and Alexander was a Greek, originally from Phrygia. Of distinguished birth, they were close friends since their childhood schooldays. Epipodius is said to have been a confirmed <a href="/wiki/Celibate" class="mw-redirect" title="Celibate">celibate</a> bachelor, who devoted his time to Christian works.
</p><p>In the aftermath of the <a href="/wiki/Persecution_in_Lyon" title="Persecution in Lyon">Persecution in Lyon</a> in the summer of 177, Epipodius and Alexander, having been denounced as Christians, left the city and retired to a nearby village. There they found refuge in the house of a poor Christian widow situated north-west of the hill of <a href="/wiki/Fourvi%C3%A8re" title="Fourvière">Fourvière</a>. They were betrayed to imperial authorities by a servant. Both men were subsequently imprisoned, tortured, and condemned. According to <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, after enduring torture on the rack, Epipodius, the younger of the two, was beheaded. Alban Butler says that Alexander, after suffering an extended and brutal beating, was crucified and died almost immediately.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Another account says that he died of the beatings and ill treatment in gaol.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Epipodius_and_Alexander&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Christians privately carried off their bodies, and buried them on a hill near the city; which place became famous afterwards for the great number of miracles, which were wrought there.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The tomb was originally outside the walls of the city, but later enclosed within them.  <a href="/wiki/St._Gregory_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory of Tours</a> says, that in the sixth century, their bodies, lay deposited with that of St. Irenæus, in the Church of St. John, now called <a href="/wiki/Saint_Irenaeus_Church,_Lyon" title="Saint Irenaeus Church, Lyon">St. Irenæus</a>, under the altar, where the relics of these two holy martyrs were found 1410.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Epipodius is venerated as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of bachelors, victims of betrayal, and victims of torture.
</p>
