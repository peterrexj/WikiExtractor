<p><b>Ursus of Solothurn</b> was a 3rd-century <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Roman</a> <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christian</a> venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. He was associated very early with the <a href="/wiki/Theban_Legion" title="Theban Legion">Theban Legion</a> and is recorded in the Roman Martyrology, with <a href="/wiki/Victor_of_Solothurn" title="Victor of Solothurn">Victor of Solothurn</a> on 30 September.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ursus_of_Solothurn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Life of Ursus</i> was written by Saint <a href="/wiki/Eucherius_of_Lyon" title="Eucherius of Lyon">Eucherius of Lyon</a> in the 5th century; it recounts that Ursus was tortured and <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> at Solothurn under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Maximian" title="Maximian">Maximian</a> and the governor Hyrtacus  for refusing to worship idols around 286. The legend is classed by <a href="/wiki/Bollandist" title="Bollandist">Bollandist</a> <a href="/wiki/Hippolyte_Delehaye" title="Hippolyte Delehaye">Hippolyte Delehaye</a> among the historical romances.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ursus_of_Solothurn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg/220px-Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="310" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg/330px-Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg/440px-Hans_Holbein_d._J._010.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2024" data-file-height="2856" /></a><figcaption>Solothurn Madonna, Hans Holbein</figcaption></figure>
<p>The first church dedicated to Ursus in Solothurn was probably built after <a href="/wiki/Victor_of_Solothurn" title="Victor of Solothurn">Victor of Solothurn</a>]'s remains were taken to <a href="/wiki/Geneva" title="Geneva">Geneva</a> in the late 7th century. The <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Meerssen" title="Treaty of Meerssen">Treaty of Meerssen</a> of 870 mentions a monastery of St. Ursus in Solothurn.<sup id="cite_ref-Widmer_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Widmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> are displayed in churches throughout Switzerland, and his coffin was found in 1519 under the choir altar of St. Ursen. His feast day is September 30.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ursus_of_Solothurn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ursus is depicted as a soldier in arms, often with his head under his arm.<sup id="cite_ref-Widmer_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Widmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He is depicted in the <a href="/wiki/Solothurn_Madonna" title="Solothurn Madonna">Solothurn Madonna</a> by <a href="/wiki/Hans_Holbein_the_Younger" title="Hans Holbein the Younger">Hans Holbein the Younger</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ursus_of_Solothurn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ursus is the patron of the  <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Solothurn_Cathedral" title="Solothurn Cathedral">cathedral</a> in <a href="/wiki/Solothurn" title="Solothurn">Solothurn</a>, <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, where his body is located. 
</p>
