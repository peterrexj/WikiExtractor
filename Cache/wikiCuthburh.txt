<p>Saint <b>Cuthburh</b> or <b>Cuthburg</b>, <b>Cuthburga</b> (<a href="/wiki/Old_English_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old English language">Old English</a>&#58; <span title="Old English (ca. 450-1100)-language text"><i lang="ang">Cūþburh</i></span>; died 31 August 725) was the first Abbess of <a href="/wiki/Wimborne_Minster_(church)" title="Wimborne Minster (church)">Wimborne Minster</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was the sister of <a href="/wiki/Ine_of_Wessex" title="Ine of Wessex">Ine</a>, King of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> and was married to the <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> king <a href="/wiki/Aldfrith" class="mw-redirect" title="Aldfrith">Aldfrith</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cuthburh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cuthburh was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Cenred_of_Wessex" title="Cenred of Wessex">Cenred of Wessex</a>. In addition to her brother Ine, she also had a brother Ingild, who was an ancestor of <a href="/wiki/Alfred_the_Great" title="Alfred the Great">Alfred the Great</a>, and a sister <a href="/wiki/Cwenburh" title="Cwenburh">Cwenburh</a>. <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her marriage to Aldfrith allied him with Ine, one of the most powerful kings in <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon England">Anglo-Saxon England</a>. Cuthburh was Aldfrith's only known wife. Aldfrith had at least two sons, <a href="/wiki/Osred_I_of_Northumbria" title="Osred I of Northumbria">Osred</a> and Offa, it is believed Cuthburh was the mother of Osred, Offa it is not certain.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It is also believed they were the parents of a daughter <a href="/wiki/Osana" title="Osana">Osana</a>, who would later be known as Saint Osana.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to a report by <a href="/wiki/Florence_of_Worcester" title="Florence of Worcester">Florence of Worcester</a>, written long afterwards, at some time before Aldfrith's death in 705 he and Cuthburh "renounced connubial intercourse for the love of God". Following this, Cuthburh entered Abbess <a href="/wiki/Hildelith" title="Hildelith">Hildelith</a>'s nunnery at <a href="/wiki/Barking_Abbey" title="Barking Abbey">Barking Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mayo_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayo-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Cuthburh is traditionally associated with the "Cuthburh" mentioned in the dedication of <a href="/wiki/Aldhelm" title="Aldhelm">Aldhelm</a>'s treatise <i>De virginitate</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> It is thought that she was in some way related to Aldhelm.<sup id="cite_ref-mayo_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayo-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> After Aldfrith's death, around 705, Cuthburh and <a href="/wiki/Cwenburh" title="Cwenburh">Cwenburh</a> established a double-monastery in her brother's kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a>, at <a href="/wiki/Wimborne" class="mw-redirect" title="Wimborne">Wimborne, Dorset</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-mayo_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayo-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is described as austere, and she communicated with prelates through a little hatch in the <a href="/wiki/Nunnery" class="mw-redirect" title="Nunnery">nunnery</a> at Wimborne.  Among <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Saint Boniface</a>'s surviving letters is an anonymous account of a vision of Abbess Cuthburh in Hell.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cuthburh died on 31 August 725 at Wimborne and is said to be buried under the wall of the chancel. <sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1558, Wimborne Minster being in need of repair, the guardians of the church wrote <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cromwell" title="Thomas Cromwell">Thomas Cromwell</a> for permission to melt down the silver reliquary containing Cuthburh's head. As a few years later, the tower collapsed, it is surmised that the reliquary was confiscated to the King's use. It is not mentioned what then happened to her head.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> associated with her is 31 August.<sup id="cite_ref-mayo_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mayo-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
