					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Byzantine monk from Constantinople</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hosios_Loukas_(nave,_south_west_bay)_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Hosios_Loukas_%28nave%2C_south_west_bay%29_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg/220px-Hosios_Loukas_%28nave%2C_south_west_bay%29_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="217" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Hosios_Loukas_%28nave%2C_south_west_bay%29_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg/330px-Hosios_Loukas_%28nave%2C_south_west_bay%29_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Hosios_Loukas_%28nave%2C_south_west_bay%29_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg/440px-Hosios_Loukas_%28nave%2C_south_west_bay%29_-_S.Stephen_the_Younger.jpg 2x" data-file-width="925" data-file-height="913" /></a><figcaption>Mosaic of Stephen in <a href="/wiki/Hosios_Loukas" title="Hosios Loukas">Hosios Loukas</a> monastery, <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><b>Saint Stephen the Younger</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el"><span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ἂγιος Στέφανος ὁ νέος</span></span></span>, <i>Hagios Stephanos ho neos</i>; 713/715 – 28 November 764 or 765) was a <a href="/wiki/Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine">Byzantine</a> monk from <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> who became one of the leading opponents of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm" title="Byzantine Iconoclasm">iconoclastic policies</a> of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_V" title="Constantine V">Constantine V</a> (r. 741–775). He was executed in 764, and became the most prominent <a href="/wiki/Iconodule" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconodule">iconodule</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated annually on <a href="/wiki/November_28_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="November 28 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">28 November</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, the <i>Life of St. Stephen the Younger</i>, is an important historical source.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg/220px-Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="280" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg/330px-Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg/440px-Triumph_orthodoxy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1482" data-file-height="1886" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Icon_of_the_Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" title="Icon of the Triumph of Orthodoxy">Byzantine icon (14th–15th centuries)</a> celebrating the definite restoration of the veneration of images in 843 (the "<a href="/wiki/Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Triumph of Orthodoxy">Triumph of Orthodoxy</a>"). Stephen the Younger is included among the <a href="/wiki/Iconodule" class="mw-redirect" title="Iconodule">iconodule</a> martyrs who are presented as witnesses to the event.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Stephen was born in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 713 or, according to the <i>Life</i>, shortly after 11 August 715. His father, Gregory, was a craftsman. His mother was called Anna, and he had two older sisters, Theodote and an unnamed one.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ODB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On <a href="/wiki/Holy_Saturday" title="Holy Saturday">Holy Saturday</a> 716, he was baptised in the <a href="/wiki/Hagia_Sophia" title="Hagia Sophia">Hagia Sophia</a> by <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Germanus_I_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Germanus I of Constantinople">Patriarch Germanus I</a>. In his sixteenth year (circa 731), he was brought by his parents to <a href="/wiki/Mt._Auxentius" class="mw-redirect" title="Mt. Auxentius">Mt. Auxentius</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bithynia" title="Bithynia">Bithynia</a>, where he became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. He visited Constantinople again for his father's funeral some years later, and brought his mother and sisters to the convent of Mt. Auxentius.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> On his 31st year (743/746), his mentor, John, died, and Stephen succeeded him, founding a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>. In his 42nd year (754/757), he retired as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> in a <a href="/wiki/Cave" title="Cave">cave</a>. He refused to accept the decisions of the iconoclast <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Hieria" title="Council of Hieria">Council of Hieria</a> (754), but it was not until circa 760 that he began suffering <a href="/wiki/Persecution" title="Persecution">persecution</a>: he was accused of sexual relations with his mother, and of illegally <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsuring</a> George Synkletos, a favourite of the Emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_V" title="Constantine V">Constantine V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-R238_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-R238-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Some modern scholars, however, reject the conventional story whereby Constantine's persecution of monks was a result of the latter championing the cause of the iconophiles. Rather, it has been suggested that the emperor's drive against monasticism had military and financial reasons, since the monasteries paid no tax and the monks were exempt from military duty. Consequently, the persecution of Stephen may have had more to do with his popularity and strong advocacy of monasticism, rather than his active opposition to iconoclasm as reported in his <i>Life</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Stephen_the_Younger,_Peter_and_Andrew_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Stephen_the_Younger%2C_Peter_and_Andrew_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/220px-Stephen_the_Younger%2C_Peter_and_Andrew_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="152" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Stephen_the_Younger%2C_Peter_and_Andrew_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/330px-Stephen_the_Younger%2C_Peter_and_Andrew_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Stephen_the_Younger%2C_Peter_and_Andrew_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/440px-Stephen_the_Younger%2C_Peter_and_Andrew_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1078" data-file-height="744" /></a><figcaption>Martyrdom of the monks Stephen the Younger, Andrew and Peter, depicted in the 11th century <a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Constantine V then sent soldiers who arrested him and brought him to a monastery in <a href="/wiki/%C3%9Csk%C3%BCdar#Chrysopolis" title="Üsküdar">Chrysopolis</a>. There again he refused to accept the decisions of Hieria, and was banished to the island of <a href="/wiki/Prokonnesos" class="mw-redirect" title="Prokonnesos">Prokonnesos</a>. In his second year of exile (circa 764), he was brought to the Phiale prison in Constantinople, and was questioned by the emperor himself. After almost a year of imprisonment in the prison of the city's <i><a href="/wiki/Praetorium" title="Praetorium">praetorium</a></i>, he was condemned to death by Constantine, and dragged by soldiers through the streets and was clubbed to death.<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-R238_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-R238-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> His corpse was dragged through the streets, while his <a href="/wiki/Human_skull" class="mw-redirect" title="Human skull">skull</a> was rescued by one of his followers and brought to the Dius Monastery. His relics are recorded in various Constantinopolitan churches and monasteries during the 13th–15th centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His body was deposited either at the <a href="/wiki/Lips_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Lips Monastery">Lips Monastery</a> (according to Russian travellers) or the <i>martyrion</i> of St. Stephen the Protomartyr in the quarter of Constantinianae,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> and his right hand was at the <a href="/wiki/Zeyrek_Mosque" title="Zeyrek Mosque">Pantokrator Monastery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> His skull, which became the subject of a special procession presided over by the <a href="/wiki/Eparch_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Eparch of Constantinople">Eparch of Constantinople</a> on his feast day (28 November), was located at the Peribleptos Monastery in the 14th century, and is today claimed to be in the possession of the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Panteleimon_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Panteleimon monastery">Saint Panteleimon monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Mount_Athos" title="Mount Athos">Mount Athos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Depiction">Depiction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Stephen_the_Younger&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Depiction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a prominent defender of the images, and iconoclasm's greatest martyr, Stephen is portrayed holding an icon or a <a href="/wiki/Diptych" title="Diptych">diptych</a> of icons, usually displaying the busts of <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>. He is also one of the witnesses in the <a href="/wiki/Icon_of_the_Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" title="Icon of the Triumph of Orthodoxy">famous Russian icon</a> celebrating the "<a href="/wiki/Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Triumph of Orthodoxy">Triumph of Orthodoxy</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-ODB_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
