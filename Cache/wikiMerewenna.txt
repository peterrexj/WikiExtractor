<p><b>Mærwynn</b>, also known as <b>St. Merewenna</b> or <b>Merwinna</b>, (<abbr title="floruit (&#39;flourished&#39;&#160;– known to have been active at a particular time or during a particular period)">fl.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;967−975</span> AD) was a 10th-century  <a href="/wiki/Abbess" title="Abbess">abbess</a> of <a href="/wiki/Romsey_Abbey" title="Romsey Abbey">Romsey Abbey</a>. She is recognised as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ODS_307_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS_307-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A6rwynn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:RomseyAbbey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/RomseyAbbey.jpg/150px-RomseyAbbey.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="113" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/RomseyAbbey.jpg/225px-RomseyAbbey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/RomseyAbbey.jpg/300px-RomseyAbbey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1600" data-file-height="1200" /></a><figcaption>Romsey Abbey</figcaption></figure>
<p>Mærwynn was the founding abbess of the reconsecrated Abbey of Romsey, and there is some certainty that she was appointed to the position by King <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceable" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceable">Edgar the Peaceable</a> on <a href="/wiki/Christmas_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Christmas day">Christmas day</a> in 974.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> While medieval legend had it that she was born in Ireland and educated by <a href="/wiki/St._Patrick" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Patrick">St. Patrick</a>, historical understanding that five centuries separate them discounts this.<sup id="cite_ref-felix_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-felix-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>She is instead known more historically from several surviving documents: a king's charter, by <a href="/wiki/Edgar_the_Peaceable" class="mw-redirect" title="Edgar the Peaceable">Edgar the Peaceable</a>, to Romsey Abbey; the medieval <a href="/wiki/Confraternity_book" title="Confraternity book">confraternity book</a> of Winchester, known as the <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/New_Minster_Liber_Vitae" title="New Minster Liber Vitae">Liber Vitae of the New Minster</a></i></span>;<sup id="cite_ref-LV_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LV-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and the <a href="/wiki/Secgan" class="mw-redirect" title="Secgan">Secgan</a> manuscript's <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-felix_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-felix-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>King Edgar sent <a href="/wiki/%C3%86lffl%C3%A6d" title="Ælfflæd">Ælfflæd</a>, his daughter,<sup id="cite_ref-ODS_157_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS_157-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> to Mærwynn for care, and she became like a foster mother to the princess.<sup id="cite_ref-LV_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-LV-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A6rwynn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mærwynn was buried at Romsey Abbey, close by to where her protégé Ælfflæd was buried. Her primary <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 10 February in both the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches. There are secondary commemorations in the Catholic Church marking the date of the translation of her relics (and of Ælfflæd's) on 29 October,<sup id="cite_ref-ODS_307_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS_307-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> with certain other secondary days of note mentioned by the Monks of Ramsgate.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Romsey_Abbey">Romsey Abbey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=M%C3%A6rwynn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Romsey Abbey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The foundations of Mærwynn's abbey have been located under the <a href="/wiki/Bell_tower" title="Bell tower">tower</a>, <a href="/wiki/Choir_stall" class="mw-redirect" title="Choir stall">choir stalls</a> and part of the <a href="/wiki/Nave" title="Nave">nave</a> of the current <a href="/wiki/Norman_architecture" title="Norman architecture">Norman</a> church. Mærwynn's abbey was the second of the four church buildings to be built on the site; it was destroyed by <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> in 1003 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
