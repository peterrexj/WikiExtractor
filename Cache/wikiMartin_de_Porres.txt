<p><b>Martín de Porres Velázquez</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Order_of_Preachers" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Preachers">OP</a></span> (9 December 1579 &#8211; 3 November 1639) was a <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peruvian</a> <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brother</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> who was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> in 1837 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1962 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>. He is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Mixed-race" class="mw-redirect" title="Mixed-race">mixed-race</a> people, <a href="/wiki/Barbers" class="mw-redirect" title="Barbers">barbers</a>, innkeepers, public health workers, and all those seeking <a href="/wiki/Racial_equality" title="Racial equality">racial harmony</a>.
</p><p>He was noted for his work on behalf of the poor, establishing an <a href="/wiki/Orphanage" title="Orphanage">orphanage</a> and a children's <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">hospital</a>. He maintained an austere lifestyle, which included <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasting</a> and <a href="/wiki/Vegetarianism" title="Vegetarianism">abstaining from meat</a>. Among the many miracles attributed to him were those of <a href="/wiki/Levitation_(paranormal)" title="Levitation (paranormal)">levitation</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bilocation" title="Bilocation">bilocation</a>, miraculous knowledge, instantaneous cures, and an ability to communicate with animals.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martin_de_Porres&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Martin was born in the city of <a href="/wiki/Lima" title="Lima">Lima</a>, <a href="/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru" title="Viceroyalty of Peru">Viceroyalty of Peru</a>, on 9 December 1579. He was the illegitimate son of a <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> nobleman, Don Juan de Porras y de la Peña, and Ana Velázquez, a freed slave of African and <a href="/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_in_Peru" class="mw-redirect" title="Indigenous peoples in Peru">Native</a> descent.<sup id="cite_ref-ewb_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-aar_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He had a sister named Juana de Porres, born two years later in 1581. After the birth of his sister, the father abandoned the family.<sup id="cite_ref-FoleyMcCloskey2009_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FoleyMcCloskey2009-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Ana Velázquez supported her children by taking in laundry.<sup id="cite_ref-fullerton_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fullerton-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He grew up in poverty and, when his mother could not support him, Martin was sent to a primary school for two years, and then placed with a barber/surgeon to learn the medical arts.<sup id="cite_ref-aar_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He spent hours of the night in prayer, a practice which increased as he grew older.
</p><p>Under Peruvian law, descendants of Africans and Native Americans were barred from becoming full members of religious orders. The only route open to Martin was to ask the Dominicans of Holy Rosary Priory in Lima to accept him as a "donado", a volunteer who performed menial tasks in the monastery in return for the privilege of wearing the habit and living with the religious community.<sup id="cite_ref-craughwell_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-craughwell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> At the age of 15 he asked for admission to the Dominican Convent of the Rosary in Lima and was received first as a servant boy, and as his duties grew he was promoted to <a href="/wiki/Almoner" title="Almoner">almoner</a>.
</p><p>Martin continued to practice his old trades of barbering and healing and was said to have performed many miraculous cures. He also took on kitchen work, laundry, and cleaning. After eight years at Holy Rosary, the <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> Juan de Lorenzana decided to turn a blind eye to the law and permit Martin to take his vows as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Dominic" title="Third Order of Saint Dominic">Third Order of Saint Dominic</a>. Holy Rosary was home to 300 men, not all of whom accepted the decision of De Lorenzana: one of the novices called Martin a "<a href="/wiki/Mulatto" title="Mulatto">mulatto</a> dog", while one of the priests mocked him for being illegitimate and descended from slaves.<sup id="cite_ref-craughwell_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-craughwell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Martin was 24, he was allowed to profess <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> as a Dominican lay brother in 1603. He is said to have several times refused this elevation in status, which may have come about due to his father's intervention, and he never became a priest.<sup id="cite_ref-ewb_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is said that when his <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> was in debt, he implored them: "I am only a poor mulatto, sell me." Martin was deeply attached to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>, and he was praying in front of it one night when the step of the altar he was kneeling on caught fire. Throughout all the confusion and chaos that followed, he remained where he was, unaware of what was happening around him.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Martin_of_porres.jpeg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Martin_of_porres.jpeg/220px-Martin_of_porres.jpeg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Martin_of_porres.jpeg/330px-Martin_of_porres.jpeg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f7/Martin_of_porres.jpeg/440px-Martin_of_porres.jpeg 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Martin_of_porres.jpeg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>A mid-twentieth-century stained glass representation of Martin de Porres in <a href="/wiki/St_Pancras_Church,_Ipswich" title="St Pancras Church, Ipswich">St Pancras Church, Ipswich</a> with a broom, rosary, parrot and monkey</div></div></div>
<p>When Martin was 34, after he had been given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> of a lay brother, he was assigned to the <a href="/wiki/Hospital" title="Hospital">infirmary</a>, where he was placed in charge and would remain in service until his death at the age of 59. He was known for his care of the sick.<sup id="cite_ref-aar_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-aar-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His superiors saw in him the virtues necessary to exercise unfailing patience in this difficult role. It was not long before miracles were attributed to him. Martin also cared for the sick outside his convent, often bringing them healing with only a simple glass of water. He ministered without distinction to Spanish nobles and to slaves recently brought from Africa.<sup id="cite_ref-ewb_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> One day an aged beggar, covered with ulcers and almost naked, stretched out his hand, and Martin took him to his own bed. When one of his brethren reproved him, Martin replied: "Compassion, my dear Brother, is preferable to cleanliness."
</p><p>When an epidemic struck Lima, there were in this single Convent of the Rosary 60 friars who were sick, many of them novices in a distant and locked section of the convent, separated from the professed. Martin is said to have passed through the locked doors to care for them, a phenomenon which was reported in the residence more than once. The professed, too, saw him suddenly beside them without the doors having been opened. Martin continued to transport the sick to the convent until the <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">provincial superior</a>, alarmed by the contagion threatening the friars, forbade him to continue to do so. His sister, who lived in the country, offered her house to lodge those whom the residence of the religious could not hold. One day he found on the street a poor Indian, bleeding to death from a dagger wound, and took him to his own room until he could transport him to his sister's hospice. The prior, when he heard of this, reprimanded him for disobedience. He was extremely edified, however, by his reply: "Forgive my error, and please instruct me, for I did not know that the precept of obedience took precedence over that of charity."<sup id="cite_ref-granger_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granger-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  The prior gave him liberty thereafter to follow his inspirations in the exercise of mercy.
</p><p>Martin did not eat meat. He begged for alms to procure necessities the convent could not provide.<sup id="cite_ref-granger_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-granger-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In normal times, Martin succeeded with his alms to feed 160 poor persons every day, and distributed a remarkable sum of money every week to the <a href="/wiki/Indigent" class="mw-redirect" title="Indigent">indigent</a>. Side by side with his daily work in the kitchen, laundry and infirmary, Martin's life is said to have reflected extraordinary gifts: ecstasies that lifted him into the air, light filling the room where he prayed, bilocation, miraculous knowledge, instantaneous cures and a remarkable rapport with animals.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He founded a residence for orphans and abandoned children in the city of Lima.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_commemoration">Death and commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martin_de_Porres&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg/200px-Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="158" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg/300px-Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg/400px-Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3916" data-file-height="3102" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Convento_Santo_Domingo_-_Lima.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The <a href="/wiki/Basilica_and_Convent_of_Santo_Domingo" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica and Convent of Santo Domingo">Basilica and Convent of Santo Domingo</a>, where de Porres is buried, in <a href="/wiki/Lima" title="Lima">Lima</a>, <a href="/wiki/Peru" title="Peru">Peru</a></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Black_saint,_Kildare.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Black_saint%2C_Kildare.jpg/220px-Black_saint%2C_Kildare.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="237" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Black_saint%2C_Kildare.jpg/330px-Black_saint%2C_Kildare.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4e/Black_saint%2C_Kildare.jpg/440px-Black_saint%2C_Kildare.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2934" data-file-height="3155" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Black_saint,_Kildare.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Devotional statue of Martin de Porres in <a href="/wiki/Kildare" title="Kildare">Kildare</a>, Ireland, depicting him with dark skin</div></div></div>
<p>Martin was a friend of both Saint <a href="/wiki/John_Macias" title="John Macias">Juan Macías</a>, a fellow Dominican lay brother, and Saint <a href="/wiki/Rose_of_Lima" title="Rose of Lima">Rose of Lima</a>, another lay Dominican. By his death on 3 November 1639, he had won the affection and respect of many of his fellow Dominicans as well as a host of people outside the priory.<sup id="cite_ref-craughwell_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-craughwell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Word of his miracles had made him known as a saint throughout the region. As his body was displayed to allow the people of the city to pay their respects, each person snipped a tiny piece of his habit to keep as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a>. It is said that three habits were taken from the body. His body was then interred in the grounds of the monastery.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>After De Porres died, the miracles and graces received when he was invoked multiplied in such profusion that his body was exhumed after 25 years and his body was supposedly <a href="/wiki/Incorruptible" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorruptible">found intact</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Myroblyte_saint" title="Myroblyte saint">exuded</a> a <a href="/wiki/Odour_of_sanctity" title="Odour of sanctity">fine fragrance</a>. Letters to Rome pleaded for his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>; the decree affirming the heroism of his virtues was issued in 1763 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> beatified Martin de Porres on 29 October 1837, and nearly 125 years later, <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> canonized him in Rome on 6 May 1962.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Dorcy1983_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Dorcy1983-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of people of <a href="/wiki/Mixed_race" class="mw-redirect" title="Mixed race">mixed race</a>, and of <a href="/wiki/Innkeeper" class="mw-redirect" title="Innkeeper">innkeepers</a>, barbers, <a href="/wiki/Public_health" title="Public health">public health</a> workers and more, with a <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> on <a href="/wiki/November_3" title="November 3">November 3</a>, also <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(Anglicanism)" title="Commemoration (Anglicanism)">commemorated</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Calendar of Saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is recognised as <i>Papa Candelo</i> in the <a href="/wiki/Afro-Caribbean" class="mw-redirect" title="Afro-Caribbean">Afro-Caribbean</a>-Catholic <a href="/wiki/Syncretism" title="Syncretism">syncretist</a> religion, which is practised in places where <a href="/wiki/African_diaspora" title="African diaspora">African diaspora</a> culture thrives such as Colombia, Venezuela, <a href="/wiki/Puerto_Rico" title="Puerto Rico">Puerto Rico</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Republic" title="Dominican Republic">Dominican Republic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cuba" title="Cuba">Cuba</a>, the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>, and his native Peru.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martin_de_Porres&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg/220px-San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="253" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg/330px-San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg/440px-San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="4600" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Mart%C3%ADn_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucci%C3%B3n_Facial_3D.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Forensic facial reconstruction of Martin de Porres</div></div></div>
<p>Martin de Porres is often depicted as a young mixed-race <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> wearing the old habit of the Dominican lay brother, a black <a href="/wiki/Scapular" title="Scapular">scapular</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hood_(headgear)" title="Hood (headgear)">capuce</a>, along with a <a href="/wiki/Broom" title="Broom">broom</a>, since he considered all work to be sacred, no matter how menial. He is sometimes shown with a dog, a cat and a mouse eating in peace from the same dish.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martin_de_Porres&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Martin's sometimes defiant attachment to the ideal of social justice achieved deep resonance in a church attempting to carry forward that ideal in today's modern world.<sup id="cite_ref-ewb_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewb-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Today, Martin is commemorated by, among other things, a school building that houses the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas_Faculty_of_Medicine_and_Surgery" title="University of Santo Tomas Faculty of Medicine and Surgery">medical</a>, <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas_College_of_Nursing" class="mw-redirect" title="University of Santo Tomas College of Nursing">nursing</a>, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=University_of_Santo_Tomas_College_of_Rehabilitation_Sciences&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="University of Santo Tomas College of Rehabilitation Sciences (page does not exist)">rehabilitation science</a> schools of the Dominican <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" title="University of Santo Tomas">University of Santo Tomas</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Philippines" title="Philippines">Philippines</a>. A programme of work is also named after him at the Las Casas Institute at <a href="/wiki/Blackfriars_Hall" class="mw-redirect" title="Blackfriars Hall">Blackfriars Hall</a>, University of Oxford.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>  And he is the titular saint of the parish of St. Martin de Porres in Poughkeepsie, New York,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> and some elementary schools. A number of Catholic churches are named after him. The <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order_in_the_United_States#Province_of_St._Martin_de_Porres_(Southern)" title="Dominican Order in the United States">Southern Province</a> of Dominicans in the United States also bears his name.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Martin_de_Porres&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1965, American composer <a href="/wiki/E._Anne_Schwerdtfeger" title="E. Anne Schwerdtfeger">E. Anne Schwerdtfeger</a> composed the <i>Mass of St. Martin de Porres</i> for chorus and organ.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 1980 novel <i><a href="/wiki/A_Confederacy_of_Dunces" title="A Confederacy of Dunces">A Confederacy of Dunces</a></i>, Ignatius Reilly contemplates praying to Martin for aid in bringing social justice to the black workers at the New Orleans factory where he works. And in music, the first track of jazz pianist <a href="/wiki/Mary_Lou_Williams" title="Mary Lou Williams">Mary Lou Williams</a>'s album <i>Black Christ of the Andes</i> is titled "St. Martin De Porres".<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are several Spanish and Mexican works regarding his life in cinema and television, starring Cuban actor Rene Muñoz, most of them referring to his mixed race, his miracles and his life of humility. The best known movies are <i>Fray Escoba</i> (Friar Broom) (1963)<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and <i>Un mulato llamado Martin</i> (A Mulatto Called Martin) (1975).<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the <i><a href="/wiki/Moone_Boy" title="Moone Boy">Moone Boy</a></i> episode "Godfellas", the character Martin Moon is shown to be named by his grandfather after San Martin de Porres. His grandfather is unable to actually remember any of San Martin's accomplishments, and simply refers to him as "one of the black ones" when asked about him.
</p><p>American singer <a href="/wiki/Madonna_(entertainer)" class="mw-redirect" title="Madonna (entertainer)">Madonna</a>'s lead single "<a href="/wiki/Like_a_Prayer_(song)" title="Like a Prayer (song)">Like a Prayer</a>" (1989) featured Martin de Porres as a character in the song's <a href="/wiki/Music_video" title="Music video">music video</a>. The portrayal of de Porres and Madonna in a romantic relationship was met with mixed criticism from the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Peru" title="Catholic Church in Peru">Catholic church in Peru</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican</a>.
</p>
