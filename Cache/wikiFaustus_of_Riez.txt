<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Faustus_of_Riez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Faustus was born between 400 and 410, and his contemporaries, <a href="/wiki/Avitus_of_Vienne" title="Avitus of Vienne">Avitus of Vienne</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sidonius_Apollinaris" title="Sidonius Apollinaris">Sidonius Apollinaris</a>, say that he was born in Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In his youth he was devoted to the study of elocution and Christian philosophy. He is thought by some to have been a lawyer but owing to the influence of his mother, famed for her sanctity, he abandoned secular pursuits as a young man and entered the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_L%C3%A9rins" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of Lérins">monastery of Lérins</a>. Here he was soon ordained to the priesthood and after about eight years, because of his extraordinary piety was chosen in 432 to be head of the monastery, in succession to Maximus who had become Bishop of Riez.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His career as abbot lasted about twenty or twenty-five years during which he attained a high reputation for his wonderful gifts as an extempore preacher and for his stern asceticism.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the death of Maximus he became Bishop of Riez. This elevation did not make any change in his manner of life; he continued his ascetic practices, and frequently returned to the monastery of Lérins to renew his fervour. He was a zealous advocate of monasticism and established many monasteries in his diocese. In spite of his activity in the discharge of his duties as bishop, he participated in all the theological discussions of his time and became known as a stern opponent of <a href="/wiki/Arianism" title="Arianism">Arianism</a> in all its forms. For this, and as is said for his view, stated below, of the corporeity of the human soul, he incurred the enmity of the Arian <a href="/wiki/Euric" title="Euric">Euric</a>, King of the <a href="/wiki/Visigoths" title="Visigoths">Visigoths</a>, who had gained possession of a large portion of Southern Gaul, and was banished from his see. His exile lasted eight years, during which time he was aided by loyal friends. On the death of Euric he resumed his labours at the head of his diocese and continued there until his death between 490 and 495.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His own diocesan flock considered him a saint and erected a <a href="/wiki/Basilica" title="Basilica">basilica</a> in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-Farmer_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Farmer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works_and_theological_position">Works and theological position</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Faustus_of_Riez&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Works and theological position">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Throughout his life Faustus was an uncompromising adversary of <a href="/wiki/Pelagius" title="Pelagius">Pelagius</a>, whom he styled <i>pestifer</i> ('plague bringer'), and equally decided in his opposition to the doctrine of <a href="/wiki/Predestination" title="Predestination">predestination</a> which he styled "erroneous, blasphemous, heathen, fatalistic, and conducive to immorality". This doctrine in its strongest form had been expounded by a presbyter named Lucidus and was condemned by two synods, at Arles and Lyons (475). At the request of the bishops who composed these synods, and especially Leontius of Arles, Faustus wrote the <i>Libri duo de Gratia Dei et humanae mentis libero arbitrio</i>, in which he argued against the doctrines of the predestinarians as well as those of Pelagius.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The work was marked by <a href="/wiki/Semipelagianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Semipelagianism">Semipelagianism</a>, and for several years was bitterly attacked. It was condemned by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Orange_(529)" title="Council of Orange (529)">Second Synod of Orange</a> in 529.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Faustus maintained that the human soul is in a certain sense corporeal, God alone being a pure spirit. The opposition to Faustus was not fully developed in his lifetime and he died with a reputation for sanctity.<sup id="cite_ref-Healy_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Healy-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Faustus wrote also: <i>Libri duo de Spiritu Sancto</i> (P.L., LXII, 9), wrongly ascribed to the Roman deacon Paschasius. His <i>Libellus parvus adversus Arianos et Macedonianos</i>, mentioned by <a href="/wiki/Gennadius_of_Massilia" title="Gennadius of Massilia">Gennadius of Massilia</a>, seems to be lost.
</p><p>His feast day is 28 September.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
