					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Carthusian monks who were killed in the Reformation</div>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg/250px-Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="284" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg/375px-Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg/500px-Vicente_Carducho._El_Paular_09.jpg 2x" data-file-width="520" data-file-height="591" /></a><figcaption>"Martirio de los cartujos de Mauerbach" (Martyrdom of the <a href="/wiki/Mauerbach_Charterhouse" title="Mauerbach Charterhouse">Mauerbach</a> Carthusians)  Vicente Carducho. 1642</figcaption></figure>
<p>The <b>Carthusian martyrs</b> are those members of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> monastic order <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">who have been persecuted and killed</a> because of their Christian faith and their adherence to the Catholic religion. As an enclosed order the Carthusians do not, on principle, put forward <a href="/wiki/Canonization#Roman_Catholic_procedure_since_1983" title="Canonization">causes</a> for their members, though causes have been promoted by others on their behalf.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_order">The order</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carthusian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: The order">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Carthusian order was founded in 1084 by <a href="/wiki/St._Bruno_of_Cologne" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Bruno of Cologne">St. Bruno of Cologne</a>, and is an <a href="/wiki/Eremitic" class="mw-redirect" title="Eremitic">eremitic</a> order, holding to the principle of withdrawal from the world to a life of silent <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplation</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prayer" title="Prayer">prayer</a>. They are often viewed as hermits that live in common, having no active apostolate outside their Charterhouse. Carthusian life is dramatically different as compared to Benedictine Monasticism, the most prevalent form in the west. Today the Carthusians are a small order comprising 25 houses worldwide with just 350 male and 75 female members.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Martyrs">The Martyrs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Carthusian_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Martyrs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/Hussite_Revolution" class="mw-redirect" title="Hussite Revolution">Hussite Revolution</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bohemia" title="Bohemia">Bohemia</a> in the 15th century Carthusian houses, as with other Catholic institutions, came under attack. In 1419 the charterhouse in <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> was burned down.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Dom Andreas, prior of <a href="/wiki/%C5%BDi%C4%8De_Charterhouse" title="Žiče Charterhouse">Žiče Charterhouse</a>, was captured during an Ottoman raid and martyred on March 3, 1529.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
The <a href="/wiki/Mauerbach_Charterhouse" title="Mauerbach Charterhouse">Mauerbach Charterhouse</a> on the outskirts of <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, was plundered and set on fire by <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman</a> troops during the 1529 <a href="/wiki/Siege_of_Vienna_(1529)" title="Siege of Vienna (1529)">Siege of Vienna</a>, and was again targeted by the Ottomans during the  1683 <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna" title="Battle of Vienna">Battle of Vienna</a>, though there seems no precise record of the names of monks killed in either assault. 
</p><p>In 1537 during the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian_Martyrs_of_London" title="Carthusian Martyrs of London">English Reformation</a> the <a href="/wiki/London_Charterhouse" title="London Charterhouse">London Charterhouse</a> was dissolved and its members imprisoned and later executed. Eighteen of these, the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian_Martyrs_of_London" title="Carthusian Martyrs of London">Carthusian Martyrs of London</a>, were beatified in 1886 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> three of these (<a href="/wiki/Augustine_Webster" title="Augustine Webster">Augustine Webster</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Houghton_(martyr)" title="John Houghton (martyr)">John Houghton</a> and <a href="/wiki/Robert_Lawrence_(martyr)" title="Robert Lawrence (martyr)">Robert Lawrence</a>) were canonized in 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> with other English martyrs as the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>.
</p><p>In 1572 during the <a href="/wiki/Dutch_Revolt" class="mw-redirect" title="Dutch Revolt">Dutch Revolt</a> the Charterhouses of <a href="/wiki/Delft" title="Delft">Delft</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roermond" title="Roermond">Roermond</a> were attacked, resulting in the deaths of Dom Justus van Schoonhoven and at least two others.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a> numerous Carthusians were persecuted with other Catholic religious and lay persons. Claude Beguignot and Lazarus Tiersot were ordained Carthusians. As priests, they were required to take the anti-Papal oath of the "Civil Constitution of the Clergy". At their refusal they were imprisoned along with eight other Carthusians in former slave ships anchored in the Charente River at <a href="/wiki/Rochefort,_Charente-Maritime" title="Rochefort, Charente-Maritime">Rochefort</a>. Like most of 800 priests and clergy confined there, they died in 1794 due to the inhumane conditions.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> They were beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1995.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1936, during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>, Carthusians were affected by the widespread anti-clericalism; two of these, from the Charterhouse of <a href="/wiki/Montalegre" title="Montalegre">Montalegre</a>, have so far been recognized.
</p><p>In September 1944, monks from the charterhouse at <a href="/wiki/Certosa_di_Farneta" title="Certosa di Farneta">Certosa di Farneta</a> opened their doors to troops from the 16th SS Panzergrenadier Division, who said they came bearing gifts for the abbey. They broke into the monastery to arrest 32 partisans and Jews being sheltered in the monastery. Some of the refugees were able to escape. Of the more than sixty killed, twelve were Carthusians.<sup id="cite_ref-Sciascia_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Sciascia-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Among the twelve Carthusians killed were two Germans, one Swiss, one Venezuelan, and one Spaniard. The remaining monks were also from diverse countries. Those killed were: 
</p>
