<p><b>Job of Pochayev</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Иов Почаевский</span>; c. 1551 – 28 October 1651), to the world <b>Ivan Ivanovich Zheleza</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Иван Иванович Железа</span>), in <a href="/wiki/Great_Schema" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Schema">Great Schema</a> John (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Иоанн</span>) was an <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Childhood_and_early_years">Childhood and early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Childhood and early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Job was born around 1551 near the city of <a href="/wiki/Kolomyia" title="Kolomyia">Kolomyia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Galicia_(Eastern_Europe)" title="Galicia (Eastern Europe)">Galicia</a>, when it was within the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_(1385%E2%80%931569)" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Poland (1385–1569)">Polish kingdom</a>.
</p><p>His pious parents John and Agapia of the Zalizo (lit. "Iron") family, named him Ivan (John) after <a href="/wiki/John_the_Baptist" title="John the Baptist">John the Baptist</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">lives</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sava" title="Saint Sava">Rastko Nemanjić</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_of_Damascus" title="John of Damascus">John of Damascus</a> and "The Ladder" by <a href="/wiki/John_Climacus" title="John Climacus">John Climacus</a> were the models of virtuous life for the young Ivan. According to his biographer and disciple Dositheus, while he was young in years he was perfect in wisdom, and the boy differed from other children by his high spiritual aspirations, with never a contradiction in his words, knowledge and actions.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Joining_Uhornytskyi_monastery">Joining Uhornytskyi monastery</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Joining Uhornytskyi monastery">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At the age of 10 he secretly left home for the <a href="/wiki/Transfiguration_of_Jesus" title="Transfiguration of Jesus">Transfiguration</a> Ugorniki <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a>, and asked the <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a> (abbot) to accept him to serve the brothers. When Ivan turned 12, he was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> as a monk and was given the name Job. Since then, he began to follow the example of the biblical character <a href="/wiki/Job_(biblical_figure)" title="Job (biblical figure)">Job</a> as a model of living. Reaching the age of 31 he was offered <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priestly</a> <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a>, which he accepted.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Transfer_to_Dubno_monastery_and_literary_work">Transfer to Dubno monastery and literary work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Transfer to Dubno monastery and literary work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the repeated offers of a local, <a href="/wiki/Konstanty_Wasyl_Ostrogski" title="Konstanty Wasyl Ostrogski">Konstantin Ostrozhsky</a>, the defender of Orthodoxy, he was transferred to the island monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Exaltation_of_the_Cross" class="mw-redirect" title="Exaltation of the Cross">Exaltation of the Cross</a> outside of <a href="/wiki/Dubno" title="Dubno">Dubno</a> that belonged to the duke's estates in the <a href="/wiki/Rivnenska_oblast" class="mw-redirect" title="Rivnenska oblast">Rivne region</a>. The monastery was arranged after the canons of <a href="/wiki/Theodore_the_Studite" title="Theodore the Studite">Theodore the Studite</a>. There, for 20 years, Job served as hegumen (abbot) and engaged himself in the writing of theological books. 
</p><p>The collection of his works, <i>The Book of the Venerable Job of Pochayiv, Written by His Own Hand</i>, contained 80 teachings, conversations, and <a href="/wiki/Sermon" title="Sermon">sermons</a> of St. Job, as well as excerpts he himself compiled from the writings of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Fathers" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Fathers">Holy Fathers</a>. It was translated into <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a> and republished in 1881 under the title <i>Pchela Pochayevskaya</i> (<i>The Bee of Pochayiv</i>), edited by the professor of the <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a> Theological Academy, N. Petrov.
</p><p>In his writings, Job defended Orthodoxy against the <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">heresies</a> (especially the <a href="/wiki/Socinianism" title="Socinianism">Socinianists</a>) that were spreading in western Malorossiya during his time, writing on the most important Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Dogma" title="Dogma">dogmas</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Trinity" title="Trinity">Trinity</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Divinity_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Divinity of Christ">divinity of Christ</a>, about the <a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">Mother of God</a>, <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> and everything that was rejected by Protestant missionaries.
</p><p>Job also critiqued <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> teachings regarding the use of <a href="/wiki/Azymes" class="mw-redirect" title="Azymes">unleavened bread</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a>, among other doctrinal differences in the face of Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Persecution" title="Persecution">persecution</a> of Orthodoxy following the <a href="/wiki/Union_of_Brest" title="Union of Brest">Union of Brest</a> (1596). Many Orthodox Christians living in Poland at the time were deprived of their rights, and attempts were made to force them to convert to Catholicism. A number of Orthodox bishops even became <a href="/wiki/Apostacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostacy">apostates</a> to <a href="/wiki/Uniatism" class="mw-redirect" title="Uniatism">Uniatism</a>. To counteract this problem, Job and others defended Orthodoxy by copying and disseminating Orthodox books. Prince Ostrozhsky was also responsible for the <a href="/wiki/Ostrog_Bible" title="Ostrog Bible">Ostrog Bible</a> (1581), the first printed edition of the complete Orthodox Bible.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hermit_withdrawal_to_Pochayev">Hermit withdrawal to Pochayev</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Hermit withdrawal to Pochayev">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pochaev-wall.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Pochaev-wall.jpg/220px-Pochaev-wall.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="89" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Pochaev-wall.jpg/330px-Pochaev-wall.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Pochaev-wall.jpg/440px-Pochaev-wall.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1899" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>A view of the <a href="/wiki/Pochayiv_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Pochayiv Lavra">Pochayiv</a> <a href="/wiki/Lavra" title="Lavra">Lavra</a> as it appears today.</figcaption></figure>
<p>On account of his growing fame, he decided to withdraw as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> into the mountain <a href="/wiki/Pochayiv_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Pochayiv Lavra">caves monastery</a> at <a href="/wiki/Pochaiv" title="Pochaiv">Pochayev</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kremenets" title="Kremenets">Kremenets</a> district. Having joined the monastery in 1604, Job was eventually elected <a href="/wiki/Hegumen" title="Hegumen">hegumen</a>.
</p><p>Job was quiet, brief in words, and the only sound heard from his lips was the <a href="/wiki/Jesus_prayer" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus prayer">Jesus prayer</a>. For many days and weeks he would retreat into his locked cave, so narrow that entry is difficult, and so small that it was impossible to sit, stand or lie conveniently. From the long periods of kneeling, his knees were covered with wounds, and wearing knee-marks into the rock floor. His disciple Dosyfey recounts that he saw <a href="/wiki/Light_of_Tabor" class="mw-redirect" title="Light of Tabor">supernatural light</a> coming from the depths of the cave, shining for two hours onto the opposite side of the church. Dosyfey records that at the sight was terrified and fell to the ground.
</p><p>Job introduced strict discipline and other reforms of monastic life. During his time in office, the monastery had to fend off incessant attacks by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Andrzej_Firlej&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Andrzej Firlej (page does not exist)">Andrzej Firlej</a>, <a href="/wiki/Castellan" title="Castellan">Castellan</a> of <a href="/wiki/Belz" title="Belz">Belz</a>, who sued the monks over his grandmother's bequest of extensive lands and a miracle-working icon of the Mother of God. In 1623, Firlej raided the monastery, taking the holy icon with him and keeping it until 1641, when a court decision finally restituted the icon to the monks.
</p><p>In 1628 Job attended the <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">Synod</a> of Kiev, called to defend the Orthodox Church against Uniatism.
</p><p>Sometime after 1642, he was tonsured into the <a href="/wiki/Great_Schema" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Schema">Great Schema</a>, and received the new monastic name of John.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_canonization">Death and canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Death and canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Jov2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Jov2.jpg/220px-Jov2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="327" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Jov2.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="303" data-file-height="450" /></a><figcaption>Icon of Saint Job of Pochayiv.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG/220px-Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="298" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG/330px-Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG/440px-Hiob_Poczajowski.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1365" data-file-height="1848" /></a><figcaption>Job of Pochayev (1551-1651), the igumen of Pochayv Monastery. Oil, canvas, the end of the 19th century<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup></figcaption></figure>
<p>Job died on 25 October 1651 and was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">glorified</a> as a saint shortly thereafter. After his death, Job appeared three times in a <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">vision</a> to Dionysius Balaban, the <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Metropolitan of Kiev">Metropolitan of Kiev</a>, and instructed him that God wanted the Metropolitan to uncover the saint's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.
</p><p>After the first two visions, Metropolitan Dionysius did not follow the order. Only after the third, when Job threatened him with misfortune should he continue to disobey, did Dionysius see in it God’s will. The same day, the Metropolitan departed for <a href="/wiki/Pochayev" class="mw-redirect" title="Pochayev">Pochayev</a> and gave orders to open the grave of Job immediately.
</p><p>This took place on 28 August 1659. His body was found to be <a href="/wiki/Incorrupt" class="mw-redirect" title="Incorrupt">incorrupt</a>, emitting a wonderful and heavenly fragrance. The relics were taken to the <a href="/wiki/Trinity_Lavra_of_St._Sergius" title="Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius">Trinity Cathedral</a> of the Lavra for veneration.
</p><p>A second "Uncovering of the Relics" of Job of Pochayev took place on 28 August 1833, at which his relics were solemnly transferred to a church <a href="/wiki/Consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Consecration">consecrated</a> to his honour which had been built at the Pochayev Lavra.
</p><p>Every year, on 28 August a great number of Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> come to Pochayev Lavra to honour Saint Job, <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerate</a> his relics, and ask for his <a href="/wiki/Intercession" title="Intercession">intercession</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Healings_and_miracles">Healings and miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Healings and miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pochaev9.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Pochaev9.jpg/220px-Pochaev9.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Pochaev9.jpg/330px-Pochaev9.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Pochaev9.jpg/440px-Pochaev9.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="768" /></a><figcaption>The Church dedicated to St. Job at the Pochayev Lavra.</figcaption></figure>
<p>During the <a href="/wiki/Zbarazh" title="Zbarazh">Zbarazh War</a> of 1675, the cloister was besieged by the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Turks</a>, who reputedly fled upon seeing the apparition of the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a> (<a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">Mother of God</a>) accompanied by angels and Job. Numerous Turkish Muslims who witnessed the event during the siege converted to Christianity afterward. One of the monastery chapels commemorates this event.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pochayiv.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Pochayiv.jpg/220px-Pochayiv.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="243" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Pochayiv.jpg/330px-Pochayiv.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Pochayiv.jpg/440px-Pochayiv.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="552" /></a><figcaption>The Battle of Pochayev during the Zbarazh War in 1675. The <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a> and Job are depicted above the monastery, defending the cloister while the battle rages below.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1759, a coach of Count <a href="/wiki/Miko%C5%82aj_Bazyli_Potocki" title="Mikołaj Bazyli Potocki">Mikołaj Bazyli Potocki</a> capsized near the monastery walls. In a fit of anger, Potocki fired at his driver three times, all without avail. Attributing this failure to divine intervention, Potocki settled in Pochayiv and started to lavish gifts upon the cloister.
</p><p>In 1773, Potocki (who was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a>) petitioned the Pope to recognize the Pochayev icon as miraculous and Job as a Catholic saint. Only the former petition was satisfied.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2010)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>On 28 October 1908 when the <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Volhynia" title="Volhynia">Volhynia</a> and the faithful celebrated the <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> of Saint Job, Job repeatedly appeared in a vision in front of the bishop and blessed the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Mysteries" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Mysteries">Holy Mysteries</a> (Body and Blood of Christ).
</p><p>The cave church of St Job contains a famous gift from Countess Orlova - a silver reliquary with relics of the saint.
</p><p>The Printshop of St. Job of Pochaev at <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Monastery_(Jordanville,_New_York)" title="Holy Trinity Monastery (Jordanville, New York)">Holy Trinity Monastery</a> in <a href="/wiki/Jordanville,_New_York" title="Jordanville, New York">Jordanville, New York</a>, is dedicated to Job, and is the principal press of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_of_Russia" title="Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia">Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia</a>, publishing liturgical and spiritual works in <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic" title="Church Slavonic">Church Slavonic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a> and <a href="/wiki/English_language" title="English language">English</a>.
</p>
<div style="clear:left;"></div>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_Days">Feast Days</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Job_of_Pochayev&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Feast Days">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> celebrates his memory on <a href="/wiki/October_28_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="October 28 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">October 28</a>, the day of his repose (for those churches which follow the <a href="/wiki/Julian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian Calendar">Julian Calendar</a> October 28 falls on November 10 of the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>, a difference of 13 days).
</p><p>On <a href="/wiki/August_28_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="August 28 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">August 28</a>/September 10 the church celebrates the anniversary of the Uncovering of his Relics.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In 1902, the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Governing_Synod" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Governing Synod">Holy Synod</a> decreed that on this day the holy relics of St Job be carried in <a href="/wiki/Crucession" class="mw-redirect" title="Crucession">procession</a> around the <a href="/wiki/Pochayiv_Lavra" class="mw-redirect" title="Pochayiv Lavra">Dormition Cathedral</a> of the Pochaev Lavra after the <a href="/wiki/Divine_Liturgy" title="Divine Liturgy">Divine Liturgy</a>.
</p><p>and <a href="/wiki/October_10_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="October 10 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">October 10</a>/23 (as one of the seven saints commemorated on the Synaxis of the Saints of Volhynia).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
