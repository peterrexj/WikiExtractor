<p><b>Abel</b> (<i>fl</i>. 744–747) served as the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Reims" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Reims">Bishop of Reims</a> in <a href="/wiki/Francia" title="Francia">Francia</a>, now modern-day <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He has sometimes been venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, particularly by the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origins">Origins</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel_of_Reims&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origins">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the late 10th century, <a href="/wiki/Folcuin" title="Folcuin">Folcuin</a> wrote that Abel had been a monk in <a href="/wiki/Lobbes_Abbey" title="Lobbes Abbey">Lobbes Abbey</a> (modern day Belgium) while Bishop Ermino (d. 737) served as abbot, and had been born in <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>. In contrast <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a>, in a letter to a priest in the English kingdom of <a href="/wiki/Mercia" title="Mercia">Mercia</a>, insisted that Abel had in fact been born and raised in England. The modern historian <a href="/wiki/Wilhelm_Levison" title="Wilhelm Levison">Wilhelm Levison</a> has suggested there were two individuals with the same name, but Eugen Ewig accepts the identification by Boniface as more accurate. If Folcuin's later testimony has any basis in reality, it may be that Abel, like his near contemporary <a href="/wiki/Ecgberht_of_Ripon" title="Ecgberht of Ripon">Ecgberht</a> (d. 729), had once visited Ireland and formed a close association.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Reims">Bishop of Reims</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel_of_Reims&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Bishop of Reims">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In March 744, <a href="/wiki/Boniface" class="mw-redirect" title="Boniface">Boniface</a> presided over the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Soissons" title="Council of Soissons">Council of Soissons</a> and the conciliar acts record that Abel was elected archbishop (but of an unspecified see). In June, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Zacharias" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Zacharias">Pope Zacharias</a> confirmed this decision by sending Abel the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> which conferred episcopal authority.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Abel is next named in the sources as one of Boniface's 'fellow bishops' who around 746 sent a letter of exhortation to <a href="/wiki/%C3%86thelbald_of_Mercia" title="Æthelbald of Mercia">Æthelbald, king of Mercia</a> as part of his wider missionary efforts. It seems that <a href="/wiki/Milo_(bishop_of_Trier)" title="Milo (bishop of Trier)">Milo (bishop of Trier)</a> in effect controlled a number of episcopal sees (despite only being a laymen), while Abel remained in office only as suffragan bishop. In 751, Boniface once more addressed a letter to Pope Zacharias, in which he lamented the injustices of lay control over the church, but his erstwhile ambitions to change this were not realised in his lifetime.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is unknown when Abel gave up his position or died. The 9th-century <i>Life of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Remigius" title="Saint Remigius">Saint Remigius</a></i>, written by <a href="/wiki/Hincmar" title="Hincmar">Hincmar</a>, Archbishop of Rheims, claims that Milo had driven Abel out from the see in favour of <a href="/wiki/Tilpin" title="Tilpin">Tilpin</a>. The date given is 748, but it also may be the case that Tilpin only succeeded in 762 or 763 when Able died. It's suggested that Abel may have retired in his later years to Lobbes Abbey to become abbot.<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commemoration">Commemoration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abel_of_Reims&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Commemoration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abel has been recognized at various times as a <a href="/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" title="Dicastery for the Causes of Saints">pre-congregation</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. In the early modern period, Abel's name was inserted in a number of <a href="/wiki/Martyrology" title="Martyrology">martyrologies</a>, under a variety of dates - despite the fact he was not martyred. Examples are the 17th-century martyrology of <a href="/wiki/Gabriel_Bucelin" title="Gabriel Bucelin">Gabriel Bucelin</a>, the <i>Martyrologium Gallicanum</i> (1637) of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Andr%C3%A9_du_Saussay&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="André du Saussay (page does not exist)">André du Saussay</a>, and <a href="/wiki/John_Mabillon" class="mw-redirect" title="John Mabillon">John Mabillon</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> include a <i>Life</i> of Abel under 5 August, compiled by John Pinius.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
