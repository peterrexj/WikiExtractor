<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Cellach</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;"><a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh" title="Archbishop of Armagh">Archbishop of Armagh</a></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">1080<br /><a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">1129<br /><a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 April</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Cellach of Armagh</b> or <b>Celsus</b> or <b>Celestinus</b> (<a href="/wiki/1080_in_Ireland" class="mw-redirect" title="1080 in Ireland">1080</a>&#8211;<a href="/wiki/1129_in_Ireland" title="1129 in Ireland">1129</a>) was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Armagh" title="Archbishop of Armagh">Archbishop of Armagh</a> and an important contributor to the reform of the <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Irish</a> church in the twelfth century. He is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> as <b>Saint Cellach</b>. Though a member of the laicised ecclesiastical dynasty of Clann Sínaig, he took holy vows and gained priestly ordination. This put an end to the anomalous state of affairs, in effect since 966, whereby the supreme head of the Irish Church had been a layman.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Following the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_R%C3%A1ith_Bressail" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Ráith Bressail">Synod of Ráith Bressail</a>, in which a <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocesan</a> structure for Ireland was established, he became the first <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan</a> <a href="/wiki/Primacy_of_Ireland" title="Primacy of Ireland">primate of all Ireland</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_background">Early life and background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cellach_of_Armagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cellach was the son of Áed mac Máele Ísu meic Amalgada of the Clann Sínnaig. Áed had been abbot of Armagh and <i>Coarb Pátraic</i> ("heir" or "successor" of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>; head of the church of Armagh) from 1074 to 1091. The Clann Sínaig, of the Uí Echdach sept of the Airthir in <a href="/wiki/Airgialla" class="mw-redirect" title="Airgialla">Airgialla</a>, had monopolised the office of abbot of Armagh since 966. In later historiography Clann Sínaig has been associated with the type of secularisation that made a church reform necessary, described by Marie Térèse Flanagan as an "hereditarily entrenched laicized ecclesiastical dynasty" and even less flatteringly denounced by <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> as that "generatio mala et adultera".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Coarb_P.C3.A1traic"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Coarb_Pátraic">Coarb Pátraic</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cellach_of_Armagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Coarb Pátraic">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following the death of his granduncle Domnall mac Amalgada in August 1105, Cellach succeeded as abbot of Armagh and Coarb Pátraic. The <a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a> notes that this was done "by the choice of the men of Ireland".<sup id="cite_ref-AU1105.3_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AU1105.3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Unlike his lay predecessors/ancestors, he sought priestly ordination, which Flanagan has described as a "decisive reform step".<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It was in accordance with the first Synod of Cashel (1101), which had legislated against laymen holding ecclesiastical offices.<sup id="cite_ref-Flanagan91416_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flanagan91416-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He received orders Saturday 23 September 1105, on "the feast of <a href="/wiki/Adomn%C3%A1n" title="Adomnán">Adomnán</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-AU1105.3_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AU1105.3-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It passed six weeks from Domnalls death to Cellachs ordination, it has been suggested by Martin Holland that this delay was in order for the ordination to take place on the Saturday-<a href="/wiki/Ember_days" title="Ember days">emberday</a> in September. Holland suggests that this may imply that the consideration for canonicity at Armagh was in "better shape" than other sources might led us to believe.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The incumbent bishop of Armagh, Cáenchomrac Ó Baígill, died in 1106. This offered the opportunity for Cellach to unite the abbatial and episcopal office, and when he the same year visited <a href="/wiki/Munster" title="Munster">Munster</a> he "assumed the orders of a noble bishop by the command of the men of Ireland".<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> That this was done in Munster may be an indication of the influence held by <a href="/wiki/Muirchertach_Ua_Briain" title="Muirchertach Ua Briain">Muirchertach Ua Briain</a> (Murrough O'Brian)<sup id="cite_ref-coleman_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coleman-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> over ecclesiastical matters. The recent bishops of Dublin and Waterford had been consecrated in <a href="/wiki/Canterbury" title="Canterbury">Canterbury</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">archbishops</a> <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anselm_of_Canterbury" title="Anselm of Canterbury">Anselm</a>; <a href="/wiki/Gilla_P%C3%A1traic" title="Gilla Pátraic">Gilla Pátraic</a> in 1074, <a href="/wiki/Donngus_Ua_hAingliu" title="Donngus Ua hAingliu">Donngus Ua hAingliu</a> in 1085, Samuel Ua hAingliu and Máel Ísu Ua hAinmire in 1096. There is no indication that Canterbury was involved in the consecration of Cellach, and when <a href="/wiki/Gille_Espaic" class="mw-redirect" title="Gille Espaic">Gille Espaic</a> was consecrated as the first bishop of <a href="/wiki/Limerick" title="Limerick">Limerick</a> this was probably done by Cellach.<sup id="cite_ref-Flanagan91416_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flanagan91416-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gilla received a letter from Anselm congratulating him on his elevation to the see of Limerick, and there was no suggestion that Anselm felt Canterbury had been slighted or ought to have been involved.<sup id="cite_ref-Flanagan91416_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Flanagan91416-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A number of visitations made by Cellach as bishop (and later archbishop) are recorded in the <a href="/wiki/Irish_annals" title="Irish annals">Irish annals</a>: He visited <a href="/wiki/Cen%C3%A9l_nE%C3%B3gain" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenél nEógain">Cenél nEógain</a> in 1106, Munster in 1106 and 1120, <a href="/wiki/Connacht" title="Connacht">Connacht</a> in 1108 and 1116, and <a href="/wiki/Mide" class="mw-redirect" title="Mide">Mide</a> in 1110.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> The records of the visitations are followed by formulas such as "and he obtained a full tribute" or "brought away his full dues", indicating the official character of ecclesiastical submission embedded in such visitation.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup> Cellach also conducted negotiations between rivalling secular rulers, in particular between Muirchertach Ua Briain and the Northern Uí Néill claimant for high-kingship, <a href="/wiki/Domnall_Ua_Lochlainn" title="Domnall Ua Lochlainn">Domnall Ua Lochlainn</a> of the Cenel nEógain. There are records of Cellach making "a year's peace" between these two in the entries of the Annals of Ulster for 1107, 1109 and 1113.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his incumbency the priory of Sts. Peter and Paul at Armagh was re-founded by Imar, the learned preceptor of St. Malachy. This was the first establishment in Ireland into which the Canons Regular of St. Augustine had been introduced.<sup id="cite_ref-coleman_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coleman-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Synod_of_R.C3.A1ith_Bressail_and_Dublin"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Synod_of_Ráith_Bressail_and_Dublin">Synod of Ráith Bressail and Dublin</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cellach_of_Armagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Synod of Ráith Bressail and Dublin">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Cellach attended and played a prominent part in the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Rathbreasail" class="mw-redirect" title="Synod of Rathbreasail">Synod of Rathbreasail</a> in 1111. This synod, presided by Gilla Espaic<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> as <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> and attended by fifty bishops, three hundred priests and over three thousand laymen, marked the transition of the Irish church from a monastic to a diocesan and parish-based church. It established two metropolitan provinces, with archbishoprics at Armagh and Cashel. Prominence was given to Armagh, making Cellach the primate of the church in Ireland. Each province consisted of twelve territorial dioceses. 
</p><p>The see of Dublin was not included, as Dublin hitherto had been under primacy from Canterbury,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;note 2&#93;</a></sup> but according to Martin Holland, a place was left open for it, in the sense that only eleven dioceses was declared under Cashel.<sup id="cite_ref-Hollandreform_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hollandreform-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Flanagan however, asserts that "the decrees of the Synod of Ráith Bressail... ...had envisaged the absorption of Dublin into the newly created adjacent diocese of Glendalough".<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When bishop Samuel of Dublin died in 1121 A.D, Cellach claimed supremacy for Armagh over Dublin. The Annals of Ulster state that he "assumed the bishopric of Áth Cliath [Dublin]" and adds that this was "by the choice of foreigners and Irish",<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> i.e. with the support of both the Norse and Irish population. There was obviously some significant resistance against this in Dublin however, subdeacon called Gréne was quickly bishop by a party in the city and sent to Canterbury, where he was consecrated by archbishop <a href="/wiki/Ralph_d%27Escures" title="Ralph d&#39;Escures">Ralph d'Escures</a> 2 October 1121. According to Holland, it took Gréne some years before he gained possession of the see after his return, but when he did Dublin "stood apart from the newly organized Irish church".<sup id="cite_ref-Hollandreform_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hollandreform-14">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Flanagan gives a different interpretation, that Gréne was accepted as bishop of Dublin as part of some subsequent agreement, "probably in return for acknowledging the primacy of Cellach".<sup id="cite_ref-ODNB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODNB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cellach_of_Armagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1129 on a visitation of Munster he died and was buried in <a href="/wiki/Lismore,_County_Waterford" title="Lismore, County Waterford">Lismore</a> at his own request. Cellach was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Malachy" title="Saint Malachy">Máel Máedóc Ua Morgair</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-coleman_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-coleman-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Visio_Tnugdali">Visio Tnugdali</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cellach_of_Armagh&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Visio Tnugdali">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Visio Tnugdali written c.1149 refers to Cellach as follows: "When Saint Ruadan had fallen silent, Tundale looked happily about him and saw Saint Patrick of Ireland, dressed in shining robes alongside many bishops decked out in their finest regalia. They were all joyful and there was no sound of any sighing! Among that blessed company Tundale could see four bishops whom he recognised. They were all good men; one of them was <b>Saint Cellach</b>, a former archbishop of Armagh, who did much good for the sake of Our Lord. Another was Malachias O'Moore, who had become archbishop of Armagh after him and gave everything that he had to the poor. He founded a large number of churches and colleges, as many as forty-four in all, endowed them with land and rents and so allowed many men of religion to serve God devotedly, although he hardly retained enough for himself to live on".
</p>
