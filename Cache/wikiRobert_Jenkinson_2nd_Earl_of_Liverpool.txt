<p><b>Robert Banks Jenkinson, 2nd Earl of Liverpool</b>, <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:100%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/Knight_of_the_Order_of_the_Garter" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight of the Order of the Garter">KG</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council of the United Kingdom">PC</a><span style="font-size:14px;">&#44;</span>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Fellow_of_the_Royal_Society" title="Fellow of the Royal Society">FRS</a></span> (7 June 1770 – 4 December 1828) was a <a href="/wiki/British_Tory" class="mw-redirect" title="British Tory">British Tory</a> statesman who served as <a href="/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Prime Minister of the United Kingdom">Prime Minister of the United Kingdom</a> from 1812 to 1827. He also held many other important cabinet offices such as <a href="/wiki/Foreign_Secretary" title="Foreign Secretary">Foreign Secretary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_and_the_Colonies" title="Secretary of State for War and the Colonies">Secretary of State for War and the Colonies</a>. He was also a member of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Lords" title="House of Lords">House of Lords</a> and served as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Lords" title="Leader of the House of Lords">leader</a>.
</p><p>As prime minister, Liverpool called for repressive measures at domestic level to maintain order after the <a href="/wiki/Peterloo_Massacre" title="Peterloo Massacre">Peterloo Massacre</a> of 1819. He dealt smoothly with <a href="/wiki/The_Prince_Regent" class="mw-redirect" title="The Prince Regent">the Prince Regent</a> when <a href="/wiki/King_George_III" class="mw-redirect" title="King George III">King George III</a> was incapacitated. He also steered the country through the period of radicalism and unrest that followed the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars" title="Napoleonic Wars">Napoleonic Wars</a>. He favoured commercial and manufacturing interests as well as the landed interest. He sought a compromise of the heated issue of <a href="/wiki/Catholic_emancipation" title="Catholic emancipation">Catholic emancipation</a>. The revival of the economy strengthened his political position. By the 1820s, he was the leader of a reform faction of "Liberal Tories" who lowered the tariff, abolished the death penalty for many offences, and reformed the criminal law. By the time of his death, however, the <a href="/wiki/Tories_(British_political_party)" title="Tories (British political party)">Tory party</a>, which had dominated the House of Commons for over 40 years, was ripping itself apart.
</p><p>
Derry says he was: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>[A] capable and intelligent statesman, whose skill in building up his party, leading the country to victory in the war against Napoleon, and laying the foundations for prosperity outweighed his unpopularity in the immediate post-Waterloo years.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Important events during his tenure as prime minister included the <a href="/wiki/War_of_1812" title="War of 1812">War of 1812</a> with the United States, the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Sixth_Coalition" title="War of the Sixth Coalition">Sixth</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hundred_Days" title="Hundred Days">Seventh Coalitions</a> against the <a href="/wiki/First_French_Empire" title="First French Empire">French Empire</a>, the conclusion of the Napoleonic Wars at the <a href="/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna" title="Congress of Vienna">Congress of Vienna</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Corn_Laws" title="Corn Laws">Corn Laws</a>, the Peterloo Massacre, the <a href="/wiki/Doctrine_of_the_Trinity_Act_1813" title="Doctrine of the Trinity Act 1813">Trinitarian Act 1812</a> and the emerging issue of Catholic emancipation.  Scholars rank him highly among all British prime ministers, but he was also called "the Arch-mediocrity" by a later <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_(UK)" title="Conservative Party (UK)">Conservative</a> prime minister, <a href="/wiki/The_Earl_of_Beaconsfield" class="mw-redirect" title="The Earl of Beaconsfield">the Earl of Beaconsfield</a> (Benjamin Disraeli).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life:_1770-1790">Early life: 1770-1790</span></h2>
<p>Jenkinson was baptised on 29 June 1770 at <a href="/wiki/St._Margaret%27s,_Westminster" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Margaret&#39;s, Westminster">St. Margaret's, Westminster</a>, the son of <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">George III</a>'s close adviser <a href="/wiki/Charles_Jenkinson,_1st_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Charles Jenkinson, 1st Earl of Liverpool">Charles Jenkinson</a>, later the first <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Earl of Liverpool">Earl of Liverpool</a>, and his first wife, Amelia Watts. Jenkinson's 19-year-old mother, who was the daughter of a senior <a href="/wiki/East_India_Company" title="East India Company">East India Company</a> official, <a href="/wiki/William_Watts_(East_India_Company_official)" title="William Watts (East India Company official)">William Watts</a>, and of his wife <a href="/wiki/Begum_Johnson" title="Begum Johnson">Begum Johnson</a>, died from the <a href="/wiki/Maternal_death" title="Maternal death">effects of childbirth</a> one month after his birth.<sup id="cite_ref-Nineteenth-Century_British_Premiers_p._82_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Nineteenth-Century_British_Premiers_p._82-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Through his mother's grandmother, Isabella Beizor, Jenkinson was descended from Portuguese settlers in India; he may also have been one-sixteenth <a href="/wiki/Indian_people" title="Indian people">Indian</a> in ancestry.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From an early age, Jenkinson had a strong influential training in economics and politics from his father, who was at the time, a leading politician and adviser to <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">King George III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cato.org_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cato.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> To give his son the highest education possible, Jenkinson was sent to be educated at <a href="/wiki/Charterhouse_School" title="Charterhouse School">Charterhouse School</a>, whose education was broader than that of the prestigious <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a>, to which many sons of noblemen were sent be educated.<sup id="cite_ref-cato.org_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cato.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> While at Charterhouse, Jenkinson's father insisted that he study political economy and read current politics. Jenkinson later matriculated at <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" title="Christ Church, Oxford">Christ Church, Oxford</a>, in 1787.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>

In the summer of 1789, he took his Grand Tour, first travelling to <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> and then to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cato.org_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cato.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In Paris, Jenkinson spent four months there to perfect his French and enlarge his social experience among Parisian Society.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> While there, he witnessed the <a id="cite_ref-cato.org_7-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cato.org-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> He spent next the three months in Oxford to complete his terms of residence, and in May 1790 was created Master of Arts.Jenkinson was educated at <a href="/wiki/Charterhouse_School" title="Charterhouse School">Charterhouse School</a> and matriculated at <a href="/wiki/Christ_Church,_Oxford" id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_career:_1790-1801">Early career: 1790-1801</span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Member_of_Parliament">Member of Parliament</span></h3>
<p>He won election to the <a href="/wiki/British_House_of_Commons" class="mw-redirect" title="British House of Commons">House of Commons</a> in 1790 for <a href="/wiki/Rye_(UK_Parliament_constituency)" title="Rye (UK Parliament constituency)">Rye</a>, a seat he would hold until 1803; at the time, however, he was below the age of assent to Parliament, so he refrained from taking his seat and spent the following winter and early spring in an extended tour of the continent. This tour took in the Netherlands and Italy; at its conclusion he was old enough to take his seat in Parliament. It is not clear exactly when he entered the Commons, but as his twenty-first birthday was not reached until almost the end of the 1791 session, it is possible that he waited until the following year.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="House_of_Commons">House of Commons</span></h3>
<p>With the help of his father's influence and his political talent, he rose relatively fast in the Tory government. In February 1792, he gave the reply to <a href="/wiki/Samuel_Whitbread_(1764%E2%80%931815)" title="Samuel Whitbread (1764–1815)">Samuel Whitbread</a>'s critical motion on the government's Russian policy. He delivered several other speeches during the session, and was a strong opposer of abolitionism and <a href="/wiki/William_Wilberforce" title="William Wilberforce">William Wilberforce</a>. He served as a member of the Board of Control for India from 1793 to 1796.
</p><p>In the defence movement that followed the outbreak of hostilities with France, Jenkinson, was one of the first of the ministers of the government to enlist in the militia. He became a <a href="/wiki/Colonel_(United_Kingdom)" title="Colonel (United Kingdom)">colonel</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Cinque_Ports_Fencibles" class="mw-redirect" title="Cinque Ports Fencibles">Cinque Ports Fencibles</a> in 1794, and his military duties led to frequent absences from the Commons. His regiment was sent to Scotland in 1796, and he was quartered for a time in <a href="/wiki/Dumfries" title="Dumfries">Dumfries</a>.
</p><p>In 1797, the then Lord Hawkesbury was the cavalry commander of the Cinque Ports Light Dragoons who ran amok following a protest against the <a href="/wiki/Militia_Act_1797" class="mw-redirect" title="Militia Act 1797">Militia Act</a> at <a href="/wiki/Tranent" title="Tranent">Tranent</a> in <a href="/wiki/East_Lothian" title="East Lothian">East Lothian</a>; twelve civilians were killed. Author James Miller wrote in 1844 that "His lordship was blamed for remaining at <a href="/wiki/Haddington,_East_Lothian" title="Haddington, East Lothian">Haddington</a>, as his presence might have prevented the outrages of the soldiery."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His parliamentary attendance also suffered from his reaction when his father angrily opposed his projected marriage with <a href="/wiki/Louisa_Jenkinson,_Countess_of_Liverpool" title="Louisa Jenkinson, Countess of Liverpool">Lady Louisa Hervey</a>, daughter of the <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Hervey,_4th_Earl_of_Bristol" title="Frederick Hervey, 4th Earl of Bristol">Earl of Bristol</a>. After Pitt and the King had intervened on his behalf, the wedding finally took place at <a href="/wiki/Wimbledon,_London" title="Wimbledon, London">Wimbledon</a> on 25 March 1795. In May 1796, when his father was created Earl of Liverpool, he took the courtesy title of <b>Lord Hawkesbury</b> and remained in the Commons. He became <b>Baron Hawkesbury</b> in his own right and was elevated to the House of Lords in November 1803, as recognition of his work as Foreign Secretary.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (May 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> He also served as <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Mint" title="Master of the Mint">Master of the Mint</a> (1799–1801).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Foreign_Secretary:1801-1804">Foreign Secretary:1801-1804</span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Entry">Entry</span></h3>
<p>As the question of <a href="/wiki/Catholic_emancipation" title="Catholic emancipation">Catholic emancipation</a> came apparent and as <a href="/wiki/George_III_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George III of the United Kingdom">the King</a> refused to pass on to law, in response, Pitt resigned as prime minister as a result. This departure left the King to choose a successor and so appointed one of Pitt's prominent allies, <a href="/wiki/Henry_Addington" title="Henry Addington">Henry Addington</a>, as the new prime minister. When Addington's government came to power, the young Liverpool entered the cabinet in 1801 as <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_(UK)" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs (UK)">Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_8-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Addington's main political agenda was peace and promised that as prime minister that, he will negotiate with the French. In this capacity he was entrusted by Addington negotiate with the <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and pursue an immediate peace deal, which he set to do, as his first duty.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated3_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated3-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Treaty_of_Amiens">Treaty of Amiens</span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars" title="French Revolutionary Wars">French Revolutionary Wars</a> begun as a direct result of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>, of which the aftermath saw a growing sentiment of <a href="/wiki/Radicalism" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Radicalism">radicalism</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:WikiProject_Disambiguation/Fixing_links" title="Wikipedia:WikiProject Disambiguation/Fixing links"><span title="Link needs disambiguation (September 2023)">disambiguation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and a shock wave of political turmoil being send across Europe. At the beginning of the Revolution, the British government led by <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">William Pitt</a> opposed its ideology and the belief that it would undermine the principles of Britain's established <a href="/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy" title="Constitutional monarchy">constitutional monarchy</a>. Also the French revolutionary ideology heavily opposed such forms of government containing a <a href="/wiki/Monarchy" title="Monarchy">monarchy</a>, while favouring a <a href="/wiki/Republic" title="Republic">republican form</a> of government, which Britain and other countries in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> opposed and wished to end such ideologies from spreading elsewhere. What followed was a decade long series of wars, known as the <a href="/wiki/Wars_of_the_Coalition" class="mw-redirect" title="Wars of the Coalition">Coalition wars</a> that resulted in Britain and its allies being defeated or being pushed to the position of economic crisis and so many opted to peace.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As Foreign Secretary, Liverpool has to face the crisis war in Europe and negotiate peace as soon as possible. As Britain and its allies in the continent were unable to defeat France in any way and the long duration of the war itself, led to a period of instability as the British war effort has been over overwhelmed by nearly a decade of war without any results. As per Addington's wishes, Liverpool opened negotiations with France. Liverpool immediately, first, communicated with the French commissary for <a href="/wiki/Prisoners_of_war" class="mw-redirect" title="Prisoners of war">prisoners of war</a> in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, <a href="/wiki/Louis_Guillaume_Otto" class="mw-redirect" title="Louis Guillaume Otto">Louis Guillaume Otto</a>, through whom he could contact and agree to <a href="/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleon Bonaparte">Napoleon Bonaparte</a>’s earlier proposals.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Liverpool stated that he “wanted to open discussions on terms for a peace agreement”, but Otto, who was under direct and strict orders from Bonaparte, begun to open negotiations by mid-September. Despite his efforts, negotiations seemed fruitless with Otto and unhappy with this result, Liverpool sent diplomat <a href="/wiki/Anthony_Merry" title="Anthony Merry">Anthony Merry</a> to <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> to open a second round of negotiations with his own French counterpart, French <a href="/wiki/Foreign_Minister" class="mw-redirect" title="Foreign Minister">Foreign Minister</a> <a href="/wiki/Talleyrand" class="mw-redirect" title="Talleyrand">Talleyrand</a> by mid-September. By this time, written negotiations followed and progressed to the point Otto met shaft a preliminary agreement on 30 September and finally in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, which was finally published the next day. Thus signing the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Amiens" title="Treaty of Amiens">Treaty of Amiens</a> and concluding the end of the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Second_Coalition" title="War of the Second Coalition">War of the Second Coalition</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Resignation">Resignation</span></h3>
<p>Most of his time as Foreign Secretary was spent dealing with the nations of France and the United States. But this was cut short, as shortly afterwards, the Addington government came under attack from the opposition and was criticised for its lack of credibility when war resumed yet again in 1803 and the start of the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Third_Coalition" title="War of the Third Coalition">War of the Third Coalition</a>. Soon the government fell into disarray and quickly led to the reappointment of Pitt as prime minister.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Home_Secretary:_1804-1809">Home Secretary: 1804-1809</span></h2>
<p>When Pitt came to power for the second time, he reappointed the former Foreign secretary and he was soon continued to serve in the cabinet as <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a> in <a href="/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Younger" title="William Pitt the Younger">Pitt the Younger</a>'s second government.
</p><p>Soon after taking the role of Prime Minister, Pitt fell ill and was sick during 1805. Despite a terminal illness, he managed the out-going war in Europe and was able to hold together his Cabinet and his country against the will-power of the French. While Pitt was seriously ill, Liverpool was in charge of the cabinet and drew up the King's Speech for the official opening of Parliament. When William Pitt died in 1806, the King needed choose a successor to office and asked the Home Secretary Liverpool to accept the post of Prime Minister, but he refused, as he believed he lacked a governing majority.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_8-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was then became <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_(UK)" class="mw-redirect" title="Leader of the Opposition (UK)">Leader of the Opposition</a> during <a href="/wiki/William_Grenville,_1st_Baron_Grenville" title="William Grenville, 1st Baron Grenville">Lord Grenville</a>'s ministry. This was the second only time that Liverpool did not hold government office between 1793 and after his retirement. In 1807, after an year in opposition, he resumed office as <a href="/wiki/Home_Secretary" title="Home Secretary">Home Secretary</a> in <a href="/wiki/William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Cavendish-Bentinck, 3rd Duke of Portland">the Duke of Portland</a>'s new ministry.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_8-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="War_Secretary:_1809-1812">War Secretary: 1809-1812</span></h2>
<p>Lord Hawkesbury, by this point, assumed the title of Lord Liverpool, as he had now become by the death of his father in December 1808. After the fall of the <a href="/wiki/Whigs_(British_political_party)" title="Whigs (British political party)">Whig</a> <a href="/wiki/Second_Portland_ministry" title="Second Portland ministry">Portland ministry</a>, saw the sudden rise of <a href="/wiki/Spencer_Perceval" title="Spencer Perceval">Spencer Perceval</a> to the office of prime minister. Perceval was reluctant appoint a fellow Tory to his cabinet, as the Whigs were splintered and unified, which would allow the Tories to govern opposed and asked Liverpool to join his new cabinet. Liverpool accepted the position of <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_and_the_Colonies" title="Secretary of State for War and the Colonies">Secretary of State for War and the Colonies</a> in Perceval's <a href="/wiki/Perceval_ministry" title="Perceval ministry">new government</a> in 1809. Liverpool's first step on taking up his new post was to elicit from General <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Arthur Wellesley</a> (the future Duke of Wellington) a strong enough statement of his ability to resist a French attack to persuade the cabinet to commit themselves to the maintenance of his small force in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>. In 1810 Liverpool was made a colonel of militia.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="War_finance">War finance</span></h3>
<p>By 1809 the political situation in Europe have changed dramatically. Napoleon has conquered most, if not almost, all of Europe except for Russia, Austria, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Europe" title="Eastern Europe">Eastern Europe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Northern_Europe" title="Northern Europe">Northern Europe</a>. The war against <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Napoleonic France">Napoleonic France</a> did not improve for Britain's war effort and is by this point, <a href="/wiki/French_Revolutionary_and_Napoleonic_Wars" title="French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars">Five coalitions</a> have failed to defeat Napoleon or bring down France. This did not help Britain overcome the <a href="/wiki/Continental_System" title="Continental System">Continental System</a>, issued by the 1806 <a href="/wiki/Berlin_Decree" title="Berlin Decree">Berlin Decree</a>, which forbade most countries of Europe from  Engaging in trade and commerce with the British and prevented them from trading British goods. This not only effected Britain and all countries as they were dependent on British supplies, mainly British allies. But to prevent Britain falling to an economic crisis, Liverpool as War Secretary has raised loans to help the Britain win the war and also help Britain's allies in need. At home, with a combination of strong government finances and sustain financial attrition was able to save Britain from falling behind the economic fallout. And by using the <a href="/wiki/Royal_Navy" title="Royal Navy">Royal Navy</a> to undermine French naval activities, a blockade across the <a href="/wiki/English_Channel" title="English Channel">English Channel</a> and with persistent but moderate military pressure on France would ultimately bear fruit and will win the war.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Prime_Minister:_1812-1827">Prime Minister: 1812-1827<span class="anchor" id="Premiership"></span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Prime_ministership_of_the_Earl_of_Liverpool" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime ministership of the Earl of Liverpool">Prime ministership of the Earl of Liverpool</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Appointment">Appointment</span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Liverpool_ministry" title="Liverpool ministry">Liverpool ministry</a></div>
<p>When <a href="/wiki/Assassination_of_Spencer_Perceval" title="Assassination of Spencer Perceval">Perceval was assassinated in May 1812</a>, <a href="/wiki/George_IV_of_the_United_Kingdom" class="mw-redirect" title="George IV of the United Kingdom">George, the Prince Regent</a>, successively tried to appoint four men to succeed him, but failed; Liverpool, the fifth choice for the post, reluctantly accepted office on 8 June 1812.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911_8-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEChisholm1911-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The cabinet proposed Liverpool as his successor with <a href="/wiki/Robert_Stewart,_Viscount_Castlereagh" title="Robert Stewart, Viscount Castlereagh">Lord Castlereagh</a> as leader in the Commons but after an adverse vote in the Lower House, they subsequently both gave their resignations. The Prince Regent, however, found it impossible to form a different coalition and confirmed Liverpool as prime minister on 8 June. No successor, in over 200 years, has been younger. Liverpool's government contained some of the future great leaders of Britain, such as Lord Castlereagh, <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a> and <a href="/wiki/William_Huskisson" title="William Huskisson">William Huskisson</a>. Liverpool is considered a skilled politician, and held together the liberal and reactionary wings of the Tory party, which his successors, Canning, <a href="/wiki/F._J._Robinson,_1st_Viscount_Goderich" title="F. J. Robinson, 1st Viscount Goderich">Goderich</a> and Wellington, had great difficulty with.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foreign_affairs">Foreign affairs</span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="War">War</span></h4>
<p>At the beginging of his premiership, Liverpool was faced directly with conflict, the <a href="/wiki/War_of_1812" title="War of 1812">War of 1812</a> with the United States and the final campaigns of the <a href="/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars" title="Napoleonic Wars">Napoleonic Wars</a> were fought during his governance. The Peninsular Campaigns saw the British army led by the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Wellington" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a> drive the French out of <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>. At the peace negotiations that followed, Liverpool's main concern was to obtain a European settlement that would ensure the independence of the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a>, Spain and <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a>, and confine France inside its pre-war frontiers without damaging its national integrity. To achieve this, he was ready to return all British colonial conquests. Within this broad framework, he gave <a href="/wiki/Robert_Stewart,_Viscount_Castlereagh" title="Robert Stewart, Viscount Castlereagh">Castlereagh</a> discretion at the <a href="/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna" title="Congress of Vienna">Congress of Vienna</a>, the next most important event of his ministry. At the congress he gave prompt approval for Castlereagh's bold initiative in making the defensive alliance with <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a> and France in January 1815. In the aftermath of the defeat of Napoleon – who had briefly been exiled to the island <a href="/wiki/Elba" title="Elba">Elba</a> and <a href="/wiki/Hundred_Days" title="Hundred Days">escaped exile and returned to rule France</a>. The allies immediately begun mobilizing their armies to fight the escaped emperor and an combined Anglo-Allied army was send under the command of Wellington to assist Britain's allies. Within few months, Napoleon was decisively defeated at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo" title="Battle of Waterloo">Battle of Waterloo</a> and peace followed through the now exhausted continent.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:BritishEmpire1815.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/BritishEmpire1815.png/220px-BritishEmpire1815.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="102" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/BritishEmpire1815.png/330px-BritishEmpire1815.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/BritishEmpire1815.png/440px-BritishEmpire1815.png 2x" data-file-width="1357" data-file-height="628" /></a><figcaption>The <a href="/wiki/British_Empire" title="British Empire">British Empire</a> at the end of the Napoleonic Wars in 1815</figcaption></figure>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Congress_of_Vienna">Congress of Vienna</span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Congress_of_Vienna.PNG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Congress_of_Vienna.PNG/220px-Congress_of_Vienna.PNG" decoding="async" width="220" height="164" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Congress_of_Vienna.PNG/330px-Congress_of_Vienna.PNG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Congress_of_Vienna.PNG/440px-Congress_of_Vienna.PNG 2x" data-file-width="1883" data-file-height="1400" /></a><figcaption><i>The Congress of Vienna</i> by <a href="/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Isabey" title="Jean-Baptiste Isabey">Jean-Baptiste Isabey</a>, 1819</figcaption></figure>
<p>After the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo" title="Battle of Waterloo">Battle of Waterloo</a> saw the conclusion of the <a href="/wiki/War_of_the_Seventh_Coalition" class="mw-redirect" title="War of the Seventh Coalition">War of the Seventh Coalition</a> and the ushering in of peace in Europe, after the continuous decade-long wars that were fought as a direct aftermath of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a> in 1789, now ceased. Despite this, Napoleon's conquests left a defining situation in Europe and all countries of Europe to fill or reconstruct many of its cracks. Soon after the victory at Waterloo and peace was secured, the nations of Europe came together to talk of securing peace. The Austrian <a href="/wiki/Foreign_Minister" class="mw-redirect" title="Foreign Minister">Foreign Minister</a> <a href="/wiki/Klemens_Von_Metternich" class="mw-redirect" title="Klemens Von Metternich">Klemens Von Metternich</a>, a skilled diplomat and capable politician, called on European countries to gather at Vienna to negotiate a peacekeeping effort, with the aim of establishing a lasting peace. The main powers: <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">Britain</a>,<a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prussia" title="Prussia">Prussia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a> and last the defeated <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> all met at Vienna for negotiations and representing their perspective countries. Lord Liverpool, as prime minister, decided to a send one of his most skilled ministers to attend the peace assembly in France, and so appointed his <a href="/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Foreign_Affairs_(UK)" class="mw-redirect" title="Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs (UK)">Foreign Secretary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Robert_Stewart,_2nd_Marquess_of_Castlereagh" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert Stewart, 2nd Marquess of Castlereagh">Lord Castlereagh</a>, to be sent to <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>. The gathering of nations at the Austrian city, is known as the <a href="/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna" title="Congress of Vienna">Congress of Vienna</a> and was attended by some of Europe's most influential diplomats and politicians and the result of this congressional meeting was the establishment of a peaceful Europe. So, as a direct result, it saw the maps of Europe being redrawn to the previous borders there before the war, opposing or eradicating <a href="/wiki/Classical_radicalism" title="Classical radicalism">radicalism</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:WikiProject_Disambiguation/Fixing_links" title="Wikipedia:WikiProject Disambiguation/Fixing links"><span title="Link needs disambiguation (September 2023)">disambiguation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and <a href="/wiki/Liberalism" title="Liberalism">liberal ideals</a>, such as that of the French Revolution, from spreading across the continent and securing a lasting peace that would see no major conflict being ever fought between the nations of Europe. This became known as the <a href="/wiki/Concert_of_Europe" title="Concert of Europe">Concert of Europe</a> and its ideals of a longstanding peace and political landscape would endure to the early <a href="/wiki/20th_Century" class="mw-redirect" title="20th Century">20th Century</a> until the start of the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a> in 1914.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Colonial_expansion">Colonial expansion</span></h4>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/British_Empire" title="British Empire">British Empire</a> in 1815 and throughout Liverpool's premiership was thriving and expanding across the globe and was raising as greatest colonial empire of all time. As the 20-year long war in Europe ended with peace being established in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>, Britain finally established itself as a main power, alongside <a href="/wiki/Austria" title="Austria">Austria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russia" title="Russia">Russia</a> and <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>. But by this point, Britain proved itself to be a far more superior power than any other nation in Europe. Through its vast empire, strong economy and military might, it made Britain one of the earliest known world power with establishing a hegemony over the globe. Through <a href="/wiki/Trade" title="Trade">trade</a> and <a href="/wiki/Commerce" title="Commerce">commerce</a>, Britain accumulated large amounts of wealth and through its <a href="/wiki/Industrialism" class="mw-redirect" title="Industrialism">industrialisation</a>, mass production of <a href="/wiki/Cotton" title="Cotton">cotton</a>, <a href="/wiki/Iron" title="Iron">iron</a>, <a href="/wiki/Wheat" title="Wheat">wheat</a> increased dramatically. Britain's colonial endeavours saw British armies conquering and subjugating new territories. In <a href="/wiki/South_Asia" title="South Asia">South Asia</a>, saw British territories gradually expanded far and beyond. The annexation of <a href="/wiki/Nepal" title="Nepal">Nepal</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Nepalese_War" title="Anglo-Nepalese War">Anglo-Nepalese War</a> saw it incorporated to <a href="/wiki/British_India" class="mw-redirect" title="British India">British India</a> as a result. Britain also expanded its borders within the <a href="/wiki/British_Raj" title="British Raj">Indian frontiers</a> during the <a href="/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War" title="Third Anglo-Maratha War">Third Anglo-Maratha War</a> against the long-time British enemy of the <a href="/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Maratha Confederacy">Maratha Confederacy</a>. In modern-day <a href="/wiki/Myanmar" title="Myanmar">Myanmar</a>, conflict with the <a href="/wiki/Third_Burmese_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Burmese Empire">Third Burmese Empire</a> led to the <a href="/wiki/First_Anglo-Burmese_War" title="First Anglo-Burmese War">First Anglo-Burmese War</a>, which saw the strategic weakening of the Burma and led to the cessation of territories in <a href="/wiki/Assam" title="Assam">Assam</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manipur" title="Manipur">Manipur</a>, <a href="/wiki/Arakan" title="Arakan">Arakan</a> and other territories south of the <a href="/wiki/Salween_River" title="Salween River">Salween River</a> as a result. Furthermore, in <a href="/wiki/South_Africa" title="South Africa">South Africa</a>, the complete integration of the <a href="/wiki/Cape_Colony" title="Cape Colony">Cape Colony</a> in <a href="/wiki/South_Africa" title="South Africa">South Africa</a> and victory over the <a href="/wiki/Xhosa_tribes" class="mw-redirect" title="Xhosa tribes">Xhosa tribes</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Xhosa_Wars" title="Xhosa Wars">Fourth Xhosa War</a> in which the Colony were able to push the tribes beyond the <a href="/wiki/Fish_River_(Namibia)" title="Fish River (Namibia)">Fish River</a> and reverse many of the Xhosa's past success. During Liverpool's tenure as Prime Minister, saw the establishment of a permanent penal colony in <a href="/wiki/Australia" title="Australia">Australia</a> and new settlements in <a href="/wiki/New_Zealand" title="New Zealand">New Zealand</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-historic-uk.com_13-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-historic-uk.com-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Domestic_Affairs">Domestic Affairs</span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Home_trouble">Home trouble</span></h4>
<p>Inevitably taxes rose to compensate for borrowing and to pay off the national debt, which led to widespread disturbance between 1812 and 1822. Around this time, the group known as <a href="/wiki/Luddites" class="mw-redirect" title="Luddites">Luddites</a> began industrial action. Throughout the period 1811–1816, there was a series of incidents of machine-breaking and many of those convicted faced execution.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated3_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated3-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Corn_Laws">Corn Laws</span></h4>
<p>Agriculture remained a problem because good harvests between 1819 and 1822 had brought down prices and evoked a cry for greater protection. When the powerful agricultural lobby in Parliament demanded protection in the aftermath, Liverpool gave in to political necessity. Under governmental supervision the notorious <a href="/wiki/Corn_Laws" title="Corn Laws">Corn Laws</a> of 1815 were passed prohibiting the import of foreign wheat until the domestic price reached a minimum accepted level. Liverpool, however, was in principle a free-trader, but had to accept the bill as a temporary measure to ease the transition to peacetime conditions. His chief economic problem during his time as Prime Minister was that of the nation's finances.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The interest on the <a href="/wiki/National_debt" class="mw-redirect" title="National debt">national debt</a>, massively swollen by the enormous expenditure of the final war years, together with the war <a href="/wiki/Pensions" class="mw-redirect" title="Pensions">pensions</a>, absorbed the greater part of normal government revenue. The refusal of the <a href="/wiki/British_House_of_Commons" class="mw-redirect" title="British House of Commons">House of Commons</a> in 1816 to continue the wartime income tax left ministers with no immediate alternative but to go on with the ruinous system of borrowing to meet necessary annual expenditure. Liverpool eventually facilitated a return to the gold standard in 1819.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Liverpool argued for the abolition of the wider slave trade at the Congress of Vienna, and at home he supported the repeal of the <a href="/wiki/Combination_Act" class="mw-redirect" title="Combination Act">Combination Laws</a> banning workers from combining into trade unions in 1824.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> In the latter year the newly formed Royal National Institution for the Preservation of Life from Shipwreck, later the <a href="/wiki/RNLI" class="mw-redirect" title="RNLI">RNLI</a>, obtained Liverpool as its first president.<sup id="cite_ref-lewis_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lewis-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Assassination_attempt">Assassination attempt</span></h4>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Peterloo_Massacre.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Peterloo_Massacre.png/220px-Peterloo_Massacre.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Peterloo_Massacre.png/330px-Peterloo_Massacre.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/98/Peterloo_Massacre.png/440px-Peterloo_Massacre.png 2x" data-file-width="1280" data-file-height="853" /></a><figcaption>Print of the <a href="/wiki/Peterloo_massacre" class="mw-redirect" title="Peterloo massacre">Peterloo massacre</a> published by Richard Carlile</figcaption></figure>
<p>The reports of the secret committees he obtained in 1817 pointed to the existence of an organised network of disaffected political societies, especially in the manufacturing areas. Liverpool told Peel that the disaffection in the country seemed even worse than in 1794. Because of a largely perceived threat to the government, temporary legislation was introduced. He suspended <a href="/wiki/Habeas_corpus" title="Habeas corpus">habeas corpus</a> in both Great Britain (1817) and Ireland (1822). Following the <a href="/wiki/Peterloo_Massacre" title="Peterloo Massacre">Peterloo Massacre</a> in 1819, his government imposed the repressive <a href="/wiki/Six_Acts" title="Six Acts">Six Acts</a> legislation which limited, among other things, free speech and the right to gather for peaceful demonstration.  In 1820, as a result of these measures, Liverpool and other cabinet ministers were targeted for assassination. They escaped harm when the <a href="/wiki/Cato_Street_conspiracy" class="mw-redirect" title="Cato Street conspiracy">Cato Street conspiracy</a> was foiled.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Catholic_emancipation">Catholic emancipation</span></h3>
<p>During the 19th century, and, in particular, during Liverpool's time in office, <a href="/wiki/Catholic_emancipation" title="Catholic emancipation">Catholic emancipation</a> was a source of great conflict. In 1805, in his first important statement of his views on the subject, Liverpool had argued that the special relationship of the monarch with the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>, and the refusal of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholics">Roman Catholics</a> to take the oath of supremacy, justified their exclusion from political power. Throughout his career, he remained opposed to the idea of Catholic emancipation, though he did see marginal concessions as important to the stability of the nation.
</p><p>The decision of 1812 to remove the issue from collective cabinet policy, followed in 1813 by the defeat of Grattan's Roman <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Relief_Bill" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Relief Bill">Catholic Relief Bill</a>, brought a period of calm. Liverpool supported marginal concessions such as the admittance of English Roman Catholics to the higher ranks of the armed forces, the magistracy, and the parliamentary franchise; but he remained opposed to their participation in parliament itself. In the 1820s, pressure from the liberal wing of the Commons and the rise of the Catholic Association in Ireland revived the controversy.
</p><p>By the date of Sir Francis Burdett's Catholic Relief Bill in 1825, emancipation looked a likely success. Indeed, the success of the bill in the Commons in April, followed by <a href="/wiki/Robert_Peel" title="Robert Peel">Robert Peel</a>'s tender of resignation, finally persuaded Liverpool that he should retire. When Canning made a formal proposal that the cabinet should back the bill, Liverpool was convinced that his administration had come to its end. <a href="/wiki/George_Canning" title="George Canning">George Canning</a> then succeeded him as Prime Minister. Catholic emancipation however was not fully implemented until the major changes of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Relief_Act_1829" title="Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829">Roman Catholic Relief Act 1829</a> under the leadership of the <a href="/wiki/Arthur_Wellesley,_1st_Duke_of_Wellington" title="Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington">Duke of Wellington</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sir_Robert_Peel" class="mw-redirect" title="Sir Robert Peel">Sir Robert Peel</a>, and with the work of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Association" title="Catholic Association">Catholic Association</a> established in 1823.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Retirement_and_death">Retirement and death</span></h2>
<p>Liverpool's first wife, Louisa, died at 54. He soon married again, on 24 September 1822, to <a href="/wiki/Mary_Jenkinson,_Countess_of_Liverpool" title="Mary Jenkinson, Countess of Liverpool">Lady Mary Chester</a>, a long-time friend of Louisa.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  Liverpool finally retired on 9 April 1827 after suffering a severe <a href="/wiki/Cerebral_hemorrhage" class="mw-redirect" title="Cerebral hemorrhage">cerebral hemorrhage</a> at his <a href="/wiki/Fife_House,_Whitehall" title="Fife House, Whitehall">Fife House residence in Whitehall</a> two months earlier,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> and asked the King to seek a successor. He suffered another minor stroke in July, after which he lingered on at <a href="/wiki/Coombe,_Kingston_upon_Thames" title="Coombe, Kingston upon Thames">Coombe</a> until a third attack on 4 December 1828 from which he died.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Having died childless, he was succeeded as <a href="/wiki/Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Earl of Liverpool">Earl of Liverpool</a> by his younger half-brother <a href="/wiki/Charles_Jenkinson,_3rd_Earl_of_Liverpool" title="Charles Jenkinson, 3rd Earl of Liverpool">Charles</a>. He was buried in <a href="/wiki/Church_of_St_Mary,_Hawkesbury" title="Church of St Mary, Hawkesbury">Hawkesbury parish church</a>, Gloucestershire, beside his father and his first wife. His personal estate was registered at under £120,000.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span></h2>
<p>Historian <a href="/wiki/Robert_William_Seton-Watson" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert William Seton-Watson">R. W. Seton-Watson</a> sums up Liverpool's strengths and weaknesses:
</p>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>No one would claim Liverpool as a man of genius, but he had qualities of tact, firmness and endurance to which historians have rarely done full justice: and thus it came about that he held the office of Premier over a period more than twice as long as any other successor of Pitt, long after peace had been restored to Europe. One reason for his ascendancy was that he had an unrivalled insight into the whole machinery of government, having filled successively every Secretaryship of State, and tested the efficiency and mutual relations of politicians and officials alike.... He had a much wider acquaintance with foreign affairs than many who have held his high office.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Liverpool was the first British Prime Minister to regularly wear long trousers instead of <a href="/wiki/Breeches" title="Breeches">knee breeches</a>. He entered office at the age of 42 years and one day, making him younger than all of his successors. Liverpool served as Prime Minister for a total of 14 years and 305 days, making him the longest-serving Prime Minister of the 19th century. As of 2022, none of Liverpool's successors has served longer.
</p><p>In London, Liverpool Street and <a href="/wiki/Liverpool_Road" title="Liverpool Road">Liverpool Road</a>, Islington, are named after Lord Liverpool. The Canadian town of <a href="/wiki/Hawkesbury,_Ontario" title="Hawkesbury, Ontario">Hawkesbury, Ontario</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Hawkesbury_River" title="Hawkesbury River">Hawkesbury River</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Liverpool_Plains" title="Liverpool Plains">Liverpool Plains</a>, New South Wales, Australia, <a href="/wiki/Liverpool,_New_South_Wales" title="Liverpool, New South Wales">Liverpool</a>, New South Wales, and the <a href="/wiki/Liverpool_River" title="Liverpool River">Liverpool River</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Territory" title="Northern Territory">Northern Territory</a> of Australia were also named after Lord Liverpool.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Lord Liverpool, as Prime Minister to whose government <a href="/wiki/Nathan_Mayer_Rothschild" title="Nathan Mayer Rothschild">Nathan Mayer Rothschild</a> was a lender, was portrayed by American actor <a href="/wiki/Gilbert_Emery" title="Gilbert Emery">Gilbert Emery</a> in the 1934 movie, <i><a href="/wiki/The_House_of_Rothschild" title="The House of Rothschild">The House of Rothschild</a></i>.
</p>
