<p><b>Deusdedit</b> (died <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/circa#English" class="extiw" title="wikt:circa">c.</a><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;664</span>) was a medieval <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, the first native-born holder of the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Canterbury">see of Canterbury</a>. By birth an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a>, he became archbishop in 655 and held the office for more than nine years until his death, probably from <a href="/wiki/Plague_of_664" title="Plague of 664">plague</a>. Deusdedit's successor as archbishop was one of his priests at Canterbury. There is some controversy over the exact date of Deusdedit's death, owing to discrepancies in the medieval written work that records his life. Little is known about his episcopate, but he was considered to be a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> after his demise. A <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">saint's life</a> was written after his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were moved from their original burial place in 1091.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Deusdedit_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A post-<a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman Conquest</a> tradition, originating with <a href="/wiki/Goscelin" title="Goscelin">Goscelin</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> gives Deusdedit's original name as <i>Frithona</i>, possibly a corruption of Frithuwine.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks67_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks67-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> He was consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Ithamar_(bishop)" title="Ithamar (bishop)">Ithamar</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rochester" title="Bishop of Rochester">Bishop of Rochester</a>, on 26 March<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook213_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook213-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> or perhaps 12&#160;March 655.<sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was the sixth archbishop after the arrival of the <a href="/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_Gregorian_mission" title="List of members of the Gregorian mission">Gregorian missionaries</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Bede176_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede176-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and the first to be a native of the island of Great Britain rather than an Italian, having been born a <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">West Saxon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks67_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks67-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Hindley45_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley45-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> One reason for the long period between the <a href="/wiki/Christianization" title="Christianization">Christianization</a> of the Kentish kingdom from <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_paganism" title="Anglo-Saxon paganism">Anglo-Saxon paganism</a> in about 600 and the appointment of the first native archbishop may have been the need for the schools established by the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_mission" title="Gregorian mission">Gregorian missionaries</a> to educate the natives to a sufficiently high standard for them to take ecclesiastical office.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit probably owed his appointment to the <a href="/wiki/See_of_Canterbury" class="mw-redirect" title="See of Canterbury">see of Canterbury</a> to a collaboration between <a href="/wiki/Eorcenberht_of_Kent" title="Eorcenberht of Kent">Eorcenberht of Kent</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cenwalh_of_Wessex" title="Cenwalh of Wessex">Cenwalh of Wessex</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Brooks67_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Brooks67-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The name Deusdedit means "God has given" in Latin,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> and had been the name of a recent pope,<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adeodatus_I" title="Pope Adeodatus I">Deusdedit</a>, in office from 615 to 618;<sup id="cite_ref-Ithamar890_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ithamar890-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> it was the practice of many of the early medieval Saxon bishops to take an adopted name, often from recent papal names.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> It is unclear when Deusdedit adopted his new name, although the historian Richard Sharpe considers it likely to have been when he was consecrated as an archbishop, rather than when he entered <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">religious life</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ithamar890_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ithamar890-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The see of Canterbury seems at this time to have been passing through a period of comparative obscurity.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> During Deusdedit's nine years as archbishop, all the new bishops in England were consecrated by <a href="/wiki/Celtic_Christianity" title="Celtic Christianity">Celtic</a> or foreign bishops, with one exception:<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley96_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley96-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit consecrated <a href="/wiki/Damian_(bishop_of_Rochester)" title="Damian (bishop of Rochester)">Damianus</a>, Ithamar's successor as Bishop of Rochester.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit did, however, found a <a href="/wiki/Nunnery" class="mw-redirect" title="Nunnery">nunnery</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_Thanet" title="Isle of Thanet">Isle of Thanet</a> and helped with the foundation of <a href="/wiki/Medeshamstede" title="Medeshamstede">Medeshamstede</a> Abbey, later <a href="/wiki/Peterborough_Cathedral" title="Peterborough Cathedral">Peterborough Abbey</a>, in 657.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley96_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley96-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was long overshadowed by <a href="/wiki/Agilbert" title="Agilbert">Agilbert</a>, bishop to the West Saxons,<sup id="cite_ref-ASE122_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE122-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and his authority as archbishop probably did not extend past his own diocese and that of <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Rochester" title="Diocese of Rochester">Rochester</a>, which had traditionally been dependent on Canterbury.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Synod of Whitby</a>, which debated whether the <a href="/wiki/Northumbria" title="Northumbria">Northumbrian</a> church should follow the Roman or the Celtic <a href="/wiki/Computus" class="mw-redirect" title="Computus">method of dating</a> <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a>, was held in 664.<sup id="cite_ref-Hindley79_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hindley79-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit does not appear to have been present, perhaps because of an outbreak of the plague prevalent in England at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE129_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE129-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Deusdedit_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Deusdedit died at some time around the Synod of Whitby, although the exact date is disputed.<sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, in the <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_ecclesiastica_gentis_Anglorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum">Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum</a></i>, states that "On the fourteenth of July in the above mentioned year, when an eclipse was quickly followed by plague and during which Bishop <a href="/wiki/Colm%C3%A1n_of_Lindisfarne" title="Colmán of Lindisfarne">Colman</a> was refuted by the unanimous decision of the Catholics and returned to his own country, Deusdedit the sixth Archbishop of Canterbury died."<sup id="cite_ref-Bede203_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede203-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> A solar eclipse occurred on 1 May 664, which would appear to make the date of Deusdedit's death 14&#160;July 664. But that conflicts with Bede's own information earlier in the <i>Historia</i>,<sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> where he claims that Deusdedit's predecessor, <a href="/wiki/Honorius_of_Canterbury" title="Honorius of Canterbury">Honorius</a>, "died on the 30th of September 653, and after a vacancy of 18 months, Deusdedit, a West Saxon was elected to the archiepiscopal see and became the 6th Archbishop. He was consecrated by Ithamar, Bishop of Rochester, on the 26th of May, and ruled the see until his death nine years, four months, and two days later."<sup id="cite_ref-Bede176_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bede176-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> If this information is accurate, then Deusdedit must have died on 28&#160;July 664.<sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Various methods of reconciling these discrepancies have been proposed. <a href="/wiki/Frank_Stenton" title="Frank Stenton">Frank Stenton</a> argues that Bede began his years on 1 September; thus the date of Honorius' death should be considered 30&#160;September 652 in modern reckoning. Further, Stenton argued that medieval copyists had introduced an error into the manuscripts of the <i>Historia</i>, and that Bede meant that the length of Deusdedit's reign was 9 years and 7 months, rather than 9 years and 4 months as stated in the manuscripts. From this, he concludes that Deusdedit's death occurred in the year September 663 to September 664. This would make the year of death correct according to the eclipse, but still leave a discrepancy on the specific day of death, for which Stenton asserted the length calculations given by Bede were more correct than the actual death date given. Thus Stenton concluded that Deusdedit died on 28 October 663.<sup id="cite_ref-ASE129_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ASE129-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Other historians, including Richard Abels, P. Grosjean, and Alan Thacker, state that Deusdedit died on 14 July 664.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Wood_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Wood-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The main argument was put forward by Grosjean, who claimed that Bede had the consecration date wrong, as 26 May was <a href="/wiki/Maundy_Thursday" title="Maundy Thursday">Maundy Thursday</a> in 655, not a date that would normally have been chosen for a consecration. Grosjean argues that the best method for resolving the conflicts is to just take 14&#160;July 664 as the date of death, and figure backwards with the length of reign given by Bede, which gives a consecration date of 12&#160;March 655.<sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Thacker and Abels agree generally, although Thacker does not give a specific consecration date beyond March.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Abels adds to Grosjean's arguments Bede's association of Deusdedit's death with that of King Eorcenberht, which Bede gives as occurring on the same day. Bede states that the plague of 664 began soon after the eclipse on 1&#160;May. Nothing in Bede contradicts the date of 14&#160;July 664 for Eorcenberht; therefore, Abels considers that date to be the best fit for the available data.<sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The historian D. P. Kirby agrees that Deusdedit died in 664, although he does not give a precise date within that year.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirby519_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirby519-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Most historians state that Deusdedit died of the plague that was prevalent in England at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Abels20_6-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels20-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Plague15_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Plague15-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Because Bede records the death of Deusdedit shortly after he mentions the outbreak of the plague, the historian <a href="/wiki/John_Maddicott" title="John Maddicott">J. R. Maddicott</a> asserts that both Deusdedit and Eorcenberht were struck suddenly with the disease and died quickly.<sup id="cite_ref-Plague15_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Plague15-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Bede is not specific on the type of plague, but Maddicott argues that because of the time of its eruption and the way it arrived in England it was probably <a href="/wiki/Bubonic_plague" title="Bubonic plague">bubonic plague</a>. Although Bede does not describe either Eorcenberht or Deusdedit's symptoms he does discuss another victim of the 664 disease, who suffered from a tumour on his thigh, resembling the characteristic groin swellings of bubonic plague.<sup id="cite_ref-Plague20_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Plague20-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Deusdedit_of_Canterbury&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Except for the bare facts of his life, little is known about Deusdedit.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-BASE_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit's successor as Archbishop of Canterbury, <a href="/wiki/Wighard" title="Wighard">Wighard</a>, had been one of his clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-Abels14_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Abels14-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit was regarded as a saint after his death, with a feast day of 14&#160;July,<sup id="cite_ref-DictSaint_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DictSaint-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> although the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bosworth_Psalter&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bosworth Psalter (page does not exist)">Bosworth Psalter</a>, a late 10th or early 11th-century <a href="/wiki/Psalter" title="Psalter">psalter</a> produced at <span class="nowrap"><a href="/wiki/St_Augustine%27s_Abbey" title="St Augustine&#39;s Abbey">St Augustine's Abbey</a></span>, gives a date of 15 July.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is designated as a major feast day, and is included along with those of a number of other early Canterbury archbishops in the Bosworth Psalter.<sup id="cite_ref-Bosworth175_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bosworth175-23">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Deusdedit was buried in the church of St Augustine's in Canterbury, but was <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relic)" title="Translation (relic)">translated</a> to the new abbey church in 1091. A <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, or saint's biography, on Deusdedit was written by Goscelin after the translation of his relics, but the work was based mainly on Bede's account;<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> the manuscript of the <i>De Sancto Deusdedit Archiepiscopo</i> survives as part of <a href="/wiki/British_Library" title="British Library">British Library</a> manuscript (ms) <a href="/wiki/Cotton_library" title="Cotton library">Cotton</a> Vespasian B.xx.<sup id="cite_ref-BL_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BL-24">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Because of the late date of the <i>Sancto</i>, Bede's <i>Historia</i> is the main source for what little is known about Deusdedit.<sup id="cite_ref-BASE_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BASE-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Other than the hagiography, there is scant evidence of a cult surrounding him.<sup id="cite_ref-DNB_1-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DNB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His shrine survived until the <a href="/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries" title="Dissolution of the monasteries">dissolution of the monasteries</a> in the 1530s.<sup id="cite_ref-ODS144_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ODS144-25">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
