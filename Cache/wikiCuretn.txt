					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">7th-century Roman Catholic Bishop</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Curetán</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Bishop</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">7th century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">8th century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> , <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Episcopal_Church" title="Scottish Episcopal Church">Scottish Episcopal Church</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Saint <b>Curetán</b> (<a href="/wiki/Medieval_Latin" title="Medieval Latin">Latin:</a> <i>Curitanus</i>, <i>Kiritinus</i>, or <i>Boniface</i>) was a <a href="/wiki/Gaels" title="Gaels">Scoto</a>-<a href="/wiki/Picts" title="Picts">Pictish</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, (fl. between 690 and 710). He is listed as one of the witnesses in the <i><a href="/wiki/C%C3%A1in_Adomn%C3%A1in" title="Cáin Adomnáin">Cáin Adomnáin</a></i>, where he is called "Curetan epscop". In the <i><a href="/wiki/Martyrology_of_Tallaght" title="Martyrology of Tallaght">Martyrology of Tallaght</a></i> he is called "of Ross Mand Bairend", and in the <i>Martyrology of O'Gorman</i> he is styled "bishop and abbot of Ross maic Bairend".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His bishopric is usually held to have been <a href="/wiki/Ross,_Scotland" title="Ross, Scotland">Ross</a>, the seat of which was at the settlement in the <a href="/wiki/Black_Isle" title="Black Isle">Black Isle</a> called <i>Ros-Maircnidh</i> or <a href="/wiki/Rosemarkie" title="Rosemarkie">Rosemarkie</a>, named after the adjacent promontory
</p><p>A hagiography of Curetán is found in the sixteenth century <a href="/wiki/Manuscript" title="Manuscript">manuscript</a> known as the <i><a href="/wiki/Aberdeen_Breviary" title="Aberdeen Breviary">Aberdeen Breviary</a></i>, where his <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i> occurs under the name "Boniface".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilliams_et_al.199194_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams_et_al.199194-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In this hagiography, his Latin name is accompanied by a story of his <a href="/wiki/Jew" class="mw-redirect" title="Jew">Hebrew</a> origins, a descendant of the sister of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Andrew" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Andrew">Saint Andrew</a>, who was first ordained as a priest by the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem">Patriarch of Jerusalem</a>, before travelling to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and becoming <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a>, later resigning and moving to <a href="/wiki/Pictland" class="mw-redirect" title="Pictland">Pictland</a>. The story is similar to that in the <i>Life of St. Serf</i>, and it has been conjectured that both were the product of the Romanizing faction in the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_Whitby" title="Synod of Whitby">Easter Controversy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilliams_et_al.199195_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams_et_al.199195-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Breviary also connects Curetán with King <a href="/wiki/Nechtan_mac_Der-Ilei" title="Nechtan mac Der-Ilei">Nechtan mac Der-Ilei</a>, whose brother Bridei was also a guarantor of the <i>Cáin Adomnáin</i> in 697. Nechtan consulted Abbot <a href="/wiki/Ceolfrith" title="Ceolfrith">Ceolfrith</a> of <a href="/wiki/Monkwearmouth%E2%80%93Jarrow_Abbey" title="Monkwearmouth–Jarrow Abbey">Monkwearmouth–Jarrow Abbey</a> regarding the dating of Easter and finding the abbot persuasive adopted the Roman practice.  <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a> stated that Nechtan placed the churches of the Picts under the protection of St. Peter. Curetán-Boniface is also associated with the churches of <a href="/wiki/Restenneth_Priory" title="Restenneth Priory">Restenneth</a> and <a href="/wiki/Invergowrie" title="Invergowrie">Invergowrie</a>, churches which, like Rosemarkie, both have dedications to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEWilliams_et_al.199194_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams_et_al.199194-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are <a href="/wiki/Toponymy" title="Toponymy">place-name</a> commemorations to Saint Curetán along <a href="/wiki/Glen_Urquhart" title="Glen Urquhart">Glen Urquhart</a>, Strathglass, Glen Glass, <a href="/wiki/Loch_Ness" title="Loch Ness">Loch Ness</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Cromarty_Firth" title="Cromarty Firth">Cromarty Firth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There are also dedications to St Peter and Boniface in Orkney. <a href="/wiki/Barbara_Yorke" title="Barbara Yorke">Barbara Yorke</a> suggests that Curetán was an influential figure in Pictland, and played a significant role, after the adoption of the "Roman Easter" and tonsure, to help bring the Pictish church into closer contact with other areas of the western church.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Curet%C3%A1n&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His liturgical celebration is 14 March.
</p><p>There is a <a href="/wiki/Clootie_well" title="Clootie well">clootie well</a> near the village of <a href="/wiki/Munlochy" title="Munlochy">Munlochy</a> on <a href="/wiki/Black_Isle" title="Black Isle">Black Isle</a> is dedicated to Saint Curetán, whose intercession is believed effective in curing sick children.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Scottish Episcopal parish in <a href="/wiki/Ardgay" title="Ardgay">Ardgay</a> is named in honor of St.Curetán.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
