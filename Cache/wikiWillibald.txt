<p><b>Willibald</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Willibaldus</i>; c. 700 – c.787) was an 8th-century <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Eichst%C3%A4tt" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Eichstätt">bishop of Eichstätt</a> in <a href="/wiki/Bavaria" title="Bavaria">Bavaria</a>.
</p><p>Information about his life is largely drawn from the Hodoeporicon (itinerary) of Willibald, a text written in the 8th century by <a href="/wiki/Huneberc" class="mw-redirect" title="Huneberc">Huneberc</a>, an <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxon" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglo-Saxon">Anglo-Saxon</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> from <a href="/wiki/Heidenheim_am_Hahnenkamm" class="mw-redirect" title="Heidenheim am Hahnenkamm">Heidenheim am Hahnenkamm</a> who knew Willibald and his brother personally.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The text of the Hodoeporicon was dictated to Huneberc by Willibald shortly before he died.
</p><p>Willibald's father was <a href="/wiki/Richard_the_Pilgrim" title="Richard the Pilgrim">Richard the Pilgrim</a>, and his mother <a href="/wiki/Wuna_of_Wessex" title="Wuna of Wessex">Wuna of Wessex</a>. His brother was <a href="/wiki/Winibald" title="Winibald">Winibald</a> and his sister was <a href="/wiki/Saint_Walburga" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Walburga">Walburga</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Willibald was well-travelled and the first known <a href="/wiki/English_people" title="English people">Englishman</a> to visit the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His shrine is at the <a href="/wiki/Eichst%C3%A4tt_Cathedral" title="Eichstätt Cathedral">Eichstätt Cathedral</a> in Germany, where his body and <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> from his journeys are preserved.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 7 July.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Willibald was born in <a href="/wiki/Wessex" title="Wessex">Wessex</a> on 21 October around the year 700. His mother, <a href="/wiki/Wuna_of_Wessex" title="Wuna of Wessex">Wuna of Wessex</a>, was reportedly a sister of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Boniface" title="Saint Boniface">Boniface</a>. His father, <a href="/wiki/Richard_the_Pilgrim" title="Richard the Pilgrim">Richard the Pilgrim</a>, was a chieftain of Wessex. At the age of three, Willibald suffered from a violent illness. His parents prayed to God, vowing to commit Willibald to a <a href="/wiki/Monastic_life" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic life">monastic life</a> if he was to be spared. Willibald survived and at the age of five entered the Benedictine monastery at <a href="/wiki/Bishop%27s_Waltham" title="Bishop&#39;s Waltham">Waldheim</a> and was educated by Abbot Egwald.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At the monastery he became accustomed to the Irish and Anglo-Saxon monastic ideal of <i>peregrinatio religiosa</i>, or pious rootlessness.<sup id="cite_ref-BrillGER_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BrillGER-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Travels">Travels</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Travels">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 721 Willibald set out on a pilgrimage to Rome with his father and brother. After departing by ship the group arrived in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a>, France visiting <a href="/wiki/Shrines" class="mw-redirect" title="Shrines">shrines</a> and spending much of their time in prayer. Eventually they arrived in <a href="/wiki/Lucca" title="Lucca">Lucca</a>, a city in northern <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. It was here that Willibald's father became gravely ill and died. After burying their father Willibald and Winibald continued on their journey, travelling through Italy until they reached Rome. Here they visited the <a href="/wiki/Archbasilica_of_St._John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbasilica of St. John Lateran">Lateran Basilica</a> and <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's</a>. They spent some time in Italy, strengthening in devotion and discipline, but soon the two brothers became ill with the <a href="/wiki/Black_Plague" class="mw-redirect" title="Black Plague">Black Plague</a> (although Mershman says it was malaria).<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Hunebrec recounts the disease and miraculous recovery:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Then with the passing of the days and the increasing heat of the summer, which is usually a sign of future fever, they were struck down with sickness. They found it difficult to breathe, fever set in, and at one moment they were shivering with cold the next burning with heat. They had caught the black plague. So great a hold had it got on them that, scarcely able to move, worn out with fever and almost at the point of death, the breath of life had practically left their bodies. But God in His never failing providence and fatherly love deigned to listen to their prayers and come to their aid, so that each of them rested in turn for one week whilst they attended to each other's needs.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Willibald left Rome in 724, heading for <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>. From there, accompanied by two unnamed companions and brother, he departed by sea, visited <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> and <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> along the way, and eventually arrived in <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>. Winnebald had, after the departure of his brother for Palestine, lived in a monastery at Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Greece.2C_Asia_Minor.2C_Cyprus_and_Syria"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Greece,_Asia_Minor,_Cyprus_and_Syria">Greece, Asia Minor, Cyprus and Syria</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Greece, Asia Minor, Cyprus and Syria">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In Asia Minor Willibald and his companions first arrived in the city of <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a>. Here they visited the tomb of <a href="/wiki/John_the_Evangelist" title="John the Evangelist">John the Evangelist</a>. They then continued on to <a href="/wiki/Patara_(Lycia)" title="Patara (Lycia)">Patara</a> in <a href="/wiki/Lycia" title="Lycia">Lycia</a>, where they waited out the winter, and then travelled to <a href="/wiki/Cape_Gelidonya" title="Cape Gelidonya">Mount Chelidonium</a>, almost dying of hunger and thirst as they attempted to cross. They departed by boat and arrived on the island of <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>. Following a stay in <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a> they reached Antadoros (now called <a href="/wiki/Tartus" title="Tartus">Tartus</a>) where they had an audience with a Greek bishop and visited the church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_John_the_Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint John the Baptist">Saint John the Baptist</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Holy_Land">Holy Land</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Holy Land">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Willibald's journey then took him and a group of seven companions to Palestine (c. 723/24 - 726/27). There he visited Nazareth. From Nazareth he went to Bethlehem, and thence into Egypt. He returned to Nazareth, and thence travelled to Cana, Capharnaum, and Jerusalem,<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> where he arrived on 11 November 725.  The pilgrimage continued to <a href="/wiki/Bethlehem" title="Bethlehem">Bethlehem</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity#Justinian&#39;s_basilica_(6th_century)" title="Church of the Nativity">Church of the Nativity</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tuqu%27#Archaeology_and_landmarks" class="mw-redirect" title="Tuqu&#39;">Thecua</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Mar_Saba" title="Mar Saba">Great Lavra</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sabbas_the_Sanctified" title="Sabbas the Sanctified">St Sabbas</a>, the church at the spot where <a href="/wiki/Philip_the_Evangelist" title="Philip the Evangelist">Philip the Evangelist</a> baptised the <a href="/wiki/Ethiopian_eunuch" title="Ethiopian eunuch">Ethiopian eunuch</a>, the port city of <a href="/wiki/Gaza_City" title="Gaza City">Gaza</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hebron" title="Hebron">Hebron</a>, <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a>'s hometown of <a href="/wiki/Lydda" class="mw-redirect" title="Lydda">Lydda</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jaffa" title="Jaffa">Joppa</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tyre,_Lebanon" title="Tyre, Lebanon">Tyre</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sidon" title="Sidon">Sidon</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sebastia,_Nablus" title="Sebastia, Nablus">Sebaste</a> and other places in <a href="/wiki/Samaria" title="Samaria">Samaria</a>, <a href="/wiki/Acre,_Israel" title="Acre, Israel">Ptolemais</a>, and again reached Tyre by way of the Ladder of Tyre.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_voyage">Return voyage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Return voyage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>After waiting for some time in Tyre, Willibald was able to sail to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>. He remained in Constantinople for some two years (c. 726/27–729) and was provided with a small room in an annex of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Apostles" title="Church of the Holy Apostles">Church of the Holy Apostles</a>. He spent part of this time in <a href="/wiki/Nicaea" title="Nicaea">Nicaea</a>, visiting a church and studying documents from the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">First Council of Nicaea</a> that was arranged by <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Constantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Constantine">Emperor Constantine</a>. He left Constantinople and sailed for <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> arriving in <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a> approximately seven years after he had left Italy.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Monte_Cassino">Monte Cassino</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Monte Cassino">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Willibald and his remaining companion, Tidbercht, joined the Benedictine community at <a href="/wiki/Monte_Cassino" title="Monte Cassino">Monte Cassino</a>. Here Willibald taught the community about his travels. He would spend over ten years (c. 729–739) at Monte Cassino and another local <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine monastery">Benedictine monastery</a> where he served roles as, "<a href="/wiki/Sacrist" class="mw-redirect" title="Sacrist">sacrist</a>, <a href="/wiki/Dean_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Dean (religion)">dean</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Ostiarius" title="Ostiarius">porter</a>."<sup id="cite_ref-Bunson_858_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bunson_858-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> According to David Farmer, his new-found monasticism was drastically shaped by his experiences in both England and Palestine, allowing him to play a major role in the reformation and future prosperity of the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It happened that in 738 Boniface, coming to Rome, asked of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_III" title="Pope Gregory III">Pope Gregory III</a> that Willibald might be sent to assist him in his missions in Germany. The pope desired to see the monk, and was much delighted with the history of his travels, and acquainted him of Boniface's request.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Eichst.C3.A4tt.2C_ordination.2C_and_missionary_work"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Eichstätt,_ordination,_and_missionary_work">Eichstätt, ordination, and missionary work</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Willibald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Eichstätt, ordination, and missionary work">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG/220px-Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG/330px-Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG/440px-Willibaldsburg_%C3%BCber_Eichst%C3%A4tt.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2400" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>The Willibaldsburg above Eichstätt</figcaption></figure>
<p>Upon arriving at Eichstätt, he was ordained a priest by Boniface on 22 July 741 and asked to begin missionary work in the area. A year later, Boniface summoned him to <a href="/wiki/Thuringia" title="Thuringia">Thuringia</a>. While travelling, Willibald encountered his brother, Winibald, whom he had not seen for over eight years.
</p><p>Shortly thereafter he returned to Eichstätt to begin his work. In 742 he and Winibald founded the <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Heidenheim,_Bavaria" title="Heidenheim, Bavaria">Heidenheim</a>. Winibald served as the first abbot. Following his death, Willibald's sister, Walburga, was appointed the first abbess of the monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In 746 Boniface consecrated Willibald bishop of Eichstätt.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to Bunson, Eichstätt was the site of Willibald's most successful missionary efforts, although specific details like the means of conversion and number of converts are not known.<sup id="cite_ref-Bunson_858_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bunson_858-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The monastery was one of the first buildings in the region and served as an important centre, "not only for the diocesan apostolate, but also for the diffusion and development of monasticism."<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Wilibald served as the bishop of the region in <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconia</a> for over four decades, living in the monastery and entertaining visitors throughout Europe who would come to hear of his journey and monasticism.
</p>
