<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Not to be confused with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Amun" title="Saint Amun">Saint Amun</a>.</div>

<p><b>Ammonas of Egypt</b> (also <i>Amtnonas</i>, <i>Ammon</i>, <i>Ammonius</i>, <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Αμμωνάς</span>) was an <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">eastern</a> <a href="/wiki/Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a>, <a href="/wiki/Christian_monasticism" title="Christian monasticism">monastic</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Desert_Fathers" title="Desert Fathers">Desert Father</a> who was born around the <a href="/wiki/4th_century" title="4th century">early 4th century</a>. He is a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. Ammonas was a disciple of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pambo" title="Pambo">Pambo</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Many of his known sayings and quotations exist in eleven sections of the <i><a href="/wiki/Sayings_of_the_Desert_Fathers" title="Sayings of the Desert Fathers">Sayings of the Desert Fathers</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ammonas is commemorated as "Ammon" on <a href="/wiki/January_10_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="January 10 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">January 10</a> in <i>The Prologue of Ohrid</i>, a <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">synaxarium</a> written by <a href="/wiki/Nikolaj_Velimirovi%C4%87" title="Nikolaj Velimirović">Saint Nikolaj Velimirović</a>. It mentions his 14-year struggle in <a href="/wiki/Wadi_El_Natrun" title="Wadi El Natrun">Scetis</a> against anger.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ammonas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Monastic_life">Monastic life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ammonas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Monastic life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ammonas moved into the Egyptian desert with his three brothers and two sisters so that they could draw nearer to God.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At some point during his life, probably during this time, he spent 14 years in <a href="/wiki/Wadi_El_Natrun" title="Wadi El Natrun">Scetis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was a trusted disciple of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a> and became his successor at the monastery on the <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://coptic-wiki.org/tag/pispir">Outer Mountain</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dayr_al-Maym%C5%ABn" title="Dayr al-Maymūn">Pispir</a> (<a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egypt</a>, east of the <a href="/wiki/Nile" title="Nile">Nile river</a>) after Anthony relocated in AD 305 (see <a class="external text" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Dayr_al-Maymūn&amp;oldid=965738108">this</a> page).
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="As_a_bishop">As a bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ammonas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: As a bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Timothy_II_of_Alexandria" title="Timothy II of Alexandria">Timothy</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria" title="Patriarch of Alexandria">Bishop of Alexandria</a> at the time, heard of Ammonas and desired to ordain him. Initially, he did not want to become a bishop even purportedly mutilated his body so that he could escape ordination.<sup id="cite_ref-:3_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:3-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nevertheless, Ammonas was eventually ordained a Christian bishop, and at least two third or fourth-century epistles are attributed to him, translated in <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a> (regarded as the most reputable<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>), <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>. They exist among the letters and exhortations of <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> There were other epistles written by a certain Ammonas, but which scholars are unable to attribute with certainty to Ammonas of Egypt, as Dr. Johannes Tromp of <a href="/wiki/Leiden_University" title="Leiden University">Leiden University</a> notes.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The epistles of Ammonas of Egypt that are historically verified to be written by him provide important insight into <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">eastern</a> Christian <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monasticism</a> in the early centuries of Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ammonas is said to have died at the beginning of the fifth century and, according to <a href="/wiki/Palladius_of_Galatia" title="Palladius of Galatia">Palladius of Galatia</a>, was buried in a chapel called Rufinianæ.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Quotations">Quotations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ammonas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Quotations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Selected from the <i>Sayings of the Desert Fathers</i> and from the Letters of Ammonas<i>.</i><sup id="cite_ref-:0_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:1_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<ul><li>"The 'narrow and hard way' (Mt 7:14) is this: to control your thoughts, and to strip yourself of your own will, for the sake of God. This is also the meaning of the sentence, 'Lo, we have left everything and followed you.' (Mt 19:27)"</li>
<li>"Sit in your cell and eat a little every day, keeping the world of the publican (the <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Prayer" title="Jesus Prayer">Jesus Prayer</a>) always in your heart, and you may be saved."</li>
<li>"If we follow our own will, God no longer sends His power which prospers and establishes all the ways of men."</li>
<li>"Unless a man denies himself and his own will, and obeys his spiritual parents (<a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">elders</a>), he will not be able to recognize God's will..."</li></ul>
