<p>Saint <b>Mellonius</b> (229-314) was an early 4th-century <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">Bishop of Rotomagus</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a>) in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_province" title="Roman province">Roman province</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gallia_Lugdunensis" title="Gallia Lugdunensis">Secunda Provincia Lugdunensis</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>). He is known only from a 17th-century 'Life' of little historical value, meaning the historicity of his existence is uncertain.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mellonius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mellonius (surnamed Probus)<sup id="cite_ref-Cormack_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cormack-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> is said to have been born near <a href="/wiki/Cardiff" title="Cardiff">Cardiff</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wales" title="Wales">Wales</a>, presumably at <a href="/wiki/St_Mellons" title="St Mellons">St Mellons</a>, although the saint there is generally thought to be Saint <a href="/wiki/Melaine" title="Melaine">Melaine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rennes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rennes">Bishop of Rennes</a>. The two have, unfortunately, been hopelessly confused in many biographies. Mellonius' story tells how he travelled to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to pay the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Britain" title="Roman Britain">British</a> tribute. He was there converted to <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_I" title="Pope Stephen I">Pope Stephen I</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who ordained him priest and later consecrated him a bishop.
</p><p>Shortly after the martyrdom of Pope Stephen in 257, Mellonius set out for Gaul. He succeeded <a href="/wiki/Nicasius_of_Rouen" title="Nicasius of Rouen">Nicasius of Rouen</a> as Bishop of Rouen in 261. After a long episcopate, in 311, he retired to a hermit's cell at a place called Hericourt, where he died A.D. 314.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Mellonius was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Avitianus" title="Avitianus">Avitianus</a>.
</p><p>He is sometimes confused with Mellonius, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Troyes" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Troyes">Bishop of Troyes</a>, from 390 to 400.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mellonius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His feast day is 22 October. In the English translation of the 1956 edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, 'St Mellon' is listed under 22 October with the citation: At Rouen, St Mellon, Bishop, who was ordained by Pope St Stephen and sent thither to preach the Gospel.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 2004 edition of the Roman Martyrology, Mellonius is listed under the same date, 22 October, with the Latin name <i>Mallóni</i>. He is mentioned as follows: 'At Rothómagi (Rouen), bishop, who in that city announced the Christian faith and handed on the episcopate'.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bishop Mellonius is depicted in a fifteenth-century illuminated Book of Hours.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mellonius was buried at Rouen, but his remains were subsequently translated to a church in <a href="/wiki/Pontoise" title="Pontoise">Pontoise</a> towards the end of the eighth century to protect them from Norse warbands. They remained there until they were lost during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mellonius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Mellons_Parish_Church_(1)_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/St_Mellons_Parish_Church_%281%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg/220px-St_Mellons_Parish_Church_%281%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/St_Mellons_Parish_Church_%281%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg/330px-St_Mellons_Parish_Church_%281%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3f/St_Mellons_Parish_Church_%281%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg/440px-St_Mellons_Parish_Church_%281%29_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1493805.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/St_Mellons_Church" title="St Mellons Church">St. Mellon's Parish Church</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>St. Mellon's Church in St. Mellons, Cardiff is dedicated to Mellonius of Rouen. A fair used to be held on his feast day in the village of St Mellons.<sup id="cite_ref-kellen_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kellen-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The church was previously dedicated to St. Lucius, but was changed upon the arrival of the Normans.<sup id="cite_ref-Cormack_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cormack-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The church at <a href="/wiki/Thi%C3%A9deville" class="mw-redirect" title="Thiédeville">Thiédeville</a> is dedicated to him at <a href="/wiki/H%C3%A9ricourt-en-Caux" title="Héricourt-en-Caux">Héricourt</a>, where there was a <a href="/wiki/Holy_well" title="Holy well">holy well</a>. There is also a church dedicated to St. Mellon in Plomelin, Brittany.
</p>
