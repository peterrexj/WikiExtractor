<p><b>Saints Abdon and Sennen</b>, variously written in early calendars and <a href="/wiki/Martyrologies" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrologies">martyrologies</a> <b>Abdo, Abdus</b>, and <b>Sennes, Sennis, Zennen</b>, are recognized by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> as <a href="/wiki/Christian_martyrs" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian martyrs">Christian Martyrs</a>, with a feast day on 30 July.<sup id="cite_ref-Martyrology_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martyrology-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In some places they have been honoured on 20 March, and the first Sunday of May.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nothing is known historically about Abdon and Sennen, and whether they can be verified. The Roman Martyrology indicates that they were martyred for their faith, and suggests they were buried on 30 July in the Cemetery of Pontianus on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Portuensis" title="Via Portuensis">Via Portuensis</a>, outside <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Martyrology_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martyrology-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Their names were subsequently removed in the twentieth century from the list in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, which is commemorated liturgically worldwide,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> but they may still be celebrated everywhere on their feast day unless in some locality an obligatory celebration is assigned to that day.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The rank of their celebration was given as "Simple" in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> and remained such until the classification was changed to that of "Commemoration" in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">General Roman Calendar of 1960</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abdon_and_Sennen&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Their <i>Acts</i>, written for the most part prior to the 9th century, describe them as <a href="/wiki/Persian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Persian people">Persian</a> nobles, captured and taken to Rome during a military campaign in the third century. There they became slaves, converted to Christianity and helped bury the Christian dead. They came to the attention of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> who had them taken in chains before the Roman Senate, where they refused to sacrifice to the Roman Gods, and so were dismembered by gladiators in the <a href="/wiki/Colosseum" title="Colosseum">Colosseum</a><sup id="cite_ref-stpat_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stpat-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ce_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ce-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  approximately in the year 250. The <i>Acts</i> certainly contain several fictitious statements about the cause, the circumstances of their coming to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and the nature of their torments. They relate that their bodies were buried by a subdeacon, "Quirinus", and later transferred in the reign of <a href="/wiki/Constantine_the_Great" title="Constantine the Great">Constantine the Great</a> (reigned 306–337) to the Cemetery of Pontianus on the road to Porto, near the gates of Rome.
</p><p>A <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a> found on a sixth century <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> supposed to contain their remains represents them receiving crowns from Christ. Several cities, notably <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a> and <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>, claim possession of their bodies, but the <a href="/wiki/Acta_Sanctorum" title="Acta Sanctorum">Acta Sanctorum</a> insist that they rest in the Basilica of <a href="/wiki/San_Marco_Evangelista_al_Campidoglio,_Rome" title="San Marco Evangelista al Campidoglio, Rome">San Marco Evangelista al Campidoglio, Rome</a>, having been brought there in 1474. They may have even had their own church in Rome, which has now been lost. The Benedictine <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Arles-sur-Tech" title="Abbey of Arles-sur-Tech">Abbey of Sainte-Marie</a> in <a href="/wiki/Arles-sur-Tech" title="Arles-sur-Tech">Arles-sur-Tech</a>, France also claims their <a href="/wiki/Tomb" title="Tomb">tomb</a>.
</p><p>Abdon and Sennen are patron saints of <a href="/wiki/Calasparra" title="Calasparra">Calasparra</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Region_of_Murcia" title="Region of Murcia">Murcia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>.  The feasts days celebrated in their honor in this Spanish town date back to the 16th century.<sup id="cite_ref-murcia_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-murcia-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
