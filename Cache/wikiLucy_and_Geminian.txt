<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saints Lucy and Geminian</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyrs</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 300<br />Rome</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>; <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">16 September (pre-1970 <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, a duplication of the 13 December feast)<br />17 September (<a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Rite" title="Byzantine Rite">Byzantine Rite</a>)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saints Lucy and Geminian</b> were venerated on 16 September as <a href="/wiki/Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Saints">saints</a> who died as <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> during the persecution of <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> in about 290 or 300 or, more precisely, in 304. Veneration for them was ancient but their story is "only known from fabulous acts."<sup id="cite_ref-Agnes_Baillie_Cunninghame_Dunbar_1904_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agnes_Baillie_Cunninghame_Dunbar_1904-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Lucy appears to be in reality the same as the <a href="/wiki/Lucy_of_Syracuse" class="mw-redirect" title="Lucy of Syracuse">Lucy of Syracuse</a> whose feast is on 13 December, but the Geminian who was venerated on 16 September seems to be a fictitious character, not to be confused with Saint <a href="/wiki/Geminianus" title="Geminianus">Geminianus</a>, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Modena-Nonantola" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Modena-Nonantola">Bishop of Modena</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_and_Geminian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the legend, Lucy was a 75-year-old <a href="/wiki/Widow" title="Widow">widow</a> and Geminian a young <a href="/wiki/Catechist" class="mw-redirect" title="Catechist">catechist</a>.  Lucy was accused by her son, Eutropius (or Euprepius), of being a Christian.  She was brought before Diocletian, who at first attempted to dissuade her and then placed her in a <a href="/wiki/Cauldron" title="Cauldron">cauldron</a> of burning <a href="/wiki/Pitch_(resin)" title="Pitch (resin)">pitch</a>; Lucy lived for three days in the cauldron.  When Diocletian heard that she was still alive, he ordered her to be carried around the city, with weights loaded on her body.<sup id="cite_ref-Agnes_Baillie_Cunninghame_Dunbar_1904_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agnes_Baillie_Cunninghame_Dunbar_1904-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Lucy was paraded by Geminian's house, the statues of <a href="/wiki/Roman_gods" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman gods">Roman gods</a> in his home shattered miraculously and a <a href="/wiki/Dove" class="mw-redirect" title="Dove">dove</a> made the sign of the cross over Geminian's head.  He followed Lucy, asking for instruction in the Christian religion as well as <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a>.  A priest named Protasius helped him with his request.  75 people were converted to Christianity by the example of Geminian.  The judge presiding at their trial was thrown from his horse on a stone bridge; his body was never found.  Lucy and Geminian were ultimately <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Agnes_Baillie_Cunninghame_Dunbar_1904_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Agnes_Baillie_Cunninghame_Dunbar_1904-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  They were buried by a woman named Maxima.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="General_Roman_Calendar">General Roman Calendar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Lucy_and_Geminian&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: General Roman Calendar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A commemoration of "Saints Lucy and Geminianus" was included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a> and remained in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> until 1969, but was then omitted as a duplication of the 13 December feast of Saint Lucy, while the Geminian mentioned in the legend of Saint Lucy seems to be a merely fictitious personage.<sup id="cite_ref-CR_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholicism" title="Traditionalist Catholicism">traditional Catholics</a> continue to observe the pre-1970 calendar.
</p>
