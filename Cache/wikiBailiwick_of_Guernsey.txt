<p>The <b><a href="/wiki/Bailiwick" title="Bailiwick">Bailiwick</a> of Guernsey</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Bailliage de Guernesey</i>; <a href="/wiki/Guern%C3%A9siais" title="Guernésiais">Guernésiais</a>: <i>Bailliage dé Guernési</i>) is a self-governing British <a href="/wiki/Crown_Dependencies" title="Crown Dependencies">Crown Dependency</a> off the coast of <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, France, comprising several of the <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a>. It has a total land area of 78 square kilometres (30&#160;sq&#160;mi) and an estimated total population of 67,334.
</p><p>The Channel Islands were part of the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy" title="Duchy of Normandy">Duchy of Normandy</a>, whose dukes became kings of England from 1066. In 1204, as a consequence of the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Le_Goulet" title="Treaty of Le Goulet">Treaty of Le Goulet</a>, insular Normandy alone remained loyal to the English Crown, leading to a political split from the mainland. Around 1290, the Channel Islands' Governor, <a href="/wiki/Otto_de_Grandson" title="Otto de Grandson">Otto de Grandson</a>, split the archipelago into two bailiwicks, establishing those parts other than <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a> as a single Bailiwick of Guernsey.<sup id="cite_ref-TCI_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TCI-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 21">&#58;&#8202;21&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>The Bailiwick is a <a href="/wiki/Parliamentary_system" title="Parliamentary system">parliamentary</a> <a href="/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy" title="Constitutional monarchy">constitutional monarchy</a>, comprising three separate jurisdictions: <a href="/wiki/Guernsey" title="Guernsey">Guernsey</a> (incorporating <a href="/wiki/Herm" title="Herm">Herm</a>), <a href="/wiki/Alderney" title="Alderney">Alderney</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sark" title="Sark">Sark</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Guernsey" title="Lieutenant Governor of Guernsey">Lieutenant Governor</a> is the representative of the <a href="/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Monarchy of the United Kingdom">British monarch</a>, who remains the <a href="/wiki/Head_of_state" title="Head of state">head of state</a>. The <a href="/wiki/States_of_Guernsey" title="States of Guernsey">States of Guernsey</a> is the parliament and government of the whole Bailiwick, though several matters are decided locally by the <a href="/wiki/States_of_Alderney" title="States of Alderney">States of Alderney</a> and Sark's Chief Pleas. The <a href="/wiki/Bailiff_of_Guernsey" title="Bailiff of Guernsey">Bailiff of Guernsey</a> is the civil head of the Bailiwick. They are also president of the <a href="/wiki/States_of_Guernsey" title="States of Guernsey">States of Guernsey</a> and head of the Bailiwick's <a href="/wiki/Judiciary" title="Judiciary">judiciary</a>. 
</p><p>The Bailiwick is self-governing and not part of the <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:15_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:15-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Its defence and international representation – as well as certain policy areas, such as <a href="/wiki/British_nationality_law" title="British nationality law">nationality law</a> – are the responsibility of the <a href="/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Government of the United Kingdom">UK Government</a>, but the Bailiwick still has a separate international identity.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/History_of_Guernsey" title="History of Guernsey">History of Guernsey</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alderney#History" title="Alderney">Alderney §&#160;History</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Sark#History" title="Sark">Sark §&#160;History</a></div>
<p>The history of the Bailiwick of Guernsey goes back to 933, when the islands came under the control of <a href="/wiki/William_Longsword" title="William Longsword">William Longsword</a>, having been annexed from the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Brittany" title="Duchy of Brittany">Duchy of Brittany</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy" title="Duchy of Normandy">Duchy of Normandy</a>. The island of Guernsey and the other <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a> formed part of the lands of <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror</a>. In 1204 France conquered mainland Normandy – but not the offshore islands of the <a href="/wiki/Bailiwick" title="Bailiwick">bailiwick</a>. The islands represent the last remnants of the medieval Duchy of Normandy.<sup id="cite_ref-HoG_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HoG-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Initially there was one governor, or co-governors working together, of the islands making up the <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a>. The title "governor" has changed over the centuries. "Warden", "keeper" and "captain" have previously been used.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The bailiff stands in for the Governor, or more recently the Lieutenant Governor, if the latter is absent, for a short term or for longer, for instance during the five years of the  <a href="/wiki/German_occupation_of_the_Channel_Islands" title="German occupation of the Channel Islands">German occupation of the Channel Islands</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Guernsey" title="Lieutenant Governor of Guernsey">Lieutenant Governor of Guernsey</a> is the Lieutenant Governor of the Bailiwick of Guernsey and, being the personal representative of the British monarch,<sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> has usually had a distinguished military service.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Originally the local courts in Guernsey were "<i><a href="/wiki/Fief" title="Fief">fiefs</a></i>" with the lord of the manor presiding. Before 1066, a superior court was introduced above the <i>fiefs</i> and below the <i>Eschequier</i> Court in <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a> and comprised the bailiff and four knights; the court heard appeals and tried criminal cases.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Otton_de_Grandson" class="mw-redirect" title="Otton de Grandson">Otton de Grandson</a>, then the governor of the Islands, delegated the civil powers to two separate bailiffs for Guernsey and <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a> before he went on crusade to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> in 1290.<sup id="cite_ref-TCI_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-TCI-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 21">&#58;&#8202;21&#8202;</span></sup> This can be assessed as the date of first creation of the two bailiwicks.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Geography">Geography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Geography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Guernsey#Geography" title="Guernsey">Guernsey §&#160;Geography</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Guernsey_location_map.svg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Guernsey_location_map.svg/220px-Guernsey_location_map.svg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="237" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Guernsey_location_map.svg/330px-Guernsey_location_map.svg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/Guernsey_location_map.svg/440px-Guernsey_location_map.svg.png 2x" data-file-width="1325" data-file-height="1426" /></a><figcaption>Islands and islets belonging to the Bailiwick of Guernsey, shown within the Channel Islands</figcaption></figure>
<p>Situated around <span class="geo-inline"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1156832818">.mw-parser-output .geo-default,.mw-parser-output .geo-dms,.mw-parser-output .geo-dec{display:inline}.mw-parser-output .geo-nondefault,.mw-parser-output .geo-multi-punct,.mw-parser-output .geo-inline-hidden{display:none}.mw-parser-output .longitude,.mw-parser-output .latitude{white-space:nowrap}</style><span class="plainlinks nourlexpansion"><a class="external text" href="https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;params=49.4_N_2.6_W_"><span class="geo-nondefault"><span class="geo-dms" title="Maps, aerial photos, and other data for this location"><span class="latitude">49°24′N</span> <span class="longitude">2°36′W</span></span></span><span class="geo-multi-punct">&#xfeff; / &#xfeff;</span><span class="geo-default"><span class="geo-dec" title="Maps, aerial photos, and other data for this location">49.4°N 2.6°W</span><span style="display:none">&#xfeff; / <span class="geo">49.4; -2.6</span></span></span></a></span></span>, Alderney, Guernsey, Herm, Sark, and some other smaller islands together have a total area of 78&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (30&#160;sq&#160;mi) and coastlines of about 50&#160;km (31&#160;mi). Elevation varies from sea level to 114&#160;m (374&#160;ft) at <a href="/wiki/Le_Moulin" title="Le Moulin">Le Moulin</a> on Sark.
</p><p>There are many smaller <a href="/wiki/List_of_islands_of_the_Bailiwick_of_Guernsey" title="List of islands of the Bailiwick of Guernsey">islands, islets, rocks and reefs</a> in the Bailiwick. Combined with a tidal range of 10 m and fast currents of up to 12 knots, this makes sailing in local waters dangerous.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Constitutional_status">Constitutional status</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Constitutional status">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Bailiwick of Guernsey is a separate jurisdiction in itself, and is in turn also three separate sub-jurisdictions. It does not form part of, and is separate from (but is not independent of, or from) the <a href="/wiki/United_Kingdom" title="United Kingdom">United Kingdom</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-GG_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The two Bailiwicks of Jersey and Guernsey together make up the <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a>.
</p><p>The Islanders have never had formal representation in the House of Commons of the British Parliament,<sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> nor in the European Parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-GG_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GG-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Those Islanders who were not somehow qualified and eligible <i>in their own right</i> to register to vote and to vote in the United Kingdom under the <a href="/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Acts" class="mw-redirect" title="Representation of the People Acts">Representation of the People Acts</a> as <i>overseas voters</i>, were excluded from the <a href="/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum" title="2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum">2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum</a>.
</p><p>Guernsey has an <a href="/wiki/Unwritten_constitution" class="mw-redirect" title="Unwritten constitution">unwritten constitution</a> arising from the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_(1259)" title="Treaty of Paris (1259)">Treaty of Paris (1259)</a>. When Henry III and the King of France came to terms over the Duchy of Normandy, the <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Norman mainland</a> fell under the <a href="/wiki/Suzerainty" title="Suzerainty">suzerainty</a> of the King of France. The Channel Islands however remained loyal to the British crown due to the loyalties of its <a href="/wiki/Seigneur" title="Seigneur">Seigneurs</a>. However, they were never absorbed into the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_England" title="Kingdom of England">Kingdom of England</a> by any Act of Union and exist as "peculiars of the Crown".
</p><p>A unique constitutional position has arisen as successive British monarchs have confirmed the liberties and privileges of the Bailiwick, often referring to the so-called <i>Constitutions of King John</i>, a legendary document supposed to have been granted by <a href="/wiki/John,_King_of_England" title="John, King of England">King John</a> in the aftermath of 1204. Governments of the Bailiwick have generally tried to avoid testing the limits of the unwritten constitution by avoiding conflict with British governments.
</p><p>This peculiar political position has often been to the benefit of islanders. Until the 19th century, the Bailiwick was generally able to be exempt from the harsher parts of Westminster legislation, while being included in favourable policies, such as <a href="/wiki/Protectionism" title="Protectionism">protectionist</a> economic policies. England, and later the United Kingdom, passively exploited the strategic benefits of the Channel Islands. For example, they were able to serve as a convenient stop-off point for trade to <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Parishes">Parishes</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Parishes">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The bailiwick comprises <a href="/wiki/Parishes_of_Guernsey" title="Parishes of Guernsey">twelve parishes</a>: Alderney, Sark and ten on mainland Guernsey (one of which includes Herm). Each parish has a parish church from the 11th century, with strong religious control exercised initially from the French Catholic church and for the last 500 years from the English church. Over the years the religious aspect of the administration of each parish has been reduced in favour of democratically elected douzeniers.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Jurisdictions">Jurisdictions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Jurisdictions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Each jurisdiction has inhabited and uninhabited islands and its own elected government. All three legal jurisdictions need <a href="/wiki/Royal_assent" title="Royal assent">royal assent</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Monarchy_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Monarchy of the United Kingdom">Monarch</a> on its primary legislation. Each jurisdiction raises its own taxation,<sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> although in 1949 Alderney transferred its rights to Guernsey.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Alderney">Alderney</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Alderney">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Alderney" title="Alderney">Alderney</a></div>
<p>With a population of around 2,200 in 7.8&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (3&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/Alderney" title="Alderney">Alderney</a> has its own parliament, the <a href="/wiki/States_of_Alderney" title="States of Alderney">States of Alderney</a>, which has ten elected members and an elected president.<sup id="cite_ref-CIBO_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CIBO-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>From 1612 Alderney had a judge appointed, with similar judicial powers to a bailiff; but on 1 January 1949 the island adopted a new constitution, giving up some independence, moving closer to Guernsey and confirming that it is part of the Bailiwick of Guernsey.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (January 2019)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Guernsey">Guernsey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Guernsey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Guernsey" title="Guernsey">Guernsey</a></div>
<p>The island of <a href="/wiki/Guernsey" title="Guernsey">Guernsey</a> has a population of around 63,000 in 62&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (24&#160;sq&#160;mi) and forms the legal and administrative centre of the Bailiwick of Guernsey. The parliament of Guernsey and of the nearby inhabited islands of <a href="/wiki/Herm" title="Herm">Herm</a>, <a href="/wiki/Jethou" title="Jethou">Jethou</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lihou" title="Lihou">Lihou</a><sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> is the <a href="/wiki/States_of_Guernsey" title="States of Guernsey">States of Guernsey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CIBO_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CIBO-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sark">Sark</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Sark">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Sark" title="Sark">Sark</a></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Sark" title="Sark">Sark</a> has a population of around 600 who live in 2 square miles (5.2&#160;km<sup>2</sup>). Its parliament (together with the inhabited island of <a href="/wiki/Brecqhou" title="Brecqhou">Brecqhou</a>)<sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> is the <a href="/wiki/Chief_Pleas" class="mw-redirect" title="Chief Pleas">Chief Pleas</a> of Sark, with 18 elected members.<sup id="cite_ref-CIBO_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CIBO-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1565, <a href="/wiki/Helier_de_Carteret" class="mw-redirect" title="Helier de Carteret">Helier de Carteret</a>, <a href="/wiki/Seigneur" title="Seigneur">Seigneur</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ouen,_Jersey" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Ouen, Jersey">St. Ouen</a> in <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a>, was granted the <a href="/wiki/Fief" title="Fief">fief</a> of Sark by <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Queen Elizabeth I</a>. He received <a href="/wiki/Letters_patent" title="Letters patent">letters patent</a> granting him Sark in perpetuity, on condition that he kept the island free of pirates and that the island was occupied by at least forty men to defend it. Despite most families coming from Jersey, Sark remained within the Bailiwick of Guernsey.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Recognition">Recognition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bailiwick_of_Guernsey&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Recognition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Three_Leopards_Shield.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Three_Leopards_Shield.jpg/220px-Three_Leopards_Shield.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="256" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/Three_Leopards_Shield.jpg/330px-Three_Leopards_Shield.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Three_Leopards_Shield.jpg 2x" data-file-width="427" data-file-height="496" /></a><figcaption>Duchy of Normandy three leopards symbol</figcaption></figure>
<p>There is no flag or coat of arms for the Bailiwick of Guernsey. In historic times, the governor would have used his personal symbols before a generic flag for use by the governor was created.
</p><p>In 1279 <a href="/wiki/Edward_I_of_England" title="Edward I of England">Edward I</a> granted a <a href="/wiki/Seal_(emblem)" title="Seal (emblem)">Seal</a> for use in the Channel Islands. In 1304 separate seals were provided to Jersey and Guernsey. The provision of separate seals is one of the earliest indications of the separate identity and personality of the two Bailiwicks. The seal comprised three leopards (or lions), a symbol taken from the original arms of the Duchy of Normandy.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The United Kingdom and <a href="/wiki/Government_of_the_United_Kingdom" title="Government of the United Kingdom">His Majesty's Government in the United Kingdom</a> are responsible for the defence and also for <i>formal</i> international, intergovernmental and consular representation of, and the foreign affairs generally, of the Bailiwick.<sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>While never a member of the European Union or its predecessors, before <a href="/wiki/Brexit" title="Brexit">Brexit</a> the Bailiwick had a special relationship with the EU, under Protocol 3 of the UK's <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Accession_1972" title="Treaty of Accession 1972">Treaty of Accession 1972</a> to the European Community.<sup id="cite_ref-MOJ_14-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MOJ-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Pooling resources with Jersey, the Bailiwick established in 2010 an office in <a href="/wiki/Brussels" title="Brussels">Brussels</a> to develop the Channel Islands' influence with the EU,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> to advise the Channel Islands' governments on European matters, and to promote economic links with the EU.<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Bailiwick of Guernsey is in the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations" title="Commonwealth of Nations">Commonwealth (Commonwealth of Nations)</a>, although not as a <i>member</i>, <i>in its own right</i>. The Bailiwick is also a member of the Commonwealth Games Federation, and competes in the <a href="/wiki/Commonwealth_Games" title="Commonwealth Games">Commonwealth Games</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1969 <a href="/wiki/Royal_Mail" title="Royal Mail">Royal Mail</a> relinquished control of postal services in the Bailiwick,<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> with Guernsey then being recognised by the <a href="/wiki/Universal_Postal_Union" title="Universal Postal Union">Universal Postal Union</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Since 1999 the Bailiwick of Guernsey has been a member of the <a href="/wiki/British%E2%80%93Irish_Council" title="British–Irish Council">British–Irish Council</a>, currently represented by the <a href="/wiki/Policy_and_Resources_Committee_of_Guernsey" class="mw-redirect" title="Policy and Resources Committee of Guernsey">Chief Minister of Guernsey</a>.
</p>
