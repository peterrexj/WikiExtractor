<p><b>Eugene Bossilkov</b>, born <b>Vincent Bossilkov</b> (b. 16 Nov 1900-11 Nov 1952), was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Passionist" class="mw-redirect" title="Passionist">Passionist</a> Congregation, <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic bishop">Roman Catholic bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nicopolis" title="Nicopolis">Nicopolis</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Communist" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist">Communist</a> campaign in <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a> against religion. He had studied in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for his doctorate at the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Oriental_Institute" title="Pontifical Oriental Institute">Pontifical Oriental Institute</a> and became a parish priest in the Danube Valley.  After becoming bishop, in 1952 he was arrested, together with many other religious, and executed for ostensible crimes against the state.  He was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1998.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eugene_Bossilkov&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Vincent Bossilkov was born to a family of <a href="/wiki/Bulgarians" title="Bulgarians">Bulgarian</a> <a href="/wiki/Latin_Rite" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin Rite">Latin Rite</a> <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholics</a> on November 16, 1900 in <a href="/wiki/Belene" title="Belene">Belene</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>. After studies, he entered the <a href="/wiki/Passionist" class="mw-redirect" title="Passionist">Passionist</a> Congregation at the age of 14.  The Passionists are an Italian <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> founded by <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a> <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_the_Cross" title="Paul of the Cross">Paul of the Cross</a> in the eighteenth century.  They have practiced in Bulgaria since 1781.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Bossilkov studied in Passionist houses in the <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">Netherlands</a> and <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a> and took the religious name Eugene. He professed his vows in 1920 and was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the  <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> in 1926.
</p><p>He had returned to Bulgaria in 1924 and had pursued theological studies. In 1927 he went to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> to take his doctorate at the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Oriental_Institute" title="Pontifical Oriental Institute">Pontifical Oriental Institute</a>, where he wrote a thesis on the Union of Bulgarians with the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> during the early 13th century. On his return to Bulgaria, Bossilkov served in various Diocesan offices, but he preferred working with the <a href="/wiki/Laity" title="Laity">laity</a>. He took up a post as <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Danube_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Danube River">Danube River</a> valley. Here his reputation for scholarship grew, and he was noted for his work with the youth of the parish.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the wake of <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet Union</a> invaded the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria" title="Kingdom of Bulgaria">Kingdom of Bulgaria</a> and installed a <a href="/wiki/Communist" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist">Communist</a> government answering to <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Stalin" title="Joseph Stalin">Joseph Stalin</a>. The new regime began to enact laws to destroy religious institutions and beliefs. At this time, Bossilkov was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">Bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nicopolis" title="Nicopolis">Nicopolis</a> in 1947.<sup id="cite_ref-Mercurio,_R_p._179_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mercurio,_R_p._179-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  From 1949 the attitude of the State to religious institutes worsened. In the same year the government deported the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Delegate" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Delegate">Apostolic Delegate</a>, seized Catholic Church property, and suppressed the religious congregations. In 1952 the government began to make mass arrests of church officials. On July 16, police seized Bossilkov in <a href="/wiki/Sofia" title="Sofia">Sophia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mercurio,_R_p._179_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mercurio,_R_p._179-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Bossilkov suffered both physical and mental <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">torture</a> in prison, where he was told to confess to being the leader of a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Cabal" title="Cabal">conspiracy</a> to subvert <a href="/wiki/Communism" title="Communism">Communism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cptryon.org_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cptryon.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  At a political "show trial", two guns supposedly seized from the Catholic college in Sophia were presented as evidence. The pistols were part of a museum exhibit.<sup id="cite_ref-cptryon.org_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cptryon.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Bossilkov was found guilty and the official sentence against him read;
</p>
<blockquote><p>By virtue of articles 70 and 83 of the <a href="/wiki/Penal_code" class="mw-redirect" title="Penal code">penal code</a>, the Court condemns the accused, Eugene Bossilkov, to be sentenced to death by <a href="/wiki/Firing_squad" class="mw-redirect" title="Firing squad">firing squad</a>, and all his goods confiscated... Dr.Eugene Bossilkov, Catholic bishop; completed his religious studies in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> and was trained by the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Vatican</a> for <a href="/wiki/Counter-revolutionary" title="Counter-revolutionary">counter-revolutionary</a> activities and <a href="/wiki/Espionage" title="Espionage">espionage</a>. He is one of the directors of a clandestine Catholic organization. He was in touch with <a href="/wiki/Diplomats" class="mw-redirect" title="Diplomats">diplomats</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Imperialist" class="mw-redirect" title="Imperialist">imperialist</a> countries and gave them information of a confidential nature. The accused convoked a diocesan council in which it was decided to combat <a href="/wiki/Communism" title="Communism">Communism</a> through religious conferences, held in <a href="/wiki/Bulgaria" title="Bulgaria">Bulgaria</a>, activities called ' a mission.' No <a href="/wiki/Appeal" title="Appeal">appeal</a> of his sentence is possible.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Bossilkov was executed by firing squad in the grounds of the prison on the night of November 11 at 11:30 pm. Thrown into a <a href="/wiki/Mass_grave" title="Mass grave">mass grave</a>, his body was never recovered.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> had mentioned Bossilkov's being condemned to death in his encyclical letter "<a href="/wiki/Orientales_Ecclesias" title="Orientales Ecclesias">Orientales Ecclesias</a>" to the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Churches" title="Eastern Catholic Churches">Oriental Churches</a> in 1952.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> It was not until 1975 that the bishop's death was confirmed, however; when a Bulgarian minister visited the Vatican and was asked directly by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> what happened to the bishop, he answered that Bossilkov had died in prison 23 years before.<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eugene_Bossilkov&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During the 1980s, supporters gathered evidence regarding Bossilkov's life and death, and put before the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. At meetings in 1993 and 1994 the Congregation declared it was favourable to the cause of Bossilkov as a Catholic <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> from a theological and juridical perspective. On March 15, 1998 Pope John Paul II declared Bossilkov "<a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
