<p><b>Reineldis</b> (also <b>Reinhild</b>, <b>Reinaldes</b>, <b>Rainelde</b> among others; <i>c.</i> 630 – <i>c.</i> 700) was a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> of the 7th century, <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Huns" title="Huns">Huns</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Reineldis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Reineldis was born in a place called <i>Condacum</i> (which is identified with either <a href="/wiki/Cond%C3%A9-sur-l%27Escaut" title="Condé-sur-l&#39;Escaut">Condé-sur-l'Escaut</a> or <a href="/wiki/Kontich" title="Kontich">Kontich</a>). She was the daughter of Duke Witger of <a href="/wiki/Lotharingia" title="Lotharingia">Lotharingia</a> and Saint <a href="/wiki/Amalberga_of_Maubeuge" title="Amalberga of Maubeuge">Amalberga of Maubeuge</a>. Her brother <a href="/wiki/Emebert" title="Emebert">Emebert</a> was a priest in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Cambrai" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Cambrai">diocese of Cambrai</a>. Her biography mentions <a href="/wiki/Gudula" title="Gudula">Gudula</a> as her sister. Her mother entered the <a href="/wiki/Religious_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious (Catholicism)">religious</a> life at <a href="/wiki/Maubeuge_Abbey" title="Maubeuge Abbey">Maubeuge Abbey</a>.
</p><p>Reineldis made a <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>. Her <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">vita</a></i>, written between 1048 and 1051 in <a href="/wiki/Lobbes_Abbey" title="Lobbes Abbey">Lobbes Abbey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
records this fact, stating that she visited <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>.
</p><p>She returned home and devoted herself to a life of <a href="/wiki/Charity_(virtue)" class="mw-redirect" title="Charity (virtue)">charitable work</a> at Saintes.<sup id="cite_ref-patrick_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patrick-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was decapitated by the <a href="/wiki/Huns" title="Huns">Huns</a> at Saintes, together with deacon Grimoaldus and her servant Gondulphus.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Reineldis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Reineldis is primarily venerated in Saintes as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the town. Some sources even indicate that Saintes owes its name to Reineldis' martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-origin_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-origin-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Parish_church" title="Parish church">parish church</a> of Saintes is dedicated to Sainte-Renelde since the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> and has preserved the <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> of Saint Reineldis. This church has a large bell tower built in the 17th century.<sup id="cite_ref-saintes_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saintes-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-tower_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tower-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Reineldis' patronage for <a href="/wiki/Eye_disease" title="Eye disease">eye diseases</a> is due to the association with a <a href="/wiki/Water_well" class="mw-redirect" title="Water well">water well</a> in Saintes known as "Sainte Renelde's well", water which is believed to cure eye diseases.<sup id="cite_ref-saintes_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-saintes-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-well_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-well-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Reineldis&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Reineldis is commonly depicted with a sword or being dragged by her hair, referring to the decapitation. She is also portrayed as a pilgrim, because of her journey to the Holy Land.
</p>
