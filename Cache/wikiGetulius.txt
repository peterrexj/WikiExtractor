					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Getulius</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Protomartyrs of <a href="/wiki/Sabina_(region)" title="Sabina (region)">Sabina</a></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Gabii (Getulius)</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">120 AD<br />Gabii</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><span class="nowrap">Major <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a></span></th><td class="infobox-data">Sant'Angelo in Pescheria, Rome</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">June 10</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saint Getulius</b> (died 120 AD) is venerated together with <b>Amantius (Amancius), Cerealus (Caerealis)</b>, and <b>Primitivus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Getulio, Amanzio, Cereale, e Primitivo</i>) as a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  They are considered to have died at <a href="/wiki/Gabii" title="Gabii">Gabii</a>.  According to tradition, Getulius was the husband of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Symphorosa" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Symphorosa">Saint Symphorosa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  Getulius is a name meaning "of the <a href="/wiki/Gaetulia" class="mw-redirect" title="Gaetulia">Gaetuli</a>", which was a tribe of <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legends_and_burial">Legends and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Getulius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legends and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to his legend, Getulius was a native of Gabii in <a href="/wiki/Sabina_(region)" title="Sabina (region)">Sabina</a>.  Getulius was an officer in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a> who resigned when he became a Christian.  He retired to his estates near <a href="/wiki/Tivoli,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Tivoli, Italy">Tivoli</a>.  Caerealis was an imperial <a href="/wiki/Legatus" title="Legatus">legate</a> sent to arrest him but was converted to Christianity by Getulius.  Primitivus was another officer sent to arrest him, but he was also converted.  Amantius was Getulius' brother.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to his Passio, all four men were tied to a stake and set alight.  However, the fire did not harm them, so they were brutally clubbed and then <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a>.
</p><p>Some say Getulius was killed on the <a href="/wiki/Via_Salaria" title="Via Salaria">Via Salaria</a> and is called the father of the Seven Martyrs and the husband of Symphorosa.  His companions are called Caerealis, Amantius, and Primitivus.  They were imprisoned, thrown into the flames but emerged unharmed, and then beaten to death with clubs.  The legend further states that Saint Symphorosa buried them in an <i>arenarium</i> on her estate.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Their seven sons (not to be confused with the seven sons of <a href="/wiki/Felicity_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Felicity of Rome">Felicity of Rome</a>) are named specifically.  According to their legend, each of them suffered a different kind of martyrdom. <b>Crescens</b> was pierced through the <a href="/wiki/Throat" title="Throat">throat</a>, <b>Julian</b> through the <a href="/wiki/Breast" title="Breast">breast</a>, <b>Nemesius</b> through the <a href="/wiki/Heart" title="Heart">heart</a>, <b>Primitivus</b> was wounded at the <a href="/wiki/Navel" title="Navel">navel</a>, <b>Justinus</b> was pierced through the <a href="/wiki/Human_back" title="Human back">back</a>, <b>Stracteus</b> (Stacteus, Estacteus) was wounded at the side, and <b>Eugenius</b> was cleft in two parts from top to bottom.
</p><p>The Martyrology of <a href="/wiki/Ado_(archbishop)" class="mw-redirect" title="Ado (archbishop)">Ado</a> states: <i>consumati sunt beati Martyres Gethulii in fundo Capriolis, viam Salariam, ab urbe Romam, plus minus miliario decimotertio, supra flumium Tiberim, in partem Savinensium</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>     By <i>Capriolis, viam Salariam</i>, Ado is referring to a place on the <a href="/wiki/Tiber_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Tiber River">Tiber River</a> later known in the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> as the <i>Corte di San Getulio</i> (today part of <a href="/wiki/Montopoli_di_Sabina" title="Montopoli di Sabina">Montopoli di Sabina</a>), because a church was built here that originally held some of the saint's <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>     In 867, Abbot Peter of Farfa moved these relics to <a href="/wiki/Farfa_Abbey" title="Farfa Abbey">his abbey</a> in a solemn ceremony.  However, Getulius' relics are also considered to lie in Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Getulius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG/250px-15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG/375px-15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG/500px-15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2048" data-file-height="1536" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:15_Portico_di_Ottavia.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The façade of Sant'Angelo in Pescheria</div></div></div>
<p>There was previously a church of San Getulio at <a href="/wiki/Teramo" title="Teramo">Teramo</a>.
</p><p>Getulius' <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> are purported to be at Rome, in the principal <a href="/wiki/Altar" title="Altar">altar</a> of the church of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Angelo_in_Pescheria" title="Sant&#39;Angelo in Pescheria">Sant'Angelo in Pescheria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The relics of his purported wife St. Symphorosa and their seven sons were transferred to the Church of Sant'Angelo in Pescheria at Rome by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II" title="Pope Stephen II">Pope Stephen II</a> in 752. A <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> was found here in 1610, bearing the inscription: <i>Hic requiescunt corpora SS. Martyrum Simforosae, viri sui Zotici (Getulii) et Filiorum ejus a Stephano Papa translata</i>.  They were placed in a glass urn during the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pius_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius IV">Pius IV</a>, who placed the relics in an <a href="/wiki/Urn" title="Urn">urn</a> of glass.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  In 1584, part of his relics were donated by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory XIII">Gregory XIII</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>, and these relics are found in a chapel near the <a href="/wiki/Villa_d%27Este" title="Villa d&#39;Este">Villa d'Este</a>.  Some relics were taken to Jesuit colleges in <a href="/wiki/India" title="India">India</a> and <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> (June 25, 1572).  On September 26, 1587, to prevent further distribution, Mariano Perbenedetti, governor of Rome, enclosed the rest of the relics at Sant'Angelo in a <a href="/wiki/Sarcophagus" title="Sarcophagus">sarcophagus</a> of marble.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  This same sarcophagus also holds relics of <a href="/wiki/Cyrus_and_John" title="Cyrus and John">Cyrus and John</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-San_Getulio_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-San_Getulio-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
