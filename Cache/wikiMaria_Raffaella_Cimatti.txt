<p><b>Maria Raffaella Cimatti</b> (7 June 1861 – 23 June 1945) was an Italian member of the <a href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suore_ospedaliere_della_misericordia" class="extiw" title="it:Suore ospedaliere della misericordia">Hospitaller Sisters of Mercy</a>, a religious institute dedicated to medical care. She was beatified by the Catholic Church in 1996.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Raffaella_Cimatti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was born <b>Santina Cimatti</b> on 7 June 1861<sup id="cite_ref-italian_bio_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-italian_bio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in Celle, a village now a part of <a href="/wiki/Faenza" title="Faenza">Faenza</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Ravenna" title="Province of Ravenna">Province of Ravenna</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-brief_bio,_English_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brief_bio,_English-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> to Giacomo Cimatti and his wife, Rosa Pasi. Her father was a farmworker and her mother a weaver. After Santina, the couple had five boys: Domenico, Paolo and Antonio, who all died in infancy, and Luigi and Vincenzo, who both later joined the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesians of Don Bosco</a>. Upon her father's death in 1882, Cimatti helped her mother by schooling her brothers, though from a young age she wanted to enter <a href="/wiki/Religious_life" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious life">religious life</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-family_information_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-family_information-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After her brothers joined the Salesians and the local <a href="/wiki/Parish_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish priest">parish priest</a> took her mother in, Cimatti, who was then freed of family obligations, went to Rome and joined the Hospitaler Sisters of Mercy in 1889.<sup id="cite_ref-Beatification_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beatification-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> There she was given the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> <b>Maria Raffaella</b> and dedicated her life to helping the sick the following year, taking her temporary <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> in 1890.<sup id="cite_ref-family_information_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-family_information-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  In 1893 she was is sent to St. Benedict Hospital in <a href="/wiki/Alatri" title="Alatri">Alatri</a>, <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Frosinone" title="Province of Frosinone">Frosinone</a>, to study nursing<sup id="cite_ref-italian_bio_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-italian_bio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and to serve as a pharmacist's assistant. She took her perpetual vows in 1905.<sup id="cite_ref-Beatification_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beatification-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Later, Cimatti was sent to nurse for her first posting at the <a href="/wiki/Umberto_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Umberto I">Umberto I</a> Hospital in <a href="/wiki/Frosinone" title="Frosinone">Frosinone</a>, where in 1921, she was also placed in charge of the convent as <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">prioress</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-italian_bio_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-italian_bio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Her primary duty during her career was as hospital pharmacist.<sup id="cite_ref-brief_bio,_English_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brief_bio,_English-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In 1928 she was sent back to the convent in Alatri, where she served as prioress until 1940, when she asked to be allowed to retire from her administrative duties and to serve the Sisters, the patients, the students and the hospital staff.<sup id="cite_ref-family_information_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-family_information-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was diagnosed with a fatal disease in 1943.<sup id="cite_ref-italian_bio_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-italian_bio-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> The following year, under the German occupation of the city during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>, she nursed the war wounded and personally interceded with the German <a href="/wiki/Generalfeldmarschall" title="Generalfeldmarschall">Generalfeldmarschall</a> <a href="/wiki/Albert_Kesselring" title="Albert Kesselring">Albert Kesselring</a> at his headquarters in Alatri when rumors of bombing the city surfaced. The city was spared.<sup id="cite_ref-Beatification_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beatification-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She died on 23 June 1945 in Alatri,<sup id="cite_ref-brief_bio,_English_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brief_bio,_English-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and buried in a little <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> which served the hospital.<sup id="cite_ref-family_information_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-family_information-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Raffaella_Cimatti&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A steady veneration of Cimatti having developed after her death, a <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a> process was opened on 6 June 1962 in Alatri by Vittorio Ottaviani, the local <a href="/wiki/Bishop_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop (Catholic Church)">bishop</a>. For five years, the Ordinary Process on the Reputation of Holiness was conducted and the <a href="/wiki/Letter_rogatory" class="mw-redirect" title="Letter rogatory">Rogatory Process</a> took place in Faenza in 1967. Between 1988 and 1989 her intercession in a miraculous healing was investigated.
</p><p>The cause was accepted for further investigation by the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a>, which led to a decree declaring her life to have been one of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> in 1994, making it possible for her <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-family_information_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-family_information-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She was beatified on 12 May 1996<sup id="cite_ref-brief_bio,_English_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-brief_bio,_English-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a><sup id="cite_ref-Beatification_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Beatification-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Square" title="St. Peter&#39;s Square">St. Peter's Square</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vatican_City" title="Vatican City">Vatican City</a>, and her remains were moved from the hospital chapel to the <a href="/wiki/Alatri_Cathedral" title="Alatri Cathedral">Cathedral of St. Paul</a> in Alatri.<sup id="cite_ref-family_information_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-family_information-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
