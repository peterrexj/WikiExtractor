<p><b>Pope Urban VIII</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Urbanus VIII</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Urbano VIII</i>; baptised 5 April 1568&#160;– 29 July 1644), born <b>Maffeo Vincenzo Barberini</b>, was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 6 August 1623 to his death, in July 1644. As <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>, he expanded the papal territory by force of arms and advantageous politicking, and was also a prominent patron of the arts and a reformer of Church missions.
</p><p>However, the massive debts incurred during his pontificate greatly weakened his successors, who were unable to maintain the papacy's longstanding political and military influence in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>. He was also an opponent of <a href="/wiki/Copernican_heliocentrism" title="Copernican heliocentrism">Copernicanism</a> and involved in the <a href="/wiki/Galileo_affair" title="Galileo affair">Galileo affair</a>. He is the last pope to date to take the pontifical name "Urban".
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg/200px-Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="274" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg/300px-Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg/400px-Caravaggio_Maffeo_Barberini.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2806" data-file-height="3850" /></a><figcaption>C. 1598 painting of Maffeo Barberini at age 30 by <a href="/wiki/Michelangelo_Merisi_da_Caravaggio" class="mw-redirect" title="Michelangelo Merisi da Caravaggio">Caravaggio</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Maffeo Vincenzo Barberini was born in April 1568, the son of Antonio <a href="/wiki/Barberini" class="mw-redirect" title="Barberini">Barberini</a>, a Florentine nobleman, and Camilla Barbadoro.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBarton1964115_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarton1964115-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was born at <a href="/wiki/Barberino_Val_d%27Elsa" title="Barberino Val d&#39;Elsa">Barberino Val d'Elsa</a> in "Tafania" house. His father died when he was only three years old and his mother took him to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>, where he was put in the charge of his uncle, Francesco Barberini, an <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_protonotary" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic protonotary">apostolic protonotary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOtt1912_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOtt1912-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the age of 16, he became his uncle's heir.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKeyvanian2005294_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeyvanian2005294-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was educated by the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> ("Jesuits"), and received a doctorate of law from the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Pisa" title="University of Pisa">University of Pisa</a> in 1589.
</p><p>In 1601, Barberini, through the influence of his uncle, was able to secure from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> appointment as a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> to the court of King <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France" title="Henry IV of France">Henry IV of France</a>. In 1604, the same pope appointed him as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Nazareth" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Nazareth">Archbishop of Nazareth</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOtt1912_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOtt1912-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> an office joined with that of Bishop of the suppressed Dioceses of Canne and Monteverde, with his residence at <a href="/wiki/Barletta" title="Barletta">Barletta</a>. At the death of his uncle, he inherited his riches, with which he bought a palace in Rome, which he made into a luxurious Renaissance residence.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_V" title="Pope Paul V">Pope Paul V</a> also later employed Barberini in a similar capacity, afterwards raising him, in 1606, to the order of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Cardinal" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Cardinal">Cardinal-Priest</a>, with the <a href="/wiki/Titular_church" title="Titular church">titular church</a> of <a href="/wiki/Church_of_San_Pietro_in_Montorio" class="mw-redirect" title="Church of San Pietro in Montorio">San Pietro in Montorio</a> and appointing him as a <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> of <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEOtt1912_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEOtt1912-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Papal_election">Papal election</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Papal election">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/1623_papal_conclave" title="1623 papal conclave">1623 papal conclave</a></div>
<p>Barberini was considered someone who could be elected as pope, though there were those such as Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Ottavio_Bandini" title="Ottavio Bandini">Ottavio Bandini</a> who worked to prevent it. Despite this, throughout 29–30 July, the cardinals began an intense series of negotiations to test the numbers as to who could emerge from the conclave as pope, with Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Ludovico_Ludovisi" title="Ludovico Ludovisi">Ludovico Ludovisi</a> dismissing Barberini's chances as long as Barberini remained a close ally of Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Scipione_Borghese" title="Scipione Borghese">Scipione Borghese</a>, whose faction Barberini supported. Ludovisi had discussions with Cardinals Farnese, Medici and Aldobrandini on 30 July about seeing to Barberini's election. The three supported his candidacy and went about securing the support of others, which led to Barberini's election just over a week later.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On 6 August 1623, at the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1623" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1623">papal conclave</a> following the death of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XV" title="Pope Gregory XV">Pope Gregory XV</a>, Barberini was chosen as Gregory XV's successor and took the name Urban VIII. His coronation had to be postponed until 29 September 1623 since the new pontiff was ill at the time of his election.
</p><p>Upon Pope Urban VIII's election, Zeno, the Venetian envoy, wrote the following description of him:<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The new Pontiff is 56 years old. His Holiness is tall, dark, with regular features and black hair turning grey. He is exceptionally elegant and refined in all details of his dress; has a graceful and aristocratic bearing and exquisite taste. He is an excellent speaker and debater, writes verses and patronises poets and men of letters.</p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Activities">Activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif/lossy-page1-220px-Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="278" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif/lossy-page1-330px-Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif/lossy-page1-440px-Dankaerts-Historis-9264.tif.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3496" data-file-height="4424" /></a><figcaption>Engraving of Pope Urban VIII</figcaption></figure>
<p>Urban VIII's papacy covered 21 years of the <a href="/wiki/Thirty_Years%27_War" title="Thirty Years&#39; War">Thirty Years' War</a>, (1618-1648) and was an eventful one, even by the standards of the day. He <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Portugal" title="Elizabeth of Portugal">Elizabeth of Portugal</a>,  <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Corsini" title="Andrew Corsini">Andrew Corsini</a> and <a href="/wiki/Conrad_of_Piacenza" title="Conrad of Piacenza">Conrad of Piacenza</a>, and issued the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bulls</a> of canonization for <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a> (founder of the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a>, "<a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>") and <a href="/wiki/Francis_Xavier" title="Francis Xavier">Francis Xavier</a> (also a Jesuit), who had been canonized by his predecessor, Pope Gregory XV.
</p><p>Despite an early friendship and encouragement for his teachings, Urban VIII was responsible for summoning the scientist and astronomer <a href="/wiki/Galileo_Galilei" title="Galileo Galilei">Galileo</a> to Rome in 1633 to recant his work. Urban VIII was opposed to <a href="/wiki/Copernican_heliocentrism" title="Copernican heliocentrism">Copernican heliocentrism</a> and he ordered <a href="/wiki/Galileo_affair" title="Galileo affair">Galileo's second trial</a> after the publication of <i><a href="/wiki/Dialogue_Concerning_the_Two_Chief_World_Systems" title="Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems">Dialogue Concerning the Two Chief World Systems</a></i>, in which Urban's point of view is argued by the character "Simplicio".
</p><p>Urban VIII practiced <a href="/wiki/Nepotism" title="Nepotism">nepotism</a> on a grand scale; various members of his family were enormously enriched by him, so that it seemed to contemporaries as if he were establishing a <a href="/wiki/Barberini" class="mw-redirect" title="Barberini">Barberini</a> dynasty.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> He elevated his brother <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Marcello_Barberini" title="Antonio Marcello Barberini">Antonio Marcello Barberini</a> (Antonio the Elder) and then his nephews <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Barberini_(seniore)" class="mw-redirect" title="Francesco Barberini (seniore)">Francesco Barberini</a> and <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Barberini" title="Antonio Barberini">Antonio Barberini</a> (Antonio the Younger) to <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a>. He also bestowed upon their brother, <a href="/wiki/Taddeo_Barberini" title="Taddeo Barberini">Taddeo Barberini</a>, the titles <i>Prince of <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Gonfalonier_of_the_Church" title="Gonfalonier of the Church">Gonfalonier of the Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">Prefect of Rome</a> and <i>Commander of <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Sant'Angelo</a></i>. Historian <a href="/wiki/Leopold_von_Ranke" title="Leopold von Ranke">Leopold von Ranke</a> estimated that during his reign, Urban VIII's immediate family amassed 105 million <a href="/wiki/Scudi" class="mw-redirect" title="Scudi">scudi</a> in personal wealth.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Urban VIII was a skilled writer of Latin verse, and a collection of <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Scriptural</a> paraphrases as well as original hymns of his composition have been frequently reprinted.
</p><p>The 1638 papal bull <i>Commissum Nobis</i> protected the existence of <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Mission_(Christian)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mission (Christian)">missions</a> in <a href="/wiki/South_America" title="South America">South America</a> by forbidding the enslavement of natives who were at the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit_Reductions" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit Reductions">Jesuit Reductions</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMooney1910_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMooney1910-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> At the same time, Urban VIII repealed the Jesuit monopoly on missionary work in <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a> and <a href="/wiki/Japan" title="Japan">Japan</a>, opening these countries to missionaries of other orders and missionary societies.<sup id="cite_ref-van_Helden_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van_Helden-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Urban VIII issued a 1624 papal bull that made the use of <a href="/wiki/Tobacco" title="Tobacco">tobacco</a> in holy places punishable by excommunication;<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECosner202114_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECosner202114-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> repealed the ban one hundred years later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPattenden2020391_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPattenden2020391-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonizations_and_beatifications">Canonizations and beatifications</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Canonizations and beatifications">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Urban VIII canonized five saints during his pontificate: <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Harding" title="Stephen Harding">Stephen Harding</a> (1623), <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Portugal" title="Elizabeth of Portugal">Elizabeth of Portugal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Conrad_of_Piacenza" title="Conrad of Piacenza">Conrad of Piacenza</a> (1625), <a href="/wiki/Peter_Nolasco" title="Peter Nolasco">Peter Nolasco</a> (1628), and <a href="/wiki/Andrew_Corsini" title="Andrew Corsini">Andrea Corsini</a> (1629). The pope also beatified 68 individuals, including the <a href="/wiki/26_Martyrs_of_Japan" title="26 Martyrs of Japan">Martyrs of Nagasaki</a> (1627).
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Consistories">Consistories</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Consistories">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Cardinals_created_by_Urban_VIII" title="Cardinals created by Urban VIII">Cardinals created by Urban VIII</a></div>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_(ca._1624-1627)_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_%28ca._1624-1627%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg/170px-Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_%28ca._1624-1627%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="270" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_%28ca._1624-1627%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg/255px-Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_%28ca._1624-1627%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_%28ca._1624-1627%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg/340px-Pietro_da_Cortona_-_Portrait_of_Urban_VIII_%28ca._1624-1627%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2238" data-file-height="3552" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of Urban VIII by <a href="/wiki/Pietro_da_Cortona" title="Pietro da Cortona">Pietro da Cortona</a>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1624–7</span></figcaption></figure>
<p>The pope created 74 cardinals in eight consistories throughout his pontificate, and this included his nephews <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Barberini_(1597%E2%80%931679)" title="Francesco Barberini (1597–1679)">Francesco</a> and <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Barberini" title="Antonio Barberini">Antonio</a>, cousin <a href="/wiki/Lorenzo_Magalotti_(cardinal)" title="Lorenzo Magalotti (cardinal)">Lorenzo Magalotti</a>, and the pope's own brother <a href="/wiki/Antonio_Marcello_Barberini" title="Antonio Marcello Barberini">Antonio Marcello</a>. He also created <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_X" title="Pope Innocent X">Giovanni Battista Pamphili</a> as a cardinal, with Pamphili becoming his immediate successor Pope Innocent X. The pope also created eight of those cardinals whom he had reserved <i><a href="/wiki/In_pectore" title="In pectore">in pectore</a></i>.
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Policy_on_private_revelation">Policy on private revelation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Policy on private revelation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>In the papal bull <i><a href="/wiki/Sanctissimus_Dominus_Noster" title="Sanctissimus Dominus Noster">Sanctissimus Dominus Noster</a></i> of 13 March 1625, Urban instructed Catholics not to venerate the deceased or represent them in the manner of saints without Church sanction. It required a bishop's approval for the publication of private revelations. Since the nineteenth century, it has become common for books of popular devotion to carry a disclaimer. One read in part: "In obedience to the decrees of Urban the Eighth, I declare that I have no intention of attributing any other than a purely human authority to the miracles, revelations, favours, and particular cases recorded in this book..."<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Politics">Politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Urban VIII's military involvement was aimed less at the restoration of <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholicism</a> in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> than at adjusting the balance of power to favour his own independence in <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>. In 1626, the <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Urbino" title="Duchy of Urbino">duchy of Urbino</a> was incorporated into the papal dominions,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECollins2009382_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECollins2009382-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and, in 1627, when the direct male line of the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Gonzaga" title="House of Gonzaga">Gonzagas</a> in <a href="/wiki/Mantua" title="Mantua">Mantua</a> became extinct, he controversially favoured the succession of the <a href="/wiki/Charles_I,_Duke_of_Mantua_and_Montferrat" class="mw-redirect" title="Charles I, Duke of Mantua and Montferrat">Duke Charles of Nevers</a> against the claims of the <a href="/wiki/Habsburgs" class="mw-redirect" title="Habsburgs">Habsburgs</a>. He also launched the <a href="/wiki/Wars_of_Castro" title="Wars of Castro">Wars of Castro</a> in 1641 against <a href="/wiki/Odoardo_Farnese" class="mw-redirect" title="Odoardo Farnese">Odoardo Farnese</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Parma_and_Piacenza" title="Duchy of Parma and Piacenza">Duke of Parma and Piacenza</a>, whom he excommunicated. <a href="/wiki/Castro,_Lazio" title="Castro, Lazio">Castro</a> was destroyed and its duchy incorporated into the Papal States.
</p><p>Urban VIII was the last pope to extend the papal territory. He fortified <a href="/wiki/Castelfranco_Emilia" title="Castelfranco Emilia">Castelfranco Emilia</a> on the Mantuan frontier and commissioned <a href="/wiki/Vincenzo_Maculani" title="Vincenzo Maculani">Vincenzo Maculani</a> to fortify the <a href="/wiki/Castel_Sant%27Angelo" title="Castel Sant&#39;Angelo">Castel Sant'Angelo</a> in Rome. Urban VIII also established an <a href="/wiki/Arsenal" title="Arsenal">arsenal</a> in the Vatican, an arms factory at <a href="/wiki/Tivoli,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Tivoli, Italy">Tivoli</a> and fortified the harbour of <a href="/wiki/Civitavecchia" title="Civitavecchia">Civitavecchia</a>.
</p><p>For the purposes of making cannon and <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Baldachin" title="St. Peter&#39;s Baldachin">the baldacchino</a> in St Peters, massive bronze girders were pillaged from the portico of the <a href="/wiki/Pantheon,_Rome" title="Pantheon, Rome">Pantheon</a> leading to the well known lampoon: <i>quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini,</i> "what the barbarians did not do, the Barberini did."<sup id="cite_ref-van_Helden_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-van_Helden-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patron_of_the_arts">Patron of the arts</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Patron of the arts">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Urban VIII expended vast sums bringing polymaths like <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_Kircher" title="Athanasius Kircher">Athanasius Kircher</a> to Rome and funding various substantial works by the sculptor and architect <a href="/wiki/Bernini" class="mw-redirect" title="Bernini">Bernini</a>, from whom he had already commissioned <i><a href="/wiki/Boy_with_a_Dragon" title="Boy with a Dragon">Boy with a Dragon</a></i> around 1617 and who was particularly favored during Urban VIII's reign. As well as <a href="/wiki/Busts_of_Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Busts of Pope Urban VIII">several portrait busts of Urban</a>, Urban commissioned Bernini to work on the family palace in Rome, the <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Barberini" title="Palazzo Barberini">Palazzo Barberini</a>, the college of the <a href="/wiki/Propaganda_Fide" class="mw-redirect" title="Propaganda Fide">Propaganda Fide</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Fontana_del_Tritone" class="mw-redirect" title="Fontana del Tritone">Fontana del Tritone</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Piazza_Barberini" title="Piazza Barberini">Piazza Barberini</a>, the <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Baldachin" title="St. Peter&#39;s Baldachin">baldacchino</a> and <i><a href="/wiki/Cathedra" title="Cathedra">cathedra</a></i> in <a href="/wiki/St_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="St Peter&#39;s Basilica">St Peter's Basilica</a> and other prominent structures in the city. Numerous members of Barberini's family also had their likeness caught in stone by Bernini, such as his brothers <a href="/wiki/Statue_of_Carlo_Barberini" title="Statue of Carlo Barberini">Carlo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bust_of_Antonio_Barberini_(Bernini)" title="Bust of Antonio Barberini (Bernini)">Antonio</a>. Urban also had rebuilt the Church of <a href="/wiki/Santa_Bibiana" title="Santa Bibiana">Santa Bibiana</a> and the Church of <a href="/wiki/San_Sebastiano_al_Palatino" title="San Sebastiano al Palatino">San Sebastiano al Palatino</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Palatine_Hill" title="Palatine Hill">Palatine Hill</a>.
</p><p>The Barberini patronised painters such as <a href="/wiki/Nicolas_Poussin" title="Nicolas Poussin">Nicolas Poussin</a> and <a href="/wiki/Claude_Lorrain" title="Claude Lorrain">Claude Lorrain</a>. One of the most eulogistic of these artistic works in its celebration of his reign, is the huge <i><a href="/wiki/Allegory_of_Divine_Providence_and_Barberini_Power_(Cortona)" class="mw-redirect" title="Allegory of Divine Providence and Barberini Power (Cortona)">Allegory of Divine Providence and Barberini Power</a></i> painted by <a href="/wiki/Pietro_da_Cortona" title="Pietro da Cortona">Pietro da Cortona</a> on the ceiling of the large salon of the Palazzo Barberini.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png/220px-Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="322" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png/330px-Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png/440px-Portland_Vase_BM_Gem4036_n4_resized_white-balanced_white_bg.png 2x" data-file-width="2300" data-file-height="3364" /></a><figcaption>The Barberini Vase, now renamed the Portland Vase</figcaption></figure>
<p>Another such acquisition, in a vast collection, was the purchase of the 'Barberini vase'. This was allegedly found at the mausoleum of the Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Severus_Alexander" title="Severus Alexander">Severus Alexander</a>  and his family at Monte Del Grano. The discovery of the vase is described by <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Santi_Bartoli" title="Pietro Santi Bartoli">Pietro Santi Bartoli</a> and referenced on page 28 of a book on The Portland Vase.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> Pietro Bartoli indicates that the vase contained the ashes of  the Roman Emperor. However, this together with the interpretations of the scenes depicted on it are the source of countless theories and disputed 'facts'. The vase remained in the Barberini family collection for some 150 years before passing through the hands of <a href="/wiki/William_Hamilton_(diplomat)" title="William Hamilton (diplomat)">Sir William Hamilton</a> Ambassador to the Royal Court in Naples. It was later sold to the <a href="/wiki/William_Cavendish-Bentinck,_3rd_Duke_of_Portland" title="William Cavendish-Bentinck, 3rd Duke of Portland">Duke and Duchess of Portland</a>, and has subsequently been known as the <a href="/wiki/Portland_Vase" title="Portland Vase">Portland Vase</a>. Following catastrophic damage, this glass vase (1-25BC) has been reconstructed three times and resides in the <a href="/wiki/British_Museum" title="British Museum">British Museum</a>. The <a href="/wiki/Portland_Vase" title="Portland Vase">Portland vase</a> itself was borrowed and near copied by <a href="/wiki/Josiah_Wedgwood" title="Josiah Wedgwood">Josiah Wedgewood</a> who appears to have added modesty drapery. The vase formed the basis of <a href="/wiki/Jasperware" title="Jasperware">Jasperware</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Later life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg/220px-Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg/330px-Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg/440px-Urban_VIII_Bernini_Musei_Capitolini.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1860" data-file-height="2475" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Pope Urban VIII sculpted by <a href="/wiki/Gian_Lorenzo_Bernini" title="Gian Lorenzo Bernini">Bernini</a> and his students between 1635 and 1640, and on display at the <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_dei_Conservatori" class="mw-redirect" title="Palazzo dei Conservatori">Palazzo dei Conservatori</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>A consequence of these military and artistic endeavours was a massive increase in papal debt. Urban VIII inherited a debt of 16 million <a href="/wiki/Italian_scudo" title="Italian scudo">scudi</a>, and by 1635 had increased it to 28 million.
</p><p>According to contemporary <a href="/wiki/John_Bargrave" title="John Bargrave">John Bargrave</a>, in 1636 members of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Spain">Spanish</a> faction of the <a href="/wiki/College_of_Cardinals" title="College of Cardinals">College of Cardinals</a> were so horrified by the conduct of Pope Urban VIII that they conspired to have him arrested and imprisoned (or killed) so that they could replace him with a new <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>; namely <a href="/wiki/Laudivio_Zacchia" title="Laudivio Zacchia">Laudivio Zacchia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bargrave_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bargrave-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> When Urban VIII travelled to <a href="/wiki/Papal_Palace_of_Castel_Gandolfo" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Palace of Castel Gandolfo">Castel Gandolfo</a> to rest, the members of the Spanish faction met in secret and discussed ways to advance their plan. But they were discovered and the pope raced back to Rome where he immediately held a <a href="/wiki/Papal_consistory" title="Papal consistory">consistory</a> and demanded to know who the new pope was. To put an end to the conspiracy, the pope decreed that all Cardinal-Bishops should leave Rome and return to their own churches.<sup id="cite_ref-bargrave_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bargrave-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>With the Spanish plan having failed, by 1640 the debt had reached 35 million scudi, consuming more than 80% of annual papal income in interest repayments.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_legacy">Death and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Death and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Giovanni_Gonnelli,_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Giovanni_Gonnelli%2C_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg/170px-Giovanni_Gonnelli%2C_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="209" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Giovanni_Gonnelli%2C_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg/255px-Giovanni_Gonnelli%2C_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e9/Giovanni_Gonnelli%2C_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg/340px-Giovanni_Gonnelli%2C_Busto_di_Urbano_VIII.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1033" data-file-height="1267" /></a><figcaption>Bust by <a href="/wiki/Giovanni_Gonnelli" title="Giovanni Gonnelli">Giovanni Gonnelli</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios,_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios%2C_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg/220px-Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios%2C_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="347" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios%2C_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg/330px-Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios%2C_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios%2C_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg/440px-Urbanus_%E2%80%93_Constitutio_contra_astrologos_iudiciarios%2C_1631_%E2%80%93_BEIC_13346228.jpg 2x" data-file-width="755" data-file-height="1192" /></a><figcaption><i>Constitutio contra astrologos iudiciarios</i>, 1631</figcaption></figure>
<p>Urban VIII's death on 29 July 1644 is said to have been hastened by chagrin at the result of the <a href="/wiki/Wars_of_Castro" title="Wars of Castro">Wars of Castro</a>. Because of the costs incurred by the city of Rome to finance this war, Urban VIII became immensely unpopular with his subjects.
</p><p>On his death, the bust of Urban VIII that lay beside the <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_the_Conservators" class="mw-redirect" title="Palace of the Conservators">Palace of the Conservators</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Capitoline_Hill" title="Capitoline Hill">Capitoline Hill</a> was rapidly destroyed by an enraged crowd, and only a quick-thinking priest saved the sculpture of the late pope belonging to the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a> from a similar fate.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Following his death, international and domestic machinations resulted in the papal conclave not electing Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Giulio_Cesare_Sacchetti" title="Giulio Cesare Sacchetti">Giulio Cesare Sacchetti</a>, who was closely associated with some members of the Barberini family. Instead, it elected Cardinal Giovanni Battista Pamphili, who took the name of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_X" title="Pope Innocent X">Innocent X</a>, as his successor at the <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1644" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1644">papal conclave of 1644</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Portrayals_in_fiction">Portrayals in fiction</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Urban_VIII&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Portrayals in fiction">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Urban VIII is a recurring character in the <i><a href="/wiki/1632_series" title="1632 series">Ring of Fire</a></i> <a href="/wiki/Alternative_history" class="mw-redirect" title="Alternative history">alternative history</a> hypernovel by <a href="/wiki/Eric_Flint" title="Eric Flint">Eric Flint</a> et al. where he is favorably portrayed.  He is especially prominent in <i>1634: The Galileo Affair</i> (in which he makes the fictional Grantville priest, Larry Mazzare, a cardinal), and in <i>1635: The Cannon Law</i>, <i>1635: The Papal Stakes</i>, and <i>1636: The Vatican Sanction</i>.  He is somewhat less favorably presented in <i><a href="/wiki/Galileo%27s_Dream" title="Galileo&#39;s Dream">Galileo's Dream</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Kim_Stanley_Robinson" title="Kim Stanley Robinson">Kim Stanley Robinson</a>. He is a sinister character in the radio play 'In Praise of Evil' by David Pownall, first broadcast on BBC Radio in 2013. The play features an imaginary meeting between the Pope and the composer Monteverdi.
</p>
