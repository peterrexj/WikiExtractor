<p><b>Maria Skobtsova</b> (20 [8 <a href="/wiki/Old_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Calendar">Old Calendar</a>] December 1891 – 31 March 1945), known as <b>Mother Maria</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Мать Мария</span>), Saint Mary (or Mother Maria) of Paris, born <b>Elizaveta Yurievna Pilenko</b> (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Елизавета Юрьевна Пиленко</span></span>), <b>Kuzmina-Karavayeva</b> (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Кузьмина-Караваева</span></span>) by her first marriage, <b>Skobtsova</b> (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Скобцова</span></span>) by her second marriage, was a <a href="/wiki/Russian_nobility" title="Russian nobility">Russian noblewoman</a>, <a href="/wiki/Poet" title="Poet">poet</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a>, and member of the <a href="/wiki/French_Resistance" title="French Resistance">French Resistance</a> during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>. She has been <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Skobtsova&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_(Saint_Petersburg)_19Jun10.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_%28Saint_Petersburg%29_19Jun10.JPG/220px-Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_%28Saint_Petersburg%29_19Jun10.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="306" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_%28Saint_Petersburg%29_19Jun10.JPG/330px-Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_%28Saint_Petersburg%29_19Jun10.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_%28Saint_Petersburg%29_19Jun10.JPG/440px-Maria_Skobtsova_Commemorative_Plaque_%28Saint_Petersburg%29_19Jun10.JPG 2x" data-file-width="678" data-file-height="944" /></a><figcaption>Maria Skobtsova Commemorative Plaque in Saint Petersburg</figcaption></figure>
<p>Born to an aristocratic family in 1891 in <a href="/wiki/Riga" title="Riga">Riga</a>, <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Latvia" title="Latvia">Latvia</a>).  She was given the name Elizaveta Pilenko.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her father died when she was a teenager, and she embraced <a href="/wiki/Atheism" title="Atheism">atheism</a>. In 1906 her mother moved the family to <a href="/wiki/St._Petersburg" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Petersburg">St. Petersburg</a>, where she became involved in radical intellectual circles. In 1910, she married a <a href="/wiki/Bolsheviks" title="Bolsheviks">Bolshevik</a> by the name of <a href="/wiki/Dmitriy_Kuz%27min-Karavaev" class="mw-redirect" title="Dmitriy Kuz&#39;min-Karavaev">Dmitriy Kuz'min-Karavaev</a>. During this period of her life she was actively involved in literary circles and wrote much poetry. Her first book, <i>Scythian Shards</i> (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Скифские черепки</span></span>), was a collection of poetry from this period. By 1913, her marriage to Dimitriy had ended. He  subsequently converted to Catholicism and became a  Catholic priest.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Through a look at the humanity of <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> — "He also died. He sweated blood. They struck his face" — she began to be drawn back into Christianity. She moved—now with her daughter, Gaiana — to the south of Russia where her religious devotion increased. Furious at <a href="/wiki/Leon_Trotsky" title="Leon Trotsky">Leon Trotsky</a> for closing the <a href="/wiki/Socialist-Revolutionary_Party" class="mw-redirect" title="Socialist-Revolutionary Party">Socialist-Revolutionary Party</a> Congress, she planned his <a href="/wiki/Assassination" title="Assassination">assassination</a>, but was dissuaded by colleagues, who sent her to <a href="/wiki/Anapa" title="Anapa">Anapa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
In 1918, after the <a href="/wiki/Bolshevik_Revolution" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolshevik Revolution">Bolshevik Revolution</a>, she was elected deputy mayor of Anapa in Southern Russia. When the <a href="/wiki/Anti-communist" class="mw-redirect" title="Anti-communist">anti-communist</a> <a href="/wiki/White_Army" title="White Army">White Army</a> took control of Anapa, the mayor fled and she became mayor of the town. The White Army put her on trial for being a Bolshevik. However, the judge was a former teacher of hers, Daniil Skobtsov, and she was acquitted. Soon the two fell in love and were married.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon, the political tide was turning again. In order to avoid danger, Elizaveta, Daniil, Gaiana, and Elizaveta's mother Sophia fled the country. Elizaveta was pregnant with her second child. They traveled first to <a href="/wiki/Georgia_(country)" title="Georgia (country)">Georgia</a> (where her son Yuri was born) and then to <a href="/wiki/Yugoslavia" title="Yugoslavia">Yugoslavia</a> (where her daughter Anastasia was born). Finally they arrived in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> in 1923. Soon Elizaveta was dedicating herself to theological studies and social work. In 1926, Anastasia died of influenza. Gaiana was sent away to <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a> to boarding school. Soon, Daniil and Elizaveta's marriage was falling apart. Yuri ended up living with Daniil, and Elizaveta moved into central Paris to work more directly with those who were most in need.
</p><p>Her bishop encouraged her to take vows as a nun, something she did only with the assurance that she would not have to live in a monastery, secluded from the world. In 1932, with Daniil Skobtov's permission, an ecclesiastical divorce was granted, and she took monastic vows. She took the religious name "Maria". Her confessor was Father <a href="/wiki/Sergei_Bulgakov" title="Sergei Bulgakov">Sergei Bulgakov</a>. Later, Fr. Dmitri Klepinin would be sent to be the chaplain of the house.
</p><p>Mother Maria made a rented house in Paris her "convent". It was a place with an open door for refugees, the needy and the lonely. It also soon became a center for intellectual and theological discussion. In Mother Maria these two elements  —  service to the poor and theology  —  went hand-in-hand.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Skobtsova&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the <a href="/wiki/Fall_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Fall of France">Fall of France</a> in 1940, Jews began approaching the house asking for <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptismal</a> certificates, which Father Dimitri would provide them. Many Jews came to stay with them. They provided shelter and helped many to flee the country. Eventually the house was closed down. Mother Maria, Fr. Dimitri, Yuri and Sophia were all arrested by the <a href="/wiki/Gestapo" title="Gestapo">Gestapo</a>. Fr. Dimitri and Yuri both died at the <a href="/wiki/Dora_concentration_camp" class="mw-redirect" title="Dora concentration camp">Dora concentration camp</a>.
</p><p>Mother Maria was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Ravensbr%C3%BCck_concentration_camp" title="Ravensbrück concentration camp">Ravensbrück concentration camp</a>. On <a href="/wiki/Holy_Saturday" title="Holy Saturday">Holy Saturday</a>, 1945, she was sent to the <a href="/wiki/Gas_chamber" title="Gas chamber">gas chamber</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Skobtsova&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Mother Maria was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">glorified</a> (canonized a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>) by act of the <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">Holy Synod</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate">Ecumenical Patriarchate</a> on 16 January 2004. The glorification of Mother Maria, together with Fr. Dimitri, Yuri, and <a href="/wiki/Ilya_Fondaminsky" title="Ilya Fondaminsky">Ilya Fondaminsky</a> took place at the Cathedral of Saint Alexander Nevsky in Paris on 1 and 2 May 2004. Their feast day is 20 July.
<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Skobtsova&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On June 24, 2020, a memorial plaque to Skobtsova was unveiled at the famous Sainte Genevieve de Bois cemetery, on the territory of the Russian necropolis.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Skobtsova is also commemorated in the <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_Church_(United_States)" title="Episcopal Church (United States)">American Episcopal Church</a>'s calendar, with a lesser feast on <a href="/wiki/July_21" title="July 21">July 21</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Mother Maria was designated as Righteous among the Nations at <a href="/wiki/Yad_Vashem" title="Yad Vashem">Yad Vashem</a> in Israel.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Example_of_poetry">Example of poetry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maria_Skobtsova&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Example of poetry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In July, 1942, when the order requiring Jews to wear the <a href="/wiki/Yellow_badge" title="Yellow badge">yellow star</a> was published, she wrote a poem entitled "Israel":
</p>
<dl><dd>Two triangles, a star,</dd>
<dd>The shield of King David, our forefather.</dd>
<dd>This is election, not offense.</dd>
<dd>The great path and not an evil.</dd>
<dd>Once more in a term fulfilled,</dd>
<dd>Once more roars the trumpet of the end;</dd>
<dd>And the fate of a great people</dd>
<dd>Once more is by the prophet proclaimed.</dd>
<dd>Thou art persecuted again, O Israel,</dd>
<dd>But what can human malice mean to thee,</dd>
