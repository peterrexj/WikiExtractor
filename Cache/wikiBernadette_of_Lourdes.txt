<p><b>Bernadette Soubirous</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˌ/: secondary stress follows">ˌ</span><span title="&#39;b&#39; in &#39;buy&#39;">b</span><span title="/ɜːr/: &#39;ur&#39; in &#39;fur&#39;">ɜːr</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;d&#39; in &#39;dye&#39;">d</span><span title="/ɛ/: &#39;e&#39; in &#39;dress&#39;">ɛ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span></span><span class="wrap"> </span><span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˌ/: secondary stress follows">ˌ</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span><span title="/uː/: &#39;oo&#39; in &#39;goose&#39;">uː</span><span title="&#39;b&#39; in &#39;buy&#39;">b</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;r&#39; in &#39;rye&#39;">r</span><span title="/uː/: &#39;oo&#39; in &#39;goose&#39;">uː</span></span>/</a></span></span>; <small>French:&#160;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[bɛʁnadɛt subiʁu]</a></span>; <a href="/wiki/Occitan_language" title="Occitan language">Occitan</a>: <i lang="oc">Bernadeta Sobirós</i> <small></small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="oc-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/Occitan" title="Help:IPA/Occitan">[beɾnaˈðetɔ suβiˈɾus]</a></span>; 7 January 1844&#160;&#8211;&#32;16 April 1879), also known as <b>Bernadette of Lourdes</b>, was the firstborn daughter of a miller from <a href="/wiki/Lourdes" title="Lourdes">Lourdes</a> (<span title="Occitan (post 1500)-language text"><i lang="oc">Lorda</i></span> in Occitan), in the <a href="/wiki/Departments_of_France" title="Departments of France">department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Hautes-Pyr%C3%A9n%C3%A9es" title="Hautes-Pyrénées">Hautes-Pyrénées</a> in France, and is best known for experiencing apparitions of a "young lady" who asked for a chapel to be built at the nearby cave-<a href="/wiki/Grotto" title="Grotto">grotto</a>. These apparitions occurred between 11 February and 16 July 1858, and the woman who appeared to her identified herself as the "<a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>".
</p><p>After a canonical investigation, Soubirous's reports were eventually declared "worthy of belief" on 18 February 1862, and the <a href="/wiki/Marian_apparition" title="Marian apparition">Marian apparition</a> became known as <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Our Lady of Lourdes">Our Lady of Lourdes</a>. In 1866, Soubirous joined the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Nevers" title="Sisters of Charity of Nevers">Sisters of Charity of Nevers</a> at their convent in <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a> where she spent the last years of her life. Her body has remained internally <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility#Causes" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The grotto where the apparitions occurred later went on to become a major <a href="/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage" title="Christian pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> site and <a href="/wiki/Marian_shrine" class="mw-redirect" title="Marian shrine">Marian shrine</a> known as the <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes">Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes</a>, attracting around five million pilgrims of all denominations each year.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> beatified Bernadette Soubirous on 14 June 1925 and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> her on 8 December 1933. Her feast day, initially specified as 18 February – the day Mary promised to make her happy, not in this life, but in the other – is now observed in most places on the date of her death, 16 April.<sup id="cite_ref-feast_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-feast-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Marie Bernarde Soubirous was the daughter of François Soubirous (1807–1871), a miller, and Louise (née Casteròt; 1825–1866), a laundress.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> She was the eldest of nine children—Bernadette, Jean (born and died 1845), Toinette (1846–1892), Jean-Marie (1848–1851), Jean-Marie (1851–1919), Justin (1855–1865), Pierre (1859–1931), Jean (born and died 1864), and a baby named Louise who died soon after her birth (1866).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Soubirous was born on 7 January 1844<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and baptized at the local parish church, St. Pierre's, on 9 January, her parents' wedding anniversary. Her godmother was Bernarde Casterot, her mother's sister, a moderately wealthy widow who owned a tavern. Hard times had fallen on France and the family lived in extreme poverty. Soubirous was a very sick child and possibly due to this only measured 1.4 m (4&#160;ft. 7in.) tall. She contracted <a href="/wiki/Cholera" title="Cholera">cholera</a> as a toddler and suffered severe <a href="/wiki/Asthma" title="Asthma">asthma</a> for the rest of her life. Soubirous attended the day school conducted by the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Nevers" title="Sisters of Charity of Nevers">Sisters of Charity and Christian Instruction</a> from <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Contrary to a belief popularized by Hollywood films, Soubirous learned very little French, only studying French in school after age 13. At that time she could read and write very little due to her frequent illness. She spoke the language of <a href="/wiki/Occitan_language" title="Occitan language">Occitan</a>, which was spoken by the local population of the <a href="/wiki/Pyrenees" title="Pyrenees">Pyrenees</a> region at that time and to a residual degree today.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Visions">Visions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Visions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Lourdes_apparitions" title="Lourdes apparitions">Lourdes apparitions</a></div>
<p>By the time of the events at the grotto, the Soubirous family's financial and social status had declined to the point where they lived in a one-room basement, formerly used as a jail, called <i>le cachot</i>, "the dungeon", where they were housed for free by her mother's cousin, André Sajoux.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 11 February 1858, Soubirous, then aged 14, was out gathering firewood with her sister Toinette and a friend near the <a href="/wiki/Grotto" title="Grotto">grotto</a> of Massabielle (Tuta de Massavielha) when she experienced her first vision. While the other girls crossed the little stream in front of the grotto and walked on, Soubirous stayed behind, looking for a place to cross where she wouldn't get her stockings wet. She finally sat down to take her shoes off in order to cross the water and was lowering her stocking when she heard the sound of rushing wind, but nothing moved. A wild rose in a natural niche in the grotto, however, did move. From the niche, or rather the dark alcove behind it, "came a dazzling light, and a white figure". This was the first of 18 visions of what she referred to as <i>aquerò</i> (pronounced <span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="und-Latn-fonipa">[ake'ɾɔ]</span>), <a href="/wiki/Gascon_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Gascon language">Gascon</a> <a href="/wiki/Occitan_language" title="Occitan language">Occitan</a> for "that". In later testimony, she called it "a small young lady" (<i>uo petito damizelo</i>). Her sister and her friend stated that they had seen nothing.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 14 February, after Sunday Mass, Soubirous, with her sister Marie and some other girls, returned to the grotto. Soubirous knelt down immediately, saying she saw the apparition again. When one of the girls threw <a href="/wiki/Holy_water" title="Holy water">holy water</a> at the niche and another threw a rock from above that shattered on the ground, the apparition disappeared.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> On her next visit, 18 February, Soubirous said that "the vision" asked her to return to the grotto every day for a <a href="/wiki/Fortnight" title="Fortnight">fortnight</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This period of almost daily visions came to be known as <i>la Quinzaine sacrée</i>, "holy fortnight." Initially, Soubirous's parents, especially her mother, were embarrassed and tried to forbid her to go. The supposed apparition did not identify herself until the seventeenth vision. Although the townspeople who believed she was telling the truth assumed she saw the Virgin Mary, Soubirous never claimed it to be Mary, consistently using the word <i>aquerò</i>. She described the lady as wearing a white veil, a blue girdle and with a yellow rose on each foot&#160;– compatible with "a description of any statue of the Virgin in a village church".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soubirous's story caused a sensation among the townspeople, who were divided in their opinions on whether or not she was telling the truth. Some believed her to have a mental illness and demanded she be put in an asylum.<sup id="cite_ref-bio_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The other contents of Soubirous's reported visions were simple and focused on the need for prayer and penance. On 25 February she explained that the vision had told her "to drink of the water of the spring, to wash in it and to eat the herb that grew there," as an act of penance. To everyone's surprise, the next day the grotto was no longer muddy but clear water flowed.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> On 2 March, at the thirteenth apparition, Soubirous told her family that the lady said that "a chapel should be built and a procession formed".<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The sixteenth vision, which Soubirous stated went on for over an hour, was on 25 March. According to her account, during that visitation, she again asked the woman for her name but the lady just smiled back. She repeated the question three more times and finally heard the lady say, in <a href="/wiki/Gascon_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Gascon language">Gascon</a> <a href="/wiki/Occitan_language" title="Occitan language">Occitan</a>, "I am the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>" (<i>Que soy era immaculada councepciou</i> in Occitan).<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Despite being rigorously interviewed by officials of both the Catholic Church and the French government, she stuck consistently to her story.<sup id="cite_ref-crawley_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-crawley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 7 April, Soubirous had another vision, during which her hand was apparently not burnt while being in contact with the flame of a candle for several minutes. On 8 June 1858, the mayor of Lourdes decided to barricade the grotto and put guards to prevent public access. On 16 July, Soubirous came back to see the grotto from the other side of the river and experienced her eighteenth and last apparition of the lady.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Results_of_her_visions">Results of her visions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Results of her visions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After investigation, Catholic Church authorities accepted the authenticity of the apparitions in 1862.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In the 160 years since Soubirous dug up the spring, 70<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> cures have been verified by the <a href="/wiki/Lourdes_Medical_Bureau" title="Lourdes Medical Bureau">Lourdes Medical Bureau</a> as "inexplicable" – after what the Catholic Church claims are "extremely rigorous scientific and medical examinations" that failed to find any other explanation. The Lourdes Commission that examined Bernadette after the visions ran an intensive analysis on the water and found that, while it had a high mineral content, it contained nothing out of the ordinary that would account for the cures attributed to it. Bernadette said that it was faith and prayer that cured the sick: "One must have faith and pray; the water will have no virtue without faith".<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soubirous's request to the local priest to build a chapel at the site of her visions eventually gave rise to a number of chapels and churches at Lourdes. The <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes">Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes</a> is now one of the major Catholic pilgrimage sites in the world. One of the churches built at the site, the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Pius_X" title="Basilica of St. Pius X">Basilica of St. Pius X</a>, can accommodate 25,000 people and was dedicated by the future <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> when he was the <a href="/wiki/Papal_Nuncio" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal Nuncio">Papal Nuncio</a> to France. In 2012, 715,000 and in 2012, 570,000 pilgrims attended pilgrimage events. 
<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_years">Later years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Later years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bernadette_soubirous_2_publicdomain.gif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Bernadette_soubirous_2_publicdomain.gif/150px-Bernadette_soubirous_2_publicdomain.gif" decoding="async" width="150" height="242" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Bernadette_soubirous_2_publicdomain.gif/225px-Bernadette_soubirous_2_publicdomain.gif 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/41/Bernadette_soubirous_2_publicdomain.gif 2x" data-file-width="289" data-file-height="466" /></a><figcaption>Bernadette in 1866, after having taken the religious habit and joining the Sisters of Charity</figcaption></figure>
<p>Disliking the attention she was attracting, Bernadette went to the <a href="/wiki/Hospice" title="Hospice">hospice</a> school run by the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Nevers" title="Sisters of Charity of Nevers">Sisters of Charity of Nevers</a> where she had learned to read and write. Although she considered joining the Carmelites, her health precluded her entering any of the strict contemplative orders. On 29 July 1866, with 42 other candidates, she took the religious habit of a <a href="/wiki/Postulant" title="Postulant">postulant</a> and joined the Sisters of Charity at their motherhouse at <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a>. Her Mistress of Novices was Sister <a href="/wiki/Marie_Therese_Vauzou" title="Marie Therese Vauzou">Marie Therese Vauzou</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-snddl_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-snddl-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> The Mother Superior at the time gave her the name Marie-Bernarde<sup id="cite_ref-bio_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> in honor of her godmother who was named "Bernarde". As Patricia A. McEachern observes, "Bernadette was devoted to Saint Bernard, her patron saint; she copied long texts related to him in notebooks and on bits of paper. The experience of becoming 'Sister Marie-Bernard' marked a turning point for Bernadette as she realized more than ever that the great grace she received from the Queen of Heaven brought with it great responsibilities."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_20-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soubirous spent the rest of her brief life at the motherhouse, working as an assistant in the infirmary<sup id="cite_ref-snddl_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-snddl-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> and later as a <a href="/wiki/Sacristy" title="Sacristy">sacristan</a>, creating ornate <a href="/wiki/Embroidery" title="Embroidery">embroidery</a> for altar cloths and <a href="/wiki/Vestments" class="mw-redirect" title="Vestments">vestments</a>. Her contemporaries admired her humility and spirit of sacrifice. One day, asked about the apparitions, she replied:<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"The Virgin used me as a broom to remove the dust. When the work is done, the broom is put behind the door again."</p></blockquote>
<p>Soubirous had followed the development of Lourdes as a pilgrimage <a href="/wiki/Shrine" title="Shrine">shrine</a> while she still lived at Lourdes but was not present for the consecration of the <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes">Basilica of the Immaculate Conception</a> there in 1876.
</p><p>Unfortunately, Soubirous's childhood bout of "cholera left ... [Bernadette] with severe, chronic asthma, and eventually she contracted <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a> of the lungs and bones."<sup id="cite_ref-:0_20-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> For several months prior to her death, she was unable to take an active part in convent life. She eventually died of her long-term illness at the age of 35 on 16 April 1879 (Easter Wednesday),<sup id="cite_ref-snddl_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-snddl-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> while praying the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">Holy Rosary</a>. On her deathbed, as she suffered from severe pain and in keeping with the Virgin Mary's admonition of "Penance, Penance, Penance," Bernadette proclaimed that "all this is good for Heaven!" Her final words were, "Blessed Mary, Mother of God, Pray for me". Soubirous' body was laid to rest in the <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gildard_Convent" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gildard Convent">Saint Gildard Convent</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Soubirous was declared blessed on 14 June 1921<sup id="cite_ref-bio_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bio-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> by Pope Pius XI. She was canonized by Pius XI on 8 December 1933, the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Conception" title="Immaculate Conception">Immaculate Conception</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruggles_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruggles-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She is celebrated in the liturgical calendar of the Catholic Church on 16 April.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> In France, her liturgical celebration is an <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">optional memorial</a> and is held on February 18,<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> which commemorates Bernadette's third vision during which the "lady" told her that she does not promise to make her happy in this world, but in the other.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Exhumations">Exhumations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Exhumations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg/250px-Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="160" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg/375px-Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg/500px-Bernadette_soubirous_exhumated_1925.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1264" data-file-height="809" /></a><figcaption>Full-body relic of Bernadette Soubirous. The photograph was taken at the last exhumation (18 April 1925). The saint died 46 years before the photo was taken; face and hands are covered with a wax coat.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Bishop Gauthey of Nevers and the Catholic Church <a href="/wiki/Exhumation" class="mw-redirect" title="Exhumation">exhumed</a> the body of Soubirous on 22 September 1909, in the presence of representatives appointed by the postulators of the cause, two doctors and a sister of the community. They claimed that although the <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">crucifix</a> in her hand and her <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> had both <a href="/wiki/Redox" title="Redox">oxidized</a>, her body appeared <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a> – preserved from <a href="/wiki/Decomposition" title="Decomposition">decomposition</a>. This was cited as one of the miracles to support her canonization. They washed and reclothed her body before burial in a new double casket.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>The church exhumed the corpse a second time on 3 April 1919, on the occasion of the approval of Bernadette's canonization. Dr. Comte, who examined the body noted, "The body is practically mummified, covered with patches of mildew and quite a notable layer of salts, which appear to be calcium salts. … The skin has disappeared in some places, but it is still present on most parts of the body."<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1925, the church exhumed the body for a third time. They took <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a>, which were sent to Rome. A precise imprint of the face was molded to make a wax mask based on the imprints and on some genuine photos to be placed on her body. This was common practice for relics in France as it was feared that the blackish tinge to the face and the sunken eyes and nose would be viewed as corruption by the public. Imprints of the hands were also taken for the presentation of the body and the making of wax casts. The remains were then placed in a gold and <a href="/wiki/Crystal" title="Crystal">crystal</a> reliquary in the Chapel of Saint Bernadette at the motherhouse in <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>
Three years later in 1928, Doctor Comte published a report on the exhumation of Soubirous in the second issue of the <i>Bulletin de I'Association medicale de Notre-Dame de Lourdes</i>.</p><blockquote><p>"I would have liked to open the left side of the <a href="/wiki/Thorax" title="Thorax">thorax</a> to take the ribs as relics and then remove the heart which I am certain must have survived. However, as the trunk was slightly supported on the left arm, it would have been rather difficult to try and get at the heart without doing too much noticeable damage. As the Mother Superior had expressed a desire for the Saint's heart to be kept together with the whole body, and as Monsignor the Bishop did not insist, I gave up the idea of opening the left-hand side of the thorax and contented myself with removing the two right ribs which were more accessible. ... What struck me during this examination, of course, was the state of perfect preservation of the skeleton, the fibrous tissues of the muscles (still supple and firm), of the ligaments, and of the skin, and above all the totally unexpected state of the liver after 46 years. One would have thought that this organ, which is basically soft and inclined to crumble, would have decomposed very rapidly or would have hardened to a chalky consistency. Yet, when it was cut it was soft and almost normal in consistency. I pointed this out to those present, remarking that this did not seem to be a natural phenomenon."<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After the third exhumation of Bernadette Soubirous's body in 1925, it was exposed permanently in a glass coffin in the chapel of the <a href="/wiki/Espace_Bernadette_Soubirous_Nevers" title="Espace Bernadette Soubirous Nevers">convent of Nevers</a>, where she spent the last years of her life after joining the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Charity_of_Nevers" title="Sisters of Charity of Nevers">Sisters of Charity of Nevers</a> in 1866.
</p><p>In the spring of 2015, the town of Lourdes lobbied for Soubirous's remains to be returned to Lourdes, a move opposed by the city of Nevers.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes">Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes</a> has a few bodily relics which traveled in several European dioceses from 2017 to be exposed for the veneration of people.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> In 2019, a new reliquary was built for the travel of the relics.<sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup> In 2022, the reliquary visited the United States and the United Kingdom. The relics first traveled to the United States from April to August 2022, visiting around thirty dioceses.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> In September and October 2022, the relics where exposed in around fifty places in England, Scotland, and Wales, including in the <a href="/wiki/Westminster_Cathedral" title="Westminster Cathedral">Westminster Cathedral</a> and at the <a href="/wiki/Carfin_Grotto" title="Carfin Grotto">Carfin Lourdes Grotto</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Places">Places</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernadette_Soubirous&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Places">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The town of <a href="/wiki/Lourdes" title="Lourdes">Lourdes</a> where Bernadette grew up and had her visions has become a major international pilgrimage site attracting millions of visitors each year. Several churches and infrastructures were built around the cave where the apparitions occurred, forming together the <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes">Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes</a>. The sanctuary is reputed for the <a href="/wiki/Lourdes_water" title="Lourdes water">Lourdes water</a> streaming inside the cave from a spring discovered by Bernadette during the apparitions, which is said to have healing properties, attracting many sick pilgrims. The most recent church in the sanctuary is the St. Bernadette Church, completed in 1988.<sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup> The sanctuary also has a museum called the St. Bernadette Museum, dedicated to the history of the apparitions.<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> In the city center of Lourdes, pilgrims can visit the house where Bernadette was born and the room where her family was staying at the time of the apparitions.<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Hundreds of <a href="/wiki/Lourdes_grotto" title="Lourdes grotto">Lourdes grottos</a> were built all over the world after the apparitions to allow believers to venerate <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Lourdes" title="Our Lady of Lourdes">Our Lady of Lourdes</a> in their own countries, specially those who could not afford to make the expensive and long trip to Lourdes. These replicas of the original cave where the apparitions occurred recreate the scenes of the apparitions, usually with a statue of Our Lady of Lourdes in a niche and a statue of Bernadette on her knees and praying or holding a candle.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Espace_Bernadette_Soubirous_Nevers" title="Espace Bernadette Soubirous Nevers">Espace Bernadette Soubirous Nevers</a>, the convent in the French town of <a href="/wiki/Nevers" title="Nevers">Nevers</a> where Bernadette Soubirous spent the rest of her life some years after the apparitions, is dedicated to her memory. This is where her body has been kept after her death. Her body is now exposed for the veneration of visitors. The city of Nevers also has a museum dedicated to the life of Bernadette, called the "Bernadette Museum".<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Several churches, shrines and catholic schools are named after St. Bernadette all over the world.
</p>
