<p>The <b>Princes' Islands</b> (<a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>: <i lang="tr">Prens Adaları</i>; the word "princes" is plural, because the name means "Islands of the Princes", <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πριγκηπονήσια</span>, <i>Pringiponisia</i>), officially just <b>Adalar</b> (English: <span lang="en">Islands</span>); alternatively the <b>Princes' Archipelago</b>; is an <a href="/wiki/Archipelago" title="Archipelago">archipelago</a> off the coast of <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>, <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Sea_of_Marmara" title="Sea of Marmara">Sea of Marmara</a>.
</p><p>The islands constitute the municipality and <a href="/wiki/Districts_of_Turkey" title="Districts of Turkey">district</a> of Adalar within <a href="/wiki/Istanbul_Province" class="mw-redirect" title="Istanbul Province">Istanbul Province</a>. With a total land area of 11&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (4.2&#160;sq&#160;mi), it is the fifth smallest <a href="/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Istanbul" title="List of districts of Istanbul">district in Istanbul</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and with a permanent population of 16,690 (2022), it is by far the least populous district in Istanbul.<sup id="cite_ref-tuik_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tuik-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>They consist of four larger islands, <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a> ("Big Island") with an area of 5.46&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (2.11&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/Heybeliada" title="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</a> ("Saddlebag Island") with an area of 2.4&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.93&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a> ("Fortress Island") with an area of 1.5&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.58&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/K%C4%B1nal%C4%B1ada" title="Kınalıada">Kınalıada</a> ("Henna Island") with an area of 1.3&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.50&#160;sq&#160;mi), and five much smaller ones, <a href="/wiki/Sedef_Adas%C4%B1" class="mw-redirect" title="Sedef Adası">Sedef Adası</a> ("Mother-of-Pearl Island") with an area of 0.157&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.061&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/Yass%C4%B1ada" title="Yassıada">Yassıada</a> ("Flat Island") with an area of 0.05&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.019&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/Sivriada" title="Sivriada">Sivriada</a> ("Sharp Island") with an area of 0.4&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.15&#160;sq&#160;mi), <a href="/wiki/Ka%C5%9F%C4%B1k_Adas%C4%B1" class="mw-redirect" title="Kaşık Adası">Kaşık Adası</a> ("Spoon Island") with an area of 0.6&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.23&#160;sq&#160;mi), and <a href="/wiki/Tav%C5%9Fan_Adas%C4%B1" title="Tavşan Adası">Tavşan Adası</a> ("Rabbit Island") with an area of 0.4&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.15&#160;sq&#160;mi).
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Tourism_and_transport">Tourism and transport</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Tourism and transport">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg/240px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="135" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg/360px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg/480px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Splendid_Palace.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4032" data-file-height="2268" /></a><figcaption>The historic Splendid Palace Hotel (1908) in <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>During the summer months, the Princes' Islands are popular destinations for day trips from Istanbul. As there is almost no motor traffic on the Islands, the only transport being bicycles and horse and cart, they are more peaceful than the city of Istanbul. They are just a short ferry ride from Istanbul, with ferries departing from <a href="/wiki/Bostanc%C4%B1" title="Bostancı">Bostancı</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kad%C4%B1k%C3%B6y" title="Kadıköy">Kadıköy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kartal" title="Kartal">Kartal</a> and <a href="/wiki/Maltepe,_Istanbul" title="Maltepe, Istanbul">Maltepe</a> on the Asian side, and from <a href="/wiki/Be%C5%9Fikta%C5%9F" title="Beşiktaş">Beşiktaş</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kabata%C5%9F,_Istanbul" class="mw-redirect" title="Kabataş, Istanbul">Kabataş</a> on the European side.  Most ferries call in turn at the four largest of the nine islands: <a href="/wiki/K%C4%B1nal%C4%B1ada" title="Kınalıada">Kınalıada</a>, <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a>, <a href="/wiki/Heybeliada" title="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</a> and, finally, <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a>. Ferry and ship services are provided by six different companies.
In spring and autumn, the islands are quieter and more pleasant, although the sea can be rough in spring, autumn and winter, and the islands are sometimes cut off from the outside world when the ferry services are cancelled due to storms and high waves. During winter, with the addition of the biting cold and the strong winds and the resulting ferry cancellations, the islands become almost deserted. As for cultural tourism, Büyükada happens to have the first and only city museum in İstanbul, the Museum of the Princes' Islands in Aya Nikola Bay.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><span class="anchor" id="Demonesi"></span><span class="anchor" id="Demonisi"></span>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_(Old_Greek_Orphanage),_Istanbul_2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_%28Old_Greek_Orphanage%29%2C_Istanbul_2.jpg/220px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_%28Old_Greek_Orphanage%29%2C_Istanbul_2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="157" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_%28Old_Greek_Orphanage%29%2C_Istanbul_2.jpg/330px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_%28Old_Greek_Orphanage%29%2C_Istanbul_2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/33/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_%28Old_Greek_Orphanage%29%2C_Istanbul_2.jpg/440px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_Rum_Yetimhanesi_%28Old_Greek_Orphanage%29%2C_Istanbul_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5183" data-file-height="3698" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Prinkipo_Greek_Orthodox_Orphanage" title="Prinkipo Greek Orthodox Orphanage">Prinkipo Greek Orthodox Orphanage</a> in <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a>, the largest wooden building in <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a> and second largest in the world.</figcaption></figure>

<p>There are several references to the islands in the ancient Greek period, when they went by the name <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Δημόνησοι</span></span> (<span style="padding-right:0.15em;"><i>Dēmónēsoi</i></span>), often transliterated as <i>Demonesi</i> or <i>Demonisi</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the period of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a>, out-of-favor princes and other royalty were <a href="/wiki/Exile" title="Exile">exiled</a> on the islands. After 1453, members of the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Dynasty" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottoman Dynasty"> Ottoman</a> sultans' family were exiled there too, whence the islands' present name. The Ottoman fleet captured the islands during the siege of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a> in 1453. During the nineteenth century, the islands became a popular resort for Istanbul's wealthy, and <a href="/wiki/Victorian_era" title="Victorian era"> Victorian-era</a> cottages and houses are still preserved on the largest of the Princes' Islands.
According to the <a href="/wiki/Census_in_the_Ottoman_Empire" title="Census in the Ottoman Empire"> Ottoman General Census</a> of 1881/82-1893, the <a href="/wiki/Kaza" title="Kaza">kaza</a> of the Princes' Islands (Adalar) had a total population of 7.937, consisting of 5.501 <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Greeks" title="Ottoman Greeks"> Greeks</a>, 533 <a href="/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire" title="Armenians in the Ottoman Empire"> Armenians</a>, 254 Muslims, 133 <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church"> Catholics</a>, 65 <a href="/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_the_Ottoman_Empire" title="History of the Jews in the Ottoman Empire">Jews</a>, 27 <a href="/wiki/Latins" title="Latins">Latins</a>, 7 <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestants</a>, 3 <a href="/wiki/Bulgarians" title="Bulgarians">Bulgarians</a> and 1.404 foreign citizens.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
The <a href="/wiki/Halki_seminary" title="Halki seminary">Halki seminary</a>, formally the Theological School of Halki (Greek: Θεολογική Σχολή Χάλκης and Turkish: Ortodoks Ruhban Okulu), was founded on 1 October 1844 on the island of Halki (Turkish: Heybeliada), the second-largest of the Princes' Islands in the Sea of Marmara. It was the main school of theology of the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople">Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople</a> until the Turkish parliament enacted a law banning private higher-education institutions in 1971.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The theological school is located at the top of the island's Hill of Hope, on the site of the Byzantine-era Monastery of the Holy Trinity. The premises of the school continue to be maintained by the monastery and are used to host conferences. It is possible to visit the island where it is located via boat in approximately one hour from the shore of Istanbul.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
In 1912 the islands had a population of 10,250 <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greeks</a> and 670 <a href="/wiki/Turkish_people" title="Turkish people">Turks</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The islands have become more and more ethnically Turkish in character due to the influx of wealthy Turkish <a href="/wiki/Jet_setter" class="mw-redirect" title="Jet setter"> jetsetters</a>, a process which began in the 1920s in the first days of the Turkish Republic when the British Yacht Club on Büyükada was appropriated as Anadolu Kulübü for Turkish parliamentarians to enjoy Istanbul in the summer. The islands are an interesting anomaly because they allow for a very rare, albeit incomplete, insight into a multicultural society in modern Turkey, possibly<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research" title="Wikipedia:No original research"><span title="The material near this tag possibly contains original research. (June 2022)">original research?</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> akin to the multicultural society that once existed during the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a> in places such as nearby Istanbul/Constantinople. Prior to the 1950s, each of the inhabited islands had significant communities of ethnic minorities of Turkey, which is now the case to a much smaller extent. Since the vast majority of the residents and visitors are Turkish, today the minority legacy is of cultural rather than demographic importance.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Notable_residents">Notable residents</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Notable residents">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Many Turks fondly remember the Islands as the home of the famous short-story writer <a href="/wiki/Sait_Faik_Abas%C4%B1yan%C4%B1k" title="Sait Faik Abasıyanık">Sait Faik Abasıyanık</a> (1906-1954) and of the football legend <a href="/wiki/Lefter_K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCkandonyadis" title="Lefter Küçükandonyadis">Lefter Küçükandonyadis</a> (1925-2012).
</p><p>After the deportation of <a href="/wiki/Leon_Trotsky" title="Leon Trotsky">Leon Trotsky</a> from the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet Union</a> in February 1929, his first residence in exile was a house in Büyükada, the largest of the Princes' Islands; he lived there for four years between 1929 and 1933.
</p><p>The famous poet <a href="/wiki/N%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_Hikmet" title="Nâzım Hikmet">Nâzım Hikmet</a> attended the <a href="/wiki/Turkish_Naval_High_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkish Naval High School">Naval Cadet School</a> in Heybeliada between 1913 and 1918.
</p><p>Famous Armenian writers and poets have lived on the islands, including <a href="/wiki/Zahrad" title="Zahrad">Zahrad</a> (1924-2007) and <a href="/wiki/Zabel_Sibil_Asadour" title="Zabel Sibil Asadour">Zabel Sibil Asadour</a> (1863-1934), both of whom lived in Kınalıada.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to <i><a href="/wiki/Konstantinoupolis_(newspaper)" title="Konstantinoupolis (newspaper)">Kōnstantinoupolis</a></i> employee Manuel Gedeon,<sup id="cite_ref-BaltaKavakp37_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BaltaKavakp37-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Greek" class="mw-redirect" title="Ottoman Greek">Ottoman Greek</a> publisher <a href="/wiki/Demetrius_Nicolaides" title="Demetrius Nicolaides">Demetrius Nicolaides</a> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1843</span>-1915) had a house in Antigone (Burgazada).<sup id="cite_ref-BaltaKavakp39_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BaltaKavakp39-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Islands">Islands</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Islands">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="B.C3.BCy.C3.BCkada"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Büyükada">Büyükada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Büyükada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_(2).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_%282%29.jpg/220px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_%282%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_%282%29.jpg/330px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_%282%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_%282%29.jpg/440px-B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada_K%C3%B6%C5%9Fk_%282%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4032" data-file-height="2268" /></a><figcaption>One of numerous <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman</a> era mansions which line the streets of <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalar_5583.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Adalar_5583.jpg/170px-Adalar_5583.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Adalar_5583.jpg/255px-Adalar_5583.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Adalar_5583.jpg/340px-Adalar_5583.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1932" data-file-height="2576" /></a><figcaption>One of numerous Ottoman era mansions in Büyükada</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a> (meaning "Big Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πρίγκηπος</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Prínkēpos</i></span>) is the largest of the nine islands comprising the Princes' Islands in the <a href="/wiki/Marmara_Sea" class="mw-redirect" title="Marmara Sea">Marmara Sea</a>, close to <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>.
As on the other islands, motorized vehicles – except service vehicles – are forbidden, so visitors explore the island by foot; by riding a bicycle (numerous bicycle shops rent them with hourly prices); or in battery powered electric vehicles which function like taxi cabs, also offering "round-the-island" sightseeing tours. Until 2020 there were horse-drawn carriages on the island, but these were phased out due to a serious equine disease.
A convent on Büyükada was the place of exile for the Byzantine empresses <a href="/wiki/Irene_(empress)" class="mw-redirect" title="Irene (empress)">Irene</a>, <a href="/wiki/Euphrosyne_Angelina" class="mw-redirect" title="Euphrosyne Angelina">Euphrosyne</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theophano_(born_Anastaso)" title="Theophano (born Anastaso)">Theophano</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zoe_(empress)" class="mw-redirect" title="Zoe (empress)">Zoe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anna_Dalassena" class="mw-redirect" title="Anna Dalassena">Anna Dalassena</a>. After his deportation from the <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet Union</a> in February 1929, <a href="/wiki/Leon_Trotsky" title="Leon Trotsky">Leon Trotsky</a> also stayed for four years on Büyükada, his first station in exile. <a href="/wiki/Princess_Fahrelnissa_Zeid" title="Princess Fahrelnissa Zeid">Princess Fahrelnissa Zeid</a> was born on the island.
There are several historical buildings on Büyükada, such as the Ayia Yorgi Church and Monastery dating back to the sixth century, the Ayios Dimitrios Church, and the Hamidiye Mosque built by <a href="/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_II" title="Abdul Hamid II">Abdul Hamid II</a>. Büyükada consists of two peaks. The one nearest the <i>iskele</i> (ferry landing), Hristos, is topped by the former Greek Orphanage, a huge wooden building now known as the Prinkipo Environmental Center.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In the valley between the two hills sit the church and monastery of Ayios Nikolaos and a former fairground called Luna Park. Visitors can take the 'small tour' of the island by buggy, leading to this point, from where it is an easy climb to Ayia Yorgi, a tiny church with a café on the grounds serving wine, chips and sausage sandwiches, this being part of the "classic" Ayia Yorgi (St. George, in Greek: Άγιος Γεώργιος) experience.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Heybeliada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_(2).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_%282%29.jpg/220px-HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_%282%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_%282%29.jpg/330px-HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_%282%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_%282%29.jpg/440px-HEYBEL%C4%B0_ADA_-_panoramio_%282%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4128" data-file-height="2322" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Turkish_Naval_High_School" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkish Naval High School">Naval Cadet School</a> in <a href="/wiki/Heybeliada" title="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</a>, the second largest of the Princes' Islands</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalar_6954.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Adalar_6954.jpg/220px-Adalar_6954.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="163" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Adalar_6954.jpg/330px-Adalar_6954.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Adalar_6954.jpg/440px-Adalar_6954.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2518" data-file-height="1862" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Halki_seminary" title="Halki seminary">Halki seminary</a> in <a href="/wiki/Heybeliada" title="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Heybeliada" title="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</a> (meaning "Saddlebag Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Χάλκη</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Chalkē</i></span>, also rendered <i>Halki</i>) is the second largest of the Princes' Islands in the <a href="/wiki/Sea_of_Marmara" title="Sea of Marmara">Sea of Marmara</a>. It is a neighbourhood in the Adalar district of <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>. The large Naval Cadet School overlooks the jetty to the left as you get off the ferry. There are two interesting pieces of architecture on the grounds of the school. One is Kamariotissa, the only remaining <a href="/wiki/Byzantine" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine">Byzantine</a> church on the island, and more importantly the last church to be built before the conquest of <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>. The other is the grave of the second English Ambassador to be sent to Constantinople by <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I of England</a>, <a href="/wiki/Edward_Barton_(diplomat)" title="Edward Barton (diplomat)">Edward Barton</a>, who chose to live on Heybeli to escape the bustle of the city.
To the right of the jetty lies the town with its bars and cafes, a hotel that stays open all year round, and many lovely wooden houses.
At the top of the central mountain is an eleventh-century <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Greek Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, it houses the <a href="/wiki/Halki_seminary" title="Halki seminary">Halki seminary</a>, the only Greek Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Seminary" title="Seminary">seminary</a> in Turkey and Theological Seminary of the <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate" class="mw-redirect" title="Ecumenical Patriarchate">Ecumenical Patriarchate</a> closed by the Turkish Government in 1971, for no reason. The monastery attracts tourists from all over <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> and <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>, and despite the Turkish Government's promise to reopen the seminary, it still remains closed.
To prevent the island from becoming polluted, the only motorized vehicles permitted on the island are service vehicles such as ambulances, fire trucks, police cars, and the like.  The only forms of transport are by <a href="/wiki/Walking" title="Walking">foot</a>, <a href="/wiki/Horse_and_buggy" title="Horse and buggy">horse and buggy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Transport" title="Transport">service transport</a>. There is no airport; the only way of getting there is by boat.
The winter population of the island is around 3,000, but in the summer, the owners of the summer houses return and the population swells to 10,000. The main summer attractions are small-scale open-air concerts sponsored by the local council, a swimming and fitness club next to the sea, and an annual Independence Day march, which is commemorated by a resident naval band touring the island.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Burgazada">Burgazada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Burgazada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Marina_of_Burgazada,_Istanbul,_on_a_summer_evening,_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/The_Marina_of_Burgazada%2C_Istanbul%2C_on_a_summer_evening%2C_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg/220px-The_Marina_of_Burgazada%2C_Istanbul%2C_on_a_summer_evening%2C_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/The_Marina_of_Burgazada%2C_Istanbul%2C_on_a_summer_evening%2C_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg/330px-The_Marina_of_Burgazada%2C_Istanbul%2C_on_a_summer_evening%2C_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/The_Marina_of_Burgazada%2C_Istanbul%2C_on_a_summer_evening%2C_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg/440px-The_Marina_of_Burgazada%2C_Istanbul%2C_on_a_summer_evening%2C_as_seen_from_the_inner_city_ferries.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3072" data-file-height="2048" /></a><figcaption>Marina of <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalar_5517.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Adalar_5517.jpg/220px-Adalar_5517.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Adalar_5517.jpg/330px-Adalar_5517.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Adalar_5517.jpg/440px-Adalar_5517.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2576" data-file-height="1932" /></a><figcaption>Ferry port of <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a> (meaning "Fortress Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἀντιγόνη</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Antigonē</i></span>) is the third largest of the Islands, a single hill 2&#160;km across. <a href="/wiki/Demetrius_I_of_Macedon" title="Demetrius I of Macedon">Demetrius I of Macedon</a>, one of the <a href="/wiki/Diadochi" title="Diadochi">Diadochi</a> (Successors) of <a href="/wiki/Alexander_the_Great" title="Alexander the Great">Alexander the Great</a>, built a fort here and named it after his father <a href="/wiki/Antigonus_I_Monophthalmus" title="Antigonus I Monophthalmus">Antigonus I Monophthalmus</a>. The island took this name, but today is generally known by the Turks simply as "Burgaz" (Turkish for "fort"). In 2003 Burgaz suffered a forest fire, losing 4 square kilometres of woodland.
Burgaz is a common setting and even a major theme for writer <a href="/wiki/Sait_Faik_Abas%C4%B1yan%C4%B1k" title="Sait Faik Abasıyanık">Sait Faik Abasıyanık</a>, where he also resided. Today, his residence is kept as a museum. At his favourite restaurant in Kalpazankaya (the counterfeiter's rock) one will also find his bronze statue enjoying the view with a glass of <a href="/wiki/Rak%C4%B1" title="Rakı">rakı</a> freshly filled everyday by the restaurant owners. Until the mid-20th century, Burgazada was predominantly <a href="/wiki/Jewish" class="mw-redirect" title="Jewish">Jewish</a>.
</p>

<h3><span id="K.C4.B1nal.C4.B1ada"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Kınalıada">Kınalıada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Kınalıada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalar_5507.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Adalar_5507.jpg/220px-Adalar_5507.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Adalar_5507.jpg/330px-Adalar_5507.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Adalar_5507.jpg/440px-Adalar_5507.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2576" data-file-height="1932" /></a><figcaption>Ferry port of <a href="/wiki/K%C4%B1nal%C4%B1ada" title="Kınalıada">Kınalıada</a></figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalar_5505.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Adalar_5505.jpg/220px-Adalar_5505.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Adalar_5505.jpg/330px-Adalar_5505.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Adalar_5505.jpg/440px-Adalar_5505.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2576" data-file-height="1932" /></a><figcaption>Coastline of <a href="/wiki/K%C4%B1nal%C4%B1ada" title="Kınalıada">Kınalıada</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/K%C4%B1nal%C4%B1ada" title="Kınalıada">Kınalıada</a> (meaning "<a href="/wiki/Henna" title="Henna">Henna</a> Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>, named after the colour of its earth; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πρώτη</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Prōtē</i></span>, "First") is the nearest island to the European and Asian side of Istanbul, about 12 kilometres (7&#160;mi) to the south.  This is one of the least forested islands, and the land has a reddish colour from the iron and copper that has been mined here.  This was the island most used as a place of exile in <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> times (the most notable exile being the former emperors <a href="/wiki/Romanos_I_Lekapenos" title="Romanos I Lekapenos">Romanos I Lekapenos</a> from 944, and <a href="/wiki/Romanos_IV_Diogenes" title="Romanos IV Diogenes">Romanos IV Diogenes</a>, after the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Manzikert" title="Battle of Manzikert">Battle of Manzikert</a>, 1071). Also, this island has a historical abbey top of it. From the 19th century to the mid-20th century, Kınalıada was predominantly Armenian, giving it the highest density of Armenians living anywhere in Istanbul, albeit mainly in summer homes. During the summer, approximately 90% of the population on the island was Armenian.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The island was also a summer retreat for the Armenian patriarchs of Istanbul.
The islands are reachable by ferry services that depart from <a href="/wiki/Kabata%C5%9F,_Istanbul" class="mw-redirect" title="Kabataş, Istanbul">Kabataş</a> on the European side. The voyage takes about 25 minutes by fast ferry and 40 minutes by regular ferry (<i>vapur</i>).
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sedef_Island">Sedef Island</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Sedef Island">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Princesislands.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Princesislands.jpg/220px-Princesislands.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Princesislands.jpg/330px-Princesislands.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Princesislands.jpg/440px-Princesislands.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="600" /></a><figcaption>A view of <a href="/wiki/Sedef_Island" title="Sedef Island">Sedef Island</a> from <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Sedef_Island" title="Sedef Island">Sedef Island</a>, (<a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>: <i lang="tr">Sedef Adası</i>, meaning "Mother-of-Pearl Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Τερέβινθος</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Terebinthos</i></span>, also in the modern corrupted form Αντιρόβυθος, <i>Antirovythos</i>) is one of the smallest islands of the archipelago, and has 108 private homes. The section that's open to the general public largely consists of a beach hamlet. The island is mostly private property and the current pine forests were largely planted by its owner Şehsuvar Menemencioğlu, who purchased the island in 1956 and also played an important role in the imposition of a strict building code to make sure that the island's nature and environment will be protected. He has forbidden the building of structures with more than 2 floors.
The island's Greek name, Terebinthos, means "<a href="/wiki/Terebinth" class="mw-redirect" title="Terebinth">terebinth</a>", which suggests a significant presence of the terebinth in earlier times. In 857 AD <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Ignatios_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Ignatios of Constantinople">Patriarch Ignatios of Constantinople</a> was exiled to the island and imprisoned for 10 years before being re-elected as Patriarch in 867 AD.
</p>
<h3><span id="Yass.C4.B1ada"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Yassıada">Yassıada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Yassıada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Adalar_Luftbild.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Adalar_Luftbild.JPG/220px-Adalar_Luftbild.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="152" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Adalar_Luftbild.JPG/330px-Adalar_Luftbild.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/Adalar_Luftbild.JPG/440px-Adalar_Luftbild.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1565" data-file-height="1084" /></a><figcaption>Adalar</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Yass%C4%B1ada" title="Yassıada">Yassıada</a> (meaning "Flat Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πλάτη</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Platē</i></span>) was used by the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantines</a> for sending prominent figures into exile. One such person was the <a href="/wiki/Armenians" title="Armenians">Armenian</a> <a href="/wiki/Patriarch" title="Patriarch">Patriarch</a> (<a href="/wiki/Catholicos" title="Catholicos">Catholicos</a>) <a href="/wiki/Saint_Narses" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Narses">Narses</a> who was first sent to this island before being imprisoned at <a href="/wiki/B%C3%BCy%C3%BCkada" title="Büyükada">Büyükada</a> in the 4th century AD. In the 11th century AD the Byzantines used the island for political prisoners. The remains of the 4 underground prison cells from this period can still be seen. The Byzantines also built a monastery and church on the island. Yassıada (Plati) was captured by the Latin Crusaders during the <a href="/wiki/Fourth_Crusade" title="Fourth Crusade">Fourth Crusade</a> in 1204.
In 1857, Yassıada was purchased by the British ambassador <a href="/wiki/Henry_Bulwer,_1st_Baron_Dalling_and_Bulwer" title="Henry Bulwer, 1st Baron Dalling and Bulwer">Henry Bulwer</a>, brother of novelist <a href="/wiki/Edward_Bulwer-Lytton,_1st_Baron_Lytton" class="mw-redirect" title="Edward Bulwer-Lytton, 1st Baron Lytton">Edward Bulwer-Lytton</a>, who built himself a mansion and a number of other structures to live undisturbed on this distant island. Henry Bulwer also organized agricultural production on the island to self-sustain his little realm at least to a certain degree, but later sold Yassıada to the <a href="/wiki/Khedive" title="Khedive">Khedive</a> of <a href="/wiki/Khedivate_of_Egypt" title="Khedivate of Egypt">Ottoman Egypt and Sudan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Khedive_Ismail" class="mw-redirect" title="Khedive Ismail">Ismail Pasha</a>; who, however, didn't construct any new buildings and completely neglected the island.
With the establishment of the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey" class="mw-redirect" title="Republic of Turkey">Republic of Turkey</a> in 1923, the island became a property of the Turkish state, and in 1947 Yassıada was handed over to the <a href="/wiki/Turkish_Navy" class="mw-redirect" title="Turkish Navy">Turkish Navy</a> which built several school buildings. Between 1960 and 1961, these school buildings became the venue for the trials of the members of the former ruling party, <a href="/wiki/Democrat_Party_(Turkey,_historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Democrat Party (Turkey, historical)">Demokrat Parti</a>, after the <a href="/wiki/1960_Turkish_coup_d%27%C3%A9tat" title="1960 Turkish coup d&#39;état">military coup of 1960</a>. Several of the defendants were sentenced to death, and three of these, including the former Prime Minister <a href="/wiki/Adnan_Menderes" title="Adnan Menderes">Adnan Menderes</a>, were taken to <a href="/wiki/%C4%B0mral%C4%B1" title="İmralı">İmralı Island</a> (further southwest, near the southern shoreline of the Sea of Marmara) and executed in 1961.
After the end of the trials, Yassıada was given back to the Turkish Navy and lessons continued to take place at the naval school buildings until 1978.
In 1993, the island became the property of <a href="/wiki/Istanbul_University" title="Istanbul University">Istanbul University</a>'s department of Marine Life and Sea Products, which used it for lessons and research. However, the strong winds on the island made life difficult for the students and eventually classes were held elsewhere.
Today, the island is a favourite location for <a href="/wiki/Scuba_diving" title="Scuba diving">scuba diving</a> schools like Balıkadam Türkiye<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> as well as amateur divers.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Sivriada">Sivriada</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Sivriada">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sivriada.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Sivriada.JPG/220px-Sivriada.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="117" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Sivriada.JPG/330px-Sivriada.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Sivriada.JPG/440px-Sivriada.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2800" data-file-height="1495" /></a><figcaption>Sivriada</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Sivriada" title="Sivriada">Sivriada</a> (meaning "Sharp Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ὀξεία</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Oxeia</i></span>) currently is deserted. The island was often used by the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine</a> clerics as a distant place for peaceful worship, and by the Byzantine emperors as a convenient prison to detain prominent people whom they deemed troublesome. The first famous person to be imprisoned on the island by the order of emperor <a href="/wiki/Nikephoros_I" title="Nikephoros I">Nikephoros I</a> was <a href="/wiki/Plato_of_Sakkoudion" title="Plato of Sakkoudion">Plato of Sakkoudion</a>, the uncle of renowned cleric <a href="/wiki/Theodore_the_Studite" title="Theodore the Studite">Theodoros Stoudites</a>, for supporting his nephew in his conflict with the emperor. Other famous people who stayed in the island for religious and political reasons were Gebon, <a href="/wiki/Bardas_Skleros#Descendants" title="Bardas Skleros">Basil Skleros</a>, <a href="/wiki/Nikephoritzes" title="Nikephoritzes">Nikephoritzes</a> (the chief minister of <a href="/wiki/Michael_VII_Doukas" title="Michael VII Doukas">Michael VII Doukas</a>), Patriarch John of Constantinople and <a href="/wiki/Patriarch_Michael_II_of_Constantinople" class="mw-redirect" title="Patriarch Michael II of Constantinople">Patriarch Michael II of Constantinople</a>. The graves of those who died in the island during the Byzantine period can still be seen today.
The ruins of a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman</a> settlement and a ninth-century Byzantine monastery can still be seen on the shore, close to the fishermen's shelter, a small wharf which is often used by yachts. The most important buildings on the island were built in the ninth century AD, including a church, a chapel dedicated to religious martyrs, a monastery on the eastern end (with its walls still seen today) and a cistern in the center of the island (a part of which can still be seen.)
</p>
<h3><span id="Ka.C5.9F.C4.B1k_Island"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Kaşık_Island">Kaşık Island</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Kaşık Island">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Kasik_island.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Kasik_island.jpg/300px-Kasik_island.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="52" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Kasik_island.jpg/450px-Kasik_island.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Kasik_island.jpg/600px-Kasik_island.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2035" data-file-height="350" /></a><figcaption><i><a href="/wiki/Ka%C5%9F%C4%B1k_Island" title="Kaşık Island">Kaşık Adası</a></i> (Spoon Island) viewed from <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Ka%C5%9F%C4%B1k_Island" title="Kaşık Island">Kaşık Island</a>, (meaning "Spoon Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Πίτα</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Pita</i></span>) is located between the islands of <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a> and <a href="/wiki/Heybeliada" title="Heybeliada">Heybeliada</a>. Kaşık Adası is officially administered by the <a href="/wiki/Burgazada" title="Burgazada">Burgazada</a> neighborhood in the Adalar district of <a href="/wiki/Istanbul" title="Istanbul">Istanbul</a>. It is the second-smallest of the Princes' Islands, with an area of 0.006&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.0023&#160;sq&#160;mi).
</p>
<h3><span id="Tav.C5.9Fan_Island"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Tavşan_Island">Tavşan Island</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=14" title="Edit section: Tavşan Island">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Tavsan_Adasi.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Tavsan_Adasi.JPG/220px-Tavsan_Adasi.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="99" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Tavsan_Adasi.JPG/330px-Tavsan_Adasi.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Tavsan_Adasi.JPG/440px-Tavsan_Adasi.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2800" data-file-height="1265" /></a><figcaption>Tavşan Adası</figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/Tav%C5%9Fan_Adas%C4%B1" title="Tavşan Adası">Tavşan Adası</a> (meaning "Rabbit Island" in <a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Νέανδρος</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Neandros</i></span>, the name of a mythological figure) is the smallest of the Princes' Islands, with an area of 0.004&#160;km<sup>2</sup> (0.0015&#160;sq&#160;mi).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Climate">Climate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Princes%27_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=15" title="Edit section: Climate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The islands experience a <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean_climate" title="Mediterranean climate">Mediterranean climate</a> (<i>Csa/Cs</i>) according to both <a href="/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification" title="Köppen climate classification">Köppen</a> and <a href="/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification" title="Trewartha climate classification">Trewartha</a> climate classifications. The islands feature some of the mildest winters in Istanbul, while the summer heat is relatively tepid, as maritime influences and a lack of urban heat island prevail. Therefore, the islands are classified as USDA <a href="/wiki/Hardiness_zone" title="Hardiness zone">hardiness zone</a> 9b, and AHS heat zone 3.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
