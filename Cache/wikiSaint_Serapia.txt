<p><b>Serapia</b> was a Roman <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, a slave and martyr, also called Seraphia or Seraphima of Syria.
</p><p>Serapia was born at <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> in the late 1st century, of Christian parents. Fleeing the persecution of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Hadrian" title="Hadrian">Hadrian</a>, she went to Italy and settled there.<sup id="cite_ref-Prado_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prado-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Having taken a vow of chastity, she disposed of her property, distributed the proceeds to the poor, and entered the service of a wealthy Roman widow named <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sabina" title="Saint Sabina">Sabina</a>, whom she converted to the faith.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Sabina then withdrew with a few devout friends to one of her country seats near Vendina in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, where they employed themselves in acts of devotion and charity.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  
</p><p>During the reign of Hadrian, Serapia was commanded to do homage to the gods of Rome. She refused and was handed over to two men so they could defile her, but her would-be assailants fell unconscious.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The governor attributed her deliverance to sorcery. They then tried to burn her with torches. By command of the judge Virilus, she was beaten with rods and then beheaded by sword.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sabina buried Serapia in her own family tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Sabina met a similar fate not long after. The <a href="/wiki/Santa_Sabina" title="Santa Sabina">Basilica of Saint Sabina</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Aventine_Hill" title="Aventine Hill">Aventine Hill</a> originally bore a dedication to both saints.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_art">In art</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Serapia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: In art">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Serapia is depicted holding a tablet or book; sometimes she appears with Saint Sabina.
</p><p>Around 1639 <a href="/wiki/Claude_Lorrain" title="Claude Lorrain">Claude Lorrain</a> painted <i><a href="/wiki/Landscape_with_the_Burial_of_St_Serapia" title="Landscape with the Burial of St Serapia">Landscape with the Burial of St Serapia</a></i> for <a href="/wiki/Philip_IV_of_Spain" title="Philip IV of Spain">Philip IV of Spain</a>; it now hangs in the <a href="/wiki/Museo_del_Prado" title="Museo del Prado">Museo del Prado</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Prado_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Prado-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
