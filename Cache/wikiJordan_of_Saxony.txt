<p><b>Jordan of Saxony</b>, (referred to in Latin as <b>Jordanis</b>, also known as <b>de Alamania</b>; c. 1190 &#8211; 1237), was one of the first leaders of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>. His feast day is February 13.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jordan belonged to the noble German family of the <a href="/wiki/Counts_of_Eberstein" class="mw-redirect" title="Counts of Eberstein">Counts of Eberstein</a>. He was born in the Castle of Borrenstrick, in the diocese of <a href="/wiki/Paderborn" title="Paderborn">Paderborn</a>. He began his studies in his native land, and was sent to complete them at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-opwest_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-opwest-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> While a student he met <a href="/wiki/Dominic_de_Guzman" class="mw-redirect" title="Dominic de Guzman">Dominic de Guzman</a>, the founder of the Order of Preachers, and was inspired by the preaching of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Reginald_of_Orleans&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Reginald of Orleans (page does not exist)">Reginald of Orleans</a> (also known as Reginald of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Gilles,_Gard" title="Saint-Gilles, Gard">Saint-Gilles</a>) to join the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>. He received the habit on <a href="/wiki/Ash_Wednesday" title="Ash Wednesday">Ash Wednesday</a>, 1220. Jordan was a Master of Arts and a grammarian, and taught in the schools of Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1221, a <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a> of the Order held in <a href="/wiki/Bologna" title="Bologna">Bologna</a> appointed Jordan <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Prior Provincial</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Master_General">Master General</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Master General">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 6 August 1221, <a href="/wiki/Saint_Dominic" title="Saint Dominic">Dominic</a> died, and in 1222 Jordan was elected as his successor as <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">Master General of the Order of Preachers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-opwest_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-opwest-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Like Dominic, Jordan was famed as a strict disciplinarian whose commitment to the <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">Rule</a> was tempered with kindness.
</p><p>During Jordan's administration, the young Order increased to over 300 priories. Jordan is particularly remembered for his eloquence in attracting candidates to join the Order. Through his lectures in university towns, he won many—allegedly well over 1,000—professors and students for the Order from the universities of Europe, among whom was <a href="/wiki/Albertus_Magnus" title="Albertus Magnus">Albertus Magnus</a> who is thought to have been recruited in <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He added four new <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">provinces</a> to the eight already existing. Twice he obtained for the Order a chair at the University of Paris and helped to found the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Toulouse" title="University of Toulouse">University of Toulouse</a>. He established the first general house of studies of the Order.<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Additionally, Jordan was a spiritual guide to many, including one of the first <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order#Divisions" title="Dominican Order">Dominican nuns</a>, <a href="/wiki/Diana_degli_Andal%C3%B2" title="Diana degli Andalò">Diana degli Andalò</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-opwest_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-opwest-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He also found time to write a number of books: a life of Dominic and several other works. Among them was the <i><a href="/wiki/Libellus_de_principiis_Ordinis_Praedicatorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Libellus de principiis Ordinis Praedicatorum">Libellus de principiis Ordinis Praedicatorum</a></i> ("Booklet on the beginnings of the Order of Preachers"), a <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> text which is both the earliest biography of Dominic and the first narrative history of the foundation of the Order.
</p><p>A section of a work by the friar Gerald de Frachet describing the lives of the first Dominicans, the <i>Lives of the Brothers</i> (<i>Vitae fratrum</i>), is dedicated to describing his character, virtue, and miracles. All of the first  <a href="/wiki/Chronicler" class="mw-redirect" title="Chronicler">chroniclers</a> of the Order describe Jordan's kindness and personal charm. He had the ability to console the troubled and to inspire the despondent with new hope.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jordan died, at the age of forty-seven, in a <a href="/wiki/Shipwreck" title="Shipwreck">shipwreck</a> on his return from <a href="/wiki/Palestine_(region)" title="Palestine (region)">Palestine</a>, where he was visiting the local <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a> of the Order. The shipwreck occurred off the coast of <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a> on 13 February 1237.<sup id="cite_ref-domint_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-domint-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Jordan was buried in the Dominican Church of St. John in <a href="/wiki/Akko" class="mw-redirect" title="Akko">Akko</a>, in present-day <a href="/wiki/Israel" title="Israel">Israel</a>.
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is 13 February.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jordan of Saxony was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XII" title="Pope Leo XII">Pope Leo XII</a> in 1825.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He is venerated as the patron of Dominican vocations.
Jordan is honored as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas_Faculty_of_Engineering" title="University of Santo Tomas Faculty of Engineering">Faculty of Engineering</a> of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Santo_Tomas" title="University of Santo Tomas">University of Santo Tomas</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Manila" title="Manila">Manila</a>, which was founded by the Dominican Order. In Colombia, he is honored as the patron saint of the Colegio Jordan de Sajonia, one of the most important private schools of Bogota.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jordan_of_Saxony&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Jordan of Saxony is credited with introducing the practice of singing the <a href="/wiki/Salve_Regina" title="Salve Regina">Salve Regina</a> in procession at the end of <a href="/wiki/Compline" title="Compline">Compline</a>, done, it is recorded, to calm the spirits of the Brothers, who were being tried by the <a href="/wiki/Devil" title="Devil">Devil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-nashville_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-nashville-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
