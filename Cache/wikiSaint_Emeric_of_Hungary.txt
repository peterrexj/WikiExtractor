<p><b>Emeric</b> (<a href="/wiki/Hungarian_language" title="Hungarian language">Hungarian</a>: <i lang="hu">Szent Imre herceg</i>), also <i>Emericus,</i> <i>Emerick, Emery, Emory</i>, and venerated as <b>Saint Emeric</b> (c. 1007 &#8211;  2 September 1031) was the son of King <a href="/wiki/Stephen_I_of_Hungary" title="Stephen I of Hungary">Stephen I of Hungary</a> and <a href="/wiki/Giselle_of_Bavaria" class="mw-redirect" title="Giselle of Bavaria">Giselle of Bavaria</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Family">Family</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Family">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Emeric is assumed<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> to be the second son of Stephen I. Named after his maternal uncle <a href="/wiki/Henry_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry II, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry II</a>, he was the only one of Stephen's sons who reached adulthood.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Emeric was educated in a strict and ascetic spirit by the Benedictine monk from Venice, <a href="/wiki/Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d" title="Gerard of Csanád">Gerard</a>, from the age of 15 to 23. He was intended to be the next monarch of Hungary, and his father wrote his <i><a href="/wiki/Admonitions" title="Admonitions">Admonitions</a></i> to prepare him for this task. His father tried to make Emeric co-heir still in his lifetime. 
</p><p>He married in the year 1022.  The identity of his wife is disputed.  Some say it was <a href="/wiki/Irene_Monomachina" class="mw-redirect" title="Irene Monomachina">Irene Monomachina</a>, a relative of Byzantine emperor <a href="/wiki/Constantine_IX_Monomachos" title="Constantine IX Monomachos">Constantine IX Monomachos</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> or a female member of the <a href="/wiki/Argyros_(Byzantine_family)" title="Argyros (Byzantine family)">Argyros family</a> to which Byzantine emperor <a href="/wiki/Romanos_III_Argyros" title="Romanos III Argyros">Romanos III Argyros</a> belonged.  Other say it was <a href="/w/index.php?title=Patricissa_of_Croatia&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Patricissa of Croatia (page does not exist)">Patricissa of Croatia</a>, the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Kre%C5%A1imir_III_of_Croatia" title="Krešimir III of Croatia">Krešimir III of Croatia</a>. Another possible person may have been <a href="/wiki/Adelaide/Rixa_of_Poland" class="mw-redirect" title="Adelaide/Rixa of Poland">Adelaide/Rixa of Poland</a> or one of her unnamed sisters.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_sainthood">Death and sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Emeric_of_Hungary&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death and sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The succession plans of Emeric's father could never be fulfilled: on 2 September 1031, at age 24, Emeric was killed by a <a href="/wiki/Boar" class="mw-redirect" title="Boar">boar</a> while hunting. It is assumed<sup id="cite_ref-BBKL_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BBKL-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> that this happened in Hegyközszentimre (presently <a href="/wiki/S%C4%83lard" title="Sălard">Sântimreu</a>, <a href="/wiki/Romania" title="Romania">Romania</a>). He was buried in the <a href="/wiki/Sz%C3%A9kesfeh%C3%A9rv%C3%A1r_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Székesfehérvár Basilica">Székesfehérvár Basilica</a>. Several wondrous <a href="/wiki/Healing" title="Healing">healings</a> and <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">conversions</a> happened at his grave, so on 5 November 1083 <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_I_of_Hungary" title="Ladislaus I of Hungary">King Ladislaus I</a> unearthed Emeric's bones in a large ceremony, and Emeric was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> for his pious life and purity along with his father and Bishop <a href="/wiki/Gerard_of_Csan%C3%A1d" title="Gerard of Csanád">Gerard of Csanád</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>.
</p><p>Emeric is most often pictured in <a href="/wiki/Plate_armour" title="Plate armour">knight's armour</a> with <a href="/wiki/Crown_(headgear)" class="mw-redirect" title="Crown (headgear)">crown</a> and <a href="/wiki/Lily" class="mw-redirect" title="Lily">lily</a>. It is believed by some Hungarians that <a href="/wiki/Amerigo_Vespucci" title="Amerigo Vespucci">Amerigo Vespucci</a>, the Italian explorer and the namesake of the <a href="/wiki/Americas" title="Americas">Americas</a>, was named after the saint, but no proof of this etymology exists.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
