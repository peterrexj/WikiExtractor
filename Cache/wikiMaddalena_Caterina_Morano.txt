<p><b>Maddalena Caterina Morano</b> (15 November 1847 - 26 March 1908) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">professed religious</a> who was a member of the <a href="/wiki/Salesian_Sisters_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesian Sisters of Don Bosco">Salesian Sisters</a>. Morano served as an educator for her entire life believing it to be her vocation; she served as a catechist in addition to being an educator.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Morano was beatified in <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a> on 5 November 1994 during the occasion of the visit of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to two Sicilian cities.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maddalena_Caterina_Morano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Maddalena Caterina Morano was born on 15 November 1847 as the sixth of eight children to Francesco Morano. Her father and older sister both died in 1855 after the death of five brothers.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The death of her father and six of her siblings meant that Morano became the sole breadwinner of the household. Her <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> uncle guided her studies while she acted as the sole provider for the household.<sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> At the age of fourteen the pastor of <a href="/wiki/Buttigliera_d%27Asti" title="Buttigliera d&#39;Asti">Buttigliera d'Asti</a> opened an educational center for children and she was hired as a teacher.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1866 she graduated as a teacher and wanted to enter religious life but was denied this due to the dependence her surviving siblings and mother had on her. Despite this she worked for just over a decade until 1878 as a teacher in <a href="/wiki/Montaldo_Torinese" title="Montaldo Torinese">Montaldo</a>; she also taught <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> in her local parish.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Morano confessed to her mother in 1877 that she wanted to become a <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> but her mother could not support herself if Morano left her. At the age of 30 the <a href="/wiki/Daughters_of_Charity_of_Saint_Vincent_de_Paul" title="Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul">Vincentians</a> rejected her desire to become a cloistered nun. Morano had enough to insure her mother's future in 1878 and so made it a focus of her own aspirations to enter the religious life.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">John Bosco</a> accepted her in joining the Salesians and she made her solemn profession on 4 September 1879; the two met as Morano was walking to Buttigliera d'Asti. Bosco dissuaded her from being a cloistered nun and asked Giovanni Cagliero invite her into Bosco's order.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She made her perpetual vows in 1880. In 1881 - at the request of the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Catania" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Catania">Archbishop of Catania</a> - tasked her with new work where she would teach as well as wash and cook.
</p><p>In 1881 she travelled to <a href="/wiki/Trecastagni" title="Trecastagni">Trecastagni</a> in <a href="/wiki/Catania" title="Catania">Catania</a> and took charge of an existing institute for women; she instilled within it principles of the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesians of Don Bosco</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Salesian_Sisters_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesian Sisters of Don Bosco">Salesian Sisters</a>. She also opened new homes and set up after-school activities in addition to training new teachers.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SDB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SDB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Morano died on 26 March 1908 of <a href="/wiki/Cancer" title="Cancer">cancer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SDB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SDB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> At her death there were eighteen Sicilian houses she opened with 142 sisters as well as 20 novices and 9 postulants. Her remains were at <a href="/wiki/Ali_Terme" class="mw-redirect" title="Ali Terme">Ali Terme</a> in <a href="/wiki/Messina" title="Messina">Messina</a> until 12 September 1939 when her remains were transferred to a Messina church the Salesians managed.<sup id="cite_ref-SM_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SM-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Maddalena_Caterina_Morano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process commenced in Catania on 12 July 1935 and spanned until 20 January 1942 in which a collation of documentation was collated in order to attest to Morano's life and her virtues as well as her works in the name of <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>. A rogatory process was then held in Turin which spanned from 18 February 1936 to 6 July 1936 and another rogatory process in <a href="/wiki/Caracas" title="Caracas">Caracas</a> was held from 14 December 1936 to 28 December 1936. One final process - a supplementary process - was held from 20 November 1947 to 12 July 1952. The apostolic process was dispensed as it was not required.
</p><p>The decree on all of her writings - vital to investigate her work and spiritual life - was approved and thus signed as being a valid addition to the cause on 29 May 1958.
</p><p>These processes took place despite the fact that the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation of Rites</a> - under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> did not grant formal approval to the cause until 9 February 1967 thus granting her the posthumous title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>.
</p><p>The Congregation of Rites decreed all the process held were valid and approved them on 19 June 1970. The postulation compiled and submitted the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in 1978 and allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to proclaim her to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 1 September 1988 on the account of her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>The miracle needed for her beatification was investigated and was decreed to be a valid and thorough investigation on 19 June 1992. The pope approved it in 1994 and allowed for her beatification to be celebrated on 5 November 1994.
</p><p>The present <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> is Pierluigi Cameroni.
</p>
