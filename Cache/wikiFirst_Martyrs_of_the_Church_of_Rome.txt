					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>The <b>First Martyrs of the <a href="/wiki/Western_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Church">Church of Rome</a></b> were <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> in the city of <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> during <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>'s persecution in 64. The event is recorded by both <a href="/wiki/Tacitus" title="Tacitus">Tacitus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Pope Clement I">Pope Clement I</a>, among others. They are celebrated in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> as an optional <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a> on 30 June. The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/True_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="True Orthodox Church">True Orthodox Church</a>, celebrate them on 30 June in their liturgical calendars as pre-1054 East-West Schism Western saints  
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg/240px-Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="126" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg/360px-Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1b/Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg/480px-Siemiradski_Fackeln.jpg 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="2105" /></a><figcaption><i><a href="/wiki/Nero%27s_Torches" title="Nero&#39;s Torches">Nero's Torches</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Henryk_Siemiradzki" title="Henryk Siemiradzki">Henryk Siemiradzki</a>, 1876</figcaption></figure>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=First_Martyrs_of_the_Church_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rome had a large Jewish population. The couple, <a href="/wiki/Priscilla_and_Aquila" title="Priscilla and Aquila">Priscilla and Aquila</a>, were tent makers from Pontus, whom <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a> met in Corinth. They had lived in Rome until the emperor <a href="/wiki/Claudius" title="Claudius">Claudius</a> ordered all the Jews to leave the city. Suetonius mentions this as due to disturbances in the city between the Jews and the followers of "<a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christus</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Claudius died in 54 AD.
</p><p>There were early Christians in Rome within a dozen or so years after the death of Jesus, though they were not the converts of Paul.  He had not yet visited them at the time he wrote his <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Romans" title="Epistle to the Romans">Epistle to the Romans</a> in 57-58 AD. Paul wrote to a community of both Jews and Gentiles.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg/240px-Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg" decoding="async" width="240" height="116" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg/360px-Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg/480px-Siemiradzki_Christian_Dirce.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2273" data-file-height="1100" /></a><figcaption>"Christian Dirce", Henryk Siemiradzki, 1897</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=First_Martyrs_of_the_Church_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In July of 64 AD, Rome was devastated by <a href="/wiki/Great_Fire_of_Rome" title="Great Fire of Rome">fire</a>. Largely made up of wooden tenements, fire was a frequent occurrence in the city. Rumor blamed the tragedy on the unpopular emperor Nero, who wanted to enlarge his palace. He accused the Christians. According to the historian Tacitus, many Christians were put to death "not so much of the crime of firing the city, as of hatred against mankind."<sup id="cite_ref-Tacitus_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tacitus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<blockquote><p>Covered with the skins of beasts, they were torn by dogs and perished, or were nailed to crosses, or were doomed to the flames and burnt, to serve as a nightly illumination, when daylight had expired. Nero offered his gardens for the spectacle, and was exhibiting a show in the circus, while he mingled with the people in the dress of a charioteer or stood aloft on a car. Hence, even for criminals who deserved extreme and exemplary punishment, there arose a feeling of compassion; for it was not, as it seemed, for the public good, but to glut one man's cruelty, that they were being destroyed.<sup id="cite_ref-Tacitus_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tacitus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Peter and Paul were probably among the victims.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=First_Martyrs_of_the_Church_of_Rome&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Roma,_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Roma%2C_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg/220px-Roma%2C_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="177" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Roma%2C_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg/330px-Roma%2C_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Roma%2C_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg/440px-Roma%2C_chiesa_dei_Santi_Protomartiri_Romani_-_Esterno.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3128" data-file-height="2522" /></a><figcaption>Santi Protomartiri a Via Aurelia Antica, Rome</figcaption></figure>
<p>This feast first came into the General Roman Calendar in the 1969 calendar reforms.  The intention of the feast is to give a general celebration of early Roman martyrs.  Prior to the calendar reforms, there were dozens of relatively minor Roman martyrs celebrated or commemorated in the calendar.  Several of these had scant historical evidence, but did benefit from immemorial tradition.  This feast is a replacement for the many Roman martyr feasts, whose absence allowed for a less cluttered and more "<a href="/wiki/Glossary_of_ancient_Roman_religion#dies_natalis" title="Glossary of ancient Roman religion">dies natale</a>" based sanctoral calendar of more major saints.  It also permitted the greater celebration of <a href="/wiki/Feria" title="Feria">ferias</a>, partially enacting the Second Vatican Council's call for the Proper of Time to take a greater precedence.  All of the early Roman martyrs retain their place in the Martyrology and can be celebrated in local calendars or privately unless impeded by a greater observance.
</p><p>The placement of the feast is directly after the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_Saints_Peter_and_Paul" title="Feast of Saints Peter and Paul">Solemnity of SS Peter and Paul</a>, who are the principal patron saints of Rome.  The subsequent martyrs are associated with this patronage.  The feast day was formerly occupied with a Commemoration of St. Paul, and fell in the <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgy)" title="Octave (liturgy)">Octave</a> of SS Peter and Paul.
</p><p>The church of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Protomartiri_a_Via_Aurelia_Antica" title="Santi Protomartiri a Via Aurelia Antica">Santi Protomartiri a Via Aurelia Antica</a> in Rome is dedicated to these first martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
