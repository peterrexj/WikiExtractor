<p><b>Gaspar de Bono i Manzón, <a href="/wiki/Minim_(religious_order)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minim (religious order)">O.M.</a></b>, (5 January 1530 &#8211; 14 July 1604) was a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Minim_(religious_order)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minim (religious order)">Order of Minims</a> and <a href="/wiki/Catholic_priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic priest">Catholic priest</a>. He is venerated as <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">blessed</a> by the Catholic Church.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaspar_de_Bono&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/De_Bono" title="De Bono">De Bono</a> was born on 5 January 1530 in <a href="/wiki/Valencia" title="Valencia">Valencia</a>, the second of the four children of Juan de Bonom (or Joan de Bonome), who had emigrated from <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>, and his wife, Isabel Juana Manzón (or Monzon), originally from Villa de <a href="/wiki/Cervera" title="Cervera">Cervera</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Lleida" title="Province of Lleida">Province of Lleida</a> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Lérida</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-PAM_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PAM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Upon his birth, he was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> at the nearby <a href="/wiki/Church_of_San_Nicol%C3%A1s_(Valencia)" title="Church of San Nicolás (Valencia)">Church of San Nicolás</a>. Having been born on the eve of the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Epiphany" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Epiphany">Feast of the Epiphany</a>, he was named for one of the <a href="/wiki/Biblical_Magi" title="Biblical Magi">Biblical Magi</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-PV_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although Gaspar's parents were very pious, the family was poor. His father was a linen weaver by trade, but when his mother was blinded three years after his birth and became unable to help in the family trade, the father was forced to sell their home and his tools and took work as a grinder and reseller. As a child, he developed a noticeable stammer, a problem from which he suffered his whole life.<sup id="cite_ref-PV_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PV-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of ten, De Bono began working with a silk merchant, but soon realized that his vocation was religious and began to study Latin while continuing to work to support his family. About 1545, he was admitted as a candidate to the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>, but, just as he was about to enter their <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a>, he was talked out of this by a brother-in-law, after which he returned home.<sup id="cite_ref-PAM_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PAM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At age twenty, De Bono joined the army of the <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Charles_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Charles V">Emperor Charles V</a>, in search of fortune. He served as a soldier for about ten years, but continued to try and live a devout and religious life, by praying, saying the <a href="/wiki/Rosary" title="Rosary">rosary</a> daily, donating to charity and frequenting places of worship. He fought in <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> where he was seriously wounded in the head. Left for dead, De Bono made a promise to enter the <a href="/wiki/Minim_(religious_order)" class="mw-redirect" title="Minim (religious order)">Minim friars</a>, founded by <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Paola" title="Francis of Paola">Francis of Paola</a>, if he survived.
</p><p>De Bono did in fact recover, and soon entered the Minim <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">Monastery</a> of San Sebastián in Valencia, which was located a short distance from his family home, receiving the <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">religious habit</a> on 16 June 1560. He professed his <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">religious vows</a> as a member of the Order on 17 June of the following year and was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> as a priest in 1562.
</p><p>De Bono soon became known for his virtue and strict compliance with the Rule of Life of the Order. He was available to all the people of the neighborhood which he knew thoroughly. He was present to comfort his parents in their final hours, his mother dying on 29 April 1583, and his father a year later. He was soon named <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">Master of novices</a> for the Catalan <a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province" title="Ecclesiastical province">Province</a> of the Order, being responsible for the founding of a Minim monastery in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>. He was elected to serve as <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">Vicar</a> Provincial.<sup id="cite_ref-PAM_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-PAM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> At the insistence of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Valencia_in_Spain" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Valencia in Spain">Archbishop of Valencia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Juan_de_Ribera" title="Juan de Ribera">Juan de Ribera</a> (now honored as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>), in 1602 he was elected Corrector <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Provincial</a> (regional superior) of Valencia. He is said to have remained humble and austere; retaining his devotions and customs, and continued to be noted for his prudence and charity throughout his life.
</p><p>De Bono died in Valencia on 14 July 1604, at the age of 74. His remains were first entombed in the Church of San Sebastián, to which the monastery was attached. After the suppression of the monastery under the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Spain_under_Joseph_Bonaparte" class="mw-redirect" title="Kingdom of Spain under Joseph Bonaparte">Napoleonic rule of Spain</a>, the urn containing his bones was moved in 1835 to the Church of San Nicolás, where he had been baptized as a baby, and enshrined under the altar of the Chapel of <a href="/wiki/Raphael_(archangel)" title="Raphael (archangel)">St. Raphael</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gaspar_de_Bono&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>De Bono was beatified by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a> on 10 September 1786. His eulogy is written in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> for 14 July: <i>At Valencia in Spain, Blessed Gaspar de Bono, a priest of the Order of Minims, who left the arms of the prince of the world for the militia of Christ the King and for the sake of the house of ' Order in the Spanish province, who ruled with prudence and charity.</i>
</p><p>Each year, on the 14 July, small celebrations are held in De Bono's honor at the Impasse of Cañete in the Carmen neighborhood, the street where he was born.
</p>
