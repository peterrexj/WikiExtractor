<p><b>Saint Malo</b> (<small>French pronunciation:&#160;&#8203;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[sɛ̃.ma.lo]</a></span>; also known as <b>Maclou</b>, <b>Maloù</b> or <b>Mac'h Low</b>, or in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> as <b>Maclovius</b> or <b>Machutus</b>, <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 27 March 520 – 15 November 621) was a Welsh mid-sixth century founder of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Malo" title="Saint-Malo">Saint-Malo</a>, a commune in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, France. He was one of the <a href="/wiki/Brittany#Religion" title="Brittany">seven founding saints of Brittany</a>.

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Malo_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Malo's name may derive from the Old <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a> <i>machlou</i>, a <a href="/wiki/Compound_(linguistics)" title="Compound (linguistics)">compound</a> of <i>mach</i> "warrant, hostage" and <i>lou</i> (or <i>loh</i>) "brilliant, bright, beautiful".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Details of Malo's career have been preserved in three <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">medieval</a> 'Lives' that seem to include incidents associated with multiple people bearing a similar name.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Malo was probably born in <a href="/wiki/Llancarfan" title="Llancarfan">Llancarfan</a> (Wales) in approximately 520. He was the son of Dervel, sister of Amwn Ddu, and therefore cousin to <a href="/wiki/St._Samson_the_Breton" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Samson the Breton">St. Samson</a>. He was placed in the abbot's care at a tender age, and grew up at the abbey, where he was ordained priest and assigned the office of preacher.<sup id="cite_ref-Emanuel_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emanuel-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Voyages_with_Brendan">Voyages with Brendan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Malo_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Voyages with Brendan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As a monk at <a href="/wiki/Llancarfan_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Llancarfan Abbey">Llancarfan Abbey</a> in Wales, Malo was known for his participation in the voyage of Saint <a href="/wiki/Brendan_the_Navigator" title="Brendan the Navigator">Brendan the Navigator</a>. Malo became Brendan's favourite <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">disciple</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to the <i>Voyage of Saint Brendan the Abbot</i>, Brendan and Malo left Llancarfan Abbey with several companions and discovered the "<a href="/wiki/Island_of_the_Blest" class="mw-redirect" title="Island of the Blest">Island of the Blest</a>". He then went to sea on a second voyage and visited the Island of <a href="/wiki/C%C3%A9zembre" title="Cézembre">Cézembre</a>, remaining there for some time.  During their travels, they encountered Maclovius, a dead giant whom Brendan temporarily revived with his holiness. Brendan baptised him before the giant returned to his grave.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It is thought that Brendan, on the occasion of his second voyage, evangelised the <a href="/wiki/Orkney_Islands" class="mw-redirect" title="Orkney Islands">Orkney Islands</a> and the northern isles of <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_(R%C3%A9guiny)_6002.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_%28R%C3%A9guiny%29_6002.JPG/220px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_%28R%C3%A9guiny%29_6002.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="341" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_%28R%C3%A9guiny%29_6002.JPG/330px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_%28R%C3%A9guiny%29_6002.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2f/%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_%28R%C3%A9guiny%29_6002.JPG/440px-%C3%89glise_Saint-Clair_%28R%C3%A9guiny%29_6002.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2009" data-file-height="3114" /></a><figcaption>St Malo and <a href="/wiki/Hoel" class="mw-redirect" title="Hoel">Hoel</a> in a stained-glass window in <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9guiny" title="Réguiny">Réguiny</a> in <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, northern France.</figcaption></figure>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Breton_evangelist">Breton evangelist</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Malo_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Breton evangelist">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At Aleth, Malo served under a venerable <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> named <a href="/wiki/Aaron_of_Aleth" title="Aaron of Aleth">Aaron</a>. Upon Aaron's death in 544, Malo continued the spiritual rule of the district subsequently known as <a href="/wiki/Saint-Malo" title="Saint-Malo">Saint-Malo</a> and was consecrated as the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Aleth" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Aleth">Bishop of Aleth</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Saint_Servan" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Servan">Saint Servan</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Grattan_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grattan-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Many miracles are related of him there.
</p><p>In old age, the disorder on the island compelled Malo to leave, but the people soon begged him to return. He obliged his people and returned to restore order. Feeling at the end of his life, Malo was determined to spend his last days in solitary penance. He died at an advanced age during a voyage from <a href="/wiki/Saint_Servan" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Servan">Aleth</a> to Archambiac (near <a href="/wiki/Archingeay" title="Archingeay">Archingeay</a>) in the province of Saintonge. Malo might have died on 15 November 621 (although this may be the death date of Saint Marcoult).<sup id="cite_ref-Emanuel_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emanuel-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Malo_(saint)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The city of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Malo" title="Saint-Malo">Saint-Malo</a> is one of the seven stages in the <i><a href="/wiki/Tro_Breizh" title="Tro Breizh">Tro Breizh</a></i> ("Tour of Brittany", in <a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>), a pilgrimage celebrating the <a href="/wiki/Seven_founding_saints_of_Brittany" class="mw-redirect" title="Seven founding saints of Brittany">seven founding saints of Brittany</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Indirectly, the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a> name of the <a href="/wiki/Falkland_Islands" title="Falkland Islands">Falkland Islands</a>, <i>Islas Malvinas</i>, can be traced to Malo, as it is derived from the <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>, <i>Îles Malouines</i> and named by <a href="/wiki/Louis_Antoine_de_Bougainville" title="Louis Antoine de Bougainville">Louis Antoine de Bougainville</a> in 1764 after the first known settlers: mariners, and fishermen from the port of Saint-Malo.<sup id="cite_ref-falklandislandsguide.wordpress.com_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-falklandislandsguide.wordpress.com-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pontoise_Cathedral" title="Pontoise Cathedral">Pontoise Cathedral</a> is dedicated to Saint Malo. <a href="/wiki/Lesmahagow_Priory" title="Lesmahagow Priory">Lesmahagow Priory</a> in <a href="/wiki/South_Lanarkshire" title="South Lanarkshire">South Lanarkshire</a> is also dedicated to him in the Latin form of his name, Machutus. He is the patron saint of the churches of St. Maughans and Llanfaenor in <a href="/wiki/Monmouthshire" title="Monmouthshire">Monmouthshire</a> and Llanfechell in <a href="/wiki/Anglesey" title="Anglesey">Anglesey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Emanuel_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Emanuel-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The place-name <a href="/wiki/Saint-Maclou" title="Saint-Maclou">Saint-Maclou</a> also refers to him.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
