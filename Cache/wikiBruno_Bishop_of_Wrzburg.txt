<p><b>Bruno of Würzburg</b> (c. 1005 – 27 May 1045), also known as <b>Bruno of Carinthia</b>, was <a href="/wiki/Archchancellor" title="Archchancellor">imperial chancellor</a> of Italy from 1027 to 1034 for <a href="/wiki/Conrad_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Conrad II, Holy Roman Emperor">Conrad II, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, to whom he was related, and from 1034 until his death <a href="/wiki/Prince-bishop_of_W%C3%BCrzburg" class="mw-redirect" title="Prince-bishop of Würzburg">prince-bishop of Würzburg</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin_and_Imperial_politics">Origin and Imperial politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_W%C3%BCrzburg)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origin and Imperial politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bruno was the son of <a href="/wiki/Conrad_I,_Duke_of_Carinthia" title="Conrad I, Duke of Carinthia">Conrad I, Duke of Carinthia</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Matilda_of_Swabia" title="Matilda of Swabia">Matilda of Swabia</a>, and thus a cousin of the <a href="/wiki/Salian" class="mw-redirect" title="Salian">Salian</a> <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor</a> Conrad II. He courted <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Poitou" title="Agnes of Poitou">Agnes of Poitou</a> on behalf of Conrad's son and successor <a href="/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry III, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Henry III</a>. Bruno laid the corner-stone of <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg_Cathedral" title="Würzburg Cathedral">Würzburg Cathedral</a>, and in 1042 dedicated the <a href="/wiki/St._Burchard%27s_Abbey,_W%C3%BCrzburg" title="St. Burchard&#39;s Abbey, Würzburg">Abbey of St. Burchard</a>, rebuilt by Abbot Willemund.<sup id="cite_ref-Hist2_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hist2-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He also accompanied Henry on his second Hungarian Campaign, during which Bruno died in an accident at Persenbeug on the <a href="/wiki/Danube" title="Danube">Danube</a> in the present <a href="/wiki/Lower_Austria" title="Lower Austria">Lower Austria</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_W%C3%BCrzburg)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The retinue of Henry III had stopped at the residence of Countess Richlinde of Ebersberg, who was faced with the task of distributing the estate of her recently deceased husband Count Adalbero II of Ebersberg. During a great banquet given by the countess a load-bearing pillar supporting the banqueting hall broke, causing the entire floor to collapse. The king was only slightly hurt but the countess, Bishop Bruno and Abbot Altmann of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ebersberg_Abbey&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ebersberg Abbey (page does not exist)">Ebersberg Abbey</a> were so badly injured that they did not survive more than a few days. The Annals of <a href="/wiki/Niederaltaich_Abbey" title="Niederaltaich Abbey">Niederaltaich</a> add a legend to the story: before the feast, at the <i><a href="/w/index.php?title=Strudengau&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Strudengau (page does not exist)">Strudengau</a></i> on the Danube near <a href="/wiki/Grein,_Austria" title="Grein, Austria">Grein</a>, the devil was supposed to have appeared to the bishop and threatened him already, but the bishop was able to repel him. Bruno's body was returned to his residence in Würzburg.
</p><p>He was succeeded by his nephew, <a href="/wiki/Adalbero_of_W%C3%BCrzburg" title="Adalbero of Würzburg">Adalbero of Würzburg</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Burial_and_cultus">Burial and cultus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_(bishop_of_W%C3%BCrzburg)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Burial and cultus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Many cathedrals were built in that period, and from 1040 Bruno began the construction of <a href="/wiki/W%C3%BCrzburg_Cathedral" title="Würzburg Cathedral">Würzburg Cathedral</a>. The consecration of the <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a> on 16 June 1045 was combined with his burial. Bruno was not formally <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by the Roman Catholic Church, but is nevertheless <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">revered</a> as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is May 17th (not the 27th, see Roman Martyrology).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bruno wrote a well-known commentary on the <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a>, to which he appended an analysis of ten Biblical hymns, consisting of extracts from the writings of the <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Church Fathers</a>.
