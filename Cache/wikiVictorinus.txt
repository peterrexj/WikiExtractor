<p><b>Marcus Piavonius Victorinus</b><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;note 1&#93;</a></sup> was <a href="/wiki/Gallic_Empire" title="Gallic Empire">emperor in the Gallic provinces</a> from 268 to 270<sup id="cite_ref-Martindale965_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martindale965-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or 269 to 271,<sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> following the brief reign of <a href="/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius_Marius" title="Marcus Aurelius Marius">Marius</a>. He was murdered by a jealous husband whose wife he had tried to seduce.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reign">Reign</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Victorinus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Reign">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg/220px-Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="108" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg/330px-Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg/440px-Victorinus_Mosaik.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3529" data-file-height="1726" /></a><figcaption>Mosaic with the name of Victorinus from <a href="/wiki/Augusta_Treverorum" title="Augusta Treverorum">Augusta Treverorum</a> (<i><a href="/wiki/Corpus_Inscriptionum_Latinarum" title="Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum">CIL</a></i> XIII, 03679 (4, p 43); <a href="/wiki/Rheinisches_Landesmuseum_Trier" title="Rheinisches Landesmuseum Trier">Rheinisches Landesmuseum Trier</a>)</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif/lossless-page1-220px-IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif.png" decoding="async" width="220" height="206" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif/lossless-page1-330px-IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif.png 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cc/IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif/lossless-page1-440px-IMP_C_VICTORINVS_PF_AVG.tif.png 2x" data-file-width="587" data-file-height="550" /></a><figcaption>Bronze double denarius of the Gallic Roman emperor Victorinus (269–271) found at <a href="/wiki/U_Thong" class="mw-redirect" title="U Thong">U Thong</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thailand" title="Thailand">Thailand</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Hailing from <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, Victorinus was born into a <a href="/wiki/Gauls" title="Gauls">Gallic</a> family of great wealth,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and was a soldier under <a href="/wiki/Postumus" title="Postumus">Postumus</a>, the first of the so-called Gallic emperors.<sup id="cite_ref-Martindale965_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martindale965-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He showed considerable ability, as he held the title of tribunus praetorianorum (tribune of the praetorians) in 266/267,<sup id="cite_ref-Martindale965_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martindale965-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and rose swiftly to become co-<a href="/wiki/Consul_(Gallic_Empire)" title="Consul (Gallic Empire)">consul</a> with Postumus in 268.<sup id="cite_ref-Southern118_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern118-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> It is also possible that Postumus then elevated him to the post of <a href="/wiki/Praetorian_prefect" title="Praetorian prefect">praetorian prefect</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Shortly after putting down a rebellion by <a href="/wiki/Laelianus" class="mw-redirect" title="Laelianus">Laelianus</a> in 269, Postumus was murdered by his own troops, who appointed Marius as emperor in his place.<sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After engineering the death of Marius, Victorinus was declared emperor by the troops located at <a href="/wiki/Augusta_Treverorum" title="Augusta Treverorum">Augusta Treverorum</a> (<a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>) in the fall of 269.<sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His principal concern was to prevent the western provinces from submitting to the central authority of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Empire" title="Roman Empire">Roman Empire</a>, a fact made clear to him from the first few weeks when only the provinces of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Gaul" title="Roman Gaul">Gaul</a>, <a href="/wiki/Germania" title="Germania">Germania</a> and <a href="/wiki/Roman_Britain" title="Roman Britain">Britain</a> recognised him. <a href="/wiki/Hispania" title="Hispania">Hispania</a> deserted the Gallic Empire and declared its loyalty to <a href="/wiki/Claudius_Gothicus" title="Claudius Gothicus">Claudius Gothicus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Claudius then sent his trusted general <a href="/wiki/Placidianus" class="mw-redirect" title="Placidianus">Placidianus</a> to south-east Gaul with instructions to bring over as many of the wavering cities as he could.<sup id="cite_ref-Southern118_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern118-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Very quickly Placidianus captured <a href="/wiki/Cularo" title="Cularo">Cularo</a> (<a href="/wiki/Grenoble" title="Grenoble">Grenoble</a>), but did not proceed any further.<sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The presence of Placidianus inspired the city of <a href="/wiki/Augustodunum_Haeduorum" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustodunum Haeduorum">Augustodunum Haeduorum</a> (<a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a>) to abandon Victorinus and declare its intention to declare for <a href="/wiki/Claudius_Gothicus" title="Claudius Gothicus">Claudius Gothicus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Southern118_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern118-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>  This forced Victorinus to march south and besiege it, where it fell after seven months, after which Victorinus’ troops plundered and destroyed the city.<sup id="cite_ref-Southern118_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern118-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Victorinus returned to <a href="/wiki/Colonia_Claudia_Ara_Agrippinensium" title="Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium">Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium</a> (<a href="/wiki/Cologne" title="Cologne">Cologne</a>) in triumph.<sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> It remains a mystery just why Claudius did not authorise Placidianus to go to the relief of Augustodunum; however, it is speculated that Claudius, who was fully engaged either in Italy against the <a href="/wiki/Alamanni" class="mw-redirect" title="Alamanni">Alamanni</a> or in the <a href="/wiki/Balkans" title="Balkans">Balkans</a> against the <a href="/wiki/Goths" title="Goths">Goths</a>, did not wish to open a second theatre of operations in Gaul, which would not only have involved a major military effort, but would also have required Claudius to assume responsibility for the defense of the <a href="/wiki/Limes_Germanicus" title="Limes Germanicus">Rhine frontier</a> had he been successful.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> There is evidence to suggest that Claudius was having some difficulties in the East, which also occupied his attention.<sup id="cite_ref-Potter266_6-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Potter266-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Victorinus was murdered at Colonia<sup id="cite_ref-Martindale965_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Martindale965-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in early 271<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> by Attitianus, one of his officers, whose wife Victorinus had supposedly seduced.<sup id="cite_ref-Southern119_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern119-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>  Since the motive was personal and not political,<sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Southern119_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern119-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Victorinus' mother, <a href="/wiki/Victoria_(Gallic_Empire)" title="Victoria (Gallic Empire)">Victoria</a> (or Vitruvia), was able to continue to hold power after the death of Victorinus and she arranged for his deification and, after considerable payment to the troops, the appointment of <a href="/wiki/Tetricus_I" title="Tetricus I">Tetricus I</a> as his successor.<sup id="cite_ref-Southern119_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Southern119-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Polfer_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Polfer-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Another military commander appears to have been proclaimed as the emperor <a href="/wiki/Domitianus_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Domitianus II">Domitianus II</a>, but was soon eliminated.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Victorinus is listed among the <a href="/wiki/Thirty_Tyrants_(Roman)" title="Thirty Tyrants (Roman)">Thirty Tyrants</a> in the <i><a href="/wiki/Historia_Augusta" title="Historia Augusta">Historia Augusta</a></i>. The dubious <i>Historia Augusta</i> equally has a short description of <a href="/wiki/Victorinus_Junior" title="Victorinus Junior">Victorinus Junior</a>, allegedly the son of Victorinus, who was appointed emperor by his family the day his father was murdered, and would have been killed immediately afterwards by the troops. The <i>Historia Augusta</i> also says that both father and son were buried near Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium in marble tombs.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
