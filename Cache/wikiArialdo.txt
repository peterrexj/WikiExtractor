<p>Saint <b>Arialdo</b> (c. 1010 – June 27, 1066) is a <a href="/wiki/Christian_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian saint">Christian saint</a> of the eleventh century. He was assassinated because of his efforts to reform the <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milanese</a> <a href="/wiki/Clergy" title="Clergy">clergy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Archdiocese_of_Milan_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Archdiocese_of_Milan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arialdo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Arialdo was the child of a noble family, born at <i>Cutiacum</i> (<a href="/wiki/Cucciago" title="Cucciago">Cucciago</a>), near <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a>. After studying in <a href="/wiki/Laon" title="Laon">Laon</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a>, he was made a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> in the cathedral city of Milan. According to Andrea da Parma, abbot of San Fedele di Strumi, who wrote a <i>Vita</i> concerning Arialdo, the church in Milan was rife with immoral clerics, fornicating, sleeping with prostitutes, lending money, and selling indulgences. According to <a href="/wiki/Henry_Charles_Lea" title="Henry Charles Lea">Henry Charles Lea</a> marriage was commonplace among the Milanese clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-Lea_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lea-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Together with Bishop of Lucca <a href="/wiki/Anselmo_da_Baggio" class="mw-redirect" title="Anselmo da Baggio">Anselmo da Baggio</a> (later Pope Alexander II), Arialdo headed the <a href="/wiki/Pataria" title="Pataria">pataria</a>, a movement that sought to reform Milan's <a href="/wiki/Simony" title="Simony">simoniacal</a> clergy.<sup id="cite_ref-Archdiocese_of_Milan_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Archdiocese_of_Milan-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Due to this, he was <a href="/wiki/Excommunicated" class="mw-redirect" title="Excommunicated">excommunicated</a> by the Bishop of Milan <a href="/wiki/Guido_da_Velate" title="Guido da Velate">Guido da Velate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Lea_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lea-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Stephen_IX" title="Pope Stephen IX">Pope Stephen IX</a> removed the excommunication and Arialdo returned to Milan to continue his efforts towards reformation. In 1069 the Pope sent <a href="/wiki/Peter_Damiani" class="mw-redirect" title="Peter Damiani">Peter Damiani</a> as legate to attempt a resolution. The issue then became less a matter of clerical conduct than the authority of Rome over Milan. Damiani was able to demonstrate that the city's beloved patron <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">St Ambrose</a> had acknowledged the precedence of the papacy.<sup id="cite_ref-Lea_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Lea-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eventually, these endeavours lead to bishop Guido da Velate's excommunication. While traveling to Rome, Arialdo was set up by emissaries of Guido and killed.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Arialdo_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Arialdo-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Arialdo&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ten months after the assassination, his body was found in <a href="/wiki/Lago_Maggiore" class="mw-redirect" title="Lago Maggiore">Lago Maggiore</a> (allegedly in a perfect state of preservation, and emitting a sweet odour). It was carried to Milan and exposed in the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Ambrogio" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Ambrogio">church of St. Ambrose</a> from <a href="/wiki/Ascension_of_Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Ascension of Jesus Christ">Ascension</a> to <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a>. Subsequently, Arialdo's body was interred in the church of St. Celsus, and in the following year, 1067, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_II" title="Pope Alexander II">Pope Alexander II</a> declared him a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-St._Arialdo_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-St._Arialdo-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
