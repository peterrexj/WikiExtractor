<p><b>Joachim</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/dʒ/: &#39;j&#39; in &#39;jam&#39;">dʒ</span><span title="/oʊ/: &#39;o&#39; in &#39;code&#39;">oʊ</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;k&#39; in &#39;kind&#39;">k</span><span title="/ɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;kit&#39;">ɪ</span><span title="&#39;m&#39; in &#39;my&#39;">m</span></span>/</a></span></span>; <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>: <span lang="he" dir="rtl">יהויקים</span> <i>Yəhōyāqīm</i>, "he whom <a href="/wiki/Tetragrammaton" title="Tetragrammaton">Yahweh</a> has set up"; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἰωακείμ</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Iōākeím</i></span>; <a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">عمران</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic" title="Romanization of Arabic">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Arabic-language romanization"><i lang="ar-Latn">ʿImrān</i></span>) was, according to Christian tradition, the husband of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Anne" title="Saint Anne">Saint Anne</a> and the father of <a href="/wiki/Mary,_the_mother_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary, the mother of Jesus">Mary, the mother of Jesus</a>. The story of Joachim and Anne first appears in the <a href="/wiki/Biblical_apocrypha" title="Biblical apocrypha">Biblical apocryphal</a> <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_James" title="Gospel of James">Gospel of James</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is 26 July, a date shared with Saint Anne.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Christian_tradition">In Christian tradition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joachim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: In Christian tradition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The story of Joachim, his wife Anne (or Anna), and the miraculous birth of their child Mary, the mother of Jesus, was told for the first time in the 2nd-century apocryphal infancy-gospel the <a href="/wiki/Gospel_of_James" title="Gospel of James">Gospel of James</a> (also called <a href="/wiki/Protoevangelium_of_James" class="mw-redirect" title="Protoevangelium of James">Protoevangelium of James</a>). Joachim was a rich and pious man, who regularly gave to the poor. However, Charles Souvay, writing in the <i>Catholic Encyclopedia</i>, says that the idea that Joachim possessed large herds and flocks is doubtful.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>At the temple, Joachim's <a href="/wiki/Sacrifice" title="Sacrifice">sacrifice</a> was rejected, as the couple's <a href="/wiki/Childlessness" title="Childlessness">childlessness</a> was interpreted as a sign of divine displeasure. Joachim consequently withdrew to the desert, where he <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasted</a> and did <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a> for 40 days. <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">Angels</a> then appeared to both Joachim and Anne to promise them a child.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Joachim later returned to Jerusalem and embraced Anne at the <a href="/wiki/City_gate" title="City gate">city gate</a>, located in the <a href="/wiki/Walls_of_Jerusalem" title="Walls of Jerusalem">Walls of Jerusalem</a>. An ancient belief held that a child born of an elderly mother who had given up hope of having offspring was destined for great things. Parallels occur in the <a href="/wiki/Old_Testament" title="Old Testament">Old Testament</a> in the case of <a href="/wiki/Sarah" title="Sarah">Sarah</a>, the wife of <a href="/wiki/Abraham" title="Abraham">Abraham</a> and mother of <a href="/wiki/Isaac" title="Isaac">Isaac</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hannah_(biblical_figure)" title="Hannah (biblical figure)">Hannah</a>, the mother of <a href="/wiki/Samuel" title="Samuel">Samuel</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> and in the New Testament in the case of the parents of <a href="/wiki/St_John_the_Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="St John the Baptist">St John the Baptist</a>.
</p><p>The cycle of <a href="/wiki/Legend" title="Legend">legends</a> concerning Joachim and Anne was included in the <a href="/wiki/Golden_Legend" title="Golden Legend">Golden Legend</a> (around 1260) by <a href="/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine" title="Jacobus de Voragine">Jacobus de Voragine</a>. This cycle remained popular in <a href="/wiki/Christian_art" title="Christian art">Christian art</a> until the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a> (1545–1563) restricted the depiction of apocryphal events.
</p><p>No liturgical celebration of Saint Joachim was included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>. It was added to the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> in 1584, for celebration on 20 March, the day after the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Joseph" title="Saint Joseph">Saint Joseph</a>. In 1738, it was transferred to the Sunday within the <a href="/wiki/Octave_(liturgical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Octave (liturgical)">Octave</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption of Mary</a>. As part of his effort to allow the liturgy of Sundays to be celebrated, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a> (term 1903–1914) transferred it to 16 August, the day after the Assumption, so Joachim may be remembered in the celebration of Mary's triumph.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> It was then celebrated as a <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_as_in_1954" class="mw-redirect" title="General Roman Calendar as in 1954">Double of the Second Class</a>, a rank that was changed in 1960 to that of <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1960" title="General Roman Calendar of 1960">Second Class Feast</a>. In the <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969 revision</a> of the General Roman Calendar, it was joined to that of Anne, for celebration on 26 July.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Churches">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a> and <a href="/wiki/Greek_Catholics" class="mw-redirect" title="Greek Catholics">Greek Catholics</a> commemorate Joachim on 9 September, the <a href="/wiki/Synaxis" title="Synaxis">Synaxis</a> of Joachim and Anne, the day after the <a href="/wiki/Nativity_of_the_Theotokos" class="mw-redirect" title="Nativity of the Theotokos">Nativity of the Theotokos</a>.
</p><p>Joachim is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> (with Anne) in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">Lesser Festival</a> on <a href="/wiki/July_26" title="July 26">26 July</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joachim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Joachim is named as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of fathers, grandfathers, grandparents, married couples, cabinet makers, and linen traders.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joachim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:172px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg/170px-Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="374" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg/255px-Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg/340px-Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2352" data-file-height="5180" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Friesach_-_Dominikanerkirche_-_Hochaltar_-_Hl_Joachim.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint Joachim</div></div></div>
<p>In medieval art he often wears a conical <a href="/wiki/Jewish_hat" title="Jewish hat">Jewish hat</a>.  He is often treated as a saint, with a <a href="/wiki/Halo_(religious_iconography)" title="Halo (religious iconography)">halo</a>, but in the Western church there was some awareness that he had quite likely died too soon to strictly be counted as a Christian.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Joachim_and_Anne_Meeting_at_the_Golden_Gate" title="Joachim and Anne Meeting at the Golden Gate">Joachim and Anne Meeting at the Golden Gate</a> was a popular subject in artistic renditions of the <a href="/wiki/Life_of_the_Virgin" title="Life of the Virgin">life of the Virgin</a>.
</p><p>Symbols associated with Saint Joachim include a book or scroll representing linen makers, a <a href="/wiki/Shepherd%27s_staff" class="mw-redirect" title="Shepherd&#39;s staff">shepherd's staff</a> for the Christian Word, and a basket of doves representing peace.  He is almost always clothed in green, the color of hope.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The name of the <a href="/wiki/San_Joaquin_River" title="San Joaquin River">San Joaquin River</a> dates to 1805–1808, when Spanish explorer <a href="/wiki/Gabriel_Moraga" title="Gabriel Moraga">Gabriel Moraga</a> was surveying east from <a href="/wiki/Mission_San_Jos%C3%A9_(California)" title="Mission San José (California)">Mission San José</a> in order to find possible sites for a mission. The name was in common use by 1810.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_Islam">In Islam</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Joachim&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In Islam">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For the father of Musa and Harun, see <a href="/wiki/Amram#Arabic" title="Amram">Amram §&#160;Arabic</a>. For other uses, see <a href="/wiki/Imran_(disambiguation)" class="mw-disambig" title="Imran (disambiguation)">Imran (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1066479718"/><table class="infobox biography vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above" style="font-size:125%;"><div class="fn" style="display:inline">Joachim</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Predecessor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Uzair" title="Uzair">Uzair</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Successor</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Zechariah_in_Islam" title="Zechariah in Islam">Zakariyya</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Islam" title="Islam">Islam</a> he is called <b>Imran</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">عمران</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic" title="Romanization of Arabic">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Arabic-language romanization"><i lang="ar-Latn">ʿImrān</i></span>), and one of his believed graves is in <a href="/wiki/Salalah" title="Salalah">Salalah</a>; which is claimed to be the "longest grave in the world" (12 metres).<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> According to the <a href="/wiki/Quran" title="Quran">Quran</a> in <a href="/wiki/Al_Imran" title="Al Imran">Al Imran</a> Imran is the father of <a href="/wiki/Mary_in_Islam" title="Mary in Islam">Maryam</a> and grandfather of <a href="/wiki/Jesus_in_Islam" title="Jesus in Islam">ʿIsa</a>.
</p>
