<p><b>John of Sahagún</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Augustine" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Augustine">OESA</a> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Juan de Sahagún</i>), (c. 1430 – 11 June 1479) was a Spanish <a href="/wiki/Augustinian_friar" class="mw-redirect" title="Augustinian friar">Augustinian friar</a> and priest. He was a leading preacher of his day, and was known as a peacemaker and reconciler of enemies among the nobles and factions of Salamanca.<sup id="cite_ref-recollects_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-recollects-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was declared a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the Catholic Church in 1690 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Sahag%C3%BAn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>John was born in the year 1419, at <a href="/wiki/Sahag%C3%BAn,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Sahagún, Spain">Sahagún</a> (or San Facondo) in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Leon" class="mw-redirect" title="Province of Leon">Province of Leon</a>. He was the oldest of the seven children of Juan González del Castrillo and Sancha Martínez, <sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> a wealthy family of the city.
</p><p>González received his early education from the monks of the <a href="https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasterio_Real_de_San_Benito_(Sahag%C3%BAn)" class="extiw" title="es:Monasterio Real de San Benito (Sahagún)">Royal Monastery of St. Benedict</a> in his native city, a leading religious and educational center in the region known as the <a href="/wiki/Cluny_Abbey" title="Cluny Abbey">Cluny</a> of Spain. He received the <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsure</a> while still a youth, according to the custom of the times, after which his father procured for him the <a href="/wiki/Benefice" title="Benefice">benefice</a> of the neighboring <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> of Tornillo. He was later introduced to <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_de_Cartagena" title="Alfonso de Cartagena">Alfonso de Cartagena</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Burgos" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Burgos">Bishop of Burgos</a> (1435–1456), who was impressed by the bright, high-spirited boy. Cartagena had him educated at his own residence, gave him several <a href="/wiki/Prebend" class="mw-redirect" title="Prebend">prebends</a>, ordained him a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> in the year 1445, and made him a <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">canon</a> at the <a href="/wiki/Burgos_Cathedral" title="Burgos Cathedral">Cathedral of Burgos</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Possessing all of these offices simultaneously caused González many qualms of conscience, as it was contrary to <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">Church law</a>. He soon resigned all, retaining only that of the Chapel of <a href="/wiki/St._Agatha" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Agatha">St. Agatha</a> in a poor neighborhood of the city, where he said <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a>, and preached the faith to the poor. He then began to lead a life of strict poverty and <a href="/wiki/Mortification_of_the_flesh" title="Mortification of the flesh">mortification</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Hoever_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hoever-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Preacher">Preacher</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Sahag%C3%BAn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Preacher">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>When his bishop died in 1456, John resigned as chaplain to pursue further studies at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Salamanca" title="University of Salamanca">University of Salamanca</a>, where for four years he applied himself to the study of <a href="/wiki/Theology" title="Theology">theology</a> and canon law, earning degrees in both.<sup id="cite_ref-villanova_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villanova-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> During this time he exercised the ministry at the chapel of the <a href="/wiki/Colegio_Mayor_de_San_Bartolom%C3%A9,_Salamanca" title="Colegio Mayor de San Bartolomé, Salamanca">College of St. Bartholomew</a> (in the Parish of St Sebastian), and held that position for nine years. As preacher, he drew large crowds; the Duke of Alba complained when John denounced not only the sins of common people, but also those of the nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-Midwest_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Midwest-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He devoted himself to <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_care" title="Pastoral care">pastoral care</a>. Owing to illness, he was obliged to undergo an operation for the removal of <a href="/wiki/Kidney_stone" class="mw-redirect" title="Kidney stone">kidney stones</a>. He vowed that if his life were spared, he would become a <a href="/wiki/Religious_order#Catholic_tradition" title="Religious order">Religious</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Friar">Friar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Sahag%C3%BAn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Friar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Burgos,_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Burgos%2C_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg/220px-Burgos%2C_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Burgos%2C_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg/330px-Burgos%2C_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/07/Burgos%2C_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg/440px-Burgos%2C_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_Burgos_023.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2736" data-file-height="3648" /></a><figcaption>Chapel of St. John, Burgos Cathedral</figcaption></figure>
<p>Upon his recovery in the year 1463, González applied for admission to the Order of Hermits of St. Augustine, at the Monastery of St. Peter, from that point on, being known simply as Brother (or Friar) John. In the following year, on August 28, 1464, John made his profession of <a href="/wiki/Solemn_vows" class="mw-redirect" title="Solemn vows">solemn vows</a> as a member of the Order. By the command of his superiors, John gave himself wholeheartedly to preaching.<sup id="cite_ref-Hoever_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Hoever-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In his sermons, John did not hesitate to publicly criticize the abuses of the powerful and the frequent public scandals of the upper classes.<sup id="cite_ref-recollects_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-recollects-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He soon made many enemies. Some women of Salamanca, embittered by the saint's strong sermon against extravagance in dress, openly insulted him in the streets and pelted him with stones until stopped by a patrol of guards.
</p><p>John was appointed <a href="/wiki/Master_of_novices" class="mw-redirect" title="Master of novices">master of novices</a>, and later in the year 1471, <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of the community.<sup id="cite_ref-Midwest_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Midwest-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He conducted the Religious under his rule more by example than by his words. He also served as delegate to various provincial chapters.
</p><p>John had a particular devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>. He was gifted with the ability to read the secrets of conscience, so that it was not easy to deceive him. Because of his reputation, he was often approached to reconcile rival groups and restore peace to the city. He was also respected as a defender of the rights of workers and the common folk.<sup id="cite_ref-villanova_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villanova-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By mid-1479 John's health began to fail and it was rumored in the city that he had been poisoned by a woman in retaliation for his condemnation of the immoral lifestyle of a public figure. True or not, John died at the monastery on 11 June 1479.<sup id="cite_ref-villanova_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-villanova-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His remains were buried in the <a href="/wiki/Old_Cathedral,_Salamanca" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Cathedral, Salamanca">Old Cathedral, Salamanca</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_of_Sahag%C3%BAn&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Soon after John's death, his cult spread throughout Spain. The process of <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a> began in 1525 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VII" title="Pope Clement VII">Pope Clement VII</a>, and in 1601 he was declared "<a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Blessed</a>" by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_VIII" title="Pope Clement VIII">Pope Clement VIII</a> in 1601.<sup id="cite_ref-recollects_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-recollects-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>New miracles were said to have been wrought through his intercession, and on 16 October 1690 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VIII" title="Pope Alexander VIII">Pope Alexander VIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> him. In 1729 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> inscribed his liturgical <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the Roman Calendar for 12 June, since 11 June, the anniversary of his death was occupied by the feast of <a href="/wiki/Barnabas" title="Barnabas">Saint Barnabas</a>. In the 1969 revision of the Roman liturgical celebration was left to local calendars because of the limited importance attributed to him on a universal level.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, the official list of saints of the Catholic Church, his feast day is 11 June.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>John's life written by <a href="/wiki/John_of_Seville" title="John of Seville">John of Seville</a> towards the end of the fifteenth century with additions in 1605 and 1619, is the one used by the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> in "Acta SS.", June, III, 112.
</p><p>In art, John is represented holding a <a href="/wiki/Chalice_(cup)" class="mw-redirect" title="Chalice (cup)">chalice</a> and host surrounded by rays of light.<sup id="cite_ref-Mershman_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Mershman-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
