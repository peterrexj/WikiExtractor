<p><b>Humbert of Romans</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1190-1200</span>, <a href="/wiki/Romans-sur-Is%C3%A8re" title="Romans-sur-Isère">Romans-sur-Isère</a> – 14 July 1277, <a href="/wiki/Valence,_Dr%C3%B4me" title="Valence, Drôme">Valence, Drôme</a>, France) was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who served as the fifth <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">Master General of the Order of Preachers</a> from 1254 to 1263.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_career">Early career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Humbert_of_Romans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Nothing is known of his early life. The earliest known details of his life show that Humbert studied both Arts and then <a href="/wiki/Canon_law_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon law (Catholic Church)">canon law</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a>, where he was then admitted as a professor. A man of deep piety, subsequently, although he had thought about joining the <a href="/wiki/Carthusians" title="Carthusians">Carthusians</a> (whom his brother had joined), he entered the Dominican Order on 30 November 1224. After his <a href="/wiki/Profession" title="Profession">profession</a>, he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Lector" title="Lector">lector</a> of theology at the Dominican priory in <a href="/wiki/Lyon" title="Lyon">Lyon</a> during 1226 and, by 1237, he had become <a href="/wiki/Prior_(ecclesiastical)" title="Prior (ecclesiastical)">prior</a> of that monastery. In 1240 he was appointed as the <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Prior Provincial</a> of <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscany</a>. His presence in Italy led to support for his candidacy in the <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1241" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1241">papal conclave</a> of 1241 (although the <a href="/wiki/Orsini_family" title="Orsini family">Orsini</a> and other noble Romans families seem to have opposed his election).
</p><p>Humbert returned to France in 1244 as Prior Provincial of the country, replacing <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Saint-Cher" title="Hugh of Saint-Cher">Hugh of Saint-Cher</a>, who had been made a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">cardinal</a>, the first from their Order. During his time as Provincial in his native land, Humbert was charged with producing a <a href="/wiki/Lectionary" title="Lectionary">lectionary</a> for use by the whole Order.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Master_General">Master General</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Humbert_of_Romans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Master General">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Humbert was elected as Master General of the Dominican Order in 1254. His first achievement was the re-organization (and consequent standardization) of the Order's <a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgy</a>. A new edition of the Order's <a href="/wiki/Constitutions" class="mw-redirect" title="Constitutions">Constitutions</a> was prepared, and measures were taken to improve discipline in the Order's houses. Further, he issued new Constitutions for all nuns associated with the Dominican Order, based on those he himself had drawn up while serving as Provincial of France. He instituted the formal collection of information of two of the Order's members, <a href="/wiki/Dominic" title="Dominic">Dominic</a>, the founder, and the martyred <a href="/wiki/Peter_of_Verona" title="Peter of Verona">Peter of Verona</a>, with the intention of seeking their <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>. As a result of this search for information, Friar Gerald de Frachet produced his <i><a href="/wiki/Lives_of_the_brethren" class="mw-redirect" title="Lives of the brethren">Vitae fratrum</a></i> (<i>Lives of the Brothers</i>).
</p><p>Humbert was a great lover of languages, and encouraged linguistic studies among the Dominicans, primarily Arabic, because of the missionary work friars were pursuing amongst those led astray or forced to convert by <a href="/wiki/Muslims" title="Muslims">Muslims</a> in the Middle East.
</p><p>In 1255, he was called to adjudicate a dispute on the Constitutions of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusians" title="Carthusians">Carthusian monks</a>. In 1256, he became the <a href="/wiki/Godparent" title="Godparent">godfather</a> of one of the children of the holy King <a href="/wiki/Louis_IX_of_France" title="Louis IX of France">Louis IX of France</a>; and, in 1258, the same king asked for his advice regarding the settlement of a dispute between various noble families. Humbert further encouraged the missionary activities of the friars, and schools to teach <a href="/wiki/Oriental_languages" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental languages">Oriental languages</a> were established in Spain.
</p><p>In governing, he demonstrated both indulgence and severity when either was required, and he combined a broad outlook with a genius for detail. Under his rule, the Order flourished in Italy, Germany, Spain, France, and England. Humbert sent missionaries to the Greeks, Hungarians, Saracens, Armenians, Syrians, Ethiopians, and Tartars. He regulated the liturgy of the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Divine Office</a>, determined the suffrages for the dead, commanded the history of the Order be recorded, and even issued minute decrees concerning the election of superiors, the reading of the Constitutions at meals, the transfer of friars from one house to another and other pertinent regulations.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Opposition to the presence of both <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominicans</a> and <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Franciscans</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Paris" title="University of Paris">University of Paris</a> during the mid-1250s led to his issuing a joint <a href="/wiki/Encyclical" title="Encyclical">encyclical</a> with the Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a>, urging that the two Orders - often in bitter dispute - should work together for their survival and the maintenance of their university chairs.
</p><p>Humbert resigned his position as Master of the Order in 1263 at the <a href="/wiki/General_Chapter" class="mw-redirect" title="General Chapter">General Chapter</a> held in <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a>, probably on account of his failing health.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writings">Writings</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Humbert_of_Romans&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Writings">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Humbert's literary production was geared mainly to the demands of his Order. He composed a <i>Letter on Regular Observance</i> (<i>Epistola de regularis observantia disciplinae</i>); a commentary on the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_Saint_Augustine" title="Rule of Saint Augustine">Rule of Saint Augustine</a> (the Rule which had been adopted by the Dominicans in 1220, though in a modified form); and a treatise on the responsibilities of various roles within the order (<i>Instructiones de officiis ordinis fratrum Praedicatorum</i>). He also composed a number of materials to help train Dominican preachers, including a collection of <i><a href="/wiki/Exemplum" title="Exemplum">exempla</a></i>, or sermon illustrations, entitled <i>De dono timoris;</i> a <a href="/wiki/Treatise" title="Treatise">treatise</a> <i>On Preaching</i> (<i>De eruditione praedicatorum</i>), instructing preachers with moral and practical advice; and a series of 'sketches' for use in composing sermons.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> One of these series contained outlines for 100 <a href="/w/index.php?title=Ad_status_sermons&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Ad status sermons (page does not exist)"><i>ad status</i> sermons</a>, detailing what should be preached to a great variety of different audiences, from hermits to cathedral canons, and from noblemen to maidservants.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Another series outlined 100 sermons for use on various occasions, both for religious occasions such as synods and ecclesiastical councils, and for secular occasions such as at tournaments or before a royal parliament.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Humbert also composed the <i>Opus tripartitum</i>, one of a number of texts by leading intellectuals commissioned by <a href="/wiki/Gregory_X" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory X">Gregory X</a> to be presented at the <a href="/wiki/Second_Council_of_Lyon" title="Second Council of Lyon">Second Council of Lyon</a> in 1274. This document defended church reform, discussed the relationship of the Church to Arabs, analyzed the causes and effects of the <a href="/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism" title="East–West Schism">East–West Schism</a>, proposed ways to go about the re-establishment of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> unity between the two wings of Christianity and promoted the mission to the heathens. It also touched on the <a href="/wiki/Recovery_of_the_Holy_Land" title="Recovery of the Holy Land">recovery of the Holy Land</a> and defended it against <a href="/wiki/Criticism_of_crusading" title="Criticism of crusading">criticism of crusading</a>.
</p>
