<p><b>Daniel Jules Alexis Brottier, <a href="/wiki/Holy_Ghost_Fathers" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Ghost Fathers">C.S.Sp.</a></b> (7 September 1876 – 28 February 1936), was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Ghost_Fathers" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Ghost Fathers">Congregation of the Holy Spirit</a> (who currently refer to themselves as Spiritans). He was awarded the <i><a href="/wiki/Croix_de_Guerre" title="Croix de Guerre">Croix de Guerre</a></i> and the <i><a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9gion_d%27honneur" class="mw-redirect" title="Légion d&#39;honneur">Légion d'honneur</a></i> for his services as a chaplain during <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a>, did missionary work in <a href="/wiki/Senegal" title="Senegal">Senegal</a>, and administered an orphanage in <a href="/wiki/Auteuil,_Paris" title="Auteuil, Paris">Auteuil</a>, a suburb of Paris. He was declared <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">venerable</a> in 1983, and then beatified on the 25 November 1984, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Brottier was born in <a href="/wiki/La_Fert%C3%A9-Saint-Cyr" title="La Ferté-Saint-Cyr">La Ferté-Saint-Cyr</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Communes_of_France" title="Communes of France">commune</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Loir-et-Cher" title="Loir-et-Cher">Loir-et-Cher</a> <a href="/wiki/Departments_of_France" title="Departments of France">Department</a> of France on 7 September 1876, the second son of Jean-Baptiste Brottier, coachman for the <a href="/wiki/Durfort_(family)" class="mw-redirect" title="Durfort (family)">Marquis Durfort</a>, and his wife Herminie (<i>née</i> Bouthe).<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> A story from his childhood recounts that his mother asked him what he would like to be when he grew up. Daniel's answer was, "I won't be either a general or a pastry chef—I will be the Pope!" His mother reminded him that to be the pope, he would first have to become a priest. Little Daniel piped up, "Well, then I'll become a priest!" <sup class="reference" id="ref_reference_name_A1"><a href="#endnote_reference_name_A1">[a]</a></sup> At the age of 10, Brottier made his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a>, and enrolled a year later in the <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a> at <a href="/wiki/Blois" title="Blois">Blois</a>. In 1896, at the age of 20, he did one year of military service at Blois.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> on 22 October 1899, after which he was assigned to teach for three years at a secondary school in <a href="/wiki/Pontlevoy" title="Pontlevoy">Pontlevoy</a>, France.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary_work_in_Africa">Missionary work in Africa</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Missionary work in Africa">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Daniel_Brottier_as_a_young_priest.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Daniel_Brottier_as_a_young_priest.jpg/275px-Daniel_Brottier_as_a_young_priest.jpg" decoding="async" width="275" height="184" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/Daniel_Brottier_as_a_young_priest.jpg/413px-Daniel_Brottier_as_a_young_priest.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1f/Daniel_Brottier_as_a_young_priest.jpg 2x" data-file-width="500" data-file-height="334" /></a><figcaption>A young Father Brottier in 1903, ready to set out for Senegal, posed for a picture with his parents, Jean-Baptiste and Herminie</figcaption></figure>
<p>Restless in his life as a teacher and determined to be a missionary, the young <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A9" title="Abbé">Abbé</a> Brottier joined the Congregation of the Holy Spirit at <a href="/wiki/Orly" title="Orly">Orly</a> in 1902. After completing his <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a>, Brottier was sent by the congregation to serve as a <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">vicar</a> in a mission <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Louis,_Senegal" title="Saint-Louis, Senegal">Saint-Louis, Senegal</a> in 1903.  He was disappointed that he had been assigned to a city rather than the more difficult interior.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nevertheless, Brottier immediately set to work. He gave weekly instructions to secondary school students, founded a center for child welfare, and published a <a href="/wiki/Parish_bulletin" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish bulletin">parish bulletin</a>, <i>The Echo of St. Louis</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His health suffered from the climate, however, and he spent a six-month period of convalescence in France in 1906.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1911 his poor health would force him to return to France for good.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After his final departure from Senegal, Brottier spent a brief, but personally significant, stay at the <a href="/wiki/Trappists" title="Trappists">Trappist</a> monastery at <a href="/wiki/L%C3%A9rins_Abbey" title="Lérins Abbey">Lérins</a>—the same island monastery associated with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>'s preparation for evangelization in Ireland. Brottier had felt called to a more contemplative life than he had been living as a missionary in Africa, but the stay at Lérins rid him of that idea. As Brottier wrote to his sisters, "I lived unforgettable hours in the recollection of the cloister in an atmosphere of sacrifice and immolation. But the lack of sleep, and especially of food, wore me down, and after a few days I had to yield to the evidence: I was not made for this kind of life".<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Even after he had left Senegal, Brottier was asked by Bishop Hyacinthe Jalabert, the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicar" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicar">Apostolic Vicar</a> of Senegal, to conduct a fund-raising campaign to build a cathedral in <a href="/wiki/Dakar" title="Dakar">Dakar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> To this end, Brottier was appointed the <a href="/wiki/Vicar_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Vicar General">Vicar General</a> of Dakar, even though he was residing in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Brottier focused on this project for seven years over two periods (i.e., 1911–1914 and 1919–1923), the interlude being a result of the <a href="/wiki/First_World_War" class="mw-redirect" title="First World War">First World War</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The so-called "<a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cath%C3%A9drale_du_Souvenir_africain_de_Dakar" class="extiw" title="fr:Cathédrale du Souvenir africain de Dakar">African Memorial Cathedral</a>" was consecrated on February 2, 1936, just a few weeks before Brottier's death.
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1062260506">.mw-parser-output .quotebox{background-color:#F9F9F9;border:1px solid #aaa;box-sizing:border-box;padding:10px;font-size:88%;max-width:100%}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatleft{margin:.5em 1.4em .8em 0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatright{margin:.5em 0 .8em 1.4em}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.centered{overflow:hidden;position:relative;margin:.5em auto .8em auto}.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatleft span,.mw-parser-output .quotebox.floatright span{font-style:inherit}.mw-parser-output .quotebox>blockquote{margin:0;padding:0;border-left:0;font-family:inherit;font-size:inherit}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-title{background-color:#F9F9F9;text-align:center;font-size:110%;font-weight:bold}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote>:first-child{margin-top:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote:last-child>:last-child{margin-bottom:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote.quoted:before{font-family:"Times New Roman",serif;font-weight:bold;font-size:large;color:gray;content:" “ ";vertical-align:-45%;line-height:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox-quote.quoted:after{font-family:"Times New Roman",serif;font-weight:bold;font-size:large;color:gray;content:" ” ";line-height:0}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .left-aligned{text-align:left}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .right-aligned{text-align:right}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .center-aligned{text-align:center}.mw-parser-output .quotebox .quote-title,.mw-parser-output .quotebox .quotebox-quote{display:block}.mw-parser-output .quotebox cite{display:block;font-style:normal}@media screen and (max-width:640px){.mw-parser-output .quotebox{width:100%!important;margin:0 0 .8em!important;float:none!important}}</style><div class="quotebox pullquote floatright" style="width:250px; ;">
<blockquote class="quotebox-quote left-aligned" style="">
<p>My secret is this: help yourself and heaven will help you. ... I have no other secret. If the good God worked miracles [at Auteuil], through Thérèse's intercession, I think I can say in all justice that we did everything, humanly speaking, to be deserving, and that they were the divine reward of our work, prayers and trust in providence.
</p>
</blockquote>
<p><cite class="left-aligned" style="">—Daniel Brottier<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup></cite>
</p>
</div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Service_during_World_War_I">Service during World War I</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Service during World War I">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>At the outbreak of the First World War, Brottier became a volunteer chaplain for France's <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/121e_r%C3%A9giment_d%27infanterie_de_ligne" class="extiw" title="fr:121e régiment d&#39;infanterie de ligne">121st Infantry Regiment</a>. He was cited six times for bravery, and awarded the <i>Croix de guerre</i> and the <i>Légion d'honneur</i>. He attributed his survival on the front lines to the intercession of St. <a href="/wiki/Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_of_Lisieux" title="Thérèse of Lisieux">Thérèse of Lisieux</a>, and built a chapel for her at <a href="/wiki/Auteuil,_Paris" title="Auteuil, Paris">Auteuil</a> when she was canonized:  the first church dedicated to the saint.<sup id="cite_ref-sanctoral_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sanctoral-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After the war, Brottier founded the <a href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Nationale_des_Combattants" class="extiw" title="fr:Union Nationale des Combattants">National Union of Servicemen</a> (<i>L'Union Nationale des Combattants</i>), an organization of French veterans of various conflicts.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Work_with_the_orphans_of_Auteuil">Work with the orphans of Auteuil</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Work with the orphans of Auteuil">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In November 1923, the Cardinal Archbishop of Paris, <a href="/wiki/Louis-Ernest_Dubois" title="Louis-Ernest Dubois">Louis-Ernest Dubois</a>, asked the Congregation of the Holy Spirit to assume charge of an orphanage in <a href="/wiki/16th_arrondissement_of_Paris" title="16th arrondissement of Paris">an arrondissement of Paris</a>, the Orphan Apprentices of Auteuil. Brottier, with his associate chaplain Yves Pichon, labored for 13 years to expand the facilities and worked for the welfare of the orphans. He dedicated his work to two aims: to save the most poor and unfortunate, and to dedicate those efforts to the intercession of Saint Thérèse.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In 1933, Brottier pioneered a program that placed the children in the households of Catholic <i><a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/paysan" class="extiw" title="wikt:paysan">paysans</a></i> associated with the Orphan Apprentices. The fruit of his labors at Auteuil included the construction of workshops, opening a printing house and a cinema, and launching magazines. At the time of his arrival, the facility was in charge of 140 orphans; when Brottier died, there were more than 1,400.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Particularly notable of Brottier's work with the orphans of Auteuil, and perhaps of his work in general, was his eagerness to expand to previously unexplored means of seeking financial support. An example of this is that he mastered the art of the camera and offered instruction on film making to the children. He even produced a popular film on the life of his personal patron, Saint Thérèse.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brottier died on 28 February 1936 in the Hospital of St. Joseph in Paris.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Fifteen thousand Parisians attended his <a href="/wiki/Funeral_Mass" class="mw-redirect" title="Funeral Mass">funeral Mass</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was buried in the Chapel of St. Thérèse in Auteuil on 5 April 1936.<sup id="cite_ref-chronologie_1-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-chronologie-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Brottier was <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">declared venerable</a> on 13 January 1983 with a decree of heroic virtue by Pope John Paul II. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by John Paul II in Paris on 25 November 1984.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The cause for his canonization was greatly advanced by the claim, in 1962, that his body was as intact as on the day of his burial.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritan_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In addition, many miracles have been attributed to his intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-spiritans_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-spiritans-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His feast day is celebrated by the Spiritan Fathers on 28 February.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Daniel_Brottier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>A residence hall at <a href="/wiki/Duquesne_University" title="Duquesne University">Duquesne University</a>—an American university founded and administered by the Spiritan Fathers—is named Brottier Hall in memory of Blessed Daniel Brottier.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Brottier Refugee Services is a Non Profit organization set up to assist private sponsors welcome refugees to Canada.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
