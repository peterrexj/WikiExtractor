<p><b>Pope Sylvester I</b> (also <b>Silvester</b>, 285 – 31 December 335) was the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> from 31 January 314 until his death.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He filled the see of Rome at an important era in the history of the <a href="/wiki/Western_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Western Church">Western Church</a>, yet very little is known of him.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The accounts of his pontificate preserved in the seventh- or eighth-century <span title="Latin-language text"><i lang="la"><a href="/wiki/Liber_Pontificalis" title="Liber Pontificalis">Liber Pontificalis</a></i></span> contain little more than a record of the gifts said to have been conferred on the church by <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> although it does say that he was the son of a Roman named Rufinus.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His feast is celebrated as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sylvester%27s_Day" title="Saint Sylvester&#39;s Day">Saint Sylvester's Day</a>, on 31 December in Western Christianity, and on 2 January in <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Christianity" title="Eastern Christianity">Eastern Christianity</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler1981_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler1981-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sylvester_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Large churches were founded and built during Sylvester I's pontificate, including <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_St._John_Lateran" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of St. John Lateran">Basilica of St. John Lateran</a>, <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Holy_Cross_in_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of the Holy Cross in Jerusalem">Basilica of the Holy Cross in Jerusalem</a>, <a href="/wiki/Old_St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="Old St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">Old St. Peter's Basilica</a> and several churches built over the graves of <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyrs</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sylvester did not attend the <a href="/wiki/First_Council_of_Nicaea" title="First Council of Nicaea">First Council of Nicaea</a> in 325, where the <a href="/wiki/Nicene_Creed" title="Nicene Creed">Nicene Creed</a> was formulated, but he was represented by two legates, Vitus and Vincentius, and he approved the council's decision.
</p><p>One of the <a href="/wiki/Symmachian_forgeries" title="Symmachian forgeries">Symmachian forgeries</a>, the <i><a href="/wiki/Constitutum_Silvestri" title="Constitutum Silvestri">Constitutum Silvestri</a></i>, is an apocryphal alleged account of a Roman council, which partially builds on legends in the <i><a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Sylvester" title="Acts of Sylvester">Acts of Sylvester</a></i> which has been preserved in Greek, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>, and in <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a> and the fictional stories of Sylvester's close relationship with the first Christian emperor. These also appear in the <i><a href="/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine" title="Donation of Constantine">Donation of Constantine</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sylvester_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Long after his death, the figure of Sylvester was embroidered upon in a fictional account of his relationship to Constantine, which seemed to successfully support the later <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gelasius_I" title="Pope Gelasius I">Gelasian doctrine</a> of <a href="/wiki/Papal_supremacy" title="Papal supremacy">papal supremacy</a>, papal <i>auctoritas</i> (authority) guiding imperial <i>potestas</i> (power), the doctrine that is embodied in the forged <i><a href="/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine" title="Donation of Constantine">Donation of Constantine</a></i> of the eighth century. In the fiction, of which an early version is represented in the early sixth-century <i><a href="/wiki/Symmachean_forgeries" class="mw-redirect" title="Symmachean forgeries">Symmachean forgeries</a></i> emanating from the curia of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Symmachus" title="Pope Symmachus">Pope Symmachus</a> (died 514), the Emperor Constantine was cured of <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leprosy</a> by the virtue of the baptismal water administered by Sylvester.<sup id="cite_ref-Russell1946_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Russell1946-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg/220px-Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="141" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg/330px-Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg/440px-Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg 2x" data-file-width="872" data-file-height="560" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sylvester_I_and_Constantine.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Pope Sylvester I and Constantine in a 1247 fresco</div></div></div>
<p>The Emperor, abjectly grateful, not only confirmed the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rome">bishop of Rome</a> as the primate above all other bishops, he resigned his imperial insignia and walked before Sylvester's horse holding the Pope's bridle as the papal groom. The Pope, in return, offered the crown of his own good will to Constantine, who abandoned Rome to the pope and took up residence in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.  "The doctrine behind this charming story is a radical one," <a href="/wiki/Norman_F._Cantor" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman F. Cantor">Norman F. Cantor</a> observes: "The pope is supreme over all rulers, even the Roman emperor, who owes his crown to the pope and therefore may be deposed by papal decree". The legend gained wide circulation; <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a> referred to this political legend in his history of the Franks, written in the 580s.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Sylvester_II" title="Pope Sylvester II">Pope Sylvester II</a>, himself a close associate of <a href="/wiki/Otto_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor">Otto III, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, chose the name <i>Sylvester</i> in imitation of Sylvester I.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-the_2nd_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-the_2nd-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg/220px-Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg/330px-Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg/440px-Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5184" data-file-height="3888" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Reliquie_di_san_silvestro_papa_nonantola_2020-08-06.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Relics of Saint Sylvester in <a href="/wiki/Territorial_Abbey_of_Nonantola" title="Territorial Abbey of Nonantola">the Abbey of Saint Sylvester</a> in <a href="/wiki/Nonantola" title="Nonantola">Nonantola</a></div></div></div>
<p>In the West, the liturgical feast of Saint Sylvester is on 31 December, the day of his burial in the <a href="/wiki/Catacomb_of_Priscilla" title="Catacomb of Priscilla">Catacomb of Priscilla</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-CathEnc_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CathEnc-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> This is now the last day in the year and, accordingly, in German-speaking countries and in some others close to them, <a href="/wiki/New_Year%27s_Eve" title="New Year&#39;s Eve">New Year's Eve</a> is known as <i><a href="/wiki/Silvester" class="mw-redirect" title="Silvester">Silvester</a></i>. In some other countries, too, the day is usually referred to as <i>Saint Sylvester's Day</i> or the <i>Feast of Saint Sylvester</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-jpost_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-jpost-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo" title="São Paulo">São Paulo</a>, Brazil, a long-distance running event called the <i><a href="/wiki/Saint_Silvester_Road_Race" title="Saint Silvester Road Race">Saint Silvester Road Race</a></i> occurs every year on 31 December.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Legendary">Legendary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sylvester_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legendary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Popesylvesterdragon.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Popesylvesterdragon.jpg/220px-Popesylvesterdragon.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="123" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Popesylvesterdragon.jpg/330px-Popesylvesterdragon.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Popesylvesterdragon.jpg/440px-Popesylvesterdragon.jpg 2x" data-file-width="723" data-file-height="405" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Popesylvesterdragon.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Pope Sylvester I portrayed slaying a dragon and resurrecting its victims, a <a href="/wiki/Fresco" title="Fresco">fresco</a> by <a href="/wiki/Maso_di_Banco" title="Maso di Banco">Maso di Banco</a></div></div></div>
<p>The <i><a href="/wiki/Donation_of_Constantine" title="Donation of Constantine">Donation of Constantine</a></i> is a document <a href="/wiki/Forgery" title="Forgery">fabricated</a> in the second half of the eighth century, purporting to be a record by the Emperor himself of his conversion, the profession of his new faith, and the privileges he conferred on Pope Sylvester I, his clergy, and their successors. According to it, Pope Sylvester was offered the imperial crown, which, however, he refused.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>"Lu Santu Papa Silvestru", a story in <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Pitr%C3%A8" title="Giuseppe Pitrè">Giuseppe Pitrè</a>'s collection of Sicilian fables, recounts the legend as follows: Constantine the king wants to take a second wife, and asks Sylvester.  Sylvester denies him permission, calling on heaven as witness; Constantine threatens him, and Sylvester, rather than give in, escapes into the woods.  Not long after, Constantine falls ill; when he is desperate of ever regaining his health he has a dream which commands him to send for Sylvester.  He obeys, and Sylvester receives Constantine's messengers in his cave and swiftly baptizes them, whereafter (having shown them several miracles) he is led back to Constantine, whom he baptizes also, and cures.  In this story, Constantine and his entourage are not pagans but Jews.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another legend has Sylvester slaying a dragon. He is often depicted with the dying beast.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Churches">Churches</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Sylvester_I&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Churches">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Among churches dedicated to St. Sylvester is <a href="/wiki/St._Sylvester,_Schwabing" title="St. Sylvester, Schwabing">St. Sylvester, Schwabing</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>, with wall paintings depicting scenes from the legends around him and <a href="/wiki/San_Silvestro_in_Capite" title="San Silvestro in Capite">San Silvestro in Capite</a> in Rome.
</p>
