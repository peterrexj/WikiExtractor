<p><b>Raymond Nonnatus</b>, <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary_of_Mercy" title="Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy">O. de M.</a> (<a href="/wiki/Catalan_language" title="Catalan language">Catalan</a>: <i lang="ca">Sant Ramon Nonat</i>, <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">San Ramón Nonato</i>, <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint Raymond Nonnat</i>, <a href="/wiki/Maltese_language" title="Maltese language">Maltese</a>: <i lang="mt">San Rajmondo Nonnato</i>), (1204 – 31 August 1240) is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> from <a href="/wiki/Catalonia" title="Catalonia">Catalonia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>. His nickname (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Nonnatus</i>, "not born") refers to his birth by <a href="/wiki/Caesarean_section" title="Caesarean section">Caesarean section</a>, his mother having died while giving birth to him.
</p><p>Raymond is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Childbirth" title="Childbirth">childbirth</a>, <a href="/wiki/Midwife" title="Midwife">midwives</a>, <a href="/wiki/Children" class="mw-redirect" title="Children">children</a>, pregnant women, and priests defending the confidentiality of confession.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_Nonnatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the traditions of the Mercedarian Order, he was born in the village of Portell (today part of <a href="/wiki/Sant_Ramon" title="Sant Ramon">Sant Ramon</a>), in the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Urgell" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Urgell">Diocese of Urgell</a>. He was taken from the womb of his mother after her death, hence his name.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some traditions describe him as the son of the local <a href="/wiki/Count" title="Count">count</a>, who is traditionally credited as the one to have performed the surgery which saved his life, others that he was born in a family of shepherds. His well-educated father planned a career for his son at the royal court of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Aragon" title="Kingdom of Aragon">Kingdom of Aragon</a>. When the boy felt drawn to religious life, his father ordered him to manage one of the family farms. What is known is that Raymond spent his childhood tending sheep and would often pray at an ancient country chapel nearby dedicated to <a href="/wiki/St._Nicholas" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Nicholas">St. Nicholas</a>. If he was of aristocratic descent, clearly his father eventually abandoned hopes for his son's social advancement.
</p><p>His father later gave him permission to take the habit with the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary_of_Mercy" title="Order of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Mercy">Mercedarians</a> at Barcelona.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The order was founded to ransom Christian captives from the <a href="/wiki/Moors" title="Moors">Moors</a> of <a href="/wiki/North_Africa" title="North Africa">North Africa</a>. Raymond was trained by the founder of that order himself, St. <a href="/wiki/Peter_Nolasco" title="Peter Nolasco">Peter Nolasco</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OM_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OM-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained a priest in 1222 and later became <a href="/wiki/Superior_general_(Christianity)" title="Superior general (Christianity)">Master General</a> of the Order.
</p><p>Raymond then set out to fulfill the goals of Order. He went to <a href="/wiki/Valencia" title="Valencia">Valencia</a>, where he ransomed 140 Christians from slavery. He then traveled to North Africa, where he was able to ransom another 250 captives in <a href="/wiki/Algiers" title="Algiers">Algiers</a>, and then went to <a href="/wiki/Tunis" title="Tunis">Tunis</a>, where he is said to have surrendered himself as a <a href="/wiki/Hostage" title="Hostage">hostage</a> for 28 captive Christians when his money ran out, in keeping with a special fourth <a href="/wiki/Religious_vows" title="Religious vows">vow</a> taken by the members of the order. He suffered in captivity as a legend states that the Moors bored a hole through his lips with a hot iron, and <a href="/wiki/Padlock" title="Padlock">padlocked</a> his mouth to prevent him from preaching. He was ransomed by his order and returned to Spain in 1239.<sup id="cite_ref-OM_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OM-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Raymond died at the <a href="/wiki/Castle_of_Cardona" title="Castle of Cardona">Castle of Cardona</a>, sixty miles from <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>, either on August 26<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> or on August 31, 1240.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> According to tradition, the local count, the friars and the town all claimed his body. To resolve this dispute, the body was placed on a blind mule, which was let loose. Unguided, it went to the nearby country chapel where he had prayed in his youth. It was there that he was buried.<sup id="cite_ref-OM_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OM-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Many miracles were attributed to him before and after his death.
</p><p>In the historiography and hagiography from the 16th century it is repeatedly claimed that upon his return to Spain in 1239, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_IX" title="Pope Gregory IX">Pope Gregory IX</a> nominated him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Deacon" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal Deacon">Cardinal Deacon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Sant%27Eustachio" title="Sant&#39;Eustachio">Sant'Eustachio</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and that he died en route to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Consequently, he is traditionally depicted as wearing the scarlet red <a href="/wiki/Mozzetta" title="Mozzetta">mozzetta</a> of a <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">cardinal</a>. However, Italian historian <a href="/wiki/Agostino_Paravicini_Bagliani" title="Agostino Paravicini Bagliani">Agostino Paravicini Bagliani</a> has established that this account resulted from a confusion of Raymond Nonnatus with Englishman <a href="/wiki/Robert_Somercotes" title="Robert Somercotes">Robert Somercote</a>, the Cardinal Deacon of S. Eustachio 1238–1241, and has concluded that Raymond was never a cardinal.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Raymond was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VII" title="Pope Alexander VII">Pope Alexander VII</a> in 1657. His feast day is celebrated on August 31.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_Nonnatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The towns of <a href="/wiki/Saint-Raymond,_Quebec" title="Saint-Raymond, Quebec">Saint-Raymond, Quebec</a>, Canada; <a href="/wiki/Or%C3%A1n,_Salta" class="mw-redirect" title="Orán, Salta">San Ramón de la Nueva Orán</a>, <a href="/wiki/Argentina" title="Argentina">Argentina</a>; <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Raimundo_Nonato" title="São Raimundo Nonato">São Raimundo Nonato</a>, Brazil; <a href="/wiki/San_Ram%C3%B3n_(canton)" title="San Ramón (canton)">San Ramón</a> in <a href="/wiki/Costa_Rica" title="Costa Rica">Costa Rica</a> are named for him. A shrine in <a href="/wiki/Buenos_Aires" title="Buenos Aires">Buenos Aires</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_S%C3%A3o_Raimundo_Nonato" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of São Raimundo Nonato">Roman Catholic Diocese of São Raimundo Nonato</a> (Raymundianus) in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a> are dedicated to him.
</p><p>In the United States, the <a href="/wiki/St._Raymond%27s_Church_(Bronx,_New_York)" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Raymond&#39;s Church (Bronx, New York)">Parish of St. Raymond</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/New_York_City" title="New York City">New York City</a> <a href="/wiki/Borough_(New_York)" class="mw-redirect" title="Borough (New York)">Borough</a> of <a href="/wiki/The_Bronx" title="The Bronx">the Bronx</a>; the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._Raymond_Nonnatus" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of St. Raymond Nonnatus">Cathedral of St. Raymond Nonnatus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Joliet,_Illinois" title="Joliet, Illinois">Joliet, Illinois</a>;<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>, St. Raymond Parish in Raymond, Illinois and St. Raymond Catholic Community in Downey, California;<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> are under his patronage. There is also a parish dedicated to him in <a href="/wiki/Juana_D%C3%ADaz,_Puerto_Rico" title="Juana Díaz, Puerto Rico">Juana Díaz, Puerto Rico</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG/250px-Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG" decoding="async" width="250" height="188" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG/375px-Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG/500px-Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3456" data-file-height="2592" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Altar_san_ramon_nonato.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Altar of Saint Raymond Nonnatus, <a href="/wiki/Mexico_City_Metropolitan_Cathedral" title="Mexico City Metropolitan Cathedral">Metropolitan Cathedral</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mexico_City" title="Mexico City">Mexico City</a></div></div></div>
<p>Due to the story of his own birth, Raymond quickly became widely invoked by women facing <a href="/wiki/Childbirth" title="Childbirth">childbirth</a>.
</p><p>Because of his limited historical importance, however, since the reforms of the Church calendar in 1969, the liturgical commemoration of Raymond's <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is no longer included among those to be <i>necessarily</i> observed wherever the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Rite" title="Roman Rite">Roman Rite</a> is celebrated,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> but, since he is included in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> for August 31, <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">Mass</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Liturgy_of_the_Hours" title="Liturgy of the Hours">Liturgy of the Hours</a> may be recited in his honor on that day as in the pre-1970 <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, which is observed by some <a href="/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Traditionalist Catholic">traditionalist Catholics</a>.
</p><p>One particular devotion is centered around the padlock that is part of his martyrdom. Locks are placed at his altar representing a prayer request to end gossip, rumours, false testimonies and other sins of the tongue. The locks are used as a visible sign of such prayer request, which first and foremost must take place interiorly, a prayer to God through St. Raymond's intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_Nonnatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He is pictured in the habit of his order surrounded by ransomed slaves, with a padlock on his butt.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_popular_culture">In popular culture</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_Nonnatus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: In popular culture">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The 2012 BBC drama series <i><a href="/wiki/Call_the_Midwife" title="Call the Midwife">Call the Midwife</a></i> features Nonnatus House, a convent of <a href="/wiki/Religious_sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious sisters">religious sisters</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a>; it is set in a deprived area of the <a href="/wiki/East_End" class="mw-redirect" title="East End">East End</a> of <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> in the 1950s. The series is based on the successful <a href="/wiki/Call_the_midwife_(book)" class="mw-redirect" title="Call the midwife (book)">memoir trilogy</a> of the same title, in which the author <a href="/wiki/Jennifer_Worth" title="Jennifer Worth">Jennifer Worth</a> used "Nonnatus House" as a pseudonym for the Anglican community of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_St_John_the_Divine" class="mw-redirect" title="Sisters of St John the Divine">Sisters of St John the Divine</a> in Whitechapel, where she actually worked.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> In the Christmas special,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> broadcast simultaneously on PBS<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> in America also, one of the plotlines features the discovery of an infant foundling on the convent doorstep, who is then dubbed <i>Raymond</i> by the nurses and sisters in honour of the closest male associated with his birth, the convent's patron. In the videogame <i><a href="/wiki/Broken_Sword:_The_Serpent%27s_Curse" class="mw-redirect" title="Broken Sword: The Serpent&#39;s Curse">Broken Sword: The Serpent's Curse</a></i> (2013-2014) San Ramon appears in a painting called "La Maledicció".
</p>
