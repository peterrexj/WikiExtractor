<p><b>Ansbert</b> (died c. 695), sometimes called <b>Ansbert of Chaussy</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> monk, abbot and <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Rouen">bishop of Rouen</a>, today regarded as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansbert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Ansbert was born at <a href="/wiki/Chaussy-sur-Epte" class="mw-redirect" title="Chaussy-sur-Epte">Chaussy-sur-Epte</a>, a village in the <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Norman</a> historical area known as the <a href="/wiki/Vexin" title="Vexin">Vexin</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Rupin_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rupin-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was born to a noble family, and was highly educated.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He had a significant professional career, and is said to have served as a senior member of the court of the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> king, <a href="/wiki/Clotaire_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotaire III">Clotaire III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As such, he was both <a href="/wiki/Lord_Chancellor_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Lord Chancellor of France">chancellor</a> and <a href="/wiki/R%C3%A9f%C3%A9rendaire_of_France" class="mw-redirect" title="Référendaire of France">referendary</a>.
</p><p>Ansbert was engaged to be <a href="/wiki/Christian_marriage" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian marriage">married</a> to another future saint, <a href="/wiki/Angadrisma" title="Angadrisma">Angadrisma</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Baudoin_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baudoin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her father, said to have been another of Clotaire's chancellors,<sup id="cite_ref-Baudoin_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baudoin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Arranged_marriage" title="Arranged marriage">arranged</a> for her to wed his colleague, but Angadrisma – later a <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patroness</a> of <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> – prayed for release from this obligation. Tradition states that dispensation was given to her after she was "struck down with <a href="/wiki/Leprosy" title="Leprosy">leprosy</a>",<sup id="cite_ref-Butler10_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler10-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> a disfiguring malady which only disappeared when she joined a <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Baudoin_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baudoin-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Butler10_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler10-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Some sources state that Ansbert later took a different bride.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler10_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler10-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Rabenstein_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rabenstein-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Religious_vocation">Religious vocation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansbert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Religious vocation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 673, the same year Clotaire died, Ansbert renounced his secular pursuits and became a monk of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine order">Benedictine order</a>. Six years later, he was elevated to abbot of his <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a>, the illustrious <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint_Wandrille" title="Abbey of Saint Wandrille">Fontenelle Abbey</a>. He followed two other saints in that office: <a href="/wiki/Wandregisel" title="Wandregisel">Wandrille</a>, the abbey's founder and first abbot, and <a href="/wiki/Lambert_of_Maastricht" title="Lambert of Maastricht">Lambert</a>, the second abbot, who vacated the office when he was named bishop of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Lyon" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Lyon">Lyons</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-COorg_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-COorg-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CSinfo_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CSinfo-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Under his leadership, Fontenelle prospered. His enterprises included a great expansion and refinement of the abbey's library,<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and the establishment of local hospitals for the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-StW1_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StW1-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During his time as abbot, Ansbert served as the <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> to King <a href="/wiki/Theodoric_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Theodoric III">Theodoric III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After several years, Ansbert was appointed <a href="/wiki/Archbishop" title="Archbishop">archbishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Rouen" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Rouen">Rouen</a> following the death of the previous officeholder, Saint <a href="/wiki/Ouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Ouen">Ouen</a>, in 683 or 684.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His former mentor Saint Lambert performed his consecration,<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and Ansbert was succeeded as abbot at Fontenelle by <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Hildebert&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Hildebert (page does not exist)">Hildebert</a> (d. 701), who is also venerated as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-StW2_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StW2-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Despite his high office and eminent reputation, Ansbert was removed from the bishopric around the year 690.<sup id="cite_ref-DaviesFouracre_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DaviesFouracre-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> By "a false accusation",<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> the powerful <i><a href="/wiki/Majordomo" title="Majordomo">majordomo</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> court, <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Heristal" class="mw-redirect" title="Pepin of Heristal">Pepin of Heristal</a>, arranged his dismissal, either because of some kind of political opposition<sup id="cite_ref-DaviesFouracre_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DaviesFouracre-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> or because Ansbert's "zeal was not well-received" and "his austere life caused offence".<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was sent into exile at the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Hautmont" title="Hautmont">Hautmont</a> where he stayed until his death, sometime between 692 and 695.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-DaviesFouracre_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DaviesFouracre-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> At some time Pepin apparently reconsidered his actions and agreed to allow Ansbert to return, but either he changed his mind again<sup id="cite_ref-DaviesFouracre_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-DaviesFouracre-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> or Ansbert died before making the trip.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_2-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ansbert&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The 12th-century chronicler <a href="/wiki/Ordericus_Vitalis" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordericus Vitalis">Ordericus Vitalis</a> relays a tale in which it was said that Ansbert's remains were desecrated and dispersed by soldiers of <a href="/wiki/Hugh_the_Great" title="Hugh the Great">Hugh the Great</a>. He asserts that the bones in question belonged to a different Ansbert, and that those of the saint were, at the time of his writing, still preserved safely at Fontenelle Abbey.<sup id="cite_ref-Forester_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Forester-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The 18th-century author <a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Alban Butler</a>, however, states that the remains were at some point transferred to <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Abbey,_Ghent" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Peter&#39;s Abbey, Ghent">St. Peter's Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, where they were destroyed by <a href="/wiki/Calvinist" class="mw-redirect" title="Calvinist">Calvinists</a> in 1578.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A collective day of remembrance for all the many saints associated with Fontenelle Abbey is celebrated on 1 March.<sup id="cite_ref-StW3_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StW3-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Saint Ansbert's own annual <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 9 February.<sup id="cite_ref-Rabenstein_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Rabenstein-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-StW2_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-StW2-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
