<p><b>Pope Alexander III</b> (c. 1100/1105 – 30 August 1181), born <b>Roland</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Rolando</i>), was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 7 September 1159 until his death in 1181.
</p><p>A native of <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, Alexander became <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a> after a <a href="/wiki/Papal_election,_1159" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal election, 1159">contested election</a>, but had to spend much of his pontificate outside <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> while several rivals, supported by Holy Roman Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I_Barbarossa" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I Barbarossa">Frederick I Barbarossa</a>, claimed the papacy. Alexander rejected Byzantine Emperor <a href="/wiki/Manuel_I_Komnenos" title="Manuel I Komnenos">Manuel I Komnenos</a>' offer to end the <a href="/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism" title="East–West Schism">East–West Schism</a>, sanctioned the <a href="/wiki/Northern_Crusades" title="Northern Crusades">Northern Crusades</a>, and held the <a href="/wiki/Third_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Third Council of the Lateran">Third Council of the Lateran</a>. The city of <a href="/wiki/Alessandria" title="Alessandria">Alessandria</a> in <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a> is named after him.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life_and_career">Early life and career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life and career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Rolando was born in <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>. From the 14th century, he was referred to as a member of the aristocratic family of <b>Bandinelli</b>, although this has not been proven.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was long thought to be the 12th-century canon lawyer and theologian Master Roland of Bologna, who composed the "Stroma" or "Summa Rolandi"—one of the earliest commentaries on the <i><a href="/wiki/Decretum_Gratiani" title="Decretum Gratiani">Decretum</a></i> of <a href="/wiki/Gratian_(jurist)" class="mw-redirect" title="Gratian (jurist)">Gratian</a>—and the "Sententiae Rolandi", a sentence collection displaying the influence of <a href="/wiki/Pierre_Ab%C3%A9lard" class="mw-redirect" title="Pierre Abélard">Pierre Abélard</a>, but John T. Noonan and Rudolf Weigand have shown this to be another Rolandus.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Alexander probably studied at Bologna, where <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Torigni" title="Robert of Torigni">Robert of Torigni</a> notes that he taught theology.<sup id="cite_ref-Pennington_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pennington-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In October 1150, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III" title="Pope Eugene III">Pope Eugene III</a> created him <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Deacon" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Deacon">Cardinal-Deacon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Santi_Cosma_e_Damiano" title="Santi Cosma e Damiano">Santi Cosma e Damiano</a>. Later he became <a href="/wiki/Cardinal-Priest" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal-Priest">Cardinal-Priest</a> of <a href="/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Marco_(Rome)" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica di San Marco (Rome)">St Mark</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Loughlin_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loughlin-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1153, he became papal <a href="/wiki/Chancellor" title="Chancellor">chancellor</a> and was the leader of the cardinals opposed to German Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I_Barbarossa" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I Barbarossa">Frederick I Barbarossa</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1156, he negotiated the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Benevento" title="Treaty of Benevento">Treaty of Benevento</a>, which restored peaceful relations between Rome and the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily" title="Kingdom of Sicily">Kingdom of Sicily</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At the <a href="/wiki/Diet_of_Besan%C3%A7on" title="Diet of Besançon">diet of Besançon</a> in October 1157, he and Cardinal <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernard_of_San_Clemente&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bernard of San Clemente (page does not exist)">Bernard of San Clemente</a> delivered a papal message that seemed to claim that Frederick I was a <a href="/wiki/Papal_fief" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal fief">papal vassal</a>, resulting in a serious diplomatic incident.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Disputed_election">Disputed election</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Disputed election">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/1159_papal_election" title="1159 papal election">1159 papal election</a></div>
<p>On 7 September 1159, Alexander III was <a href="/wiki/1159_papal_election" title="1159 papal election">chosen</a> to succeed <a href="/wiki/Adrian_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Adrian IV">Adrian IV</a> as <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">pope</a>. A minority of the cardinals, however, elected the cardinal priest Octavian, who assumed the name of <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159-1164)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159-1164)">Victor IV</a> and became the German Emperor's <a href="/wiki/Antipope" title="Antipope">antipope</a>. The situation was critical for Alexander III, because according to many chronicles of the time (perhaps exaggerating), Barbarossa's antipope received the approval of most of the kingdoms of Europe, with the exception of the kingdoms of Portugal, Sicily, Aragon and Castile. However, in 1161, King <a href="/wiki/G%C3%A9za_II_of_Hungary" title="Géza II of Hungary">Géza II of Hungary</a> <a href="/wiki/Concordat_of_1161" title="Concordat of 1161">signed an agreement</a> and recognised Alexander III as the rightful pope and declared that the supreme spiritual leader was the only one who could exercise the rite of <a href="/wiki/Investiture" title="Investiture">investiture</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> This meant that Alexander's legitimacy was gaining strength, as soon proved by the fact that other monarchs, such as the king of France and King <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II of England</a>, recognized his authority. Because of imperial strength in Italy, Alexander was forced to reside outside Rome for a large part of his pontificate.<sup id="cite_ref-Pennington_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pennington-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When news reached him of the death of Victor in 1164, he openly wept, and scolded the cardinals in his company for rejoicing at the end of the rival antipope.<sup id="cite_ref-mir_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mir-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>However, the dispute between Alexander III, <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159-1164)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159-1164)">Victor IV</a> and Victor's successors <a href="/wiki/Paschal_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Paschal III">Paschal III</a> and <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Calixtus_III" class="mw-redirect" title="Antipope Calixtus III">Calixtus III</a> (who had the German imperial support) continued until Frederick Barbarossa's defeat at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano" title="Battle of Legnano">Legnano</a> in 1176, after which Barbarossa finally (in the <a href="/wiki/Peace_of_Venice" class="mw-redirect" title="Peace of Venice">Peace of Venice</a> of 1177) recognized Alexander III as pope.<sup id="cite_ref-Loughlin_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Loughlin-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> On 12 March 1178, Alexander III returned to Rome, which he had been compelled to leave twice: the first time between 1162 and 23 November 1165. When Alexander was arrested by supporters of the imperialist <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Victor_IV_(1159-1164)" class="mw-redirect" title="Antipope Victor IV (1159-1164)">Victor IV</a>, he was freed by <a href="/wiki/Oddone_Frangipane" title="Oddone Frangipane">Oddone Frangipane</a>, who sent him to safety in the <a href="/wiki/Campania" title="Campania">Campania</a>.  Alexander again left Rome in 1167, travelling first to <a href="/wiki/Benevento" title="Benevento">Benevento</a> and later moving to various strongholds such as those of <a href="/wiki/Anagni" title="Anagni">Anagni</a>, <a href="/wiki/Palestrina" title="Palestrina">Palestrina</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ferentino" title="Ferentino">Ferentino</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tusculum" title="Tusculum">Tusculum</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Veroli" title="Veroli">Veroli</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pennington_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pennington-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pontificate">Pontificate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Pontificate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Politics">Politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Politics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_(FindID_227560).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_%28FindID_227560%29.jpg/220px-Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_%28FindID_227560%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="129" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_%28FindID_227560%29.jpg/330px-Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_%28FindID_227560%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_%28FindID_227560%29.jpg/440px-Medieval_Papal_Bull_of_Pope_Alexander_III_%28FindID_227560%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1500" data-file-height="878" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Bulla_(seal)" title="Bulla (seal)">Bulla</a> of Alexander III.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Alexander III was the first pope known to have paid direct attention to missionary activities east of the Baltic Sea. He had created the <a href="/wiki/Archbishopric_of_Uppsala" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishopric of Uppsala">Archbishopric of Uppsala</a> in Sweden in 1164,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> probably at the suggestion of his close friend Archbishop <a href="/wiki/Eskil_of_Lund" title="Eskil of Lund">Eskil of Lund</a> – exiled in <a href="/wiki/Ville-sous-la-Fert%C3%A9" title="Ville-sous-la-Ferté">Clairvaux</a>, France, due to a conflict with the Danish king. The latter appointed a Benedictine monk <a href="/wiki/Bishop_Fulco" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop Fulco">Fulco</a> as a bishop in <a href="/wiki/Estonia" title="Estonia">Estonia</a>. In 1171, Alexander became the first pope to address the situation of the Church in <a href="/wiki/Finland" title="Finland">Finland</a>, with Finns allegedly harassing priests and only relying on God in time of war.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In the bull <i><a href="/wiki/Non_parum_animus_noster" title="Non parum animus noster">Non parum animus noster</a></i>, in 1171 or 1172, he gave papal sanction to ongoing <a href="/wiki/Northern_Crusades" title="Northern Crusades">crusades against pagans in northern Europe</a>, promising remission of sin for those who fought there. In doing so, he legitimized the widespread use of <a href="/wiki/Forced_conversion" title="Forced conversion">forced conversion</a> as a tactic by those fighting in the Baltic.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1166, Alexander received an embassy from the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Manuel_I_Komnenos" title="Manuel I Komnenos">Manuel I</a>. The Byzantine ambassador, the <i><a href="/wiki/Sebastos" title="Sebastos">sebastos</a></i> Iordanos,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> relayed that Manuel would end the <a href="/wiki/Great_Schism_of_1054" class="mw-redirect" title="Great Schism of 1054">Great Schism</a> of the eastern and western churches if Alexander would recognize him as emperor. As emperor, Manuel would supply the pope with men and money to restore his authority in Italy. Alexander gave an evasive answer, but in 1168 he rejected outright the same proposal from a second Byzantine embassy. His stated reason amounted to it being too difficult. He appears to have feared Byzantine domination of Italy if the pope owed his position to its support.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Besides checkmating Barbarossa, Alexander humbled King Henry II of England for the murder of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a> in 1170, to whom he was unusually close, later <a href="/wiki/Canonizing" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonizing">canonizing</a> Becket in 1173.<sup id="cite_ref-Norton2006p193_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norton2006p193-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> This was the second English saint canonized by Alexander, the first being <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor" title="Edward the Confessor">Edward the Confessor</a> in 1161.<sup id="cite_ref-Norton2006p193_17-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Norton2006p193-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Nonetheless, he confirmed the position of Henry as <a href="/wiki/Lordship_of_Ireland" title="Lordship of Ireland">Lord of Ireland</a> in 1172.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:ManifestisProbatum.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/ManifestisProbatum.jpg/170px-ManifestisProbatum.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="223" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/ManifestisProbatum.jpg/255px-ManifestisProbatum.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/ManifestisProbatum.jpg/340px-ManifestisProbatum.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3076" data-file-height="4027" /></a><figcaption><i>Manifestis Probatum</i>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Through the <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Manifestis_Probatum" title="Manifestis Probatum">Manifestis Probatum</a></i>, issued on 23 May 1179, Alexander recognized the right of <a href="/wiki/Afonso_I_of_Portugal" title="Afonso I of Portugal">Afonso I</a> to proclaim himself <a href="/wiki/King_of_Portugal" class="mw-redirect" title="King of Portugal">King of Portugal</a> – an important step in the process of Portugal becoming a recognized independent kingdom. Afonso had been using the title of king since 1139.<sup id="cite_ref-Janet_Laughland_Nelson_2001_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Janet_Laughland_Nelson_2001-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Efforts_at_reform">Efforts at reform</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Efforts at reform">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:B_alexander_III2_(cropped).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/B_alexander_III2_%28cropped%29.jpg/170px-B_alexander_III2_%28cropped%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="170" height="268" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/B_alexander_III2_%28cropped%29.jpg/255px-B_alexander_III2_%28cropped%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/B_alexander_III2_%28cropped%29.jpg/340px-B_alexander_III2_%28cropped%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="368" data-file-height="580" /></a><figcaption>Alexander III depicted in a fresco by <a href="/wiki/Spinello_Aretino" title="Spinello Aretino">Spinello Aretino</a> <small>(before 1410, <a href="/wiki/Palazzo_Pubblico" title="Palazzo Pubblico">Palazzo Pubblico</a> in <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>)</small></figcaption></figure>
<p>Even as a fugitive, Alexander enjoyed the favour and protection of <a href="/wiki/Louis_VII_of_France" title="Louis VII of France">Louis VII of France</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (September 2017)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>In 1163 Alexander summoned clergy and prelates from England, France, Italy, and Spain to the Council of Tours to address, among other things, the unlawful division of ecclesiastical benefices, clerical usury, and lay possession of tithes.<sup id="cite_ref-Pennington_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pennington-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In March 1179, Alexander III held the <a href="/wiki/Third_Council_of_the_Lateran" title="Third Council of the Lateran">Third Council of the Lateran</a>, one of the most important mediaeval church councils, reckoned by the Catholic Church as the eleventh <a href="/wiki/Ecumenical_council" title="Ecumenical council">ecumenical council</a>. Its acts embodied several of the Pope's proposals for the betterment of the condition of the Church, among them the law requiring that no one could be elected pope without the votes of two-thirds of the cardinals.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The rule was altered slightly in 1996, but was restored in 2007. The period from 1177, which saw the submission of both emperor Frederick and anti-pope Calixtus III, and this synod/council marked the summit of Alexander III's power.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nevertheless, soon after the close of the synod, the <a href="/wiki/Commune_of_Rome" title="Commune of Rome">Roman Republic</a> forced Alexander III to leave the city, which he never re-entered, and on 29 September 1179, some nobles set up the <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Innocent_III" title="Antipope Innocent III">Antipope Innocent III</a>. By the judicious use of money, however, Alexander III got him into his power, so that he was deposed in January 1180. In 1181, Alexander III <a href="/wiki/Excommunication" title="Excommunication">excommunicated</a> King <a href="/wiki/William_I_of_Scotland" class="mw-redirect" title="William I of Scotland">William I of Scotland</a> and put the kingdom under an <a href="/wiki/Interdict" title="Interdict">interdict</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ecclesial_activities">Ecclesial activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Ecclesial activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Cardinals_created_by_Alexander_III" title="Cardinals created by Alexander III">Cardinals created by Alexander III</a></div>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:San_giovanni_in_laterano,_interno,_navata_interna_dx,_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi,_1658-59.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/San_giovanni_in_laterano%2C_interno%2C_navata_interna_dx%2C_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi%2C_1658-59.jpg/200px-San_giovanni_in_laterano%2C_interno%2C_navata_interna_dx%2C_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi%2C_1658-59.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="300" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/San_giovanni_in_laterano%2C_interno%2C_navata_interna_dx%2C_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi%2C_1658-59.jpg/300px-San_giovanni_in_laterano%2C_interno%2C_navata_interna_dx%2C_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi%2C_1658-59.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/San_giovanni_in_laterano%2C_interno%2C_navata_interna_dx%2C_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi%2C_1658-59.jpg/400px-San_giovanni_in_laterano%2C_interno%2C_navata_interna_dx%2C_sepolcro_di_alessandro_III_con_scultura_di_domenico_guidi%2C_1658-59.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2304" data-file-height="3456" /></a><figcaption>The tomb of Pope Alexander III in the Basilica of Saint John Lateran</figcaption></figure>
<p>Throughout his pontificate, Alexander III elevated 68 cardinals in fifteen consistories which included two of his successors, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_III" title="Pope Urban III">Urban III</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_III" title="Pope Clement III">Clement III</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Galdino_della_Sala" class="mw-redirect" title="Galdino della Sala">a cardinal</a> whom he would later canonize as a saint.
</p><p>Alexander III beatified no one during his papacy but he did canonize ten saints which included notable figures of the age such as <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Alexander_III&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He died at <a href="/wiki/Civita_Castellana" title="Civita Castellana">Civita Castellana</a> on 30 August 1181. It was one week short of twenty-two years since his election, the longest papacy since <a href="/wiki/Pope_Adrian_I" title="Pope Adrian I">Adrian I</a> in the 8th century.
</p>
