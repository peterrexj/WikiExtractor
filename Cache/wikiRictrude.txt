					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p><b>Rictrude</b> (Rictrudis, Richtrudis, Richrudis) (c. 614–688) was abbess of <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes_Abbey" title="Marchiennes Abbey">Marchiennes Abbey</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>. The main early source for her life is the <i>Vita Rictrudis</i>, commissioned by the abbey, and written in 907 by <a href="/wiki/Hucbald" title="Hucbald">Hucbald</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rictrude&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>She was from a noble family in <a href="/wiki/Gascony" title="Gascony">Gascony</a>, the daughter of Ernoldo, lord of Toulouse.<sup id="cite_ref-santi_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-santi-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She married <a href="/wiki/Adalbard" title="Adalbard">Adalbard</a>, Duke of Douai, despite the opposition of some members of her family. In Hucbald's account, this is shown deliberately as a model marriage.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It is unclear whether she had four or five children. Her four known children were: <a href="/wiki/Clotsinda" class="mw-redirect" title="Clotsinda">Clotsinda</a>, successor as abbess,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Adalsinda" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalsinda">Adalsinda</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eusebia and <a href="/wiki/Maurantius" class="mw-redirect" title="Maurantius">Maurantius</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The couple opened their castle to the poor and disadvantaged. The hermit-monk <a href="/wiki/Richarius" title="Richarius">Richarius</a> was a family friend.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Around the year 630, Adalbald founded <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes_Abbey" title="Marchiennes Abbey">Marchiennes Abbey</a>, on the advice of <a href="/wiki/Amandus" title="Amandus">Amandus</a> of Maastricht. In 643, Rictrude made it a <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a>.
</p><p>Adalbard was murdered in obscure circumstances around 652, near Périgueux during a subsequent expedition to Aquitaine, probably by his wife's relatives still bitter about the marriage to an enemy of her people. After her husband's death, Rictrude resisted royal pressure to remarry and retired to Marchiennes Abbey, with her daughters and became abbess.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> In this she was supported by Amandus, Abbot of Elnon. Rectrude died in 688.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Rictrude is recognized as a Catholic saint; her feast day is 12 May. Her four children are also saints.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Clotsinda">Clotsinda</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Rictrude&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Clotsinda">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born around 638, Clotsinda was a younger daughter of Rictrude and <a href="/wiki/Adalbard" title="Adalbard">Adalbard</a> duke of <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> In 688, she succeeded her mother as the second abbess of the <a href="/wiki/Double_monastery" title="Double monastery">double monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Marchiennes_Abbey" title="Marchiennes Abbey">Marchiennes Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> She died around 714.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
Her siblings <a href="/wiki/Maurontius_of_Douai" title="Maurontius of Douai">Maurontius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Adalsinda" class="mw-redirect" title="Adalsinda">Adalsinda</a> and Eusebia are also honored as saints.
</p><p>Her feast day is of May 5; Closinda is especially venerated in <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> In the Orthodox faith, she is commemorated on June 30.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
