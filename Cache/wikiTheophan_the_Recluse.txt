<p><b>Theophan the Recluse</b>, also known as <b>Theophan Zatvornik</b> or <b>Theophanes the Recluse</b> (Russian: Святитель Феофан Затворник Вышенский, епископ Тамбовский;<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> January 10, 1815 – January 6, 1894), is a well-known <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theophan_the_Recluse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theophan the Recluse, also known as Theophan Zatvornik or Theophanes the Recluse or (in the Library of Congress Name Authority File, and therefore in many library catalogs) as "Ḟeofan, Saint, Bishop of Tambov and Shatsk, 1815–1894", was born on January 10, 1815, as Georgy Vasilievich Govorov (<span title="Russian-language text"><span lang="ru">Георгий Васильевич Говоров</span></span>), in the village of <a href="/wiki/Izmalkovsky_District" title="Izmalkovsky District">Chernavsk</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Oryol_Governorate" title="Oryol Governorate">Oryol Governorate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Empire" title="Russian Empire">Russian Empire</a>. His father was a Russian Orthodox priest. He was educated in the seminaries at <a href="/wiki/Livny" title="Livny">Livny</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oryol" title="Oryol">Oryol</a> and <a href="/wiki/Kyiv" title="Kyiv">Kyiv</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Career">Career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theophan_the_Recluse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1841 he was ordained, became a <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monk</a>, and adopted the name Theophan. He later became the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Bishop_of_Tambov&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Bishop of Tambov (page does not exist)">Bishop of Tambov</a>.
</p><p>He is especially well-known today through the many books he wrote concerning the spiritual life, especially on the subjects of the Christian life and the training of youth in the faith. He also played an important role in translating the <i><a href="/wiki/Philokalia" title="Philokalia">Philokalia</a></i> from <a href="/wiki/Church_Slavonic" title="Church Slavonic">Church Slavonic</a> into Russian. The <i>Philokalia</i> is a classic of Orthodox <a href="/wiki/Spirituality" title="Spirituality">spirituality</a>, composed of the collected works of a number of <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Church Fathers</a> which were edited and placed in a four volume set in the 17th and 18th centuries. A persistent theme is developing an interior life of continuous prayer,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> learning to "pray without ceasing" as <a href="/wiki/Paul_of_Tarsus" class="mw-redirect" title="Paul of Tarsus">St. Paul</a> teaches in his <a href="/wiki/First_Epistle_to_the_Thessalonians" title="First Epistle to the Thessalonians">first letter to the Thessalonians</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theophan_the_Recluse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theophan the Recluse died on January 6, 1894, and lay in state for three days in his church. Even after that length of time there was no sign of decay in his unembalmed body. He was buried in the Kazan church of the Vysha Monastery.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Spiritual_Life_and_How_to_Be_Attuned_To_It"><i>The Spiritual Life and How to Be Attuned To It</i></span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theophan_the_Recluse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: The Spiritual Life and How to Be Attuned To It">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><i>The Spiritual Life and How to Be Attuned To It</i> was originally written in response to Theophan's encounter with a young woman. While at a ball, this upper class Moscow woman began having irrational thoughts about the meaning of life and the immortality of man. After contacting Theophan, the two began corresponding through letters, the lady writing on her spiritual difficulties and Theophan responding with spiritual advice. This correspondence had a significant impact on the woman; she later became a nun.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The Saint Herman Press, the publisher of the illustrated edition of <i>The Spiritual Life and How to Be Attuned to It</i>, notes that it was of great importance to Theophan that the young woman should "be able to keenly hear the right 'tone' of spiritual life".<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_as_a_saint">Veneration as a saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theophan_the_Recluse&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration as a saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Theophan was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by the Local Council of the Russian Orthodox Church of 1988.  The act of canonization declared that his "deep theological understanding of the Christian teaching, as well as its performance in practice, and, as a consequence of this, the loftiness and holiness of the life of the <i>sviatitel'</i> allow for his writings to be regarded as a development of the teaching of the Holy Fathers, preserving the same Orthodox purity and Divine enlightenment." His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated January 6 or January 10.
</p>
