<p><b>Constantine of Murom</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: Святой Блаженный <a href="/wiki/Knyaz" title="Knyaz">Князь</a> Константин) (11th century - 1129) known as <b>Saint Constantine the Blessed</b> was a direct descendant of <a href="/wiki/Vladimir_I_of_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Vladimir I of Kiev">Vladimir I of Kiev</a> and the son of <a href="/wiki/Svyatoslav_II_of_Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Svyatoslav II of Kiev">Prince Svyatoslav</a> of <a href="/wiki/Chernigov" class="mw-redirect" title="Chernigov">Chernigov</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Constantine_of_Murom&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At Constantine's request, his father assigned him to rule the city of Murom, which at the time was inhabited by pagans, that he might spread <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> in that region. According to the traditional account of his life, Constantine first sent his son Michael to Murom as his emissary, but the inhabitants threw him from the walls, so Constantine was obliged to take the city by force.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  However, he made no headway in converting them to Christianity and at one point they became so angry that a mob made their way to his fortress intending to storm the place. According to legend, when he appeared before them bearing what is now known as the <a href="/wiki/Murom" title="Murom">Murom</a>-<a href="/wiki/Ryazan" title="Ryazan">Ryazan</a> <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a>, they quieted down and bowed reverently before the holy image.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His son Fyodor, with his support, continued the missionary work in the surrounding countryside.
</p><p>At the place of the murder of his son Michael, the wooden church of the Annunciation was reportedly founded by Prince Constantine in 1205.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A new cathedral was built on the site in 1553. It became a monastery under Ivan the Terrible and was ruined by the Poles during the Time of Troubles. In 1664 it was rebuilt by wealthy merchant and arts patron Tarasy Tsvetnov. The monastery features the relics of Constantin and his sons Mikhail and Fyodor. According to a legend recorded by local aristocrat and amateur historian Alexander Yepanchin, each midnight the monastery's gates disappear, and Constantine, Mikhail and Fyodor, clad in regal attire, ride out in a gilded carriage and head to the Cathedral of the Nativity of Mary, where they are met by Pyotr and Fevronia. After praying there, they guard and patrol the city.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Mother_of_God.2C_.22The_Star_Most_Bright.22"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Mother_of_God,_&quot;The_Star_Most_Bright&quot;">Mother of God, "The Star Most Bright"</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Constantine_of_Murom&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Mother of God, &quot;The Star Most Bright&quot;">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg/220px-Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="274" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg/330px-Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg/440px-Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg 2x" data-file-width="643" data-file-height="800" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Theotokos_the_Most_Bright_Star.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Theotokos the Most Bright Star</div></div></div>
<p>The icon shows a full-length representation of the Mother of God with a star sending forth rays of light in the background. The origin of its name is connected with the poetic hymns in honor of the Theotokos. Standing on her right hand is the infant Jesus. At her feet are Murom saints: Constantine, Mikhail and Fyodor arrayed in princely robes and <a href="/wiki/Peter_and_Fevronia_of_Murom" title="Peter and Fevronia of Murom">Peter, Fevronia</a> and Uliania clad in monastic habits. In the bottom part of the icon is the following inscription: 'This Marvelous Image Is the Star Most Bright of the Most Holy Lady Mother of God Queen of Heaven."<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Constantine_of_Murom&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 1547 <a href="/wiki/Macarius,_Metropolitan_of_Moscow" title="Macarius, Metropolitan of Moscow">Metropolitan Macarius of Moscow</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> Constantine as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Makaryev_Monastery" title="Makaryev Monastery">Monastery of Makaryev</a>, He is commemorated in the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> on <a href="/wiki/May_21_(Eastern_Orthodox_liturgics)" title="May 21 (Eastern Orthodox liturgics)">May 21</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His wife Irene is also venerated at Murom.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
