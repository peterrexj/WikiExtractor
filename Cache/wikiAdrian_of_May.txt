					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
Saint <b>Adrian of May</b> (sometimes given as "Magridin") (d. 875) was a martyr-saint of ancient <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, whose <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cult</a> became popular in the 14th century. He is commemorated on 3 December. He may have been a bishop of <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_St_Andrews" title="Archbishop of St Andrews">Saint Andrews</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_martyrdom">Life and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_of_May&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory,_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory%2C_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg/220px-Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory%2C_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="143" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory%2C_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg/330px-Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory%2C_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory%2C_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg/440px-Saint_Adrian%27s_Priory%2C_Isle_of_May_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1055460.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="415" /></a><figcaption>St Mary the Virgin Priory, Isle of May</figcaption></figure>
<p>Little is known of the life of this <a href="/wiki/Scottish_people" title="Scottish people">Scottish</a> saint and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. He is held by some to have been an <a href="/wiki/Irish_people" title="Irish people">Irish</a> <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>, with the <a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Gaelic</a> name of <a href="/wiki/Ethernan" title="Ethernan">Ethernan</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-keys_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keys-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> who, though he might have been the Bishop of St. Andrews, was drawn to remote locations and had built a series of monasteries and hermitages on the <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_May" title="Isle of May">Isle of May</a> (which is five miles (8&#160;km) out to sea in the <a href="/wiki/Firth_of_Forth" title="Firth of Forth">Firth of Forth</a>) and along the coast of <a href="/wiki/Fife" title="Fife">Fife</a>. Later he withdrew from his see of <a href="/wiki/St._Andrews" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Andrews">St. Andrews</a> due to the invading <a href="/wiki/Danes" title="Danes">Danes</a> and took refuge on the island.
</p><p>What is known is that about A.D. 875, marauding <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Vikings</a> invaded the island of May. They then slaughtered the entire population of the monastery, traditionally numbered at six thousand six hundred.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The island was then abandoned for centuries.
</p><p>In 1145, King <a href="/wiki/David_I_of_Scotland" title="David I of Scotland">David I of Scotland</a> gave the island to <a href="/wiki/Reading_Abbey" title="Reading Abbey">Reading Abbey</a> in <a href="/wiki/Berkshire" title="Berkshire">Berkshire</a>, <a href="/wiki/England" title="England">England</a>, at which point, the island again became a religious centre. The English monks started the erection of a small <a href="/wiki/Isle_of_May_Priory" title="Isle of May Priory">monastery</a> dedicated to St. Mary the Virgin, with a shrine to St. Ethernan.<sup id="cite_ref-Archaeology_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Archaeology-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Early building was hampered due to raiding parties of Scandinavians who had settled in <a href="/wiki/Orkney" title="Orkney">Orkney</a>. The privations and isolation of the location finally led the monks to transfer the island in some manner to the Bishop of St. Andrews in A.D. 1288. Shortly thereafter, in 1296, war broke out between the Kingdoms of Scotland and England over territorial claims along the border between the two realms. This was paralleled in a legal fight between the abbey and the bishop over who actually owned the island. Initially, the abbey was confirmed as the lawful owner. This, however, was overturned in 1313 and the island was declared a part of the <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocese</a>. In consequence of this, English forces attacked the island and destroyed the monastery. After the conclusion of hostilities, the island became an important symbol of national pride, and pilgrimages to May became a common feature of religious life for the Scottish people.<sup id="cite_ref-Archaeology_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Archaeology-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Royal_pilgrims">Royal pilgrims</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_of_May&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Royal pilgrims">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to the chronicle of <a href="/wiki/Mathieu_d%27Escouchy" title="Mathieu d&#39;Escouchy">Mathieu d'Escouchy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Guelders" title="Mary of Guelders">Mary of Guelders</a> visited the Isle of May and the shrine of St Adrian when she first arrived in Scotland in 1449.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He mentions that the cemetery contained the bodies of many holy men, and was surrounded by a high wall.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/James_IV_of_Scotland" title="James IV of Scotland">James IV</a> came on 3 May 1504, and 10 May 1506 on board the <i><a href="/wiki/Scottish_warship_Margaret" title="Scottish warship Margaret">Margaret</a></i>. The <a href="/wiki/Treasurer_of_Scotland" title="Treasurer of Scotland">royal treasurer's accounts</a> mention that James IV wore new yellow breeches. The clerks of the Chapel Royal sang on the island, and James supported a hermit.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 24 August 1539 <a href="/wiki/Mary_of_Guise" title="Mary of Guise">Mary of Guise</a> and <a href="/wiki/James_V_of_Scotland" title="James V of Scotland">James V</a> made a pilgrimage to the Isle of May. They took three ships, the <i>Unicorn</i>, the <i>Little Unicorn</i>, and the <i><a href="/wiki/English_ship_Mary_Willoughby" title="English ship Mary Willoughby">Mary Willoughby</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> It was believed that a visit to the shrine of St Adrian could help a woman become pregnant.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> In October 1540 James V commissioned a reliquary for a bone of St Adrian of May from the court goldsmith John Mosman, to be made from Scottish gold.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Post-Reformation">Post-Reformation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_of_May&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Post-Reformation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>When the <a href="/wiki/Scottish_Reformation" title="Scottish Reformation">Scottish Reformation</a> took hold in the 16th century, public devotion to the saints—and thus pilgrimages to the site—came to a halt and the Protestant bishop of St. Andrews soon decided to sell the island into private ownership. Slowly the island sank into ruin, with most of the surviving monastic buildings disappearing. Recently the island has become the site of archaeological excavations seeking the remains of the original monastic community which died at the hands of the Vikings.<sup id="cite_ref-keys_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-keys-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_of_May&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Ethernan">Ethernan</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Adrian_of_May&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Ethernan">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Ethernan was a 7th century Scottish monk martyred by the Picts and believed to be buried on the Isle of May. which became the centre of his <a href="/wiki/Cult_of_saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Cult of saints">cult</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-:1_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:1-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He was honored in a number of places in Scotland, including <a href="/wiki/Madderty" title="Madderty">Madderty</a> and <a href="/wiki/Aberdeenshire_(historic)" title="Aberdeenshire (historic)">Aberdeenshire</a><sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> It appears that <a href="/wiki/Pilgrim" title="Pilgrim">pilgrims</a> came to the Isle of May to pray at his shrine for healing.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At some point during the Middle Ages, Ethernan got conflated with Adrian of May, whose shrine attracted pilgrims for the next several centuries. His cult is most likely a misremembering of Ethernan from a time when the Picts had ceased to function as an ethnic group within Scotland and ancient martyrdoms in Britain and Ireland were commonly attributed to Vikings.<sup id="cite_ref-:2_13-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:2-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
