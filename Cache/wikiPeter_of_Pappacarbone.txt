<p><b>Peter of Pappacarbone</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">San Pietro di Pappacarbone</i>) (died 4 March 1123) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.  He was abbot of <a href="/wiki/La_Trinit%C3%A0_della_Cava" title="La Trinità della Cava">La Trinità della Cava</a>, located at <a href="/wiki/Cava_de%27_Tirreni" title="Cava de&#39; Tirreni">Cava de' Tirreni</a>.  Born in <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a>, he had first been a monk at Cava under <a href="/wiki/Leo_I_of_Cava" title="Leo I of Cava">Leo I of Cava</a>.  He then was at <a href="/wiki/Cluny_Abbey" title="Cluny Abbey">Cluny</a> from 1062 to 1068 and later became bishop of <a href="/wiki/Policastro_Bussentino" title="Policastro Bussentino">Policastro</a> in 1079.
</p><p>He later resigned his see and returned to Cava. Abbot Leo I appointed him coadjutor. When Peter became abbot himself, his administration was so strict that he caused strife in the abbey.  He thus withdrew temporarily before being recalled and serving for several decades as abbot until his death.  He was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Constabilis" title="Constabilis">Constabilis</a>, who had served as Peter's coadjutor.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_of_Pappacarbone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first four abbots of Cava were officially recognized as saints on December 21, 1893, by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The first four abbots are <a href="/wiki/Alferius" title="Alferius">Alferius</a> (Alferio), the founder and first abbot (1050); <a href="/wiki/Leo_I_of_Cava" title="Leo I of Cava">Leo I</a> (1050–79); Peter of Pappacarbone (1079–1123); and <a href="/wiki/Constabilis" title="Constabilis">Constabilis</a>.
</p>
