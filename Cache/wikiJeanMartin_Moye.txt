<p><b>Jean-Martin Moye</b> (written later in his life as Moÿe)<sup id="cite_ref-JMM_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JMM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/French_people" title="French people">French</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">Catholic priest</a> who served as a missionary in China and was the founder of the Sisters of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_Divine_Providence" title="Congregation of Divine Providence">Congregation of Divine Providence</a>. He also organized the first expression of <a href="/wiki/Consecrated_life" title="Consecrated life">consecrated life</a> among the women of China. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church in 1954.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Martin_Moye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Moye was born on 27 January 1730 in the village of <a href="/wiki/Cutting,_Moselle" title="Cutting, Moselle">Cutting</a>, then located within the <a href="/wiki/Bailiwick" title="Bailiwick">Bailiwick</a> of <a href="/wiki/Dieuze" title="Dieuze">Dieuze</a>, within the autonomous <a href="/wiki/Lorraine_(duchy)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorraine (duchy)">Duchy of Lorriane</a>, a part of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire" title="Holy Roman Empire">Holy Roman Empire</a>, now in the <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">French</a> <a href="/wiki/French_departments" class="mw-redirect" title="French departments">Department</a> of <a href="/wiki/Moselle_(department)" title="Moselle (department)">Moselle</a>. He was the sixth of the thirteen children of Jean Moye and Anne Catharine Demange,<sup id="cite_ref-Maes_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maes-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> part of a long-established and prosperous farming family of the region. The fervent Catholic faith of the family can be seen in the fact that, apart from Jean-Martin, a younger brother also became a priest, as well as five of his first cousins, and later two of his nephews.
</p><p>Moye had an uneventful childhood, growing up on his family's extensive holdings. He received his basic education from his older brother, Jean-Jacques, a seminarian, who taught him until his untimely death in 1744 at the age of 24. Moye completed his education at the College of <a href="/wiki/Pont-%C3%A0-Mousson" title="Pont-à-Mousson">Pont-à-Mousson</a>, following which he studied philosophy at the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> College of <a href="/wiki/Strasbourg" title="Strasbourg">Strasbourg</a>. In the fall of 1751 he then entered the local <a href="/wiki/Diocese" title="Diocese">diocesan</a> Seminary of Saint-Simon in <a href="/wiki/Metz" title="Metz">Metz</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Maes_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maes-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> the same one at which his brother had studied. There one of his professors included <a href="/wiki/Canon_(priest)" class="mw-redirect" title="Canon (priest)">Canon</a> François Thiébaut, a noted <a href="/wiki/Bible" title="Bible">Biblical</a> scholar of the era, who would later serve as the representative of the local clergy to the <a href="/wiki/Estates_General_(France)" title="Estates General (France)">Estates General</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Priesthood">Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Martin_Moye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Moye was <a href="/wiki/Holy_Orders" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Orders">ordained</a> a priest on 9 March 1754 by <a href="/wiki/Louis-Joseph_de_Montmorency-Laval" title="Louis-Joseph de Montmorency-Laval">Louis-Joseph de Montmorency-Laval</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Metz" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Metz">Bishop of Metz</a>. Upon his ordination, he was granted a <a href="/wiki/Benefice" title="Benefice">benefice</a> by King <a href="/wiki/Stanislas_Leszczynski" class="mw-redirect" title="Stanislas Leszczynski">Stanislas Leszczynski</a>, the last <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Lorraine" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Lorraine">Duke of Lorraine</a>, of the income generated from the Chapel of St. Andrew in the cemetery of Dieuze. This income allowed him to accept the poorly paid office of <a href="/wiki/Vicar" title="Vicar">Vicar</a> for three different <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parishes</a> in Metz, one of which, the Parish of the Holy Cross (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Sainte-Croix</i>), had Canon Thiébaut as <a href="/wiki/Pastor" title="Pastor">pastor</a>. He then undertook a number of different ministries as part of his service, among them acting as <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> for the seminarians of Saint-Simon.<sup id="cite_ref-JMM_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JMM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The parish extended well beyond the city limits, and Moye undertook the spiritual care of the members of the parish living in the small and isolated <a href="/wiki/Hamlet_(place)" title="Hamlet (place)">hamlets</a> in the countryside. Through this service he became aware of the need of education for the girls of the region, who lacked any access to schools. He conceived of a project to remedy this situation by placing volunteer teachers in these rural locations. The first volunteer was a <a href="/wiki/Working_class" title="Working class">working class</a> woman, Marguerite Lecomte, whom he stationed in the hamlet of Saint-Hubert on 14 January 1762.<sup id="cite_ref-Leny_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leny-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> She would remain in this post without disturbance throughout the upheavals of the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>. Volunteers were quickly sent out to various other locations, going out as far as <a href="/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau" title="Freiburg im Breisgau">Freiburg im Breisgau</a>, then in the <a href="/wiki/Further_Austria" title="Further Austria">Habsburg dominion</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Writer">Writer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Martin_Moye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Writer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Out of the desire to provide the faithful of the parish with means to deepen their spiritual lives, Moye began to publish some <a href="/wiki/Tract_(literature)" title="Tract (literature)">tracts</a>, in collaboration with a younger colleague, the <a href="/wiki/Abb%C3%A9" title="Abbé">Abbé</a> Louis Jobal de Pagny (1737–1766). The first, in 1762, was a pamphlet entitled <i>Du soin extrème qu'on doit avoir du Baptême des enfants</i>. It treated the <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> of newborn infants, especially <a href="/wiki/Stillbirth" title="Stillbirth">stillborn</a> babies. It was a development of <i>Abrégé de l’Embryologie sacrée</i>, a work by a <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicilian</a> <a href="/wiki/Moral_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Moral theology">moral theologian</a>, Francesco Cangiamiglia, which had just been published in Paris, having originally been published in Sicily in 1745 with ecclesiastical approval.<sup id="cite_ref-JMM_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JMM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1764 they re-published an older work by the 17th-century spiritual writer, <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic_Church)" title="Cardinal (Catholic Church)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Pierre_de_B%C3%A9rulle" title="Pierre de Bérulle">Pierre de Bérulle</a>, <i>Élévation à Dieu sur le mystère de l’Incarnation</i> (<i>Being raised to God through the mystery of the <a href="/wiki/Incarnation" title="Incarnation">Incarnation</a></i>) with their commentaries on the text. Later, after the unexpected death of Jobal, Moye published a small pamphlet entitled <i>Recueil de diverses pratiques de piété</i> (<i>A Collection of various pious Practices</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-JMM_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JMM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Opposition">Opposition</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Martin_Moye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Opposition">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Moye's work with rural education and his writings provoked criticism from certain elements of the city. He was accused with recklessness for his sending young women to live in the isolated hamlets of the countryside. He was further accused of rigorism in his dealing with penitents, as well as making unfair criticisms of both the clergy and of <a href="/wiki/Midwife" title="Midwife">midwives</a> in his writings on Baptism. They prevailed on Bishop de Montmorency-Laval to take action against the two authors. As a result, in May 1762, the bishop ordered Moye to suspend the sending out of volunteers – though those already in the countryside were left in their situations. He further transferred him from Metz to serve as vicar of Dieuze. As this was his native region, Moye did not consider it a punishment, but worried about the future of his volunteers, who were coming to be called the "poor Sisters". His coworkers in the project assured him that the setback was only temporary.
</p><p>Moye was again accused of an extreme rigidity in his dealing with the people of the parish, such as those who came to him for <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confession</a>. He also opposed the traditional festivities celebrated by the peasants during the year. This time the bishop responded more severely, and, during <a href="/wiki/Holy_Week" title="Holy Week">Holy Week</a> of 1767, the most sacred period of the Christian year, Moye was suspended from his post.<sup id="cite_ref-Leny_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leny-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Over the course of the next year and a half, until 1768, he moved from parish to parish, providing the pastors with what help he could provide. Finally he was given refuge by the <a href="/wiki/Grand_Prior" class="mw-redirect" title="Grand Prior">Grand Prior</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Saint-Di%C3%A9" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Saint-Dié">Abbey of Saint-Dié</a>, an <a href="/wiki/Abbey_nullius" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey nullius">abbey nullius</a>, independent of local bishops, where he was asked to help run a kind of <a href="/wiki/Minor_seminary" title="Minor seminary">minor seminary</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Missionary">Missionary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Martin_Moye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Missionary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>During his time at the abbey, Moye had two important developments in his life, the first being making the acquaintance of a local priest, Antoine Raulin, who had worked to develop education in the region. He also came to the decision to offer his services as a missionary to Asia. That following October he enrolled in the seminary of the <a href="/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society" title="Paris Foreign Missions Society">Foreign Missions Society of Paris</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Maes_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Maes-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which specialized in that work. He returned to Lorraine the following spring, where he visited the volunteers, now a <a href="/wiki/Religious_institute" title="Religious institute">religious institute</a> called the <b>Sisters of Providence</b>, as well as preaching <a href="/wiki/Parish_mission" class="mw-redirect" title="Parish mission">parish missions</a> throughout the region. Apparently believing that he would not return from China, where he was to be sent, he formally renounced his family inheritance.
</p><p>After completing the training period at the seminary, Moye was assigned to serve in the <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicariate" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicariate">Apostolic Vicariate</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Chengdu" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Chengdu">Sichuan</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Leny_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Leny-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He then put the care of the Sisters of Providence in the hands of two colleagues who were admirers of their work, one of them being Raulin. He also appointed Marie Morel as their first Mother Superior. He left France for China on 30 December 1771. He would spend ten years in the Chinese missions, not returning to Paris until 6 June 1784. Nine years of mission work, frequently interrupted by persecution and imprisonment, made him realize the necessity of Chinese help. In 1782 he founded the "Christian Virgins", religious women following the rules of the Congregation of Providence at home, devoting themselves to the care of the sick and to the Christian instruction of Chinese women and children in their own homes.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Jean-Martin_Moye&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Exhausted and ill, Moye returned to France in 1784. He resumed the direction of the Sisters of Divine Providence and evangelized Lorraine and Alsace by preaching missions. The <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a> of 1791 drove him into exile, and with his Sisters he retired to <a href="/wiki/Trier" title="Trier">Trier</a>. After the capture of the city by the French troops, <a href="/wiki/Typhoid_fever" title="Typhoid fever">typhoid fever</a> broke out and, helped by his Sisters, he devoted himself to hospital work. He contracted the disease and died in 1793.
</p><p>Moye was buried in the cemetery of the cathedral. The cemetery, however, was closed in 1808 and paved over to form the Konstantinsplatz of the city. His grave has never been identified.<sup id="cite_ref-JMM_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-JMM-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
