<p><b>Odile of Alsace</b>, also known as <b>Odilia</b> and <b>Ottilia</b>, born c. 662 - c. 720 at <a href="/wiki/Mont_Sainte-Odile" title="Mont Sainte-Odile">Mont Sainte-Odile</a>), is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>. She is a <a href="/wiki/Patroness_saint" class="mw-redirect" title="Patroness saint">patroness saint</a> of good eyesight and of the region of <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odile_of_Alsace&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Odile was the daughter of <a href="/wiki/Etichon" class="mw-redirect" title="Etichon">Etichon</a> (also known as Athich, Adalrich or Aldaric), Duke of <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a> and founder of the <a href="/wiki/Etichonids" title="Etichonids">Etichonid</a> noble family. According to the 9th century "Life of Odilia", she was born blind. Her father did not want her because she was a girl and handicapped, so her mother Bethswinda had her brought to Palma (perhaps present day <a href="/wiki/Baume-les-Dames" title="Baume-les-Dames">Baume-les-Dames</a> in <a href="/wiki/Burgundy_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy (region)">Burgundy</a>), where she was raised by peasants there.<sup id="cite_ref-web_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-web-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A tenth-century legend relates that when she was twelve, Odile was taken into a nearby monastery.  Whilst there, the itinerant bishop <a href="/wiki/Erhard_of_Regensburg" title="Erhard of Regensburg">Erhard of Regensburg</a> was led, by an angel it was said, to Palma where he baptised her Odile (Sol Dei), whereupon she <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miraculously</a> recovered her sight.  Her younger brother Hughes had her brought home again, which enraged Etichon so much that he accidentally killed his son. Odile miraculously revived him, and left home again.
</p><p>She fled across the <a href="/wiki/Rhine" title="Rhine">Rhine</a> to a cave or cavern in one of two places (depending on the source:  the <a href="/wiki/Musbach_valley" title="Musbach valley">Musbach valley</a> near <a href="/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau" title="Freiburg im Breisgau">Freiburg im Breisgau</a>, Germany, or <a href="/wiki/Arlesheim" title="Arlesheim">Arlesheim</a> near <a href="/wiki/Basel" title="Basel">Basel</a>, <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>.) Supposedly, the cliff face opened up in order to rescue her from her plight.  In the cave, she hid from her father. When he tried to follow her, he was injured by falling rocks and gave up.
</p><p>When Etichon fell ill, Odile returned to nurse him. He finally gave up resisting his headstrong daughter and founded the Augustine <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Cenobium" class="mw-redirect" title="Cenobium">community</a> of Mont Ste. Odile (also known as <a href="/wiki/Hohenburg_Abbey" class="mw-redirect" title="Hohenburg Abbey">Hohenburg Abbey</a>) for her.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in the Hochwald (Hohwald), Bas-Rhin, where Odile became abbess and where Etichon was later buried. Some years later Odile was shown the site of <a href="/wiki/Niedermunster_Abbey,_Alsace" title="Niedermunster Abbey, Alsace">Niedermünster</a> at the foot of the mountain by <a href="/wiki/St._John_the_Baptist" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John the Baptist">St. John the Baptist</a> in a vision.  There she founded a second monastery, including a hospital. Here, the head and an arm of St. <a href="/wiki/Lazarus_of_Marseille" class="mw-redirect" title="Lazarus of Marseille">Lazarus of Marseille</a> were displayed but later transferred to <a href="/wiki/Andlau" title="Andlau">Andlau</a>. The buildings of the Niedermünster burned down in 1542, but the local well is still said to cure eye diseases.
</p><p>St. Odile died about 720 at the convent of Niedermünster. At the insistent prayers of her sisters she was returned to life, but after describing the beauties of the afterlife to them, she took communion by herself and died again.<sup id="cite_ref-web_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-web-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She was buried at Ste. Odile.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odile_of_Alsace&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG/220px-Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG/330px-Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG/440px-Alsace_Mont_Sainte-Odile_24.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1200" data-file-height="1600" /></a><figcaption>A depiction of St. Odile in <a href="/wiki/Mont_Sainte-Odile" title="Mont Sainte-Odile">Mont Sainte-Odile</a>, Alsace, France.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The cult of St. Odile spread rapidly, and spread outside France to Germany.  She was mentioned in the litanies of <a href="/wiki/Freising" title="Freising">Freising</a>, <a href="/wiki/Utrecht" title="Utrecht">Utrecht</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ratisbon" class="mw-redirect" title="Ratisbon">Ratisbon</a> at least from the 9th century.  Amongst the common people, pilgrimages to her shrine were popular, and were by no means limited to the masses; from <a href="/wiki/Charlemagne" title="Charlemagne">Charlemagne</a> onwards, emperors also conducted pilgrimages in her honour.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Indeed, Charlemagne granted immunity to the convent at Hohenberg, which was later officially ratified by <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Pious" title="Louis the Pious">Louis the Pious</a> on 9 March 837.<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By the 14th century, Odile's cult had grown so strong that her relics were split and removed to <a href="/wiki/Corbie" title="Corbie">Corbie</a>, <a href="/wiki/Prague" title="Prague">Prague</a> and <a href="/wiki/Einsiedeln" title="Einsiedeln">Einsiedeln</a>.  She enjoyed especial popularity in <a href="/wiki/Strasbourg" title="Strasbourg">Strasbourg</a>.  The strength of her cult is supposed to have been a result of her patronage of the blind and partially sighted, which was especially pertinent in a time before the invention of <a href="/wiki/Spectacles" class="mw-redirect" title="Spectacles">spectacles</a>.
</p><p>St. Odile was long considered the patron of Alsace and eye patients, at least since before the 16th century; however, this was made official in 1807 by pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_VII" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius VII">Pius VII</a>. Her feast day is 13 December.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Cultural_representations">Cultural representations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odile_of_Alsace&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cultural representations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As the patroness of ocular afflictions and ear diseases, St. Odile is often depicted with a pair of eyes on a book - particularly fine examples of such images can be found from the 14th-16th centuries.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  A notable sculpture of her exists at the <a href="/wiki/Bavarian_National_Museum" title="Bavarian National Museum">Bavarian National Museum</a> in <a href="/wiki/Munich" title="Munich">Munich</a>, which portrays the event of her baptism.  The <a href="/wiki/Delphinium" title="Delphinium">larkspur</a> is connected to St. Odile as well and is believed to cure eye diseases in popular medicine and superstition.
</p><p>A <i>Life of St. Odilia</i> was written about the 10th century, mostly dedicated to the retelling of her legend, the antagonism of her father, and the death of her brother Hughes.  From internal evidence, it seems that it was based upon an earlier, 8th century <i>Life</i>;<sup id="cite_ref-Kirsch_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> however, as an account of her life, it cannot be considered to have much historical validity.<sup id="cite_ref-dictionary_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-dictionary-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p><a href="/wiki/Mont_Sainte-Odile" title="Mont Sainte-Odile">Mont Sainte-Odile</a> in Alsace, is named for her.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Places_dedicated_to_Saint_Odile">Places dedicated to Saint Odile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odile_of_Alsace&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Places dedicated to Saint Odile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="St._Odile_pilgrim.27s_chapel.2C_near_Freiburg"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="St._Odile_pilgrim's_chapel,_near_Freiburg">St. Odile pilgrim's chapel, near Freiburg</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Odile_of_Alsace&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: St. Odile pilgrim&#039;s chapel, near Freiburg">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Musbach_valley" title="Musbach valley">valley of the Musbach</a>, a small river that runs near Freiburg im Breisgau, pilgrims have venerated St. Odile for centuries. In ca. 1300 a chapel was built; the present church was started in 1503 and finished in the 18th century. The church is built adjacent to a <a href="/wiki/Spring_(hydrosphere)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spring (hydrosphere)">spring</a> whose water contains <a href="/wiki/Radon" title="Radon">radon</a>, which is supposedly beneficial to eyesight. In the 18th century the spring became part of the church building: in 1714 the source was included by enlarging the building, in 1780 the cave with the source in it was renovated and decorated in the fanciful style of the time.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
