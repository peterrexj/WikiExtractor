<p><b>Alphonsus Liguori</b>, CSsR (27 September 1696 – 1 August 1787), sometimes called <b>Alphonsus Maria de Liguori</b> or <b>Saint Alphonsus Liguori</b>, was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Catholic_bishop" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic bishop">Catholic bishop</a>, spiritual writer, composer, musician, artist, poet, lawyer, <a href="/wiki/Scholastic_philosopher" class="mw-redirect" title="Scholastic philosopher">scholastic philosopher</a>, and theologian. He founded the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Most_Holy_Redeemer" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer">Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer</a>, known as the Redemptorists, in November 1732.
</p><p>In 1762 he was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sant%27Agata_dei_Goti" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sant&#39;Agata dei Goti">Bishop of Sant'Agata dei Goti</a>. A prolific writer, he published nine editions of his <i><a href="/wiki/Moral_Theology_(Liguori)" title="Moral Theology (Liguori)">Moral Theology</a></i> in his lifetime, in addition to other devotional and ascetic works and letters. Among his best known works are <i><a href="/wiki/The_Glories_of_Mary" title="The Glories of Mary">The Glories of Mary</a></i> and <i>The Way of the Cross</i>, the latter still used in parishes during Lenten devotions.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> in 1839 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a> and proclaimed a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_the_Church" title="Doctor of the Church">Doctor of the Church</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> in 1871. One of the most widely read Catholic authors, he is the patron saint of confessors.<sup id="cite_ref-Miller_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miller-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born in <a href="/wiki/Marianella" class="mw-redirect" title="Marianella">Marianella</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>, on 27 September 1696. He was the eldest of seven children of Giuseppe Liguori, a naval officer and Captain of the Royal Galleys, and Anna Maria Caterina Cavalieri. Two days after he was born, he was baptized at the Church of Our Lady the Virgin as Alphonsus Mary Anthony John Cosmas Damian Michael Gaspard de' Liguori. The family was of noble lineage, but the branch to which Liguori belonged had become somewhat impoverished.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Liguori learned to ride and fence but was never a good shot because of poor eyesight.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Myopia" class="mw-redirect" title="Myopia">Myopia</a> and chronic <a href="/wiki/Asthma" title="Asthma">asthma</a> precluded a military career so his father had him educated in the legal profession. He was taught by tutors before entering the University of Naples, where he graduated with doctorates in civil and canon law at 16.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He remarked later that he was so small at the time that he was almost buried in his doctor's gown and that all the spectators laughed.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> When he was 18, like many other nobles, he joined the Confraternity of Our Lady of Mercy, with whom he assisted in the care of the sick at the hospital for "incurables".<sup id="cite_ref-Baltimore_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Baltimore-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He became a successful lawyer. He was thinking of leaving the profession and wrote to someone, "My friend, our profession is too full of difficulties and dangers; we lead an unhappy life and run risk of dying an unhappy death".<sup id="cite_ref-Tannoja_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tannoja-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> At 27, after having lost an important case, the first he had lost in eight years of practising law, he made a firm resolution to leave the profession of law.<sup id="cite_ref-Miller_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Miller-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Moreover, he heard an interior voice saying: "Leave the world, and give yourself to me."<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Calling_to_the_Priesthood">Calling to the Priesthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Calling to the Priesthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1723, he decided to offer himself as a novice to the <a href="/wiki/Oratory_of_St._Philip_Neri" class="mw-redirect" title="Oratory of St. Philip Neri">Oratory of St. Philip Neri</a> with the intention of becoming a priest. His father opposed the plan, but after two months (and with his Oratorian confessor's permission), he and his father compromised: he would study for the priesthood, but not as an Oratorian, and would live at home.<sup id="cite_ref-newadvent_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-newadvent-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was ordained on 21 December 1726, at the age of 30. He lived his first years as a priest with the homeless and the marginalized youth of Naples. He became very popular because of his plain and simple preaching. He said: "I have never preached a sermon which the poorest old woman in the congregation could not understand".<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He founded the <i>Evening Chapels</i>, which were managed by the young people themselves. The chapels were centres of prayer and piety, preaching, community, social activities, and education. At the time of his death, there were 72, with over 10,000 active participants. His sermons were very effective at converting those who had been alienated from their faith.
</p><p>Liguori suffered from <a href="/wiki/Scrupulosity" title="Scrupulosity">scruples</a> much of his adult life and felt guilty about the most minor issues relating to sin.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Moreover, Liguori viewed scruples as a blessing at times and wrote: "Scruples are useful in the beginning of conversion.... they cleanse the soul, and at the same time make it careful".<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1729, Liguori left his family home and took up residence at the <a href="/wiki/Naples_Eastern_University" class="mw-redirect" title="Naples Eastern University">Chinese Institute</a> in Naples.<sup id="cite_ref-Tannoja_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tannoja-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> It was there that he began his missionary experience in the interior regions of the Kingdom of Naples, where he found people who were much poorer and more abandoned than any of the street children in Naples. In 1731, while he was ministering to earthquake victims in the town of Foggia, Alphonsus said he had a vision of the Virgin Mother in the appearance of a young girl of 13 or 14, wearing a white veil.<sup id="cite_ref-Tannoja_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tannoja-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Congregation_of_the_Most_Holy_Redeemer">Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On 9 November 1732, he founded the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Most_Holy_Redeemer" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer">Congregation of the Most Holy Redeemer</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-parish_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> when Sister Maria Celeste Crostarosa told him that it had been revealed to her that he was the one that God had chosen to found the congregation. He founded the congregation with the charism of preaching popular missions in the city and the countryside. Its goal was to teach and preach in the slums of cities and other poor places. They also fought <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenism</a>, a heresy that preached an excessive moral rigorism: "the penitents should be treated as souls to be saved rather than as criminals to be punished". He is said never to have refused absolution to a penitent.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A gifted musician and composer, he wrote many popular hymns and taught them to the people in parish missions. In 1732, while he was staying at the Convent of the Consolation, one of his order's houses in the small city of Deliceto in the province of Foggia in Southeastern Italy, Liguori wrote the Italian carol "<a href="/wiki/Tu_scendi_dalle_stelle" title="Tu scendi dalle stelle">Tu scendi dalle stelle</a>" ("From Starry Skies Descending") in the musical style of a pastorale. The version with Italian lyrics was based on his original song written in <a href="/wiki/Neapolitan_language" title="Neapolitan language">Neapolitan</a>, which began <i><span title="Neapolitan-language text"><i lang="nap">Quanno nascette Ninno</i></span></i> ("When the child was born"). As it was traditionally associated with the <a href="/wiki/Zampogna" title="Zampogna">zampogna</a>, or large-format Italian <a href="/wiki/Bagpipes" title="Bagpipes">bagpipe</a>, it became known as <i><span title="Italian-language text"><i lang="it">Canzone d'i zampognari</i></span></i>, the "Carol of the Bagpipers".<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Bishop_of_Sant.27_Agata_de_Goti"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Sant'_Agata_de_Goti">Bishop of Sant' Agata de Goti</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Bishop of Sant&#039; Agata de Goti">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Liguori was consecrated Bishop of Sant'Agata dei Goti in 1762.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He tried to refuse the appointment by using his age and infirmities as arguments against his consecration. He wrote sermons, books, and articles to encourage devotion to the Blessed Sacrament and the Blessed Virgin Mary. He first addressed ecclesiastical abuses in the diocese, reformed the seminary and spiritually rehabilitated the clergy and faithful. He suspended those priests who celebrated Mass in less than 15 minutes and sold his carriage and episcopal ring to give the money to the poor. In the last years of his life, he suffered a painful sickness and bitter persecution from his fellow priests, who dismissed him from the Congregation that he had founded.<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Liguori-reliquia.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Liguori-reliquia.jpg/250px-Liguori-reliquia.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="187" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Liguori-reliquia.jpg/375px-Liguori-reliquia.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e6/Liguori-reliquia.jpg/500px-Liguori-reliquia.jpg 2x" data-file-width="550" data-file-height="412" /></a><figcaption>San Alfonso María de Ligorio, at the Basilica in Pagani, Italy</figcaption></figure>
<p>By May 1775, Alphonus was "deaf, blind, and laden with so many infirmities, that he has no longer even the appearance of a man", and his resignation was accepted by the recently crowned <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI" title="Pope Pius VI">Pope Pius VI</a>. He continued to live with the Redemptorist community in <a href="/wiki/Pagani,_Italy" class="mw-redirect" title="Pagani, Italy">Pagani, Italy</a>, where he died on 1 August 1787.<sup id="cite_ref-Tannoja_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Tannoja-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration_and_legacy">Veneration and legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Veneration and legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg/250px-Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="375" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg/375px-Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg/500px-Scultura_bronzea_di_Sant%27Alfonso_Maria_de_Liguori.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3557" data-file-height="5335" /></a><figcaption>Town square of <a href="/wiki/Pagani,_Campania" title="Pagani, Campania">Pagani, Campania</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 15 September 1816 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 26 May 1839 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="Pope Gregory XVI">Pope Gregory XVI</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1949, the Redemptorists founded the <a href="/wiki/Alphonsian_Academy" title="Alphonsian Academy">Alphonsian Academy</a> for the advanced study of Catholic <a href="/wiki/Moral_theology" class="mw-redirect" title="Moral theology">moral theology</a>. He was named the patron of confessors and moral theologians by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> on 26 April 1950, who subsequently wrote of him in the encyclical <i><a href="/wiki/Haurietis_aquas" title="Haurietis aquas">Haurietis aquas</a></i>.
</p><p>In bestowing the title of "Prince of Moral Theologians", the church also gave the "unprecedented honour she paid to the Saint in her Decree of 22 July 1831, which allows confessors to follow any of St. Alphonsus's own opinions without weighing the reasons on which they were based".<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The church did not bestow this unique privilege lightly but was due to the extraordinary combination of exceptional knowledge and understanding of church teachings combined with the great precision in which he wrote.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Works">Works</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Works">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Overview">Overview</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Overview">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Liguori was a prolific and popular author.<sup id="cite_ref-parish_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-parish-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> He was proficient in the arts, his parents having had him trained by various masters, and he was a musician, painter, poet and author at the same time. Liguori wrote 111 works on spirituality and theology.<sup id="cite_ref-Philippines_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Philippines-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The 21,500 editions and the translations into 72 languages that his works have undergone attest to the fact that he is one of the most widely read Catholic authors.
</p><p>His best-known musical work is his Christmas <a href="/wiki/Hymn" title="Hymn">hymn</a> <i><span title="Neapolitan-language text"><i lang="nap">Quanno Nascetti Ninno</i></span></i>, later translated into Italian by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> as <i><a href="/wiki/Tu_scendi_dalle_stelle" title="Tu scendi dalle stelle">Tu scendi dalle stelle</a></i> ("From Starry Skies Thou Comest").
</p><p>
A strong defender of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, Liguori said<b>:</b></p><blockquote><p>To reject the divine teaching of the Catholic Church is to reject the very basis of reason and revelation, for neither the principles of the one nor those of the other have any longer any solid support to rest on; they can then be interpreted by every one as he pleases; every one can deny all truths whatsoever he chooses to deny. I therefore repeat: If the divine teaching authority of the Church, and the obedience to it, are rejected, every error will be endorsed and must be tolerated."<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>

<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Catholic_moral_theology#Birth_of_manualism" title="Catholic moral theology">Catholic moral theology §&#160;Birth of manualism</a></div>
<p>Liguori's greatest contribution to the Catholic Church was in the area of moral theology. His masterpiece was <i>The <a href="/wiki/Moral_Theology_(Liguori)" title="Moral Theology (Liguori)">Moral Theology</a></i> (1748), which was approved by the Pope himself<sup id="cite_ref-SHMI_5-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SHMI-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and was born of Liguori's pastoral experience, his ability to respond to the practical questions posed by the faithful and his contact with their everyday problems. He opposed sterile legalism and strict rigourism. According to him, those were paths closed to the Gospel because "such rigour has never been taught nor practised by the Church". His system of moral theology is noted for its prudence, avoiding both laxism and excessive rigour. He is credited with the position of <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Probabilism#.C3.86quiprobabilism" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Probabilism">Aequiprobabilism</a>, which avoided <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenist rigorism</a> as well as <a href="/wiki/Laxism" class="mw-redirect" title="Laxism">laxism</a> and simple <a href="/wiki/Probabilism" title="Probabilism">probabilism</a>. Since its publication, it has remained in Latin, often in 10 volumes or in the combined 4-volume version of Gaudé. It saw only recently its first publication in translation, in an English translation made by Ryan Grant and published in 2017 by Mediatrix Press. The English translation of the work is projected to be around 5 volumes.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Mariology">Mariology</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Alphonsus_Liguori&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Mariology">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His Mariology, though mainly pastoral in nature, rediscovered, integrated and defended that of St <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a>, St <a href="/wiki/Ambrose_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Ambrose of Milan">Ambrose of Milan</a> and other fathers; it represented an intellectual defence of Mariology in the 18th century, the <a href="/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment" title="Age of Enlightenment">Age of Enlightenment</a>, against the rationalism to which contrasted his fervent Marian devotion.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
