<p><b>Tarsicius</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> or <b>Tarcisius</b><sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> was a <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the early <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> church who lived in the 3rd century. The little that is known about him comes from a metrical inscription by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Damasus I</a>, who was pope in the second half of the 4th century.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="History">History</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarcisius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: History">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The only positive information concerning this Roman martyr is found in a poem composed in his honour by Pope Damasus (366–384), who compares him to the <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Stephen" title="Saint Stephen">Saint Stephen</a> and says that, as Stephen was <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoned</a> by a crowd, so Tarcisius, carrying the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament" title="Blessed Sacrament">Blessed Sacrament</a>, was attacked by a group and beaten to death.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Nothing else definite is known concerning Tarcisius. Since Damasus compares him to Stephen, he may have been a deacon; however, a 6th-century account makes him an <a href="/wiki/Acolyte" title="Acolyte">acolyte</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> According to one version of the detailed legend that developed later, Tarcisius was a young boy during one of the fierce 3rd-century Roman persecutions, probably during the reign of <a href="/wiki/Emperor_Valerian" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor Valerian">Emperor Valerian</a> (253–259). One day, he was entrusted with the task of bringing the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> to condemned Christians in prison.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He preferred death at the hands of a mob rather than deliver to them the Blessed Sacrament which he was carrying.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarcisius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was originally buried in the <a href="/wiki/Catacombs_of_San_Callisto" class="mw-redirect" title="Catacombs of San Callisto">Catacombs of San Callisto</a> and the inscription by Damasus was placed later on his tomb.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Some time later his <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were moved to the <a href="/wiki/San_Silvestro_in_Capite" title="San Silvestro in Capite">San Silvestro in Capite</a> church in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">feast day</a> is celebrated on 15 August; that day is widely observed as the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Feast of the Assumption</a>, therefore he is not mentioned in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a>, but only in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarcisius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He is the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Altar_server" title="Altar server">altar servers</a> and <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">first communicants</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bittle_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bittle-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarcisius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His story was greatly expanded by <a href="/wiki/Cardinalate" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinalate">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Wiseman" title="Nicholas Wiseman">Nicholas Wiseman</a>, who portrays him as a young acolyte in his novel <i><a href="/wiki/Fabiola_(novel)" title="Fabiola (novel)">Fabiola, or the Church in the Catacombs</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-stevens_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stevens-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Saint-Tharcisius,_Quebec" title="Saint-Tharcisius, Quebec">municipality of Saint-Tharcisius</a> in <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>, is named after him,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> as well as a 35 kilogram (77 lb) <a href="/wiki/Bell_(instrument)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bell (instrument)">bell</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Stephansdom" class="mw-redirect" title="Stephansdom">Stephansdom</a> in <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, Austria.  
</p><p>Saint <a href="/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_S%C3%A1nchez_del_R%C3%ADo" title="José Sánchez del Río">José Sánchez del Río</a> was nicknamed "Tarcisius".
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Poem_by_Damasus">Poem by Damasus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tarcisius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Poem by Damasus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The first five lines say that both Stephen (the <a href="/wiki/Protomartyr" class="mw-redirect" title="Protomartyr">protomartyr</a>) and Tarcisius are equal in merit, and Stephen's death (as recorded in the <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles" title="Acts of the Apostles">Acts of the Apostles</a>) is retold poetically. The last four lines can be translated as:
</p><p><i>When an insane gang pressed saintly Tarcisius, who was carrying the sacraments of Christ, to display them to the profane, he preferred to be killed and give up his life rather than betray to rabid dogs the heavenly body.</i>
</p>
