<p><b>Timothy</b> or <b>Timothy of Ephesus</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Τιμόθεος</span></span>; <i>Timótheos</i>, meaning "honouring God" or "honoured by God"<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>) was an early Christian <a href="/wiki/Evangelism" title="Evangelism">evangelist</a> and the first <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> who tradition relates died around the year AD 97.
</p><p>Timothy was from the <a href="/wiki/Lycaonia" title="Lycaonia">Lycaonian</a> city of <a href="/wiki/Lystra" title="Lystra">Lystra</a><sup id="cite_ref-:0_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> or of <a href="/wiki/Derbe" title="Derbe">Derbe</a><sup id="cite_ref-biblehub.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-biblehub.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ReferenceA_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ReferenceA-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> in <a href="/wiki/Asia_Minor" class="mw-redirect" title="Asia Minor">Asia Minor</a>, born of a <a href="/wiki/Jews" title="Jews">Jewish</a> mother who had become a Christian believer, and a <a href="/wiki/Greeks" title="Greeks">Greek</a> father. The <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Apostle Paul</a> met him during his <a href="/wiki/Missionary_journeys_of_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Missionary journeys of Paul">second missionary journey</a> and he became Paul's companion and missionary partner along with <a href="/wiki/Silas" title="Silas">Silas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a> indicates that Timothy traveled with Paul the Apostle, who was also his mentor. He is addressed as the recipient of the <a href="/wiki/First_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="First Epistle to Timothy">First</a> and <a href="/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="Second Epistle to Timothy">Second</a> Epistles to Timothy. While included in the <a href="/wiki/Pauline_epistles#Authenticity" title="Pauline epistles">Pauline epistles</a> of the <a href="/wiki/New_Testament#Pauline_letters_to_persons" title="New Testament">New Testament</a>, First and Second Timothy are considered by many biblical scholars to be <a href="/wiki/Pseudoepigraphical" class="mw-redirect" title="Pseudoepigraphical">pseudoepigraphical</a> and not written by <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Timothy was a native of Lystra or of Derbe in Lycaonia (<a href="/wiki/Early_centers_of_Christianity#Anatolia" class="mw-redirect" title="Early centers of Christianity">Anatolia</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-stelca_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stelca-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> When <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a> and <a href="/wiki/Barnabas" title="Barnabas">Barnabas</a> first visited Lystra, Paul healed a person crippled from birth, leading many of the inhabitants to accept his teaching. When he returned a few years later with Silas, Timothy was already a respected member of the Christian congregation, as were his grandmother <a href="/wiki/Lois_(Bible)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lois (Bible)">Lois</a> and his mother <a href="/wiki/Eunice_(Bible)" class="mw-redirect" title="Eunice (Bible)">Eunice</a>, both Jews. In 2 Timothy 1:5,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> his mother and grandmother are noted as eminent for their piety and faith. Timothy is said to have been acquainted with the Scriptures since childhood. In 1 Corinthians 16:10,<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> there is a suggestion that he was by nature reserved and timid: "When Timothy comes, see that you put him at ease among you, for he is doing the work of the Lord".<sup id="cite_ref-fwec_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fwec-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Timothy's father was a Greek Gentile. Thus Timothy had not been <a href="/wiki/Circumcision_controversy_in_early_Christianity" title="Circumcision controversy in early Christianity">circumcised</a> and Paul now ensured that this was done, according to Acts 16:1-3,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> to ensure Timothy's acceptability to the Jews whom they would be evangelizing. According to <a href="/wiki/John_William_McGarvey" title="John William McGarvey">John William McGarvey</a>:<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> "Yet we see him in the case before us, circumcising Timothy with his own hand, and this 'on account of certain Jews who were in those quarters'". This did not compromise the decision made at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Jerusalem" title="Council of Jerusalem">Council of Jerusalem</a>, that <a href="/wiki/Non-Jewish" class="mw-redirect" title="Non-Jewish">gentile</a> believers were not required to be circumcised, since, as the son of a Jewish mother, Timothy was Jewish by birth.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry">Ministry</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ministry">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img alt="Statue of Saint Timothy at Saint Timothy&#39;s Church in The Villages, Florida." src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg/220px-Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="428" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg/330px-Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg/440px-Statue_of_Saint_Timothy.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1852" data-file-height="3607" /></a><figcaption>Statue of Saint Timothy at Saint Timothy's Church in <a href="/wiki/The_Villages,_Florida" title="The Villages, Florida">The Villages, Florida</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Timothy became St Paul's disciple, and later his constant companion and co-worker in preaching.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_18-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> In the year 52, Paul and <a href="/wiki/Silas" title="Silas">Silas</a> took Timothy along with them on their journey to <a href="/wiki/Macedonia_(ancient_kingdom)" title="Macedonia (ancient kingdom)">Macedonia</a>. <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine</a> extols his zeal and disinterestedness in immediately forsaking his country, his house, and his parents, to follow the apostle, to share in his poverty and sufferings.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> Timothy may have been subject to ill health or "frequent ailments", and Paul encouraged him to "use a little wine for your stomach's sake".<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When Paul went on to Athens, Silas and Timothy stayed for some time at Beroea and <a href="/wiki/Thessalonica" class="mw-redirect" title="Thessalonica">Thessalonica</a> before joining Paul at <a href="/wiki/Corinth" title="Corinth">Corinth</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> <a rel="nofollow" class="external text" href="https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Thessalonians+3:1–6&amp;version=nkjv">1 Thessalonians 3:1–6</a> suggests that from Corinth, Paul sent Timothy back to Thessalonika to enquire about the community's continued faith, reporting back that it was in good shape. Timothy next appears in Acts during Paul's stay in Ephesus (54–57), and in late A. D. 56 or early 57 Paul sent him forth to Macedonia with the aim that he would eventually arrive at Corinth. Timothy arrived at Corinth just after Paul's letter <a href="/wiki/1_Corinthians" class="mw-redirect" title="1 Corinthians">1 Corinthians</a> reached that city.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Timothy was with Paul in Corinth during the winter of 57–58 when Paul dispatched his <a href="/wiki/Letter_to_the_Romans" class="mw-redirect" title="Letter to the Romans">Letter to the Romans</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> According to Acts 20:3–6,<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Timothy was with Paul in Macedonia just before <a href="/wiki/Passover" title="Passover">Passover</a> in 58; he left the city before Paul, going ahead of him to await Paul in <a href="/wiki/Troad" title="Troad">Troas</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> "That is the last mention of Timothy in Acts", Raymond Brown notes.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> In the year 64, Paul left Timothy at Ephesus, to govern that church.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His relationship with Paul was close. Timothy's name appears as the co-author on <a href="/wiki/2_Corinthians" class="mw-redirect" title="2 Corinthians">2 Corinthians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Philippians" class="mw-redirect" title="Philippians">Philippians</a>, <a href="/wiki/Colossians" class="mw-redirect" title="Colossians">Colossians</a>, <a href="/wiki/1_Thessalonians" class="mw-redirect" title="1 Thessalonians">1 Thessalonians</a>, <a href="/wiki/2_Thessalonians" class="mw-redirect" title="2 Thessalonians">2 Thessalonians</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_Philemon" title="Epistle to Philemon">Philemon</a>. Paul wrote to the Philippians about Timothy, "I have no one like him."<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> When Paul was in prison and awaiting martyrdom, he summoned his faithful friend Timothy for a last farewell.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_18-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That Timothy was jailed at least once during the period of the writing of the New Testament is implied by the writer of <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_the_Hebrews" title="Epistle to the Hebrews">Hebrews</a> mentioning Timothy's release at the end of the epistle.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although not stated in the New Testament, other sources have records of the apostle's death. The apocryphal <i><a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Timothy" title="Acts of Timothy">Acts of Timothy</a></i> states that in the year 97 AD, the 80-year-old bishop tried to halt a procession in honor of the goddess <a href="/wiki/Diana_(mythology)" title="Diana (mythology)">Diana</a> by preaching <a href="/wiki/The_Gospel" class="mw-redirect" title="The Gospel">the Gospel</a>. The angry pagans beat him, dragged him through the streets, and <a href="/wiki/Stoning" title="Stoning">stoned him to death</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_18-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Timothy is venerated as an apostle, saint, and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, with his feast day on 22 January. The <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> venerates Timothy together with <a href="/wiki/Titus_(Biblical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Titus (Biblical)">Titus</a> by a <a href="/wiki/Memorial_(liturgy)" title="Memorial (liturgy)">memorial</a> on 26 January, the day after the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Conversion_of_Saint_Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast of the Conversion of Saint Paul">Feast of the Conversion of Saint Paul</a>. From the 13th century until 1969 the feast of Timothy (alone) was on 24 January, the day before that of the Conversion of Saint Paul.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Along with Titus and <a href="/wiki/Silas" title="Silas">Silas</a>, Timothy is commemorated by the <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_America" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church in America">Evangelical Lutheran Church in America</a> on 26 January. Timothy's feast is kept by the <a href="/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod" title="Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod">Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod</a> on 24 January.
</p><p>Timothy is honored on the calendars of the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> (with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Titus" title="Saint Titus">Titus</a>) on <a href="/wiki/January_26" title="January 26">26 January</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the 4th century, the relics of Timothy were transferred from Ephesus to Constantinople and placed in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_the_Holy_Apostles" title="Church of the Holy Apostles">Church of the Holy Apostles</a> near the tombs of Andrew and Luke.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_18-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Later on in the 13th century, the relics seem to have been taken to Italy by a count returning from the crusades, and buried around 1239 in the <a href="/wiki/Termoli_Cathedral" title="Termoli Cathedral">Termoli Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup> The remains were rediscovered in 1945, during restoration work.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Timothy is invoked against stomach and intestinal disorders.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Pastoral_Epistles">Pastoral Epistles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Pastoral Epistles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main articles: <a href="/wiki/First_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="First Epistle to Timothy">First Epistle to Timothy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="Second Epistle to Timothy">Second Epistle to Timothy</a></div>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_Epistles" class="mw-redirect" title="Pastoral Epistles">Pastoral Epistles</a>, <a href="/wiki/Pauline_epistles" title="Pauline epistles">Pauline epistles</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Authorship_of_the_Pauline_epistles" title="Authorship of the Pauline epistles">Authorship of the Pauline epistles</a></div>
<p>There are two letters to Timothy attributed to <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a>: the <a href="/wiki/First_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="First Epistle to Timothy">First Epistle to Timothy</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_Timothy" title="Second Epistle to Timothy">Second Epistle to Timothy</a>. These – along with the <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_Titus" title="Epistle to Titus">Epistle to Titus</a> – are called the <a href="/wiki/Pastoral_Epistles" class="mw-redirect" title="Pastoral Epistles">Pastoral Epistles</a> and are considered pseudepigraphic by many modern scholars, including <a href="/wiki/Bart_D._Ehrman" title="Bart D. Ehrman">Bart Ehrman</a>, <a href="/wiki/Raymond_Collins_(priest)" title="Raymond Collins (priest)">Raymond Collins</a>, and <a href="/wiki/David_Aune" title="David Aune">David E. Aune</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-34" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-34">&#91;34&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="First_Timothy_.28c._62.E2.80.9364.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="First_Timothy_(c._62–64)">First Timothy (c. 62–64)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: First Timothy (c. 62–64)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The author of this epistle writes to Timothy concerning the organization of the church and Timothy's own leadership within the body. Major themes include the use of <a href="/wiki/Torah" title="Torah">The Law</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-35" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-35">&#91;35&#93;</a></sup> warnings against <a href="/wiki/Heresy" title="Heresy">false doctrine</a> such as <a href="/wiki/Encratites" title="Encratites">Encratism</a>, instructions for prayer,<sup id="cite_ref-36" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-36">&#91;36&#93;</a></sup> roles of women in the church, qualifications for leaders of the church,<sup id="cite_ref-37" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-37">&#91;37&#93;</a></sup> and the treatment of widows, elders, masters, youth, and church members in general.<sup id="cite_ref-38" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-38">&#91;38&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Second_Timothy_.28c._62.E2.80.9364.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Second_Timothy_(c._62–64)">Second Timothy (c. 62–64)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Second Timothy (c. 62–64)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>According to the letter, Paul urges Timothy not to have a "spirit of timidity" and not to "be ashamed to testify about our Lord".<sup id="cite_ref-39" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-39">&#91;39&#93;</a></sup> He also entreats Timothy to come to him before winter, and to bring Mark with him. Paul clearly anticipates his being put to death and realities beyond in his valedictory found in 2 Timothy 4:6–8.<sup id="cite_ref-40" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-40">&#91;40&#93;</a></sup> He exhorts his son "Timothy" to all diligence and steadfastness in the face of false teachings, with advice about combating them with reference to the teachings of the past, and to patience under persecution,<sup id="cite_ref-41" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-41">&#91;41&#93;</a></sup> and to a faithful discharge of all the duties of his office,<sup id="cite_ref-42" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-42">&#91;42&#93;</a></sup> with all the solemnity of one who was about to appear before the Judge of the quick and the dead (God).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Acts_of_Timothy">Acts of Timothy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Timothy&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Acts of Timothy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Acts_of_Timothy" title="Acts of Timothy">Acts of Timothy</a></div>
<p>The <b>Acts of Timothy</b> (Acta Timothei) is a work of New Testament apocrypha, most likely from the 5th century. The Acts tell how Paul had consecrated Timothy as bishop during <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a>'s reign on the occasion of a visit to <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a> which they made together. Then, under <a href="/wiki/Nerva" title="Nerva">Nerva</a>, Timothy suffers a martyr's death during a <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> festival.<sup id="cite_ref-43" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-43">&#91;43&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-44" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-44">&#91;44&#93;</a></sup> Timothy "attempts to put an end to the wild and violent goings-on but himself falls victim to the orgies."<sup id="cite_ref-45" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-45">&#91;45&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-46" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-46">&#91;46&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Acts of Timothy contain almost as much material about <a href="/wiki/John_the_Evangelist" title="John the Evangelist">John the Evangelist</a>, who was also a resident of <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a> as they do about Timothy.<sup id="cite_ref-47" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-47">&#91;47&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
