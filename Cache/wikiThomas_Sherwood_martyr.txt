font-weight: normal;">Blessed</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Thomas Sherwood</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyr</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">c. 1551<br /><a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">7 February 1578<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (aged 27)<br /><a href="/wiki/Tyburn" title="Tyburn">Tyburn</a>, <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">Beatified</a></th><td class="infobox-data">29 December 1886, <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Saint_symbolism" title="Saint symbolism">Attributes</a></th><td class="infobox-data">holding a scroll</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Thomas Sherwood</b> (c. 1551&#8211;1578) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Layman" class="mw-redirect" title="Layman">layman</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>. He was arrested on suspicion of treason, having visited a house where it seems that Mass was secretly celebrated.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Sherwood_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sherwood was born in <a href="/wiki/Greater_London" title="Greater London">London</a> of Henry and Elizabeth Sherwood, Catholics who both endured imprisonment for their faith. His father, Henry Sherwood, was a woolen draper. Thomas was a cousin of <a href="/wiki/Francis_Tregian_the_Elder" title="Francis Tregian the Elder">Francis Tregian</a> who was later imprisoned for sheltering <a href="/wiki/Cuthbert_Mayne" title="Cuthbert Mayne">Cuthbert Mayne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the age of fifteen Thomas left school to assist his father in the woolen business for the next ten years. In 1576 he decided to travel to the new <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a> at <a href="/wiki/Douai" title="Douai">Douai</a> and study for the priesthood. He subsequently returned home to adjust his accounts, and obtain funds to support his studies.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the city he was a visitor to the house of Lady Tregonwell of Dorsetshire, where it seems that Mass was secretly offered. The woman's son, by her first marriage, Protestant George Marten, resented this. Happening to see Sherwood in the street in Chancery Lane, he began to cry "Stop the traitor" aloud. In this way he managed to have Thomas brought before a judge.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Although there was no proof of any kind against him, he implicated himself by answering openly on the issue of the Queen's supremacy. Being examined before the Recorder as to his opinion of the <a href="/wiki/Regnans_in_Excelsis" title="Regnans in Excelsis">bull</a> of <a href="/wiki/Pius_V" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius V">Pius V</a> and as to whether an excommunicated queen held lawful sovereignty, he denied all knowledge of both Bull and excommunication, but expressed his opinion that if the queen were indeed excommunicated her rule could not be lawful.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Once he had been imprisoned in the <a href="/wiki/Tower_of_London" title="Tower of London">Tower of London</a>, and at the orders of the <a href="/wiki/Privy_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="Privy Council">Privy Council</a>, his lodgings were searched and a large sum of money, approximately 20-30 pounds, which Thomas had borrowed to help his sick father, was removed. The funds were confiscated.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Sherwood_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Twice <a href="/wiki/Rack_(torture)" title="Rack (torture)">racked</a> with a view to extracting details of houses where the Roman Catholic <a href="/wiki/Mass_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Mass in the Catholic Church">Mass</a> was celebrated, Thomas kept silent. As a result, he was then thrown into a dungeon to rot, where he endured hunger and cold for three winter months.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The cell was below the water line, and as the Thames rose with the tide, rats would be driven into the dark chamber. The only concession that <a href="/wiki/William_Roper" title="William Roper">William Roper</a>, <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More" title="Thomas More">Thomas More</a>'s son-in-law, could obtain was permission to supply him with straw to lie upon.<sup id="cite_ref-whitfield_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-whitfield-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Roper sent money to Sherwood's jailer in order to purchase food, but the funds were returned as the Lieutenant would not allow Sherwood benefit of any alms.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After a hasty trial, and the sentence of hanging, drawning and quartering, carried out on 7 February 1578 at <a href="/wiki/Tyburn,_London" class="mw-redirect" title="Tyburn, London">Tyburn</a>, where he was cut down while still alive. Sherwood was 27 years of age.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Sherwood's mother, being repeatedly discovered at Mass, was intermittently imprisoned for a total of fourteen years. In the end, she died in prison, having exhausted whatever funds she had to provide for her maintenance. His brother, John, left for the continent, where he joined the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Jesuits</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Thomas_Sherwood_(martyr)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Thomas Sherwood is said to have been a small man, witty, cheerful and loved by many.<sup id="cite_ref-Pollen_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Pollen-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> "equipollently" by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XIII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XIII">Leo XIII</a>, by means of a decree of 29 December 1886.
</p>
