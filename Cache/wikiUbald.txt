<p><b>Ubald of Gubbio</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Ubaldo</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ubaldus</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Ubalde</i>; ca. 1084–1160) was a <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">medieval</a> <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Gubbio" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Gubbio">bishop of Gubbio</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, today venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>. <a href="/wiki/Saint_Ubaldo_Day" title="Saint Ubaldo Day">Saint Ubaldo Day</a> is still celebrated at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Ubaldo,_Gubbio" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Ubaldo, Gubbio">Basilica of Sant'Ubaldo</a> in Gubbio in his honor, as well as at <a href="/wiki/Jessup,_Pennsylvania" title="Jessup, Pennsylvania">Jessup, Pennsylvania</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ubald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Born <b>Ubaldo Baldassini</b> Gubbio, the only son of <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">noble</a> parents Rovaldo and Guiliana Baldassini.<sup id="cite_ref-assoc_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-assoc-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was a relative of <a href="/wiki/Sperandia" title="Sperandia">Sperandia</a>, abbess of a <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">Camaldolese</a> monastery at <a href="/wiki/Cingoli" title="Cingoli">Cingoli</a>.
</p><p>Ubald was baptized in the church of San Giovanni and named after his uncle. Ubald's parents died while he was still very young, and he was raised by his uncle, the bishop of Gubbio.<sup id="cite_ref-sus_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was educated by the prior of the <a href="/wiki/Cathedral" title="Cathedral">cathedral</a> church of his native city, where he also became a <a href="/wiki/Canon_regular" title="Canon regular">canon regular</a>.
</p><p>Ubald entered the Monastery of St. Secondo in the same city, where he remained for some years. He was ordained about 1114. Recalled by his bishop, he returned to the cathedral monastery. The bishop made him prior of his cathedral that he might reform several abuses in the behaviour of the canons. He learned that <a href="/wiki/Peter_de_Honestis" title="Peter de Honestis">Peter de Honestis</a> some years before had established a community of canons regular at the monastery of Santa Maria in Portofuori at Ravenna. He also heard that Peter had given special statutes to the canons, which had been approved by Pope <a href="/wiki/Paschal_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Paschal II">Paschal II</a>. Ubald went there, where he remained for three months, to learn the details and the practice of their rules, wishing to introduce them among his own canons of Gubbio. He did so upon his return.<sup id="cite_ref-Allaria_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allaria-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After some years, the chapter house and cloister burned down. Ubaldus looked upon this as a favourable opportunity of leaving his post, and become a hermit. With this in mind he made his way to that of Font-Avellano, where he found Peter of Rimini, to whom he communicated his design of quitting the world. Peter opposed the notion as a dangerous temptation, and exhorted him to return to his former vocation, in which God had fixed him for the good of others. Ubald therefore, returned to Gubbio, rebuilt the cloisters, and rendered his chapter more flourishing than it had ever been.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ubald had donated his patrimony to the poor and to the restoration of monasteries. Several bishoprics were offered to him, but he refused them all. However, in 1128, the episcopal See of Gubbio becoming vacant, he was sent, with some clerics, by the population to ask for a new bishop from Pope <a href="/wiki/Honorius_II" class="mw-redirect" title="Honorius II">Honorius II</a> who consecrated Ubald and sent him back to Gubbio. To his people he became a perfect pattern of all Christian virtues, and a powerful protector in all their spiritual and temporal needs.<sup id="cite_ref-Allaria_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Allaria-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ubald was known for his patience and heroic gentleness, and was considered to have the gift of healing. Once it happened, that in repairing the wall of the city, the workmen encroached upon his vineyard. The bishop mildly put them in mind of it, and desired them to forbear. The overseer of the work moved with fury, scornfully pushed him into a great heap of mortar. The good bishop got up all covered with lime and dirt, without making the least expostulation. The people demanded that the overseer, in punishment for the offence, should be banished, and his goods confiscated. The saint endeavoured to make it pass for an accident; but when that could not satisfy the people, who knew how it happened, he being desirous to deliver the man out of the hands of the magistrates, maintained that the cognizance of the misdemeanour belonging to his own court, he would take care to do himself justice. Ubald then pardoned him.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1151, Perugia and a number of other towns allied against Gubbio. Ubaldo became commander of the Gubbio forces, which saw an overwhelming victory, which the populace attributed to the miraculous intervention of its bishop. In 1155, after the sack of nearby <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a> He later met with Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa" title="Frederick Barbarossa">Frederick Barbarossa</a>, who subsequently spared the city from attack.<sup id="cite_ref-sus_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The people came to regard their bishop as the city's protector.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died in 1160<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> after a long and painful illness of two years.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Ubald&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Numerous miracles were attributed to him during his life and after his death. A number of miraculous cures were attributed to his intercession. The life of Ubaldo was written by Theobald, his immediate successor in the episcopal see, commissioned by Barbarossa.
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg/220px-Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="285" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg/330px-Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg/440px-Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg 2x" data-file-width="454" data-file-height="589" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Gubbio_Corsa_Ceri.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The festival of <a href="/wiki/La_Corsa_dei_Ceri" class="mw-redirect" title="La Corsa dei Ceri">La Corsa dei Ceri</a> at Gubbio.  The statue of Saint Ubaldo leads the procession, followed by ceri topped with the statues of <a href="/wiki/Saint_George" title="Saint George">Saint George</a> and Saint <a href="/wiki/Anthony_the_Great" title="Anthony the Great">Anthony the Great</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>At the solicitation of Bishop Bentivoglio <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_III" title="Pope Celestine III">Pope Celestine III</a> canonized Ubald in 1192. The body of Ubaldo, had at first been buried in the cathedral church by the Bishops of <a href="/wiki/Perugia" title="Perugia">Perugia</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cagli" title="Cagli">Cagli</a>. In 1194 it was moved to the small <a href="/wiki/Oratory_(worship)" title="Oratory (worship)">oratory</a> on the top of Mount Ingino overlooking the city, where in 1508, at the wish of the Duke of <a href="/wiki/Urbino" title="Urbino">Urbino</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Canons_Regular_of_the_Lateran" title="Canons Regular of the Lateran">Canons Regular of the Lateran</a> had built a church. Now known as the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Sant%27Ubaldo,_Gubbio" title="Basilica of Sant&#39;Ubaldo, Gubbio">Basilica of Sant'Ubaldo</a>, it is frequented by numerous pilgrims. Dante mentions Ubald in the Divine Comedy (Heaven Canto XI): “Between Tupino and the stream that falls down from the blest Ubaldo's chosen hill the slope is green a lofty mount below". Outside of Italy, a finger relic of Ubald is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Coll%C3%A9giale_Saint-Thi%C3%A9baut,_Thann" class="mw-redirect" title="Collégiale Saint-Thiébaut, Thann">Saint-Theobald collegiate church</a> of <a href="/wiki/Thann,_Haut-Rhin" title="Thann, Haut-Rhin">Thann, Haut-Rhin</a> (<a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The devotion to the saint is very popular throughout Umbria, but especially at Gubbio. The feast of their <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a>, called the Festival of Ceri, is celebrated by the inhabitants of the country round with great solemnity, there being religious and civil processions which call to mind the famous festivities of the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a> in Italy. A celebration like the Corsa dei Ceri is held also in Jessup, Pennsylvania, where people carry out the same festivities as the residents of Gubbio do by "racing" the three statues through the streets during the Memorial Day weekend. The event in Gubbio may be a survival of a similar rite described in the pre-Christian <a href="/wiki/Iguvine_Tablets" title="Iguvine Tablets">Iguvine Tablets</a><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
