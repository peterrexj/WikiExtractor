<p><b>Peter Claver</b> <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">SJ</a> (<a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Pedro Claver y Corberó</i>; 26 June 1580 – 8 September 1654) was a <a href="/wiki/Spanish_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Spanish people">Spanish</a> <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> <a href="/wiki/Priesthood_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood (Catholic Church)">priest</a> and <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> born in <a href="/wiki/Verd%C3%BA" title="Verdú">Verdú</a> (<a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>) who, due to his life and work, became the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of enslaved people, the Republic of <a href="/wiki/Colombia" title="Colombia">Colombia</a>, and ministry to <a href="/wiki/African_Americans" title="African Americans">African Americans</a>. 
</p><p>During the 40 years of his ministry in the New Kingdom of Granada, it is estimated he personally baptized around 300,000 people and heard the <a href="/wiki/Confession_(religion)" title="Confession (religion)">confessions</a> of over 5,000 people per year. He is also patron saint for <a href="/wiki/Merchant_shipping" class="mw-redirect" title="Merchant shipping">seafarers</a>. He is considered a heroic example of what should be the Christian praxis of love and of the exercise of human rights.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>The Congress of the Republic of Colombia declared <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar#September" title="General Roman Calendar">September 9</a> as the Human Rights national Day in his honor.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Claver&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Claver was born in 1580 into a devoutly Catholic and prosperous farming family in the <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spanish</a> village of <a href="/wiki/Verd%C3%BA" title="Verdú">Verdú</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-suau_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Urgell" title="Urgell">Urgell</a>, located in the <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Lleida" title="Province of Lleida">Province of Lleida</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a> about 54 miles (87&#160;km) from <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>. He was born 70 years after <a href="/wiki/King_Ferdinand_of_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="King Ferdinand of Spain">King Ferdinand of Spain</a> set the colonial slavery culture into motion by authorizing the purchase of 250 African enslaved people in <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a> for his territories in <a href="/wiki/New_Spain" title="New Spain">New Spain</a>.
</p><p>Later, as a student at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Barcelona" title="University of Barcelona">University of Barcelona</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-suau_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Claver was noted for his intelligence and piety. After two years of study there, Claver wrote these words in the notebook he kept throughout his life: "I must dedicate myself to the service of God until death, on the understanding that I am like a enslaved person."<sup id="cite_ref-peterclaver1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-peterclaver1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="In_the_New_World">In the New World</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Claver&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: In the New World">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>After he had completed his studies, Claver entered the <a href="/wiki/Society_of_Jesus" class="mw-redirect" title="Society of Jesus">Society of Jesus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Tarragona" title="Tarragona">Tarragona</a> at the age of 20. When he had completed the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a>, he was sent to study philosophy at <a href="/wiki/Palma,_Mallorca" class="mw-redirect" title="Palma, Mallorca">Palma, Mallorca</a>. While there, he came to know the <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">porter</a> of the college, St. <a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Rodriguez" title="Alphonsus Rodriguez">Alphonsus Rodriguez</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Laybrother" class="mw-redirect" title="Laybrother">laybrother</a> known for his holiness and gift of <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecy</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Rodriguez felt that he had been told by God that Claver was to spend his life in service in the colonies of <a href="/wiki/New_Spain" title="New Spain">New Spain</a>, and he frequently urged the young student to accept that calling.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Peter_Claver.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Peter_Claver.jpg/220px-Peter_Claver.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="275" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Peter_Claver.jpg/330px-Peter_Claver.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Peter_Claver.jpg/440px-Peter_Claver.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2400" data-file-height="3000" /></a><figcaption>Portrait of St. Peter Claver in the museum <a href="/wiki/Palace_of_Inquisition" class="mw-redirect" title="Palace of Inquisition">Palace of Inquisition</a>, <a href="/wiki/Cartagena,_Colombia" title="Cartagena, Colombia">Cartagena, Colombia</a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Claver volunteered for the Spanish colonies and was sent to the <a href="/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Granada" title="New Kingdom of Granada">New Kingdom of Granada</a>, where he arrived in the port city of <a href="/wiki/Cartagena,_Colombia" title="Cartagena, Colombia">Cartagena</a> in 1610.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Required to spend six years studying theology before being ordained a priest, he lived in Jesuit houses at <a href="/wiki/Tunja" title="Tunja">Tunja</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1" title="Bogotá">Bogotá</a>. During those preparatory years, he was deeply disturbed by the harsh treatment and living conditions of the black enslaved people who were brought from Africa.
</p><p>By this time, the <a href="/wiki/Slave_trade" class="mw-redirect" title="Slave trade">slave trade</a> had been established in the Americas for about a century. Local Native Americans were considered physically ill-suited to work in the gold and silver mines. Mine owners met their labor requirements by importing blacks from Angola and Congo, whom they purchased in West Africa for four crowns a head or bartered for goods and sold in America for an average two hundred crowns apiece. Others were captured at random, especially able-bodied males and females deemed suitable for labor.<sup id="cite_ref-kopc_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kopc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cartagena was a slave-trading hub and 10,000 slaves poured into the port yearly, crossing the Atlantic from West Africa under conditions so foul that an estimated one-third died in transit. Although the slave trade was condemned by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_III" title="Pope Paul III">Pope Paul III</a> and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII" title="Pope Urban VIII">Urban VIII</a> had issued a papal decree prohibiting slavery,<sup id="cite_ref-kopc_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kopc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> (later called "supreme villainy" by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a>), it was a lucrative business and continued to flourish.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Claver's predecessor in his eventual lifelong mission, <a href="/wiki/Alonso_de_Sandoval" title="Alonso de Sandoval">Alonso de Sandoval</a>, was his mentor and inspiration.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Sandoval devoted himself to serving the enslaved people for 40 years before Claver arrived to continue his work. Sandoval attempted to learn about their customs and languages; he was so successful that, when he returned to <a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, he wrote a book in 1627 about the nature, customs, rites and beliefs of the Africans. Sandoval found Claver an apt pupil. When he was solemnly professed in 1622, Claver signed his final <a href="/wiki/Religious_profession" title="Religious profession">profession</a> document in <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a> as: <i>Petrus Claver, aethiopum semper servus</i> (Peter Claver, servant of the Ethiopians [i.e. Africans] forever).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ministry_to_the_enslaved">Ministry to the enslaved</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Claver&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Ministry to the enslaved">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG/220px-131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG/330px-131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG/440px-131_Cathedral_San_Pedro_Claver_Dome_Cartagena.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="3000" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Iglesia_de_San_Pedro_Claver,_Cartagena" title="Iglesia de San Pedro Claver, Cartagena">Church of St. Peter Claver</a> in <a href="/wiki/Cartagena,_Colombia" title="Cartagena, Colombia">Cartagena, Colombia</a>, where Claver lived and ministered</figcaption></figure><p>Whereas Sandoval had visited the slaves where they worked, Claver preferred to head for the wharf as soon as a slave ship entered the port. Boarding the ship, he entered the filthy and diseased holds to treat and minister to their badly treated, terrified human cargo, who had survived a voyage of several months under miserable conditions. It was difficult to move around on the ships, because those trafficking in slaves filled them to capacity. The slaves were often told they were being taken to a land where they would be eaten. Claver wore a cloak, which he would lend to anyone in need. A legend arose that whoever wore the cloak received lifetime health and was cured of all disease. After the enslaved people were herded from the ship and penned in nearby yards to be scrutinized by crowds of buyers, Claver joined them with medicine, food, bread, lemons. With the help of interpreters and pictures which he carried with him, he gave basic instructions.<sup id="cite_ref-ewtn_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ewtn-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Once baptized, Claver saw the slaves as fellow Christians, and encouraged others to treat them as such. During the season when slavers were not accustomed to arrive, he traveled the country, visiting plantation after plantation, to give spiritual consolation to the slaves.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> During his 40 years of ministry it is estimated that he personally catechized and baptized 300,000 slaves.  He would then follow up on them to ensure that as Christians they received their Christian and <a href="/wiki/Civil_rights" class="mw-redirect" title="Civil rights">civil rights</a>. His mission extended beyond caring for the slaves, however. He preached in the city square, to sailors and traders and conducted country missions, returning every spring to visit those he had baptized, ensuring that they were treated humanely. During these missions, whenever possible he avoided the hospitality of planters and overseers; instead, he would lodge in the same quarters as the slaves.<sup id="cite_ref-peterclaver1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-peterclaver1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Claver's work on behalf of the enslaved did not prevent him from ministering to the souls of well-to-do members of society, traders and visitors to Cartagena (including Muslims and English Protestants) and condemned criminals, many of whom he spiritually prepared for death; he was also a frequent visitor at the city's hospitals. Through years of unremitting toil and the force of his own unique personality, the slaves situation slowly improved. In time he became a moral force, the <a href="/wiki/Apostle" title="Apostle">Apostle</a> of Cartagena.<sup id="cite_ref-peterclaver1_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-peterclaver1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Illness.2C_and_death"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Illness,_and_death">Illness, and death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Claver&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Illness, and death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG/220px-038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG/330px-038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG/440px-038_Bones_of_San_Pedro_Claver_in_Cathedral.JPG 2x" data-file-width="4000" data-file-height="3000" /></a><figcaption>The bones of Claver under an altar at the Church of St. Peter Claver in Cartagena</figcaption></figure>
<p>In the last years of his life Peter was too ill to leave his room. He lingered for four years, largely forgotten and neglected, physically abused and starved by an ex-slave who had been hired by the Superior of the house to care for him. He never complained about his treatment, accepting it as a just punishment for his sins.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He died on 8 September 1654.
</p><p>When the news of his death spread throughout Cartagena many came to his room to pay their last respects. Such was his reputation for holiness that the room was stripped bare of anything that might serve as a <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relic</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ignatian_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ignatian-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The city magistrates, who had previously considered him a nuisance for his persistent advocacy on behalf of the enslaved people, ordered a public funeral and he was buried with pomp and ceremony. The scope of Claver's ministry, which was prodigious even before considering the astronomical number of people he baptized, was realized only after his death.
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> in 1888 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a>, along with the Jesuit <a href="/wiki/Porter_(monastery)" title="Porter (monastery)">porter</a>, <a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Rodriguez" title="Alphonsus Rodriguez">Alphonsus Rodriguez</a>. In 1896 Pope Leo also declared Claver the patron of missionary work among all African peoples.<sup id="cite_ref-suau_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-suau-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> His body is preserved and venerated in the church of the Jesuit residence, now renamed in his honor.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<p>Many organizations, missions, parishes, religious congregations, schools and hospitals bear the name of St. Peter Claver and also claim to continue the Mission of Claver as the following:
</p>
<ul><li>The <a href="/wiki/Knights_of_Peter_Claver" title="Knights of Peter Claver">Knights of Peter Claver</a>, Inc., is the largest African-American Catholic fraternal organization in the United States. In 2006, a unit was established in San Andres, Colombia. The Order was founded in Mobile, Alabama, and is presently headquartered in New Orleans.<sup id="cite_ref-kopc_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kopc-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Claver's mission continues today in the work of the Apostleship of the Sea (AoS)<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and his inspiration remains among port chaplains and those who visit ships in the name of the church, through the AoS.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>The <a href="/wiki/Missionary_Sisters_of_St._Peter_Claver" title="Missionary Sisters of St. Peter Claver">Missionary Sisters of St. Peter Claver</a> are a religious congregation of women dedicated to serving the spiritual and social needs of the poor around the world, particularly in Africa. They were founded in Austria by the Blessed <a href="/wiki/Mary_Theresa_Led%C3%B3chowska" title="Mary Theresa Ledóchowska">Mary Theresa Ledóchowska</a> in 1894.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Among the many parishes dedicated to St. Peter Claver are those in Lexington, Kentucky,<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> West Hartford, Connecticut,<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Macon, Georgia,<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> New Orleans, Louisiana,<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> Simi Valley, California,<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> St. Paul, Minnesota,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>  Sheboygan, Wisconsin,<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> Montclair, New Jersey,<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Baltimore, Maryland,<sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> Huntington, West Virginia,<sup id="cite_ref-24" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> and Nairobi, Kenya.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li>Among the many schools dedicated to St. Peter Claver are those in Decatur, Georgia,<sup id="cite_ref-26" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> and Pimville, South Africa.<sup id="cite_ref-27" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> The oldest African American school in the Diocese of St. Petersburg, and the oldest African American school still functioning in the State of Florida, is the St. Peter Claver Catholic School.<sup id="cite_ref-28" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup></li></ul>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Congress_of_the_Republic_of_Colombia" class="mw-redirect" title="Congress of the Republic of Colombia">Congress of the Republic of Colombia</a> declared September 9 as the <a href="/wiki/Human_Rights" class="mw-redirect" title="Human Rights">Human Rights</a> national Day in his honor.<sup id="cite_ref-29" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-30" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Controversy">Controversy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Peter_Claver&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Controversy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His canonization has caused angst among some due to his own slaveholding and treatment of enslaved people (including corporal punishment), and it is alleged that these matters may have initially stalled his canonization. Dr. Katie Grimes of <a href="/wiki/Villanova_University" title="Villanova University">Villanova</a> has gone so far as to call Claver a "<a href="/wiki/White_supremacy" title="White supremacy">White Supremacist</a>" and has accused the Catholic Church of "White supremacism" for championing him.<sup id="cite_ref-31" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That said, the sources cited by Grimes' in her criticism stated that Claver allowed uncommon freedom for the enslaved people he purchased (using them in his ministry rather than for hard labor), and resorted to physical punishment solely to prevent what he saw as immoral behavior.<sup id="cite_ref-32" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-33" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-33">&#91;33&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
