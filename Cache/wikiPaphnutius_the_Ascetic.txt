					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Egyptian anchorite of the fourth century</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Saint Paphnutius the Ascetic</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Churches" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Churches">Eastern Orthodox Churches</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches" title="Oriental Orthodox Churches">Oriental Orthodox Churches</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">15 February &amp; 25 September (<a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Byzantine Christianity</a>)<br />15 <a href="/wiki/Meshir" title="Meshir">Meshir</a> (<a href="/wiki/Coptic_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Christianity">Coptic Christianity</a>)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Saint Paphnutius the Ascetic</b> (<a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: Ⲁⲃⲃⲁ Ⲡⲁⲫⲛⲟⲩϯ), also known as <b>Paphnutius the Hermit</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Egyptians" title="Egyptians">Egyptian</a> <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">anchorite</a> of the fourth century. He is most famous for his accounts of the lives of many hermits of the <a href="/wiki/Egypt" title="Egypt">Egyptian</a> desert, such as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Onuphrius" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Onuphrius">Saint Onuphrius</a>.
</p><p>Saint Paphnutius was the disciple of <a href="/wiki/Macarius_of_Egypt" title="Macarius of Egypt">Saint Macarius the Great</a> who was an anchorite in the Egyptian desert.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His feast is celebrated on <a href="/wiki/15_February" class="mw-redirect" title="15 February">15 February</a> and <a href="/wiki/25_September" class="mw-redirect" title="25 September">25 September</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Christianity" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Christianity">Coptic Christianity</a>, his feast is observed on 15 <a href="/wiki/Meshir" title="Meshir">Meshir</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was visited by <a href="/wiki/Cassian_the_ascetic" class="mw-redirect" title="Cassian the ascetic">Cassian</a> in 395, when he was ninety.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is also the subject of the play <i><a href="/wiki/Paphnutius_(play)" title="Paphnutius (play)">Paphnutius</a></i> by <a href="/wiki/Hrosvit" class="mw-redirect" title="Hrosvit">Hrosvit</a> (ca. 935-ca. 1001), a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> nun from <a href="/wiki/Saxony" title="Saxony">Saxony</a>, in which he converts the courtesan Thais to Christianity. This play in turn has become the subject of study, most notably by Sandro Sticca.
</p>
