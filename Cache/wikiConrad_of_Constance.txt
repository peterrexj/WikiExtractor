<p><b>Conrad of Constance</b> (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Konrad von Konstanz</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Conradus, Curtius</i>; <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 900 – 26 November 975) was a German <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> and <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conrad_of_Constance&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Conrad was a member of the powerful <a href="/wiki/Elder_Welfs" class="mw-redirect" title="Elder Welfs">Welf</a> family, son of Count Heinrich of <a href="/wiki/Weingarten_(W%C3%BCrttemberg)" class="mw-redirect" title="Weingarten (Württemberg)">Altdorf</a>. After an education at the cathedral school in <a href="/wiki/Konstanz" title="Konstanz">Constance</a>, he became provost of <a href="/wiki/Constance_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Constance Cathedral">Constance Cathedral</a> and in 934 was made <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Constance" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Constance">Bishop of Constance</a>.
</p><p>It is counted as one of his achievements that he avoided becoming enmeshed in the politics of the day and reserved his energies for his episcopal duties. He was nevertheless close to <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Otto I</a>, whom he accompanied to <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a> in 962. Conrad made three pilgrimages to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> as well as a number to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a>. He founded a number of churches on the episcopal estates and the hospital at <a href="/wiki/Kreuzlingen" title="Kreuzlingen">Kreuzlingen</a>, named after a portion of the <a href="/wiki/True_Cross" title="True Cross">True Cross</a> which Conrad brought back from <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> and presented to it.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conrad_of_Constance&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His remembrance as a role model for courage was entirely due to the efforts of Bishop Ulrich I of Dillingen, Bishop of Constance (1111–1127). The <i>Vita</i> of Conrad was written in about 1120 by Udalschalk, a monk of <a href="/wiki/St._Ulrich%27s_and_St._Afra%27s_Abbey,_Augsburg" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Ulrich&#39;s and St. Afra&#39;s Abbey, Augsburg">St. Ulrich's and St. Afra's Abbey, Augsburg</a>, of which he later became abbot (1124 or 1127 to c. 1150 or 1151). Its late date doubtless accounts for its lack of content, notwithstanding which, Conrad was canonised at the <a href="/wiki/First_Lateran_Council" class="mw-redirect" title="First Lateran Council">First Lateran Council</a> in 1123.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Conrad_of_Constance&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Conrad is sometimes represented as a bishop holding a chalice with a <a href="/wiki/Spider" title="Spider">spider</a> in it or over it. This refers to a story that once when he was celebrating mass a spider fell into the chalice. Spiders were believed at that time to be deadly poisonous, but Conrad nevertheless drank the Blood of Christ, which had been transubstantiated from wine, with the spider in it, as a token of faith.
</p><p>See <a href="/wiki/Cultural_depictions_of_spiders" title="Cultural depictions of spiders">Cultural depictions of spiders</a>
</p>
