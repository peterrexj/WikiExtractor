<p><b>Venerius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Venerio</i>) was <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Milan" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Milan">Archbishop of Milan</a> from 400 (or 401) to 408. He is honoured as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and his feast day is May 6.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Venerius_(bishop_of_Milan)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Almost nothing is known about the life of Venerius before his election as bishop of Milan. According to the 5th-century historian, <a href="/wiki/Paulinus_the_Deacon" title="Paulinus the Deacon">Paulinus</a>, Venerius was a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and he was present at the death of <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a> in 397. Venerius was elected bishop after the death of <a href="/wiki/Simplician" title="Simplician">Simplician</a> in the winter between 400 and 401. He was already bishop of Milan when he received a request by a provincial <a href="/wiki/Synod" title="Synod">synod</a> held on June 18, 401 at <a href="/wiki/Carthage" title="Carthage">Carthage</a> to send in North Africa some clerics from Milan. One of the clerics sent was actually Paulinus.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Venerius is also known from a letter written to him by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Anastasius_I" title="Pope Anastasius I">Pope Anastasius I</a> concerning the condemnation of the ideas of the <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origenists</a>. He is also mentioned in a letter of the same pope to <a href="/wiki/John_II,_Bishop_of_Jerusalem" class="mw-redirect" title="John II, Bishop of Jerusalem">John II, Bishop of Jerusalem</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 404 Venerius, along with <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_I" title="Pope Innocent I">Pope Innocent I</a> and <a href="/wiki/Chromatius" title="Chromatius">Chromatius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Aquileia" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Aquileia">bishop of Aquileia</a>, took a stand in favour of <a href="/wiki/St._John_Chrysostom" class="mw-redirect" title="St. John Chrysostom">St. John Chrysostom</a> who has been unjustly banned from <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, writing in his favour to <a href="/wiki/Honorius_(emperor)" title="Honorius (emperor)">Honorius</a>, the Western emperor, who sent this letter to his brother, <a href="/wiki/Arcadius" title="Arcadius">Arcadius</a>, the Eastern emperor. This intercession, however, availed nothing.<sup id="cite_ref-Cazzani_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cazzani-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Venerius died on May 4, 408, and he was buried in the <a href="/wiki/San_Nazaro_in_Brolo" title="San Nazaro in Brolo">Church of Saint Nazarius and Celsus</a> in Milan.<sup id="cite_ref-Ruggeri_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Ruggeri-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> A late tradition, with no historical basis, associates Venerius with the Milan's family of the Oldrati.
</p>
