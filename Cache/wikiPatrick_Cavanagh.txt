<p>

The <b>Wexford Martyrs</b> were Matthew Lambert, Robert Myler, Edward Cheevers, Patrick Cavanagh (<a href="/wiki/Irish_language" title="Irish language">Irish</a>: Pádraigh Caomhánach), John O'Lahy, and another <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> whose name is unknown. In 1581, they were found guilty of treason for aiding in the escape of <a href="/wiki/James_Eustace,_3rd_Viscount_Baltinglass" title="James Eustace, 3rd Viscount Baltinglass">James Eustace, 3rd Viscount Baltinglass</a>; for refusing to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> which declared <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I of England</a> to be the head of the Church; and for conveying a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit order">Jesuit</a> and other Catholic priests and laymen out of Ireland. On 5 July 1581, they were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wexford" title="Wexford">Wexford</a>, <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland" title="Republic of Ireland">Ireland</a>. They were subsequently <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McNeill_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McNeill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wexford_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/The_Pale" title="The Pale">Pale</a> the predominant religion was Catholic, and the Catholics saw a growing threat from the Protestant-dominated government, a perception supported by their marked decline in participation within the kingdom's government. English-born Protestants increasingly occupied positions of authority. The people of the Pale resented taxes on their property for the government's military policy against the <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_nobility_of_Ireland" title="Gaelic nobility of Ireland">Gaelic nobility of Ireland</a> and rebellious <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Normans" class="mw-redirect" title="Hiberno-Normans">Hiberno-Normans</a>. Troops were also billeted upon their lands. James Eustace's father, Viscount Roland, had been imprisoned by the Elizabethan administration for his opposition.<sup id="cite_ref-webb_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the summer of 1580, James Eustace, Viscount Baltinglass, apparently prompted almost entirely by religious motives, raised an army in <a href="/wiki/County_Wicklow" title="County Wicklow">County Wicklow</a>, in support of the  Earl of Desmond's separate uprising in Munster. The Viscount's allies included the coalition of <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clans</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Fiach_McHugh_O%27Byrne" title="Fiach McHugh O&#39;Byrne">Fiach McHugh O'Byrne</a>. At first the uprising was successful, but Baltinglass did not coordinate his efforts with those of Desmond and could not sustain the conflict. He and his followers were outlawed. Forty-five were hanged in Dublin. James Eustace escaped to Munster, where Desmond was still in revolt. After Desmond was killed he left for Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-webb_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Escape_of_Viscount_Baltinglass">Escape of Viscount Baltinglass</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wexford_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Escape of Viscount Baltinglass">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pursued by English troops after the collapse of the Second Desmond Rebellion, James Eustace and his chaplain, Father Robert Rochford, eventually found refuge with Matthew Lambert, a Wexford baker. Lambert fed them and arranged with five sailor acquaintances for safe passage by ship for them. Lambert was betrayed, along with sailors Patrick Cavanagh, Edward Cheevers, Robert Myler, John O'Lahy, and one other. They were arrested, imprisoned, and tortured, before being executed in Wexford on 5 July 1581.<sup id="cite_ref-McNeill_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McNeill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Canonization_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Canonization-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 181">&#58;&#8202;181&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>The authorities heard of the plan beforehand and Matthew was arrested together with his five sailor friends. Thrown into prison, they were questioned about politics and religion. Lambert's reply was: "I am not a learned man. I am unable to debate with you, but I can tell you this, I am a Catholic and I believe whatever our Holy Mother the Catholic Church believes." They were found guilty of treason and hanged, drawn, and quartered in Wexford on 5 July 1581.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
