<p>The <b>Wexford Martyrs</b> were <b>Matthew Lambert</b>, <b>Robert Meyler</b>, <b>Edward Cheevers</b> and <b>Patrick Cavanagh</b>. In 1581, they were found guilty of <a href="/wiki/High_treason_in_the_United_Kingdom" title="High treason in the United Kingdom">high treason</a> for aiding in the escape of <a href="/wiki/James_Eustace,_3rd_Viscount_Baltinglass" title="James Eustace, 3rd Viscount Baltinglass">James Eustace, 3rd Viscount Baltinglass</a>; for similarly conveying a <a href="/wiki/Jesuit_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit order">Jesuit</a> and other Catholic priests and laymen out of Ireland; and for refusing to take the <a href="/wiki/Oath_of_Supremacy" title="Oath of Supremacy">Oath of Supremacy</a> which declared <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_I_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Elizabeth I of England">Elizabeth I of England</a> to be the <a href="/wiki/Supreme_Head_of_the_Church_of_England" title="Supreme Head of the Church of England">Supreme Head of the Church</a> within her dominions. On 5 July 1581, they were <a href="/wiki/Hanged,_drawn_and_quartered" title="Hanged, drawn and quartered">hanged, drawn and quartered</a> in <a href="/wiki/Wexford" title="Wexford">Wexford</a>, <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland" title="Republic of Ireland">Ireland</a>. They were <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1992 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-McNeill_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McNeill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Background">Background</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wexford_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Background">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/The_Pale" title="The Pale">Pale</a> the predominant religion was Catholic, and the Catholics saw a growing threat from the Protestant-dominated government, a perception supported by their marked decline in participation within the kingdom's government. English-born Protestants increasingly occupied positions of authority. The people of the Pale resented taxes on their property for the government's military policy against the <a href="/wiki/Gaelic_nobility_of_Ireland" title="Gaelic nobility of Ireland">Gaelic nobility of Ireland</a> and rebellious <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Normans" class="mw-redirect" title="Hiberno-Normans">Hiberno-Normans</a>. Troops were also billeted upon their lands. James Eustace's father, Viscount Roland, had been imprisoned by the Elizabethan administration for his opposition.<sup id="cite_ref-webb_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the summer of 1580, James Eustace, Viscount Baltinglass, apparently prompted almost entirely by religious motives, raised an army in <a href="/wiki/County_Wicklow" title="County Wicklow">County Wicklow</a>, in support of the  Earl of Desmond's separate uprising in Munster. The Viscount's allies included the coalition of <a href="/wiki/Irish_clan" class="mw-redirect" title="Irish clan">Irish clans</a> led by <a href="/wiki/Fiach_McHugh_O%27Byrne" title="Fiach McHugh O&#39;Byrne">Fiach McHugh O'Byrne</a>. At first the uprising was successful, but Baltinglass did not coordinate his efforts with those of Desmond and could not sustain the conflict. He and his followers were outlawed. Forty-five were hanged in Dublin. James Eustace escaped to Munster, where Desmond was still in revolt. After Desmond was killed he left for Spain.<sup id="cite_ref-webb_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-webb-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Escape_of_Viscount_Baltinglass">Escape of Viscount Baltinglass</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wexford_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Escape of Viscount Baltinglass">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Pursued by Crown forces after the defeat of the Second Desmond Rebellion, James Eustace and his chaplain, Father Robert Rochford, eventually found refuge with Matthew Lambert, a Wexford baker. Lambert fed them and arranged with five sailor acquaintances for safe passage by ship for them. Lambert was betrayed, along with sailors Patrick Cavanagh, Edward Cheevers, Robert Myler,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> John O'Lahy, and one other. They were arrested, imprisoned, and tortured, before being executed in Wexford on 5 July 1581.<sup id="cite_ref-McNeill_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McNeill-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Canonization_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Canonization-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 181">&#58;&#8202;181&#8202;</span></sup>
</p><p>The authorities heard of the plan beforehand and Matthew was arrested together with his five sailor friends. Thrown into prison, they were questioned about politics and religion. Lambert's reply was: "I am not a learned man. I am unable to debate with you, but I can tell you this, I am a Catholic and I believe whatever our Holy Mother the Catholic Church believes." They were found guilty of treason and hanged, drawn, and quartered in Wexford on 5 July 1581.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_two_martyrs">Other two martyrs</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wexford_Martyrs&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Other two martyrs">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>There were apparently six martyrs but only four of them were beatified in 1992, perhaps due to a lack of sufficient evidence for the existence of the other two martyrs. One of them was believed to have been called <i>John O' Lahy</i> and the other was an anonymous individual. Nothing is known about these two men. Their names are usually excluded from the group of 17 <a href="/wiki/Irish_Catholic_Martyrs" title="Irish Catholic Martyrs">Irish martyrs</a> whom <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">John Paul II</a> named among the blessed.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> 
</p>
