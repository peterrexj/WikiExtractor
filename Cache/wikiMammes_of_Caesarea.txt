<p><b>Saint Mammes (Mamas, Mammas, Mammet) of Caesarea</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Μάμας</span>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Mammès</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Mamante</i>; <a href="/wiki/Spanish_language" title="Spanish language">Spanish</a>: <i lang="es">Mamés</i>;  <a href="/wiki/Portuguese_language" title="Portuguese language">Portuguese</a>: <i lang="pt">São Mamede</i>) was a child-<a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the 3rd century, who was martyred at <a href="/wiki/Kayseri" title="Kayseri">Caesarea</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-vailhe_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-vailhe-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> His parents, Theodotus and Rufina, were also martyred.<sup id="cite_ref-kirsch_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kirsch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg/220px-Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="219" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg/330px-Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg/440px-Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg 2x" data-file-width="770" data-file-height="768" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_mammes_and_Duke_Alexander.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Mammes and Duke Alexander</i>, <a href="/wiki/Tapestry" title="Tapestry">tapestry</a> by <a href="/wiki/Jean_Cousin_the_Elder" title="Jean Cousin the Elder">Jean Cousin the Elder</a>, ca. 1541.</div></div></div>
<p>Born in prison to parents who had been jailed for being Christians, Mammes became an orphan when his parents were executed.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> After their death, Mammes was raised by a rich widow named Ammia, who died when Mammes was 15 years old.
</p><p>According to legend, Mammes was tortured for his faith by the governor of Caesarea and was then sent before the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Emperor">Roman Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Aurelian" title="Aurelian">Aurelian</a>, who tortured him again. The Mammes legend states that an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> then liberated him and ordered him to hide on a mountain near Caesarea.
</p><p>Mammes was later thrown to the lions, but he managed to make the beasts docile by preaching to them. Afterward, a lion remained with him as a companion.  Accompanied by the lion, he visited Duke Alexander, who sentenced him to death.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was struck in the stomach with a <a href="/wiki/Trident" title="Trident">trident</a>.  Bleeding, Mammes dragged himself to a spot near a <a href="/wiki/Theater" class="mw-redirect" title="Theater">theater</a> before his soul was carried into heaven by angels.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The center of his cult was situated at Caesarea and later shifted to Langres when his relics were brought there.<sup id="cite_ref-autogenerated1_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-autogenerated1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
The <a href="/wiki/Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Mamm%C3%A8s_de_Langres" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathédrale Saint-Mammès de Langres">Cathédrale Saint-Mammès</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Langres" title="Langres">Langres</a>, is dedicated to him.  Mammes is the chief <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron</a> of the diocese.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Lebanon">Lebanon</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Lebanon">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Saint Mammes is also a popular saint in <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a> with many churches and convents named in his honor. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Deir_Mimas" title="Deir Mimas">Deir Mimas</a> in Lebanon. Grand festivities are organized each year when the town celebrates the feast of Saint Mamas on September 15. The town has both an orthodox monastery, which overlooks the Litani valley of Deirmimas, and a Melkite catholic church, located inside the village, named after Saint Mammes.
</p><p>Mammes is also honored in Kfarhata, which is adjacent to <a href="/wiki/Zgharta" title="Zgharta">Zgharta</a>. The Church of Saint Mamas in <a href="/wiki/Ehden" title="Ehden">Ehden</a> was built in 749 A.D. and is one of the oldest <a href="/wiki/Maronite" class="mw-redirect" title="Maronite">Maronite Catholic</a> churches in <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>. Lebanon is also home to the Saint Mamas Church of <a href="/wiki/Baabdat" title="Baabdat">Baabdat</a>, which was built in the 16th century.
</p><p>Another Maronite Lebanese monastery called Dayr Mar Mamas is being restored by the town's folks of the rural village of Bechaaleh (also spelled Bechealeh) located in the district of Batroun in the north of Lebanon.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cyprus">Cyprus</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Cyprus">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:182px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Mamas_lion_01.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/38/Mamas_lion_01.jpg" decoding="async" width="180" height="240" class="thumbimage" data-file-width="180" data-file-height="240" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Mamas_lion_01.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Mamas, the lamb and the lion: <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> in the church at <a href="/wiki/Morphou" title="Morphou">Morphou</a></div></div></div>
<p>In <a href="/wiki/Cyprus" title="Cyprus">Cyprus</a>, Mammes is popularly known as a poor <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> who lived in a cave near the town of <a href="/wiki/Morphou" title="Morphou">Morphou</a>. According to local legend, he lived in very poor circumstances, and when the authorities tried to <a href="/wiki/Taxation" class="mw-redirect" title="Taxation">tax</a> him, he evaded them. Soldiers were sent out and they captured him, but on the way back to town, Mammes saw a lion attacking a <a href="/wiki/Domestic_sheep" class="mw-redirect" title="Domestic sheep">lamb</a>, escaped the soldiers, saved the lamb, jumped on the lion's back, and rode it into town. His bravery earned him exemption from taxation.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Mamas Monastery is the third most important place of worship for the Greek Orthodox in Cyprus after <a href="/wiki/St._Barnabas" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Barnabas">St. Barnabas</a> tomb at <a href="/wiki/Famagusta" title="Famagusta">Famagusta</a> and <a href="/wiki/Apostolos_Andreas_Monastery" title="Apostolos Andreas Monastery">Apostolos Andreas Monastery</a> at Karpasia.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Greece">Greece</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Greece">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>There are numerous churches dedicated to Mammes of Caesarea in Greece and there are even villages in <a href="/wiki/Greece" title="Greece">Greece</a> named <a href="/wiki/Agios_Mamas,_Chalkidiki" title="Agios Mamas, Chalkidiki">Agios Mamas</a> (Άγιος Μάμας in Greek) after him in <a href="/wiki/Chalkidiki" title="Chalkidiki">Chalkidiki</a>, <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a> and <a href="/wiki/Laconia" title="Laconia">Laconia</a>.
</p>
<h3><span id="Spain_.28San_Mam.C3.A9s.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Spain_(San_Mamés)">Spain (San Mamés)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Spain (San Mamés)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Pilgrims on their way to <a href="/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela" title="Santiago de Compostela">Santiago de Compostela</a> diffused his cult into <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>.
</p><p>A statue depicting Mammes and a lion can be found in the Casa de la Misericordia in <a href="/wiki/Bilbao" title="Bilbao">Bilbao</a>, which was once the <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a> of San Mamés and whose current chapel holds an alleged piece of bone from his cranium.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The home stadium used by <a href="/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao" title="Athletic Bilbao">Athletic Bilbao</a> is called <a href="/wiki/San_Mam%C3%A9s_Stadium_(2013)" title="San Mamés Stadium (2013)">San Mamés Stadium</a>, and players of that club are called the "<i>lions</i> of San Mamés". 
</p><p>His head is said to rest in the parish church of Santa María Magdalena in <a href="/wiki/Zaragoza" title="Zaragoza">Zaragoza</a>. 
</p><p>An alternative legend states that he was devoured by lions.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (November 2020)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>At <a href="/wiki/T%C3%A1bara" title="Tábara">Tábara</a>, also in Spain, he is venerated alongside <a href="/wiki/Saint_Blaise" title="Saint Blaise">Saint Blaise</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Italy_.28San_Mamete.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Italy_(San_Mamete)">Italy (San Mamete)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Italy (San Mamete)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_(Como)_2015-10-25.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_%28Como%29_2015-10-25.JPG/220px-Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_%28Como%29_2015-10-25.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="124" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_%28Como%29_2015-10-25.JPG/330px-Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_%28Como%29_2015-10-25.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_%28Como%29_2015-10-25.JPG/440px-Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_%28Como%29_2015-10-25.JPG 2x" data-file-width="3648" data-file-height="2048" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Chiesetta_di_sant%27Amate_a_circa_1623_m_s.l.m._-_Plesio_(Como)_2015-10-25.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The chapel of Sant'Amate on the mountain <a href="/wiki/Monte_Grona" title="Monte Grona">Monte Grona</a> in <a href="/wiki/Plesio" title="Plesio">Plesio</a> municipality, nearby <a href="/wiki/Como" title="Como">Como</a></div></div></div>
<p>In some regions of Italy, particularly <a href="/wiki/Brianza" title="Brianza">Brianza</a>, north of <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a>, San Mamete is particularly venerated by new mothers who wish to produce abundant breastmilk for a newborn. Special rites took place until recent times: the women who felt the need to ask the help of the Saint went to the church bringing some bread and cheese, which they placed upon the altar. The mother was obliged to offer the bread and cheese to the first person she encountered. This was hoped to achieve San Mamete's mercy through a lot of <i>pan di fioeu</i> (<a href="/wiki/Milanese_dialect" title="Milanese dialect">Milanese</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brianzoeu" class="mw-redirect" title="Brianzoeu">Brianzoeu</a> for "bread of children", i.e. milk).<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span id="Portugal_.28S.C3.A3o_Mamede.29"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Portugal_(São_Mamede)">Portugal (São Mamede)</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Mammes_of_Caesarea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Portugal (São Mamede)">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_S%C3%A3o_Mamede" title="Battle of São Mamede">Battle of São Mamede</a> is considered a crucial event in the foundation of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal" title="Kingdom of Portugal">Kingdom of Portugal</a>. A number of places in <a href="/wiki/Portugal" title="Portugal">Portugal</a> are named after this Christian saint; <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Mamede_(Lisbon)" title="São Mamede (Lisbon)">São Mamede (Lisbon)</a>, a quarter of <a href="/wiki/Lisbon" title="Lisbon">Lisbon</a>; <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Mamede_de_Este" class="mw-redirect" title="São Mamede de Este">São Mamede de Este</a>, a town in <a href="/wiki/Braga" title="Braga">Braga</a>; <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Mamede_de_Infesta" title="São Mamede de Infesta">São Mamede de Infesta</a>, a town in <a href="/wiki/Matosinhos" title="Matosinhos">Matosinhos</a>; the <a href="/wiki/Igreja_de_S%C3%A3o_Mamede" title="Igreja de São Mamede">Igreja de São Mamede</a>, a church building in <a href="/wiki/%C3%89vora" title="Évora">Évora</a>; the <a href="/wiki/Serra_de_S%C3%A3o_Mamede" title="Serra de São Mamede">Serra de São Mamede</a>, a mountain range in <a href="/wiki/Portalegre_District" title="Portalegre District">Portalegre District</a>; a town in <a href="/wiki/Trofa_Municipality" class="mw-redirect" title="Trofa Municipality">Trofa</a>, and <a href="/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Mamede,_Para%C3%ADba" title="São Mamede, Paraíba">São Mamede, Paraíba</a>, a municipality in the state of <a href="/wiki/Para%C3%ADba" title="Paraíba">Paraíba</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>.
</p>
