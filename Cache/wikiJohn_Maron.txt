<p><b>John Maron</b> (<a href="/wiki/Arabic_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Arabic language">Arabic</a>: <span lang="ar" dir="rtl">يوحنا مارون</span>, <i>Youhana Maroun</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Ioannes Maronus</i>; <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>: <span lang="syc" dir="rtl">ܝܘܚܢܢ ܡܪܘܢ</span>) (628, <a href="/wiki/Sirmaniyah" title="Sirmaniyah">Sirmaniyah</a> or <a href="/wiki/Sarmin" title="Sarmin">Sarmin</a>, present <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a> – 707, Kfarhy, <a href="/wiki/Lebanon" title="Lebanon">Lebanon</a>), was a <a href="/wiki/Syriac_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Syriac people">Syriac</a> monk, and the first <a href="/wiki/Maronite" class="mw-redirect" title="Maronite">Maronite</a> <a href="/wiki/List_of_Maronite_Patriarchs" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Maronite Patriarchs">Patriarch</a>. He is revered as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Maronite_Church" title="Maronite Church">Maronite Church</a> and universal <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, and is commemorated on March 2. He died and was buried in Kfarhy near <a href="/wiki/Batroun" title="Batroun">Batroun</a>, in Lebanon, where a shrine is dedicated to him.
</p><p>Jérôme Labourt, writing in the <i><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia" title="Catholic Encyclopedia">Catholic Encyclopedia</a></i> says that John Maron's "very existence is extremely doubtful<span class="nowrap">&#160;</span>... if he existed at all, it was as a simple monk".<sup id="cite_ref-Labourt_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Labourt-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> French theologian <a href="/wiki/Eus%C3%A8be_Renaudot" title="Eusèbe Renaudot">Eusèbe Renaudot</a> similarly held doubts regarding John Maron's existence.<sup id="cite_ref-Labourt_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Labourt-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Other scholarship has assessed John Maron as having existed and served as Maronite Patriarch when invasions by <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine emperor">Byzantine emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Justinian_II" title="Justinian II">Justinian II</a> were repulsed and the Maronite people gained a greater degree of political independence.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Maron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/7th_century_in_Lebanon" title="7th century in Lebanon">7th century in Lebanon</a></div>
<p>Maronite history prior to the sixteenth century is problematic as so many points are obscure.<sup id="cite_ref-Labourt_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Labourt-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> According to Maronite sources, John was born in Sarum, a town located south of the city of <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sjm_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sjm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was the son of Agathon and Anohamia. He was called John the Sarumite since his father was governor of Sarum.  His paternal grandfather, Prince Alidipas, was the nephew of Carloman, a Frankish Prince, and governed Antioch. John was educated in Antioch and the Monastery of Saint Maron, studying mathematics, sciences, philosophy, theology, philology and scripture. He became a monk at the monastery, adding the name Maron to his own.
</p><p>John studied <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a> and <a href="/wiki/Patrology" class="mw-redirect" title="Patrology">patrology</a> in <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-sjm_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sjm-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Returning to Saint Maron's, he wrote on such diverse topics as teaching, rhetoric, the sacraments, management of Church property, legislative techniques, and liturgy. He composed the Eucharistic Prayer which still bears his name. As a young priest he engaged himself in ecumenical debates with the Monophysites. Noted as a teacher and preacher, he explained the doctrine of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon" title="Council of Chalcedon">Council of Chalcedon</a> (which focused on the nature of Jesus as both God and human), wrote a series of letters to the faithful against <a href="/wiki/Monophysitism" title="Monophysitism">Monophysitism</a> which Beit-Marun then adopted, after which he purportedly travelled <a href="/wiki/Syria" title="Syria">Syria</a> to explain the heresy.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_first_Maronite_Patriarch">The first Maronite Patriarch</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Maron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The first Maronite Patriarch">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/8th_century_in_Lebanon" title="8th century in Lebanon">8th century in Lebanon</a></div>
<p>The Patriarch of Antioch, <a href="/wiki/Anastasius_II_of_Antioch" title="Anastasius II of Antioch">Anastasius II</a> was martyred in 609. With the ongoing <a href="/wiki/Byzantine%E2%80%93Sasanian_War_of_602%E2%80%93628" title="Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628">Byzantine–Sasanian War</a> and general unrest in the area, Constantinople began to appoint a series of titular patriarchs.<sup id="cite_ref-Gervers_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gervers-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Maronite sources give the date of John Maron's election to Patriarch of Antioch and All the East as 685.<sup id="cite_ref-Gervers_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gervers-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> John received the approval of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Sergius_I" title="Pope Sergius I">Pope Sergius I</a>,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (October 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and became the first Maronite Patriarch.
</p>
