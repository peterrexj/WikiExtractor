<p><b>Saint Evodius</b> or <b>Euodias</b> (died circa 69) was an <a href="/wiki/Early_Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Christian">Early Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/List_of_Patriarchs_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Patriarchs of Antioch">bishop of Antioch</a>, <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_succession" title="Apostolic succession">succeeding</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a>. He is regarded as one of the first identifiable <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a>, and venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Evodius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Little is known of the life of Evodius. At the time, <a href="/wiki/Antioch" title="Antioch">Antioch</a> was an opulent and cosmopolitan city where both <a href="/wiki/Hellenization" title="Hellenization">Hellenized</a> Jews and pagans were influenced by <a href="/wiki/Monotheism" title="Monotheism">monotheism</a>. 
Saint Peter became the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Antioch">bishop of Antioch</a> and led the church there. 
</p><p>Evodius was one of the 72 disciples of Christ.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He eventually succeeded Peter as bishop of Antioch when Peter left for <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Rome" title="Ancient Rome">Rome</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Evodius was <a href="/wiki/List_of_Patriarchs_of_Antioch" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Patriarchs of Antioch">bishop of Antioch</a> until 66 <a href="/wiki/AD" class="mw-redirect" title="AD">AD</a>, and was succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch" title="Ignatius of Antioch">Saint Ignatius of Antioch</a>. <a href="/wiki/Catholic_tradition" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic tradition">Catholic tradition</a> says it is likely that Saint Evodius died of natural causes, however, the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> tradition holds that he was <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyred</a> under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Nero" title="Nero">Nero</a> in 66 AD.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As one of the first pagans to come to the new church, he is venerated as a saint in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, <a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodox Church">Oriental Orthodox Church</a>, as well as the <a href="/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East" title="Assyrian Church of the East">Assyrian Church of the East</a>. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 6 May in the Catholic Church and 7 September in the Eastern Orthodox Church.
</p><p>In one of his many writings, Evodius claims that <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a> gave birth to <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Jesus Christ</a>, the Saviour, when she was fifteen.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2023)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
