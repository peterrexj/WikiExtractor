<p>Saint <b>Eliphius</b> or <b>Eloff</b> (<i>Élophe, Éliphe, Alophe</i>) is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.  Tradition holds that he was the child of a king of <a href="/wiki/Scotia" title="Scotia">Scotia</a>, and preached in <a href="/wiki/Toul" title="Toul">Toul</a>, where he converted 400 people to Christianity.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  He was accompanied by his siblings: <b>St. Eucharius</b>, and three sisters, <b>Menna, Libaria, and Susanna</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-lanigan_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanigan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  
Tradition also makes him a <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Toul" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Toul">bishop of Toul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-macmanus_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-macmanus-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In regard to their alleged royal and Irish birth, James Henthorn Todd states “that we cannot place much reliance on the statement that they were the children of a king of Scotia.  Their names are not Irish.”<sup id="cite_ref-henthorn_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-henthorn-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  In the 12th century, Abbot Rupert of Duitz (<i>Rupertus Tuitiensis</i>), author of the <i>Acts</i> of the saint, was of the opinion that Eliphius and his siblings were natives of Toul.<sup id="cite_ref-henthorn_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-henthorn-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In his <i>History of Lorraine</i>, <a href="/wiki/Antoine_Augustin_Calmet" title="Antoine Augustin Calmet">Antoine Augustin Calmet</a> does not mention Scotia (which can refer to Ireland or Scotland) and states that according to some scholars, Eliphius could be a native of <a href="/wiki/Soulosse-sous-Saint-%C3%89lophe" title="Soulosse-sous-Saint-Élophe">Soulosse-sous-Saint-Élophe</a> or Gran;<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> as well as the princely son of a man named Baccius.<sup id="cite_ref-lanigan_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanigan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-henthorn_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-henthorn-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>All of the siblings, except Menna,<sup id="cite_ref-lanigan_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lanigan-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> were beheaded at or near Toul, at the order of <a href="/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate" class="mw-redirect" title="Julian the Apostate">Julian the Apostate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Eliphius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>They were buried at Mount Eliph.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>  The <i><a href="/wiki/Lorsch_Codex" class="mw-redirect" title="Lorsch Codex">Lorsch Codex</a></i> mentions the donation of the relics of St. Eliphius to the <a href="/wiki/Great_St._Martin_Church,_Cologne" title="Great St. Martin Church, Cologne">Great St. Martin Church, Cologne</a> and was the second named patron of the church.<sup id="cite_ref-Paul_Clemen_page_354_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Paul_Clemen_page_354-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>  These relics were later transferred to Toul.<sup id="cite_ref-Paul_Clemen_page_354_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Paul_Clemen_page_354-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
