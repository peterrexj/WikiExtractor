					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">3rd-century Romano-British Christian martyrs</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">

</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saints</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Julius and Aaron</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data"><span title="circa">c.</span> 304 AD<br />Caerleon, Britain, Roman Empire</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Honoured&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">1 July (<abbr title="Traditional">trad.</abbr><sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>)<br />22 June (<a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Wales" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Church in Wales">Cath.</a><sup id="cite_ref-marro_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marro-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>)<br /><span class="nowrap">20 June (<a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Wales" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic Church in Wales">Cath.</a><sup id="cite_ref-litoff_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-litoff-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and <a href="/wiki/Church_in_Wales" title="Church in Wales">Ang.</a><sup id="cite_ref-ciw_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ciw-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>)</span></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Julius</b> and <b>Aaron</b> (also <b>Julian</b>) were two <a href="/wiki/Christianity_in_Roman_Britain" title="Christianity in Roman Britain">Romano-British Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/List_of_Welsh_saints" title="List of Welsh saints">saints</a> who were martyred around the third century. Along with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Alban" title="Saint Alban">Saint Alban</a>, they are the only named Christian martyrs from <a href="/wiki/Roman_Britain" title="Roman Britain">Roman Britain</a>. Most historians place the martyrdom in <a href="/wiki/Caerleon" title="Caerleon">Caerleon</a>, although other suggestions have placed it in <a href="/wiki/Chester" title="Chester">Chester</a> or <a href="/wiki/Leicester" title="Leicester">Leicester</a>. Their <a href="/wiki/Gwyl_Mabsant" class="mw-redirect" title="Gwyl Mabsant">feast day</a> was traditionally celebrated on 1 July,<sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> but it is now observed together with Alban on 20 June by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican</a> Churches.<sup id="cite_ref-litoff_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-litoff-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-ciw_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ciw-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The earliest surviving account of Julius and Aaron comes from <a href="/wiki/Gildas" title="Gildas">Gildas</a>, a monk writing in Western Britain during the sixth century. How accurate his account of events that occurred three centuries before is remains unknown. Gildas' account was later repeated by the eighth-century <a href="/wiki/Anglo-Saxons" title="Anglo-Saxons">Anglo-Saxon</a> monk <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>. References to Julius and Aaron were included in the work of later medieval authors like <a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth" title="Geoffrey of Monmouth">Geoffrey of Monmouth</a> and <a href="/wiki/Giraldus_Cambrensis" class="mw-redirect" title="Giraldus Cambrensis">Giraldus Cambrensis</a>.
</p><p>Gildas implied that a <a href="/wiki/Martyrium_(architecture)" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrium (architecture)">martyrium</a> dedicated to Julius and Aaron was present by the sixth century, and a chapel dedicated to the saints certainly in existence near Caerleon by the ninth century, when it was recorded in a land charter. In the early twelfth century, the church passed into the property of the new <a href="/wiki/Goldcliff_Priory" title="Goldcliff Priory">Goldcliff Priory</a>, and by 1142 had been renamed in dedication to St Alban as well as Julius and Aaron, reflecting the growing popularity of the former's cult. In later centuries, the chapel's associations with Julius and Aaron were forgotten. By the time of the sixteenth-century <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>, when the chapel was abandoned and perhaps converted into a barn, it was solely referred to as a Church of Saint Alban. The building fell into dilapidation and no longer survives.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg/220px-Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg/330px-Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c4/Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg/440px-Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1024" data-file-height="683" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Aerial_view_of_Caerleon_Roman_amphitheatre.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Romano-British amphitheatre in Caerleon, the settlement where Julius and Aaron were reportedly martyred</div></div></div>
<p>Julius and Aaron are two of the three Christian martyrs recorded as having lived in Roman Britain, the other being <a href="/wiki/St_Alban" class="mw-redirect" title="St Alban">St Alban</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHenig1984125Watts199110_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenig1984125Watts199110-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Nothing is known of them except for their martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941342Farmer2011_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941342Farmer2011-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The name "Aaron" is <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a> and might suggest an individual of Jewish heritage.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEHenig1984125Petts200388_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenig1984125Petts200388-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> The name was exceptionally rare in both Jewish and Christian contexts at that time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200139_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200139-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The name "Julius" was extremely common among the soldiers at Caerleon, reflecting either descent from one of the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Julio-Claudian_Coloniae&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Julio-Claudian Coloniae (page does not exist)">Julio-Claudian Coloniae</a> or a name taken on enlistment in the army.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200139_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200139-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Although Caerleon was a major military base in western Britain, there was a civilian settlement association with the fort, and thus Julius and Aaron could have been civilians rather than soldiers.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPetts200331_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetts200331-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Date_of_martyrdom">Date of martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Date of martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The Roman military fort at Caerleon held the <a href="/wiki/Legio_II_Augusta" title="Legio II Augusta">Legio II Augusta</a>, and witnessed much usage under the Roman Emperors <a href="/wiki/Septimius_Severus" title="Septimius Severus">Septimius Severus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Caracalla" title="Caracalla">Caracalla</a> although saw a reduction in its occupation during the third century, when much of the legion was stationed elsewhere.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200138_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200138-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
The fort fell out of use between circa 287 and 296, when many of its buildings were demolished.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200138Petts200331_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200138Petts200331-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> The core of the legion were reassigned to the <a href="/wiki/Richborough_Castle" title="Richborough Castle">Richborough Fort</a> in modern <a href="/wiki/Kent" title="Kent">Kent</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200138–39_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200138–39-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Given the abandonment of the fort at the end of the third century, it is unlikely that Julius and Aaron would have been killed as part of the anti-Christian persecutions which took place in the early fourth century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPetts200331_9-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetts200331-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is more probable that they might have been executed during one of the phases of anti-Christian agitation which broke out in the empire during the mid-third century, particularly between 249 and 251 under Emperor <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decius</a> and then between 257 and 259 under <a href="/wiki/Valerian_(emperor)" title="Valerian (emperor)">Valerian</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPetts200331_9-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetts200331-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> More broadly, Jeremy K. Knight noted that it probably took place at some point between Septimius Severus' prohibition on anyone converting to Christianity and <a href="/wiki/Aurelian" title="Aurelian">Aurelian</a>'s death in 275, for his successor <a href="/wiki/Constantius_Chlorus" title="Constantius Chlorus">Constantius Chlorus</a> did not reportedly execute Christians but restricted himself to the demolition of their churches.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200139_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200139-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Gildas_and_Bede">Gildas and Bede</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Gildas and Bede">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The primary evidence for Julius and Aaron comes from the writings of <a href="/wiki/Gildas" title="Gildas">Gildas</a>, who was writing somewhere in western Britain during the early to mid sixth century.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200138_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200138-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> There remains a question as to how the events that likely took place in third-century Caerleon were transmitted to Gildas, writing three centuries later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200139,_41_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200139,_41-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> There is also the question as to how accurate his information about the events of the Romano-British past was; some of his claims, such as that <a href="/wiki/Hadrian%27s_Wall" title="Hadrian&#39;s Wall">Hadrian's Wall</a> was built by Septimius Severus, were incorrect.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141,_42_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141,_42-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> Jeremy K. Knight believed that Gildas' information on Julius and Aaron should be taken seriously, for he was "a muddled, but honest, witness" to the information he received.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200142_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200142-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gildas' <i><a href="/wiki/De_Excidio_et_Conquestu_Britanniae" title="De Excidio et Conquestu Britanniae">De Excidio et Conquestu Britanniae</a></i> is the first surviving source to mention them,<sup id="cite_ref-Garcia_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Garcia-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> and he writes that during the <a href="/wiki/Diocletian" title="Diocletian">Diocletian</a> persecution, "God... kindled up among us bright luminaries of holy martyrs.... Such were St. Alban of Verulam, Aaron and Julius, citizens of the city of legions and the rest, of both sexes, who in different places stood their ground in the Christian contest."<sup id="cite_ref-The_Ruin_of_Britain_17-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-The_Ruin_of_Britain-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Bede" title="Bede">Bede</a>, drawing on <a href="/wiki/Gildas" title="Gildas">Gildas</a>, says in the <i><a href="/wiki/Ecclesiastical_History_of_the_English_People" title="Ecclesiastical History of the English People">Ecclesiastical History of the English People</a></i> that in the same persecution during which Alban was martyred, so "suffered Aaron and Julius, citizens of Caerleon, and many others of both sexes throughout the land. After they had endured many horrible physical tortures, death brought an end to their struggles."<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bede repeats what Gildas has said but adds no additional information about the two martyrs.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200138_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200138-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Alternative_suggested_locations">Alternative suggested locations</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Alternative suggested locations">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Historians have generally identified Caerleon as the location of the pair's martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBreeze201631_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBreeze201631-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
There is some evidence to suggest that the martyrdom may have occurred not in <a href="/wiki/Caerleon" title="Caerleon">Caerleon</a> but in <a href="/wiki/Chester" title="Chester">Chester</a>. When Gildas first mentions Julius and Aaron, he says that they were martyred in the "City of Legions", or <i>legionum urbis</i>. That could have referred to a number of legionary fortresses, including Chester and <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>, both of which carry the name in a number of sources.<sup id="cite_ref-Garcia_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Garcia-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> Archaeological excavations at an amphitheatre in Chester have uncovered a structure that may have been used for public executions in the Diocletian period and the possible remains of an early medieval church, which might be related to a Roman martyrdom site.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2016, Andrew Breeze argued that <a href="/wiki/Leicester" title="Leicester">Leicester</a> may have been the location of Aaron and Julius' martyrdom.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBreeze2016_21-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBreeze2016-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrium">Martyrium</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Martyrium">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Gildas' account implies that a <a href="/wiki/Martyrium_(architecture)" class="mw-redirect" title="Martyrium (architecture)">martyrium</a> of Julius and Aaron existed at Caerleon.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200140_22-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200140-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup> Bolstering this is the fact that the twelfth-century <a href="/wiki/Book_of_Llandaff" title="Book of Llandaff">Book of Llandaff</a> contained a late ninth-century charter that mentions the existence of such a martyrium.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941340Knight200140_23-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941340Knight200140-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup> This charter described a grant of land to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Llandaff" title="Bishop of Llandaff">Bishop of Llandaff</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Nudd_(bishop)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Nudd (bishop) (page does not exist)">Nudd</a> which encompassed the <i>Territorium Sanctorum Martirum Julii et Aaron</i> or the <i>Merthir Iún et Aaron</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941340Knight200140_23-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941340Knight200140-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-sabi_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sabi-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Possible archaeological evidence for the existence of the early medieval martyrium comes in the form of a ninth-century sculptured cross slab which was found at Bulmore Farm, near the site of the later medieval martyrium, shortly before 1862. Stylistically part of the Gwent Group cross-slabs, most other examples come from major early churches such as <a href="/wiki/St_Cadoc%27s_Church,_Caerleon" title="St Cadoc&#39;s Church, Caerleon">St Cadoc's Church, Caerleon</a>, <a href="/wiki/St._Arvans" title="St. Arvans">St Arvans Church</a> near <a href="/wiki/Chepstow" title="Chepstow">Chepstow</a>, and St Tatheus's Church in <a href="/wiki/Caerwent" title="Caerwent">Caerwent</a>. <sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200140–41_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200140–41-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:227px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org,uk_-_754834.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org%2Cuk_-_754834.jpg/225px-Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org%2Cuk_-_754834.jpg" decoding="async" width="225" height="168" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org%2Cuk_-_754834.jpg/338px-Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org%2Cuk_-_754834.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org%2Cuk_-_754834.jpg/450px-Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org%2Cuk_-_754834.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="479" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Newport_wetlands_-_geograph.org,uk_-_754834.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>View toward the <a href="/wiki/Bristol_Channel" title="Bristol Channel">Bristol Channel</a> at <a href="/wiki/Goldcliff" class="mw-redirect" title="Goldcliff">Goldcliff</a>, with the site of Goldcliff Priory (Hill Farm house and outbuildings) in the distance</div></div></div>
<p>The next surviving textual reference to the martyrium dates from two centuries later.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941341_25-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941341-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> The "<i>ecclesiam Iulii et Aron</i>" is one of two churches mentioned in a record dating from circa 1113 recording how the local Norman landowner <a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Chandos&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Robert of Chandos (page does not exist)">Robert of Chandos</a> donated land in the area, including both churches, to the <a href="/wiki/Bec_Abbey" title="Bec Abbey">Le Bec</a> monastery in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> so that they might establish a <a href="/wiki/Priory" title="Priory">priory</a> on it.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941341_25-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941341-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup> Both the church of Saints Julius and Aaron and the Church of St Trinity again are mentioned in confirmations of the endowment of <a href="/wiki/Goldcliff_Priory" title="Goldcliff Priory">Goldcliff Priory</a>, one produced in c.1154–58, and the other in 1204 by <a href="/wiki/Hubert_Walter" title="Hubert Walter">Hubert Walter</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941341_25-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941341-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A dedication to St Alban was later added to the chapel of St Julius and St Aaron.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941341Knight200140_26-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941341Knight200140-26">&#91;26&#93;</a></sup> In 1142, the monks of Goldcliff had a confirmation of their property produced which referred to the martyrium as the <i>ecclesiam sanctorum Iulii et Aaron atque Albani</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941342_27-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941342-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Saint Alban's popularity was growing in the twelfth century, with various ecclesiastical sites housing relics associated with him. The monks at Goldcliff may have wanted to connect their chapel to this growing cult in particular because Aaron and Julius were mentioned alongside Alban as Romano-British martyrs in the work of Gildas and Bede.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941342_27-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941342-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup> Between 1113 and 1143 they likely obtained what were regarded as bones of Alban to use as relics in the chapel, thus giving substance to its renaming.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941342_27-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941342-27">&#91;27&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the fifteenth century, Goldcliff Priory was passed from the ownership of Bec Abbey to <a href="/wiki/Tewkesbury_Abbey" title="Tewkesbury Abbey">Tewkesbury Abbey</a> and then on to <a href="/wiki/Eton_College" title="Eton College">Eton College</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941343_28-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941343-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> Over time, the martyrium lost its associations with Julius and Aaron and came to be associated solely with Alban.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> This chapel of St Alban remained in existence until the <a href="/wiki/English_Reformation" title="English Reformation">English Reformation</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> In 1495, its three <a href="/wiki/Procurator_(canon_law)" class="mw-redirect" title="Procurator (canon law)">procurators</a> leased a Caerleon tenement to a man named John Matthews.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> By 1624, the area, known as Mount St Albans, was the seat of the Powell family.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> A lease document produced in 1728 lists the associated lands, which included "<i>Cae'r Fynwent</i>" ("The Field of the Graveyard") and "<i>Cae'r Scubor</i>" (The Field of the Barn").<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> The barn mentioned in this document may have been the former martyrium, for several lesser churches in <a href="/wiki/Monmouthshire" title="Monmouthshire">Monmouthshire</a> were converted into barns at this time.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1798 the historian <a href="/wiki/William_Coxe_(historian)" title="William Coxe (historian)">William Coxe</a> described the site as being marked by a <a href="/wiki/Yew_tree" class="mw-redirect" title="Yew tree">yew tree</a>; he added that in 1785 "several stone coffins were discovered in digging for the foundations of a new house".<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTECoxe180195Knight200141_30-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTECoxe180195Knight200141-30">&#91;30&#93;</a></sup>
The site of the martyrium is now a sloping field adjacent to houses known as Mount St Alban.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> In 1941, the historian Wilhelm Levinson expressed his hope that archaeologists would excavate the site to learn more about the original creation of the martyrium.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevinson1941344_31-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevinson1941344-31">&#91;31&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Medieval_literature">Medieval literature</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Medieval literature">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_of_Monmouth" title="Geoffrey of Monmouth">Geoffrey of Monmouth</a> introduced Julius and Aaron into his discussion of <a href="/wiki/King_Arthur" title="King Arthur">King Arthur</a>'s crown wearing at Caerleon. According to Geoffrey of Monmouth's account, Arthur's Caerleon contained two great churches, one a <a href="/wiki/Nunnery" class="mw-redirect" title="Nunnery">nunnery</a> dedicated to Julius and the other a house of <a href="/wiki/Regular_canons" class="mw-redirect" title="Regular canons">regular canons</a> devoted to Aaron which contained a university.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup> These two churches were fictitious, although in later centuries <a href="/wiki/Antiquarianism" class="mw-redirect" title="Antiquarianism">antiquarians</a> assumed that they were real and made attempts to locate them.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEKnight200141_29-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnight200141-29">&#91;29&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Geoffrey's account influenced that of later writers like <a href="/wiki/Gerald_of_Wales" title="Gerald of Wales">Gerald of Wales</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTELevison1941343_28-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTELevison1941343-28">&#91;28&#93;</a></sup> In turn, Gerald's portrayal of Aaron and Julius influenced the account produced by the antiquarian <a href="/wiki/John_Leland_(antiquary)" title="John Leland (antiquary)">John Leland</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBreeze201631_19-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBreeze201631-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Modern">Modern</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Julius_and_Aaron&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Modern">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:202px;"><a href="/wiki/File:SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church,_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church%2C_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg/200px-SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church%2C_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church%2C_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg/300px-SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church%2C_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church%2C_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg/400px-SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church%2C_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:SS_Julius_and_Aaron_Church,_Newport_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1736824.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>SS Julius and Aaron Church, Newport</div></div></div>
<p>In the sixteenth century, knowledge of Aaron and Julius was spread through the publication of printed editions of Gildas' <i>De Excidio</i> (1525) and Bede's <i>Ecclesiastical History</i> (1565).<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBreeze201630–31_32-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBreeze201630–31-32">&#91;32&#93;</a></sup> In doing so, the saints became familiar to both Roman Catholic and Protestant communities.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEBreeze201631_19-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBreeze201631-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The 2004 edition of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> recognizes the martyrs as being martyred after Alban during the persecution of Diocletian by the legionaries of <i>Brittania Minor</i> (<a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>), during which many 'arrived at the glorious city [of heaven] after enduring painful tortures and severe flogging'.<sup id="cite_ref-marro_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-marro-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Roman Martyrology indexes Aaron and Julius under 22 June, but since it is also the date when Saints <a href="/wiki/John_Fisher" title="John Fisher">John Fisher</a> and <a href="/wiki/Thomas_More" title="Thomas More">Thomas More</a> are celebrated, the current <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> liturgical calendar for Wales commemorates them together with St Alban, on 20 June.<sup id="cite_ref-litoff_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-litoff-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
