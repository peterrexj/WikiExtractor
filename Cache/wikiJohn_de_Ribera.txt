<p><b>Juan de Ribera</b> (<a href="/wiki/Seville" title="Seville">Seville</a>, Spain, 20 March 1532 &#8211; <a href="/wiki/Valencia,_Spain" class="mw-redirect" title="Valencia, Spain">Valencia</a>, 6 January 1611) was an influential figure in 16th and 17th century Spain.  Ribera held appointments as Archbishop and Viceroy of Valencia, <a href="/wiki/Latin_Patriarchate_of_Antioch" title="Latin Patriarchate of Antioch">Latin Patriarchate of Antioch</a>, Commander in Chief, president of the Audiencia, and Chancellor of the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Valencia" title="University of Valencia">University of Valencia</a>.  He was beatified in 1796 and canonized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> in 1960.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Ribera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Juan de Ribera's father was <a href="/wiki/Pedro_Af%C3%A1n_de_Ribera" title="Pedro Afán de Ribera">Pedro Afán de Ribera</a>, <a href="/wiki/Viceroy_of_Naples" class="mw-redirect" title="Viceroy of Naples">Viceroy of Naples</a> and Duke of Alcala. His mother died when he was very young. 
</p><p>Ribera studied at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Salamanca" title="University of Salamanca">University of Salamanca</a>. Ordained as priest in 1557, Pope <a href="/wiki/Pius_IV" class="mw-redirect" title="Pius IV">Pius IV</a> appointed him <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Badajoz" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Badajoz">Bishop of Badajoz</a> on 27 May 1562 at the age of 30.  There he dedicated himself to teaching the <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to Roman Catholics and counteracting <a href="/wiki/Protestantism" title="Protestantism">Protestantism</a>. He was appointed as the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Valencia_in_Spain" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Valencia in Spain">Archbishop of Valencia</a> on 3 December 1568. In 1599 he ordained <a href="/wiki/Alfonso_Coloma" title="Alfonso Coloma">Alfonso Coloma</a> as Bishop of Barcelona. <a href="/wiki/Philip_III_of_Spain" title="Philip III of Spain">King Philip III of Spain</a> later appointed him <a href="/wiki/List_of_Spanish_Viceroys_of_Valencia" class="mw-redirect" title="List of Spanish Viceroys of Valencia">Viceroy of Valencia</a> in 1602, and thus he became both the religious and the civil authority.  In this role he founded the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Royal_College_of_Corpus_Christi,_Valencia&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Royal College of Corpus Christi, Valencia (page does not exist)">Museum of the Patriarch</a>, known among Valencians as the <i>College of Saint John</i>, entrusted to the formation of priests according to the spirit and the dispositions of the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>.
</p>

<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos">Expulsion of the Moriscos</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Ribera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Expulsion of the Moriscos">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos" title="Expulsion of the Moriscos">Expulsion of the Moriscos</a></div>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg/200px-Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="152" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg/300px-Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg/400px-Francisco_Domingo_Marques_El_Beato_Juan_de_Ribera_en.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1324" data-file-height="1008" /></a><figcaption>Juan de Ribera in the expulsion of the Moriscos by <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Domingo_Marqu%C3%A9s" title="Francisco Domingo Marqués">Francisco Domingo Marqués</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>As archbishop, Ribera dealt with the issue of Valencia's large <a href="/wiki/Morisco" title="Morisco">Morisco</a> population, descendants of Muslims who converted to Christianity at threat of exile.  The Moriscos had been kept separate from the main population by a variety of decrees that prohibited them from holding public office, entering the priesthood, or taking certain other positions; as a result, the Moriscos had maintained their own culture rather than assimilated.  Some of them did, in fact, still practice forms of crypto-Islam.<sup id="cite_ref-lyn43_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lyn43-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Ribera despised the Moriscos as heretics and traitors, a dislike he shared with much of Valencia's Christian populace.<sup id="cite_ref-lyn44_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lyn44-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  With the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Lerma" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Lerma">Duke of Lerma</a>, Ribera helped convince Philip III to at least expel the Moriscos instead. Ribera helped sell the plan by noting that all the property of the Moriscos could be impounded to provide money for the treasury.<sup id="cite_ref-lyn44_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lyn44-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  In 1609, the <a href="/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos" title="Expulsion of the Moriscos">expulsion of the Moriscos</a> from Spain was decreed.  Ribera's original proposal was in fact more extreme: he favored <a href="/wiki/Slavery" title="Slavery">enslaving</a> the entire Morisco population for work in <a href="/wiki/Galley" title="Galley">galleys</a>, mines, and <a href="/wiki/Slavery_in_the_Spanish_New_World_colonies" class="mw-redirect" title="Slavery in the Spanish New World colonies">abroad</a>.  Ribera said that Philip III could do so "without any scruples of conscience," but this proposal was rejected.<sup id="cite_ref-lyn44_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lyn44-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> If the Moriscos were to be expelled, Ribera favored enslaving and Christianizing at least the children of the Moriscos "for the good of their souls" and exiling the parents.  This was also rejected, though children under 16 years of age who wished to remain in Spain were allowed, an offer very few took.<sup id="cite_ref-lyn46_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lyn46-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Juan_de_Ribera&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2><p>
Efforts to canonize Ribera, who himself had been active in attempting to canonize <a href="/wiki/Ignatius_of_Loyola" title="Ignatius of Loyola">Ignatius of Loyola</a>, began shortly after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-ehlers_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ehlers-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Two concerns were raised about his possible sainthood: his failure to hold a provincial council as mandated by the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Trent" title="Council of Trent">Council of Trent</a>, and his role in the <a href="/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos" title="Expulsion of the Moriscos">expulsion of the Moriscos</a>.  His supporters played up Ribera's adherence to other parts of the Council of Trent, and tried to present the Moriscos as unconvertible ("[His conversion attempts] had no more effect on the Moriscos as if they had been stones").<sup id="cite_ref-ehlers_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ehlers-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  Still, efforts proceeded apace, with various admiring biographies (<i>vidas</i>) of Ribera being published.  Ribera was beatified in 1796.  In 1960, his canonization was completed under the auspices of <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a>. </p><div style="clear:both;"></div>
