<p><b>Saint William of Volpiano</b> (Italian: <i>Guglielmo da Volpiano</i>; French: <i>Guillaume de Volpiano</i>, also <b>of Dijon</b>, <b>of Saint-Benignus</b>, or <b>of Fécamp</b>; June/July 962 – 1 January 1031) was a <a href="/wiki/Northern_Italian" class="mw-redirect" title="Northern Italian">Northern Italian</a> monastic reformer, composer, and founding abbot of numerous abbeys in <a href="/wiki/Burgundy" title="Burgundy">Burgundy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_(Holy_Roman_Empire)" title="Kingdom of Italy (Holy Roman Empire)">Italy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_career">Life and career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=William_of_Volpiano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life and career">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1096954695/mw-parser-output/.tmulti">.mw-parser-output .tmulti .multiimageinner{display:flex;flex-direction:column}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .trow{display:flex;flex-direction:row;clear:left;flex-wrap:wrap;width:100%;box-sizing:border-box}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .tsingle{margin:1px;float:left}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .theader{clear:both;font-weight:bold;text-align:center;align-self:center;background-color:transparent;width:100%}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .thumbcaption{background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .text-align-left{text-align:left}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .text-align-right{text-align:right}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .text-align-center{text-align:center}@media all and (max-width:720px){.mw-parser-output .tmulti .thumbinner{width:100%!important;box-sizing:border-box;max-width:none!important;align-items:center}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .trow{justify-content:center}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .tsingle{float:none!important;max-width:100%!important;box-sizing:border-box;text-align:center}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .tsingle .thumbcaption{text-align:left}.mw-parser-output .tmulti .trow>.thumbcaption{text-align:center}}</style><div class="thumb tmulti tleft"><div class="thumbinner multiimageinner" style="width:279px;max-width:279px"><div class="trow"><div class="tsingle" style="width:277px;max-width:277px"><div class="thumbimage"><a href="/wiki/File:Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg/275px-Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg" decoding="async" width="275" height="147" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg/413px-Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg/550px-Orta_Isola_San_Giulio.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1022" data-file-height="546" /></a></div><div class="thumbcaption">Isola San Giulio; William of Volpiano was born here in 962 AD</div></div></div><div class="trow"><div class="tsingle" style="width:277px;max-width:277px"><div class="thumbimage"><a href="/wiki/File:Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg/275px-Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg" decoding="async" width="275" height="219" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg/413px-Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg/550px-Fecamp_Abbey_Chevet10.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2140" data-file-height="1704" /></a></div><div class="thumbcaption"><a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_F%C3%A9camp" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Fécamp">Abbey of Fécamp</a></div></div></div></div></div>
<p>Not much is known about him. The main source is a <i><a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">Vita</a></i> of the monk <a href="/wiki/Raoul_Glaber" class="mw-redirect" title="Raoul Glaber">Raoul Glaber</a>, a novice who accompanied William and who sometimes regarded his master as a rival, but also as a mentor who encouraged his work as a chronicler.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>William was born on the family <a href="/wiki/Citadel" title="Citadel">citadel</a> on the <a href="/wiki/San_Giulio_Island" title="San Giulio Island">island of San Giulio</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lake_Orta" title="Lake Orta">Lake Orta</a>, <a href="/wiki/Province_of_Novara" title="Province of Novara">Novara</a>, <a href="/wiki/Piedmont" title="Piedmont">Piedmont</a>. The son of Count Robert of <a href="/wiki/Volpiano" title="Volpiano">Volpiano</a>, he was born during an assault on the citadel by the <a href="/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Otto I, Holy Roman Emperor">Emperor Otto</a>.  The assault being successful, Otto became the sponsor and <a href="/wiki/Patronage" title="Patronage">patron</a> of Count Robert's son.
</p><p>The fourth son of Count Robert, in 969, at the age of seven, he began his education at the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> abbey at Locadio, <a href="/wiki/Vercelli" title="Vercelli">Vercelli</a>.  He became a monk at this abbey. In 987, he became a monk at the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Cluny" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Cluny">Abbey of Cluny</a> under <a href="/wiki/Saint_Majolus" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Majolus">Saint Majolus</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-potts_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-potts-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Zealous for reform, Saint Majolus had reorganized <a href="/wiki/Saint-Sernin_Abbaye" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-Sernin Abbaye">Saint Sernin Abbey</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne_River" class="mw-redirect" title="Rhône River">Rhône River</a>.
</p><p>William was ordained in 990 and served as abbot of Saint Benignus' Abbey at Dijon, dedicated to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Benignus_of_Dijon" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Benignus of Dijon">Saint Benignus of Dijon</a>. Under William's direction, and his zeal for the <a href="/wiki/Cluniac_reform" class="mw-redirect" title="Cluniac reform">Cluniac reform</a>, St. Benignus' became a center of spirituality, education, and culture.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It also became the mother house of some forty other monasteries in <a href="/wiki/Burgundy_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy (region)">Burgundy</a>, <a href="/wiki/Duchy_of_Lorraine" title="Duchy of Lorraine">Lorraine</a>, <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a>, and northern Italy.
</p><p>In 1001, he was called to rebuild the destructed <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_F%C3%A9camp" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Fécamp">Abbey of Fécamp</a><sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> (present-day department of <a href="/wiki/Seine-Maritime" title="Seine-Maritime">Seine-Maritime</a>) by <a href="/wiki/Richard_II_of_Normandy" class="mw-redirect" title="Richard II of Normandy">Richard II</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-potts_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-potts-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> where the <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Normandy" title="Duke of Normandy">Dukes of Normandy</a> had their palace and had chosen to be buried. William had to supervise the (re)construction and to found several abbeys in Normandy (<a href="/wiki/Bernay_Abbey" title="Bernay Abbey">Bernay</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Mont_Saint-Michel" class="mw-redirect" title="Mont Saint-Michel">Mont Saint-Michel</a>). In 1015 he became abbot of <a href="/wiki/Jumi%C3%A8ges_Abbey" title="Jumièges Abbey">Jumièges Abbey</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-potts_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-potts-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was chosen as building <a href="/wiki/General_contractor" title="General contractor">contractor</a> for <a href="/wiki/Mont_Saint-Michel" class="mw-redirect" title="Mont Saint-Michel">Mont Saint-Michel</a> in the 11th century. He designed the <a href="/wiki/Romanesque_architecture" title="Romanesque architecture">Romanesque</a> church of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a>, daringly placing the transept crossing at the top of the mount.  Many underground <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypts</a> and <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapels</a> had to be built to compensate for this weight.  These formed the basis for the supportive upward structure that can be seen today. He also rebuilt the <a href="/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-Germain-des-Pr%C3%A9s" class="mw-redirect" title="Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés">Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés</a>.
</p><p>William died of <a href="/wiki/Natural_causes" class="mw-redirect" title="Natural causes">natural causes</a> at Fécamp.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (December 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
