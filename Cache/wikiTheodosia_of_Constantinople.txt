<p>Saint <b>Theodosia of Constantinople</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Ἁγία Θεοδοσία ἡ Κωνσταντινουπολίτισσα</span>, <small><a href="/wiki/Romanization_of_Greek" title="Romanization of Greek">romanized</a>:&#160;</small><span title="Greek-language romanization"><i lang="el-Latn">Hagia Theodosia hē Kōnstantinoupolitissa</i></span>) is a <a href="/wiki/Christian" class="mw-redirect" title="Christian">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nun</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> who lived through and opposed the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm" title="Byzantine Iconoclasm">Byzantine Iconoclasm</a> of the seventh and eight centuries.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodosia_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to a biography published by the <a href="/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America" title="Orthodox Church in America">Orthodox Church in America</a>, Theodosia "was born in answer to the fervent prayers of her parents." When they died, she was sent to be raised at the women's Monastery of Holy Martyr Anastasia in Constantinople. She "distributed to the poor of what remained of her parental inheritance" after which she became a nun.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Martyrdom">Martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodosia_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>On January 19, 729, at the beginning of the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Iconoclasm" title="Byzantine Iconoclasm">iconoclastic</a> <a href="/wiki/Persecution" title="Persecution">persecutions</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Byzantine Emperor">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Leo_III_the_Isaurian" title="Leo III the Isaurian">Leo III the Isaurian</a> demanded that an <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a> which stood over the grand <a href="/wiki/Chalke_Gate" class="mw-redirect" title="Chalke Gate">Chalke Gate</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Great_Palace_of_Constantinople" title="Great Palace of Constantinople">imperial palace</a> be removed.<sup id="cite_ref-mb299_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mb299-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-sc82_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sc82-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Anastasius_of_Constantinople" title="Anastasius of Constantinople">Anastasius of Constantinople</a> ordered that the church comply.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>While an officer was executing the order, a group of women gathered to prevent the operation. Among them was Theodosia, who shook the ladder strongly until the officer fell from it. The man died from his injuries, and Theodosia was arrested and brought to the <i><a href="/wiki/Forum_of_the_Ox" title="Forum of the Ox">Forum Bovis</a></i>. There, she was executed by having a ram's horn hammered through her neck.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodosia_of_Constantinople&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Following the <a href="/wiki/Triumph_of_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Triumph of Orthodoxy">Triumph of Orthodoxy</a> over iconoclasm, she was recognized as a martyr and saint, and her body was kept and venerated in the church of <i>Hagia Euphemia en to Petrio</i>, in the quarter named <i>Dexiokratianai</i>, named after the houses owned here by one <i>Dexiokrates</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-ja151_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ja151-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> After the beginning of the fourteenth century, the church was renamed for her, and may correspond to the <a href="/wiki/Mosque" title="Mosque">mosque</a> of <a href="/wiki/G%C3%BCl_Mosque" title="Gül Mosque">Gül</a>.  A gate in the sea <a href="/wiki/Walls_of_Constantinople" title="Walls of Constantinople">walls of Constantinople</a>, the <i>Gate of Hagia Theodosia</i> (<a href="/wiki/Turkish_language" title="Turkish language">Turkish</a>: <i><a href="/wiki/Ayakap%C4%B1" title="Ayakapı">Ayakapı</a></i>) was named after her church. It corresponds to the modern neighborhood of <a href="/wiki/Ayakap%C4%B1" title="Ayakapı">Ayakapı</a>, along the <a href="/wiki/Golden_Horn" title="Golden Horn">Golden Horn</a>.
</p><p>Theodosia became one among the most venerated saints in Constantinople. She is invoked particularly by the infirm, her fame increased by the recovery of a deaf-mute in 1306.<sup id="cite_ref-ja151_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ja151-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Catholic Church celebrates her memorial on its original date of 18 July, while the Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic Churches transferred her commemoration to 29 May.
</p>
