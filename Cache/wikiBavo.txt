<p><b>Saint Bavo of Ghent</b> (also known as <b>Bavon</b>, <b>Allowin</b>, <b>Bavonius</b>,<sup id="cite_ref-tait_edinburgh_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tait_edinburgh-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> <b>Baaf</b>; AD 622–659) is a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. He was the son of <a href="/wiki/Pepin_of_Landen" title="Pepin of Landen">Pepin of Landen</a> and the brother of saints <a href="/wiki/Begga" title="Begga">Begga</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gertrude_of_Nivelles" title="Gertrude of Nivelles">Gertrude of Nivelles</a>.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bavo_of_Ghent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bavo was born near <a href="/wiki/Li%C3%A8ge" title="Liège">Liège</a>, to a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> noble family that gave him the name <b>Allowin</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His father was Pippin of Landen, the <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_the_Palace" class="mw-redirect" title="Mayor of the Palace">Mayor of the Palace</a> of <a href="/wiki/Austrasia" title="Austrasia">Austrasia</a>, and his mother <a href="/wiki/Itta_of_Metz" title="Itta of Metz">Itta of Metz</a>.
</p><p>A wild, young aristocrat of Brabant, he contracted a beneficial marriage, and had a daughter. He was a soldier who led an undisciplined and disorderly life. Shortly after the death of his wife, Bavo decided to reform after hearing a sermon preached by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Amand" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Amand">Saint Amand</a> on the emptiness of material things.<sup id="cite_ref-tait_edinburgh_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tait_edinburgh-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On returning to his house he distributed his wealth to the poor, and then received the tonsure from Amand.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>For some time thereafter, Bavo joined Amand in the latter's missionary travels throughout <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> and <a href="/wiki/Flanders" title="Flanders">Flanders</a>. On one occasion, Bavo met a man whom he had sold into slavery years before. Wishing to atone for his earlier deed, Bavo had the man lead him by a chain to the town jail. Bavo built an <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> on his grounds and became a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>. He distributed his belongings to the poor and lived as a recluse, first in a hollow tree and later in a cell in the forest by the Abbey. 
</p><p>He died at the Abbey in <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, in what is today <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bavo_of_Ghent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bavo is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a>, <a href="/wiki/Zellik" title="Zellik">Zellik</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Lauwe_(Belgium)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lauwe (Belgium)">Lauwe</a> in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Haarlem" title="Haarlem">Haarlem</a> in <a href="/wiki/Netherlands" title="Netherlands">the Netherlands</a>. His feast in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> is <a href="/wiki/October_1_(Orthodox_Liturgics)" class="mw-redirect" title="October 1 (Orthodox Liturgics)">October 1</a>.
</p><p>He is most often shown in Christian art as a <a href="/wiki/Knight" title="Knight">knight</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Sword" title="Sword">sword</a> and <a href="/wiki/Falcon" title="Falcon">falcon</a>. The most popular scene is the moment of his conversion, which has many stories attached to it. Because he is so often shown with a falcon, he came to be considered the patron saint of <a href="/wiki/Falconry" title="Falconry">falconry</a>. In medieval Ghent, taxes were paid on Bavo's feast day, and it is for this reason he is often shown holding a <a href="/wiki/Coin_purse" title="Coin purse">purse</a> or <a href="/wiki/Money_bag" title="Money bag">money bag</a>.
</p><p>According to <a href="/wiki/Rodulfus_Glaber" title="Rodulfus Glaber">Rodulfus Glaber</a>, the city of <a href="/wiki/Bamberg" title="Bamberg">Bamberg</a> is named after him, with Bamberg meaning "Mount of Bavo".
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bavo_of_Ghent&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Several churches are dedicated to him, including:
</p>
<ul><li><a href="/wiki/Saint_Bavo_Cathedral" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Bavo Cathedral">Saint Bavo Cathedral</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Ghent" title="Ghent">Ghent</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/wiki/Sint-Bavokerk" class="mw-redirect" title="Sint-Bavokerk">Sint-Bavokerk</a> and <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Bavo" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Saint Bavo">Cathedral of Saint Bavo</a>, both in <a href="/wiki/Haarlem" title="Haarlem">Haarlem</a></li>
<li>Sint-Bavokerk in <a href="/wiki/Heemstede" title="Heemstede">Heemstede</a>, <a href="/wiki/Lauwe_(Belgium)" class="mw-redirect" title="Lauwe (Belgium)">Lauwe</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Zellik" title="Zellik">Zellik</a></li>
<li>Saint Bavo Church and School, in <a href="/wiki/Mishawaka,_Indiana" title="Mishawaka, Indiana">Mishawaka, Indiana</a><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup></li>
<li><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sint-Bavokerk_(Wilrijk)&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Sint-Bavokerk (Wilrijk) (page does not exist)">Sint-Bavokerk (Wilrijk)</a><span class="noprint" style="font-size:85%; font-style: normal;">&#160;&#91;<a href="https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint-Bavokerk_(Wilrijk)" class="extiw" title="nl:Sint-Bavokerk (Wilrijk)">nl</a>&#93;</span> in <a href="/wiki/Wilrijk" title="Wilrijk">Wilrijk</a></li></ul>
<p>His picture is also part of the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Antwerp" title="Antwerp">Antwerp</a> suburb <a href="/wiki/Wilrijk" title="Wilrijk">Wilrijk</a>.
<a href="/wiki/Rembrandt" title="Rembrandt">Rembrandt</a> painted a <i>Saint Bavo</i>, dated between 1662 and 1665.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
