<p><b>Seraphim of Sarov</b> (<a href="/wiki/Russian_language" title="Russian language">Russian</a>: <span lang="ru">Серафим Саровский</span>; 30 July&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 19 July&#93;&#160;1754 (or 1759) – 14 January&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 2 January&#93;&#160;1833), born <b>Prókhor Isídorovich Moshnín (Mashnín)</b> [Про́хор Иси́дорович Мошни́н (Машни́н)], is one of the most renowned Russian saints and is venerated in the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a>, and the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a>. He is generally considered the greatest of the 18th-century <a href="/wiki/Starets" title="Starets"><i>startsy</i></a> (elders). Seraphim extended the <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monastic</a> teachings of <a href="/wiki/Contemplation" title="Contemplation">contemplation</a>, <a href="/wiki/Theoria" class="mw-redirect" title="Theoria">theoria</a> and <a href="/wiki/Self-denial" title="Self-denial">self-denial</a> to the layperson. He taught that the purpose of the Christian life was to <a href="/wiki/Descent_of_the_Holy_Spirit" class="mw-redirect" title="Descent of the Holy Spirit">receive the Holy Spirit</a>. Perhaps his most popular quotation amongst his devotees is "acquire a peaceful spirit, and thousands around you will be saved."
</p><p>Seraphim was <a href="/wiki/Glorification" title="Glorification">glorified</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church" title="Russian Orthodox Church">Russian Orthodox Church</a> in 1903.
</p>

<p>Born 19 July (O.S.) 1754, Seraphim was <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptized</a> with the name of Prochor, after <a href="/wiki/Prochorus_(deacon)" title="Prochorus (deacon)">Prochorus</a>, one of the first <a href="/wiki/Seven_Deacons" title="Seven Deacons">Seven Deacons</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Early_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Early Church">Early Church</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">disciple</a> of <a href="/wiki/John_the_Evangelist" title="John the Evangelist">John the Evangelist</a>. His parents, Isidore and Agathia Moshnin, lived in <a href="/wiki/Kursk,_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Kursk, Russia">Kursk, Russia</a>. His father was a merchant. According to <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> tradition, a <a href="/wiki/Wonderworking" class="mw-redirect" title="Wonderworking">wonderworking</a> <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a> (<a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a>), <a href="/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Kursk" class="mw-redirect" title="Our Lady of Kursk">Our Lady of Kursk</a>, healed the young boy. Seraphim later experienced a number of <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">visions</a>.
</p><p>In 1775, at the age of 17, he visited Dorothea of Kiev.
</p><p>In 1777, at the age of 19, he joined the <a href="/wiki/Sarov" title="Sarov">Sarov</a> monastery as a <a href="/wiki/Novice" title="Novice">novice</a> (<i>poslushnik</i>). He was officially <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> (took his <a href="/wiki/Monk#Eastern_Christianity" title="Monk">monastic vows</a>) in 1786 and given the religious name of Seraphim (which means "fiery" or "burning" in <a href="/wiki/Hebrew_language" title="Hebrew language">Hebrew</a>). Shortly afterwards, he was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> a <a href="/wiki/Hierodeacon" title="Hierodeacon">hierodeacon</a> (monastic <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>). In 1793, he was ordained as a <a href="/wiki/Hieromonk" title="Hieromonk">hieromonk</a> (monastic <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>) and became the spiritual leader of the Diveyevo Convent, which has since come to be known as the <a href="/wiki/Serafimo-Diveevsky_Monastery" class="mw-redirect" title="Serafimo-Diveevsky Monastery">Seraphim-Diveyevo Convent</a>.
</p><p>Soon after this, Seraphim retreated to a log cabin in the woods outside <a href="/wiki/Sarov" title="Sarov">Sarov</a> monastery and led a solitary lifestyle as a <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> for 25 years. During this time his feet became swollen to the point that he had trouble walking. Sarov's eating and <a href="/wiki/Fasting" title="Fasting">fasting</a> habits became more strict. At first he ate bread obtained from the monastery and vegetables from his garden, then only vegetables. For three years, he ate only grass.<sup id="cite_ref-Johnson_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Johnson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Motovilov.gif" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Motovilov.gif/220px-Motovilov.gif" decoding="async" width="220" height="320" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Motovilov.gif/330px-Motovilov.gif 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b1/Motovilov.gif 2x" data-file-width="350" data-file-height="509" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Motovilov.gif" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><a href="/wiki/Nicholas_Motovilov" class="mw-redirect" title="Nicholas Motovilov">Nicholas Motovilov</a>, one of Seraphim's most well-known <a href="/wiki/Disciple_(Christianity)" title="Disciple (Christianity)">disciples</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>One day, while chopping wood, Seraphim was attacked by a gang of thieves who beat him mercilessly with the handle of his own axe. He never resisted, and was left for dead. The robbers never found the money they sought, only an <a href="/wiki/Icon" title="Icon">icon</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a> (Virgin Mary) in his hut. Seraphim had a hunched back for the rest of his life. However, at the thieves' trial he pleaded to the judge for mercy on their behalf.  He spent five months in the monastery, recovering from his injuries and then returned to the wilderness.<sup id="cite_ref-Johnson_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Johnson-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After this incident Seraphim spent 1,000 successive nights on a rock in continuous prayer with his arms raised to the sky, a feat of <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> deemed miraculous by the Eastern Orthodox Church, especially considering the pain from his injuries.
</p><p>In 1815, in obedience to a reputed <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">spiritual experience</a> that he attributed to the Virgin Mary, Seraphim began admitting <a href="/wiki/Pilgrims" class="mw-redirect" title="Pilgrims">pilgrims</a> to his hermitage as a <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a>. He soon became immensely popular due to his reputation for <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturge" class="mw-redirect" title="Thaumaturge">healing powers</a> and gift of <a href="/wiki/Prophecy" title="Prophecy">prophecy</a>. Hundreds of pilgrims per day visited him, drawn as well by his ability to answer his guests' questions before they could ask.
</p><p>As extraordinarily harsh as Seraphim often was to himself, he was kind and gentle toward others – always greeting his guests with a <a href="/wiki/Prostration" title="Prostration">prostration</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Kiss_of_peace" title="Kiss of peace">kiss</a>, and exclaiming <a href="/wiki/Paschal_greeting" title="Paschal greeting">"Christ is risen!"</a>, and calling everyone "My joy". He died while kneeling before a tenderness icon of the Theotokos which he called "Joy of all Joys". This icon is kept currently in the chapel of the residence of the Patriarch of Moscow.
</p>

<p>There was a widespread popular belief in Russia that a saint's remains were supposed to be <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorrupt</a>, which was not the case with Seraphim as was officially ascertained by a commission that researched his grave in January 1903. This, however, did not deter canonisation, spearheaded by archimandrite <a href="/wiki/Seraphim_Chichagov" title="Seraphim Chichagov">Seraphim Chichagov</a> as well as popular veneration.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> At the end of January (O.S.) 1903, the <a href="/wiki/Most_Holy_Synod" title="Most Holy Synod">Most Holy Synod</a>, having received approval from <a href="/wiki/Emperor_of_All_Russia" class="mw-redirect" title="Emperor of All Russia">Emperor</a> <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia" title="Nicholas II of Russia">Nicholas II</a>, announced Seraphim's forthcoming glorification.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> In early July 1903, his relics were transferred from their original burial place to the church of Saints Zosimus and <a href="/wiki/Sabbatius" class="mw-redirect" title="Sabbatius">Sabbatius</a>. Nicholas II and <a href="/wiki/Alexandra_Feodorovna_(Alix_of_Hesse)" title="Alexandra Feodorovna (Alix of Hesse)">Tsarina Alexandra</a> provided a new cypress coffin to receive the relics. The solemn canonisation (discovery of the relics) festivities took place in Sarov on 19 July (1 August) 1903 and were attended by the Tsar, his wife, his mother Empress <a href="/wiki/Maria_Feodorovna_(Dagmar_of_Denmark)" title="Maria Feodorovna (Dagmar of Denmark)">Maria Feodorovna</a>, and other senior members of the Imperial Family.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 18 July 1903, Metropolitan Anthony Vadkovsky of St. Petersburg officiated at the Last <a href="/wiki/Pannikhida" class="mw-redirect" title="Pannikhida">Pannikhida</a> (Memorial Service) in the Dormition Cathedral at Sarov, with the royal family in attendance. These would be the last prayers offered for Seraphim as a departed servant of God; from that time forward, prayers would instead be addressed to him as a saint. On 19 July, Seraphim's birthday, the late liturgy began at 8:00.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="margin-left:0.1em; white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Please_clarify" title="Wikipedia:Please clarify"><span title="The text near this tag may need clarification or removal of jargon. (July 2021)">clarification needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> At the <a href="/wiki/Little_Entrance" class="mw-redirect" title="Little Entrance">Little Entrance</a>, twelve <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">archimandrites</a> lifted the coffin from the middle of the church and carried it around the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Holy_table&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Holy table (page does not exist)">holy table</a> (altar), then placed it into a special shrine which had been constructed for it.
</p><p>The festivities at Sarov ended with the <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecration</a> of the first two churches dedicated to Saint Seraphim. The first had been constructed over his <a href="/wiki/Monastic_cell" title="Monastic cell">monastic cell</a> in the wilderness of Sarov. The second church was consecrated on 22 July at the <a href="/wiki/Diveyevo" title="Diveyevo">Diveyevo</a> <a href="/wiki/Convent" title="Convent">convent</a>.
</p><p>Following the <a href="/wiki/Bolshevik_Revolution" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolshevik Revolution">Bolshevik Revolution</a>, <a href="/wiki/Soviet_Union" title="Soviet Union">Soviet</a> authorities severely persecuted religious groups.  As part of their <a href="/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians" title="Persecution of Christians">persecution of Christians</a>, they confiscated many relics of saints, including Seraphim. Furthermore, his biographer <a href="/wiki/Seraphim_Chichagov" title="Seraphim Chichagov">Seraphim Chichagov</a>, who came to become a <a href="/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop" title="Metropolitan bishop">metropolitan</a>, was arrested, sentenced to death and executed by firing squad in 1937 (and is also celebrated as a Russian Orthodox saint).
</p><p>In 1991, Seraphim's relics were rediscovered after being hidden in a <a href="/wiki/Religion_in_the_Soviet_Union" title="Religion in the Soviet Union">Soviet anti-religious</a> museum for seventy years. This caused a sensation in post-Soviet Russia and throughout the Orthodox world. A <a href="/wiki/Crucession" class="mw-redirect" title="Crucession">crucession</a> (religious <a href="/wiki/Procession" title="Procession">procession</a>) escorted the relics, on foot, all the way from Moscow to Diveyevo Convent, where they remain to this day.
</p><p>On 19 October 2016, some relics of Seraphim were launched into space aboard the <a href="/wiki/Soyuz_MS-02" title="Soyuz MS-02">Soyuz MS-02</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-tass_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tass-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Seraphim is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Commemoration_(observance)" class="mw-redirect" title="Commemoration (observance)">commemoration</a> on <a href="/wiki/January_2" title="January 2">2 January</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> also referred to him as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Seraphim_and_Old_Believers">Seraphim and Old Believers</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Seraphim_of_Sarov&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Seraphim and Old Believers">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1096954695/mw-parser-output/.tmulti"/><div class="thumb tmulti tleft"><div class="thumbinner multiimageinner" style="width:248px;max-width:248px"><div class="trow"><div class="tsingle" style="width:122px;max-width:122px"><div class="thumbimage"><a href="/wiki/File:Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg/120px-Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg" decoding="async" width="120" height="213" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg/180px-Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg/240px-Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_chapel.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2988" data-file-height="5312" /></a></div><div class="thumbcaption">Chapel at the pilgrim spot</div></div><div class="tsingle" style="width:122px;max-width:122px"><div class="thumbimage"><a href="/wiki/File:Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg/120px-Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg" decoding="async" width="120" height="213" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg/180px-Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg/240px-Seraphim_of_Sarov_-_kneeling_stone.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2988" data-file-height="5312" /></a></div><div class="thumbcaption">Stone where Seraphim prayed for 1000 nights</div></div></div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Serafim_sarofckii.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Serafim_sarofckii.jpg/220px-Serafim_sarofckii.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Serafim_sarofckii.jpg/330px-Serafim_sarofckii.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/Serafim_sarofckii.jpg/440px-Serafim_sarofckii.jpg 2x" data-file-width="800" data-file-height="1067" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Serafim_sarofckii.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue portraying Saint Seraphim praying on the rock (Korennaya monastery, <a href="/wiki/Kursk_Oblast" title="Kursk Oblast">Kursk Oblast</a>).</div></div></div>
<p>The available information about relations between Seraphim of Sarov and Russian <a href="/wiki/Old_Believers" title="Old Believers">Old Believers</a> tradition is somewhat contradictory. On the one hand, in all the memoirs and biographies, and in the collections of his sayings, he is undoubtedly portrayed as a convinced supporter of the reforms in the church and the official hierarchy.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> On the other hand, on <a href="/wiki/Icons" class="mw-redirect" title="Icons">icons</a> of Seraphim he is usually depicted with a <a href="/wiki/Lestovka" title="Lestovka">lestovka</a> in his left hand,<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> and in some cases even in old Russian, Old-Believers-style monastic garments (with a peculiar <a href="/wiki/Klobuk" title="Klobuk">klobuk</a>, and an old-fashioned cast bronze cross), as it is with these objects that he is depicted on the only lifetime portrait of him.<sup id="cite_ref-staroverforum_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staroverforum-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The lestovka used by St.Seraphim is preserved up to this time among his personal belongings.<sup id="cite_ref-ooke_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ooke-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to some sources, the known problems with the beatification of Seraphim of Sarov did happen exactly due to his general support and sympathy towards the Old Believers tradition,<sup id="cite_ref-kutuzovedinover_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kutuzovedinover-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> in which case the negative assessment of the old rite, ascribed to him, would have been interpreted as inventions of his followers, who tried to put their teacher in the most favorable light in the eyes of the official church functionaries.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> It was also suggested that Seraphim could have descended from a family of <a href="/wiki/Edinoverie" title="Edinoverie">Old Ritualists</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-kutuzovedinover_16-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-kutuzovedinover-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> or from a family of secret, cryptic Old Believers (that were widespread in northern and eastern areas of Russia),<sup id="cite_ref-staroverforum_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staroverforum-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> possibly with consequent gradual shift towards <a href="/wiki/Edinoverie" title="Edinoverie">edinoverie</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-ooke_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ooke-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In spite of some (alleged) controversy, Seraphim was known, at least at the level of official <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, for his rejection of the Russian old rites.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The majority of old believers authors doubt virtually all the facts known about Seraphim, as well as the very legitimacy of his <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatification</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> and his name is invariably used in interdenominational polemics.<sup id="cite_ref-staroverforum_14-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staroverforum-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<li id="cite_note-3"><span class="mw-cite-backlink"><b><a href="#cite_ref-3">^</a></b></span> <span class="reference-text">A manual for Russian clergy published in <a href="/wiki/Kiev" class="mw-redirect" title="Kiev">Kiev</a> in 1913 had a special detailed explanation for this case that stressed that <a href="/wiki/Incorruptibility" title="Incorruptibility">incorruptibility</a> of a saint′s remains was not a <a href="/wiki/Sine_qua_non" title="Sine qua non">sine qua non</a>: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>″Некоторые утверждают, будто мощи святых всегда и непременно суть совершенно нетленные, то есть совершенно целые, нисколько неразрушенные и неповрежденные тела. Но понимание слова „мощи“ непременно в смысле целого тела, а не частей его и преимущественно костей, – неправильно, и вводит в разногласие с греческой церковию, так как у греков вовсе не проповедуется учения, что мощи означают целое тело, и мощи наибольшей части святых в Греции и на Востоке (равно как и на Западе) суть кости. Самое нетление мощей всецерковным сознанием не считается общим непременным признаком для прославления святых угодников. История прославления святых показывает, что некоторые из чтимых нами святых были прославлены до открытия их мощей, некоторые прославлены спустя то или другое время и даже очень значительное время после открытия их мощей; мощи довольно многих прославленных святых никогда не были открываемы. Мощи многих святых и в настоящее время находятся под спудом: в большей части случаев это значит, что мощи совсем не были открываемы, в меньшей части значит, что были открываемы, но потом опять закрыты. Мощи святых, когда они нетленны, составляют чудо, но лишь дополнительное к тем чудесам, которые творятся чрез их посредство. Доказательство святости святых составляют те чудеса, которые творятся при их гробах, или от их мощей, а мощи, целые тела или только кости, суть дарованные нам, для поддержания в нас живейшего памятования о небесных молитвенниках за нас, священные и святые останки некоторых святых, которых мы должны чтить, как таковые, и суть те земные посредства, чрез которые Господь наиболее прославляет Свою чудодейственную силу.″</p></blockquote> <link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"/><blockquote class="templatequote"><p><i>tr. "Some argue that the relics of the saints are always and certainly completely incorruptible, that is, completely whole, not at all undisturbed and undamaged bodies. But the understanding of the word "power" is certainly in the sense of the whole body, and not of its parts and mainly of the bones, is wrong, and introduces it into disagreement with the Greek Church, since the Greeks do not preach the doctrine at all that relics mean the whole body, and the power of the largest part the saints in Greece and in the East (as well as in the West) are bones. The very incorruption of relics by the all-church consciousness is not considered a common indispensable sign for the glorification of saints. The history of the glorification of the saints shows that some of the saints we venerate were glorified before the discovery of their relics, some were glorified after one time or another and even a very significant time after the discovery of their relics; the relics of quite a few illustrious saints have never been revealed. The relics of many saints are still under wraps: in most cases this means that the relics were not opened at all, in a smaller part it means that they were opened, but then closed again. The relics of the saints, when they are incorruptible, constitute a miracle, but only in addition to those miracles that are performed through them. Proof of the holiness of the saints are the miracles that are performed at their tombs, or from their relics, and relics, whole bodies or only bones, are those given to us, to maintain in us the most vivid remembrance of heavenly prayer books for us, the sacred and holy remains of some saints, which we must honor, as such, and are those earthly means through which the Lord most glorifies His miraculous power."</i></p></blockquote> (<a href="https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D1%83%D0%BB%D0%B3%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%B2,_%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%B0%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87" class="extiw" title="ru:Булгаков, Сергей Васильевич">С.
