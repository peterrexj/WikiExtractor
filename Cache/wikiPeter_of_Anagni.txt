<p><b>Peter of Anagni</b> (died 3 August 1105) was a <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monk, bishop and <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Born in <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a>, he entered the Benedictines and so distinguished himself as a monk that <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a> appointed him <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Anagni" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Anagni">Bishop of Anagni</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> As bishop, he improved the spiritual welfare of the city and started rebuilding the <a href="/wiki/Anagni_Cathedral" title="Anagni Cathedral">city´s cathedral</a>. He was then sent as <a href="/wiki/Papal_legate" title="Papal legate">papal legate</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Byzantine Empire</a> where he was able to convince Emperor <a href="/wiki/Michael_VII_Doukas" title="Michael VII Doukas">Michael VII Doukas</a> to provide funds and craftsmen to building of the cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-Gasper_Giles_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gasper_Giles-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-Cath.org_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cath.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The new cathedral also included a hospital where, contrary to modern hospitals, accommodation and care was provided for free not only to the sick but also to travellers.<sup id="cite_ref-Charles_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Charles-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Peter joined in 1096 the forces of <a href="/wiki/Bohemond_of_Taranto" class="mw-redirect" title="Bohemond of Taranto">Bohemond of Taranto</a><sup id="cite_ref-Cath.org_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Cath.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> during the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a> on their way to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a> and later returned by way of Constantinople, <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> and Salerno.<sup id="cite_ref-MQS_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MQS-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Peter died on 3 August 1105.<sup id="cite_ref-MQS_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MQS-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was canonized in 1109 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paschal_II" title="Pope Paschal II">Pope Paschal II</a>, a mere four years after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-fenicchia2_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-fenicchia2-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> His feast is on 3 August<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
