<p><b>Bernardo da Corleone</b> (born <b>Filippo Latini</b>, 6 February 1605 – 12 January 1667) was a  Sicilian <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Capuchin</a> <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was a cobbler like his father until the latter died and he became a violent-tempered soldier who was quick to challenge to a duel those who offended him or the causes he believed in. But one duel went too far and he almost killed his opponent; he fled to the Franciscans in <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> where he experienced a radical conversion and repentance for his previous life.
<a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> canonized him 10 June 2001.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernard_of_Corleone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Filippo Latini was born in 1605 in <a href="/wiki/Corleone" title="Corleone">Corleone</a> as the third of six children to Leonardo and Francesca Latini; he was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> just hours after his birth. His brothers and sisters were pious;<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> one brother became a priest. Filippo had no formal schooling.
</p><p>His father was a shoemaker and Latini learned the trade from him. Leonardo was so compassionate that he opened his home to the poor to wash and to be fed. The death of his father in March 1620 prompted Filippo to continue on in his profession for a while to support his mother, but he soon left to become a soldier on 1 May 1618. He took up <a href="/wiki/Historical_European_martial_arts#Fencing" title="Historical European martial arts">fencing</a> and became quite skilled. But he possessed a boiling temper and was quick to challenge men to a duel. His single fault — according to two witnesses during the beatification trials — was that "he was quick to draw his sword at the slightest provocation".<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His life was not noted for its moral content and he had quite a formidable reputation for that reason.
</p><p>Latini was not that devout but he would defend old people and other helpless and defenseless persons against violence. He made frequent visits to a local crucifix and ensured that a lamp be kept burning before it; he was a devotee of <a href="/wiki/Francis_of_Assisi" title="Francis of Assisi">Francis of Assisi</a>. In the summer of 1624 he became involved in a duel which cost his opponent, the professional assassin Vito Canino, his arm.<sup id="cite_ref-cffa_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cffa-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> This incident was something that a large number of people witnessed and it caused an uproar which saw him nicknamed as "the finest blade in Sicily".<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> To escape from the man's avengers he sought refuge with the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Friars_Minor_Capuchin" title="Order of Friars Minor Capuchin">Order of Friars Minor Capuchin</a> for this duel had shaken him to the core. He later begged forgiveness to his opponent who befriended him after their differences were resolved. 
</p><p>During his time with the friars, he began to reflect on his life and to repent his life of anger and violence. He appealed for admission to the order as a religious and on 13 December 1632 entered their <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a> at <a href="/wiki/Caltanissetta" title="Caltanissetta">Caltanissetta</a> where he received the habit and the <a href="/wiki/Religious_name" title="Religious name">religious name</a> <i>Bernardo</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His devotion became severe: he scourged himself seven times a week. His sleep was limited to three hours a night on a narrow board with a block of wood under his head to act as a pillow. He fasted for the most part on bread and water.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He would wear the most worn <a href="/wiki/Religious_habit" title="Religious habit">habit</a> available and slept in the most uncomfortable <a href="/wiki/Monastic_cell" title="Monastic cell">cell</a> in the house. One result of this was that he suffered from rheumatism for much of his later life. He worked long hours and had a special concern for the sick; he ended up growing into a man known for his gentleness and compassion.
</p><p>He lived a simple life, moving from one friary to another in the province. Latini was stationed in Corleone (1635–36) before being transferred to Bivona (1636–37) and <a href="/wiki/Castronovo_di_Sicilia" title="Castronovo di Sicilia">Castronovo</a> (1637–38). In Castronovo he used to go through the streets with a large pot on his shoulders to give minestrone to the poor.<sup id="cite_ref-cffa_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cffa-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was then transferred to <a href="/wiki/Castelvetrano" title="Castelvetrano">Castelvetrano</a> (1638–39) and <a href="/wiki/Sambuca" title="Sambuca">Sambuca</a> (1639–40) which lasted just over a decade. He then moved to Bisaquino (1640–41) and to <a href="/wiki/Ciminna" title="Ciminna">Ciminna</a> (1641–42) before being moved to Chiusa (1642–43) and then to Castronovo (1643–44) once again. Latini was then sent to <a href="/wiki/Agrigento" title="Agrigento">Agrigento</a> (1644–46) and then returned to Castronovo (1646–47) before setting off for <a href="/wiki/Caltabellotta" title="Caltabellotta">Caltabellotta</a> (1647–48) and then to <a href="/wiki/Burgio" title="Burgio">Burgio</a> (1648–50) before going back to Chiusa (1650–51). He was then sent to <a href="/wiki/Partinico" title="Partinico">Partinico</a> (1651–52). He spent the last fifteen years of his life in Palermo. In the meantime he met his former opponent and now-friend Vito Canino in 1654 and then twice more in 1659 and 1663.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bernard possessed a strong devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Madonna</a> and encouraged others in this devotion. His biographers claim that the Blessed Mother appeared to him and placed the <a href="/wiki/Christ_Child" title="Christ Child">Infant Jesus</a> in his arms. It is also claimed that she gave him knowledge of the date of his death four months in advance. In the friaries he served as either a cook or assistant cook but towards the end of his life dealt with washing and managing the clothes of his compatriots.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>He died in <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> on 12 January 1667 at 2 pm after having been moved to the convent's hospital wing on 7 January; his funeral procession was extensive due to the fame he had acquired during his life. On his deathbed he kept repeating: "Let's go" in anticipation of his <i>dies natalis</i> (birth into <a href="/wiki/Heaven_(Christianity)" class="mw-redirect" title="Heaven (Christianity)">heaven</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-cffa_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cffa-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Numerous miracles reported to have occurred at his grave were recorded.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bernard_of_Corleone&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process opened in Palermo in an informative process that began in 1673 and concluded sometime later before an apostolic process was opened in 1681 and closed at the end of the decade on 16 December 1689. The formal introduction to the cause came under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIII" title="Pope Benedict XIII">Pope Benedict XIII</a> on 18 December 1725 and Latini became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> as a result of this. The confirmation of his <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> allowed for <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XIII" title="Pope Clement XIII">Pope Clement XIII</a> to title him as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 2 February 1762 while the same pope beatified him later on 15 May 1768 at <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>One final miracle was needed for him to be canonized as a saint and one such was investigated in its diocese of origin; the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> reviewed it and validated the process on 22 May 1998. The medical panel of experts approved this miracle on 12 May 1999 as did the theologians on 24 September 1999 and the C.C.S. on 21 March 2000. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> approved this miracle on 1 July 2000 and formalized the date for sainthood in a <a href="/wiki/Papal_consistory" title="Papal consistory">consistory</a> on 13 March 2001. John Paul II canonized Latini as a saint on 10 June 2001 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-korea_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-korea-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
