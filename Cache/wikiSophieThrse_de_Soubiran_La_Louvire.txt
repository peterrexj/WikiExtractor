<p><b>Sophie-Thérèse de Soubiran La Louvière</b> <small>French pronunciation:&#160;&#8203;</small><span title="Representation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)" class="IPA" lang="fr-Latn-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/French" title="Help:IPA/French">[maʁi teʁɛz d subiʁã la luvjɛʁ]</a></span> (16 May 1834 - 7 June 1889) was a French Roman Catholic nun who established the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Marie-Auxiliatrice" title="Sisters of Marie-Auxiliatrice">Sisters of Marie-Auxiliatrice</a>. She adopted the name of <b>Marie of the Sacred Heart</b> in 1877 after she had become a nun.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> beatified her on 20 October 1946 after the recognition of two miracles found to have been attributed to her intercession.<sup id="cite_ref-auto_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sophie-Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_de_Soubiran_La_Louvi%C3%A8re&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Sophie-Thérèse de Soubiran La Louvière was born in 1834 in <a href="/wiki/France" title="France">France</a> to Joseph Paul Comte de Soubiran and Noemi de Gélis. She received her <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on 29 June 1845.
</p><p>At the age of 20 she renounced her plans to become a <a href="/wiki/Carmelite" class="mw-redirect" title="Carmelite">Carmelite</a> nun in order to achieve the aims that her priest uncle Louis de Soubiran had set out for his parish. She attended a retreat under the <a href="/wiki/Jesuit" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesuit">Jesuit</a> Paul Ginhac and decided to establish her own religious institution in 1864 with a focus on girls. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Pope Pius IX</a> - on 19 December 1868 - issued a Decree of Praise for the new order.
</p><p>Trouble began in 1869, the year after the community was authorized, with the profession of a new sister, who claimed to be a widow. Louvière trained her to keep the books of the order. In 1870 she fled to <a href="/wiki/London" title="London">London</a> due to the <a href="/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War" title="Franco-Prussian War">Franco-Prussian War</a> and returned home after the <a href="/wiki/Treaty_of_Frankfurt_(1871)" title="Treaty of Frankfurt (1871)">Treaty of Frankfurt</a> in May 1871. When she returned, she found herself accused of financial mismanagement by the new sister, with evidence out of the books. As a result, Louvière was forced to leave the community. It took a few years for her to find a community that would accept her; to support herself in the meantime, she took in embroidery. Finally, she was accepted by the Paris monastery of Our Lady of Charity in 1874, where she took vows three years later under the name Marie of the Sacred Heart.
</p><p>Her health began to take a steep decline after 1881 and she taught at various places in France. Despite the pain she endured she continued to teach <a href="/wiki/Catechism" title="Catechism">catechism</a> to people. She died on 7 June 1889 with her last words being: "Come, Lord Jesus, come!"<sup id="cite_ref-auto_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-auto-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Two years later, a new Mary Help of Christians superior reviewed the allegations, as Louvière's book-keeping nemesis had fled the convent, and her husband had come looking for her. As a result of the subsequent examination, it was discovered that the fled book-keeper had embezzled monies of the order, and falsified the books so as to throw the blame on Louvière. The founder was vindicated, and her reputation was restored.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Sainthood">Sainthood</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Sophie-Th%C3%A9r%C3%A8se_de_Soubiran_La_Louvi%C3%A8re&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Sainthood">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The beatification process started in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> on 9 May 1934 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> which granted her the title of <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. The two processes that ensured were ratified in a decree on 22 March 1938. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> conferred upon her the title of <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 7 August 1940 after the recognition of her life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a>.
</p><p>Two miracles attributed to her were approved in 1945 and Pius XII beatified her on 20 October 1946.
</p>
