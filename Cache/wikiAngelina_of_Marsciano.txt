<p><b>Angelina of Marsciano</b>, <a href="/wiki/T.O.R." class="mw-redirect" title="T.O.R.">T.O.R.</a> (or <b>Angelina of Montegiove</b>; 1357 – 14 July 1435) was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sister" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sister">Religious Sister</a> and foundress, and is a <i><a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beata</a></i> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>. She founded a <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/congregation" class="extiw" title="wikt:congregation">congregation</a> of <a href="/wiki/Religious_Sisters" class="mw-redirect" title="Religious Sisters">Religious Sisters</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis#Third_Order_Regular" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Franciscan Third Order Regular</a>, known today as the Franciscan Sisters of Blessed Angelina. She is generally credited with the founding of the Third Order Regular for women, as her <a href="/wiki/Religious_congregation" title="Religious congregation">religious congregation</a> marked the establishment of the first <a href="/wiki/Franciscan" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Franciscan</a> community of women living under the <a href="/wiki/Monastic_rule" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastic rule">Rule</a> of the Third Order Regular authorized by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V" title="Pope Nicholas V">Pope Nicholas V</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cathen_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathen-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Unlike the <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a> of the Franciscan movement, the <a href="/wiki/Poor_Clares" title="Poor Clares">Poor Clare</a> <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>, they were not an <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Enclosed religious order">enclosed religious order</a>, but have been active in serving the poor around them for much of their history.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She is commemorated by the Franciscans on June 4; her liturgical feast is July 13.<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angeline_of_Marsciano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angeline_of_Marsciano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1357, Angelina was born in her ancestral <a href="/wiki/Castle_of_Montegiove" title="Castle of Montegiove">Castle of Montegiove</a>, some 40 kilometers from <a href="/wiki/Orvieto" title="Orvieto">Orvieto</a>, in <a href="/wiki/Umbria" title="Umbria">Umbria</a>, then part of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>. She was the daughter of Jacopo Angioballi, the Count of <a href="/wiki/Marsciano" title="Marsciano">Marsciano</a>, and of Anna, the daughter of the Count of <a href="/wiki/Corbara" title="Corbara">Corbara</a>, which is why sometimes she is also referred to as <b>Angelina of Corbara</b>.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Left orphaned and alone, except for one sister, by the age of six, she was raised by her grandparents.<sup id="cite_ref-Grieco_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grieco-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Angelina was married at age 15 to Giovanni da Terni, the Count of <a href="/wiki/Civitella_del_Tronto" title="Civitella del Tronto">Civitella del Tronto</a>, in the <a href="/wiki/Abruzzo" title="Abruzzo">Abruzzo</a> region, within the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>, but he died only two years later, leaving her a childless widow. His death left Angelina in charge of his castle and estate.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It was then that Angelina made the decision to dedicate her life to God (it would appear that she had considered being a nun before she was married). She was clothed as a <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Francis" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Francis">Franciscan tertiary</a> and, with several companions, began an apostolic mission around the countryside of the kingdom, preaching the values of <a href="/wiki/Repentance" title="Repentance">repentance</a> and <a href="/wiki/Virginity" title="Virginity">virginity</a>, as well as service to those in need.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angelina's progress was arrested by the disturbance she caused in the communities where she called for young women to adopt religious life. She was doubly charged with sorcery, the imagined origin of her sway over women, and heresy, because of her allegedly <a href="/wiki/Manichaeism" title="Manichaeism">Manichean</a> opposition to marriage. Angelina defended herself before <a href="/wiki/Ladislaus_of_Naples" title="Ladislaus of Naples">Ladislas</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">King of Naples</a>, who dismissed the charges, but expelled her and her companions from the kingdom, in order to avoid further complaints.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angelina then went to <a href="/wiki/Assisi" title="Assisi">Assisi</a>, where she stopped to rest and to pray  at the <a href="/wiki/Basilica_of_Santa_Maria_degli_Angeli" class="mw-redirect" title="Basilica of Santa Maria degli Angeli">Basilica of Santa Maria degli Angeli</a>, the cradle of the Franciscan Order. There she experienced a <a href="/wiki/Vision_(religion)" class="mw-redirect" title="Vision (religion)">vision</a>, wherein God instructed her to found a <a href="/wiki/Cloister" title="Cloister">cloistered</a> monastery under the Rule of the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan#Third_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan">Third Order of Saint Francis</a> in <a href="/wiki/Foligno" title="Foligno">Foligno</a>. The local bishop approved the plans with little hesitation, as they meant an end to her troublesome active ministry.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Foundress">Foundress</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angeline_of_Marsciano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Foundress">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Angelina settled in Foligno about 1394. She soon joined the Monastery of St. Anna, a small community of women Franciscan tertiaries, which had been founded in 1388 by the Blessed <a href="/w/index.php?title=Paoluccio_Trinci&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Paoluccio Trinci (page does not exist)">Paoluccio Trinci</a>, (died 1390) a Franciscan <a href="/wiki/Friar" title="Friar">friar</a> who had been related to her sister through marriage. Known as the "Monastery of the Countesses"—due to the social standing of most of its members, he had established it out of his vision of having these noble women of the city serve as an <a href="/wiki/Evangelization" class="mw-redirect" title="Evangelization">evangelizing</a> force in their society. The women lived ascetic lives in the monastery, and, not being nuns, followed a very informal structure, free to come and go as they wished, that they might be able to serve the poor and sick of the region.<sup id="cite_ref-FW_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FW-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Angelina took a leadership role in the small group and began to organize their lives into a more regular form. By 1397 she was considered the leader of the twelve founding members. In 1403 she was able to obtain a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">papal bull</a> from <a href="/wiki/Pope_Boniface_IX" title="Pope Boniface IX">Pope Boniface IX</a> which formally recognized the status of the house as a monastery. The reputation of the community in Foligno was so successful that quickly communities of Franciscan tertiary women (called <i>bizocche</i> locally) throughout the region sought to affiliate with them. Communities under her authority were soon established in <a href="/wiki/Florence" title="Florence">Florence</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spoleto" title="Spoleto">Spoleto</a>, Assisi, and <a href="/wiki/Viterbo" title="Viterbo">Viterbo</a>, along with eleven others, before Angelina's death in 1435.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-FW_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FW-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The diverse communities were recognized as a congregation by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_V" title="Pope Martin V">Pope Martin V</a> in 1428. This decree also allowed them to elect a <a href="/wiki/Minister_General" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister General">Minister General</a>  (a title since reserved for the head of the friars) who would have the right of <a href="/wiki/Canonical_visitation" title="Canonical visitation">canonical visitation</a> of the other communities. The congregation held its first general elections in 1430, in which Angela was elected their first Minister General. In this office, she developed the Statutes for the congregation, to be followed by all its houses.<sup id="cite_ref-FW_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FW-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This degree of independence was not welcomed by the <a href="/wiki/Franciscans" title="Franciscans">Friars Minor</a>, who had been granted complete authority over the tertiaries that same year. The Minister General of the friars, <a href="/w/index.php?title=Guglielmo_da_Casala&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Guglielmo da Casala (page does not exist)">Guglielmo da Casala</a>, demanded that the Third Order Sisters of the congregation be confirmed under obedience to him. Angelina had to submit and, in a public ceremony held in the friars' church in Foligno on 5 November 1430, vowed obedience to the local <a href="/wiki/Provincial_Superior" class="mw-redirect" title="Provincial Superior">Minister Provincial</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FW_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FW-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>This act of obedience, however, was repudiated by the <a href="/wiki/Chapter_(religion)" title="Chapter (religion)">chapter</a> of the community at Santa Anna, saying that it was invalid due to having been forced under duress and without their approval. The <a href="/wiki/Holy_See" title="Holy See">Holy See</a> confirmed their autonomy the following year. To avoid the potential for future repetition of this conflict, the congregation put themselves under the obedience of their local bishops, with their <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual direction</a> to come from the friars of the <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_Regular_of_St._Francis_of_Penance" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order Regular of St. Francis of Penance">Third Order Regular of St. Francis of Penance</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FW_6-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FW-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy_and_veneration">Legacy and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Angeline_of_Marsciano&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Angeline died on July 14, 1435<sup id="cite_ref-foley_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-foley-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Blessed Angelina was interred in the Church of St. Francis in Foligno upon her death. Her remains were removed to a grander shrine in 1492.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her <a href="/wiki/Cult_(religious_practice)" title="Cult (religious practice)">cultus</a> was approved in 1825.<sup id="cite_ref-patron_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-patron-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Due to the requirement of keeping their communities small and simple, Angelina's congregation gained greatest popularity in the 15th and 16th centuries. In 1428, they had been put briefly by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Martin_V" title="Pope Martin V">Pope Martin V</a> under the jurisdiction of the Friars Minor, with a specific mandate for the education and instruction of young girls. Even so, their work was fairly apostolic until they were required to become an <a href="/wiki/Enclosed_religious_order" class="mw-redirect" title="Enclosed religious order">enclosed religious order</a> in 1617, having taken solemn vows with a strict separation from the affairs of the external world, limited to the education of girls within the cloister. With a 1903 lift of papal enclosure, a wider apostolate was again permitted, and the congregation became known as the Franciscan Sisters of Blessed Angelina. As of 1750, they consisted of 11 houses and 80 members.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_4-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As of the year A.D. 2000, they have houses in <a href="/wiki/Brazil" title="Brazil">Brazil</a>, <a href="/wiki/Madagascar" title="Madagascar">Madagascar</a> and <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, as well as in Italy.<sup id="cite_ref-Grieco_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Grieco-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
