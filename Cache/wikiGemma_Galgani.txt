<p><b>Gemma Umberta Maria Galgani</b> (12 March 1878 – 11 April 1903), also known as <b>Gemma of Lucca</b>, was an <a href="/wiki/Italian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian people">Italian</a> <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a>, venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> since 1940. She has been called the "daughter of the Passion" because of her profound imitation of the <a href="/wiki/Passion_of_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Passion of Christ">Passion of Christ</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> She is especially venerated in the Congregation of the Passion of Jesus (<a href="/wiki/Passionists" title="Passionists">Passionists</a>).
</p>

<p>Gemma Umberta Maria Galgani was born on 12 March 1878, in the hamlet of Camigliano in the provincial town of <a href="/wiki/Capannori" title="Capannori">Capannori</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Gemma was the fifth of eight children and the first daughter; her father, Enrico Galgani, was a prosperous <a href="/wiki/Pharmacist" title="Pharmacist">pharmacist</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-germanus-1_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-germanus-1-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon after Galgani's birth, the family relocated north from Camigliano to a large new home in the <a href="/wiki/Tuscany" title="Tuscany">Tuscan</a> city of <a href="/wiki/Lucca" title="Lucca">Lucca</a> in a move which was undertaken to facilitate an improvement in the children's education.<sup id="cite_ref-woodham_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-woodham-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Gemma's mother, Aurelia Galgani, contracted <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>. Because of this hardship, Gemma was placed in a private nursery school run by Elena and Ersilia Vallini when she was two-and-a-half years old.
</p><p>Several members of the Galgani family died during this period. Their firstborn child, Carlo, and Gemma's little sister Giulia died at an early age. On 17 September 1885, Aurelia Galgani died from tuberculosis, which she had suffered from for five years, and Gemma's beloved brother Gino died from the same disease while studying for the priesthood.<sup id="cite_ref-Bell_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (March 2023)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Education">Education</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gemma_Galgani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Education">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Galgani was sent to a Catholic half-boarding school in Lucca run by the <a href="/wiki/Oblates_of_the_Holy_Spirit" title="Oblates of the Holy Spirit">Oblates of the Holy Spirit</a>. She excelled in French, arithmetic, and music. At the age of nine, Galgani was allowed to receive her first communion.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Adolescence">Adolescence</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gemma_Galgani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Adolescence">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At age 16, Galgani developed spinal <a href="/wiki/Meningitis" title="Meningitis">meningitis</a>, but recovered. She attributed her extraordinary cure to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart of Jesus</a> through the intercession of <a href="/wiki/Gabriel_of_Our_Lady_of_Sorrows" title="Gabriel of Our Lady of Sorrows">Gabriel of Our Lady of Sorrows</a> and <a href="/wiki/Marguerite_Marie_Alacoque" class="mw-redirect" title="Marguerite Marie Alacoque">Marguerite Marie Alacoque</a>. Galgani had a particular devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-woodham_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-woodham-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Shortly after turning 19, Galgani was <a href="/wiki/Orphan" title="Orphan">orphaned</a>, and thereafter she was responsible for the upbringing of her younger siblings, which she did with her aunt Carolina. She declined two <a href="/wiki/Proposal_of_marriage" class="mw-redirect" title="Proposal of marriage">marriage proposals</a><sup id="cite_ref-woodham_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-woodham-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and became a <a href="/wiki/Housekeeper_(domestic_worker)" title="Housekeeper (domestic worker)">housekeeper</a> with the Giannini family.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Mysticism">Mysticism</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gemma_Galgani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Mysticism">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Further information: <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">Mysticism</a> and <a href="/wiki/Religious_ecstasy" title="Religious ecstasy">Religious ecstasy</a></div>
<p>According to a biography written by her spiritual director, Germano Ruoppolo, Galgani began to display signs of the <a href="/wiki/Stigmata" title="Stigmata">stigmata</a> on 8 June 1899, at the age of twenty-one. She stated that she had spoken with her <a href="/wiki/Guardian_angel" title="Guardian angel">guardian angel</a>, Jesus, the <a href="/wiki/Mary_(mother_of_Jesus)" class="mw-redirect" title="Mary (mother of Jesus)">Virgin Mary</a>, and other saints—especially Gabriel of Our Lady of Sorrows. According to her testimonies, she sometimes received special messages from them about current or future events. With her health in decline, Ruoppolo directed her to pray for the disappearance of her stigmata; she did so and the marks ceased.<sup id="cite_ref-Bell_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="noprint Inline-Template" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citing_sources" title="Wikipedia:Citing sources"><span title="This citation requires a reference to the specific page or range of pages in which the material appears. (March 2023)">page&#160;needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> She said that she resisted the devil's attacks often.
</p><p>Galgani was frequently found in a state of ecstasy. She has also been reputed to levitate: she claimed that on one occasion, when her arms were around the crucifix in her dining room and she was kissing the wound on the side of the Crucified, she found herself raised from the floor.<sup id="cite_ref-cruz_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Stigmata">Stigmata</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gemma_Galgani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Stigmata">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2><p>
Galgani is said to have experienced stigmata on 8 June 1899, on the eve of the <a href="/wiki/Feast_of_the_Sacred_Heart" title="Feast of the Sacred Heart">feast of the Sacred Heart</a>. She wrote: <style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>I felt an inward sorrow for my sins, but so intense that I have never felt the like again ... My will made me detest them all, and promise willingly to suffer everything as expiation for them. Then the thoughts crowded thickly within me, and they were thoughts of sorrow, love, fear, hope and comfort.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote><p> In the subsequent rapture, Gemma saw her guardian angel in the company of the Blessed Virgin Mary:
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>The Blessed Virgin Mary opened her mantle and covered me with it. At that very moment, Jesus appeared with his wounds all open; blood was not flowing from them, but flames of fire which in one moment came and touched my hands, feet and heart. I felt I was dying, and should have fallen down but for my Mother (Blessed Virgin Mary) who supported me and kept me under her mantle. Thus I remained for several hours. Then my Mother kissed my forehead, the vision disappeared and I found myself on my knees; but I still had a keen pain in my hands, feet and heart. I got up to get into bed and saw that blood was coming from the places where I had the pain. I covered them as well as I could and then, helped by my guardian angel, got into bed.<sup id="cite_ref-cruz_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cruz-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>The physician Pietro Pfanner, who had known Galgani since her childhood, examined her stigmata. In his opinion, those were semblance of <a href="/wiki/Hysteria" title="Hysteria">hysterical</a> behaviour, and he suspected Gemma may have suffered from a form of <a href="/wiki/Neurosis" title="Neurosis">neurosis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Bell_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 61–63">&#58;&#8202;61–63&#8202;</span></sup> Pfanner examined Galgani and noted spots of blood on the palms of her hands but when he ordered the blood to be wiped away with a wet towel there was no wound. He concluded the phenomenon to be self-inflicted. On another occasion Galgani's foster mother Cecilia Giannini observed a <a href="/wiki/Sewing_needle" title="Sewing needle">sewing needle</a> on the floor next to her.<sup id="cite_ref-Bell_6-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Bell-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup><sup class="reference nowrap"><span title="Page / location: 61–63">&#58;&#8202;61–63&#8202;</span></sup>
Psychologist and skeptic <a href="/wiki/Donovan_Rawcliffe" class="mw-redirect" title="Donovan Rawcliffe">Donovan Rawcliffe</a> claimed in a book published nearly half a century after her death that her stigmata were caused by "self-inflicted wounds of a major hysteric."<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Reception">Reception</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gemma_Galgani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Reception">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_(HS85-10-31647).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_%28HS85-10-31647%29.jpg/220px-La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_%28HS85-10-31647%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="292" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_%28HS85-10-31647%29.jpg/330px-La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_%28HS85-10-31647%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_%28HS85-10-31647%29.jpg/440px-La_seraphique_Gemma_Galgani_%28HS85-10-31647%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2936" data-file-height="3896" /></a><figcaption>Gemma Galgani, published in 1916</figcaption></figure>
<p>Galgani was well known in the vicinity of Lucca before her death, especially to those in <a href="/wiki/Poverty" title="Poverty">poverty</a>. Opinions of her were divided. Some people admired her extraordinary virtues and referred to her as "the virgin of Lucca" out of pious respect and admiration. Others mocked her (including her younger sister, Angelina, who apparently used to make fun of Galgani during such experiences).<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Death.2C_canonization_and_veneration"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Death,_canonization_and_veneration">Death, canonization and veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Gemma_Galgani&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Death, canonization and veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2><p>
In early 1903, Galgani was diagnosed with <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>, and went into a long and often painful decline accompanied by several mystical phenomena. One of the religious nursing sisters who attended to her stated, <link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r996844942"></p><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>We have cared for a good many sick people, but we have never seen anything like this.</p></blockquote><p> At the beginning of Holy Week 1903, her health quickly deteriorated, and by <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> she was suffering tremendously, dying in a small room across from the Giannini house on 11 April 1903, <a href="/wiki/Holy_Saturday" title="Holy Saturday">Holy Saturday</a>. 
</p><p>After a thorough examination of her life by the Church, Gemma Galgani was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> on 14 May 1933 and canonized on 2 May 1940.<sup id="cite_ref-sgg-46_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-sgg-46-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Galgani's relics are housed at the <a href="/wiki/Sanctuary_of_Santa_Gemma,_Lucca" title="Sanctuary of Santa Gemma, Lucca">Sanctuary of Santa Gemma</a> associated with the Passionist <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> in Lucca, Italy. Since 1985, her heart is housed in the Santuario de Santa Gema, in <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>, <a href="/wiki/Spain" title="Spain">Spain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Gemma Galgani's confessor Germano Ruoppolo wrote a book about her.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The bronze figure on her tomb in Lucca was created by sculptor <a href="/wiki/Francesco_Nagni" title="Francesco Nagni">Francesco Nagni</a>.
</p>
