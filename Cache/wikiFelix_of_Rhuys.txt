<p><b>Saint Felix of Rhuys</b> (died 1038) was a <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Breton</a> <a href="/wiki/Benedictine_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine Order">Benedictine</a> <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a> and <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, who re-founded <a href="/wiki/Saint-Gildas-de-Rhuys" title="Saint-Gildas-de-Rhuys">Saint-Gildas-de-Rhuys</a> Abbey.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Rhuys&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Felix was born of wealthy parents in <a href="/wiki/Quimper,_Finist%C3%A8re" class="mw-redirect" title="Quimper, Finistère">Quimper</a> around 970.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He had a great regard for Saint <a href="/wiki/Paul_Aurelian" title="Paul Aurelian">Paul Aurelian</a> who had built a monastery at Lampoul on <a href="/wiki/Ushant" title="Ushant">Ushant</a>, and whose relics, around 960, had been translated to <a href="/wiki/Fleury_Abbey" title="Fleury Abbey">Fleury Abbey</a>. Felix became a recluse on <a href="/wiki/Ushant" title="Ushant">Ushant</a>. He left his hermitage during the Norman invasions to take refuge at Fleury in <a href="/wiki/Saint-Beno%C3%AEt-sur-Loire" title="Saint-Benoît-sur-Loire">Saint-Benoît-sur-Loire</a>, where he was welcomed by <a href="/wiki/Abbo_of_Fleury" title="Abbo of Fleury">Abbo of Fleury</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Geoffrey_I,_Duke_of_Brittany" title="Geoffrey I, Duke of Brittany">Geoffrey I, Duke of Brittany</a> asked the Abbot of Fleury to re-establish <a href="/wiki/Saint-Gildas-de-Rhuys" title="Saint-Gildas-de-Rhuys">Rhuys Abbey</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> which had been founded by <a href="/wiki/Saint_Gildas" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Gildas">Saint Gildas</a> in the 6th century on the <a href="/wiki/Gulf_of_Morbihan" title="Gulf of Morbihan">Gulf of Morbihan</a>, and had been destroyed by the <a href="/wiki/Normans" title="Normans">Normans</a>. Father Abbot entrusted Felix with the task of rebuilding. The original abbey had been built in wood on the remains of a Roman oppidum; Felix built in stone. Begun in 1008, the reconstruction ended in 1032 with the consecration of the church by Judicaël, bishop of Vannes and brother of the Duke. Félix died on March 4, 1038.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 4 March.
</p>
