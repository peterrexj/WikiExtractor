<p><b>Wulfstan</b><sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;a&#93;</a></sup> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1008</span>&#160;– 20 January 1095) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Worcester" title="Bishop of Worcester">Bishop of Worcester</a> from 1062 to 1095. He was the last surviving pre-<a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest">Conquest</a> bishop. Wulfstan is a <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Western_Christianity" title="Western Christianity">Western</a> Christian churches.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Denomination">Denomination</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfstan_(died_1095)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Denomination">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His denomination as Wulfstan II is to indicate that he is the second Bishop Wulfstan of <a href="/wiki/Worcester,_England" title="Worcester, England">Worcester</a>. This, however, does not prevent confusion, since the first <a href="/wiki/Wulfstan_II,_Archbishop_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Wulfstan II, Archbishop of York">Bishop Wulfstan</a>&#160;&#8211;&#32;his maternal uncle&#160;&#8211;&#32;is also called Wulfstan II to denote that <i>he</i> was the second <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a> called Wulfstan.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfstan_(died_1095)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/History_of_Worcestershire#Medieval" title="History of Worcestershire">History of Worcestershire §&#160;Medieval</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Worcester#History" title="Anglican Diocese of Worcester">Anglican Diocese of Worcester §&#160;History</a></div>
<p>Wulfstan was born about 1008 at <a href="/wiki/Long_Itchington" title="Long Itchington">Long Itchington</a> in the <a href="/wiki/English_county" class="mw-redirect" title="English county">English county</a> of <a href="/wiki/Warwickshire" title="Warwickshire">Warwickshire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Walsh631_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Walsh631-3">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> His family lost their lands around the time King <a href="/wiki/Canute_the_Great" class="mw-redirect" title="Canute the Great">Cnut of England</a> came to the throne.<sup id="cite_ref-Fleming41_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fleming41-4">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He was probably named after his uncle, <a href="/wiki/Wulfstan_II,_Archbishop_of_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Wulfstan II, Archbishop of York">Wulfstan II, Archbishop of York</a>. Through his uncle's influence, he studied at monasteries in <a href="/wiki/Evesham,_Worcestershire" class="mw-redirect" title="Evesham, Worcestershire">Evesham</a> and <a href="/wiki/Peterborough" title="Peterborough">Peterborough</a>, before becoming a clerk at <a href="/wiki/Worcester,_England" title="Worcester, England">Worcester</a>. During this time, his superiors, noting his reputation for dedication and chastity, urged him to join the priesthood. Wulfstan was ordained shortly thereafter, in 1038, and soon joined a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> of <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictines</a> at Worcester.
</p><p>Wulfstan served as <a href="/wiki/Treasurer" title="Treasurer">treasurer</a> and <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of Worcester, and from 1034 onwards served as the parish priest of <a href="/wiki/Hawkesbury,_Gloucestershire" title="Hawkesbury, Gloucestershire">Hawkesbury</a>, <a href="/wiki/Gloucestershire" title="Gloucestershire">Gloucestershire</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOWorc_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOWorc-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> When <a href="/wiki/Ealdred_(archbishop_of_York)" title="Ealdred (archbishop of York)">Ealdred</a>, the bishop of Worcester as well as the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_York" title="Archbishop of York">Archbishop of York</a>, was required to relinquish Worcester by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_II" title="Pope Nicholas II">Pope Nicholas</a>, Ealdred decided to have Wulfstan appointed to Worcester.  In addition, Ealdred continued to hold a number of the manors of the diocese.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams150_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams150-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Wulfstan was consecrated Bishop of Worcester on 8 September 1062,<sup id="cite_ref-Handbook224_9-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Handbook224-9">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> by Ealdred.  It would have been more proper for him to have been consecrated by the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Canterbury" title="Archbishop of Canterbury">Archbishop of Canterbury</a>, whose province Worcester was in.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams150_8-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams150-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Wulfstan had deliberately avoided consecration by the current archbishop of Canterbury, <a href="/wiki/Stigand" title="Stigand">Stigand</a>, since Stigand's own consecration had been <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">uncanonical</a>. Wulfstan still acknowledged that the see of Worcester was a suffragan of Canterbury. He made no profession of obedience to Ealdred, instead offering a profession of obedience to Stigand's successor <a href="/wiki/Lanfranc" title="Lanfranc">Lanfranc</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wulfstan was a confidant of <a href="/wiki/Harold_Godwinson" title="Harold Godwinson">Harold Godwinson</a>, who helped secure the bishopric for him.<sup id="cite_ref-Fleming79_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Fleming79-11">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A social reformer, Wulfstan struggled to bridge the gap between the old and new regimes, and to alleviate the suffering of the poor. He was a strong opponent of the <a href="/wiki/Slave_trade" class="mw-redirect" title="Slave trade">slave trade</a>, and together with Lanfranc, was mainly responsible for ending the <a href="/wiki/Bristol_slave_trade" title="Bristol slave trade">trade from Bristol</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After the <a href="/wiki/Norman_conquest_of_England" class="mw-redirect" title="Norman conquest of England">Norman conquest of England</a>, Wulfstan was the only English-born bishop to retain his diocese for any significant time after the Conquest (all others had been replaced or succeeded by Normans by 1075).<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>  William noted that pastoral care of his diocese was Wulfstan's principal interest.
</p><p>In 1072 Wulfstan signed the <a href="/wiki/Accord_of_Winchester" title="Accord of Winchester">Accord of Winchester</a>. In 1075, Wulfstan and the Worcestershire <a href="/wiki/Fyrd" title="Fyrd">fyrd</a> militia countered the <a href="/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Earls" title="Revolt of the Earls">Revolt of the Earls</a>, when various magnates attempted a rebellion against <a href="/wiki/William_the_Conqueror" title="William the Conqueror">William the Conqueror</a>.
</p><p>Wulfstan founded the <a href="/wiki/Great_Malvern_Priory" title="Great Malvern Priory">Great Malvern Priory</a>, and undertook much large-scale rebuilding work, including <a href="/wiki/Worcester_Cathedral" title="Worcester Cathedral">Worcester Cathedral</a>, <a href="/wiki/Hereford_Cathedral" title="Hereford Cathedral">Hereford Cathedral</a>, <a href="/wiki/Tewkesbury_Abbey" title="Tewkesbury Abbey">Tewkesbury Abbey</a>, and many other churches in the Worcester, <a href="/wiki/Hereford" title="Hereford">Hereford</a> and <a href="/wiki/Gloucester" title="Gloucester">Gloucester</a> areas.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (June 2008)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> After the Norman Conquest, he claimed that the <a href="/wiki/Oswaldslow_(hundred)" class="mw-redirect" title="Oswaldslow (hundred)">Oswaldslow</a>, a "<a href="/w/index.php?title=Triple_hundred&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Triple hundred (page does not exist)">triple hundred</a>" administered by the bishops of Worcester, was free of interference by the local <a href="/wiki/Sheriff" title="Sheriff">sheriff</a>.  This right to exclude the sheriff was recorded in the <a href="/wiki/Domesday_Book" title="Domesday Book">Domesday Book</a> in 1086.  Wulfstan also administered the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Lichfield" title="Diocese of Lichfield">diocese of Lichfield</a> when it was vacant between 1071 and 1072.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams149_15-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams149-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>As bishop, he often assisted the archbishops of York with <a href="/wiki/Consecration" title="Consecration">consecrations</a>, as they had few suffragan bishops. In 1073 Wulfstan helped <a href="/wiki/Thomas_of_Bayeux" title="Thomas of Bayeux">Thomas of Bayeux</a> consecrate Radulf as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Orkney" title="Bishop of Orkney">Bishop of Orkney</a>, and in 1081 helped consecrate <a href="/wiki/William_de_St-Calais" title="William de St-Calais">William de St-Calais</a> as <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Durham" title="Bishop of Durham">Bishop of Durham</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams150_8-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams150-8">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_(3118695706).jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_%283118695706%29.jpg/220px-Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_%283118695706%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_%283118695706%29.jpg/330px-Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_%283118695706%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_%283118695706%29.jpg/440px-Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_%283118695706%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1771" data-file-height="1328" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Holy_Trinity_Long_Itchington_St_Wulfstan_(3118695706).jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Stained glass depicting Wulfstan at the church of the Holy Trinity, <a href="/wiki/Long_Itchington" title="Long Itchington">Long Itchington</a></div></div></div>
<p>Wulfstan was responsible for the compilation by <a href="/wiki/Hemming_(monk)" title="Hemming (monk)">Hemming</a> of the second <a href="/wiki/Cartulary" title="Cartulary">cartulary</a> of Worcester.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams145_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams145-16">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> He was close friends with <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Hereford" class="mw-redirect" title="Robert of Hereford">Robert Losinga</a>, the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Hereford" title="Bishop of Hereford">Bishop of Hereford</a>, who was well known as a mathematician and astronomer.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams149_15-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams149-15">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wulfstan died 20 January 1095 after a protracted illness, the last surviving pre-Conquest bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> After his death, an altar was dedicated to him in Great Malvern Priory, next to those of <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Cantilupe" class="mw-redirect" title="Thomas Cantilupe">Thomas Cantilupe</a> and <a href="/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor" title="Edward the Confessor">King Edward the Confessor</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Wulfstan_(died_1095)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>At <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> of 1158, <a href="/wiki/Henry_II_of_England" title="Henry II of England">Henry II</a> and his wife <a href="/wiki/Eleanor_of_Aquitaine" title="Eleanor of Aquitaine">Eleanor of Aquitaine</a> visited <a href="/wiki/Worcester_Cathedral" title="Worcester Cathedral">Worcester Cathedral</a> and placed their crowns on the shrine of Wulfstan, vowing not to wear them again. Their son <a href="/wiki/John,_King_of_England" title="John, King of England">King John</a> is buried at Worcester Cathedral.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon after Wulfstan's death, a <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>, or saint's life, was written about him in <a href="/wiki/Old_English" title="Old English">English</a> by his former chancellor Colman.  It was translated into Latin by the medieval chronicler and historian <a href="/wiki/William_of_Malmesbury" title="William of Malmesbury">William of Malmesbury</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Williams170_19-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Williams170-19">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>  Wulfstan was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> on 14 May 1203 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Pope Innocent III</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-BHOWorc_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BHOWorc-5">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> One of the <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracles</a> attributed to Wulfstan was the curing of King <a href="/wiki/Harold_Godwinson" title="Harold Godwinson">Harold</a>'s daughter. The recently founded Victorine priory in <a href="/wiki/Celbridge" title="Celbridge">Celbridge</a>, Ireland (paid for by <a href="/wiki/Adam_de_Hereford" title="Adam de Hereford">Adam de Hereford</a>) was named <a href="/wiki/St._Wolstan%27s_Priory" title="St. Wolstan&#39;s Priory">St. Wolstan's Priory</a> in his honour.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2021, St. Mary's Church, Hawkesbury, where Wulfstan served as incumbent from 1034, installed a <a href="/wiki/Ring_of_bells" title="Ring of bells">ring</a> of eight bells in their tower. The largest bell, weighing 600&#160;kg, is named in honour of Wulfstan.<sup id="cite_ref-:0_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-:0-6">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Wulfstan is <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">remembered</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Church_of_England" title="Church of England">Church of England</a> with a <a href="/wiki/Lesser_Festival_(Anglicanism)" title="Lesser Festival (Anglicanism)">lesser festival</a><sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> and on the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church calendar</a><sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> on 19 January.
</p>
