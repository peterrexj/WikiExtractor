<p><b>Margaret Clitherow</b> (1556 – 25 March 1586) was an English <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a> and <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> known as "the Pearl of <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>". She was <a href="/wiki/Pressed_to_death" class="mw-redirect" title="Pressed to death">pressed to death</a> for refusing to enter a plea to the charge of harbouring Catholic priests. She was canonised in 1970 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Clitherow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Margaret Clitherow was born in 1556,<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> one of five children of Thomas and Jane Middleton. Her father was a respected businessman, a wax-<a href="/wiki/Chandlery" title="Chandlery">chandler</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sheriff#Great_Britain_and_Ireland" title="Sheriff">Sheriff</a> of York in 1564,<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> who died when Margaret was fourteen. In 1571, she married John Clitherow, a wealthy butcher and a chamberlain of the city, and bore him three children; the family lived at today's <a href="/wiki/10%E2%80%9311_The_Shambles" title="10–11 The Shambles">10–11 The Shambles</a>.
</p><p>She converted to Roman Catholicism in 1574. Although her husband, John Clitherow, belonged to the Established Church, he was supportive as his brother William was a Roman Catholic priest.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He paid her fines for not attending church services. She was first imprisoned in 1577 for failing to attend church, and two more incarcerations at <a href="/wiki/York_Castle" title="York Castle">York Castle</a> followed.<sup id="cite_ref-York_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-York-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> Her third child, William, was born in prison.<sup id="cite_ref-McSheffery_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McSheffery-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Black_Swan,_Peasholme_Green,_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/The_Black_Swan%2C_Peasholme_Green%2C_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg/220px-The_Black_Swan%2C_Peasholme_Green%2C_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/The_Black_Swan%2C_Peasholme_Green%2C_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg/330px-The_Black_Swan%2C_Peasholme_Green%2C_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/The_Black_Swan%2C_Peasholme_Green%2C_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg/440px-The_Black_Swan%2C_Peasholme_Green%2C_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg 2x" data-file-width="640" data-file-height="480" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:The_Black_Swan,_Peasholme_Green,_York._-_geograph.org.uk_-_529306.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The Black Swan, Peasholme Green, York</div></div></div>
<p>Margaret risked her life by harbouring and maintaining priests, which was made a capital offence by the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits,_etc._Act_1584" title="Jesuits, etc. Act 1584">Jesuits, etc. Act 1584</a>. She provided two chambers, one adjoining her house and, with her house under surveillance, she rented a house some distance away, where she kept priests hidden and Mass was celebrated through the thick of the persecution.<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Her home became one of the most important hiding places for fugitive priests in the north of England. Local tradition holds that she also housed her clerical guests in <a href="/wiki/The_Black_Swan,_York" title="The Black Swan, York">The Black Swan</a> at <a href="/wiki/Peasholme_Green" title="Peasholme Green">Peasholme Green</a>, where the Queen's agents were lodged.<sup id="cite_ref-McSheffery_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-McSheffery-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She sent her older son, Henry, to the <a href="/wiki/English_College,_Douai" title="English College, Douai">English College</a>, relocated in Reims, to train for the priesthood. Her husband was summoned by the authorities to explain why his oldest son had gone abroad, and in March 1586 the Clitherow house was searched.<sup id="cite_ref-Stevens_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Stevens-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> A frightened boy revealed the location of the <a href="/wiki/Priest_hole" title="Priest hole">priest hole</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-York_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-York-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Margaret was arrested and called before the York <a href="/wiki/Assize_Court" class="mw-redirect" title="Assize Court">assizes</a> for the crime of harbouring Catholic <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priests</a>. She refused to plead, thereby preventing a trial that would entail her three children being made to testify, and being subjected to torture. She was sentenced to death. Although pregnant with her fourth child,<sup id="cite_ref-Camm_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Camm-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> she was executed on <a href="/wiki/Lady_Day" title="Lady Day">Lady Day</a>, 1586, (which also happened to be <a href="/wiki/Good_Friday" title="Good Friday">Good Friday</a> that year) in the Toll Booth at Ouse Bridge, by being <a href="/wiki/Peine_forte_et_dure" title="Peine forte et dure">crushed to death by her own door</a>, the standard inducement to force a plea.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The two sergeants who should have carried out the execution hired four desperate beggars to do it instead. She was stripped and had a handkerchief tied across her face then laid across a sharp rock the size of a man's fist, the door from her own house was put on top of her and loaded with an immense weight of rocks and stones so that the sharp rock would break her back. Her death occurred within fifteen minutes, but her body was left for six hours before the weight was removed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Clitherow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Clitherow was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> in 1929 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI" title="Pope Pius XI">Pope Pius XI</a> and <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonised</a> on 25 October 1970<sup id="cite_ref-Green_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Green-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> among the <a href="/wiki/Forty_Martyrs_of_England_and_Wales" title="Forty Martyrs of England and Wales">Forty Martyrs of England and Wales</a>. Their feast day in the current Roman Catholic calendar is 4 May in England and 25 October in Wales. She is also commemorated in England on 30 August, along with martyrs <a href="/wiki/Anne_Line" title="Anne Line">Anne Line</a> and <a href="/wiki/Margaret_Ward" title="Margaret Ward">Margaret Ward</a>. The three were officially added to the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church liturgical calendar</a> with a feast day on 30 August.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>A relic, said to be her hand, is housed in the <a href="/wiki/Bar_Convent" title="Bar Convent">Bar Convent</a> in York.<sup id="cite_ref-York_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-York-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>St. Margaret's Shrine is at <a href="/wiki/35_The_Shambles" title="35 The Shambles">35–36 The Shambles</a>. John Clitherow had his <a href="/wiki/Butcher%27s_shop" class="mw-redirect" title="Butcher&#39;s shop">butcher's shop</a> at 35.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> However, the street was re-numbered in the 18th century, so it is thought their house was actually opposite.<sup id="cite_ref-Green_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Green-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Margaret_Clitherow&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:177px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg/175px-Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg" decoding="async" width="175" height="117" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg/263px-Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e5/Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg/350px-Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5184" data-file-height="3456" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Sanctuaire_St_Margaret_Clitherow_York_2.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>The shrine to <a href="/wiki/Saint_Margaret_Clitherow" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Margaret Clitherow">Saint Margaret Clitherow</a> on <a href="/wiki/The_Shambles" title="The Shambles">The Shambles</a>, <a href="/wiki/York" title="York">York</a>, 2018</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:177px;"><a href="/wiki/File:MC-photo.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/MC-photo.jpg/175px-MC-photo.jpg" decoding="async" width="175" height="143" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/MC-photo.jpg/263px-MC-photo.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/MC-photo.jpg/350px-MC-photo.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1473" data-file-height="1200" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:MC-photo.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Commemorative plaque on the Ouse Bridge, York</div></div></div>
<p>Margaret Clitherow is the patroness of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Women%27s_League" title="Catholic Women&#39;s League">Catholic Women's League</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
Church - Saint Margaret Clitherow - Grahame Park, Colindale London. Several schools in England are named after her, including those in <a href="/wiki/Bracknell" title="Bracknell">Bracknell</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brixham" title="Brixham">Brixham</a>, <a href="/wiki/Manchester" title="Manchester">Manchester</a>, <a href="/wiki/Middlesbrough" title="Middlesbrough">Middlesbrough</a>, Thamesmead SE28, <a href="/wiki/London_Borough_of_Brent" title="London Borough of Brent">Brent</a>, London NW10 and <a href="/wiki/Tonbridge" title="Tonbridge">Tonbridge</a>. The Roman Catholic primary school in <a href="/wiki/Nottingham" title="Nottingham">Nottingham</a>'s <a href="/wiki/Bestwood" class="mw-redirect" title="Bestwood">Bestwood</a> estate is named after Clitherow.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> In the United States, St Margaret of York Church and School in <a href="/wiki/Loveland,_Ohio" title="Loveland, Ohio">Loveland</a>, a suburb of <a href="/wiki/Cincinnati,_Ohio" class="mw-redirect" title="Cincinnati, Ohio">Cincinnati, Ohio</a>, is also named after her.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> Another school named after her is St Margaret Clitherow RC Primary School, located next to Stevenage Borough Football Club.
</p><p>She is a co-patroness of the Latin Mass Society, which organises an annual pilgrimage to York in her honour. A group of parishes in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Liverpool" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Liverpool">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Liverpool</a>, <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart_Roman_Catholic_Church,_Hindsford" title="Sacred Heart Roman Catholic Church, Hindsford">Sacred Heart</a> in <a href="/wiki/Hindsford" title="Hindsford">Hindsford</a>, St Richard's in <a href="/wiki/Atherton,_Greater_Manchester" title="Atherton, Greater Manchester">Atherton</a>, Holy Family in <a href="/wiki/Boothstown" title="Boothstown">Boothstown</a>, St Ambrose Barlow in <a href="/wiki/Astley,_Greater_Manchester" title="Astley, Greater Manchester">Astley</a>, St Gabriel's, Higher Folds in <a href="/wiki/Leigh,_Greater_Manchester" title="Leigh, Greater Manchester">Leigh</a> are now united as a single community with St Margaret Clitherow as its patron.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> The former Parishes of Sacred Heart and Holy Family in Rochdale in the Diocese of Salford have also been united under the patronage of St Margaret.
</p><p>The English poet and Jesuit priest <a href="/wiki/Gerard_Manley_Hopkins" title="Gerard Manley Hopkins">Gerard Manley Hopkins</a> wrote a poem honouring "God's daughter Margaret Clitheroe."<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> The poem, entitled "Margaret Clitheroe" was among fragments and unfinished poems of Hopkins discovered after his death and is a tribute to the woman, to her faith and courage, and to the manner of her death.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2008, a commemorative plaque was installed at the Micklegate end of York's <a href="/wiki/Ouse_Bridge,_York" class="mw-redirect" title="Ouse Bridge, York">Ouse Bridge</a> to mark the site of her martyrdom; the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Middlesbrough" title="Bishop of Middlesbrough">Bishop of Middlesbrough</a> unveiled it in a ceremony on 29 August 2008.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 2014, the futuristic dystopian novel <i>I Am Margaret</i> by Corinna Turner was first published in English.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The author cited St. Margaret Clitherow as a major influence (along with the <a href="/wiki/Never_Let_Me_Go_(2010_film)" title="Never Let Me Go (2010 film)">2010 film adaptation</a> of <a href="/wiki/Never_Let_Me_Go_(novel)" title="Never Let Me Go (novel)">Never Let Me Go</a>, the 2005 novel by <a href="/wiki/Kazuo_Ishiguro" title="Kazuo Ishiguro">Kazuo Ishiguro,</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Young_adult_fiction" title="Young adult fiction">Young Adult fiction</a> series <a href="/wiki/The_Hunger_Games" title="The Hunger Games">The Hunger Games</a> by <a href="/wiki/Suzanne_Collins" title="Suzanne Collins">Suzanne Collins</a>). At one point the novel was even marked by a US publisher as “a retelling of Saint Margaert Clitherow’s story.” However, although the titular heroine Margaret lives in an age of <a href="/wiki/Religious_persecution" title="Religious persecution">religious persecution</a>, with some elements such as ‘<a href="/wiki/Pursuivant" title="Pursuivant">pursuivants</a>’ drawing recognizably on the <a href="/wiki/Elizabethan_era" title="Elizabethan era">Elizabethan</a> penal times, the author has stated that it is not a strict retelling and was not intended to be. The novel was a finalist in the CALA Awards 2016 and another book in the series was nominated for the <a href="/wiki/Carnegie_Medal_(literary_award)" title="Carnegie Medal (literary award)">CILIP Carnegie Medal Award 2016</a>. The novel has since been translated into <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a> and a <a href="/wiki/Stage_adaptation" class="mw-redirect" title="Stage adaptation">stage adaptation</a> by <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Novelist" title="Novelist">novelist</a> Fiorella de Maria was published in 2020.
</p>
