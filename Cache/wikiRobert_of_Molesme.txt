<p><b>Robert of Molesme</b> (1028 &#8211; 17 April 1111) was  an <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a>, one of the founders of the <a href="/wiki/Cistercian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian Order">Cistercian Order</a> and is honored as a <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Molesme&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Robert was born about 1029 near Troyes, a younger son of Thierry and Ermengarde, nobles of <a href="/wiki/Champagne_(province)" title="Champagne (province)">Champagne</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Staley_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Staley-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He entered the <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> abbey of Montier-la-Celle near <a href="/wiki/Troyes" title="Troyes">Troyes</a> at age fifteen and rose to the office of prior.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was made the abbot of Saint Michel-de-Tonnerre around the year 1070, but he soon discovered that the monks were quarrelsome and disobedient, so he returned to Montier-la-Celle.<sup id="cite_ref-Gildas_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gildas-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Meanwhile, two hermits from a group of monks that had settled at Collan went to Rome and asked <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a> to give them Robert as their superior. The pope granted their request, and as of 1074 Robert served as their leader. Soon after, Robert moved the small community to Molesme in the valley of Langres in <a href="/wiki/Burgundy_(region)" class="mw-redirect" title="Burgundy (region)">Burgundy</a>. Initially, the establishment consisted of only huts made of branches surrounding a chapel in the forest, dedicated to the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Trinity" class="mw-redirect" title="Holy Trinity">Holy Trinity</a>. <a href="/wiki/Molesme_Abbey" title="Molesme Abbey">Molesme Abbey</a> quickly became known for its piety and sanctity, and Robert's reputation as a saintly man grew.<sup id="cite_ref-Gildas_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Gildas-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> It is because of this reputation that in 1082 <a href="/wiki/Bruno_of_Cologne" title="Bruno of Cologne">Bruno of Cologne</a> came to Robert seeking advice. He lived with Robert's community for a time before going on to found the Grande Chartreuse, the first <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> monastery.
</p><p>In 1098 there were 35 dependent priories of Molesme, and other annexes and some priories of nuns. Donors from the surrounding area vied with one another in helping the monks; soon they had more than they needed, slackened their way of life and became tepid.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Benefactors sent their children to the abbey for education and other non-monastic activities began to dominate daily life. The vast land holdings they had acquired required a large number of employees. As the community grew increasingly wealthy, it began to attract men seeking entry for the wrong reasons. They caused a division among the brothers, challenging Robert's severity. Robert twice tried to leave Molesme but was ordered back by the <a href="/wiki/Pope" title="Pope">Pope</a>.
</p>
<h2><span id="C.C3.AEteaux"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Cîteaux">Cîteaux</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Robert_of_Molesme&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Cîteaux">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Foundersofciteaux.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Foundersofciteaux.jpg/250px-Foundersofciteaux.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="274" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5f/Foundersofciteaux.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="350" data-file-height="384" /></a><figcaption>Modern icon of the founders of Citeaux Abbey: Saints Robert, Alberic and Stephen Harding venerate the Blessed Virgin Mary</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1098,<sup id="cite_ref-VessGCSU_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-VessGCSU-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Robert and twenty-one of his monks left Molesme with the intention of never returning. Renaud, the viscount of <a href="/wiki/Beaune" title="Beaune">Beaune</a>, gave this group a desolate valley in a deep forest;<sup id="cite_ref-Staley_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Staley-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> there they founded <a href="/wiki/C%C3%AEteaux_Abbey" title="Cîteaux Abbey">Cîteaux Abbey</a>. <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Harding" title="Stephen Harding">Stephen Harding</a> and <a href="/wiki/Alb%C3%A9ric_of_C%C3%AEteaux" class="mw-redirect" title="Albéric of Cîteaux">Alberic</a> – two of Robert's monks from Molesme – were pivotal in founding the new house. The archbishop of Lyons, being persuaded that they could not subsist there without  the endorsement of an influential churchman, wrote in their favour to Eudo, duke of Burgundy. Eudo paid for the construction they had begun, helped the monks finance their operating expenses and gave them much land and cattle. The bishop of Challons elevated the new monastery to the canonical status of an abbey.
</p><p>In 1099, the monks of Molesme asked Robert to return and agreed to submit entirely to his interpretation of the <a href="/wiki/Rule_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Rule of St. Benedict">Rule of St. Benedict</a>; the local bishop also pressured Robert to return. He agreed and Molesme became a major center for the Benedictines under his tutelage. Albéric was made successor abbot at Cîteaux, with Stephen Harding as prior.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Robert died on 17 April 1111. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III" title="Pope Honorius III">Pope Honorius III</a> <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> him in 1222. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> is also on 17 April,<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> with the <a href="/wiki/Benedictines" title="Benedictines">Benedictines</a> celebrating him, along with <a href="/wiki/Alberic_of_C%C3%AEteaux" title="Alberic of Cîteaux">Alberic of Cîteaux</a> and <a href="/wiki/Stephen_Harding" title="Stephen Harding">Stephen Harding</a> on February 26.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <i>Vie de saint Robert de Molesme</i> was written by Guy, his immediate successor as abbot of Molesme.<sup id="cite_ref-Butler_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Butler-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
