					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Swiss abbot and bishop of Lausanne</div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg/220px-Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="403" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg/330px-Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg/440px-Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg 2x" data-file-width="700" data-file-height="1281" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Amadeus_von_lausanne.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Statue of Blessed Amadeus of Lausanne in the church of <a href="/wiki/Eschenbach_Abbey" title="Eschenbach Abbey">Eschenbach Abbey</a></div></div></div>
<p><b>Amadeus of Lausanne</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;1110</span> &#8211; 27 August 1159) was a <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> monk, abbot of <a href="/wiki/Hautecombe_Abbey" title="Hautecombe Abbey">Hautecombe Abbey</a> and the twenty-third Catholic <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Lausanne" title="Bishop of Lausanne">Bishop of Lausanne</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Amadeus was born around 1110 in the castle of <a href="/wiki/Chatte" title="Chatte">Chatte</a>, west of <a href="/wiki/Grenoble" title="Grenoble">Grenoble</a>. His father was Count Amadeus the Elder of <a href="/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand" title="Clermont-Ferrand">Clermont</a>, Count of Hauterives (Drôme) in France and a member of the royal family of <a href="/wiki/Franconia" title="Franconia">Franconia</a>. After his wife's death in 1119, he left this world with 16 of his vassals and entered the Cistercian monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Bonnevaux_Abbey_(Is%C3%A8re)" class="mw-redirect" title="Bonnevaux Abbey (Isère)">abbey of Bonnevaux</a>. His son, Amadeus, received his first education in Bonnevaux. In 1121 young Amadeus moved to the Benedictine monastery of Cluny, and his father also spent some time in Cluny to look after his son's education.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Amadeus senior founded a number of monasteries: Léoncel, <a href="/wiki/Mazan_Abbey" title="Mazan Abbey">Mazan Abbey</a>, and Montpeyroux. He was also involved with the founding of <a href="/wiki/Tami%C3%A9_Abbey" title="Tamié Abbey">Tamié Abbey</a>. He returned to Bonnevaux, where he died around 1150. He is considered "Blessed" and his memorial day is January 14.<sup id="cite_ref-Odden_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Odden-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The son soon moved on to the court of Emperor <a href="/wiki/Henry_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry V, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry V</a> in order to learn life as a knight and prepare for an aristocratic career. After Henry's death in 1125, and  unsatisfied with court life, Amadeus chose to enter a Cistercian monastery, this time choosing the famous <a href="/wiki/Clairvaux_Abbey" title="Clairvaux Abbey">Clairvaux Abbey</a>, then led by <a href="/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux" title="Bernard of Clairvaux">Bernard of Clairvaux</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Per_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Per-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Abbot_of_Hautecombe">Abbot of Hautecombe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Abbot of Hautecombe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1139 he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Hautecombe_Abbey" title="Hautecombe Abbey">Hautecombe Abbey</a> in Savoy to serve as its abbot; 200 monks came under his responsibility. One of his most momentous decisions was to move the community from its location to a new site on the shores of the <a href="/wiki/Lac_du_Bourget" title="Lac du Bourget">Lac du Bourget</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Per_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Per-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> “Through him, the monastery achieved a high level of spiritual perfection and temporal prosperity.”<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Bishop_of_Lausanne">Bishop of Lausanne</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Bishop of Lausanne">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p><a href="/wiki/Pope_Lucius_II" title="Pope Lucius II">Pope Lucius II</a> elevated Amadeus to the <a href="/wiki/Diocese_of_Lausanne" class="mw-redirect" title="Diocese of Lausanne">bishopric of Lausanne</a> in 1144. The abbot was reluctant to accept, but was consecrated on January 21, 1145.  He was often met with opposition, once having to flee the city because of violent residents. It was a difficult diocese, and in a letter he tells of how his vestments were stained red by the blood of a murdered man while the bishop tried in vain to protect him.<sup id="cite_ref-Per_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Per-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He met with the guardian bailiff of the city, <a href="/wiki/Berthold_IV,_Duke_of_Z%C3%A4hringen" title="Berthold IV, Duke of Zähringen">Berthold IV, Duke of Zähringen</a> to arrange cooperative relations. Amadeus' service as a bishop led to a period of spiritual and administrative stability for the region. Amadeus was particularly keen on educating the clergy better and leading them to deeper religious observance.
</p><p>He was often in contact with the ecclesiastical and secular authorities of his day. Letters and charters attest to his contacts with King <a href="/wiki/Conrad_III_of_Germany" title="Conrad III of Germany">Conrad III</a>, with Emperor <a href="/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" class="mw-redirect" title="Frederick I, Holy Roman Emperor">Frederick I Barbarossa</a> and the Cistercian <a href="/wiki/Pope_Eugene_III" title="Pope Eugene III">Pope Eugene III</a>, whom Amadeus knew from his days in Clairvaux. For a time, he served as the legal guardian for Blessed <a href="/wiki/Humbert_III,_Count_of_Savoy" title="Humbert III, Count of Savoy">Humbert III, Count of Savoy</a>, when Humbert's father <a href="/wiki/Amadeus_III,_Count_of_Savoy" title="Amadeus III, Count of Savoy">Amadeus III, Count of Savoy</a> died in the <a href="/wiki/Second_Crusade" title="Second Crusade">Second Crusade</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Around 1155, Emperor Frederick I Barbarossa appointed Bishop Amadeus chancellor of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Arles" title="Kingdom of Arles">Kingdom of Burgundy</a>. Amadeus of Lausanne died in <a href="/wiki/Lausanne" title="Lausanne">Lausanne</a> in 1159, aged 49. He was interred in the <a href="/wiki/Lausanne_Cathedral" title="Lausanne Cathedral">cathedral</a> before the Altar of the Holy Cross. In 1911, his tomb was rediscovered in the cathedral of Lausanne, and it still contained some relics.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Spirituality">Spirituality</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Spirituality">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As bishop, Amadeus often went on retreat at Haut-Crêt Abbey, located 15 km east of Lausanne. His Marian devotions are famous, but he also venerated <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Rome" title="Agnes of Rome">St. Agnes</a> a great deal because her memorial day (January 21) was the day Amadeus was born, began school, entered the <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novitiate</a>, took his monastic <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_counsels" title="Evangelical counsels">vows</a>, was made abbot, and consecrated a bishop.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Marian_Homilies">Marian Homilies</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Marian Homilies">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The eight Marian sermons are his most famous writings.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> As a result, he is often quoted as a classic proponent of <a href="/wiki/Marian_devotions" title="Marian devotions">Marian piety</a> in the 12th century. The seventh homily is particularly relevant, since <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> quoted from it in his <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_constitution" title="Apostolic constitution">Apostolic Constitution</a> <i><a href="/wiki/Munificentissimus_Deus" title="Munificentissimus Deus">Munificentissimus Deus</a></i> (1950) regarding the <a href="/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary" title="Assumption of Mary">Assumption of Mary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Veneration was reportedly approved in 1710 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Clement_XI" title="Pope Clement XI">Pope Clement XI</a>. His cult was confirmed on December 9, 1903 (the group "Theobald of Vienna and his 16 companions") by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_X" title="Pope Pius X">Pope Pius X</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Feast_day">Feast day</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Feast day">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His memorial day is January 28, but the day of his death on August 27 is also mentioned. In the diocese of Lausanne-Geneva-Friborg, he is commemorated on August 30.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amadeus_of_Lausanne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Amadeus of Lausanne is depicted as a Cistercian bishop with princely insignia who receives a pair of gloves from the Mother of God.
</p>
