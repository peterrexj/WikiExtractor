<p><b>Miroslav Bulešić</b> (13 May 1920 &#8211; 24 August 1947) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Croatian_people" class="mw-redirect" title="Croatian people">Croatian</a> <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He studied in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> before being recalled to his native <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a> where he was ordained in 1943 during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a> prior to two parish postings where he became a vocal critic of <a href="/wiki/Communism" title="Communism">Communism</a>. He renewed his parishes through well-organized pastoral activities and was a promoter of frequent sacramental reception. But his <a href="/wiki/Anti-communism" title="Anti-communism">criticism of Communism</a> saw him make enemies who soon set upon and succeeded in killing him.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was beatified in Croatia on 28 September 2013 on the recognition of the fact that he was killed "in odium fidei" ("in hatred of the faith"); Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presided over the beatification on the behalf of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Miroslav_Bule%C5%A1i%C4%87&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG/170px-Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="227" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG/255px-Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG/340px-Grave_of_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2112" data-file-height="2816" /></a><figcaption>Tomb from 1958 to 2003.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Miroslav Bulešić was born on 13 May 1920 in Zabroni (Čabrunići) – a village in <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a>, at that time in the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy" title="Kingdom of Italy">Kingdom of Italy</a>. He was born to Miha Bulešić and Lucija Butković. His siblings were Maria, Lucija, Zora and Beppo.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He received his <a href="/wiki/Baptism" title="Baptism">baptism</a> on 23 May 1920 in the parish church at Roveria (Juršići). In his childhood he first learned the truths of the faith from a small book that Bishop <a href="/wiki/Juraj_Dobrila" title="Juraj Dobrila">Juraj Dobrila</a> had written and the book focused on the spiritual needs of the Croatian faithful.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His initial education was spent in Roveria where his religious education teacher was the priest Ivan Pavić. In 1930, he decided to commence his studies for the <a href="/wiki/Priesthood" class="mw-redirect" title="Priesthood">priesthood</a> and after a brief stint at the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesian</a> Collegio San Luigi in <a href="/wiki/Gorizia" title="Gorizia">Gorizia</a> (Gorica) commenced his ecclesial studies at <a href="/wiki/Koper" title="Koper">Capodistria</a> (Koper) in 1931. He was there until 1939 after he did grammar school from 1931 to 1936 and then his <a href="/wiki/Lyceum" title="Lyceum">lyceum</a> until 1939 when he passed his final examinations.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Pavić sent him a letter of recommendation and said that he was a "wise, frank, pious and good" <a href="/wiki/Seminarian" class="mw-redirect" title="Seminarian">seminarian</a>. In the fall of 1939 the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Pore%C4%8D-Pula" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Poreč-Pula">Bishop of Parenzo-Pola</a> (Poreč-Pula) Trifone Pederzolli sent him to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> for further studies.
</p><p>He studied at the <a href="/wiki/Gregoriana" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregoriana">Gregoriana</a> for his philosophical and theological studies from the fall of 1939 until the summer of 1943 during <a href="/wiki/World_War_II" title="World War II">World War II</a>; the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Zagreb" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Zagreb">Archbishop of Zagreb</a> <a href="/wiki/Aloysius_Stepinac" title="Aloysius Stepinac">Aloysius Stepinac</a> provided him financial support for his studies at the Gregoriana. From 1939 to 1940 he lived at the <a href="/wiki/French_Academy_in_Rome" title="French Academy in Rome">French Academy</a> in Rome but spent the remainder of that time residing in the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Lombard_Seminary" title="Pontifical Lombard Seminary">Pontifical Lombard Seminary</a>. On 31 October 1942, he was present in <a href="/wiki/St._Peter%27s_Basilica" title="St. Peter&#39;s Basilica">St. Peter's Basilica</a> when <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII_1942_consecration_to_the_Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary" title="Pope Pius XII 1942 consecration to the Immaculate Heart of Mary">consecrated the world</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary" title="Immaculate Heart of Mary">Immaculate Heart of Mary</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His bishop recalled him later to his native <a href="/wiki/Istria" title="Istria">Istria</a> at the beginning of spring in 1943 for <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> and he received his ordination to the priesthood on 11 April 1943 from his bishop Raffaele Mario Radossi in the parish of <a href="/wiki/Svetvin%C4%8Denat" title="Svetvinčenat">Sanvincenti</a> (Svetvinčenat), in Istria.<sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Bulešić celebrated his first <a href="/wiki/Mass_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Mass in the Catholic Church">Mass</a> two weeks later on 26 April in his old parish.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He was assigned to the <a href="/wiki/Baderna" title="Baderna">Baderna</a> parish in the fall of 1943 where the <a href="/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans" title="Yugoslav Partisans">Yugoslav Partisan</a> and <a href="/wiki/Royal_Italian_Army_during_World_War_II" title="Royal Italian Army during World War II">Italian</a> forces fought with each other but was later transferred to the parish of <a href="/wiki/Kanfanar" title="Kanfanar">Canfanaro</a> (Kanfanar) in the autumn of 1945.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was considered to be bold and fearless, but was perceived as a great threat and he alluded to this in 1944 in his journal.
</p><p>Bulešić's interventions in parish life made church events more attractive to people and he introduced adorations to the <a href="/wiki/Sacred_Heart" title="Sacred Heart">Sacred Heart</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary" title="Immaculate Heart of Mary">Immaculate Heart of Mary</a> in the parish; he encouraged parishioners to recite <a href="/wiki/Rosaries" class="mw-redirect" title="Rosaries">rosaries</a> together and to receive the sacraments on a frequent basis more so for the children. But the Communists wanted to prevent the faithful from attending Masses and so introduced civil weddings and funerals but people still continued to attend the Masses and listen to their popular priest's homilies. In 1946, some <a href="/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Yugoslavia" class="mw-redirect" title="Communist Party of Yugoslavia">Communists</a> were watching people file into the parish and realized that the priest had to be killed for them to exert control over the people.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The regime even approached his family members to have them convince him to return to <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a>, but he refused and said to them: "There is a bigger need for priests right here." His relatives also advised him to be careful lest he be killed but he refused to do this. In 1946, he was made the vice-principal and a teacher of seminarians at <a href="/wiki/Pazin" title="Pazin">Pazin</a> and, at the start of March 1947, returned a large cross to its original place in the atrium after unknown hooligans removed it.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As the weeks went on it became clear to him that he might be targeted and killed and told his seminarians that to be a priest meant that the shedding of their blood for the faith was an attribute a priest needed to possess. In late June 1947, he wrote in his journal directed to <a href="/wiki/God_in_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="God in Catholicism">God</a>: "If it is Your will, I wish to come to You as soon as possible".
</p><p>From 19 August 1947, he accompanied the ecclesial delegate Jakob Ukmar (1878–1971) to several parishes such as <a href="/wiki/Buzet" title="Buzet">Buzet</a> for <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Confirmation in the Catholic Church">Confirmation</a> celebrations and on 23 August celebrated one such Mass.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> But it was interrupted when Communists burst in and attempted to stop the Mass but he rushed to the <a href="/wiki/Tabernacle" title="Tabernacle">tabernacle</a> and defended it. He was pale but calm and said: "You can pass through here only over my dead body" to which the vandals asked whether he was afraid to go to <a href="/wiki/Lani%C5%A1%C4%87e" title="Lanišće">Lanišće</a> to which he said: "You can die only once."<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He went to Lanišće on 24 August 1947 for a Confirmation service and, after Mass, he and Ukmar went to the parish house where, fifteen minutes later, others who were late for the Mass were confirmed. But around 11 AM, several Communist supporters burst into the house and stabbed him to death multiple times in the neck after having been pinned to the ground.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CH_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CH-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He had been close to the door when the attack happened while Ukmar fled to the bedroom but was beaten a minute later and left in a pool of blood on the poor though the attackers believed that he was dead. Blood was all over the walls in the aftermath of the attack and Bulešić twice cried out: "<a href="/wiki/Jesus" title="Jesus">Jesus</a>, take my soul!" The regime did not allow for his remains to be buried in Svetvinčenant but to Lanišće instead, though his remains were later relocated in 1958 to the former. His remains were reinterred later on in 2003.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Miroslav_Bule%C5%A1i%C4%87&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Spomenik,_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Spomenik%2C_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG/170px-Spomenik%2C_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG" decoding="async" width="170" height="244" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Spomenik%2C_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG/255px-Spomenik%2C_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Spomenik%2C_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG/340px-Spomenik%2C_Miroslav_Bulesic.JPG 2x" data-file-width="1792" data-file-height="2568" /></a><figcaption>Tomb from 2003.</figcaption></figure>
<p>The process of beatification began under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> on 10 August 1992 after the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> issued the "<a href="/wiki/Nihil_obstat" title="Nihil obstat">nihil obstat</a>" ("nothing against" the cause) and titled him as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> - it was the first official stage in the process. There was an effort to commence the cause in 1956 but the Communists banned this and so an informative process had opened in Rome in 1957 before the cause was transferred to the forum of Poreč-Pula on 28 March 2000 while the diocesan investigation took place.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The diocesan process lasted from 24 August 1997 until 11 September 2004 and later received C.C.S. validated in Rome. The <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> was submitted to the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> in 2010 and the theologians approved the cause on 30 March 2012. The C.C.S. approved it also on 20 November 2012 while <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> approved his beatification on 20 December 2012 after confirming that the late priest died "in odium fidei" ("in hatred of the faith"). The date for the beatification celebration was announced on 12 February 2013.<sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was <a href="/wiki/Beatified" class="mw-redirect" title="Beatified">beatified</a> on 28 September 2013 in Istria; <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">Cardinal</a> <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presided over the celebration on the behalf of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> who referred to the beatification on 29 September in his <a href="/wiki/Angelus" title="Angelus">Angelus</a> address. There were about 670 priests and 17 000 pilgrims in attendance as was the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Belgrade" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Belgrade">Belgrade archbishop</a> <a href="/wiki/Stanislav_Ho%C4%8Devar" title="Stanislav Hočevar">Stanislav Hočevar</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AZ_3-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AZ-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> assigned to the cause is Jure Bogdan.
</p>
