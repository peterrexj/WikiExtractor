<p><b>Menas of Egypt</b> (also <b>Mina</b>, <b>Minas</b>, <b>Mena</b>, <b>Meena</b>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Άγιος Μηνάς</span>, <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>: <span lang="cop"><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1107333977">.mw-parser-output .script-coptic{font-family:"Sophia Nubian","Noto Sans Coptic","FreeSerif","Quivira","Segoe UI Historic","Segoe UI Symbol","New Athena Unicode","MPH 2B Damase","Arial Coptic","Analecta","Antinoou"}</style><span class="script-coptic">Ⲁⲃⲃⲁ Ⲙⲏⲛⲁ</span></span>; 285 – c. 309), a martyr and <a href="/wiki/Thaumaturgy" title="Thaumaturgy">wonder-worker</a>, is one of the most well-known Coptic saints in the East and the West, due to the many miracles that are attributed to his intercession and prayers. Menas was a Coptic soldier in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_army" title="Roman army">Roman army</a> martyred because he refused to recant his Christian faith. The common date of his commemoration is November 11, which occurs 13 days later (November 24) on the <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a>.
</p><p>His feast day is celebrated every year on <a href="/wiki/Hathor_(month)" title="Hathor (month)">15 Hathor</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria" class="mw-redirect" title="Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria">Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria</a>, which corresponds to <a href="/wiki/November_24" title="November 24">November 24</a> on the <a href="/wiki/Gregorian_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregorian Calendar">Gregorian Calendar</a>. In <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox churches</a> that follow the old style or <a href="/wiki/Julian_calendar" title="Julian calendar">Julian calendar</a>, it is likewise celebrated on November 24. In the Eastern Orthodox churches that follow the new style or <a href="/wiki/Revised_Julian_calendar" title="Revised Julian calendar">Revised Julian calendar</a>, as well as in the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a>, it is celebrated on November 11.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Origin_of_his_name">Origin of his name</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Origin of his name">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Menas was his original name, according to the story his mother called him "Mēna" because she heard a voice saying <i>amēn</i>. Minas (Μηνᾶς) is how he is known in Greek and Armenian, while in Coptic he is known as "Mīna" (مينا).
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life_and_martyrdom">Life and martyrdom</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Life and martyrdom">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg/220px-St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="295" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg/330px-St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg/440px-St_Menas_and_boatman.jpg 2x" data-file-width="503" data-file-height="675" /></a><figcaption>St. Menas and boatman on a <a href="/wiki/Makuria" title="Makuria">Makurian</a> manuscript from <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 1000</figcaption></figure>
<p>There are many sources written in different languages (<a href="/wiki/Koine_Greek" title="Koine Greek">Koine Greek</a>, <a href="/wiki/Coptic_language" title="Coptic language">Coptic</a>, <a href="/wiki/Old_Nubian_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Old Nubian language">Old Nubian</a>, <a href="/wiki/Ge%27ez_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Ge&#39;ez language">Ge'ez</a>, <a href="/wiki/Latin" title="Latin">Latin</a>, <a href="/wiki/Syriac_language" title="Syriac language">Syriac</a>, <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>) relating to Menas.<sup id="cite_ref-ucl_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ucl-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Menas was born in Egypt in 285 in the city of <a href="/wiki/Zawyat_Razin" title="Zawyat Razin">Nikiou</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> which lay in the vicinity of <a href="/wiki/Memphis,_Egypt" title="Memphis, Egypt">Memphis</a>. His parents were ascetic <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> but did not have any children for a long time. His father's name was Eudoxios and his mother's name was Euphemia. On the feast of the Virgin <a href="/wiki/Mary,_mother_of_Jesus" title="Mary, mother of Jesus">Mary, mother of Jesus</a>, Euphemia was praying with tears before an icon of the Virgin for God to give her a son. A sound came from the icon saying "Amen". A few months later, Euphemia gave birth to a boy and named him Menas.<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Eudoxios, a ruler of one of the administrative divisions of <a href="/wiki/Egypt_(Roman_province)" class="mw-redirect" title="Egypt (Roman province)">Egypt</a>, died when Menas was fourteen years old. At the age of fifteen Menas joined the Roman army and was given a high rank due to his father's reputation. Most sources state that he served in <a href="/wiki/K%C3%BCtahya" title="Kütahya">Cotyaeus</a> in <a href="/wiki/Phrygia" title="Phrygia">Phrygia</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> although some say his appointment was in <a href="/wiki/Algeria" title="Algeria">Algeria</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Three years later he left the army, longing to devote his whole life to <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>, and headed towards the desert to live a different kind of life.
</p><p>After spending five years as a hermit, Menas saw in a revelation the <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angels</a> crowning the martyrs with glorious crowns, and longed to join those martyrs. While he was thinking about it, he heard a voice saying: "Blessed are you Menas because you have been called to the pious life from your childhood. You shall be granted three immortal crowns: one for your celibacy, another for your asceticism, and a third for your martyrdom." Menas subsequently hurried to the ruler, declaring his Christian faith.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The soldiers who executed Menas set his body on fire for three days but the body remained unharmed. Menas' sister then bribed the soldiers and managed to carry the body away. She embarked on a ship heading to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a>, where she placed his body in a church.
</p><p>When the time of persecution ended, during the papacy of <a href="/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria" title="Athanasius of Alexandria">Athanasius of Alexandria</a> the pope had a vision of an <a href="/wiki/Angel" title="Angel">angel</a> appearing to him and ordering him to load Menas' body on a camel and head towards the <a href="/wiki/Libyan_Desert" title="Libyan Desert">Libyan Desert</a>. At a certain spot near a water well at the end of <a href="/wiki/Lake_Mariout" title="Lake Mariout">Lake Mariout</a>, not far from Alexandria, the camel stopped and wouldn't move. The Christians took this a sign from God and buried Menas' body there.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Berbers" title="Berbers">Berbers</a> of <a href="/wiki/Cyrenaica" title="Cyrenaica">Pentapolis</a> rose against the cities around Alexandria. As the people were getting ready to face them, the Roman governor decided to secretly take the body of Menas with him to be his deliverer and his strong protector. Through Menas' blessings, the governor overcame the Berbers and returned victorious. However, he decided not to return the body to its original place and wanted to take it to Alexandria. On the way back, as they passed by Lake Mariout at the same spot where the body was originally buried, the camel carrying the body knelt down and would not move. People moved the body to another camel, but the second camel would not move either.<sup id="cite_ref-treasures_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-treasures-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The governor finally realized that this was God's command. He made a coffin from decay-resistant wood and placed the silver coffin in it.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg/220px-Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="329" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg/330px-Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg/440px-Pilgrim_flask_St._Menas_Louvre_MN1469.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1625" data-file-height="2427" /></a><figcaption><a href="/wiki/Terracotta" title="Terracotta">Terracotta</a> pilgrim's <a href="/wiki/Menas_flask" class="mw-redirect" title="Menas flask">Menas flask</a> impressed with Saint Mina between two camels, Byzantine, probably made at <a href="/wiki/Abu_Mena" title="Abu Mena">Abu Mina</a>, Egypt, c. 6th–7th century. (Louvre Museum)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Most versions of the story state that the location of the tomb was then forgotten until its miraculous rediscovery by a local shepherd. A shepherd was feeding his sheep in that location, and a sick lamb fell on the ground. As it struggled to get on its feet again, its scab was cured. The story spread quickly and the sick who came to this spot recovered from whatever illnesses they had just by lying on the ground. The Ethiopian <a href="/wiki/Synaxarium" title="Synaxarium">Synaxarium</a> describes <a href="/wiki/Constantine_I" class="mw-redirect" title="Constantine I">Constantine I</a> sending his sick <a href="/wiki/Constantina" title="Constantina">daughter</a> to the shepherd to be cured, and credits her with finding Menas' body, after which Constantine ordered the construction of a church at the site. Some versions of the story replace Constantine with the late-5th century emperor <a href="/wiki/Zeno_(emperor)" title="Zeno (emperor)">Zeno</a>, but <a href="/wiki/Archaeology" title="Archaeology">archaeologists</a> have dated the original foundation to the late 4th century.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> According to the Zeno version, his daughter was leprous and his advisors suggested that she should try that place, and she did. At night Menas appeared to the girl and informed her that his body was buried in that place. The following morning, Zeno's daughter was cured and she related her vision about the saint to her servants. Zeno immediately ordered Mina's body to be dug out and a cathedral to be built there.
</p><p>After his martyrdom in the early fourth century, Menas acquired a reputation for miraculous healing powers. The cult of Saint Menas was centered on <a href="/wiki/Abu_Mena" title="Abu Mena">Abu Mena</a> near Alexandria.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
Sick people from all over the Christian world used to visit that city and were healed through the intercessions of Menas, who became known as the <i>Wonders' Maker</i>. Today, numerous little clay <a href="/wiki/Menas_flask" class="mw-redirect" title="Menas flask">Menas flasks</a>, or bottles for holy water or oil on which the saint's name and picture are stamped, are found by archeologists in diverse countries around the <a href="/wiki/Mediterranean" class="mw-redirect" title="Mediterranean">Mediterranean</a> world, such as <a href="/wiki/Heidelberg" title="Heidelberg">Heidelberg</a> in Germany, <a href="/wiki/Milan" title="Milan">Milan</a> in Italy, <a href="/wiki/Dalmatia" title="Dalmatia">Dalmatia</a> in <a href="/wiki/Croatia" title="Croatia">Croatia</a>, <a href="/wiki/Marseille" title="Marseille">Marseille</a> in France, <a href="/wiki/Dongola" title="Dongola">Dongola</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sudan" title="Sudan">Sudan</a>, <a href="/wiki/Meols" title="Meols">Meols</a> (<a href="/wiki/Cheshire" title="Cheshire">Cheshire</a>) in England, and the holy city of <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a>, as well as modern <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eritrea" title="Eritrea">Eritrea</a>. Pilgrims would buy these bottles and take them back to their relatives.<sup id="cite_ref-cocn_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cocn-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Patronage">Patronage</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Patronage">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Menas is the patron saint of many German and Swiss towns. He was venerated as the protector of pilgrims and merchants.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> St. Menas is also noted for healing various illnesses.<sup id="cite_ref-oca_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-oca-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Iconography">Iconography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Iconography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Menas is generally shown between two camels, the animals that, according to the legend, returned his body to Egypt for burial.<sup id="cite_ref-ucl_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-ucl-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_saint">Military saint</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Military saint">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Most likely Mina of Mareotis, Mina of Cotyaes, and Mina of Constantinople, are all the same person honored in different places.<sup id="cite_ref-mershman_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-mershman-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
Menas is sometimes called <i>Mina the Soldier</i> also called the "Wonder worker" in the West, where he is venerated as a <a href="/wiki/Military_saint" title="Military saint">military saint</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="New_Monastery_and_Cathedral_of_Saint_Mina">New Monastery and Cathedral of Saint Mina</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: New Monastery and Cathedral of Saint Mina">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>As soon as <a href="/wiki/Pope_Cyril_VI_of_Alexandria" title="Pope Cyril VI of Alexandria">Pope Cyril VI of Alexandria</a> became pope and patriarch on <a href="/wiki/Mark_the_Evangelist" title="Mark the Evangelist">Saint Mark</a>'s Throne, he began to put the foundations for a great <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> close to the remains of the old city. Today, the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_Saint_Mina" title="Monastery of Saint Mina">Monastery of Saint Mina</a> is one of the most famous <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monasteries</a> in Egypt. The relics of Saint Mina, as well as that of Pope Cyril VI of Alexandria lie in this monastery.  The cathedral of Saint Mina was destroyed during the Arab invasions of the 7th century.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="El_Alamein_battle">El Alamein battle</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Menas_of_Egypt&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: El Alamein battle">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Pottery_pilgrim_bottle,_for_storing_water_from_the_spring_of_Saint_Menas._Byzantine_period._From_Alexandria,_Egypt._The_Petrie_Museum_of_Egyptian_Archaeology,_London.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Pottery_pilgrim_bottle%2C_for_storing_water_from_the_spring_of_Saint_Menas._Byzantine_period._From_Alexandria%2C_Egypt._The_Petrie_Museum_of_Egyptian_Archaeology%2C_London.jpg/220px-thumbnail.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="233" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Pottery_pilgrim_bottle%2C_for_storing_water_from_the_spring_of_Saint_Menas._Byzantine_period._From_Alexandria%2C_Egypt._The_Petrie_Museum_of_Egyptian_Archaeology%2C_London.jpg/330px-thumbnail.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9d/Pottery_pilgrim_bottle%2C_for_storing_water_from_the_spring_of_Saint_Menas._Byzantine_period._From_Alexandria%2C_Egypt._The_Petrie_Museum_of_Egyptian_Archaeology%2C_London.jpg/440px-thumbnail.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3397" data-file-height="3597" /></a><figcaption>Pottery pilgrim bottle, for storing water from the spring of Saint Menas. Byzantine period. From Alexandria, Egypt. The Petrie Museum of Egyptian Archaeology, London</figcaption></figure>
<p>According to orthodox Christian belief, in June 1942, during the North-Africa campaign that was decisive for the outcome of the Second World War, the German forces under the command of General Rommel were on their way to Alexandria, and happened to make a halt near a place which the Arabs call <a href="/wiki/El_Alamein" title="El Alamein">El Alamein</a>. An ancient ruined church nearby in Abu Mena was dedicated to Saint Menas; there some people say he is buried. Here the weaker Allied forces, including some Greeks, confronted the numerically and militarily superior German army, and the result of the coming <a href="/wiki/First_Battle_of_El_Alamein" title="First Battle of El Alamein">battle of El Alamein</a> seemed certain. During the first night of engagement, at midnight, Saint Menas came out of his ruined church and appeared in the midst of the German camp at the head of a caravan of camels, exactly as he was shown on the walls of the ruined church in one of the frescoes depicting his miracles. This astounding and terrifying apparition so undermined German morale that it contributed to the brilliant victory of the Allies. <a href="/wiki/Winston_Churchill" title="Winston Churchill">Winston Churchill</a> said of this victory: "Now this is not the end. It is not even the beginning of the end, but it is, perhaps, the end of the beginning." He also wrote: "Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein, we never had a defeat.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
