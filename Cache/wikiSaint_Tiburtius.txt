					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">Christian martyr</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">This article is about the martyr beheaded c. 286 (feast day 11 August). For the brother-in-law of St. Cecilia, see <a href="/wiki/Tiburtius,_Valerian,_and_Maximus" title="Tiburtius, Valerian, and Maximus">Tiburtius, Valerian, and Maximus</a>.</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Tiburtius</div></th></tr><tr><td colspan="2" class="infobox-image"><span class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Frameless"><a href="/wiki/File:Tiburtius.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Tiburtius.jpg/220px-Tiburtius.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="374" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/81/Tiburtius.jpg 1.5x" data-file-width="275" data-file-height="467" /></a></span><div class="infobox-caption">Tiburtius walking on coals</div></td></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Martyrs</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">3rd century<br />Rome</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy" class="mw-redirect" title="Oriental Orthodoxy">Oriental Orthodoxy</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">11 August</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Tiburtius</b>, according to Christian legend, was a Christian martyr and saint. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is 11 August which is the same as <a href="/wiki/Saint_Susanna" title="Saint Susanna">Saint Susanna</a>. The two were not related, but are sometimes associated because they are venerated on the same day.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Hagiography">Hagiography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Tiburtius&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Hagiography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The story is related in the legend of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Sebastian" title="Saint Sebastian">Sebastian</a> that <a href="/wiki/Chromatius_of_Rome" title="Chromatius of Rome">Agrestius Chromatius</a>, allegedly <a href="/wiki/Praefectus_urbis" class="mw-redirect" title="Praefectus urbis">prefect of Rome</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-allegedly_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allegedly-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> condemned several Christians to death. The prefect, however, was converted by <a href="/wiki/Mark_and_Marcellian" title="Mark and Marcellian">Tranquillinus</a>, father of <a href="/wiki/Mark_and_Marcellian" title="Mark and Marcellian">Mark and Marcellian</a>, and baptized by <a href="/wiki/Polycarp" title="Polycarp">Polycarp</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tiburtius was the only son of Agrestius Chromatius; he was also baptized through the persuasion of Sebastian, who was his <a href="/wiki/Godparent" title="Godparent">godfather</a> in baptism, according to this legend.<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tiburtius lay hidden during <a href="/wiki/Diocletianic_Persecution" title="Diocletianic Persecution">the persecution by Roman Emperor Diocletian</a> in his father's house. Accused by a traitor, he was brought before the (allegedly) prefect Fabianus<sup id="cite_ref-allegedly_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-allegedly-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and tried. He confessed his faith, which he confirmed by a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a>, for, protecting himself only by the sign of the cross, he walked barefoot over red-hot coals without suffering any injury. But the miracle was ascribed to <a href="/wiki/Magic_(paranormal)" class="mw-redirect" title="Magic (paranormal)">magic</a> and Tiburtius was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> at the third milestone of the <a href="/wiki/Via_Labicana" title="Via Labicana">Via Labicana</a> in the year 286. The spot of execution was called "at the two <a href="/wiki/Bay_Laurel" class="mw-redirect" title="Bay Laurel">laurel</a> trees" (<i>ad duas lauros</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-Meier_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Meier-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Tiburtius is mentioned in 23 epigram of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">pope Damasus I</a> (366–384):
</p>
<dl><dd>When the sword cut the pious entrails of the mother,</dd>
<dd>the outstanding martyr, despising the prince of the world,</dd>
<dd>seeks the heights of heaven in the company of Christ.</dd>
<dd>Here for you will ever remain saintly honour and praises.</dd>
<dd>Kind Tiburtius, beloved of God, I beg you take care of Damasus.</dd></dl>
<p>Tiburtius is spoken of in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a> for 11 August in the following terms: "At Rome, in the cemetery at the two laurel trees at the third milestone on the Via Labicana, Saint Tiburtius, martyr, whose praises <a href="/wiki/Pope_Damasus_I" title="Pope Damasus I">Pope Saint Damasus</a> sang."<sup id="cite_ref-MR_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-MR-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The commemoration of him that was included in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> was removed in <a href="/wiki/Mysterii_Paschalis" title="Mysterii Paschalis">1969</a>, because "apart from his name, the only thing known of him is that he was buried in the <i>Inter duas lauros</i> cemetery on the Via Labicana on an 11 August".<sup id="cite_ref-CR_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CR-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
