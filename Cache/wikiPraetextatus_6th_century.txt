					<div id="contentSub"><div id="mw-content-subtitle"><span class="mw-redirectedfrom">(Redirected from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Praetextatus_(6th_century)&amp;redirect=no" class="mw-redirect" title="Praetextatus (6th century)">Praetextatus (6th century)</a>)</span></div></div>
					
					
					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><p>
Saint <b>Prætextatus</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Prétextat/Prix de Rouen</i>; died 25 February 586), also spelled <i>Praetextatus</i>, <i>Pretextat(us)</i>, and known as <b>Saint Prix</b>, was the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Rouen" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Rouen">bishop of Rouen</a> from 549 until his <a href="/wiki/Assassination" title="Assassination">assassination</a> in 586. He appears as a prominent character in <a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">Gregory of Tours</a>’ <i>Historia Francorum</i> (<i>History of the Franks</i>).<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> This is the principal source from which information on his life can be drawn. He features in many of its most notable passages, including those pertaining to his trial in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> (in 577) and his rivalry with the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian_dynasty" title="Merovingian dynasty">Merovingian</a> Queen <a href="/wiki/Fredegund" title="Fredegund">Fredegund</a>. The events of his life, as portrayed by Gregory of Tours, have been important in the development of modern understandings of various facets of Merovingian society, such as law, the rivalry between kings and bishops, church councils, and the power of queens.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pr%C3%A6textatus_(bishop_of_Rouen)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span id="Marriage_of_Merovech_and_Brunhild.2C_trial_and_exile"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Marriage_of_Merovech_and_Brunhild,_trial_and_exile">Marriage of Merovech and Brunhild, trial and exile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pr%C3%A6textatus_(bishop_of_Rouen)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Marriage of Merovech and Brunhild, trial and exile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>He was present at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Paris_557" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Paris 557">Council of Paris 557</a>, where marriages within certain degrees of <a href="/wiki/Consanguinity" title="Consanguinity">consanguinity</a> were declared incestuous, and at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Tours_567" class="mw-redirect" title="Council of Tours 567">Council of Tours</a> (566). In 575, Prætextatus presided as minister over the wedding of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Merovech_of_Soissons&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Merovech of Soissons (page does not exist)">Merovech of Soissons</a>, son of King <a href="/wiki/Chilperic_I" title="Chilperic I">Chiperic I of Neustria</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Brunhilda_of_Austrasia" title="Brunhilda of Austrasia">Brunhild</a>, the widow of King <a href="/wiki/Sigebert_I" title="Sigebert I">Sigebert I</a> of Austrasia and Merovech’s own aunt, in his diocese of <a href="/wiki/Rouen" title="Rouen">Rouen</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> King Chiperic opposed this marriage and later brought charges against Prætextatus, accusing him of bribing his people with gifts to turn them against his kingship. Prætextatus was temporarily banished and a council of bishops convened in the Church of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Saint Peter</a> the Apostle in Paris to decide his fate. At this council, which took place in 577, Chilperic accused Prætextatus of further wrongdoing, including conspiracy, contravening the <a href="/wiki/Canon_law" title="Canon law">canons</a> (church law) and theft. It is heavily implied by Gregory of Tours that these accusation were false, as is noted by Edward James.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Once the King had retired Gregory spoke up for the bishop, giving a long speech on the topic. Prætextatus eventually confessed to his crimes, although it is implied by Gregory that he was manipulated into doing so. Chilperic then forged canons that stipulated a bishop in Prætextatus’ position must be excommunicated. Gregory again spoke up for him, but to no avail: Prætextatus was forced into exile on the island of <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a>.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Return_from_Exile">Return from Exile</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pr%C3%A6textatus_(bishop_of_Rouen)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Return from Exile">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Prætextatus returned from his enforced <a href="/wiki/Exile" title="Exile">exile</a> at the request of the people of Rouen sometime after the death of Chilperic in 584.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Upon returning he went to the <a href="/wiki/Burgundians" title="Burgundians">Burgundian</a> <a href="/wiki/Guntram" title="Guntram">King Guntram</a> asking for an investigation into the case that had brought against him in 577. Fredegund, the now widow of Chilperic, opposed this request and argued that Praetexatus should not be awarded his old diocese on account of his having been exiled following the decision of forty-five bishops. Guntram moved to call another council to address this issue but before it could be convened Bishop Ragnemond of Paris spoke on Prætextatus’ behalf, saying that the proper course of action in his case would have been to have made him do <a href="/wiki/Penance" title="Penance">penance</a>, not have him exiled.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> As a result, Prætextatus was reinstated as bishop of Rouen.
</p><p>Around this time Praetexatus made an appearance at the <a href="/wiki/Synod_of_M%C3%A2con" title="Synod of Mâcon">Council of Mâcon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> At this council he read some prayers that he had composed himself while in exile, although they were received somewhat negatively. <a href="/wiki/Yitzhak_Hen" title="Yitzhak Hen">Yitzhak Hen</a> has cited this as an example of the ‘<a href="/wiki/Liturgy" title="Liturgy">liturgical</a> creativity’ prevalent at the time.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pr%C3%A6textatus_(bishop_of_Rouen)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:221px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg/219px-Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg" decoding="async" width="219" height="158" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg/329px-Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg/438px-Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2327" data-file-height="1681" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fredegunda_visita_a_pretextato_en_su_lecho_de_mueret.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Frédégonde visite Prétextat sur son lit de mort</i> (Fredegund visits Prætextatus on his deathbed), painting by <a href="/wiki/Lawrence_Alma-Tadema" title="Lawrence Alma-Tadema">Lawrence Alma-Tadema</a>, of the bishop's enemy enjoying his suffering.</div></div></div><p>Praetextus was assassinated at his own church in Rouen on 24 February 586.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Gregory of Tours implies strongly that this was arranged by Queen Fredegund. The assassination, he writes, occurred shortly following a "bitter exchange of words" between the two.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> Fredegund visited Prætextatus on his deathbed. At this meeting he accused her of having organised the whole thing. It is likely that this was a fair accusation given that Fredegund later had the man who committed the act beaten – this man subsequently implicated her in the assassination plot.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> It is not, however, certain that Fredegund was behind the assassination, as is exemplified by a later passage in which King Guntram refers to the event as remaining worthy of investigation.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Prætextatus’ assassin was killed, after his confession, by a man referred to as his nephew, which suggests the bishop had at least one sibling.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> This is the only reference to any of his family members.
</p><p>Joaquin Martinez Pizarro has argued that Praextextatus’ rivalry with the ‘<a href="/wiki/Jezebel" title="Jezebel">Jezebel</a>-like Fredegund’ is part of a <a href="/wiki/Typology_(theology)" title="Typology (theology)">typological</a> tradition, adhered to by Gregory of Tours, that made contemporary rivalries between bishops and rulers echo <a href="/wiki/Old_Testament" title="Old Testament">Old Testament</a> rivalries between prophets and kings.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pr%C3%A6textatus_(bishop_of_Rouen)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Prætextatus’ life is notable for various reasons. His heavy participation in a number of <a href="/wiki/Category:6th-century_church_councils" title="Category:6th-century church councils">church councils</a>, one of which was his own trial, mean he took part in some of the most revealing events of the Merovingian period, at least in terms of the workings of the church and its relationship with secular politics. Hen states that these councils ‘were [...] a political stage, where bishops could fight each other over power, control and prestige, and where the King and his men could settle accounts with unfaithful bishops’.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> She cites Prætextatus’ trial as a prime example. Moreover, Gregory Halfond has stated that at this trial, even though he is being tried for treason and stealing royal property, Prætextatus still had to be brought before an <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">episcopal</a> court: this highlights the power of the Merovingian church, even against aggressive kings like Chilperic.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Historians have also suggested that the trial of Prætextatus serves as a key example of Gregory of Tours’ <a href="/wiki/Pedagogy" title="Pedagogy">pedagogical</a> bent, whereby many of the events he portrays are meant to serve as instructive examples of proper church practice. Martin Heinzelmann cites Gregory's speech on Prætextatus’ behalf as the chief example of this.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> As such, this event in Prætextatus’ life was an important part of Gregory's literary aims.
</p><p>Prætextatus has been <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonized</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.
</p>
