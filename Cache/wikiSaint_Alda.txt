<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;">Saint</div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Aldobrandesca</div></th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">1249<br /><a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, <a href="/wiki/Italy" title="Italy">Italy</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">1309<br />Italy</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Roman Catholicism</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints" title="Calendar of saints">Feast</a></th><td class="infobox-data">April 26</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Aldobrandesca</b> (also known as <b>Aldo</b>, <b>Alda</b>, <b>Aude, Blanca,</b> and <b>Bruna</b>)<sup id="cite_ref-blessedalda_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blessedalda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> (1249, <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, Italy – 1309) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-staldo_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staldo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Nurse" class="mw-redirect" title="Nurse">nurse</a>.
</p><p>When Aldobrandesca was a young woman, her parents arranged a marriage for her, which she reluctantly agreed to, but she grew to love her husband, anyway.  He died young, leaving her with no children, but she chose to remain celibate, dedicate herself to prayer, and give away all her belongings.  She joined the <a href="/wiki/Humiliati" title="Humiliati">Humiliati</a>, an Italian religious order of women that worked with the poor and the ill.<sup id="cite_ref-loyolapress_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyolapress-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-staldo_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staldo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  She also felt called to reform prostitutes and "the fallen".<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>  It was reported that she wore a <a href="/wiki/Cilice" title="Cilice">hair shirt</a> to make penance for her erotic memories of her husband, which seemed to work, although she made "ever more extreme bodily penances"<sup id="cite_ref-loyolapress_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyolapress-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> to deal with the temptation.
</p><p>She was "greatly honoured"<sup id="cite_ref-blessedalda_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-blessedalda-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in Siena and called "a popular curiosity in the town"<sup id="cite_ref-loyolapress_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyolapress-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> due to her many reported miracles, ecstasies, and trances.  According to Loyola Press, she was a saint because of her charitable works, not her trances, demonstrated by her forgiveness towards a group of people who abused her during a trance, despite the excruciating pain they caused her.<sup id="cite_ref-loyolapress_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyolapress-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She ministered to the sick and the poor, moving into the hospital in Siena towards the end of her life, "subjecting herself to great mortifications".<sup id="cite_ref-staldo_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staldo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>  She is believed to have experienced ecstasies and visions, and healed at least four people.  She performed charitable works everyday, up to her death in 1309.<sup id="cite_ref-staldo_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staldo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-loyolapress_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-loyolapress-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Her <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is April 26.<sup id="cite_ref-staldo_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-staldo-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
