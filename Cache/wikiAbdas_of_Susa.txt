<p><b>Abdas</b>, (also <b>Abda</b>, <b>Abdias</b>, and <b>Audas</b>) was <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of <a href="/wiki/Susa" title="Susa">Susa</a> in <a href="/wiki/Iran" title="Iran">Iran</a>. <a href="/wiki/Socrates_Scholasticus" class="mw-redirect" title="Socrates Scholasticus">Socrates of Constantinople</a> calls him "bishop of <a href="/wiki/Iran" title="Iran">Persia</a>".<sup id="cite_ref-socrates_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-socrates-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He was executed under the orders of <a href="/wiki/Shah" title="Shah">shah</a> <a href="/wiki/Yazdegerd_I" title="Yazdegerd I">Yazdegerd I</a> after to refusing to rebuild a <a href="/wiki/Zoroastrian" class="mw-redirect" title="Zoroastrian">Zoroastrian</a> <a href="/wiki/Fire_temple" title="Fire temple">fire temple</a> that he had destroyed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Abdas_of_Susa&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abdas was born in fourth-century <a href="/wiki/Chaldoran_County" title="Chaldoran County">Chaldor</a> to a <a href="/wiki/Zoroastrian" class="mw-redirect" title="Zoroastrian">Zoroastrian</a> mother, who educated him in matters of virtue. After Abdas reached adulthood, he was ordained a Christian priest, and built up in his hometown a monastery and a school, which grew to have around 60 teachers. Abdas baptized many converts in Chaldor, which caused the magi to arrest him. In prison, Abdas was subjected to humiliations, hunger and pain, but remained a Christian until his release. Abdas became a bishop in Kaskhar (<a href="/wiki/Susa" title="Susa">Susa</a>).<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Abdas was an associate of <a href="/wiki/Maruthas_of_Martyropolis" title="Maruthas of Martyropolis">Maruthas of Martyropolis</a>. Abdas is supposed to have helped <a href="/wiki/Maruthas" class="mw-redirect" title="Maruthas">Maruthas</a> in driving out a demon from King Yezdegerd's son.<sup id="cite_ref-socrates_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-socrates-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> However, his impetuosity, put an end to the good relations between the Persian king and the Christian community. In <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr><span style="white-space:nowrap;">&#8201;419-420</span>, Abdas, in cooperation with a band of Christian priests and laymen, levelled a Zoroastrian <a href="/wiki/Fire_temple" title="Fire temple">fire temple</a>, for which they were summoned to court to answer for their actions. Yazdegerd I reputedly asked Abdas; "Since you are the chief and leader of these men, why do you allow them to despise our kingdom, to transgress against our command, and to act in accordance with their own will? Do you demolish and destroy our houses of worship and the foundations of our fire temples, which we have received from the fathers of our fathers to honor?"<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPayne201547_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayne201547-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> While Abdas hesitated to answer, a priest in his entourage replied back, saying "I demolished the foundation and extinguished the fire because it is not a house of God, nor is the fire the daughter of God."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPayne201547_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayne201547-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Demolishing a fire temple was reportedly a way of broadcasting the "victory of Christianity."<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPayne201547_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayne201547-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Abdas ultimately refused to have the fire temple rebuilt, and was as a result, he was clubbed to death.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p><p>Abdas' companions included the priests Hashu and Isaac, the secretary Ephrem, the hypodeacon Papa, the laymen Daduk and Durdan, and Papa, a brother of Abdas himself were also killed. His feast day is 16 May.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2022)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
