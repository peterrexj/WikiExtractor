<p><b>Pope Gregory XVI</b> (<a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Gregorius XVI</i>; <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Gregorio XVI</i>; born <b>Bartolomeo Alberto Cappellari</b>; 18 September 1765 – 1 June 1846) was head of the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> and ruler of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> from 2 February 1831 to his death in June 1846.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPham2004187_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPham2004187-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> He had adopted the name <b>Mauro</b> upon entering the <a href="/wiki/Religious_order_(Catholic)" title="Religious order (Catholic)">religious order</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">Camaldolese</a>.
</p><p>Strongly conservative and traditionalist, he opposed democratic and modernising reforms in the Papal States and throughout <a href="/wiki/Europe" title="Europe">Europe</a>, seeing them as fronts for revolutionary <a href="/wiki/Leftism" class="mw-redirect" title="Leftism">leftism</a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (March 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> Against these trends, Gregory XVI sought to strengthen the religious and political authority of the papacy, a position known as <a href="/wiki/Ultramontanism" title="Ultramontanism">ultramontanism</a>. In the encyclical <i><a href="/wiki/Mirari_vos" title="Mirari vos">Mirari vos</a></i>, he pronounced it "false and absurd, or rather mad, that we must secure and guarantee to each one liberty of conscience." He encouraged missionary activity abroad and <a href="/wiki/Abolitionism" title="Abolitionism">condemned the slave trade</a>, which at the time of his pontificate was increasingly suppressed. 
</p><p>He is the most recent pope to take the pontifical name "<a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Pope Gregory (disambiguation)">Gregory</a>", the last to govern the Papal States for the whole duration of his pontificate, and the most recent not to have be a bishop when elected. Until the accession of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Francis" title="Pope Francis">Pope Francis</a> in 2013, he was the most recent pope to have been a priest of a religious order.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Bartolomeo Alberto Cappellari was born at <a href="/wiki/Belluno" title="Belluno">Belluno</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Republic_of_Venice" title="Republic of Venice">Republic of Venice</a>, on 18 September 1765, to an <a href="/wiki/Nobility_of_Italy" title="Nobility of Italy">Italian lower noble family</a>. His parents were from a small village named Pesariis, in <a href="/wiki/Friuli" title="Friuli">Friuli</a>. His father was a lawyer. At the age of eighteen Bartolomeo Cappellari joined the order of the <a href="/wiki/Camaldolese" title="Camaldolese">Camaldolese</a><sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000336_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000336-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> (part of the <a href="/wiki/Benedictine" class="mw-redirect" title="Benedictine">Benedictine</a> monastic family) and entered the <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_St._Michael_(Murano)" class="mw-redirect" title="Monastery of St. Michael (Murano)">Monastery of San Michele</a> in <a href="/wiki/Murano" title="Murano">Murano</a>, near <a href="/wiki/Venice" title="Venice">Venice</a>. He was ordained a priest in 1787.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> As a Camaldolese <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a>, Cappellari rapidly gained distinction for his <a href="/wiki/Christian_theology" title="Christian theology">theological</a> and linguistic skills, and was assigned to teach philosophy and theology at San Michele in 1787, at the age of 22.
</p><p>In 1790, at the age of 25, he was appointed <i>censor librorum</i> for his Order, as well as for the Holy Office at Venice.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He went to Rome in 1795 and in 1799 published a polemic against the Italian <a href="/wiki/Jansenism" title="Jansenism">Jansenists</a> titled <i>II Trionfo della Santa Sede</i> ("The Triumph of the Holy See"),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000337_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000337-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> which passed through various editions in Italy and was translated into several European languages. In 1800 he became a member of the Academy of the Catholic Religion, founded by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII" title="Pope Pius VII">Pope Pius VII</a> (1800–1823), to which he contributed memoirs on theological and philosophical questions. In 1805, at the age of 40, he was appointed abbot of the <a href="/wiki/San_Gregorio_Magno_al_Celio" title="San Gregorio Magno al Celio">Monastery of San Gregorio</a> on Rome's <a href="/wiki/Caelian_Hill" title="Caelian Hill">Caelian Hill</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPham2004322_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPham2004322-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>When the army of the French Emperor <a href="/wiki/Napoleon" title="Napoleon">Napoleon</a> took Rome and arrested and deported Pius VII to France in 1809, Cappellari fled to <a href="/wiki/Murano" title="Murano">Murano</a>, where he taught in the <a href="/wiki/San_Michele_in_Isola" title="San Michele in Isola">Monastery of St. Michele</a> of his Order, where he had first become a monk. From there he and a group of monks moved their little college to <a href="/wiki/Padua" title="Padua">Padua</a> in 1814. After Napoleon's final defeat, the <a href="/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna" title="Congress of Vienna">Congress of Vienna</a> re-established the sovereignty of the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a> over central Italy and Cappellari was called back to Rome to assume the post of <a href="/wiki/Vicar_general" title="Vicar general">vicar general</a> of the Camaldolese Order. He was then appointed as Counsellor to the <a href="/wiki/Inquisition" title="Inquisition">Inquisition</a>, and later promoted to be Consultor (29 February 1820) and then, on 1 October 1826, <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">Prefect</a> of the Congregation of <i><a href="/wiki/Propaganda_Fide" class="mw-redirect" title="Propaganda Fide">Propaganda Fide</a></i> ("Propagation of the Faith"),<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPham2004322_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPham2004322-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> which dealt with all missionary work outside of the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Empire" title="Spanish Empire">Spanish Empire</a>, including missions to the non-Catholic states in Europe.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Twice he was offered a bishopric and twice he refused.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Cardinal">Cardinal</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Cardinal">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg/200px-Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg" decoding="async" width="200" height="150" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg/300px-Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg/400px-Grand_Gala_Berlin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3648" data-file-height="2736" /></a><figcaption>"The Grand Gala Berlin" is a luxury carriage constructed in Rome during the first half of the nineteenth century. It is the work of two pontiffs: <a href="/wiki/Leo_XII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XII">Leo XII</a>, who called for it to be produced in the years 1824–1826, and Gregory XVI, who requested some important modifications.</figcaption></figure>
<p>On 21 March 1825, Cappellari was created <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholicism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholicism)">cardinal</a> <i>in pectore</i> (published 13 March 1826) by Pope <a href="/wiki/Leo_XII" class="mw-redirect" title="Leo XII">Leo XII</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000335_8-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000335-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> and shortly afterwards he was asked to negotiate a <a href="/wiki/Concordat" title="Concordat">concordat</a> to safeguard the rights of Catholics in the <a href="/wiki/Low_Countries" title="Low Countries">Low Countries</a>, a diplomatic task which he completed successfully. He also negotiated a peace on behalf of <a href="/wiki/Armenian_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Armenian Catholic">Armenian Catholics</a> with the <a href="/wiki/Ottoman_Empire" title="Ottoman Empire">Ottoman Empire</a>. He publicly condemned the <a href="/wiki/November_Uprising" title="November Uprising">Polish revolutionaries</a>, who he thought were seeking to undermine Russian Tsar <a href="/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia" title="Nicholas I of Russia">Nicholas I</a>'s efforts to support the <a href="/wiki/Bourbon_Restoration_in_France" title="Bourbon Restoration in France">Catholic royalist cause</a> in France by forcing him to divert his troops to suppress the uprising in Poland.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Cappellari had never travelled outside Italy and was most familiar with Venice and Rome. He spoke Italian and Latin fluently, but no other European languages, and did not understand European politics.<sup id="cite_ref-owen_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-owen-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> However, he was proficient in <a href="/wiki/Armenian_language" title="Armenian language">Armenian</a>, and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Haruti%27iwn_Awgerian&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Haruti&#39;iwn Awgerian (page does not exist)">Haruti'iwn Awgerian</a> (<a href="/w/index.php?title=Pascal_Aucher&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pascal Aucher (page does not exist)">Pascal Aucher</a>)'s 1827 Venice edition of works attributed to <a href="/wiki/Severian_of_Gabala" title="Severian of Gabala">Severian of Gabala</a> and translated into Armenian was dedicated to him.
</p>

<p>On 2 February 1831, after a fifty-day <a href="/wiki/Papal_conclave,_1830%E2%80%9331" class="mw-redirect" title="Papal conclave, 1830–31">conclave</a>, Cappellari was unexpectedly chosen to succeed <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_VIII" title="Pope Pius VIII">Pope Pius VIII</a> (1829–30). His election was influenced by the fact that the cardinal considered the most <i><a href="/wiki/Papabile" title="Papabile">papabile</a></i>, <a href="/wiki/Giacomo_Giustiniani" title="Giacomo Giustiniani">Giacomo Giustiniani</a>, was <a href="/wiki/Jus_exclusivae" title="Jus exclusivae">vetoed</a> by King <a href="/wiki/Ferdinand_VII_of_Spain" title="Ferdinand VII of Spain">Ferdinand VII of Spain</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> There then arose a deadlock between the other two major candidates, <a href="/wiki/Emmanuele_De_Gregorio" class="mw-redirect" title="Emmanuele De Gregorio">Emmanuele De Gregorio</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bartolomeo_Pacca" title="Bartolomeo Pacca">Bartolomeo Pacca</a>.  What finally drove the cardinals to make a decision was a message from the Duke of Parma notifying them that revolt was about to break out in the northern Papal States.<sup id="cite_ref-owen_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-owen-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> To resolve the impasse, the cardinals turned to Cappellari, but it took eighty-three ballots for the canonically required two-thirds majority to be reached.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the time of election, Cardinal Cappellari was not yet a bishop: he is the most recent man to be elected pope prior to his episcopal consecration. He was consecrated as bishop by <a href="/wiki/Bartolomeo_Pacca" title="Bartolomeo Pacca">Bartolomeo Pacca</a>, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Ostia_and_Velletri" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Ostia and Velletri">Bishop of Ostia and Velletri</a> and <a href="/wiki/Dean_of_the_Sacred_College_of_Cardinals" class="mw-redirect" title="Dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals">dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPham2004322_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPham2004322-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> with <a href="/wiki/Pietro_Francesco_Galleffi" title="Pietro Francesco Galleffi">Pietro Francesco Galleffi</a>, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Porto_e_Santa_Rufina" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Porto e Santa Rufina">Bishop of Porto e Santa Rufina</a> and <a href="/wiki/Sub-dean_of_the_Sacred_College_of_Cardinals" class="mw-redirect" title="Sub-dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals">sub-dean of the Sacred College of Cardinals</a>, and Tommasso Arezzo, Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Sabina" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Sabina">Bishop of Sabina</a>, acting as co-consecrators.
</p><p>The choice of Gregory XVI as his <a href="/wiki/Regnal_name" title="Regnal name">regnal name</a> was influenced by the fact that he had been <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> of the Monastery of San Gregorio on the Coelian Hill for more than twenty years, and in honour of <a href="/wiki/Gregory_XV" class="mw-redirect" title="Gregory XV">Gregory XV</a>, the founder of the Congregation for the Propaganda (Propagation of the Faith).<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The Monastery of S. Gregorio was the same abbey from which <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I" title="Pope Gregory I">Pope Gregory I</a> had dispatched missionaries to England in 596.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Actions">Actions</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Actions">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>The <a href="/wiki/July_Revolution" title="July Revolution">revolution of 1830</a>, which overthrew the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Bourbon" title="House of Bourbon">House of Bourbon</a>, had just inflicted a severe blow on the Catholic royalist party in France.  Almost the first act of the new French government was to seize <a href="/wiki/Ancona" title="Ancona">Ancona</a>, thus throwing <a href="/wiki/Italian_unification" class="mw-redirect" title="Italian unification">Italy</a>, and particularly the <a href="/wiki/Papal_States" title="Papal States">Papal States</a>, into a state of confusion and political upheaval.  In the course of the struggle that ensued, it was more than once necessary to call in <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy%E2%80%93Venetia" title="Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia">Austrian</a> troops to fight the red-shirted republicans engaged in a guerrilla campaign.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000276_12-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000276-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> The conservative administration of the Papal States postponed their promised reforms after a series of bombings and assassination attempts.  The replacement of <a href="/wiki/Tommaso_Bernetti" title="Tommaso Bernetti">Tommaso Bernetti</a> by <a href="/wiki/Luigi_Lambruschini" title="Luigi Lambruschini">Luigi Lambruschini</a> as <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_Secretary_of_State" title="Cardinal Secretary of State">Cardinal Secretary of State</a> in 1836 did nothing to appease the situation.
</p><p>In the northern territories the leaders of the revolt were middle-class gentry opposed to the general inefficiency of the government.<sup id="cite_ref-owen_10-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-owen-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Governance_of_the_papal_states">Governance of the papal states</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Governance of the papal states">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Gregory XVI and Cardinal Lambruschini opposed basic technological innovations such as gas lighting and railways,<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000276_12-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000276-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> believing that they would promote commerce and increase the power of the <a href="/wiki/Bourgeoisie" title="Bourgeoisie">bourgeoisie</a>, leading to demands for liberal reforms which would undermine the <a href="/wiki/Monarchy" title="Monarchy">monarchical</a> power of the Pope over central Italy. Gregory XVI in fact banned railways in the Papal States, calling them <i>chemins d'enfer</i> ("road to <a href="/wiki/Hell" title="Hell">hell</a>", a play on the French for railroad, <i>chemin de fer</i>, literally "iron road").<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEPham200420–21_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPham200420–21-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Insurrection" class="mw-redirect" title="Insurrection">insurrections</a> at Viterbo in 1836, in various parts of the Legations in 1840, at <a href="/wiki/Ravenna" title="Ravenna">Ravenna</a> in 1843 and at <a href="/wiki/Rimini" title="Rimini">Rimini</a> in 1845, were followed by wholesale executions and draconian sentences of hard labour and <a href="/wiki/Exile" title="Exile">exile</a>, but they did not bring the unrest within the Papal States under the control of the authorities.  Gregory XVI made great expenditures for defensive, architectural and engineering works, having a monument to Pope Leo XII built by Giuseppe Fabris in 1837.<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000276_12-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000276-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> He also lavished patronage on such scholars as <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Mai" title="Angelo Mai">Angelo Mai</a>, <a href="/wiki/Giuseppe_Mezzofanti" class="mw-redirect" title="Giuseppe Mezzofanti">Giuseppe Mezzofanti</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Gaetano_Moroni" title="Gaetano Moroni">Gaetano Moroni</a>.  This largesse, however, significantly weakened the finances of the Papal States.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Condemnation_of_the_slave_trade">Condemnation of the slave trade</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Condemnation of the slave trade">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:GREGORYXVI.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/GREGORYXVI.jpg/220px-GREGORYXVI.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="324" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/GREGORYXVI.jpg/330px-GREGORYXVI.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/GREGORYXVI.jpg/440px-GREGORYXVI.jpg 2x" data-file-width="794" data-file-height="1168" /></a><figcaption>Monument to Gregory XVI in Saint Peter's Basilica</figcaption></figure>
<p>In 1839, Gregory XVI issued an apostolic letter against the <a href="/wiki/Atlantic_slave_trade" title="Atlantic slave trade">Atlantic slave trade</a>, <i><a href="/wiki/In_supremo_apostolatus" title="In supremo apostolatus">In supremo apostolatus</a></i>, in which he wrote:<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000339_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000339-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r996844942">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 40px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>[W]e have judged that it belonged to Our pastoral solicitude to exert Ourselves to turn away the Faithful from the inhuman slave trade in Negroes and all other men. ... [D]esiring to remove such a shame from all the Christian nations, having fully reflected over the whole question and having taken the advice of many of Our Venerable Brothers the Cardinals of the Holy Roman Church, and walking in the footsteps of Our Predecessors, We warn and adjure earnestly in the Lord faithful Christians of every condition that no one in the future dare to vex anyone, despoil him of his possessions, reduce to servitude, or lend aid and favour to those who give themselves up to these practices, or exercise that inhuman traffic by which the Blacks, as if they were not men but rather animals, having been brought into servitude, in no matter what way, are, without any distinction, in contempt of the rights of justice and humanity, bought, sold, and devoted sometimes to the hardest labour. ...
We reprove, then, by virtue of Our Apostolic Authority, all the practices abovementioned as absolutely unworthy of the Christian name. By the same Authority We prohibit and strictly forbid any Ecclesiastic or lay person from presuming to defend as permissible this traffic in Blacks under no matter what pretext or excuse, or from publishing or teaching in any manner whatsoever, in public or privately, opinions contrary to what We have set forth in this Apostolic Letter.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Other_activities">Other activities</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Other activities">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Encyclicals">Encyclicals</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=10" title="Edit section: Encyclicals">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/List_of_encyclicals_of_Pope_Gregory_XVI" title="List of encyclicals of Pope Gregory XVI">List of encyclicals of Pope Gregory XVI</a></div>
<p>Other important encyclicals issued by Pope Gregory XVI were <i>Sollicitudo ecclesiarum</i>, which stated that in the event of a change of government, the church would negotiate with the new government for placement of bishops and vacant dioceses (issued 1831);<sup id="cite_ref-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000339_14-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcBrien2000339-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> <i><a href="/wiki/Mirari_Vos" class="mw-redirect" title="Mirari Vos">Mirari Vos</a></i>, on liberalism and religious indifferentism (issued on 15 August 1832); <i><a href="/wiki/Quo_graviora_(1833)" title="Quo graviora (1833)">Quo graviora</a></i>, on the Pragmatic Constitution in the <a href="/wiki/Rhineland" title="Rhineland">Rhineland</a> (issued on 4 October 1833); <i><a href="/wiki/Singulari_Nos" title="Singulari Nos">Singulari Nos</a></i>, on the ideas of <a href="/wiki/Hugues_F%C3%A9licit%C3%A9_Robert_de_Lamennais" class="mw-redirect" title="Hugues Félicité Robert de Lamennais">Hugues Félicité Robert de Lamennais</a> (issued on 25 June 1834), and <i>Commissum divinitus</i> (17 May 1835) on church and state.<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonizations_and_beatifications">Canonizations and beatifications</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=11" title="Edit section: Canonizations and beatifications">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<p>Gregory XVI canonized <a href="/wiki/Veronica_Giuliani" title="Veronica Giuliani">Veronica Giuliani</a>, an Italian mystic, during his papacy. During his reign, five saints were <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonized</a> (notably <a href="/wiki/Alphonsus_Liguori" title="Alphonsus Liguori">Alphonsus Liguori</a>) and thirty-three Servants of God were <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">declared Blessed</a> (including the Augustinian <a href="/wiki/Simon_of_Cascia" title="Simon of Cascia">Simon of Cascia</a>). In addition, many new <a href="/wiki/Religious_order_(Catholic)" title="Religious order (Catholic)">religious orders</a> were founded or supported and the devotion of the faithful to the <a href="/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_the_Catholic_Church" title="Veneration of Mary in the Catholic Church">Blessed Virgin Mary</a> increased, both in private and public life.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h4><span class="mw-headline" id="Consistories">Consistories</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=12" title="Edit section: Consistories">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h4>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Cardinals_created_by_Gregory_XVI" title="Cardinals created by Gregory XVI">Cardinals created by Gregory XVI</a></div>
<p>The pope created 75 cardinals in 24 consistories, in which the pope elevated 35 cardinals "<i><a href="/wiki/In_pectore" title="In pectore">in pectore</a></i>", including his future successor <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX" title="Pope Pius IX">Giovanni Maria Mastai-Ferretti</a>, who became Pope Pius IX. The pope also created six additional cardinals <i>in pectore</i>, though the pope died before these names could be revealed, therefore cancelling their appointments to the cardinalate.
</p><p>In 1836, the pope wanted to nominate Charles Joseph Benoît Mercy d'Argenteau to the College of Cardinals, but the archbishop refused the nomination because he did not wish to leave his family and home for a possible position in the Roman Curia. Gregory XVI nominated four <i>in pectore</i> cardinals on 21 April 1845 and one on 24 November 1845; Gregory XVI also named another <i>in pectore</i> cardinal in the 12 July 1841 consistory, never revealing his name. According to Philippe Boutry, Alerame Maria Pallavicini (the <a href="/wiki/Prefecture_of_the_Papal_Household" title="Prefecture of the Papal Household">Master of the Sacred Palace</a>) was the <i>in pectore</i> cardinal announced on 24 November 1845, however, Pope Pius IX refused to publish his name upon his ascension to the papacy less than a year later.<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pope_Gregory_XVI&amp;action=edit&amp;section=13" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>On 20 May 1846, he felt himself failing in health. A few days later, he was taken ill with facial <a href="/wiki/Erysipelas" title="Erysipelas">erysipelas</a>. At first, the attack was not thought to be very serious, but on 31 May, his strength suddenly failed, and it was seen that the end was near.<sup id="cite_ref-Toke_3-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Toke-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Gregory XVI died on 1 June 1846 at 9:15&#160;am at age 80. That morning, he received the <a href="/wiki/Extreme_Unction" class="mw-redirect" title="Extreme Unction">Extreme Unction</a> from the sub-sacristan Agostino Proja. After his funeral, he was buried in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Basilica" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Basilica">Saint Peter's Basilica</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
