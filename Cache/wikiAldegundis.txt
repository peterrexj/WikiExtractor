<p><b>Aldegund</b> (<abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 639–684), also <b>Aldegundis</b> or <b>Aldegonde</b>, was a <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> <a href="/wiki/Order_of_St._Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of St. Benedict">Benedictine</a> abbess who is honored as a saint by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a> in France and by the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Orthodox Church</a>.
</p><p>Aldegund was closely related to the <a href="/wiki/Merovingian" class="mw-redirect" title="Merovingian">Merovingian</a> royal family. Her parents, afterwards honored as St. <a href="/wiki/Waldebert" class="mw-redirect" title="Waldebert">Walbert</a>, Count of <a href="/wiki/Gu%C3%AEnes" title="Guînes">Guînes</a>, and St. Bertilla de Mareuil, lived in the <a href="/wiki/County_of_Hainaut" title="County of Hainaut">County of Hainaut</a>. She is the most famous of what Aline Hornaday calls the "Maubeuge Cycle" of <a href="/wiki/Category:Merovingian_saints" title="Category:Merovingian saints">Merovingian saints</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Aldegund was urged to marry, but she chose the life of the cloister.  Having allegedly walked across the waters of the <a href="/wiki/Sambre" title="Sambre">Sambre</a>, she had built on its banks a small hospital at <a href="/wiki/Malbode" class="mw-redirect" title="Malbode">Malbode</a>, which later became, under the name <a href="/wiki/Maubeuge_Abbey" title="Maubeuge Abbey">Maubeuge Abbey</a>, a famous abbey of Benedictine nuns, though at a later date these were replaced by canonesses. <sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>She bore with fortitude the <a href="/wiki/Breast_cancer" title="Breast cancer">breast cancer</a> that eventually killed her.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Saint Aldegund's Catholic liturgical feast is kept on January 30.
</p><p>She has been supposed to be the sister of Saint <a href="/wiki/Waltrude" title="Waltrude">Waltrude</a> (Waudru).<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
She was succeeded by her nieces, Waltrude's daughters, <a href="/wiki/Aldetrude" title="Aldetrude">Aldetrude</a> and then <a href="/wiki/Madelberte_of_Maubeuge" title="Madelberte of Maubeuge">Madelberte</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>There are several early <i><a href="/wiki/Biography" title="Biography">Lives</a></i>, but none by contemporaries. Several of these, including the tenth-century biography by Hucbald, are printed by the <a href="/wiki/Bollandists" class="mw-redirect" title="Bollandists">Bollandists</a> (Acta SS., January 11, 1034–35).
</p>
