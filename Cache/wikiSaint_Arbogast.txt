<p><b>Saint Arbogast of Strassburg</b>; <a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Arbogast von Straßburg</i>; <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Arbogast de Strasbourg</i>; <a href="/wiki/Latin_language" class="mw-redirect" title="Latin language">Latin</a>: <i lang="la">Arbogastus</i>; <span title="circa">c.</span> 600s – 700 AD)  was a 7th-century missionary in the <a href="/wiki/Frankish_Empire" class="mw-redirect" title="Frankish Empire">Frankish Empire</a> and an early <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Strasbourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Strasbourg">Bishop of Strasbourg</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Biography">Biography</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Arbogast&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Biography">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Only little historical facts of his life can be stated with certainty, other than that he came to Francia, was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Strasbourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Strasbourg">Bishop of Strasbourg</a> and was venerated from the early medieval period as the saint who brought Christianity to the <a href="/wiki/Alsace" title="Alsace">Alsace</a>. Because of this, the given name <a href="/wiki/Arbogast_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Arbogast (disambiguation)">Arbogast</a> became especially popular in the region. His origin is variously given as <a href="/wiki/Hiberno-Scottish_mission" title="Hiberno-Scottish mission">Scotland or Ireland</a>, or <a href="/wiki/Aquitania" class="mw-redirect" title="Aquitania">Aquitania</a>.
</p><p>According to the <i>vita</i>, a 10th-century hagiographical account of his life, Arbogast found a warm friend in the Merovingian King <a href="/wiki/Dagobert_II_of_Austrasia" class="mw-redirect" title="Dagobert II of Austrasia">Dagobert II of Austrasia</a>, who reigned Austrasia 673-679. On Dagobert's accession, Arbogast was appointed <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Strasbourg" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Strasbourg">Bishop of Strasbourg</a>, and was famed for sanctity and miracles.
</p><p>Still according to the <i>vita</i>, Arbogast brought back to life Dagobert's son, Siegebert, who had been killed by a fall from his horse. Siegebert had been boar hunting with his father’s huntsmen in forests along the Ill River near Ebersheim, and became separated from the others. He encountered a boar, and his startled horse reared, throwing him and trampling him while his foot was caught in his stirrup. His companions found him and took him home, where he died the next day. King Dagobert summoned Arbogast, and the holy man prayed to Saint Mary, mother of Jesus: as she had carried the life of the entire world, would she not intercede for the life of this one boy? Siegebert stood up in his burial shroud.  When the king offered bishop Arbogast money in reward, he declined, suggesting instead that land be donated to build a cathedral at Strasbourg.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>According to the <i>vita</i>, he died in 678 and was buried outside of the city. He was buried either in the old Roman necropolis or on the side of Hangman's Hill, where a gallows was located and only malefactors were interred. The site of his burial was subsequently deemed suitable for a church, and a chapel to was built in honor of St Michael. Arbogast is commemorated on 21 July.
</p><p>Arbogast appears on the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">coat of arms</a> of <a href="/wiki/Batzendorf" title="Batzendorf">Batzendorf</a>.
</p>
