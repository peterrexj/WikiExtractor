<p><b>Amphilochius of Pochayiv</b> (<a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_language" title="Ukrainian language">Ukrainian</a>: <span lang="uk">Амфілохій Почаївський</span>) was a 20th-century <a href="/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Ukrainian Orthodox">Ukrainian Orthodox</a> <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>, from <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast" title="Ternopil Oblast">Ternopil Oblast</a> of western <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amphilochius_of_Pochayiv&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Amphilochius of <a href="/wiki/Pochaiv" title="Pochaiv">Pochaiv</a> was born <b>Yakov Varnavovich Golovatyuk</b> on 27 November 1894<sup id="cite_ref-lavra_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lavra-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> in the village of Mala Ilovytsia (Mala Ilowica in Polish), in <a href="/wiki/Kremenets_Raion" title="Kremenets Raion">Kremenets Raion</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast" title="Ternopil Oblast">Ternopil Oblast</a> in western <a href="/wiki/Ukraine" title="Ukraine">Ukraine</a>, at that time part of <a href="/wiki/Austria-Hungary" title="Austria-Hungary">Austria-Hungary</a>. The village of Mala Ilovytsia is located in the Ilovetska valley, which is 7&#160;km (4&#160;mi) wide, and is surrounded by the <a href="/wiki/Kremenets" title="Kremenets">Kremenets</a> mountains. It is the northernmost village of <a href="/wiki/Ternopil_Oblast" title="Ternopil Oblast">Ternopil Oblast</a>, off the main roads and surrounded by forested areas.
</p><p>Yakov was one of 10 children. His father, Varnava, was an able craftsman, making shoe lasts and sleighs, and had a reputation as a skilled bone setter. Yakov often assisted his father in caring for people who came for help.<sup id="cite_ref-lavra_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lavra-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In 1912 he was drafted into the army of the Tsar. During the First World War he served in Lutsk and then in Tomsk, where he was a medical attendant at the hospital. Later he was posted to the front as a field medic. He was captured as a prisoner of war by the Austrians,<sup id="cite_ref-lavra_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-lavra-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and sent to the Alps, where for three years he worked for a farmer. Eventually he managed to flee.<sup id="cite_ref-baptist_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-baptist-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Joining_Pochaiv_Lavra">Joining Pochaiv Lavra</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Amphilochius_of_Pochayiv&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Joining Pochaiv Lavra">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Having returned home, he intended to marry but his experiences during the war, drew him to religious life and the <a href="/w/index.php?title=Pochaiv_Lavar&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Pochaiv Lavar (page does not exist)">Pochaiv monastery</a>. Yakiv went to the Uspens’ka (<a href="/wiki/Dormition" class="mw-redirect" title="Dormition">Dormition</a>) Lavra in Pochaiv in 1925.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He constructed sleighs and wheels, and sang in the choir, always in the spirit of humility and obedience.<sup id="cite_ref-tacoma_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tacoma-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> On 8 July 1932 he was <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> through <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure"><i>postryh</i></a> (clipping of hair ceremony) and took the name Joseph (Yosyp in Ukrainian). On 27 September 1936, he was ordained a hieromonk.<sup id="cite_ref-Kutash_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kutash-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Amphilochius spent much time praying in front of the miraculous <a href="/wiki/Icons" class="mw-redirect" title="Icons">icons</a> of Pochaiv. He was appointed the caretaker of the God's Mother's Foot. <a href="/wiki/Mother_of_God" class="mw-redirect" title="Mother of God">God's Mother's</a> Foot is a rock footprint left after <a href="/wiki/Marian_apparition" title="Marian apparition">apparition</a> of <a href="/wiki/Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin Mary">Virgin Mary</a> in Pochaiv around 1240.
</p><p>His earlier work with his father and experience as a medic during the war gave him some skill in healing, and many people came to the Lavra seeking his help. The <a href="/wiki/Archimandrite" title="Archimandrite">Archimandrite</a> Abbot of the monastery of the <a href="/wiki/Lavra" title="Lavra">lavra</a> blessed Hieromonk Joseph in this work and allowed him to settle in a small house near the cemetery of the monastery, so as not to disturb the other monks. There the venerable Amphilochius spent 20 years ministering to the sick.<sup id="cite_ref-Kutash_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kutash-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> He was credited with the gift of healing.<sup id="cite_ref-tacoma_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tacoma-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Soviet regime which seized power after the war wished to stem the constant stream of people seeking healing from the pious monk. Fr. Joseph resisted their attempts to close the Lavra, and was subsequently incarcerated in a psychiatric hospital where he was injected with drugs.<sup id="cite_ref-Kutash_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Kutash-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Eventually, he was released through he good offices of <a href="/wiki/Svetlana_Alliluyeva" title="Svetlana Alliluyeva">Svetlana Alliluyeva</a>, whom he had previously met.<sup id="cite_ref-baptist_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-baptist-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Upon his release the authorities forbade him to return to the Lavra and so he settled in his native Ilovytsia with his nephew, where for many years he continued to serve the people, frequently conducting a <a href="/wiki/Paraklesis#Moleben" title="Paraklesis">Moleben</a> with the Blessing of the Waters in his yard. It was believed that God gifted him with the grace of prophecy and the performing of miracles.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>One day, at the instigation of the local authorities, he was severely beaten and left in an icy swamp. When found, he was taken to the lavra, where, as they thought that he was dying, he was tonsured into the Great Schema and given the name Amphilochius, in honor of Holy Hierarch Amphilochius of Iconium. Amphilochius slowly recovered. As he did not have a residence permit to stay at the Lavra, he returned to the village and resumed his ministry of healing.<sup id="cite_ref-tacoma_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-tacoma-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He died on 1 January 1971 at the age of seventy-six. On 23 April 2002, Pochaev Elder Schema-abbot Amphilochius was glorified as a saint.<sup id="cite_ref-baptist_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-baptist-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
