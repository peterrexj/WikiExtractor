<p>Saint <b>Tryphon  of Campsada</b> (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span lang="el">Τρύφων</span>; also spelled <i>Trypho</i>, <i>Trifon</i>, <i>Triphon</i>) was a 3rd-century Christian <a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">saint</a>. He is venerated by the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic">Roman Catholic</a> and <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> churches as a <a href="/wiki/Great_martyr" title="Great martyr">great martyr</a> and <a href="/wiki/Holy_Unmercenaries" title="Holy Unmercenaries">holy unmercenary</a>.
</p><p>Saint Tryphon was formerly celebrated jointly with Saints <b>Respicius and Nympha</b> on 10 November in the <a href="/wiki/Liturgical_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Liturgical calendar">liturgical calendar</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a> from the eleventh century until the twentieth,<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> and remains on the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar_of_1962" class="mw-redirect" title="General Roman Calendar of 1962">liturgical calendar</a> of the extraordinary form of the Roman rite. Saint Tryphon continues to be celebrated (separately) on 1 February&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 14 February&#93; on both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church_liturgical_calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox Church liturgical calendar">Orthodox liturgical calendar</a> and the Roman <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Calendar of Saints">Calendar of Saints</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tryphon,_Respicius,_and_Nympha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint Tryphon is said to have been born at Kampsada in <a href="/wiki/Phrygia" title="Phrygia">Phrygia</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Turkey" title="Turkey">Turkey</a>),<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> and as a boy took care of <a href="/wiki/Geese" class="mw-redirect" title="Geese">geese</a>. His name is derived from the <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Greek" title="Ancient Greek">Greek</a> τρυφή (<a href="/wiki/Tryph%C3%A9" title="Tryphé"><i>tryphe</i></a>) meaning "softness, delicacy". He acquired fame as a healer, especially of animals, and is considered one of the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Unmercenaries" title="Holy Unmercenaries">Holy Unmercenaries</a>, particularly invoked on farms.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Decius" title="Decius">Decian</a> <a href="/wiki/Decian_persecution" title="Decian persecution">persecution</a> he was taken to <a href="/wiki/Nicaea" title="Nicaea">Nicaea</a> about the year 250 and was <a href="/wiki/Torture" title="Torture">tortured</a> in a horrible manner. He was <a href="/wiki/Decapitation" title="Decapitation">beheaded</a> with a sword after he had converted the <a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/heathen" class="extiw" title="wikt:heathen">heathen</a> <a href="/wiki/Prefect" title="Prefect">prefect</a> Licius. Fabulous stories are interwoven with his <a href="/wiki/Hagiography" title="Hagiography">hagiography</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tryphon,_Respicius,_and_Nympha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is on 1 February&#160;&#91;<a href="/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates" title="Old Style and New Style dates">O.S.</a> 14 February&#93; in both the <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><sup id="cite_ref-GS_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GS-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> and (now) in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic Church</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He is greatly <a href="/wiki/Veneration" title="Veneration">venerated</a> in the Eastern Orthodox Church, in which he is also the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Gardeners" class="mw-redirect" title="Gardeners">gardeners</a> and <a href="/wiki/Winemaking" title="Winemaking">winegrowers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In Serbia, North Macedonia and Bulgaria, St Tryphon is celebrated by vine growers.<sup id="cite_ref-guide_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-guide-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> The celebrations are a <a href="/wiki/Fertility_rite" title="Fertility rite">fertility rite</a> intended to encourage the growth of the vines, and it is also thought that human infertility can be cured on this day.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Russia, Tryphon is venerated as a patron of birds.<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> In <a href="/wiki/Russian_icons" title="Russian icons">Russian icons</a> of the saint, he is often shown holding a <a href="/wiki/Falcon" title="Falcon">falcon</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> a reference to a <a href="/wiki/Miracle" title="Miracle">miracle</a> attributed to his <a href="/wiki/Intercessory_prayer" class="mw-redirect" title="Intercessory prayer">intercessions</a>. Prayers attributed to him are used against infestations of <a href="/wiki/Rodents" class="mw-redirect" title="Rodents">rodents</a> and <a href="/wiki/Locusts" class="mw-redirect" title="Locusts">locusts</a>; one such prayer appears in the <i><a href="/wiki/Euchologion" title="Euchologion">Great Euchologion</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> Saint Trifon is often depicted with a <a href="/wiki/Merlin_(bird)" title="Merlin (bird)">merlin</a> on hand as he was traditionally venerated by Moscow <a href="/wiki/Hunter" class="mw-redirect" title="Hunter">hunters</a>.
</p><p>Many churches were dedicated to him, and the <a href="/wiki/Byzantine_Empire" title="Byzantine Empire">Eastern Emperor</a>, <a href="/wiki/Leo_VI_the_Wise" title="Leo VI the Wise">Leo VI the Wise</a> (d. 912), delivered a eulogy in Tryphon's honour. He is the protector saint of the town of <a href="/wiki/Kotor" title="Kotor">Kotor</a> in Montenegro, and one of the patron saints of Moscow.<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Tryphon" class="mw-redirect" title="Cathedral of Saint Tryphon">Cathedral of Saint Tryphon</a> in <a href="/wiki/Kotor" title="Kotor">Kotor</a> is dedicated to him.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tryphon,_Respicius,_and_Nympha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>His <a href="/wiki/Relic" title="Relic">relics</a> were first buried in his native Kampsada. Later, they were <a href="/wiki/Translation_(relics)" class="mw-redirect" title="Translation (relics)">translated</a> to <a href="/wiki/Constantinople" title="Constantinople">Constantinople</a>, and then to Rome.<sup id="cite_ref-OCA_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-OCA-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-GS_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-GS-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> His head is kept in the <a href="/wiki/Kotor_Cathedral" title="Kotor Cathedral">Kotor Cathedral</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Respicius_and_Nympha">Respicius and Nympha</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Tryphon,_Respicius,_and_Nympha&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Respicius and Nympha">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In the <a href="/wiki/Latin_Church" title="Latin Church">Latin Church</a>, 10 November was formerly a feast day associated with Saint Tryphon and two other saints, of whom little is known: Respicius and Nympha.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> While Saint Trypho is still listed in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Martyrology" title="Roman Martyrology">Roman Martyrology</a>, Respicius and Nympha have been omitted.
</p><p>In about 1005, the <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> <a href="/w/index.php?title=Theodoric_of_Fleury&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Theodoric of Fleury (page does not exist)">Theodoric of Fleury</a> wrote, on the basis of earlier written legends, an account of Tryphon in which Respicius appears as Tryphon's companion.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (July 2015)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> The <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of both were preserved, together with those of a holy <a href="/wiki/Virgin" class="mw-redirect" title="Virgin">virgin</a> named Nympha, at the <a href="/wiki/Santo_Spirito_in_Sassia" title="Santo Spirito in Sassia">Hospital of the Holy Ghost in Sassia</a>. The church of this hospital was a cardinal's title, which, together with the <a href="/wiki/Relics" class="mw-redirect" title="Relics">relics</a> of these saints, was transferred by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_V" title="Pope Pius V">Pope Pius V</a> to the Church of St. Augustine in 1566.
</p><p>One tradition held that Nympha (Ninfa)<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (August 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> was a virgin martyr from <a href="/wiki/Palermo" title="Palermo">Palermo</a> who was put to death for the faith at the beginning of the fourth century. According to other versions of the legend, when the <a href="/wiki/Vandals" title="Vandals">Vandals</a> invaded <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a>, she fled from Palermo to the Italian mainland and died in the sixth century at <a href="/wiki/Savona" title="Savona">Savona</a>. The feast of her translation is observed at Palermo on 19 August.<sup id="cite_ref-CE_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CE-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Some believe that there were two saints of this name.  Before 1624 Palermo had four patron saints, one for each of the four major parts of the city. They were <a href="/wiki/Agatha_of_Sicily" title="Agatha of Sicily">Saint Agatha</a>, <a href="/wiki/Christina_of_Bolsena" title="Christina of Bolsena">Saint Christina</a>, Saint Nympha, and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Olivia" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Olivia">Saint Olivia</a>.  Their images are displayed at the <a href="/wiki/Quattro_Canti" title="Quattro Canti">Quattro Canti</a>, in the centre of Palermo.
</p>
