<p><b>Titus</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/aɪ/: &#39;i&#39; in &#39;tide&#39;">aɪ</span><span title="&#39;t&#39; in &#39;tie&#39;">t</span><span title="/ə/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;about&#39;">ə</span><span title="&#39;s&#39; in &#39;sigh&#39;">s</span></span>/</a></span></span> <a href="/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key" title="Help:Pronunciation respelling key"><i title="English pronunciation respelling"><span style="font-size:90%">TY</span>-təs</i></a>; <a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Τίτος</span></span>; <i>Títos</i>) was an early Christian missionary and church leader, a companion and disciple of <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a>, mentioned in several of the <a href="/wiki/Pauline_epistles" title="Pauline epistles">Pauline epistles</a> including the <a href="/wiki/Epistle_to_Titus" title="Epistle to Titus">Epistle to Titus</a>. He is believed to be a Gentile converted to Christianity by Paul and, according to tradition, he was consecrated as Bishop of the Island of <a href="/wiki/Crete" title="Crete">Crete</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Smith,_William_1975._Pp._701_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith,_William_1975._Pp._701-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Titus brought a fundraising letter from Paul to <a href="/wiki/Corinth" title="Corinth">Corinth</a>, to collect for the poor in Jerusalem. According to <a href="/wiki/Jerome" title="Jerome">Jerome</a>, Titus was the <a href="/wiki/Amanuensis" title="Amanuensis">amanuensis</a> of this epistle (<a href="/wiki/Second_Epistle_to_the_Corinthians" title="Second Epistle to the Corinthians">2 Corinthians</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Later, on Crete, Titus appointed <a href="/wiki/Presbyter" title="Presbyter">presbyters</a> (elders) in every city and remained there into his old age, dying in <a href="/wiki/Gortyna" title="Gortyna">Gortyna</a>, near the city of Candia (modern <a href="/wiki/Heraklion" title="Heraklion">Heraklion</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-Smith,_William_1975._Pp._701_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Smith,_William_1975._Pp._701-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Titus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Titus was a Greek, apparently from Crete who is said to have studied Greek philosophy and poetry in his early years.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He seems to have been converted by Paul, whereupon he served as Paul's secretary and interpreter. In the year 49, Titus accompanied Paul to the council held at Jerusalem, on the subject of the <a href="/wiki/Moses" title="Moses">Mosaic</a> rites.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Towards the close of the year 56, Paul, as he himself departed from Asia, sent Titus from <a href="/wiki/Ephesus" title="Ephesus">Ephesus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Ancient_Corinth" title="Ancient Corinth">Corinth</a>, with full commission to remedy the fallout precipitated by Timothy's delivery of 1 Corinthians<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> and Paul's "Painful Visit",<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> particularly a significant personal offense and challenge to Paul's authority by one unnamed individual.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> During this journey, Titus served as the courier for what is commonly known as the "Severe Letter", a Pauline missive that has been <a href="/wiki/Lost_works" class="mw-redirect" title="Lost works">lost</a> but is referred to in 2 Corinthians.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>After success on this mission, Titus journeyed north and met Paul in <a href="/wiki/Macedonia_(Roman_province)" title="Macedonia (Roman province)">Macedonia</a>. There the apostle, overjoyed by Titus' success,<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> wrote 2 Corinthians. Titus then returned to Corinth with a larger entourage, carrying 2 Corinthians with him. Paul joined Titus in Corinth later. From Corinth, Paul then sent Titus to organize the collections of alms for the Christians at Jerusalem. Titus was therefore a troubleshooter, peacemaker, administrator, and missionary.
</p><p>Early church tradition holds that Paul, after his release from his first imprisonment in Rome, stopped at the island of Crete to preach. Due to the needs of other churches, requiring his presence elsewhere, he ordained his disciple Titus as bishop of that island,<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and left him to finish the work he had started. Chrysostom says that this is an indication of the esteem Paul held for Titus.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Paul summoned Titus from Crete to join him at Nicopolis in Epirus.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> Later, Titus traveled to <a href="/wiki/Early_centers_of_Christianity#Salona" class="mw-redirect" title="Early centers of Christianity">Dalmatia</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-losservatore_13-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-losservatore-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/New_Testament" title="New Testament">New Testament</a> does not record his death.
</p><p>It has been argued that the name "Titus" in 2 Corinthians and Galatians is nothing more than an informal name used by Timothy, implied already by the fact that even though both are said to be long-term close companions of Paul, they never appear in common scenes.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> The theory proposes that a number of passages—1 Cor. 4:17, 16.10; 2 Cor. 2:13, 7:6, 13–14, 12:18; and Acts 19.22—all refer to the same journey of a single individual, Titus-Timothy. <a href="/wiki/2_Timothy" class="mw-redirect" title="2 Timothy">2 Timothy</a> seems to dispute this, by claiming that Titus has gone to Dalmatia.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup> The fact that Paul made a point of circumcising Timothy<sup id="cite_ref-16" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> but refused to circumcise Titus<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup> would indicate that they are different men, although certain manuscripts of Galatians<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> have been taken (by <a href="/wiki/Marius_Victorinus" class="mw-redirect" title="Marius Victorinus">Marius Victorinus</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup> for example) to indicate that Paul <i>did</i> circumcise Titus.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Titus&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The feast day of Titus was not included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>. When added in 1854, it was assigned to 6 February.<sup id="cite_ref-20" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-20">&#91;20&#93;</a></sup> In 1969, the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a> assigned the feast to <a href="/wiki/January_26" title="January 26">26 January</a> so as to celebrate the two disciples of Paul, Titus and Timothy, the day after the feast of the <a href="/wiki/Conversion_of_St._Paul" class="mw-redirect" title="Conversion of St. Paul">Conversion of St. Paul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-21" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-21">&#91;21&#93;</a></sup> The <a href="/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_America" title="Evangelical Lutheran Church in America">Evangelical Lutheran Church in America</a> celebrates these two, together with <a href="/wiki/Silas" title="Silas">Silas</a>, on the <a href="/wiki/January_26" title="January 26">same date</a> while he is honored on the calendars of the <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Church_of_England)" title="Calendar of saints (Church of England)">Church of England</a> and <a href="/wiki/Calendar_of_saints_(Episcopal_Church)" title="Calendar of saints (Episcopal Church)">Episcopal Church</a> (with <a href="/wiki/Saint_Timothy" title="Saint Timothy">Timothy</a>) on <a href="/wiki/January_26" title="January 26">26 January</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-22" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-22">&#91;22&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-23" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-23">&#91;23&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a> commemorates Titus on 25 August and on <a href="/wiki/Seventy_disciples" title="Seventy disciples">4 January</a>. His relics, now consisting of only his skull, are venerated in the Church of St. Titus, <a href="/wiki/Heraklion" title="Heraklion">Heraklion</a>, Crete, to which it was returned in 1966<sup id="cite_ref-The_Orthodox_Messenger,_v._8(7/8),_July/Aug_1997_24-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-The_Orthodox_Messenger,_v._8(7/8),_July/Aug_1997-24">&#91;24&#93;</a></sup> after being removed to Venice during the Turkish occupation.
</p><p>Titus is the patron saint of the <a href="/wiki/United_States_Army_Chaplain_Corps" class="mw-redirect" title="United States Army Chaplain Corps">United States Army Chaplain Corps</a>. The Corps has established the Order of Titus Award, described by the Department of Defense:
</p>

The Order of Titus is awarded for meritorious contributions to the unique and highly visible Unit Ministry Team Observer Controller Program. The award recognizes the great importance of realistic, doctrinally guided combat ministry training in ensuring the delivery of prevailing religious support to the American Soldier.<sup id="cite_ref-25" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-25">&#91;25&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
