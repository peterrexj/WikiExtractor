<p><b>Hildegard Lea Burjan</b> (née <i>Hildegard Freund</i>; 30 January 1883 – 11 June 1933) was a <a href="/wiki/Germans" title="Germans">German</a> <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Roman Catholic</a> convert from <a href="/wiki/Judaism" title="Judaism">Judaism</a> and the founder of the Sisterhood of Caritas Socialis.<sup id="cite_ref-SC_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Burjan set up several organizations for the promotion of women's rights and for the rights of all workers and their families and this even saw her elected to the <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Parliament of Austria">Austrian Parliament</a> where she served until her retirement due to ill health.<sup id="cite_ref-HB_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The beatification process commenced under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in 1982, and <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> named her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> in 2007; that same pope beatified Burjan in 2012 though Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> presided over the celebration on the pope's behalf.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hildegard_Burjan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Hildegard Freund was born to non-practicing <a href="/wiki/Jewish" class="mw-redirect" title="Jewish">Jewish</a> parents in the <a href="/wiki/German_Empire" title="German Empire">German Empire</a> in 1883 as the second-born to Abraham and Berta Freund.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>The Freunds relocated to <a href="/wiki/Berlin" title="Berlin">Berlin</a> in 1895, where she was sent to high school and the Freunds relocated once more in 1899 to <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a> where she studied in <a href="/wiki/Zurich" class="mw-redirect" title="Zurich">Zurich</a> at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Zurich" title="University of Zurich">college</a> there. Freund graduated from high school in 1903 after the completion of her examinations and then began her college studies.<sup id="cite_ref-HB_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Freund studied German literature as well as philosophical and sociological studies under the two <a href="/wiki/Protestant" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant">Protestant</a> professors Saitschik and Foerster in <a href="/wiki/Switzerland" title="Switzerland">Switzerland</a>, and then in Berlin, when she returned in 1907 to also start an economics course; she obtained a <a href="/wiki/Ph.D." class="mw-redirect" title="Ph.D.">doctorate</a> in 1908.<sup id="cite_ref-SC_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 2 May 1907 she married the <a href="/wiki/Hungary" title="Hungary">Hungarian</a> entrepreneur Alexander Burjan (26 November 1882 - 6 November 1973).<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In October 1908 she suffered from severe renal colic that forced her hospitalization on 9 October at the Saint Hedwig Catholic Hospital in Berlin where she underwent several operations.<sup id="cite_ref-SC_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-HB_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Just before <a href="/wiki/Easter" title="Easter">Easter</a> 1909, the condition got to the point where doctors gave up all hope but she was healed from this grave illness after Easter on 12 April 1909. It was during her hospitalization that she came to admire the work of the <a href="/wiki/Sisters_of_Mercy_of_St._Borromeo" title="Sisters of Mercy of St. Borromeo">Sisters of Saint Charles Borromeo</a>. This illness prompted her <a href="/wiki/Religious_conversion" title="Religious conversion">conversion</a> to the Roman Catholic Church; she was <a href="/wiki/Baptized" class="mw-redirect" title="Baptized">baptized</a> on 11 August 1909 and her husband followed in August 1910. Burjan moved with her husband to the Austrian capital of <a href="/wiki/Vienna" title="Vienna">Vienna</a>, where she bore her sole daughter Lisa on 27 August 1910 despite possible risk, as past pregnancies had threatened her life. The doctors advised her to have an abortion, but she refused and exclaimed: "that would be murder!" Burjan soon started to take interest in the social issues of the nation and took a particular interest in the working conditions and spiritual welfare of the poor.
</p><p>In September 1912 she gave an address at the annual gathering of women's leagues, and in 1912 founded the "Christian Women Working at Home" and in 1918 the group dubbed "Social Help". The former group offered social protection and better wages as well as legal assistance. Her social activism was also due to inspiration she received from the late <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII's</a> 1881 <a href="/wiki/Rerum_Novarum" class="mw-redirect" title="Rerum Novarum">Rerum Novarum</a> on social teachings and conditions.<sup id="cite_ref-SC_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 16 April 1914 she gave another talk at another such annual gathering and discussed the topic of women's rights and the importance of women in the workplace.<sup id="cite_ref-HB_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> During <a href="/wiki/World_War_I" title="World War I">World War I</a> she defended the idea of women replacing men in factories and hailed the trend even after the war. The prelate <a href="/wiki/Ignaz_Seipel" title="Ignaz Seipel">Ignaz Seipel</a> said that he had never met a more enthusiastic or wise politician as Burjan while Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Friedrich_Gustav_Piffl" title="Friedrich Gustav Piffl">Friedrich Gustav Piffl</a> dubbed her as "the conscience of the Parliament". On 3 December 1918 she earned a seat on the district council and became the vice-chair to the chairman of the Christian Socials Leopold Kunschak.
</p><p>Her main achievement remains the founding of a religious congregation for serving the poor, and on 4 October 1919 founded the congregation titled the "Sisterhood of Caritas Socialis". The first ten women joined that October at a special <a href="/wiki/Mass_(liturgy)" title="Mass (liturgy)">mass</a>. In 1918 she became active in the <a href="/wiki/Christian_Social_Party_(Austria)" title="Christian Social Party (Austria)">Christian-Socials</a>, and in 1919 became one of the first female members of the <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Austria" class="mw-redirect" title="Parliament of Austria">Austrian Parliament</a>; she spoke in the Parliament for the first time on 12 March 1919 and at one point filed a petition for the extension of legal rights of expectant and nursing mothers.<sup id="cite_ref-HB_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HB-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Burjan concerned herself with issues such as equal wages for men and women and social protection for the working class as well as social and spiritual care for poor families. Burjan was invited to run in the 1920 elections and was proposed as the Minister for Social Affairs but declined due to poor health; she informed the executive committee for the Christian Socials that she would not re-contest but retire instead.
</p><p>On <a href="/wiki/Pentecost" title="Pentecost">Pentecost</a> in 1933, she suffered a severe and painful renal inflammation. Burjan died on 11 June 1933. The ailing woman murmured on her deathbed: "how beautiful it will be to go to rest in <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a>!" and then kissed a <a href="/wiki/Crucifix" title="Crucifix">Crucifix</a> and spoke her final words: "Dear <a href="/wiki/Jesus_Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Jesus Christ">Savior</a> - make all men lovable so that You might love them. Enrich them with Yourself alone!"<sup id="cite_ref-SC_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SC-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The order that she founded received diocesan approval in 1936 and then papal approval from <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII" title="Pope John XXIII">Pope John XXIII</a> in 1960. Her remains were later moved in 2005.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Hildegard_Burjan&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The official start to the cause came on 7 January 1982 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a>, after the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> issued the official "<a href="/wiki/Nihil_obstat" title="Nihil obstat">nihil obstat</a>" to it. The cognitional process was also held from 1982 until 1983, and the C.C.S. later validated both causes on 24 May 1985 before receiving the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> dossier over a decade later in 1999.
</p><p>The board of theologians voiced their assent to the cause on 24 March 2006 as did the C.C.S. on 6 March 2007. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI" title="Pope Benedict XVI">Pope Benedict XVI</a> confirmed that Burjan had lived a life of <a href="/wiki/Heroic_virtue" title="Heroic virtue">heroic virtue</a> and named her as <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 6 July 2007. The process for a miracle needed for beatification spanned from 30 January 2001 to 12 March 2001 in the place it occurred in, and it received C.C.S. validation on 22 February 2002. The medical board met on 15 March 2007 but did not come up with a conclusive verdict so met once again on 10 December 2009 and approved it; theologians followed on 25 January 2008 as did the C.C.S. on 7 June 2011. The pope approved this miracle on 27 June 2011 and delegated Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Angelo_Amato" title="Angelo Amato">Angelo Amato</a> to preside over the beatification in Vienna in his name on 29 January 2012.
</p><p>The current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> for this cause is Dr. Andrea Ambrosi.
</p><p>On 11 June 2015 a memorial was revealed and blessed at a special Mass held at Saint Stephen's Cathedral. Suffragan bishop (Weihbischof) Helmut Krätzl and arch bishop (Erzbischof)  Peter Zurbriggen were present for the blessing.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
