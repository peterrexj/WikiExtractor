<p><b>Raymond of Capua</b>, (ca. 1303 – 5 October 1399) was a leading member of the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a> and served as its <a href="/wiki/Master_of_the_Order_of_Preachers" title="Master of the Order of Preachers">Master General</a> from 1380 until his death. First as <a href="/wiki/Provincial_superior" title="Provincial superior">Prior Provincial</a> of <a href="/wiki/Lombardy" title="Lombardy">Lombardy</a> and then as Master General of the Order, Raymond undertook the restoration of Dominican religious life. For his success in this endeavor, he is referred to as its "second founder".<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Raymond worked also for the return of the <a href="/wiki/Papacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Papacy">papacy</a> to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> and for a solution to the <a href="/wiki/Western_schism" class="mw-redirect" title="Western schism">Western schism</a>. The important <a href="/wiki/Mysticism" title="Mysticism">mystic</a> and author, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>, accepted him as a <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_director" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual director">spiritual director</a> because of his burning passion for the Church and for the revival of religious life. He was <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> by the Catholic Church in 1899.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Capua&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He was born "Raymond della Vigna" about 1330 in <a href="/wiki/Capua" title="Capua">Capua</a> (then part of the <a href="/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples" title="Kingdom of Naples">Kingdom of Naples</a>), a member of a prominent family of that city, and was a descendant of <a href="/wiki/Pietro_della_Vigna" title="Pietro della Vigna">Pietro della Vigna</a> (a figure mentioned in <a href="/wiki/Dante" class="mw-redirect" title="Dante">Dante</a>'s <i><a href="/wiki/Divine_Comedy" title="Divine Comedy">Divine Comedy</a></i>). In 1350, while a student of law at the <a href="/wiki/University_of_Bologna" title="University of Bologna">University of Bologna</a>, he entered the <a href="/wiki/Dominican_Order" title="Dominican Order">Dominican Order</a>. For the next twenty-five years he worked as a spiritual director or as a teacher in various communities of the Order.
</p><p>Raymond was first assigned to <a href="/wiki/Montepulciano" title="Montepulciano">Montepulciano</a>, where he served as a <a href="/wiki/Chaplain" title="Chaplain">chaplain</a> to a monastery of <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a> of the Dominican <a href="/wiki/Second_Order_(religious)" class="mw-redirect" title="Second Order (religious)">Second Order</a>. He was the first biographer of their venerated former <a href="/wiki/Prioress" class="mw-redirect" title="Prioress">prioress</a>, <a href="/wiki/Agnes_of_Montepulciano" title="Agnes of Montepulciano">Agnes of Montepulciano</a>, who had died about fifty years earlier. He was then stationed in Rome, to serve as the <a href="/wiki/Prior" title="Prior">prior</a> of the friars at <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_sopra_Minerva" title="Santa Maria sopra Minerva">Santa Maria sopra Minerva</a>. Later he was sent to <a href="/wiki/Siena" title="Siena">Siena</a>, where he was assigned by the Master General to be the spiritual director and <a href="/wiki/Confessor" title="Confessor">confessor</a> to the noted <a href="/wiki/Third_Order_of_St._Dominic" class="mw-redirect" title="Third Order of St. Dominic">Dominican tertiary</a>, <a href="/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena" title="Catherine of Siena">Catherine of Siena</a>.
</p><p>Raymond spent the next six years advising her and hearing her confessions. While there, Raymond gradually learned to trust her holiness and her judgment. This was sealed when he became involved in nursing victims of a plague in 1374. When he contracted the disease himself and lay near death, Catherine came and sat at his bedside until he recovered. Knowing how close he was to death, Raymond credited his recovery to her prayers.
</p><p>By 1374 Raymond had come to the attention of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XI" title="Pope Gregory XI">Pope Gregory XI</a>, then living in <a href="/wiki/Avignon_Papacy" title="Avignon Papacy">Avignon</a>, as a result of his connection to Catherine, and also for his novel ways of confronting issues like the <a href="/wiki/Crusades" title="Crusades">Crusades</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Holy_Land" title="Holy Land">Holy Land</a>, the return of the papacy to Rome, and the general reform of the Church. He was well known for his ability to pass seamlessly from dealing with spiritual and supernatural considerations to the more mundane matters of practical politics. For four years Raymond accompanied Catherine in her journeys, and went to Avignon to act as an intermediary between her and the pope. Catherine had such faith in the commitment of the pope to the cause of a Crusade, that she sent a personal letter to the infamous <a href="/wiki/English_people" title="English people">English</a> <a href="/wiki/Pirate" class="mw-redirect" title="Pirate">pirate</a>, <a href="/wiki/John_Hawkwood" title="John Hawkwood">John Hawkwood</a>, asking him to re-direct his efforts to the service of God in this cause.
</p><p>Pope Gregory would finally return to Rome in 1377, but he died in 1378. The refusal of the French <a href="/wiki/Cardinal_(Catholic)" class="mw-redirect" title="Cardinal (Catholic)">cardinals</a> to accept the election of his successor, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a>, led to the <a href="/wiki/Western_schism" class="mw-redirect" title="Western schism">Great Western schism</a> that lasted 39 years, with one pope in Rome and another in <a href="/wiki/Avignon_Papacy" title="Avignon Papacy">Avignon</a>. This schism divided Europe. Raymond, like Catherine, supported the Roman papacy and defended its legitimacy.
</p><p>In 1379 by command of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_VI" title="Pope Urban VI">Pope Urban VI</a> Raymond was examined by Fra. Giacomo Altoviti who promoted him to the grade of Master of Theology.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the year 1380, Catherine died and Raymond was elected Master General of Dominican Order. He then divided his time between Italy and Germany. In the Caterinian spirit of reform, he gave a new spiritual vitality to the Order. Raymond favored the development of a new interpretation of "observance", for which he drew upon the <a href="/wiki/Franciscan_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Franciscan Order">Franciscan example</a>. In this work he gained the designation of being the second founder of the Order of the Preachers.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Raymond_of_Capua&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Raymond was buried first in <a href="/wiki/Nuremberg" title="Nuremberg">Nuremberg</a> (now Germany), where he died, but his body was later moved to <a href="/wiki/Naples" title="Naples">Naples</a>, to the Church of <a href="/wiki/San_Domenico_Maggiore" title="San Domenico Maggiore">San Domenico Maggiore</a>. In 1899 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII" title="Pope Leo XIII">Pope Leo XIII</a> <a href="/wiki/Beatification" title="Beatification">beatified</a> him on the 500th anniversary of his death.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
