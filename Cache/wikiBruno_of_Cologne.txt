<p><b>Bruno of Cologne</b>, O.Cart. (<a href="/wiki/German_language" title="German language">German</a>: <i lang="de">Bruno von Köln</i>, <a href="/wiki/Italian_language" title="Italian language">Italian</a>: <i lang="it">Bruno di Colonia</i>;c. 1030 – 6 October 1101), venerated as <b>Saint Bruno</b>, was the founder of the <a href="/wiki/Carthusian" class="mw-redirect" title="Carthusian">Carthusian</a> Order. He personally founded the order's first two communities. He was a celebrated teacher at <a href="/wiki/Reims" title="Reims">Reims</a>, and a close advisor of his former pupil, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a>. His feast day is October 6.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Bruno was born in Cologne about the year 1030. According to tradition, he belonged to the family of Hartenfaust, or Hardebüst, one of the principal families of the city.<sup id="cite_ref-cathency_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathency-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Little is known of his early years, except that he studied theology in the present-day French city of Reims before returning to his native land. His Feast Day was announced to be October 6.
</p><p>His education completed, Bruno returned to Cologne, where he was most likely ordained a priest around 1055 and provided with a canonry at St. Cunibert's. In 1056 Bishop Gervais recalled him to Reims, where the following year he found himself head of the Episcopal school, which at the time included the direction of the schools and the oversight of all the educational establishments of the diocese. For eighteen years, from 1057 to 1075, he maintained the prestige which the school of Reims attained under its former masters, Remi of Auxerre, and others.<sup id="cite_ref-cathency_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathency-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bruno led the school for nearly two decades, acquiring an excellent reputation as a philosopher and theologian.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Among his students were Eudes of Châtillon, afterwards <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_II" title="Pope Urban II">Pope Urban II</a>, Rangier, Cardinal and Bishop of Reggio, Robert, Bishop of Langres, and a large number of prelates and abbots.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Chancellor_of_the_Diocese_of_Reims">Chancellor of the Diocese of Reims</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Chancellor of the Diocese of Reims">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1075, Bruno was appointed chancellor of the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Reims" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Reims">Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Reims</a>, which involved him in the daily administration of the diocese. Meanwhile, the pious Bishop <a href="/wiki/Gervais_de_Ch%C3%A2teau-du-Loir" title="Gervais de Château-du-Loir">Gervais de Château-du-Loir</a>, a friend to Bruno, had been succeeded by <a href="/wiki/Manasses_I_(archbishop_of_Reims)" title="Manasses I (archbishop of Reims)">Manasses de Gournai</a>, a violent aristocrat with no real vocation for the Church. In 1077, at the urging of Bruno and the clergy at Reims, de Gournai was suspended at a council at <a href="/wiki/Autun" title="Autun">Autun</a>. Manasses responded, in typical eleventh century fashion, by having his retainers pull down the houses of his accusers.<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He confiscated their goods, sold their benefices, and even appealed to the pope. Bruno discreetly avoided the cathedral city until in 1080 a definite sentence, confirmed by popular riot, compelled Manasses to withdraw<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> and take refuge with <a href="/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor" title="Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor">Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor</a>, the fierce opponent of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_VII" title="Pope Gregory VII">Pope Gregory VII</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Refusal_to_become_a_bishop">Refusal to become a bishop</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Refusal to become a bishop">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:252px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg/250px-Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg" decoding="async" width="250" height="286" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg/375px-Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg/500px-Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg 2x" data-file-width="520" data-file-height="595" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Carducho._Pinturas_del_claustro_de_El_Paular_12.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div><i>Saint Bruno refuses the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Reggio_Calabria-Bova" title="Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Reggio Calabria-Bova">archbishopric of Reggio di Calabria</a></i>, by <a href="/wiki/Vincenzo_Carducci" title="Vincenzo Carducci">Vincenzo Carducci</a>, <a href="/wiki/Monastery_of_El_Paular" title="Monastery of El Paular">Chartreuse of el Paular</a>.</div></div></div>
<p>On the verge of being made bishop himself, Bruno instead followed a vow he had made to renounce secular concerns and withdrew, along with two of his friends, Raoul and Fulcius, also canons of Reims.<sup id="cite_ref-Herald_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herald-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Bruno's first thought on leaving Reims seems to have been to place himself and his companions under the direction of an eminent solitary, <a href="/wiki/Robert_of_Molesme" title="Robert of Molesme">Robert of Molesme</a>, who had recently (1075) settled at Sèche-Fontaine, near <a href="/wiki/Molesme" title="Molesme">Molesme</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Langres" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Langres">Roman Catholic Diocese of Langres</a>, together with a band of other <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermits</a>, who were later on (in 1098) to form the <a href="/wiki/Cistercians" title="Cistercians">Cistercians</a>. But he soon found that this was not his vocation. After a short stay he went with six of his companions to <a href="/wiki/Hugh_of_Ch%C3%A2teauneuf" title="Hugh of Châteauneuf">Hugh of Châteauneuf</a>, Bishop of Grenoble. The bishop, according to the pious legend, had recently had a vision of these men, under a chaplet of seven stars, and he installed them himself in 1084 in a mountainous and uninhabited spot in the lower Alps of the <a href="/wiki/Dauphin%C3%A9" title="Dauphiné">Dauphiné</a>, in a place named <a href="/wiki/Grande_Chartreuse" title="Grande Chartreuse">Chartreuse</a>,<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> not far from <a href="/wiki/Grenoble" title="Grenoble">Grenoble</a>. With St. Bruno were: Landuin, Stephen of Bourg, Stephen of Die (canons of St. Rufus), Hugh the Chaplain and two laymen, Andrew and Guerin, who afterwards became the first <a href="/wiki/Lay_brother" title="Lay brother">lay brothers</a>.
</p><p>They built an oratory with small individual cells at a distance from each other where they lived isolated and in poverty,<sup id="cite_ref-Foley_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Foley-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> entirely occupied in prayer and study, for these men had a reputation for learning, and were frequently honored by the visits of St. Hugh who became like one of themselves.
</p><p>At the time, Bruno's pupil, Eudes of Châtillon, had become pope as Urban II (1088). Resolved to continue the work of reform commenced by Gregory VII and being obliged to struggle against <a href="/wiki/Antipope_Clement_III" title="Antipope Clement III">Antipope Clement III</a> and Emperor Henry IV, he was in dire need of competent and devoted allies and called his former master to Rome in 1090.<sup id="cite_ref-cna_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cna-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>It is difficult to assign the place which Bruno occupied in Rome, or his influence in contemporary events, because it remained entirely hidden and confidential. Lodged in the <a href="/wiki/Lateran" title="Lateran">Lateran</a> with the pope himself, privy to his most private councils, he worked as an advisor but wisely kept in the background, apart from the fiercely partisan rivalries in Rome and within the curia. Shortly after his arrival in Rome, the papal party was forced to evacuate to the south by the arrival of Henry IV with his own antipope in tow.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_bruno.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/San_bruno.jpg/150px-San_bruno.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="147" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/San_bruno.jpg/225px-San_bruno.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/San_bruno.jpg/300px-San_bruno.jpg 2x" data-file-width="340" data-file-height="334" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_bruno.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Bruno of Cologne</div></div></div>
<p>Bruno resisted efforts to name him Archbishop of <a href="/wiki/Reggio_Calabria" title="Reggio Calabria">Reggio Calabria</a>, deferring instead in favor of one of his former pupils nearby in a <a href="/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict" class="mw-redirect" title="Order of Saint Benedict">Benedictine</a> abbey near <a href="/wiki/Salerno" title="Salerno">Salerno</a>. Instead Bruno begged to return again to his solitary life. His intention was to rejoin his brethren in <a href="/wiki/Dauphin%C3%A9" title="Dauphiné">Dauphiné</a>, as a letter addressed to them makes clear.<sup id="cite_ref-Herald_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Herald-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> But the will of Urban II kept him in Italy, near the papal court, to which he could be called at need.
</p><p>Bruno did not attend the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Clermont" title="Council of Clermont">Council of Clermont</a>, where Urban preached the <a href="/wiki/First_Crusade" title="First Crusade">First Crusade</a>, but seems to have been present at the <a href="/wiki/Council_of_Benevento" title="Council of Benevento">Council of Benevento</a> (March, 1091). His part in history is effaced.
</p><p>The place for his new retreat, chosen in 1091 by Bruno and some followers who had joined him, was in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Squillace" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Squillace">Roman Catholic Diocese of Squillace</a>, in a small forested high valley, where the band constructed a little wooden chapel and cabins. His patron there was <a href="/wiki/Roger_I_of_Sicily" title="Roger I of Sicily">Roger I of Sicily</a>, Count of <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> and <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a> and uncle of the Duke of Apulia, who granted them the lands they occupied, and a close friendship developed. Bruno went to the Guiscard court at <a href="/wiki/Mileto" title="Mileto">Mileto</a> to visit the count in his sickness (1098 and 1101), and to baptize his son, <a href="/wiki/Roger_II_of_Sicily" title="Roger II of Sicily">Roger</a> (1097), the future King of Sicily. But more often Roger went into retreat with his friends, where he erected a simple house for himself. Through his generosity, the monastery of St. Stephen was built in 1095, near the original hermitage dedicated to the Virgin.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>At the turn of the new century, the friends of St. Bruno died one after the other: Urban II in 1099; Landuin, the prior of the <a href="/wiki/Grande_Chartreuse" title="Grande Chartreuse">Grande Chartreuse</a>, his first companion, in 1100; Count Roger in 1101. Bruno followed on 6 October 1101 in <a href="/wiki/Serra_San_Bruno" title="Serra San Bruno">Serra San Bruno</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-duffy_6-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-duffy-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Bruno.27s_legacy"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Bruno's_legacy">Bruno's legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Bruno&#039;s legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG/220px-Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG" decoding="async" width="220" height="165" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG/330px-Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG/440px-Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2288" data-file-height="1712" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Notre_Dame_de_Casalibus_1.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Notre Dame de Casalibus, Dauphiné</div></div></div>
<p>After his death, the Carthusians of Calabria, following a frequent custom of the Middle Ages, dispatched a roll-bearer, a servant of the community laden with a long roll of parchment, hung round his neck, who travelled through Italy, France, Germany, and England, stopping to announce the death of Bruno, and in return, the churches, communities, or chapters inscribed upon his roll, in prose or verse, the expression of their regrets, with promises of prayers. Many of these rolls have been preserved, but few are so extensive or so full of praise as that about St. Bruno. A hundred and seventy-eight witnesses, of whom many had known the deceased, celebrated the extent of his knowledge and the fruitfulness of his instruction. Strangers to him were above all struck by his great knowledge and talents. But his disciples praised his three chief virtues&#160;— his great spirit of prayer, extreme mortification, and devotion to the Blessed Virgin.
</p><p>Both the churches built by him in the desert were dedicated to the Blessed Virgin: Our Lady of Casalibus in Dauphiné and <a href="/w/index.php?title=Our_Lady_Della_Torre&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Our Lady Della Torre (page does not exist)">Our Lady Della Torre</a> in Calabria; faithful to his inspirations, the Carthusian Statutes proclaim the Mother of God the first and chief patron of all the houses of the order, whoever may be their particular patron. He is also the eponym for <a href="/wiki/San_Bruno_Creek" title="San Bruno Creek">San Bruno Creek</a> in <a href="/wiki/California" title="California">California</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Inscription_in_the_Roman_Calendar">Inscription in the Roman Calendar</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Bruno_of_Cologne&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Inscription in the Roman Calendar">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Bruno_(Manuel_Pereira,_MRABASF_E-18)_02.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/San_Bruno_%28Manuel_Pereira%2C_MRABASF_E-18%29_02.jpg/220px-San_Bruno_%28Manuel_Pereira%2C_MRABASF_E-18%29_02.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="399" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/San_Bruno_%28Manuel_Pereira%2C_MRABASF_E-18%29_02.jpg/330px-San_Bruno_%28Manuel_Pereira%2C_MRABASF_E-18%29_02.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/San_Bruno_%28Manuel_Pereira%2C_MRABASF_E-18%29_02.jpg/440px-San_Bruno_%28Manuel_Pereira%2C_MRABASF_E-18%29_02.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1533" data-file-height="2778" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:San_Bruno_(Manuel_Pereira,_MRABASF_E-18)_02.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>St Bruno, by Manuel Pereira (1652, <a href="/wiki/Real_Academia_de_Bellas_Artes_de_San_Fernando" title="Real Academia de Bellas Artes de San Fernando">R.A.B.A.S.F.</a>, <a href="/wiki/Madrid" title="Madrid">Madrid</a>).</div></div></div>
<p>Bruno was buried in the little cemetery of the hermitage of Santa Maria. In 1513, his bones were discovered with the epitaph <i>"Haec sunt ossa magistri Brunonis"</i> (these are the bones of the master Bruno) over them. Since the Carthusian Order maintains a strict observance of humility, Saint Bruno was never formally <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a>. He was not included in the <a href="/wiki/Tridentine_Calendar" class="mw-redirect" title="Tridentine Calendar">Tridentine Calendar</a>, but in the year 1623 <a href="/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XV" title="Pope Gregory XV">Pope Gregory XV</a> included him in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> for celebration on 6 October.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Saint Bruno has long been regarded the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Calabria" title="Calabria">Calabria</a> and one of the patron saints of <a href="/wiki/Germany" title="Germany">Germany</a>.
</p><p>A writer as well as founder of his order, Saint Bruno composed commentaries on the <a href="/wiki/Psalms" title="Psalms">Psalms</a> and on the <a href="/wiki/Epistle" title="Epistle">Epistles</a> of <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul the Apostle</a>. Two letters of his also remain, his profession of faith, and a short elegy on contempt for the world which shows that he cultivated poetry. St Bruno's <i>Commentaries</i> reveal that he knew a little Hebrew and Greek; he was familiar with the <a href="/wiki/Church_Fathers" title="Church Fathers">Church Fathers</a>, especially <a href="/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo" title="Augustine of Hippo">Augustine of Hippo</a> and <a href="/wiki/Ambrose" title="Ambrose">Ambrose</a>. "His style," said Dom Rivet, "is concise, clear, nervous and simple, and his Latin as good as could be expected of that century: it would be difficult to find a composition of this kind at once more solid and more luminous, more concise and more clear."<sup id="cite_ref-cathency_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cathency-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In Catholic art, Saint Bruno can be recognized by a <a href="/wiki/Skull_(symbolism)" class="mw-redirect" title="Skull (symbolism)">skull</a> that he holds and contemplates, with a book and a cross. He may be crowned with a <a href="/wiki/Halo_(religious_symbol)" class="mw-redirect" title="Halo (religious symbol)">halo</a> of seven <a href="/wiki/Star" title="Star">stars</a>; or with a roll bearing the <a href="/wiki/Coat_of_arms" title="Coat of arms">device</a> <i>O Bonitas.</i>
</p>
