<p><b>Salomon</b> (<a href="/wiki/Breton_language" title="Breton language">Breton</a>: <i lang="br">Salaün</i>) (died 874) was <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Rennes" title="Count of Rennes">Count of Rennes</a> and <a href="/wiki/Count_of_Nantes" class="mw-redirect" title="Count of Nantes">Nantes</a> from 852 and <a href="/wiki/Duke_of_Brittany" class="mw-redirect" title="Duke of Brittany">Duke of Brittany</a> from 857 until his death by assassination. He used the title King of Brittany intermittently after 868. In 867, he was granted the counties of <a href="/wiki/Avranches" title="Avranches">Avranches</a> and <a href="/wiki/Coutances" title="Coutances">Coutances</a>. 
</p><p>In popular tradition within Brittany he was <a href="/wiki/Canonised" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonised">canonised</a> as "Saint Salomon" after his death and raised to the rank of <a href="/wiki/Martyr" title="Martyr">martyr</a>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Rise_under_Erispoe">Rise under Erispoe</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomon,_King_of_Brittany&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Rise under Erispoe">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Salomon was the son of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Riwallon_III&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Riwallon III (page does not exist)">Riwallon III</a> of <a href="/wiki/Poher" title="Poher">Poher</a>. In 851, <a href="/wiki/Charles_the_Bald" title="Charles the Bald">Charles the Bald</a>, after his defeat at the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Jengland" title="Battle of Jengland">Battle of Jengland</a>, made peace with <a href="/wiki/Erispoe" title="Erispoe">Erispoe</a>, the Breton duke, and granted him the counties of <a href="/wiki/County_of_Rennes" class="mw-redirect" title="County of Rennes">Rennes</a> and <a href="/wiki/County_of_Nantes" title="County of Nantes">Nantes</a> and the <a href="/wiki/Pays_de_Retz" title="Pays de Retz">Pays de Retz</a> in <a href="/wiki/Poitou" title="Poitou">Poitou</a> as far as the river <a href="/wiki/Mayenne_(river)" title="Mayenne (river)">Mayenne</a>. In 852, Salomon swore an oath to Charles and became his loyal follower (<i>fidelis</i>); in return, in a manner similar to <a href="/wiki/Erispoe" title="Erispoe">Erispoe</a>, he was granted Rennes, Nantes, and Retz as a "third" of Brittany to be held from Charles in fee.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He and Erispoe were the <i>dominatores</i> of Rennes in 853. Salomon was the most powerful aristocrat at Erispoe's court.
</p>
<h2><span id="Revolt_of_858.E2.80.93863"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Revolt_of_858–863">Revolt of 858&#8211;863</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomon,_King_of_Brittany&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Revolt of 858–863">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Probably because he feared losing his benefices (which he held under Erispoe) if <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_Stammerer" title="Louis the Stammerer">Louis the Stammerer</a> were allowed to become king at <a href="/wiki/Le_Mans" title="Le Mans">Le Mans</a>, Salomon colluded with the otherwise unknown Almarchus to assassinate his cousin Erispoe and seize the Breton throne in 857. In 858, he was behind the large-scale revolt of the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> nobles of <a href="/wiki/Neustria" title="Neustria">Neustria</a> against Charles the Bald.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Bretons were involved in the chasing of Louis from Le Mans in Spring that year. In September, <a href="/wiki/Louis_the_German" title="Louis the German">Louis the German</a> marched as far as <a href="/wiki/Orl%C3%A9ans" title="Orléans">Orléans</a>, where a Breton delegation from Salomon met him and took oaths on Salomon's behalf.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 859, a synod met at <a href="/wiki/Savonni%C3%A8res" title="Savonnières">Savonnières</a> near <a href="/wiki/Toul" title="Toul">Toul</a> and tried to order Salomon to remember his oath of 852 and to resume paying the <a href="/wiki/Tribute" title="Tribute">tribute</a> which Brittany had paid in years past.<sup id="cite_ref-smith105_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith105-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>By 862, Salomon was the centre of the revolt against Charles the Bald, though he had not made war on the king himself since 860.<sup id="cite_ref-smith105_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith105-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In that year he hired the services of a band of <a href="/wiki/Vikings" title="Vikings">Vikings</a> with which to fight <a href="/wiki/Robert_the_Strong" title="Robert the Strong">Robert the Strong</a>, who himself had hired mercenary Vikings to help him.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Salomon also lent a force of Bretons to aid Louis the Stammerer, now in league with the rebels, in his war with Robert.<sup id="cite_ref-smith105_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith105-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 863, Charles gathered an army and began marching on Brittany, but held off near <a href="/wiki/Entrammes" title="Entrammes">Entrammes</a> and negotiated a peace with Salomon whereby western <a href="/wiki/County_of_Anjou" title="County of Anjou">Anjou</a> was recognised as a part of Brittany and the <a href="/wiki/Lay_abbacy" class="mw-redirect" title="Lay abbacy">lay abbacy</a> of Saint-Aubin in <a href="/wiki/Angers" title="Angers">Angers</a> was granted to Salomon, who commended himself to Charles and paid tribute.<sup id="cite_ref-smith105_7-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-smith105-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span id="Revolt_of_865.E2.80.93867"></span><span class="mw-headline" id="Revolt_of_865–867">Revolt of 865&#8211;867</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomon,_King_of_Brittany&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Revolt of 865–867">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Salomon did not give up his war with Robert or his alliance with the Vikings quite so readily, however. In 865 and 866, the Vikings and Bretons ravaged the vicinity of Le Mans and Robert was killed in the <a href="/wiki/Battle_of_Brissarthe" title="Battle of Brissarthe">Battle of Brissarthe</a> against the Vikings allied with the Bretons.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup> This was the start of a new insurrection; even <a href="/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_I" title="Pope Nicholas I">Pope Nicholas I</a> wrote letters to Salomon urging him to resume the halted tribute payments. Charles marched on Brittany in 867, but Salomon sent his son-in-law <a href="/wiki/Pasquitan,_Duke_of_Brittany" class="mw-redirect" title="Pasquitan, Duke of Brittany">Pascweten</a> to negotiate a peace at <a href="/wiki/Compi%C3%A8gne" title="Compiègne">Compiègne</a> in August. Charles sent hostages to Salomon and Pascweten swore oaths of fealty to Charles on Salomon's behalf.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Peace_and_regalia">Peace and regalia</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Salomon,_King_of_Brittany&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Peace and regalia">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>This peace was to last until the end of Salomon's life. Charles rewarded his now faithful vassal with a gift of regalia in 868, including a golden, jewelled crown.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> It is also likely that Salomon's two-year-old son Wigo was baptised on this occasion and that Charles acted as godfather to him, thus making Salomon and Charles linked by "blood" as co-fathers.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Though Salomon thereafter began to call himself king, he was not king in any official capacity, as an eleventh-century historian at <a href="/wiki/Redon" class="mw-redirect" title="Redon">Redon</a> monastery wrote:
</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Salomon was called king, not because it was true in fact, but because he wore a gold coronet and purple robes by a grant of the Emperor Charles, and for this reason was designated by this name.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Salomon expended some effort in the mid-860s trying to have Pope Nicholas send the <a href="/wiki/Pallium" title="Pallium">pallium</a> to the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Dol" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Dol">Bishop of Dol</a> to create an archdiocese for all the Breton bishoprics, which did not recognise the <a href="/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Tours" class="mw-redirect" title="Archdiocese of Tours">Archdiocese of Tours</a>, their legal metropolitan.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup> Salomon may have wanted an archbishop which was pliable to his wishes or who could consecrate him as king. Perhaps he simply wished to break the deadlock which had ensued following <a href="/wiki/Nominoe" title="Nominoe">Nominoe's</a> deposition of five Breton bishops a decade and a half earlier.
</p><p>In 874, a conspiracy involving Pascweten, <a href="/wiki/Gurvand,_Duke_of_Brittany" class="mw-redirect" title="Gurvand, Duke of Brittany">Wrhwant</a>, and Wigo, son of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Riwallon,_Count_of_Cornouaille&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Riwallon, Count of Cornouaille (page does not exist)">Riwallon, Count of Cornouaille</a>, plotted to kill Salomon.<sup id="cite_ref-14" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup> This they did, though they quickly fell out with each other and a civil war followed until 876.
</p>
