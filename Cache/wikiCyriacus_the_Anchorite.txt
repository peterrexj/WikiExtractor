<p><b>Saint Kyriakos</b> the <a href="/wiki/Anchorite" title="Anchorite">Anchorite</a> (also known as <i>Cyriacus the Hermit</i>) (<a href="/wiki/Greek_language" title="Greek language">Greek</a>: <span title="Ancient Greek (to 1453)-language text"><span lang="grc">Ὅσιος Κυριακός ὁ Ἀναχωρητής</span></span>, <i>Hosios Kyriakos ho Anachōrētēs</i>) was born in <a href="/wiki/Corinth" title="Corinth">Corinth</a> in the year 448.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyriacus_the_Anchorite&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His father was a priest named John and his mother's name was Eudoxia. Kyriakos had Bishop Peter of Corinth for a relative, who made Kyriakos a reader in church.
</p><p>Cyriacus's studies of the <a href="/wiki/Scriptures" class="mw-redirect" title="Scriptures">Scriptures</a> encouraged him to want what the culture deemed a pure and saintly life.  Cyriacus, before turning 18, attended a church service on Matthew 16:24 ("Whosoever would to come after Me, let him deny himself and raise up his cross and follow Me") that so deeply moved him he immediately he went to the harbour, got onto a ship, and set off to <a href="/wiki/Jerusalem" title="Jerusalem">Jerusalem</a> instead of going home.
</p><p>After visiting holy places of <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a>, Cyriacus dwelt for several months at a <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastery</a> not far from Sion. This placed him in obedience to <a href="/wiki/Hegumenos" class="mw-redirect" title="Hegumenos">Hegumenos</a> Abba Eustorgius. With his support he made his way to the wilderness <a href="/wiki/Lavra" title="Lavra">Lavra</a> of <a href="/wiki/St._Euthymius" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Euthymius">St. Euthymius</a> the Great (January 20).<sup id="cite_ref-goarch_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goarch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> St. Euthymius was impressed by him and tonsured him into the monastic schema and placed him under the guidance of <a href="/wiki/Gerasimus_of_the_Jordan" title="Gerasimus of the Jordan">St. Gerasimus</a> (March 4). He then pursued <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">asceticism</a> at the Jordan in the monastery of <a href="/wiki/Theoctistus_of_Palestine" title="Theoctistus of Palestine">St. Theoctistus</a>.
</p><p>Gerasimus, seeing that Cyriacus was still quite young, ordered him to live in the community with the brethren. Gerasimus became something of a mentor to the young man and each Sunday imparted the Holy Mysteries of his faith to his disciple. The young monk took to monastic obediences: he prayed fervently, he slept little, he ate food only every other day, and nourished himself with bread and water.
</p><p>After the death of Gerasimos, the twenty-seven-year-old Cyriacus intended to return to the Lavra of Euthymios, but he had since died. Therefore, Cyriacus asked for a solitary cell where he pursued asceticism in silence. During this period he communicated only with the monk Thomas. Later even that ended as Thomas was sent to <a href="/wiki/Alexandria" title="Alexandria">Alexandria</a> where he was consecrated bishop. This meant Cyriacus spent ten years in total silence. At 37 years of age he was ordained to the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a>.
</p><p>When a split occurred between the monasteries of Euthymius and Theoctistus, Cyriacus withdrew to the Souka monastery of <a href="/wiki/St._Chariton" class="mw-redirect" title="St. Chariton">St. Chariton</a> (September 28). At this monastery they received even <a href="/wiki/Tonsure" title="Tonsure">tonsured</a> monks as <a href="/wiki/Novitiate" title="Novitiate">novices</a>, and so was Cyriacus received. He toiled at the regular monastic obediences. After several years, Cyriacus was ordained priest and chosen <a href="/wiki/Canonarch" title="Canonarch">canonarch</a> and did this obedience for eighteen years. He would spend thirty years at the monastery of St. Chariton.
</p><p>Strict fasting and devotion distinguished Cyriacus even among the ascetics of the Lavra. In his cell each night he read the <a href="/wiki/Psalter" title="Psalter">Psalter</a>, interrupting the reading only to go to church at midnight. The ascetic slept very little. When the monk reached seventy years of age, he went to the Natoufa wilderness taking with him his disciple John.<sup id="cite_ref-goarch_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goarch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In the desert the hermits fed themselves only with bitter herbs, which <a href="/wiki/Hagiographical" class="mw-redirect" title="Hagiographical">hagiographical</a> accounts say were rendered edible.<sup id="cite_ref-goarch_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-goarch-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> After five years one of the inhabitants found out about the ascetics and brought to them his son and Cyriacus reportedly healed him. From that time many people began to approach the monk with their needs, but he sought complete solitude and fled to the Rouva wilderness, where he dwelt five years more. But the sick still came for him so he helped who he could.
</p>
<figure typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_(Menologion_of_Basil_II).jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/300px-Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg" decoding="async" width="300" height="203" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/450px-Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3e/Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg/600px-Cyriacus_the_Anchorite_%28Menologion_of_Basil_II%29.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1095" data-file-height="740" /></a><figcaption>Miniature from the <i><a href="/wiki/Menologion_of_Basil_II" title="Menologion of Basil II">Menologion of Basil II</a></i></figcaption></figure>
<p>At his 80th year of life  Cyriacus fled to the hidden Sousakim wilderness, where two dried up streams passed by. The brethren of the Souka monastery came to him seven years after his arrival, beseeching his spiritual help during the time of debilitating hunger and illness. After his intervention seemed to improve the situation they wished him to return to the monastery. Therefore, he settled in a cave which St. Chariton had once lived.
</p><p>Cyriacus worked against the religious movement <a href="/wiki/Origen" title="Origen">Origenism</a>. By prayer and by word, he led many to abandon it for a strengthened Orthodox faith. According to <a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox" class="mw-redirect" title="Eastern Orthodox">Eastern Orthodox</a> tradition the <a href="/wiki/Theotokos" title="Theotokos">Theotokos</a> appeared to him to strengthen him in his efforts against Origenism. After the deaths of <a href="/wiki/Nonnus" title="Nonnus">Nonnus</a> and <a href="/wiki/Leontius_of_Byzantium" title="Leontius of Byzantium">Leontius</a>, which it is said he predicted, the movement ceased to spread.
</p><p>At the age of ninety-nine, Cyriacus again went off to Susakim and lived there with his disciple John. This period is full of several legends involving him saving people from lions or his prayers causing rain to fall.
</p><p>For the two years before his death Cyriacus returned to the monastery and again settled into the cave of Chariton. Until the end of his life he was never idle in prayer or work. Before his death Cyriacus summoned the brethren and blessed them all. He quietly fell asleep in the Lord, having lived 109 years. He died in the year 557.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Cyriacus_the_Anchorite&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>

</p>
