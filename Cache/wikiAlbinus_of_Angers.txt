<p><b>Saint Albinus of Angers</b> (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Saint-Aubin</i>) (c. 470 – March 1, 550), also known as <b>Saint Albin</b> (<span class="rt-commentedText nowrap"><span class="IPA nopopups noexcerpt" lang="en-fonipa"><a href="/wiki/Help:IPA/English" title="Help:IPA/English">/<span style="border-bottom:1px dotted"><span title="/ˈ/: primary stress follows">ˈ</span><span title="/æ/: &#39;a&#39; in &#39;bad&#39;">æ</span><span title="&#39;l&#39; in &#39;lie&#39;">l</span><span title="&#39;b&#39; in &#39;buy&#39;">b</span><span title="/i/: &#39;y&#39; in &#39;happy&#39;">i</span><span title="&#39;n&#39; in &#39;nigh&#39;">n</span></span>/</a></span></span>) in English, was a French <a href="/wiki/Abbot" title="Abbot">abbot</a> and <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a>.  Born to a <a href="/wiki/Nobility" title="Nobility">noble</a> <a href="/wiki/Gallo-Roman" class="mw-redirect" title="Gallo-Roman">Gallo-Roman</a> family at <a href="/wiki/Vannes" title="Vannes">Vannes</a>, <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a>, St. Albinus was a <a href="/wiki/Monk" title="Monk">monk</a> and from 504 C.E. Abbot of Tintillac (which no longer stands; its location has not been satisfactorily identified).  His reputation spread during the twenty-five years in which he served as abbot. In 529, St. Albinus was elected, against his wishes, <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Angers" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Angers">Bishop of Angers</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Episcopate">Episcopate</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Albinus_of_Angers&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Episcopate">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>He campaigned against <a href="/wiki/Incestuous" class="mw-redirect" title="Incestuous">incestuous</a> weddings, such as those that occurred between members of the nobility.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-forum.com_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-forum.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He participated in the Third Council of Orléans (538) after <a href="/wiki/Childebert_I" title="Childebert I">King Childebert</a> granted permission for this council to take place.<sup id="cite_ref-catholic-forum.com_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-catholic-forum.com-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Albinus sought advice from <a href="/wiki/Caesarius_of_Arles" title="Caesarius of Arles">St. Caesarius of Arles</a> after seeing laxity in other bishops.
</p><p>Tradition states that he helped all in distress, using diocesan funds to free hostages from pirates. Another tradition states that he clashed with King Childebert, who had imprisoned a woman called Etherie, from <a href="/w/index.php?title=Douille&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Douille (page does not exist)">Douille</a> near Angers. Unable to secure her release Albinus visited her in prison, and the soldier who tried to resist him fell dead at his feet. This so impressed the king that he allowed St. Albinus to bail her out.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Another legend relates that St. Albinus once prayed far into the night for some men imprisoned in the Tower of Angers.  Suddenly a great stone collapsed from the wall, allowing their escape.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death_and_burial">Death and burial</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Albinus_of_Angers&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Death and burial">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>St. Albinus died in 550 and was buried in the church of Saint-Pierre at Angers.  In 556, a church was dedicated to him and his body was buried in its <a href="/wiki/Crypt" title="Crypt">crypt</a>. Near this church an abbey arose, called <i>Saint-Aubin</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Albinus_of_Angers&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:152px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg/150px-Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg" decoding="async" width="150" height="200" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg/225px-Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg/300px-Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg 2x" data-file-width="600" data-file-height="800" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Angers_tour_saint_aubin.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Saint-Aubin, Angers.</div></div></div>
<p><a href="/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours" title="Gregory of Tours">St. Gregory of Tours</a> remarks on the cult of St. Albinus, which later became diffused in Germany, England, and Poland, making St. Albinus a popular saint during the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> <a href="/wiki/Venantius_Fortunatus" title="Venantius Fortunatus">St. Venantius Fortunatus</a>, a near contemporary, wrote a life of this saint.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Churches were dedicated to him right across the continent as far as Poland, and he was sometimes seen as the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> for protection from <a href="/wiki/Pirate" class="mw-redirect" title="Pirate">pirate</a> attack. This may have been based on the tradition that he bought back parishioners who had been taken captive by pirates sailing up the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a> river. Later the tradition was reinforced by a miracle recorded in the 10th century, when the walled town of <a href="/wiki/Gu%C3%A9rande" title="Guérande">Guérande</a>, near the mouth of the <a href="/wiki/Loire" title="Loire">Loire</a>, prayed to St. Albinus for help and found their attackers miraculously defeated.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/Middle_Ages" title="Middle Ages">Middle Ages</a>, Nicholas Belfort notably wrote describing miracles performed at the tomb of St. Aubin after the year 1000 AD. Belfort was a Regular Canon in the monastery of St John the Baptist by the city of <a href="/wiki/Soissons" title="Soissons">Soissons</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Thus, his fame increased and many coastal villages chose Aubin as their patron saint. Presumably this is the reason why <a href="/wiki/Saint_Aubin,_Jersey" title="Saint Aubin, Jersey">St. Aubin</a> in <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a> bears his name, preserving the name of an ancient chapel that has long since disappeared. St. Aubin is venerated there in the <a href="/wiki/Anglicanism" title="Anglicanism">Anglican</a> church of <a href="/wiki/St_Aubin_on_the_Hill" title="St Aubin on the Hill">St Aubin on the Hill</a>. (See <a href="/wiki/Saint-Aubin_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Saint-Aubin (disambiguation)">Saint-Aubin</a> for other places named for him.)
</p><p>St. Albinus' <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a> is celebrated on March 1.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
