<p><b>Luigi Versiglia</b>, <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">S.D.B.</a> (5 June 1873 – 25 February 1930) was an Italian <a href="/wiki/Catholic" class="mw-redirect" title="Catholic">Catholic</a> <a href="/wiki/Prelate" title="Prelate">prelate</a> and professed member from the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesians of Don Bosco</a> who served as the first <a href="/wiki/Apostolic_Vicar" class="mw-redirect" title="Apostolic Vicar">Apostolic Vicar</a> of <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Shaozhou" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Shaozhou">Shaoguan</a> from 1920 until his murder.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was also a former <a href="/wiki/Novice_master" title="Novice master">novice master</a> noted for his strict austerities and discipline but for his loving and compassionate care of the poor and defenceless.<sup id="cite_ref-AT_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AT-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> He led the first Salesian expedition to China in 1906 and remained there until his death functioning for the people in various capacities such as a gardener and barber.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His beatification was celebrated in 1983 and he was later canonized as a saint on 1 October 2000 in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Versiglia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Luigi Versiglia was born on 5 June 1873 in <a href="/wiki/Oliva_Gessi" title="Oliva Gessi">Oliva Gessi</a> in <a href="/wiki/Pavia" title="Pavia">Pavia</a>. He served as an <a href="/wiki/Altar_server" title="Altar server">altar server</a> in his childhood. In 1885 his parents sent him to school at one of the oratories that the <a href="/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco" title="Salesians of Don Bosco">Salesians of Don Bosco</a> managed though he never entertained notions of becoming a priest instead of a <a href="/wiki/Veterinarian" title="Veterinarian">veterinarian</a> as he wished.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-AT_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AT-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He studied there in Valdocco from 1885 to 1889 before deciding to become a Salesian himself.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> But <a href="/wiki/John_Bosco" title="John Bosco">Giovanni Bosco</a> also noticed Versiglia's diligence and saw something in him that he wanted to discuss with the child. He told him that he wanted to meet with him soon to tell him important. This happened in 1887 though the actual meeting never took place for Bosco fell ill and died in 1888. Versiglia wondered from that point what it was Bosco wished to speak with him about.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He entered the order after <a href="/wiki/Michele_Rua" title="Michele Rua">Michele Rua</a> received him into it.<sup id="cite_ref-AT_3-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AT-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> He made his solemn profession on 11 October 1889 and he received his <a href="/wiki/Doctorate" title="Doctorate">doctorate</a> in philosophical studies from the <a href="/wiki/Pontifical_Gregorian_University" title="Pontifical Gregorian University">Gregorian</a> in <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in 1893 (he was there since 1890). His <a href="/wiki/Ordination" title="Ordination">ordination</a> to the priesthood was celebrated on 21 December 1895 and he was later appointed as <a href="/wiki/Novice_master" title="Novice master">novice master</a> at <a href="/wiki/Genzano_di_Roma" title="Genzano di Roma">Genzano</a> in Rome from 1896 until 1905 where he became known for his strict austerities and discipline.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> But Versiglia needed to receive a special dispensation to be ordained since he was under the canonical age requirement.
</p><p>He led the first expedition for Salesian missionaries into <a href="/wiki/China" title="China">China</a> in 1906 and arrived at <a href="/wiki/Macau" title="Macau">Macau</a> on 7 January.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> The priest began his work and established a motherhouse for the order at Macau while opening a new mission in the Shaoguan region where <a href="/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV" title="Pope Benedict XV">Pope Benedict XV</a> appointed him as its first apostolic vicar in 1920. Versiglia received his <a href="/wiki/Episcopal_consecration" class="mw-redirect" title="Episcopal consecration">episcopal consecration</a> in 1921 at the Canton Cathedral where the Salesian <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a> <a href="/wiki/Carlo_Braga" title="Carlo Braga">Carlo Braga</a> was stationed at the <a href="/wiki/Organ_(music)" title="Organ (music)">organ</a> for the event. He founded an <a href="/wiki/Orphanage" title="Orphanage">orphanage</a> as well as several <a href="/wiki/Aged_care" class="mw-redirect" title="Aged care">aged care homes</a> and two <a href="/wiki/Leper_colonies" class="mw-redirect" title="Leper colonies">leper colonies</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> But he tended to the needs of his people and served in various capacities for them such as their gardener or barber.
</p><p>He and Callisto Caravario - fellow Salesian whom he knew well - were travelling at noon on 25 February 1930 via boat along a river to the Lin-Chow mission when <a href="/wiki/Bolshevik" class="mw-redirect" title="Bolshevik">Bolshevik</a> pirates boarded their ship.<sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The pirates planned on abducting and enslaving three girls on the ship but the two priest shielded them.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_5-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> But the pirates managed to subdue the pair and knocked them unconscious after striking them with their rifle butts before binding them and going to rummage through their possessions in their luggage. The men discarded the crucifixes as the men came to with the two hearing each other's confession.<sup id="cite_ref-AT_3-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AT-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-SQPN_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SQPN-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> The pair were then dragged ashore into a <a href="/wiki/Thicket" title="Thicket">thicket</a> where Versiglia knew he would die so pleaded for the men to spare Caravario. But the retort came: "No" because "the foreign devils must all die".<sup id="cite_ref-AT_3-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-AT-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Five rifle shots ended the pair's lives. Their remains were not discovered until 27 February and the girls captured were released within the week.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His remains were interred at the Lin Kong-How Cathedral but the <a href="/wiki/Red_Guards_(China)" class="mw-redirect" title="Red Guards (China)">Red Guards</a> vandalized this place during the <a href="/wiki/Cultural_Revolution_-_China" class="mw-redirect" title="Cultural Revolution - China">Cultural Revolution</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EC_4-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EC-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>On 2 October 2010 the Salesian cardinal <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Zen_Ze-kiun" class="mw-redirect" title="Joseph Zen Ze-kiun">Joseph Zen Ze-kiun</a> led a torchlight commemoration to the pair on 2 October 2010 to mark a decade since the pair were canonized as saints.<sup id="cite_ref-SEB_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-SEB-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Canonization">Canonization</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Luigi_Versiglia&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Canonization">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The process for the pair's canonization opened in both Shaozhou and in <a href="/wiki/Turin" title="Turin">Turin</a> in an informative process that spanned from 31 December 1934 until its closure in 1935 while his spiritual writings received theological approval on 18 March 1949. The formal introduction to the cause came on 13 June 1952 under <a href="/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII" title="Pope Pius XII">Pope Pius XII</a> and he became titled as a <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>. There was also a second process that was held but split into two: one was held in <a href="/wiki/Hong_Kong" title="Hong Kong">Hong Kong</a> from 1953 to 1954 and the other was held in Turin from 1953 to 1957. Both processes received validation from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_Rites" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for Rites">Congregation for Rites</a> on 5 July 1963 when the cause moved to Rome for further investigation.
</p><p>The officials from the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> and their consultants approved the cause on 3 February 1976 with the C.C.S. members alone meeting and approving the cause on 11 May 1976. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI" title="Pope Paul VI">Pope Paul VI</a> approved their beatification on 13 November 1976 determining that the two were killed "in odium fidei" (in hatred of the faith) but died in 1978 before he could celebrate their beatification as did his successor <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_I" title="Pope John Paul I">Pope John Paul I</a> a month later. <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> celebrated the beatification on 15 May 1983.
</p><p>Their canonization would have depended on one miracle being attributed to them but this was waivered. Their cause was unified to that of others killed in China on 11 January 2000 with John Paul II signing a decree "de signis" on 22 January 2000 which waivered the required miracle for canonization. The date for the celebration was determined at a gathering of cardinals held on 10 March 2000 and Versiglia - and that of 119 others - was canonized as a saint in <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter%27s_Square" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Peter&#39;s Square">Saint Peter's Square</a> on 1 October 2000.
</p>
