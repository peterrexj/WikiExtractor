<p><b>Pere Tarrés i Claret</b> (30 May 1905 – 31 August 1950) was a <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholic Church">Catholic</a> doctor from <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a>, who became a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>. Claret served as a doctor during the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a>, and administered to the poor and to those who suffered from <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>; he also co-established a clinic for those who could not afford adequate medical treatment.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He received beatification from <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> in <a href="/wiki/Loreto,_Marche" title="Loreto, Marche">Loreto</a> on 5 September 2004.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_i_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tleft"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg/220px-Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="344" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg/330px-Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg/440px-Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg 2x" data-file-width="2273" data-file-height="3557" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Esgl%C3%A9sia_Sarria_tomba_PereTarres_0084.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Tomb.</div></div></div>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:222px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg/220px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="348" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg/330px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/14/Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg/440px-Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg 2x" data-file-width="5106" data-file-height="8068" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Barcelona_Cathedral_Interior_-_Blessed_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_by_Montserrat_Garc%C3%ADa_Rius.jpg" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Monument in <a href="/wiki/Barcelona_Cathedral" title="Barcelona Cathedral">Barcelona Cathedral</a></div></div></div>
<p>Pere Tarrés i Claret was born in 1905 in <a href="/wiki/Manresa" title="Manresa">Manresa</a> to Francesc Tarrés Puigdellívol and Carme Claret Masats; his two sisters Francesca and Maria later became <a href="/wiki/Nun" title="Nun">nuns</a>. As a child he helped the local chemist and store owner Josep Balaguer who encouraged the child to continue his studies in medicine.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> From the age of nine he studied with the <a href="/wiki/Jesuits" title="Jesuits">Jesuits</a>; he received <a href="/wiki/Confirmation_(Catholic_Church)" class="mw-redirect" title="Confirmation (Catholic Church)">Confirmation</a> on 31 May 1910 and his <a href="/wiki/First_Communion" title="First Communion">First Communion</a> on 1 May 1913.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He moved to <a href="/wiki/Barcelona" title="Barcelona">Barcelona</a> in October 1921 in order to prepare for his studies in medicine; it was there that he decided to become a medical practitioner with the sole aim of helping other people.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> On 12 March 1925 he gave his first lecture on medicine at the Moral and Instructive Centre de Gracia. His father died in July 1925 and his mother suffered a severe accident not long after that gave her a physical disability.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> On the occasion of <a href="/wiki/Christmas" title="Christmas">Christmas</a> 1927 he made a vow of <a href="/wiki/Chastity" title="Chastity">chastity</a>. During his period of studies he received <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_direction" title="Spiritual direction">spiritual direction</a> from Jaume Serra who encouraged Claret to enter a particular Christian federation.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> In the 1930s he began to attend meetings of the Federation of Young Christians of Catalonia – upon Serra's advice – and soon became one of its most prominent of leaders. He later was made its president and was known for his openness to others' ideas and for his stark enthusiasm. He maintained frequent correspondence with several colleagues in the federation and later ascended to the post of vice-president of the federal council. In 1935 he was made an official of the new diocesan committee and was later made an official in the archdiocesan committee – he received the recommendation of the Cardinal <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Tarragona" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Tarragona">Archbishop of Tarragona</a> <a href="/wiki/Francisco_Vidal_y_Barraquer" title="Francisco Vidal y Barraquer">Francisco Vidal y Barraquer</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Claret was also involved in work with the <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Action" title="Catholic Action">Catholic Action</a> movement. In 1936 he graduated with a degree in medicine.
</p><p>During the <a href="/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War" title="Spanish Civil War">Spanish Civil War</a> he lived as a "refugee" since anti-religious sentiment spread across the state. Claret administered the sacraments to those whom he could reach in secret. On 18 April 1936 he went to <a href="/wiki/Montserrat_(mountain)" title="Montserrat (mountain)">Montserrat</a> for the <a href="/wiki/Spiritual_Exercises_of_Ignatius_of_Loyola" class="mw-redirect" title="Spiritual Exercises of Ignatius of Loyola">spiritual exercises</a> but was adjourned in July 1936 due to the onslaught of the conflict in the area.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> In May 1938 he was forced to enter the republican military for medical aid and was stationed there for a total period of eight months until January 1939 and was even promoted to captain; while in service he wrote about the horrors of the conflict he witnessed in his "War Diary".<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His experiences made him feel that <a href="/wiki/God" title="God">God</a> wanted him to be a "doctor of souls" and so he became a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a>; he commenced his studies for the priesthood on 29 September 1939 in Barcelona. It was during his studies that his mother died in 1941 at the time he received the <a href="/wiki/Minor_orders" title="Minor orders">minor orders</a>; he was elevated to the <a href="/wiki/Diaconate" class="mw-redirect" title="Diaconate">diaconate</a> on 22 March 1942.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Claret was <a href="/wiki/Ordained" class="mw-redirect" title="Ordained">ordained</a> to the priesthood on 30 May 1942. Claret's secret for a fulfilling spiritual life was to possess a strong ardent devotion to the <a href="/wiki/Eucharist" title="Eucharist">Eucharist</a> and for filial love to the <a href="/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary" class="mw-redirect" title="Blessed Virgin Mary">Mother of God</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-HS_1-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-HS-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> After his ordination he was made the parochial vicar of the parish of <a href="/wiki/Sant_Esteve_Sesrovires" title="Sant Esteve Sesrovires">Sant Esteve Sesrovires</a> and in 1943 was sent to the Pontifical University of Salamanca for theological studies; he entered on 13 November 1944. He earned his theological degree in 1944.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He co-established – with Dr. Gerrado Manresa – his own clinic dedicated to the Blessed Mother for the ill but in particular for those who suffered from <a href="/wiki/Tuberculosis" title="Tuberculosis">tuberculosis</a>; he also ensured that the clinic would be able to cater to those people who could not afford adequate medical treatment. His automobile became what he called his "instrument of work" as a means of evangelizing people.<sup id="cite_ref-CO_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-CO-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He was diagnosed with <a href="/wiki/Lymphatic_cancer" class="mw-redirect" title="Lymphatic cancer">lymphatic cancer</a> on 17 May 1950 and so offered his suffering to God; he said that his death was a chance "to die in a continual act of love and suffering ... worthy of the Heavenly Father". He died in his clinic on 31 August 1950. His remains were re-located to the parish church of San Vicente de Sarria on 6 November 1975.<sup id="cite_ref-BC_2-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-BC-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Posthumous_foundation">Posthumous foundation</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_i_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Posthumous foundation">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 1985 the <a href="/wiki/Archbishop_of_Barcelona" class="mw-redirect" title="Archbishop of Barcelona">Archbishop of Barcelona</a> <a href="/wiki/Narc%C3%ADs_Jubany_Arnau" title="Narcís Jubany Arnau">Narcís Arnau</a> founded the Foundation Pere Tarrés in honor of the late priest, a nonprofit devoted to charitable works.<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Beatification">Beatification</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s_i_Claret&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Beatification">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<div class="thumb tright"><div class="thumbinner" style="width:212px;"><a href="/wiki/File:Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG" class="image"><img alt="" src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG/210px-Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG" decoding="async" width="210" height="157" class="thumbimage" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG/315px-Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG/420px-Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG 2x" data-file-width="2592" data-file-height="1936" /></a>  <div class="thumbcaption"><div class="magnify"><a href="/wiki/File:Fundaci%C3%B3_Pere_Tarr%C3%A9s.JPG" class="internal" title="Enlarge"></a></div>Foundation Pere Tarrés.</div></div></div>
<p>The beatification process received the approval of the <a href="/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints" class="mw-redirect" title="Congregation for the Causes of Saints">Congregation for the Causes of Saints</a> on 19 June 1980 and so granted Claret the title <a href="/wiki/Servant_of_God" title="Servant of God">Servant of God</a>, initiating the first stage of the process. The diocesan process in Barcelona was opened in 1982 and closed on 25 April 1990 after almost a decade of collating documentation and investigating Claret's life. Documentation was sent to <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> on 26 March 1993 and received validation from Roman officials, allowing the submission of the <a href="/wiki/Positio" title="Positio">Positio</a> in 1997 in order for further investigations to begin.
</p><p>The miracle required for his beatification was investigated in its diocese of origin in a process that spanned from 2 March 1990 until 29 October 1991; all documents and testimonies were submitted to Roman officials by 2000. The C.C.S approved the cause on 5 April 2005, allowing <a href="/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II" title="Pope John Paul II">Pope John Paul II</a> to proclaim Claret to be <a href="/wiki/Venerable" class="mw-redirect" title="Venerable">Venerable</a> on 22 June 2004. He beatified Claret in <a href="/wiki/Loreto,_Marche" title="Loreto, Marche">Loreto</a> on 5 September 2004. Dr. Silvia Mónica Correale is the current <a href="/wiki/Postulator" title="Postulator">postulator</a> of the cause for <a href="/wiki/Canonization" title="Canonization">canonization</a>.
</p>
