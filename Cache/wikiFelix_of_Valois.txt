<p><b>Felix of Valois</b>, OSST (<a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>: <i lang="fr">Félix de Valois</i>; April 16, 1127 – November 4, 1212) was a French <a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church_sexual_abuse_cases" title="Catholic Church sexual abuse cases">Catholic</a> former <a href="/wiki/Cistercian" class="mw-redirect" title="Cistercian">Cistercian</a> hermit and a co-founder (with <a href="/wiki/John_of_Matha" title="John of Matha">John of Matha</a>) of the <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Trinitarian Order</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Life">Life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Valois&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Life">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p><a href="/wiki/Alban_Butler" title="Alban Butler">Butler</a> says that Felix was born in 1127.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> He was surnamed Valois because he was a native of the province of Valois. Tradition holds that he renounced his possessions and retired to a dense forest in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Meaux" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Meaux">Diocese of Meaux</a>, where he gave himself to prayer and contemplation.<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> Much later sources sometimes identify him with Hugh (II), supposed son of <a href="/wiki/Ralph_I,_Count_of_Vermandois" title="Ralph I, Count of Vermandois">Ralph I, Count of Vermandois</a> by <a href="/wiki/Eleanor_of_Champagne" title="Eleanor of Champagne">Eleanor of Champagne</a>.
</p><p><a href="/wiki/John_of_Matha" title="John of Matha">John of Matha</a>, a young nobleman, a native of Provence, and doctor of divinity, who was lately ordained priest, having heard of the holy hermit of Cerfroid, sought him out, and put himself under his direction.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> John proposed to him the project of founding an order for the redemption of captives.<sup id="cite_ref-okane_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-okane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> Felix, though seventy years of age, readily agreed.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="The_Trinitarians">The Trinitarians</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Valois&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: The Trinitarians">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"/><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">Main article: <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Trinitarian Order</a></div>
<p>Felix, in company with John, set out for <a href="/wiki/Rome" title="Rome">Rome</a> in the depth of winter and arrived there in January 1198, the beginning of the pontificate of <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_III" title="Pope Innocent III">Innocent III</a>. They had letters of recommendation from the <a href="/wiki/Bishop_of_Paris" class="mw-redirect" title="Bishop of Paris">Bishop of Paris</a>, and the new pope received them with kindness and lodged them in his palace. Though little in favor of new orders, Innocent III granted approval to this enterprise in a <a href="/wiki/Papal_bull" title="Papal bull">Bull</a> of 17 December 1198,<sup id="cite_ref-moeller_5-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moeller-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> under the named of the <a href="/wiki/Trinitarian_Order" class="mw-redirect" title="Trinitarian Order">Order of the Holy Trinity for the Redemption of Captives</a>. Innocent appointed John of Matha superior-general and commissioned the Bishop of Paris and the Abbot of Saint Victor to draw up for the institute a rule, which he subsequently confirmed.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Felix returned to France to establish the Order. He was received with great enthusiasm, and <a href="/wiki/King_Philip_Augustus" class="mw-redirect" title="King Philip Augustus">King Philip Augustus</a> authorized the institute in France and fostered it by signal benefactions.<sup id="cite_ref-okane_4-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-okane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Margaret_of_Blois" class="mw-redirect" title="Margaret of Blois">Margaret of Blois</a> donated 20 acres (81,000&#160;m<sup>2</sup>) of the wood where Felix had built his first <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a>, and on almost the same spot he erected the famous Monastery of <a href="/wiki/Brumetz" title="Brumetz">Brumetz</a>, the motherhouse of the Order. Within forty years, the Order possessed six hundred monasteries in every part of Europe. John was obliged to go to Rome to found a house of the Order, the church of which, <a href="/wiki/Santa_Maria_in_Domnica" title="Santa Maria in Domnica">Santa Maria in Navicella</a>, still stands on the <a href="/wiki/Caelian_Hill" title="Caelian Hill">Caelian Hill</a>. Felix remained in France to look after the interests of the congregation. He founded a house in <a href="/wiki/Paris" title="Paris">Paris</a> attached to the Church of <a href="/w/index.php?title=Saint_Maturinus&amp;action=edit&amp;redlink=1" class="new" title="Saint Maturinus (page does not exist)">Saint Maturinus</a>, which afterwards became famous under Robert Guguin, master general of the order.<sup id="cite_ref-okane_4-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-okane-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Felix died amongst his fellow Trinitarians at their motherhouse in <a href="/wiki/Brumetz" title="Brumetz">Cerfroid</a> on November 4, 1212.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_2-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Valois&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Though no bull of his canonization is extant, it is the tradition of his institute that he was <a href="/wiki/Canonized" class="mw-redirect" title="Canonized">canonized</a> by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Urban_IV" title="Pope Urban IV">Pope Urban IV</a> on May 1, 1262. Du Plessis tells us that his feast was kept in the <a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Meaux" title="Roman Catholic Diocese of Meaux">Diocese of Meaux</a> as early as the year 1215. On October 21, 1666, <a href="/wiki/Pope_Alexander_VII" title="Pope Alexander VII">Pope Alexander VII</a> confirmed his status as a saint because of his immemorial cult.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> In 1679 Felix's feast was transferred to November 20 by <a href="/wiki/Pope_Innocent_XI" title="Pope Innocent XI">Pope Innocent XI</a>, when it was placed in the <a href="/wiki/General_Roman_Calendar" title="General Roman Calendar">General Roman Calendar</a> because, since 1613, November 4 was the feast day of <a href="/wiki/Charles_Borromeo" title="Charles Borromeo">Charles Borromeo</a><sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> In 1969, his feast was restored to November 4, his <i><a href="/wiki/Glossary_of_ancient_Roman_religion#dies_natalis" title="Glossary of ancient Roman religion">dies natalis</a></i>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Felix_of_Valois&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Legacy">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Saint-Felix-de-Valois is a village in the province of Quebec. St. Felix de Valois Parish is located in Bankstown, Australia.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
St. Felix Church in Clifton Springs, New York is named after him. It is now part of Saint Peter's Roman Catholic Parish in the Diocese of Rochester, New York. The current church building was built in 1895 and the name of the parish was changed at that time from St. Agnes to St. Felix by the pastor Felix O'Hanlon.
