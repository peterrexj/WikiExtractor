					<div id="mw-content-text" class="mw-body-content mw-content-ltr" lang="en" dir="ltr"><div class="mw-parser-output"><div class="shortdescription nomobile noexcerpt noprint searchaux" style="display:none">5th century deacon and first bishop of Ireland</div>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1033289096">.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+link+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}</style><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">For other people named Palladius, see <a href="/wiki/Palladius_(disambiguation)" class="mw-redirect mw-disambig" title="Palladius (disambiguation)">Palladius (disambiguation)</a>.</div>
<p class="mw-empty-elt">
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1066479718">.mw-parser-output .infobox-subbox{padding:0;border:none;margin:-3px;width:auto;min-width:100%;font-size:100%;clear:none;float:none;background-color:transparent}.mw-parser-output .infobox-3cols-child{margin:auto}.mw-parser-output .infobox .navbar{font-size:100%}body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-header,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-subheader,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-above,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-title,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-image,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-full-data,body.skin-minerva .mw-parser-output .infobox-below{text-align:center}</style><table class="infobox vcard"><tbody><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-above n" style="background-color:gold;"><div class="honorific-prefix" style="display:inline;font-size: 77%; font-weight: normal;"><a href="/wiki/Saint" title="Saint">Saint</a></div><br /><div style="display:inline;" class="fn">Palladius</div></th></tr><tr><th colspan="2" class="infobox-header" style="background-color:gold;">Bishop of Ireland</th></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Born</th><td class="infobox-data">Early 5th century</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Died</th><td class="infobox-data">457–461</td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label">Venerated&#160;in</th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Catholic_Church" title="Catholic Church">Catholic Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Church" title="Eastern Orthodox Church">Eastern Orthodox Church</a><br /><a href="/wiki/Anglican_Communion" title="Anglican Communion">Anglican Communion</a></td></tr><tr><th scope="row" class="infobox-label"><a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">Patronage</a></th><td class="infobox-data"><a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, <a href="/wiki/Kincardineshire" title="Kincardineshire">Kincardineshire</a></td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><b>Palladius</b> (<a href="/wiki/Floruit" title="Floruit">fl.</a> AD 408–431; died <abbr title="circa">c.</abbr> 457/461) was the first <a href="/wiki/Bishop" title="Bishop">bishop</a> of the <a href="/wiki/Christians" title="Christians">Christians</a> of <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Ireland</a>, preceding <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Saint Patrick</a>. It is possible that some elements of their life stories were later conflated in Irish tradition. Palladius was a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a> and member of one of the prominent families in <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>. <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_I" title="Pope Celestine I">Pope Celestine I</a> consecrated him a bishop and sent him to Ireland "to the <i>Scotti</i> believing in Christ"<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>.
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Armorica">Armorica</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Palladius_(bishop_of_Ireland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Armorica">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The Palladii were thought to be amongst the most noble families of <a href="/wiki/Gaul" title="Gaul">Gaul</a>, and several of them held high ranks in the <a href="/wiki/Christianity_in_Gaul" title="Christianity in Gaul">Church of Gaul</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-moran_2-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moran-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Palladius was the son of Exuperantius of <a href="/wiki/Poitiers" title="Poitiers">Poitiers</a>, of whom the contemporary pagan poet <a href="/wiki/Rutilius_Claudius_Namatianus" title="Rutilius Claudius Namatianus">Rutilius Claudius Namatianus</a> wrote on his 416 voyage: "Then on the eve of going I send back to his studies and the city Palladius, the hope and honour of my race. That eloquent youth had been sent of late from the lands of the Gauls to learn the laws of the Roman courts. My son in affection and kinsman by blood, he holds the fondest ties of my regard. Even now his father Exuperantius trains the <a href="/wiki/Armorica" title="Armorica">Armoric</a> sea-board to love the recovery of peace; he re-establishes the laws, brings freedom back and suffers not the inhabitants to be their servants' slaves."<sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup>  Exuperantius was apparently <i><a href="/wiki/Praefectus_praetorio_Galliarum" class="mw-redirect" title="Praefectus praetorio Galliarum">praefectus praetorio Galliarum</a></i> ("Praetorian prefect of the Gallic provinces") when he was killed in an army mutiny at <a href="/wiki/Arles" title="Arles">Arles</a> in 424.
</p><p>Palladius was married and had a young daughter. He is described as a friend and younger kinsman by <a href="/wiki/Rutilius_Claudius_Namatianus" title="Rutilius Claudius Namatianus">Namatianus</a>. In Rome, he kissed his family goodbye in the manner of the <a href="/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament" title="Apostles in the New Testament">Apostles</a>, and lived as an <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sicily" title="Sicily">Sicily</a> around 408/409, giving his daughter to a convent on that island. He seems to have been ordained as a <a href="/wiki/Priest" title="Priest">priest</a> around 415. He lived in Rome between 418&#8211;429,<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (February 2018)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup> and appears to be the "Deacon Palladius", responsible for urging <a href="/wiki/Pope_Celestine_I" title="Pope Celestine I">Pope Celestine I</a> to send the bishop <a href="/wiki/Germanus_of_Auxerre" title="Germanus of Auxerre">Germanus</a> to <a href="/wiki/Sub-Roman_Britain" title="Sub-Roman Britain">Britain</a>, where he guided "the Britons back to the Catholic faith."<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p><a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a> describes Palladius as a <a href="/wiki/Deacon" title="Deacon">deacon</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> Butler and P.F. Moran say that Palladius was a  Deacon of Rome, as it is unlikely that a deacon of Auxerre would exercise the influence in Rome that many have assigned to Palladius; and that it is in accordance with St Prosper's usage to indicate the Roman deacon by the simple title "diaconus."<sup id="cite_ref-moran_2-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moran-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Historian <a href="/wiki/Kathleen_Hughes_(historian)" title="Kathleen Hughes (historian)">Kathleen Hughes</a> regards it as more probable that he was a deacon of <a href="/wiki/Germanus_of_Auxerre" title="Germanus of Auxerre">St Germanus</a>, and that Germanus sent him to Rome,<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> where he received a commission from the Holy See for Germanus to travel to Britain in response to a request from the bishops there for assistance in combatting <a href="/wiki/Pelagianism" title="Pelagianism">Pelagianism</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-cusack_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cusack-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Ireland">Ireland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Palladius_(bishop_of_Ireland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Ireland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>In 431, "Palladius, having been ordained by Pope Celestine, is sent as first bishop to the <a href="/wiki/Ireland" title="Ireland">Scotti</a> believing in <a href="/wiki/Christ" class="mw-redirect" title="Christ">Christ</a>", according to the <i>Chronicle</i> of <a href="/wiki/Prosper_of_Aquitaine" title="Prosper of Aquitaine">Prosper of Aquitaine</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> Palladius landed at <a href="/wiki/Arklow" title="Arklow">Arklow</a>. <a href="/wiki/Auxilius_of_Ireland" title="Auxilius of Ireland">Auxilius</a>, <a href="/wiki/Secundinus" title="Secundinus">Secundinus</a>, and <a href="/wiki/Iserninus" title="Iserninus">Iserninus</a> are missioners identified with St. Patrick, but more recent research associates them not with Patrick but with Palladius.<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Irish writers who chronicled the life of St Patrick state that St Palladius preached in Ireland before St Patrick, although he was soon banished by the <a href="/wiki/List_of_kings_of_Leinster" title="List of kings of Leinster">King</a> of <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, and returned to North Britain.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_10-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup> According to <a href="/wiki/Muirchu_moccu_Machtheni" class="mw-redirect" title="Muirchu moccu Machtheni">Muirchu</a> (who lived two centuries later) in the <i><a href="/wiki/Book_of_Armagh" title="Book of Armagh">Book of Armagh</a></i>, <i>God hindered him...and neither did those fierce and cruel men receive his doctrine readily, nor did he himself wish to spend time in a strange land, but returned to him who sent him</i>.<sup id="cite_ref-moran_2-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-moran-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup> Palladius was accompanied by four companions: Sylvester and Solinus, who remained after him in Ireland, and Augustinus and Benedictus, who followed him to Britain but returned to their own country after his death.<sup id="cite_ref-cusack_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-cusack-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup> Palladius is most strongly associated with <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, particularly with <a href="/wiki/Clonard,_County_Meath" title="Clonard, County Meath">Clonard, County Meath</a>. 
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Scotland">Scotland</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Palladius_(bishop_of_Ireland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Scotland">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>According to <a href="/wiki/Muirchu_moccu_Machtheni" class="mw-redirect" title="Muirchu moccu Machtheni">Muirchu</a>, Palladius arrived among the Scots in North Britain after he left Ireland.<sup id="cite_ref-11" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>  Scottish church tradition holds that he presided over a Christian community there for about 20 years. A cluster of dedications in the <a href="/wiki/Kincardineshire" title="Kincardineshire">Mearns</a> in <a href="/wiki/Scotland" title="Scotland">Scotland</a>, in the village of <a href="/wiki/Auchenblae" title="Auchenblae">Auchenblae</a>, are believed to mark his last resting place. As late as the reign of <a href="/wiki/James_V" class="mw-redirect" title="James V">James V</a>, royal funds were disbursed for the fabrication of a new reliquary for the church there, and an annual "Paldy Fair" was held at least until the time of the <a href="/wiki/Protestant_Reformation" class="mw-redirect" title="Protestant Reformation">Reformation</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Death">Death</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Palladius_(bishop_of_Ireland)&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Death">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>His date of death is unknown; however, the <a href="/wiki/Annals_of_Ulster" title="Annals of Ulster">Annals of Ulster</a> contain the following references:
</p>
<ul><li>457 "Repose of the elder Patrick, as some books state"</li>
<li>461 "Here some record the repose of Patrick"</li>
<li>492 "The Irish state here that Patrick the Archbishop died."</li>
<li>493 "Patrick... apostle of the Irish, rested on the 16th day before the Kalends of April..."</li></ul>
<p>Thus, it is possible that later writers confused Palladius and <a href="/wiki/Saint_Patrick" title="Saint Patrick">Patrick</a>. If the earlier dates of 457/461 indeed refer to him, then it seems that the actual St Patrick died much later about 492/493. Patrick's mission was largely confined to <a href="/wiki/Ulster" title="Ulster">Ulster</a> and <a href="/wiki/Connacht" title="Connacht">Connacht</a>, while Palladius seems to have been active in <a href="/wiki/Leinster" title="Leinster">Leinster</a>, particularly in the area around <a href="/wiki/Clonarney_(civil_parish)" title="Clonarney (civil parish)">Clonard</a>.
</p><p>The <a href="/wiki/Vita_tripartita" class="mw-redirect" title="Vita tripartita">Vita tripartita</a> states that he died at <i>Cell Fine</i> (thought to be modern-day <a href="/wiki/Killeen_Cormac" title="Killeen Cormac">Killeen Cormac</a>, <a href="/wiki/County_Kildare" title="County Kildare">County Kildare</a>), where he left his books, writing tablet and relics of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Peter" title="Saint Peter">Peter</a> and <a href="/wiki/Paul_the_Apostle" title="Paul the Apostle">Paul</a>. Alban Butler, citing <a href="/wiki/Hector_Boethius" class="mw-redirect" title="Hector Boethius">Hector Boethius</a> and Camden, says that he died at Fordun, fifteen miles south of <a href="/wiki/Aberdeen" title="Aberdeen">Aberdeen</a>, around the year 450.<sup id="cite_ref-butler_10-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-butler-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
