<p><b>Sir John Joseph Caldwell Abbott</b> <span class="noexcerpt nowraplinks" style="font-size:85%; font-weight:normal;"><a href="/wiki/King%27s_Privy_Council_for_Canada" title="King&#39;s Privy Council for Canada">PC</a>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Queen%27s_Counsel" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen&#39;s Counsel">QC</a>&#32;<a href="/wiki/Knight_Commander_of_the_Order_of_St_Michael_and_St_George" class="mw-redirect" title="Knight Commander of the Order of St Michael and St George">KCMG</a></span> (March 12, 1821&#160;– October 30, 1893) was a Canadian lawyer and politician who served as the third <a href="/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Canada" class="mw-redirect" title="Prime minister of Canada">prime minister of Canada</a> from 1891 to 1892. He held office as the leader of the <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada_(1867%E2%80%931942)" title="Conservative Party of Canada (1867–1942)">Conservative Party</a>.
</p><p>Abbott was born in what is now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Andr%C3%A9-d%27Argenteuil,_Quebec" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-André-d&#39;Argenteuil, Quebec">Saint-André-d'Argenteuil</a>, Quebec. He studied law at <a href="/wiki/McGill_University" title="McGill University">McGill University</a> and became one of <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>'s best-known lawyers, later returning to McGill as a professor of law and earning a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Civil_Law" title="Doctor of Civil Law">Doctor of Civil Law</a> degree. He was perhaps best known for his successful defence of the perpetrators of the <a href="/wiki/St._Albans_Raid" title="St. Albans Raid">St. Albans Raid</a>. Abbott involved himself in politics from a young age, signing the <a href="/wiki/Montreal_Annexation_Manifesto" title="Montreal Annexation Manifesto">Montreal Annexation Manifesto</a> in 1849&#160;&#8211;&#32;which he later regretted&#160;&#8211;&#32;and winning election to the <a href="/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_the_Province_of_Canada" title="Legislative Assembly of the Province of Canada">Legislative Assembly of the Province of Canada</a> in 1860. In the lead-up to <a href="/wiki/Confederation_of_Canada" class="mw-redirect" title="Confederation of Canada">Confederation</a> he was a prominent advocate for the rights of <a href="/wiki/English-speaking_Quebecers" title="English-speaking Quebecers">English-speaking Quebecers</a>.
</p><p>In the <a href="/wiki/1867_Canadian_federal_election" title="1867 Canadian federal election">1867 federal election</a>, Abbott was elected to the new <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada" title="House of Commons of Canada">House of Commons of Canada</a> as a member of the <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada_(1867%E2%80%931942)" title="Conservative Party of Canada (1867–1942)">Conservative Party</a>. A telegram leaked from his office played a key part in the <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Scandal" title="Pacific Scandal">Pacific Scandal</a> of 1873, which led to the downfall of <a href="/wiki/John_A._Macdonald" title="John A. Macdonald">John A. Macdonald</a>'s first government. Abbott was appointed to the <a href="/wiki/Senate_of_Canada" title="Senate of Canada">Senate</a> in 1887, in order to become <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Government_in_the_Senate_(Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Leader of the Government in the Senate (Canada)">leader of the Government in the Senate</a>. He became prime minister in June 1891 following Macdonald's death in office.  He was the first native-born Canadian prime minister, both Macdonald and <a href="/wiki/Alexander_Mackenzie_(politician)" title="Alexander Mackenzie (politician)">Alexander Mackenzie</a> having been born in Scotland. Abbott was 70 years old at the time, and served only until November 1892 when he retired due to ill health. He died the following year.<sup id="cite_ref-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" />
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_life">Early life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Early life"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abbott was born in St. Andrews, <a href="/wiki/Lower_Canada" title="Lower Canada">Lower Canada</a> (now <a href="/wiki/Saint-Andr%C3%A9-d%27Argenteuil,_Quebec" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint-André-d&#39;Argenteuil, Quebec">Saint-André-d'Argenteuil</a>, Quebec), to Harriet (née Bradford) and the Rev. <a href="/wiki/Joseph_Abbott_(Canadian_priest)" title="Joseph Abbott (Canadian priest)">Joseph Abbott</a>, an <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> <a href="/wiki/Missionary" title="Missionary">missionary</a> from <a href="/wiki/Little_Strickland" title="Little Strickland">Little Strickland</a>, England. In 1849, Abbott married <a href="/wiki/Mary_Bethune_Abbott" title="Mary Bethune Abbott">Mary Martha Bethune</a> (1823–1898), a relative of Dr. <a href="/wiki/Norman_Bethune" title="Norman Bethune">Norman Bethune</a>, a daughter of <a href="/wiki/Anglican" class="mw-redirect" title="Anglican">Anglican</a> <a href="/wiki/Clergyman" class="mw-redirect" title="Clergyman">clergyman</a> and <a href="/wiki/McGill_University" title="McGill University">McGill</a> acting president <a href="/wiki/John_Bethune_(principal)" title="John Bethune (principal)">John Bethune</a>, and a granddaughter of the <a href="/wiki/Presbyterian" class="mw-redirect" title="Presbyterian">Presbyterian</a> minister <a href="/wiki/John_Bethune_(Canadian_minister)" title="John Bethune (Canadian minister)">John Bethune</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup><sup id="cite_ref-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The couple had four sons and four daughters, many of whom died without descendants. Their eldest surviving son, William Abbott, married the daughter of Colonel <a href="/wiki/John_Hamilton_Gray_(Prince_Edward_Island_politician)" title="John Hamilton Gray (Prince Edward Island politician)">John Hamilton Gray</a>, a <a href="/wiki/Father_of_Confederation" class="mw-redirect" title="Father of Confederation">Father of Confederation</a> and <a href="/wiki/Premier_(Canada)" title="Premier (Canada)">premier</a> of <a href="/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island" title="Prince Edward Island">Prince Edward Island</a>. Abbott was also the great-grandfather of <a href="/wiki/Canadians" title="Canadians">Canadian</a> actor <a href="/wiki/Christopher_Plummer" title="Christopher Plummer">Christopher Plummer</a> and the first cousin (once removed) of <a href="/wiki/Maude_Abbott" title="Maude Abbott">Maude Abbott</a>, one of <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>'s earliest female medical graduates and an expert on congenital heart disease.
</p><p>Abbott was an <a href="/wiki/Orange_Order_in_Canada" title="Orange Order in Canada">Orangeman</a> and <a href="/wiki/Freemason" class="mw-redirect" title="Freemason">Freemason</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-stjohnslodgedc.org_4-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-stjohnslodgedc.org-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Military_service">Military service</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Military service"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Abbott had served in the local militia “since boyhood”, being appointed an Ensign in the 2nd Montreal Militia Battalion in 1847.<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> In 1849 he was a signatory to the <a href="/wiki/Montreal_Annexation_Manifesto" title="Montreal Annexation Manifesto">Montreal Annexation Manifesto</a>, calling for union of the Canadas with the United States, resulting in the withdrawal of his commission in the militia. By 1850 however, he was reinstated and appointed a Captain in the 4th Montreal Militia Battalion.<sup id="cite_ref-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His recruitment of a battalion of 300 men, known as the <a href="/wiki/17th_Duke_of_York%27s_Royal_Canadian_Hussars#11th_Regiment_Argenteuil_Rangers" title="17th Duke of York&#39;s Royal Canadian Hussars">11th Argenteuil Rangers</a>, during the <a href="/wiki/Trent_Affair" title="Trent Affair">Trent Affair</a> of 1861 may have been designed to atone for what he later described as the “sins of youth” and to enhance his political credentials, as much as to express his concern for his country's safety. On March 14, 1862, he was appointed Major in the <a href="/wiki/17th_Duke_of_York%27s_Royal_Canadian_Hussars#11th_Regiment_Argenteuil_Rangers" title="17th Duke of York&#39;s Royal Canadian Hussars">11th (Argenteuil Rifles) Volunteer Militia</a>, being promoted to Lieutenant-Colonel on March 21.<sup id="cite_ref-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>He commanded the regiment on the border multiple times throughout the <a href="/wiki/Fenian_Raids" class="mw-redirect" title="Fenian Raids">Fenian Raids</a>, on March 8, 1866, they were called out for active service and were stationed in various villages throughout <a href="/wiki/Argenteuil_Regional_County_Municipality" title="Argenteuil Regional County Municipality">Argenteuil</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-8" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-8">&#91;8&#93;</a></sup> On June 11, 1866, the Rangers were called out to serve at <a href="/wiki/Cornwall,_Ontario" title="Cornwall, Ontario">Cornwall</a>, and then <a href="/wiki/Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu" title="Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu">Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu</a>, and a reporter from Montreal stated: 
</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1211633275">.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 32px}.mw-parser-output .templatequote .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;padding-left:1.6em;margin-top:0}</style><blockquote class="templatequote"><p>"I learned on Sunday afternoon that troops were to be sent to St. Johns [Saint-Jean] by special train; and managed to procure permission to come out with them. The troops sent forward were a part of the force recently garrisoning Cornwall, a portion of the 25th Regt. under Col. Fan, and the Argenteuil Rangers under Lieut. Col. The Hon. J.J.C. Abbott."<sup id="cite_ref-9" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-9">&#91;9&#93;</a></sup></p></blockquote>
<p>Abbott retired from the militia as a Lieutenant Colonel, commanding the 11th Battalion, on June 22, 1883.
</p><p>Taunted by his political opponents in March 1889 for his “disloyalty” in 1849, he explained that he considered his military service, and his commission as an officer and later commanding officer of the 11th Argenteuil Battalion of militia, to be evidence that his youthful error had been forgiven.<sup id="cite_ref-10" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-10">&#91;10&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legal_career">Legal career</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Legal career"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>Abbott graduated as a <a href="/wiki/Bachelor_of_Civil_Law" title="Bachelor of Civil Law">Bachelor of Civil Law</a> from McGill College (now <a href="/wiki/McGill_University" title="McGill University">McGill University</a>) in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a> in 1847,<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> and in the same year was initiated in the St. Paul's <a href="/wiki/Masonic_Lodge" class="mw-redirect" title="Masonic Lodge">Masonic Lodge</a>, No. 374, E.R., in <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-12" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-12">&#91;12&#93;</a></sup> In 1867, he graduated as a <a href="/wiki/Doctor_of_Civil_Law" title="Doctor of Civil Law">Doctor of Civil Law</a> (DCL). Most of his legal practice was in <a href="/wiki/Corporate_law" title="Corporate law">corporate law</a>; however, his most celebrated court case was the defence of at first fourteen, then upon release and recapture, four of those fourteen <a href="/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America" title="Confederate States of America">Confederate</a> agents who had <a href="/wiki/St._Albans_Raid" title="St. Albans Raid">raided St. Albans</a>, Vermont, from Canadian soil during the <a href="/wiki/American_Civil_War" title="American Civil War">American Civil War</a>. Abbott successfully argued that the Confederates were belligerents rather than criminals and therefore should not be extradited. He began lecturing in <a href="/wiki/Commercial_law" title="Commercial law">commercial</a> and <a href="/wiki/Criminal_law" title="Criminal law">criminal law</a> at <a href="/wiki/McGill_University" title="McGill University">McGill</a> in 1853, and in 1855 he became a professor and <a href="/wiki/Dean_(education)" title="Dean (education)">dean</a> of its Faculty of Law, where <a href="/wiki/Wilfrid_Laurier" title="Wilfrid Laurier">Wilfrid Laurier</a>, a future prime minister of <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a>, was among his students. He continued in this position until 1880.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> In 1862, he was made <a href="/wiki/Queen%27s_Counsel" class="mw-redirect" title="Queen&#39;s Counsel">Queen's Counsel</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Upon his retirement, <a href="/wiki/McGill_University" title="McGill University">McGill</a> named him emeritus professor, and in 1881 appointed him to its Board of Governors.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Politics">Politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Politics"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<link rel="mw-deduplicated-inline-style" href="mw-data:TemplateStyles:r1033289096"><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable">See also: <a href="/wiki/Electoral_history_of_John_Abbott" title="Electoral history of John Abbott">Electoral history of John Abbott</a></div>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_involvement">Early involvement</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Early involvement"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>In 1849, he signed the <a href="/wiki/Montreal_Annexation_Manifesto" title="Montreal Annexation Manifesto">Montreal Annexation Manifesto</a> calling for <a href="/wiki/Canada" title="Canada">Canada</a> to join the <a href="/wiki/United_States" title="United States">United States</a>, an action which he regretted later as a youthful error. He eventually joined the <a href="/wiki/Loyal_Orange_Lodge" class="mw-redirect" title="Loyal Orange Lodge">Loyal Orange Lodge</a> of <a href="/wiki/British_North_America" title="British North America">British North America</a>, well known as a pro-British organization. Abbott first ran for <a href="/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada" title="Parliament of Canada">Canada's Legislative Assembly</a> in 1857 in the <a href="/wiki/Argenteuil_(electoral_district)" class="mw-redirect" title="Argenteuil (electoral district)">Argenteuil</a> district, northwest of <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-3" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> Defeated, he challenged the election results on the grounds of voting list irregularities and was eventually awarded the seat in 1860. He served as solicitor general for <a href="/wiki/Lower_Canada" title="Lower Canada">Lower Canada</a> (Quebec) representing the liberal administration of <a href="/wiki/John_Sandfield_Macdonald" title="John Sandfield Macdonald">John Sandfield Macdonald</a> and <a href="/wiki/Louis-Victor_Sicotte" title="Louis-Victor Sicotte">Louis Sicotte</a>, from 1862 until 1863.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He reluctantly supported Canada's confederation, fearing the reduction of the political power of <a href="/wiki/Lower_Canada" title="Lower Canada">Lower Canada</a>'s English-speaking minority. In 1865, he converted to a conservative.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> His proposal to protect the electoral borders of 12 English Quebec constituencies was eventually incorporated into the <a href="/wiki/Constitution_Act,_1867" title="Constitution Act, 1867"><i>British North America Act, 1867</i></a>.<sup class="noprint Inline-Template Template-Fact" style="white-space:nowrap;">&#91;<i><a href="/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed" title="Wikipedia:Citation needed"><span title="This claim needs references to reliable sources. (April 2021)">citation needed</span></a></i>&#93;</sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="National_politics">National politics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: National politics"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Abbott was elected to the <a href="/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada" title="House of Commons of Canada">House of Commons</a> in 1867 as member for Argenteuil. He was removed from his seat by petition in 1874 following his involvement in the <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Scandal" title="Pacific Scandal">Pacific Scandal</a>. He narrowly lost the <a href="/wiki/1878_Canadian_federal_election" title="1878 Canadian federal election">1878 election</a>, then won in February 1880, only to have his victory declared void because of bribery allegations. He was, however, subsequently elected in a by-election in August 1881. In 1887, <a href="/wiki/John_A._Macdonald" title="John A. Macdonald">Macdonald</a> appointed him to the <a href="/wiki/Senate_of_Canada" title="Senate of Canada">Senate</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-6" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup> He served as <a href="/wiki/Leader_of_the_Government_in_the_Senate_(Canada)" class="mw-redirect" title="Leader of the Government in the Senate (Canada)">Leader of the Government in the Senate</a> from May 12, 1887, to October 30, 1893 (including his term as Prime Minister) and as <a href="/wiki/Minister_without_Portfolio" class="mw-redirect" title="Minister without Portfolio">Minister without Portfolio</a> in <a href="/wiki/John_A._Macdonald" title="John A. Macdonald">Macdonald's</a> cabinet. He also served two one-year terms as <a href="/wiki/Mayor_of_Montreal" title="Mayor of Montreal">mayor of Montreal</a> from 1887 to 1889.
</p>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:SirJohnAbbott2.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/SirJohnAbbott2.jpg/220px-SirJohnAbbott2.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="286" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/SirJohnAbbott2.jpg/330px-SirJohnAbbott2.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/aa/SirJohnAbbott2.jpg 2x" data-file-width="369" data-file-height="480" /></a><figcaption>Abbott as Mayor of Montreal.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Abbott was involved in the promotion of several railway projects, including the <a href="/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway" title="Canadian Pacific Railway">Canadian Pacific Railway</a>  (CPR) (of which he served as president). He worked to incorporate and arrange financing for the first CPR syndicate. As legal advisor to its main financier, Sir <a href="/wiki/Hugh_Allan" title="Hugh Allan">Hugh Allan</a>, Abbott was the recipient of the infamous telegram from Prime Minister Macdonald during the <a href="/wiki/1872_Canadian_federal_election" title="1872 Canadian federal election">1872 Canadian federal election</a> campaign which read "I must have another ten thousand; will be the last time of calling; do not fail me; answer today." This telegram was stolen from Abbott's office and published, breaking the 1873 <a href="/wiki/Pacific_Scandal" title="Pacific Scandal">Pacific Scandal</a> which brought down Macdonald's government. Abbott was subsequently a key organizer of a second syndicate which eventually completed the construction of Canada's first transcontinental railway in 1885, serving as its solicitor from 1880 to 1887 and as a director from 1885 to 1891.
</p>

<p>When Prime Minister Macdonald died in office, Abbott supported <a href="/wiki/John_Sparrow_David_Thompson" title="John Sparrow David Thompson">John Thompson</a> to succeed him, but reluctantly accepted the plea of the divided <a href="/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada_(historical)" class="mw-redirect" title="Conservative Party of Canada (historical)">Conservative party</a> that he should lead the government, though he considered himself a <a href="/wiki/Caretaker_government" title="Caretaker government">caretaker</a> prime minister for his seventeen months in office. He was one of just two Canadian Prime Ministers, the other being <a href="/wiki/Mackenzie_Bowell" title="Mackenzie Bowell">Mackenzie Bowell</a>, to have held the office while serving in the Senate rather than the House of Commons.<sup id="cite_ref-13" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-13">&#91;13&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Soon after Abbott assumed office in 1891, Canada was plunged into an economic recession; later that same year he faced another challenge as the McGreevy-Langevin scandal came to light, revealing that <a href="/wiki/Hector-Louis_Langevin" title="Hector-Louis Langevin">Hector-Louis Langevin</a>, former Minister of Public Works in the Conservative government, had conspired with contractor <a href="/wiki/Thomas_McGreevy" title="Thomas McGreevy">Thomas McGreevy</a> to defraud the government.
</p><p>Despite the political toll on his party, Abbott dealt with the backlog of government business awaiting him after Macdonald's death, including reform of the civil service and revisions of the criminal code. He attempted in 1892 to negotiate a new treaty of reciprocity with the United States, but failed to reach an agreement.
</p><p>During his term, there were 52 by-elections, 42 of which were won by the Conservatives, increasing their majority by 13 seats—evidence of Abbott's effectiveness as prime minister. One year into his time as prime minister, Abbott attempted to turn the office over to Thompson, but this was rejected due to anti-<a href="/wiki/Roman_Catholicism" class="mw-redirect" title="Roman Catholicism">Catholic</a> sentiment in the Tory caucus.<sup id="cite_ref-EB_11-7" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-EB-11">&#91;11&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Later_life">Later life</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Later life"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-left" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg/220px-Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="293" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg/330px-Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg/440px-Funeral_monument_John_Joseph_Abbott.jpg 2x" data-file-width="3024" data-file-height="4032" /></a><figcaption>Abbott's funeral monument in <a href="/wiki/Mount_Royal_Cemetery" title="Mount Royal Cemetery">Mount Royal Cemetery</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Suffering from the early stages of cancer of the brain, Abbott's health failed in 1892 and he retired to private life, whereupon <a href="/wiki/John_Sparrow_Thompson" class="mw-redirect" title="John Sparrow Thompson">Sir John Thompson</a> finally became Prime Minister. Abbott died less than a year later at the age of 72.
</p><p>Sir John Abbott is buried in the <a href="/wiki/Mount_Royal_Cemetery" title="Mount Royal Cemetery">Mount Royal Cemetery</a>, <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-Parks_Grave_14-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Parks_Grave-14">&#91;14&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legacy">Legacy</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=John_Abbott&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Legacy"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street,_Montreal.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street%2C_Montreal.jpg/220px-Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street%2C_Montreal.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="152" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street%2C_Montreal.jpg/330px-Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street%2C_Montreal.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street%2C_Montreal.jpg/440px-Sherbrooke_at_Stanley_Street%2C_Montreal.jpg 2x" data-file-width="743" data-file-height="514" /></a><figcaption>Sir John Abbott's house on <a href="/wiki/Sherbrooke_Street" title="Sherbrooke Street">Sherbrooke Street</a>, <a href="/wiki/Montreal" title="Montreal">Montreal</a>, <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a></figcaption></figure>
<p><a href="/wiki/John_Abbott_College" title="John Abbott College">John Abbott College</a> in <a href="/wiki/Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue,_Quebec" class="mw-redirect" title="Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue, Quebec">Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue</a>, <a href="/wiki/Quebec" title="Quebec">Quebec</a>, near Abbott's 300-acre (1.2&#160;km<sup>2</sup>) country estate (Boisbriant), is named after him.
</p><p>He was named a <a href="/wiki/Persons_of_National_Historic_Significance" title="Persons of National Historic Significance">Person of National Historic Significance</a> by the <a href="/wiki/Government_of_Canada" title="Government of Canada">Government of Canada</a> in 1938.<sup id="cite_ref-15" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-15">&#91;15&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>His "most memorable" political comment is "I hate politics."<sup id="cite_ref-Duffy_16-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-Duffy-16">&#91;16&#93;</a></sup> The full quote was "I hate politics and what are considered their appropriate measures. I hate notoriety, public meetings, public speeches, caucuses and everything that I know of which is apparently the necessary incident of politics—except doing public work to the best of my ability."<sup id="cite_ref-17" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-17">&#91;17&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>In their 1999 look at the <a href="/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Canada" title="List of prime ministers of Canada">Canadian prime ministers</a> through <a href="/wiki/Jean_Chr%C3%A9tien" title="Jean Chrétien">Jean Chrétien</a>, <a href="/wiki/J.L._Granatstein" class="mw-redirect" title="J.L. Granatstein">J.L. Granatstein</a> and <a href="/wiki/Norman_Hillmer" title="Norman Hillmer">Norman Hillmer</a> included a survey of Canadian historians ranking the prime ministers. Abbott's term of service was considered below par and was ranked #17 out of 20 (up to then).<sup id="cite_ref-18" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-18">&#91;18&#93;</a></sup> When the survey was repeated in 2016, Abbott was ranked 7th out of ten "short-term" prime ministers with a score of 1.8 out of 5.<sup id="cite_ref-19" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-19">&#91;19&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
