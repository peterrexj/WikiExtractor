<p><b>Saint Helier</b> (died 555) was a 6th-century ascetic <a href="/wiki/Hermit" title="Hermit">hermit</a>. He is the <a href="/wiki/Patron_saint" title="Patron saint">patron saint</a> of <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a> in the <a href="/wiki/Channel_Islands" title="Channel Islands">Channel Islands</a>, and in particular of the town and <a href="/wiki/Parish" title="Parish">parish</a> of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Helier" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Helier">Saint Helier</a>, the island's <a href="/wiki/Capital_(political)" class="mw-redirect" title="Capital (political)">capital</a>. He is also invoked as a healing saint for diseases of the skin and eyes.
</p>

<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Legend">Legend</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Legend">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Early_years">Early years</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Early years">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Hellerius or Helier was born to <a href="/wiki/Paganism" title="Paganism">pagan</a> parents in <a href="/wiki/Tongeren" title="Tongeren">Tongeren</a> (now in <a href="/wiki/Belgium" title="Belgium">Belgium</a>). His father was Sigebert, a nobleman from Tongres and his mother was Lusigard.<sup id="cite_ref-post_1-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-post-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Having had difficulties conceiving a child, they turned to a Christian teacher named Cunibert, who advised them to pray to God and that when they had a child they must hand him over to God, and that he, Cunibert, would bring him up in the <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christian</a> faith.<sup id="cite_ref-post_1-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-post-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup> Their prayers having been answered, Helier was born, but Helier's father, the <a href="/wiki/Franks" title="Franks">Frankish</a> governor of that place, eventually grew angry at the influence Cunibert exerted over his precocious son, who was already causing consternation with his youthful miracles. Helier's father had Cunibert killed, whereupon Helier fled.
</p><p>Helier's wanderings led him to the <a href="/wiki/Cotentin" class="mw-redirect" title="Cotentin">Cotentin</a> where he sought retreat from the distractions of the world in the <a href="/wiki/Monastery" title="Monastery">monastic</a> community of <a href="/wiki/Saint_Marcouf" class="mw-redirect" title="Saint Marcouf">Saint Marculf</a> at Nantus (Nanteuil, now <a href="/wiki/%C3%8Eles_Saint-Marcouf" title="Îles Saint-Marcouf">St.-Marcouf-de-l'Isle</a> in <a href="/wiki/Manche" title="Manche">Manche</a>).<sup id="cite_ref-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-2">&#91;2&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Jersey">Jersey</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: Jersey">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg/220px-Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="273" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg/330px-Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg/440px-Hermitage_St_Helier_Jersey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1041" data-file-height="1290" /></a><figcaption>A medieval chapel was constructed over "St. Helier's Bed", the hollow in the rock where Helier sheltered. The Hermitage rock is the focus of the annual pilgrimage</figcaption></figure>
<p>Helier, however, found the <a href="/wiki/Monasticism" title="Monasticism">monastic</a> community did not provide the quiet he required to devote himself fully to a life of contemplation. Marculf had received pleas from the few inhabitants of the island called Gersut, or Agna, now called <a href="/wiki/Jersey" title="Jersey">Jersey</a>, which was all but depopulated due to repeated attacks by <a href="/wiki/Viking" class="mw-redirect" title="Viking">Vikings</a>.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_3-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> The inhabitants requested someone to help them, and bring the <a href="/wiki/Gospel" title="Gospel">gospel</a> to them as they had no shepherd to guide them.
</p><p>Marculf sent Helier, and a companion Saint Romard, to Jersey where he found a small community of fishermen on the sand dunes where the modern town of St Helier was to develop. Helier settled on a tidal islet, nowadays known as the Hermitage Rock, next to L'Islet,<sup id="cite_ref-4" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-4">&#91;4&#93;</a></sup> the <a href="/wiki/Tidal_island" title="Tidal island">tidal island</a> now occupied by the 16th century <a href="/wiki/Elizabeth_Castle" title="Elizabeth Castle">Elizabeth Castle</a>. Romard would travel back and forth between the <a href="/wiki/Hermitage_(religious_retreat)" title="Hermitage (religious retreat)">hermitage</a> on this rock and the fishing village.
</p><p>From his vantage point on his rock, Helier could see the sails of approaching attackers and would signal to the shore, whereupon the inhabitants would scatter into the surrounding marshes, thereby frustrating the attackers' bloodlust. Small dark clouds on the horizon are still known in <a href="/wiki/J%C3%A8rriais" title="Jèrriais">Jèrriais</a> as <i>les vailes dé St. Hélyi</i> (the sails of St. Helier).
</p><p>Helier remained at his hermitage in fasting and prayer for about fifteen years. The story is that around 555 he was martyred<sup id="cite_ref-5" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-5">&#91;5&#93;</a></sup> by marauding pirates who beheaded him with an axe – hence the crossed axes on the parish crest.<sup id="cite_ref-post_1-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-post-1">&#91;1&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Miracles">Miracles</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Miracles">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Helier is recorded as performing one healing miracle in Jersey, curing a lame man named Anquetil.
</p><p>Once while Marculf was visiting, a band of raiders arrived. They prayed and made the sign of the cross and a great storm arose that destroyed the raiders ships.<sup id="cite_ref-bbc_3-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bbc-3">&#91;3&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Though Helier starved himself to <a href="/wiki/Asceticism" title="Asceticism">ascetic</a> weakness, legend holds that he had the strength, when he was beheaded by attackers, to pick up his head and walk to shore. According to the hagiography, Romard discovered Helier's body on the beach still clutching his head in his hands, placed it in a boat and set off for the mainland. The boat, guided by the hand of God, arrived at <a href="/wiki/Br%C3%A9ville-sur-Mer" title="Bréville-sur-Mer">Bréville-sur-Mer</a> where a reputedly miraculous healing <a href="/wiki/Spring_(hydrosphere)" class="mw-redirect" title="Spring (hydrosphere)">spring</a> arose on the spot where Helier's body rested overnight. A church was founded next to the spring, which is now topped by a statue and still attracts those seeking a cure.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Veneration">Veneration</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Veneration">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg/220px-Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="135" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg/330px-Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg/440px-Helier_pilgrimage_2005_Jersey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1573" data-file-height="962" /></a><figcaption>In 2005, the tides necessitated an early morning start to the pilgrimage.</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg/220px-Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="210" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg/330px-Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg/440px-Helier_pilgrimage_eucharist_2005_Jersey.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1242" data-file-height="1187" /></a><figcaption>Helier pilgrimage eucharist 2005</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="mw-default-size mw-halign-right" typeof="mw:File/Thumb"><a href="/wiki/File:Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg" class="mw-file-description"><img src="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg/220px-Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg" decoding="async" width="220" height="81" class="mw-file-element" srcset="//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg/330px-Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg 1.5x, //upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg/440px-Saint_H%C3%A9lyi_p%C3%A8lerinnage_2009_13.jpg 2x" data-file-width="1588" data-file-height="583" /></a><figcaption>Pilgrimage in 2009.</figcaption></figure>
<p>Marculf founded an abbey on L'Islet not far from Helier's hermitage and named it for his pupil. It was later visited by <a href="/wiki/Samson_of_Dol" title="Samson of Dol">Samson of Dol</a>.
</p><p>Churches dedicated to Helier can be found in <a href="/wiki/Rennes" title="Rennes">Rennes</a>, St. Helier, <a href="/wiki/Beuzeville" title="Beuzeville">Beuzeville</a> (<a href="/wiki/Eure" title="Eure">Eure</a>), Amécourt (Eure), Barentin (Seine-Maritime), Monhoudou (<a href="/wiki/Sarthe" title="Sarthe">Sarthe</a>). Evidence of veneration of the saint can be found in <a href="/wiki/La_Hague" title="La Hague">La Hague</a> in the Cotentin at <a href="/wiki/Querqueville" title="Querqueville">Querqueville</a> and also at Omonville-la-Rogue where a 13th-century <a href="/wiki/Mural" title="Mural">mural</a> in the church of St. John the Baptist links Helier with <a href="/wiki/Thomas_Becket" title="Thomas Becket">Thomas Becket</a>.
</p><p>Helier is remembered in Jersey for having brought <a href="/wiki/Christianity" title="Christianity">Christianity</a> to the island, but is better known in <a href="/wiki/Normandy" title="Normandy">Normandy</a> and <a href="/wiki/Brittany" title="Brittany">Brittany</a> as a healing saint. Besides the healing springs at St. Hellier and Bréville, there is also a healing spring at <a href="/wiki/Saint-Jouan-des-Gu%C3%A9rets" title="Saint-Jouan-des-Guérets">Saint-Jouan-des-Guérets</a> (<a href="/wiki/Ille-et-Vilaine" title="Ille-et-Vilaine">Ille-et-Vilaine</a>), where Helier's name has been deformed by <a href="/wiki/Folk_etymology" title="Folk etymology">folk etymology</a> to <i>St. Délier</i> (<i>délier</i> meaning to untie in <a href="/wiki/French_language" title="French language">French</a>, which may refer to the power to loosen the bonds of illness). There is also a <a href="/wiki/Chapel" title="Chapel">chapel</a> of St. Helier in the cathedral of <a href="/wiki/Trenton,_New_Jersey" title="Trenton, New Jersey">Trenton, New Jersey</a>.
</p><p>The traditional year of his martyrdom is AD 555. His <a href="/wiki/Feast_day" class="mw-redirect" title="Feast day">feast day</a>, marked in Jersey by an annual municipal and ecumenical <a href="/wiki/Pilgrimage" title="Pilgrimage">pilgrimage</a> to the Hermitage, is on July 16.<sup id="cite_ref-societe_6-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-societe-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> The Hermitage is depicted on the <a href="/wiki/Coins_of_the_Jersey_pound" class="mw-redirect" title="Coins of the Jersey pound">Jersey 2 pence coin</a> and on the 2010 issue <a href="/wiki/Jersey_pound" title="Jersey pound">Jersey £10 note</a>.
</p><p>In 1870 Hermitage Rock was joined to L'Islet (Elizabeth Castle) by a breakwater.
</p>
<h3><span class="mw-headline" id="Relics">Relics</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Relics">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h3>
<p>Helier's relics were sent to the abbey of Bellus-Beccus at <a href="/wiki/Beaubec-la-Rosi%C3%A8re" title="Beaubec-la-Rosière">Beaubec-la-Rosière</a> (Seine-Maritime)) where they remained until the destruction of the <a href="/wiki/Abbey" title="Abbey">abbey</a> during the <a href="/wiki/French_Revolution" title="French Revolution">French Revolution</a>.
</p>
<h2><span class="mw-headline" id="Commentary">Commentary</span><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=Helier&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Commentary">edit</a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></h2>
<p>The <i>Life of Saint Helier</i> was written in, or after the 10th century.
</p><p>G.R. Balleine was critical of the Passion of St. Helier, noting that "its chronology is absurd. St. Helier was born, we are told, 'after the death of wicked <a href="/wiki/Brunhilda_of_Austrasia" title="Brunhilda of Austrasia">Queen Brunehild</a>, when <a href="/wiki/Childebert_III_of_the_Franks" class="mw-redirect" title="Childebert III of the Franks">Childebert governed the Francs</a>'. This must be <a href="/wiki/Childebert_III" title="Childebert III">Childebert III</a>, who came to the throne in 693. But Helier became a disciple of St. Marculf, who died in 558; and 'according to one account he was buried by the famous eighth century <a href="/wiki/Willibrord" title="Willibrord">Bishop Willebrod</a>.' In other words he was baptised 150 years before he was born, and buried, while still a young man, two hundred years later."<sup id="cite_ref-societe_6-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-societe-6">&#91;6&#93;</a></sup> 
</p><p>Charles Grosset notes that the Passion of St. Helier, written in the 10th or 11th century, draws upon two very much earlier lives of St. Marculf (A and B), and amends them to suit the narrative.
Grosset's conclusion is that the life of St. Helier is extremely poorly documented, and like Balleine, he considers it largely fictional. He sees the writer as having "been given the task of writing a life of the hermit Helier, who lived in Jersey and has a few bare facts known about him: the cave where he lived, healing of the sick, and death at the hands of pirates. He discovered a similar sounding name to Helier in the district of Tongres, and also a hermit called Eletus in the Life of St. Marcouf. He did not hesitate to identify Helier with the near namesake in Tongres, or to make an identification with Eletus, taking the story of a miracle set on an island whose place-name was not to be found on the map."
</p><p>According to A.M. Bellows, the oldest Life of St. Marculf mentions an island called Agna with only thirty inhabitants and a hermit called Eletus. This has been identified with Jersey and St. Helier, but this is largely a reading back into the story the identifications made in the Passion of St. Helier, a much later work. In Bellows's opinion that Jersey could have so few inhabitants (thirty) at the time compared to Guernsey, in the much better documented visits of St. Sampson, stretches credulity too far. If a Channel Island is chosen, one the size of Herm would be more suitable.<sup id="cite_ref-bellows_7-0" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bellows-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>That there was a town given the name "St. Helier" is not by itself proof that Helier existed, or, if he did exist, visited Jersey. The original attribution might have been to St. Hilary of Poitiers, and became corrupted over time, particularly during the Dark Ages, when the Diocese of Dol was laid waste by invasions of pagans. However, the hermitage rock and linked Priory on the Islet of Elizabeth Castle have a long history. There would certainly seem to be enough evidence to support the idea of a hermit, and later, an eremitic community which gradually evolved.<sup id="cite_ref-bellows_7-1" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bellows-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p><p>Further, the Passion of St. Helier was written at a much later date, when the original attributions had been masked by time; it is clearly a work which draws upon any available sources of other Saints for stories, and it is this Life that makes the identification of Marculf's Eletus with Helier.<sup id="cite_ref-bellows_7-2" class="reference"><a href="#cite_note-bellows-7">&#91;7&#93;</a></sup>
</p>
